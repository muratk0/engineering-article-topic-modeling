Cleaning unit thoroughly degreases parts

Dürr Ecoclean presents the EcoCCore machine for solvent-based parts cleaning. The machine, which operates under full vacuum conditions, comes with two flood tanks, heat recovery, and full-flow plus bypass filtration capabilities. It is designed to use halogenated hydrocarbons or modified alcohols. Switching from one solvent to the other is simple, making the machine adaptable to changes in the production process.

The new preliminary vapor degreasing process sends the oil-rich solvent into a distillation system rather than into the flood tank. This helps minimize oil deposits in the flood tank and prevents an undesirable oil saturation of the solvent. This allows thorough degreasing of parts that will be coated or laser welded after cleaning. Ultrasonics and filtration can be used simultaneously. With this feature, particles are removed from the work chamber throughout this process step.

CNC machine simulation/verification software streamlines NC validation work flow

CGTech will feature Vericut version 8. The CNC machine simulation, verification, and optimization software simulates all types of CNC machining, including drilling and trimming of composite parts, waterjet, riveting, robots, mill/turn, and parallel kinematics. The software operates independently but also can be integrated with leading CAM systems.

Machine simulation detects collisions and near-misses among all machine tool components such as axis slides, heads, turrets, rotary tables, spindles, tool changers, fixtures, workpieces, and cutting tools. Users also can set up near-miss zones around components to check for close calls and detect over-travel errors. Machine movements and material replacement can be simulated while stepping or playing backwards.

The software can read 3-D solid model data for the simulation process. Many of the 3-D models are available via the Machining Cloud app, and this version of the software has been designed to take advantage of more Machining Cloud metadata.

Magnetic-bed drag flight conveyor removes fines, chips from coolant

Jorgensen’s Mag-Drag

®

magnetic-bed drag flight conveyors are designed to remove broken or fine cast-iron or steel chips from coolant in metal cutting applications.

Steel and cast-iron chips and fines flow with the coolant onto the load section of the conveyor.

Gravity causes larger chips to settle to the bottom, while finer chips (still in suspension) are attracted by a series of magnets located underneath the conveyor. All of the chips then are conveyed out by the drag cleats.

The conveyors are suitable for use in chip removal, coolant filtration, plasma cutting, sawing, and feeder/transfer applications. They can be custom-engineered to meet specifications for length and width, with cleated belt sizes of 1.5- and 2.5-in. pitch and with a choice of belt designs, drive systems, safety equipment, and controls.
