# Growing interest in digital manufacturing technologies

[TARİH: 01.11.2020 ISMR]

## HP DIGITAL MANUFACTURING TRENDS REPORT Key Areas of Opportunity: Economic Advancement, Accelerated Innovation, Increased Collaboration and Sustainable Production

- 99% of global respondents believe that digital manufacturing technologies can lead to economic growth
- 90% of respondents indicated that collaboration across sectors to embrace new digital manufacturing technologies is important
- 79% of respondents said that additive manufacturing/3D printing helps their company be more agile
- TRANSFORMATIVE: 77% of respondents said they are investigating new production/supply chain models
- Automotive, industrial and healthcare were called out as the industries most ripe for innovation in additive manufacturing/3D printing over the next five years.

Digital manufacturing technologies in the UK are spurring economic advancement, agility, innovation and sustainable production. This comes as nearly all respondents (99%) surveyed in HP’s latest Digital Manufacturing Trend Report believe that digital manufacturing technologies can lead to economic growth. As a result, said HP, ‘decision-makers are looking to transform their business models, with 77% of those surveyed indicating that they will invest in digital manufacturing technologies such as industrial 3D printing, within the next 12 months’.

HP and members of its customer community, along with industry thought leaders, showcased the power of digital manufacturing at its annual global Innovation Summit. HP’s Digital Manufacturing Trend Report underpinned the Summit’s panel discussions, examining the current state and future outlook for digital manufacturing technologies. The benefits of 3D printing were also highlighted.

“The most cited benefit of 3D printing technology by UK respondents is increased speed to market, with 79% of those surveyed agreeing that additive manufacturing/3D printing is helping their businesses to become more agile, whilst four-in-five (86%) are expecting to benefit from economic growth after adopting this technology,” HP told ISMR.

“Manufacturing decision-makers also see opportunities for accelerated innovation with additive manufacturing/3D printing, which is fuelling new areas of innovation in which UK companies are investigating. For example, 91% of respondents are looking to explore how mass customisation could be applicable to their business if parts could be personalised through additive manufacturing/3D printing.”

The medical, industrial and automotive industries were also highlighted as those most ripe for innovation in additive manufacturing/3D printing over the next five years.

“The global manufacturing sector is clearly signalling a desire for greater supply chain resiliency, more manufacturing flexibility, increased speed of innovation and stronger environmental sustainability,” said George Brasher, MD UK & Ireland, HP. “They are seeing industrial 3D printing as a way to not only lower costs and go to market faster, but as a unique competitive advantage that accelerates innovation for customers.”

Digital manufacturing technologies are driving new opportunities for collaboration in the UK, with 82% in HP’s survey indicating that their company have plans to collaborate with government entities on digital manufacturing products.

“Sustainable production continues to be high on the agenda of most manufacturing decisionmakers. Almost nine in ten (89%) agree that the recyclability of 3D-printed powders and parts is a business imperative, whilst respondents were also keen (88%) to see governments incentivise the investment in and development of socially and environmentally beneficial digital manufacturing technologies,” concluded the report.

“The top new areas of innovation, which companies are investigating, are mass customisation for direct-to-consumer products, production on demand and digital warehousing/virtual inventory. In particular, 93% of respondents want to explore the innovation that mass customisation provides and believe it could be applicable to their business if every part could be personalised through 3D printing/additive manufacturing,” said the report.

“Around 81% of respondents indicated that collaboration across sectors to embrace new digital manufacturing technologies is important. Approximately 82% indicated that their company has future plans to collaborate with government entities on digital manufacturing products,” it added.

To address the skills gap, 63% of the report’s respondents want to offer training services to professionals to prepare for additive manufacturing/3D printing, and 52% want to connect companies, governments, institutions and citizens to work together to make investments in educational and jobskilling programmes.

The HP Digital Manufacturing Report is a global study by HP Inc. It was conducted by SME Media/Research across three continents and nine geographic areas including Canada; China; France; Germany; Japan; Mexico; Singapore; the UK and the U.S. Fieldwork was conducted from 28 July – 11 August 2020 across 2,175 3D printing and digital manufacturing decision-makers.

## Report highlights

99% of respondents believe that digital manufacturing technologies can lead to economic growth.

■ 90% of respondents are looking to evolve their business models because of the current world business environment.

77% of respondents indicated that they will invest in digital manufacturing technologies in the next 12 months, with 86% indicating an expected investment increase in additive manufacturing/3D printing.

79% of respondents said that additive manufacturing/3D printing helps their company to be more agile, 79% attested that it is a viable alternative to traditional manufacturing and 75% cited it as a backup to traditional manufacturing.

■ 92% of respondents said they are investigating new production/supply chain models, with 51% in localised production and hybrid models, and 42% in distributed supply chain.
