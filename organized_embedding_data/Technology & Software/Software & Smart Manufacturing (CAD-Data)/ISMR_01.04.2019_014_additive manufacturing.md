# ADDITIVE MANUFACTURING

[TARİH: 01.04.2019 ISMR]

## MAXIMISING PRODUCTION EFFICIENCY

By using 3D printed tools, jigs and fixtures, Volkswagen Autoeuropa has reduced cycle time operation, labour and the need for reworking, while improving tool ergonomics, at a tenth of the usual cost.

The goal was to develop customised solutions to address specific problems, re-engineer application of concepts applied on auxiliary assembly, get rid of paperwork and shrink implementation time.

The initial concept is converted to a digital file, then sent to a 3D printer. Afterwards, the part is post-processed and evaluated through functional testing, saving both money and time. The results, said Volkswagen Autoeuropa, are cost and time improvements, robustness and process flexibility as well as innovation.

## Introduction

Traditionally, subtractive processes focus on manufacturing tools for mass production or the production of stringent requirement components. In contrast, the additive approach dominates rapid prototyping processes – providing much greater flexibility in the necessary construction time.

With Ultimaker's 3D printers, Volkswagen Autoeuropa could test solutions without having to contact suppliers, saving considerable time. As a result, the manufacturer told ISMR, purchasing costs were reduced (by 91% when compared to working with external suppliers), implementation time cut (95%), ergonomic improvements were made, assembly processes and quality indices improved and potential problems were easily anticipated at the concept stage.

ISMR SAYS:

“3D printing enabled Volkswagen Autoeuropa to test construction and assembly tools, reduce its development times by 95% and avoid the bureaucratic process of dealing with suppliers. Volkswagen Autoeuropa now has a number of Ultimaker 3D printers in operation, with 93% of manufactured tools previously made externally now created in-house.”

"Volkswagen Autoeuropa's short-term goal is to create more prototypes, gauges, tools and spare parts in-house, reducing development time and acceptance testing even further. We provide the flexibility and autonomy for continuous improvement, with a direct impact on ergonomics and quality. In the future, it will look to further expand these benefits – with the large series production which will be applied to the final product," explained Ultimaker.

## Challenge and solution

Before working with Ultimaker, Volkswagen Autoeuropa used third-party suppliers to manufacture its tools. The process took several weeks, especially when multiple designs or assemblies were required. It also meant more paperwork, quotations and the adoption of a trial-and-error approach, all of which were holding up the tool manufacturing process – at additional cost.

After validating the concept in 2014, Volkswagen Autoeuropa now has a number of Ultimaker 3D printers in operation, with 93% of manufactured tools previously made externally now created in-house. Within two years, its assembly-tooling cost savings rose – from 70% to 95%. Ultimaker enabled Volkswagen Autoeuropa to test solutions without having to contact suppliers, reducing the time taken by an average of eight weeks.

## Results

Volkswagen Autoeuropa can now avoid processes that waste the company’s time and money, which benefits the team and improves the final product. As Luis Pascoa, Pilot Plant Manager, Volkswagen Autoeuropa, explained: "It's a simple process – we just convert our idea to a 3D file, send to the 3D printer, postprocess the part, evaluate with functional testing and finish by implementing the idea."

"Our technology enabled Volkswagen Autoeuropa to make even complex concepts a reality; develop ideas quickly and easily; 3D-print concepts on the same day as design – shrinking the development process from months to a matter of days and reduce costs by testing prototypes, rather than redesigning or altering an existing mould," added Ultimaker.

The reduction in time taken and cost to produce prototypes led to a higher ROI, a better quality product and more customer satisfaction.

“3D printing developments result in a 91% cost reduction and 95% reduction in tool development time. Ultimaker makes it possible to improve tool ergonomics by 28% and the final product quality by 35%.”

— Helena Trincheiras, Pilot Plant Engineer, Volkswagen Autoeuropa

"The reduction in time taken and cost to produce prototypes led to a higher ROI, a better quality product and more customer satisfaction. Innovative 3D printed products from Volkswagen Autoeuropa are being used in several applications and are considered best practices in the Volkswagen group. 3D printing enabled it to test construction and assembly tools, reduce its development times by 95% and avoid the bureaucratic process of dealing with suppliers. By printing prototypes in-house, Volkswagen Autoeuropa achieved a 91% cost reduction (approximately 325,000 per year)," it concluded.
