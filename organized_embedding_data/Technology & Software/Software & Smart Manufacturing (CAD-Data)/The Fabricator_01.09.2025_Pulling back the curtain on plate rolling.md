# Pulling back the curtain on plate rolling

[TARİH: 01.09.2025 The Fabricator]

Tech Spotlight

Electric motors and advanced controls help even novice machine operators to avoid expensive rework

By

Dan Davis

Davi’s AI-Vision technology uses a laser scan to capture hundreds of data points from the plate’s curved surface and then applies AI to analyze the information. Instructions are then fed to the equipment’s controls, and the plate rolling machine’s side rolls are maneuvered into position so that form can be done with minimal adjustments.

Davi’s MCE e-POWER all-electric plate rolling machine and the AI-Vision laser scanning technology get metal fabricators closer to being able to produce consistent forms without an experienced machine operator behind the controls.

M

etal fabricating companies, in general, have claimed to have had trouble finding skilled workers for years. Now, imagine the situation in which shops that specialize in the fabrication of tanks and vessels find themselves: They are looking for machine operators with expertise in the art of plate rolling.

Plate rolling technology has come a long way since the days of simple pyramid-style machines. In fact, it wasn’t that long ago that initial-pinch three-roll machines—where a machine operator had to feed the plate in for an initial prebend, remove the plate, rotate it 180 degrees, and feed the plate in the opposite direction to create the final spherical shape—was considered state of the art.

Today, four-roll plate rolling is the technology of choice for fab shops. The rolls are always in contact with the plate, and the prebend and subsequent forms all can be done without removing the plate from the rollers. Plate rolling jobs now can be done in a few minutes, when in the past, they might take much longer.

But challenges still await even the most knowledgeable of plate rolling machine operators. The material is never the same.

"In some instances, if the plate is large enough, you might find that even across the plate, it’s different," said Bryan Noack, national service manager, Davi. "That variability is one of our greatest challenges.

"If you’re looking at a press brake, you’re only bending across one line, but when you’re plate rolling, there’s an infinite amount of points that you’re forming and variability across the plate, especially on low-grade steels."

Even an operator-friendly four-roll machine without CNC capabilities still requires an expert’s touch. For example, an increase in pinch pressure without a corresponding side roll adjustment could result in a roll that is too tight, leading to some very expensive scrap. That’s why these types of machines have unofficial manuals—spiral notebooks with handwritten notes for specific procedures and variable conditions—or even tape measures stuck on machines with Sharpie marks on them to note suggested forming adjustments.

The next generation of plate rolling technology promises to be able to overcome the need for experienced and talented machine operators. Davi believes its fully electric plate rolling machine with laser measuring technology for in-process quality monitoring during forming could make it possible for even a novice to turn out forms that meet customer specifications on the first try.

The Era of Fully Electric Plate Rolling

Electric plate rolling machines have been around for a few years now, but those were actually hybrid machines, where electric motors handled plate rotation and hydraulics still powered forming. It was an improvement over fully hydraulic machines because the hydraulics on the hybrid machines could be dedicated solely to forming, not rotating the material.

Davi officials report that its MCE e-Power electric machine, where every single axis is controlled by electrical inverters, can deliver a positioning accuracy of 0.05 in., compared to the typical 0.1 in. for standard machines. Because it is not limited by a finite reservoir of hydraulic fluid, the machine can deliver rotation and forming movements smoothly and with consistent power, which is helpful in creating complex shapes of various radii.

"An operator can theoretically control every axis at the same time," Noack said.

A New Vision

Davi also has introduced its AI-Vision laser scanner technology to take advantage of the precision that the all-electric MCE e-POWER can deliver. Instead of relying on a laser to focus on a single point on the plate during rolling, AI-Vision uses what the company calls a "laser blade," which covers hundreds of data points across the curved surface.

"That’s where the artificial intelligence comes in," Noack said. "It can capture all that data and then weed out the erroneous factors that affect the forming process."

For example, the laser scan might pick up on a hole cut in the plate, and the AI can exclude that from any computations related to adjusting pressure during forming. The AI also is capable of minimizing the influence of hard spots, material irregularities, surface mill scale, and even shadows on the material, all of which could skew measurements.

After the data is collected, AI and an algorithm are used to calculate the necessary adjustments that get the plate closer to final specifications. The CNC is able to take that information and automatically tweak the side roll positioning.

AI-enhanced plate rolling also means that the machine can learn and discern the inconsistencies between various types and grades of material. As the machine is used, it will have collected enough data to account for the multiple variables and correct the rolling process without operator intervention.

This type of smart manufacturing technology doesn’t totally eliminate an operator’s involvement, however.

"As I’m in the field teaching fabricators how to use this machine, I tell them the operator has to build consistency into the process," Noack said. "Once a consistent process is in place, the only big variable remaining is the material, which none of us can control."

As of now, Noack said the all-electric plate rolling machines can handle material up to about 2.5 in. thick.

Metal fabricators without CNCs on their plate rolling machines aren’t totally excluded from taking advantage of this AI-powered quality tool. Another Davi measurement system, iRadius, which functions like an app on a smartphone, is used as the conduit for delivering necessary adjustments to the equipment’s roll after the curved plate is scanned and the data is reviewed. An operator can look at the information and manually alter the rolls, getting them closer to a quality form and reducing the risk of rework or even scrapping of the plate.

Technology introductions like this are one more step in changing an art into a repeatable and consistent manufacturing process. Understanding the ins and outs of an older machine and the mechanical nuances of rolling plate on it are no longer a requirement thanks to these advancements.
