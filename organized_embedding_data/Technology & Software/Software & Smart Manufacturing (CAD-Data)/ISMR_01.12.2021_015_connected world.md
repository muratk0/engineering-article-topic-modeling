# CONNECTED WORLD

[TARİH: 01.01.2021 ISMR]

## More and more machine manufacturers “are realising that accelerating their digitalisation plans in response to the pandemic will give them a competitive advantage,” explained Dr. Bernard Cubizolles, Senior Global Marketing Manager, GE Digital LLC. “Digitalising their machines also gives manufacturers increased control over their machinery.”

Industry is facing major challenges. Customers are demanding new, individual, high-quality and inexpensive products in shorter and shorter periods of time. At the same time, goods must be manufactured from increasingly scarce resources, as sustainably as possible. To meet these requirements, research and business are turning to the digitalisation of manufacturing in which real and virtual worlds converge in an Internet of Things, services and data.

Here machines, workpieces, means of transport and goods are equipped with embedded systems, i.e. tiny computers, sensors and actuators and are connected with one another. This paves the way for the next jump in manufacturing, Industry 4.0.

## The digital transformation of industry

But how will the smart manufacturing of the future work?

“In the future all machines, from the [cutting] machine to the welding robot, will be networked with one another. And every workpiece will have its own embedded system, storing various pieces of information for example of the customer, the workpiece’s desired configuration and its destination. It will be possible to uniquely identify and localise raw parts. Not only will they know the processing steps necessary, but these parts will also be networked with the production machines and will be able to communicate with one another to decide exactly when they are to undergo which production step,” explained industrial research specialist, Fraunhofer-Gesellschaft.
