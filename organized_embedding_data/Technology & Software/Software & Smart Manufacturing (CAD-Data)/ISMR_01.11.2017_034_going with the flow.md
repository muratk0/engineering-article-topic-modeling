# GOING WITH THE FLOW

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

Stephen Bruner, VP Marketing, OMAX Corporation

ISMR SAYS:

"After launching two new products this year, OMAX showcased its entire waterjet portfolio at this year's FABTECH exhibition in Chicago"

ISMR sat down with Stephen Bruner, its VP Marketing, for his view on market trends, user expectations and the business climate in 2018.

OMAX® Corporation is a leading manufacturer of advanced abrasive waterjet systems. Owner of the OMAX, MAXIEM and GlobalMAX brands, the company designs waterjet systems that feature intuitive software, efficient pump technology and a wide range of accessories. It also designs, manufactures, assembles and tests components as a complete system.

The OMAX® 60120 JetMachining® Center was on display at Blechexpo in Stuttgart, Germany, and uses advanced abrasive waterjet technology to cut complex parts measuring up to five feet x ten feet. This bridge-style machine is equipped with the company's intuitive Intelli-MAX® Software Suite to deliver faster cutting speeds and increased uptime. When combined with the EnduroMAX® Pump, the machine is designed to provide high levels of precision.

After launching two new products this year, the GlobalMAX and ProtoMAX waterjet cutting systems, the US manufacturer showcased its entire waterjet portfolio at this year's FABTECH exhibition in Chicago. ISMR sat down with Stephen Bruner, its VP Marketing, for his view on market trends, user expectations and the business climate in 2018.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes, achievements or awards?

SB: 2017 has been a very exciting year for us. We launched the GlobalMAX industrial waterjet (1508, 1530 and 2040 models) and we just launched a new product, ProtoMAX. This is a compact personal waterjet for academic institutions, metal shops and hobbyists etc.

The GlobalMAX has three-axis capability. It has strong linear positional accuracy and the table features an innovative drive system. We spent 10,000 hours of accelerated testing on it before releasing it into the market. We launched it earlier this year in Beijing and have already taken it to several international exhibitions.

ProtoMAX is a personal waterjet with a very powerful 5hp pump delivering about 30,000 psi pressure for strong cutting capability. The compact system can fit through a doorway (12 x 12 cutting area) – it is very suitable for universities/academic institutions, technical/vocational schools, shop classes and maker stations, hobbyists etc. and can fit nicely into a garage. We launched it on 24 September 2017 and took it to FABTECH in Chicago.

We also won several awards this year. One of our products was a finalist for the R&D100 awards. We were also given a U.S. government award for companies that use grant monies to manufacture products that are a commercial success. We also received a U.S. National Science Foundation award. I am particularly proud to confirm that, this year, we also achieved our ISO 9001/2015 certification.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

SB: We see that economies are growing well this year. The U.S. and Asian economies are growing well and Europe is very strong this year.

## New abrasive waterjet line at EMO 2017

At EMO Hannover 2017, OMAX® Corporation showcased its newly released GlobalMAX 1530 abrasive waterjet. The OMAX 60120 and the latest generation MicroMAX abrasive waterjet systems were also on display during the show from 18-23 September 2017 in Hannover, Germany. Visitors could watch live cutting demonstrations on all three waterjets and see time studies run on the control software by software experts.

The bridge-style OMAX 60120 abrasive waterjet can cut complex parts of up to 1.52m x 3.05 m. The 60120 machine at EMO was fitted with a Tilt-A-Jet cutting head, demonstrating how OMAX waterjets can cut with virtually zero taper on part edges without slowing down cutting speed, suitable for work requiring tight tolerances and quick turn-around times.

Visitors to the OMAX stand also saw the latest generation of the MicroMAX with OMAX's proprietary linear traction drive system that uses optical encoders to provide 0.1 micron positional accuracy. The MicroMAX's cutting table is mounted on a set of vibration isolators to minimise transmission of external vibration from the tank. A tank-cooling package allows control of the tank water temperature, helping to avoid any dimensional changes in the material being machined.

This translates into strengthening of demand for waterjet cutters. This is a great trend and we have no reason to believe that this trend will change, so we have a positive outlook for 2018.

In general, the machine tool industry has been through some tough years, where growth has stagnated or demand has dropped off, but 2017 has been a real turning point. Around 60% of our sales are made in the U.S.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers and how are you addressing these issues for them?

SB: I would nail it down to two things; equipment uptime and equipment versatility. Uptime can range from how quickly it takes an employee to learn how to use a machine and its software to keeping the machine running for as long as possible.

We know that when demand is strong, it is hard to find skilled employees. Manufacturers want machine tools that are intuitive and easy to use, where not a lot of training is involved. One of the ways that we meet that need is to continuously develop our Intelli-MAX control software so that it is intuitive and easy to use. Users can import different CAD drawings into the software, which also has an Intelli-TRACE feature that can path a part created from an image. It is smart, fourth generation software that will figure out the best pathway to cut a part for an operator.

We are also focusing on further developing the equipment itself. A good example is our EnduroMAX pump, which needs fewer maintenance cycles for longer uptime. We also released a garnet removal system this year so that users do not have to take their waterjets offline to get the garnet out of the tank. The system removes and pumps the garnet to a location that can be up to 100 feet away from the waterjet itself. These initiatives are all focused upon keeping waterjet owners cutting parts and not spending precious time maintaining machines and training employees. We are looking for the maximum amount of machine uptime.

We also provide software, hardware, pumps and a range of equipment for waterjet cutting systems – in effect, we are a one-stop shop (one of the very few left). As a fully integrated supplier of abrasive waterjets, this gives our customers the reassurance they need that spare parts, service and help is all from one source. We are not dependent on other manufacturers or suppliers.

Customers are also interested in machine versatility. Abrasive waterjet is a "material-agnostic" cutting process that lacks the dependencies or limitations (such as magnetic or reflective properties) of other technologies. Having such a machine tool, with built-in versatility, is good for our customers – they can compete for a wide range of business. The maximum metal thickness that our waterjets can cut is 12 inches.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal waterjet cutting markets?

SB: The move towards automated and smart systems is a key trend. The idea is that you can have a shop full of machine tools that can be controlled from one central point. Those machine tools also provide data and analytics to predict when maintenance is needed or if potential problems are on the horizon.

We continue to develop our software systems and waterjet cutting systems with that goal in mind. The Waterjet 4.0 approach intuitively makes sense.

ISMR: What was the focus of your booth presence at FABTECH this November?

SB: At FABTECH, we introduced our GlobalMax waterjet to the U.S. market. We also promoted the ProtoMAX waterjet at the show. It was the first trade fair where OMAX had its complete product line on the booth, which was pretty exciting for us.
