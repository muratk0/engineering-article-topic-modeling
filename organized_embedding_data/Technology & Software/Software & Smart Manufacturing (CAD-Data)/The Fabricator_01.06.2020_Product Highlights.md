Autonomous bin picking kit designed for machine tending applications

The ActiNav application kit from Universal Robots is designed to help companies of all sizes simplify the integration of autonomous bin picking of parts and accurate placement in machines using collaborative robots. The system synchronously handles vision processing, collision-free motion planning, and autonomous real-time robot control.

The system requires no vision or robotic programming expertise but is based on a teach-bydemonstration principle using a six-step, wizardguided setup process integrated into the cobot teach pendant.

The system autonomously inserts parts into CNC or processing machines for drilling, deburring, welding, trimming, and tapping. The high-resolution 3D sensor and CAD matching enables high-accuracy picks powered by the autonomous motion module, which determines how to pick the part, then controls the robot to pick the part and place it in a fixture each time. The autonomous motion control enables the system to operate inside deep bins.

Welding station designed for heavy-duty welding

Pemamek has introduced the Pema Nozzle welding station for pressure vessels and other heavy-duty welding applications. It automates the nozzle welding operation to help cut the labor hours required for multilayer welding processes and reduce defects. Built-in lasers scan groove geometry in the workpieces, which typically contain large prepared grooves and thick plates, and automatically create robotic welding programs that adapt to the varying groove layers by continuously welding the grooves until a solid bond is formed. Any discrepancies between the preprogrammed groove geometry and the actual laser-scanned geometry are adjusted using WeldControl software.

Production data monitoring, diagnostics, tracking, and robot programming can be performed by one operator on a one-panel control system on the shop floor.

SMAW/liftGTAW inverter delivers 180 amps at 25% duty cycle

ESAB Welding & Cutting Products has introduced the Miniarc Rogue ES 180i, a portable SMAW/lift GTAW inverter. It features adjustable hot start, adjustable arc force, and power factor correction. The 18.2-lb. unit is 13.5 by 6 by 10.4 in.; delivers a maximum output of 180 amps at 25% duty cycle; and uses 115- to 230-V, 50-/60-Hz primary power.

The inverter’s control technology produces a smooth welding arc with all types of stick electrodes, including E70818 and E6010, and it can run 5/32-in. electrodes. The lift GTAW function provides positive arc starts without the use of high frequency. It delivers a stable GTAW arc down to 10 amps for work on thin metal or delicate components.

Welding helmet communicates with power source via Bluetooth

Fronius has developed the Vizor Connect welding helmet, which communicates with a power source via Bluetooth. Using the manufacturer’s PreTrigger technology, the power source sends a signal to the welding helmet as soon as the user presses the on button on the welding torch, allowing the helmet to darken before the arc is ignited. The helmet also eliminates the problem of interference from external light and darkens even in demanding welding situations to help ensure safety and comfort, the company reports.

The autodarkening filter cartridge remains in place even at the lowest currents and if the arc is concealed and in constrained positions, as long as the arc is burning. The level of protection applicable to the welding process is adjusted automatically between DIN 5 and DIN 12. In this way, the darkness level is adapted to arc intensity at all times. The welder also can switch to manual mode and adjust the darkness infinitely as required.

When the helmet is not darkened, it provides a clear view with a brightness level of 2.5, as well as real-color perception delivered by a UV and infrared filter.

Multiprofile system combines three cutting actions in one machine

HGG’s new MPC 450 2.0 multiprofile cutting machine combines three cutting actions into one piece of equipment. The 3D machine can be configured to cut a variety of profiles, including pipes, box sections, angle bars, and flat sheet metal strips. Its capabilities are multiplied with the incorporation of the manufacturer’s ProCAM software and a laser scanner. Users can configure the equipment to cut pipes, positioning material on pipe supports and clamping it in three chuck jaws; box sections, positioning material on pipe supports in rotators and then clamping it in two diagonally opposite jaws of the chuck; or profiles from bar flat stock, positioning and individually fixing material on a flat cutting table.

The machine is programmed using the manufacturer’s MDI or ProCAD software, which converts CAD files into ready-to-cut formats. ProGRAM software provides a direct link from independent workstations to the cutting machine.
