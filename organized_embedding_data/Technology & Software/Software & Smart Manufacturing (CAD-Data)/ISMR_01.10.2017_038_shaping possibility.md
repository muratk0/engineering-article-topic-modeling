Hypertherm discussed its technologies, as well as new ideas on cutting applications and end-user perspectives on Industry 4.0, with ISMR.

ISMR SAYS:

"Hypertherm presented its new X-Definition™ class of plasma at the Schweissen and Schneiden exhibition

Possibility', cutting specialist Hypertherm stormed into the Schweissen and Schneiden exhibition in Düsseldorf with new technologies and striking new ideas on applications and enduser perspectives on Industry 4.0 and the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT). Interactive tables allowed visitors to browse product information and the Virtual Reality game 'Be amaze-d' provided its booth visitors with an interactive experience.

Hypertherm designs and manufactures advanced cutting products for use in a variety of industries such as shipbuilding, manufacturing and automotive repair. Its product lines include plasma, laser and waterjet cutting systems, in addition to CNC motion and height controls, CAM nesting software, robotic software and consumables. The company's reputation for cutting innovation dates back nearly 50 years to 1968, with its water injection plasma cutting. The 100 per cent associate-owned company has more than 1,400 associates along with operations and partner representation worldwide.

At its booth at the Schweissen and Schneiden exhibition, Hypertherm presented its new X-Definition™ class of plasma as well as networked live cutting demonstrations. Metal artist, Marcel Boonen, showed the advanced capabilities of the latest generation of Powermax® plasma cutters, the Powermax45 XP, by creating steel paintings and other metal art live on the stand. Through a live, online connection with Hypertherm's Cutting Technology Centre at its EMEA headquarters in the Netherlands, the cut quality and faster cut speeds of its new XPR300™ and X-Definition™ plasma were demonstrated to show visitors. In addition to the cutting schedule, visitors could also request demonstrations.

## X-definition cutting

Hypertherm introduced a new generation cutting system in its Powermax® line of light industrial plasma cutters, advanced technologies for high-end automated plasma cutting at EuroBLECH 2016. Advanced HyPerformance® plasma technologies, including SureCut™ applications like True Hole® and True Bevel™ as well as HyPrecision™ Waterjet cutting, making use of advanced connectivity options that support Industry 4.0 applications, were highlighted on its booth at Schweissen and Schneiden.

"The major breakthrough product for us here at Schweissen and Schneiden, is the XPR300. We have been working on this now for seven years and it truly is a breakthrough technology in plasma. XPR300 was launched earlier this year. We are known for highdefinition cutting generally, but the XPR300 moves plasma processing into X-definition cutting, which means the best plasma cut quality over the life of the consumables," Theo Cornielje, Regional Director for the EMEA region, Hypertherm told ISMR.

"There has been a trend for people to think mainly of laser for material cutting - they do not instantly recognise plasma as a modern and up-to-date technology. The XPR300 changes this and people will really see the attributes of plasma cutting on this system."

Hypertherm's X-Definition™ plasma class incorporates new technologies such as Vented Water Injection™ (VWI), plasma dampening and Vent-to-Shield technologies. VWI technology generates water around the cutting surface to optimise the quality of the product being cut. The result is squarer cut edges, markedly less angularity and strong surface finish on non-ferrous metals such as aluminium and stainless steel, Hypertherm told ISMR.

"A strong USP for this technology is a significant reduction in operating cost on different materials. It can almost cut this in half for operators on most materials. We are now moving towards cut quality on stainless steel and aluminium which, on certain material thicknesses, can be better than laser cut quality. If the material is thicker than 5- or 6mm, plasma can be a preferred technology, especially as regards cutting speed. So, we are repositioning plasma cutting into areas where it needs to be," explained Cornielje.

"There is a lot of smart technology in the power source, which can have a very strong impact on consumable life. When users cut material (in day-to-day operations), the system should stop while it is still cutting or on the material. In some systems, however, the torch runs off the plate at the end of the cutting cycle. This drastically reduces the life of consumables. The new power sources recognise this and automatically move into 'ramdown' that saves a considerable amount of consumable life. It is far less operatordependent than past systems."

The system also delivers a lot of data, directly from the power supply. This fits with Industry 4.0 and IoT principles. The user can see what is happening on the product and how efficiently is it being used etc. All the critical data for users is provided by the system itself. The system is supplied with different variations of gas consoles (the power supply is always the same). Depending upon the type of material a user wishes to cut, he can select the required gas console.

"Angularity, particularly of stainless steel, has always been a challenge i.e. to get close to straight cutting. We have made tremendous progress on cut quality and flexibility as well as significantly reducing operating costs. Cost is always a much better indicator than price. The first systems are already operational with European customers (over 100) and we are getting some very positive feedback on them," added Theo Cornelje.

## Looking to the future

In terms of strategy and philosophy Hypertherm has consistently followed two major routes; it aims to better understand the end-user of its products and it has focused on becoming a multi-technology systems supplier.

"For a number of years now, we have been getting to know and understand the needs and application requirements of the end-users of our products. This is also important so that we can develop products that address current and future customer needs. We are always looking to simplify cutting operations - it is getting harder and harder for users to find and keep trained operators. This means ensuring that people get what they pay for and aligning ourselves with our partners to ensure that end-user needs are fulfilled," Theo Cornelje told ISMR.

In terms of becoming a multi-technology systems supplier, Hypertherm has taken steps in this direction with the acquisition of a U.S. waterjet manufacturer some years ago. It now develops its own line of waterjet pumps and has made considerable progress on reducing the operating costs of these pumps for users. It continues to invest in expanding its waterjet portfolio as well as its waterjet teams around the world.

"We are also still exploring the laser space, albeit to a smaller extent," Cornelje told ISMR. "We already manufacture consumables and are examining how we can position ourselves in the market and not become a 'me too' player. We also intend to expand into other metalworking technologies, including new or neighbouring technologies to metal cutting. Our mission has always been to cut the cost of cutting."

As a result of opportunities that end-users have signalled to Hypertherm, it is now focusing much more on the software that it provides.

"Everything is software-driven now and we have a significant software division in Hypertherm. Without improving and owning what you are doing in software, it is virtually impossible to impact the cutting application. We want to be able to deliver a better experience to customers, led by software and Industry 4.0/IoT. However, in all of this, Big Data has to be manageable and valuable for users," concluded Theo Cornelje.

Proudly celebrating its 70th anniversary this year, LCM is taking an industrial path that connects the future of metal forming technology and blurs the boundaries between different industries. As one of the most senior press manufacturers in Taiwan, LCM sees 2017 as a year of new initiatives – redefining the concept of metal forming and targeting its next goals and objectives to meeting the future requirements of advanced manufacturing.

ISMR talked to LCM's President, Patrick Wang, the third generation of the company's founding family, for an in-depth understanding of the advanced hydraulic systems offered by the company. It also asked Patrick Wang to share his views on the company's prospects in the year of its 70th anniversary.

## A global outlook

"Lien Chieh Machinery (LCM) was founded in 1947. Our expertise is in developing hydraulic presses and related technological innovations. In the early days, the company manufactured basic hydraulic equipment (e.g. table lifters for plywood) and later turned its hand to exploring hot forming technology. Today, we have expanded the range of LCM's hydraulic presses and transformed traditional metalforming techniques for higher performances – especially in hydroforming and deep drawing," Patrick Wang, LCM President, told ISMR.

"LCM's presses can be found today in many modern industries. We supply sectors including automotive; energy; electronics; home appliances; construction; composite materials; military/defence; green transportation and top industrial labs."

LCM has witnessed altering tides in the sheet metal forming industry. Decades of hand-on experience has enabled it to analyse and study current trends in the sheet metal sector. Patrick Wang told ISMR that the company's strength is in filling the gaps between users' ever stricter production standards and stamping press performance.

"LCM has lived through seven decades of global metal forming development and has experienced many different phases of change in this industry. In short, the identity of hydraulic press manufacturers keeps transforming – from the early days of high labour reliance, large energy consumption and relatively higher environmental cost to a revolutionised industry today. We concentrate nowadays on building energy-efficient, automated and eco-friendly presses," he explained.

Patrick Wang emphasised its strong global outlook which aims to satisfy different customer structures. LCM's international service centres are set up across continents.
