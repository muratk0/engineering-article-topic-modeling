# GOING WITH THE GRAIN

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

Rapid production, increased accuracy and significant material savings resulted from a bespoke macro developed for Shine Food Machinery by Radan.

ISMRSAYS:

“A commercial kitchen manufacturer uses Radan software for its sheetmetal nesting operations”

A bespoke Radan macro, written specially for a commercial kitchen manufacturer, has overcome a major design issue by revolutionising its sheet metal project nesting.

Shine Food Machinery needs the grain flow and top faces on its sheet metal components to be consistent, for the aesthetics of its product. And with the macro, Radan's project nesting ensures that Shine's requirements are met every time.

Neil Thorne, Senior Design Engineer, Shine, described Radan as software that "runs the factory" and is "a vital and integral part of our processes".

## A customised service

Shine has been trading for over forty years, delivering over £100 million of commercial catering equipment/environments in the last decade. Its vast customer base ranges from small cafes and schools to large £2.5 million contracts with the UK Ministry of Defence. Shine's solutions are all delivered in-house from its headquarters in Newport, Wales. Projects start from design through to manufacturing and installation.

Neil Thorne explained: "After we've won a contract, we'll design the individual components for the kitchen, such as the extraction systems, tabling and servery counters, in SolidWorks.

Once the client approves the design and site dimensions are taken, we then provide the factory floor with manufacturing drawings. The components are nested, laser cut, folded, welded and assembled. The finished product is then inspected before being delivered to site to meet installation dates."

With each job completely bespoke, Shine manufactures thousands of different components per year, each requiring its own CNC code. For example, a full servery counter alone could contain 200 different components. Shine uses Radraft, Radprofile and Radnest software to cut every sheet metal product with a Bystronic Bysprint 3015 3kW laser.

"It's very important that the parts we manufacture are accurate and within tolerance. Without this, we would encounter problems at the assembly stage. We find Radan flatpatterns and nests the components extremely accurately from SolidWorks," said Neil Thorne.

## Development of the 'F'-stop

"For the aesthetics of our finished installations, we’re governed by grain direction on the sheets. We can’t have neighbouring cabinets with the grain going in different directions…the grain all has to go the same way. This was a problem for us because, when the project was going into Radan, the software was nesting in the best orientation for sheet utilisation, and sometimes we didn't have the correct top face and the grain was in the wrong direction. We needed to overcome this without having to nest everything manually,” explained Neil Thorne.

After considerable discussions with Radan's brand manager, Olaf Körner, and Radan's team of developers, a macro was created specifically for Shine to solve this issue.

"As a company, Radan was challenged by Shine's specific requirements. The high volume of parts in a project, and the high-quality materials used, required a highly automated – and, at the same time – very reliable process. With input from the engineers at Shine, we put together a specification which allowed us to put a process known as the 'F-stop' in place, which allows Shine to produce the kitchens with minimal risk of costly errors," explained Olaf Körner.

At the design stage in SolidWorks, a 5mm letter 'F' is put on the top face of the panel, which indicates the correct grain direction. Neil Thorne explained: "Thanks to the macro, when Radan is flat patterning it determines from the F-Stop which is the top surface and which way the grain has to go. Radan then nests with the correct top face and the grain going in the right direction, rather than purely to optimise sheet usage. This is extremely important for the aesthetics of our finished installations. And it stops issues on the factory floor; previously things could be nested either in the wrong orientation or upside down, and even folded the wrong way in the press brake, and had to be scrapped. The amount of scrap we now produce has reduced dramatically."

Due to the success of the F-stop macro developed for Shine, Radan is now working to include this feature in a future general release of the software to benefit all its CAD/CAM

customers. Shine has also seen huge time-saving benefits from the standard features of the software.

"From client drawing approval to getting our products to site requires fairly quick turnaround, so as soon as the drawings are approved we need to get them onto the factory floor and manufactured as quickly as possible. Radan enables us to do this, taking days off flat patterning for a big contract," explained Neil Thorne.

"A school, which would have previously taken a few days, now takes just 45 minutes," added design engineer, Steffan Owen.

## An efficient process

Although the volume of scrappage produced has been largely reduced, Radan also allows these remnants to be optimised.

"For offcuts, we use the remnants feature. Our scrap used to go in the bin, but now every offcut is put back in the system and Radan looks at a remnant first, rather than using a new sheet. That way, we're also saving on material," explained Steffan Owen.

Using the projects and labelling functions has also increased efficiency at Shine.

"By using the projects function, we know where everything is. The factory floor relies on it a lot, along with the labelling system. All parts are labelled automatically which is crucial for identifying which components are for which job, what the contract is and the item number. Previously the labels would be handwritten for every part, which was time-consuming – any contract could have well over 1000 individual parts. Now the labels are printed automatically, and our laser operative simply peels them off and puts them on the part, so that every part can be readily identified on the factory floor," added Owen.

The increase in efficiency and production rate in the cutting operation has introduced a need for further investment in the factory to stop a current bottleneck at the bending stage. Shine will shortly be commissioning a third press brake to benefit fully from the increasingly rapid rate at which the flat components are coming off the laser.

In summary, Neil Thorne said: "Radan runs the factory. It's a vital and integral part of our process. It cuts down the amount of time we spend nesting, especially with the F-stop macro. Everything's organised and easy to identify with the labelling system, which is vital to us as many contracts are run with thousands of components. With Radan, we benefit from fast production, accuracy and material savings from the reduction in errors and optimisation of remnants."
