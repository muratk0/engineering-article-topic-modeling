For example, the rapid growth of ecommerce over the years is showing no signs of slowing, with online sales due to account for over 50% of retail sales over the next decade. To keep up with rising demand, industries are turning to solutions which can supplement the existing workforce with robotics, paving the way for the collaborative robot or cobot. By augmenting human labour with cobots that can take on the repetitive or even dangerous tasks, the human workforce can be freed to perform the skilled tasks out of reach of machines.

But, as robotic technology becomes increasingly more sophisticated, enabling robots to perform roles with accuracy, consistency and safety, it has also advanced the ability of robot-to-robot communication around the facility. So, what does this increased robot collaboration mean for businesses?

## The future of collaborative businesses

The more we try to replicate human activity through robotics, the more sensors are required to replace the things that we take for granted as humans. While it is possible to replace a human in many picking and manufacturing aspects, there are still intricacies which can only be handled by a human worker. This is where cobots offer great benefits.

Tasks which can be automated costeffectively will be transitioned, not only helping to reduce time costs but also through error rate reduction and saving on resources required in error correction. Above all, the real gain from cobots is from identifying the tasks in a facility that can be automated or improved with automation, but still keep the human worker doing the tasks which are currently too expensive or intricate to do via a robot.

Often, the process must be changed to adapt for automation or part automation, so the human might be doing part of a task differently to how they did it before.

As technology costs reduce, the capability of the robot increases. Collaboration will switch completely over the next decade; from humans doing all or some of the work, through more and more task sharing and then eventually transitioning to the robot doing virtually all the work needed in a repetitive process.

For human-robot collaboration, tablets, touchscreens, buttons, barcode scans and voice systems can all be used to enable humans to instruct AMRs on tasks to be performed.

All transport collaboration. Currently, there are various vehicle types in operation, a mix of manual drive and autonomous. Often different vehicles have no means to communicate. This leads to transport inefficiencies; vehicles interfering with other vehicles’ pathways; vehicles arriving at points at the wrong times etc. If all vehicles communicate and are consequently aware of each other, intelligent routing will optimise transport to avoid congestion and wait times.

Communication will remain a key factor for human-robot collaboration. If we frequently arrange to meet at a specific time and have no means of communication, it is highly likely that (at some point) one or both parties will end up waiting or being delayed. Alternatively, if you communicate via email, text or call, both parties will remain updated and could spend their time more productively.

## ‘Tea room’ for robots

Robots still need ‘time out’. After spending hours constantly moving around, they will need ‘break time’ to recharge.

Most facilities will have a place to charge robots if there is a regular waiting point but, in many cases, the mobile vehicle does need to move to a charging area where others will be gathered. If the robots are moving around for an eight-hour shift, they are likely to be parked in the charging area for two-eight hours. If the robots do not need to use the charging room, they will certainly need to use the maintenance room.

As we service our cars at fixed intervals, robots need preventative maintenance. The parts that need the most inspection and care take some time to understand, and so the maintenance programs are usually based on years of statistics and performance results. This should prevent them from spending too much time in the robot ‘tea room’ of the future.
