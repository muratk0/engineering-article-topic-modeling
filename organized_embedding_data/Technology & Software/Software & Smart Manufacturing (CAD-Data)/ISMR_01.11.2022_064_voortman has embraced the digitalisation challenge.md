# Voortman has embraced the digitalisation challenge and pursued solutions which make use of the latest digital developments, investing in growth.

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

Voortman Steel Machinery, with more than 500 dedicated employees, has been designing and developing CNC-controlled, high-end machines and solutions for the steel fabrication and steel manufacturing industry for 50 years. With a wide range of products for beam, plate, pipe, flat and angle processing, as well as painting and shotblasting, it has installed numerous systems worldwide through a network of international subsidiaries.

Voortman Steel Machinery and Voortman Steel Construction are part of the Voortman Steel Group. The companies within the Voortman Steel Group reinforce and support each other, and their customers, by exchanging knowledge and market information. As each other's most strategic supplier and customer, synergy is achieved in areas such as innovation, testing, data-analysis etc.

Voortman is continuously developing new solutions and machines and is eager to share its knowledge and experience with customers. It offers interactive webinars and technical support/expertise, covering a broad range of topics and designed to inspire users and outline new ways of working.

## New investments

Voortman has embraced the digitalisation challenge and pursued solutions which make use of the latest digital developments. In that vein, it has poured investment into a new engineering centre and a hyper-modern Experience Centre at Rijssen, The Netherlands.

“We are currently building a huge new engineering centre, which has workspace for more than 250 engineering colleagues, at our head office in The Netherlands. This new building will combine development of our machinery and software solutions. We already have more software engineers in our company then people who build the machine hardware and our expectation is that the number of developers will further increase to keep up with demand in the market. We are asked more and more questions from customers about digitalisation and how to embed this properly in the production process. With our latest innovations, we can answer these questions and help our customers to do a better job," commented Michan Katerbarg, Voortman Steel Machinery's marketing manager.

The Voortman Experience Centre combines the showroom of Voortman Steel Machinery with a high-tech academy for engineers and machine operators, internally and externally. It offers machine and technology demonstrations and organizes events for (potential) buyers, suppliers, branch organisations and other interested parties. Multiple Voortman machines are up and running in the showroom, and custom demonstrations (for specific customer parts) can also be held. This is possible through a physical visit to the showroom but also via a live online demonstration.

“Our mission is to provide solutions to customers which optimise their performance, while challenging ourselves and thinking ahead, focusing on innovation and continuous learning. Making small installations and complex, large machine lines successful has become our specialty over the years. Our service does not end once the complete solution has been installed and delivered. Our service, combined with expertise and the high degree of automation and flexibility in our machinery and solutions, enable customers to optimise their performance. This is especially important in rapidly changing and volatile markets," added Katerbarg.

New Voortman software solutions include EVI (Extended Voortman Insights) which "shows, provides insight to and informs" customers on their machine status.

"Whether the machine is on, off, running or waiting, EVI always stays in touch. It keeps customers up to date with machine status and monitors production either through the EVI app on a mobile phone/ tablet or if users are logged into the EVI webpage on a laptop. The connection is very accessible. If the machine is off or not running properly, users are immediately notified on their devices. It is like an instant message service. This increases the machine's output by preventing downtime as well as improving overall production time. It also avoids the need for the operator to constantly stay physically next to the machine," explained Katerbarg.

Voortman's VACAM operating software, designed to be user-friendly, is included in all its machines. System component behaviour is the same on all machines running VACAM software, and operators can switch between machines without worrying about where commands and settings on the control screen are located after initial VACAM training.

“Our strategic focus will be on our current markets by working even more closely with our customers to help them to get more out of their production processes. We invest heavily in software solutions like EVI and VACAM and have launched a web shop where our customers can easily order their consumables.

Service Level Agreements provide strong service and maintenance. We really act as a partner to our customers and work with them to make them more efficient and successful," Katerbarg told ISMR.

## Key technology launches

Voortman Steel Machinery exhibited for the eleventh time, with an interactive booth, at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Germany, and at FABTECH in Atlanta (USA). Presenting its latest developments in unmanned production, multi-processing, flexibility and real-time feedback, it conducted various demonstrations and showcased its latest machines at the international sheet-metal exhibition.

"Visitors to EuroBLECH were introduced to the world's first automated plate sorting solution, the Voortman Plate Sorter. It is an innovative solution for sorting finished plate parts, building project buffers and optimising logistics route. The Voortman pipe-cutting unit was another new machine at the Hanover trade fair. The TrueVolt technology is combined with a predictive model based on the full cut. This results in constant cutting quality," Katerbarg explained.

"Customers want to perform more production processes on one machine to keep up with the requests of their customers. We therefore developed the Voortman pipe-cutting unit (which can be attached to our moving gantry, plate-processing machines). This increases the number of different end products which can be made on a machine, without users having to invest in a completely new machine," he added.

The manufacturer also showcased the Voortman V633, a rapid drilling, milling and marking machine, at the trade fair in Hanover. "We believe this machine is the fastest in the world," Voortman told ISMR.

"The new Voortman Plate Sorter is the world's first automated plate-sorting solution which will change the entire logistical routing of plate parts to the welding area, with maximum efficiency as a result. The days of sorting plate parts at the machine, missing parts at the welding area and complete chaos in your plate and welding area are over. Multiple phases and projects can be nested together in one plate, increasing efficiency and reducing material costs. Is the whole plate processed? Simply unload the finished parts from the cutting table, put them unsorted in a bin and move the bin to the Voortman Plate Sorter. It will do the rest. It scans and identifies the plates; checks their quality; sorts them into the right bin and stores them in a storage tower," explained Michan Katerbarg.

"This year's EuroBLECH was the stage for us to introduce our latest machines. The aisles around the Voortman V310 with the Voortman pipe-cutting unit and the Voortman Plate Sorter were pleasantly crowded. On the exhibition stand, we set up a special corner with our Red Tooling System (RTS) and presented options for maintenance and support. Service is a very important part of the machine package," commented Katerbarg.

The Red Tooling System (RTS) is a stock cabinet filled with the tooling and consumables needed by customers. All the tools for machines are therefore available at one central point in a machine shop. The RTS system features smart software to give insights on and maintain stock levels. A dashboard indicates current stock levels. When stock needs to be replenished, users receive a notification. Shortly afterwards, Voortman will send over the parts needed to replenish the stock.

"With RTS, we help users with the tool management and ordering process. The automatic ordering process means there are always (new) tools available that fit machines or programs. Maximum uptime of machines is therefore guaranteed and users do not have to worry about the continuity and quality of their production processes," Michan Katerbarg told ISMR.

## Automation in focus

High levels of autonomy speed up part production, reduce machine downtime and enable 24/7 manufacturing.

“Automation is one of our core values. Many of our customers want to run their machines more efficiently, so automation is key for them. Current industry issues, such as labour shortages and flexibility, were hot topics at EuroBLECH. Automation is one of the solutions we can offer to overcome the shortage of welders. The Voortman Fabricator showcases our skills in fully automatic assembling and welding. We have also developed our own Multi System Integration (MSI) solution which means that all our machines can work simultaneously," Katerbarg told ISMR.

“A huge challenge in the steel industry is finding enough skilled labour to get the work done. Although this is a challenge, we also see this as an opportunity to come up with solutions which make it possible to do more work with less people. The skilled workers who create beautiful steel end products can focus on the more difficult and challenging work, while we provide solutions for standardised and easier work. A good example is the Voortman Fabricator iX which we have developed. It is an automatic beam-welding machine which can be placed in a fully automated steel fabrication line," he added.

MSI, also referred to as Multi System Integration, opens the door to fully automated beam production by connecting machines with cross transports, roller conveyors, product buffers and material sensors. Production data only needs to be imported once and MSI then automatically distributes the data to all machines integrated in a production system. Manually transporting materials through the production line can be eliminated by creating one integrated production system.

Leach Structural Steelwork Ltd. in the UK uses a fully automated beam line from Voortman.

In fully automated Voortman processing lines, operators do not need to be present to manually move material between machines with joysticks, but instead can allow the series of sensors and software communication to work on most tasks between loading the raw product and unloading the finished part on the outfeed. Manual control is also available so that operators can step in with manual intervention when needed.

"We have also developed one-button automation on our plate machines. Our next-level VACAM software automates plate production and provides lots of real-time feedback. Our machines have the largest touch panels in the market, and an easy-to-use interface makes it easy to navigate through them. But what really sets our machines apart is what's under the hood. One-button automation means that even the toughest processes can be carried out by anyone, even if they have never done it before," explained Katerbarg.

Unmanned production is a key feature of Voortman's plate-processing machines.

"Our vision is that there's no need to be constantly present at the machine. When it comes to processing plates on the Voortman V310, simply cluster jobs, buffer multiple plates and activate the Unmanned Mode to bypass manual interventions for longer periods. The machine lets you know when to be present at the machine via the Operator Dashboard. During times when no one's needed at the machine, value can be added in other areas in the workshop," outlined Katerbarg.

## Looking to the future

The global steel-processing industry has shown itself to be dynamic and flexible in the face of multiple challenges this year. These include supply chain issues; skyrocketing energy prices; Russia's invasion of Ukraine, pandemic outbreaks and rising inflation levels. Despite these headwinds, the industry is embracing new technologies and focusing firmly on the future with new innovations, renewed strategies and new product/ technology launches.

"We see a lot of different challenges for the industries we serve, but one of the most common challenges is simply having the right knowledge in-house to produce products in the most efficient way. We help our customers by developing machinery and software which already embeds the production process knowledge, and will make the production of end products easier and more efficient i.e. our one-button automation solution on plate-processing machines," confirmed Katerbarg.

"However, it is very hard to predict the future in the current global business climate. Since we can't predict the future, we will simply continue developing new solutions for the current challenges that our customers are facing. Up until now, our solutions have found their way into the market and are used on a global scale. We will continue this approach of continuous innovation and development to cope with the unpredictable global business climate, and work with our customers to help them get more out of their production processes," he concluded.
