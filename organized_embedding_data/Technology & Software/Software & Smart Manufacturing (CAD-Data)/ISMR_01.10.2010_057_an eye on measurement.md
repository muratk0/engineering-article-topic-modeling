# AN EYE ON MEASUREMENT

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

The InspecVision Opti-scan 3D promises to transform the edge measuring process during sheet metal production.

The Opti-scan3D system provides both surface and edge measurements with a level of accuracy for edge measurement that, says the manufacturer, is not achievable with standard 2D and 3D scanners. Current scanners rely upon an absence of measurements - the size of the hole is estimated based on the point at which the measurements end which can compromise accuracy. Precision of edge measurement of sheet and pressed metal which is not completely flat is critical and the Opti-scan3D system provides a more advanced solution.

The Opti-scan3D combines the surface measurements from the fringe interferometry with edge measurements calculated by the integrated patented silhouette measurement system to produce the ‘world’s first hybrid surface and edge measurement system’.

An image of the silhouette is used to determine the edges, while bands of light and phase shift technology calculate the height of each pixel in the image. Flat parts can be examined with up to 40 million measurements in under ten seconds, while the surface and edges of formed parts can be compared to a 3D model in around one minute. Flat parts can be inspected with up to 40 million measurements in less than 10 seconds by scanning a bar code or double clicking a file.

Ease of use is another advantage of the Opti-scan3D; place the part on the table, click a button and within approximately one minute a complete 3D model of the part is displayed on screen. The wireless, handheld Opti-Probe is covered with optical markers to enable cameras to calculate the position of the probe in 3D, enabling assemblies to be checked with a single camera. Accuracy and measuring volume can be increased by adding more cameras.

## THE ART OF WELDING

Magnesium alloys are widely utilised in metal manufacturing with applications as far ranging as structural, electronic and vehicle components. Employing a reliable method of joining these reactive metals together and to alloys is therefore paramount for continued development.

“Welding and Joining of Magnesium Alloys” provides a detailed review of both established and new techniques for magnesium alloy welding and the characteristics, limitations and applications. The subject is examined from the characteristics of magnesium alloy welded joints through to corrosion and protection principles and measures for improved resistance. Component design of welding materials represents part of the material synopsis provided.

Both the weldability of magnesium alloys and weldability to other metals is assessed as well as the preparation required for welding and surface treatment. Particular welding and joining technologies are explored in detail with certain chapters examining hybrid laser-arc welding, laser welding and resistance spot welding. Joining techniques are further discussed in the form of mechanical and adhesive bonding with practical applications highlighted.

The book is a comprehensive reference for producers of primary magnesium and those using magnesium alloys in the welding, automotive and other such industries, as well as academic researchers in metallurgy and materials science.
