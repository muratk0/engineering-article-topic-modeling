# 10 REASONS TO ROBOTISE

[TARİH: 01.06.2012 ISMR]

By delivering consistent performance with minimal overheads, robots are proven to help companies more confidently predict factors such as cost of production, turnaround times, output levels and product quality, which can in turn be used to inform future investment. Robots are financially affordable and offer long-term savings while fulfilling a major role in helping boost the competitiveness of manufacturing industries.

Holding back the use of robots in manufacturing is the belief that the technology is too sophisticated for most basic manufacturing applications, says ABB. Robot technology is mature. It is well tried and tested and the variety of robots available means that virtually any manufacturing line can benefit from the technology. It is no longer the domain of large scale manufacturers like the automotive sector but is now available to all businesses, including small and medium size enterprises (SMEs) – who often do not realise they are potentially the greatest benefactor.

Speaking about the advantages of using robotic equipment, Nigel Platt explains “We are continuing to work with UK businesses to show them how, by embracing robotic technology, they can transform production, improve productivity, profitability and encourage long term sustainability. To help manufacturers with their decision, we created the top 10 reasons to invest in robots which highlight the top 10 benefits you would expect to enjoy, including:

1. Reduced operating costs
2. Improved product quality and consistency
3. Improved quality of work for employees
4. Increased production output rates
5. Increased production manufacturing flexibility
6. Reduced material waste and increased yield
7. Compliance with safety rules and improved workplace health and safety
8. Reduced labour turnover and difficulty of recruiting workers
9. Reduced capital costs (inventory, work-inprogress, etc.)
10. Save space in high value manufacturing areas.”

The latest product introductions from ABB also include two new palletising robots, three palletising grippers and programming software that helps our customers improve process results and product quality, as well as increasing productivity, reducing programming and cycle times and cutting operating costs.

## Man and machine

Integrated in the IRC5 robot controller, SafeMove enables operators and robots to work together without compromising on safety to ensure safe, predictable robot motion. The software offers a host of safety functions and enables more compact cells as it restricts robot motion to precisely what is needed for the application. SafeMove was demonstrated on the MACH stand with ABB’s IRB1600 robot, suitable for arc welding, machine tending and material handling applications.

In fact, safety was one of the key themes of the day with additional safety products from Jokab Safety’s (part of the ABB Group) Quick-Guard fencing system. Quick-Guard is a flexible fencing system that is designed to offer improved safety, prevent unauthorised access to cells and reduce noise pollution.

## Investing in the future

“With the current upturn in the UK manufacturing sector, there has never been a more important time to ensure the present and future generation of engineers are trained to take advantage of the potential growth opportunities that robots can bring,” says Nigel Platt. “This requires them to be equipped with the skills needed to ensure the best levels of performance from their installed robots.”

With this in mind, ABB are making sure the resources are in place to train and educate the next generation of engineers, with investment already made in their robot training centre in Milton Keynes (UK).

Nigel Platt explains, “ABB’s commitment to training the UK manufacturing sector and the next generation of engineers is undeniable. We need to encourage more UK manufacturers, colleges and training schools to embrace the opportunities and value of training in robotic technology.” As the UK’s largest dedicated industrial robotic technology training school, the centre has already taught hundreds of operators across the UK manufacturing industry how to optimise their production processes using robotic technology.

Nigel continues, “Of course, optimising a production process does not stop once robots have been installed. In today’s competitive environment, business profitability often relies on demanding production schedules that don’t always leave time for exhaustive or repeated equipment health checks. With manufacturers often lacking the resources, knowledge and time to regularly check robotic equipment, outsourcing the monitoring of a robot’s health is an invaluable service helping the technology to continue performing and delivering the benefits as expected.”

## Efficient examples

As an example, ABB highlights Witter Towbars, a leading UK towbar manufacturer, who approached ABB with the equipment for new welding cells to help improve plant efficiency and control, while providing full traceability of weld parameters by enabling the monitoring of all resulting weld data.

Working closely with Witter Towbars, ABB examined the key stages and requirements of the production line to determine what the robots would need to achieve and how. The answer was to provide Witter Towbars with 10 welding cells, each featuring an ABB IRB 1600ID robot. Since being installed, the robots have helped the plant achieve a 20 per cent improvement in efficiency. The robot’s ability to complete 360-degree welds has also reduced cycle times and improved both path accuracy and the overall quality of the weld.

Another example is the Youngman Group who have achieved flexible production and improved productivity after an investment in two fullautomated welding cells, complete with ABB’s IRB 1600 robots. The installation is helping the company to compete rigorously against imports from lower cost economies, while aiding flexible production and reducing stockholding.

The investment was agreed following a management buy-out when it became clear to new management that investment in its UK operation made more sense than continuing to outsource manufacturing processes to China. There had been little investment in the Youngman business leading up to the buy-out. All of the main production processes were manual, including cutting, cropping and welding aluminium tubes, while the manufacturing volume flexibility was only achieved by increasing the use of temporary skilled labour.

## The future of robotics

As the suitability of robotic technology continues to expand for a wide range of applications and industries, the International Federation of Robotics (IFR) predicts a burgeoning robot population, comprised of more than 17 million service robots and 1.2 million industrial robots. With robotic technology becoming less costly, easier to operate and more compact, its use is already spreading beyond the traditional large-scale automotive-type adopters and can now help support and meet the individual needs of even small to medium enterprises (SMEs).
