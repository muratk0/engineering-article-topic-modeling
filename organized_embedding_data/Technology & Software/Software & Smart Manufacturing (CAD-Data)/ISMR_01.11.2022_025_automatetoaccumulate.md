# AUTOMATETOACCUMULATE

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

Dutch systems integrator AWL is making the business case for automation, even for small batches, with a new cobot welding cell.

ISMR SAYS:

“AWL's continuous investments in innovation, strong partnerships and skilled employees have formed the basis of its growth.“

For manufacturers taking their first tentative steps on the automation ladder, Dutch system integrator AWL offers a range of innovative equipment and project expertise. The private company, with over 750 employees worldwide, aims to help its customers grow their businesses and meet target production volumes, as manufacturers grapple with increasing welder and skilled staff shortages.

AWL designs and builds smart (modular) machines. Its strength lies in three disciplines: joining technologies, (logistics) automation and assembly techniques. Its portfolio includes laser welding; arc welding; resistance welding; glued joints; vision systems; product handling; quality control and traceability. The company invests heavily in research and development.

"At AWL, we focus on the automotive, metalworking and logistics industries, where high levels of automation and flexibility are essential. With branches in the Netherlands; the Czech Republic; China; Mexico; the United States and the United Kingdom, we support our customers worldwide. In a quickly shifting market, we are convinced that, in the coming years, automation and robotisation will shape the world in which we live and work," it told ISMR.

## Next steps in automation

At this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Germany, AWL was promoting the 'next step' in automation for manufacturers on its booth.

"It may be that a manufacturer wants to automate as a next step or is already automating and wants to go further. The machine on our stand is an entry-level Qube machine, featuring a cobot that collaborates with an operator to produce small batches of products. We are making the business case for automation, even for small batches, and want to accompany our customers on every automation step that they take," Gerard Ramaker, Account Manager Automotive, AWL, told ISMR at EuroBLECH.

At EuroBLECH 2022, AWL specialists discussed challenges and requirements with booth visitors and explained how the company designs and implements its concepts and solutions. A particular focus was on showing manufacturers how to take the next step with its capabilities in joining technologies; (logistics) automation; assembly techniques; advanced robotics and machine vision. Booth visitors could watch live welding on the AWL booth or weld their own giveaways, as well as view AWL's latest vision technologies.

"There are always reasons for customers to take the next step," added Niels van't Hul, Business Unit Manager General Industry, AWL. "The shortage of skilled labour is a key challenge, particularly in markets like the U.S. and Europe, and is curtailing our customers' growth. All the real craftmanship that has been built up over the last 40/50 years is disappearing, as people retire. So, there is a great opportunity now to transfer that knowledge to a new generation. If manufacturers can do that by working with welders on the weld quality that they want for products and make the transition to automation, they are making best use of the skilled people in their company. If they wait a few more years, they will be gone....," he warned.

AWL's expertise in laser-welding technology has seen it become a regular supplier to the automotive industry. Every year, AWL machines make parts for ten million cars worldwide. The electric vehicle industry holds a lot of opportunity for the Dutch systems integrator.

"We tend to work with Tier 1 or Tier 2 manufacturers in the automotive sector and have a global footprint, following our customers and working on projects all over the world," Gerard Ramaker highlighted. "We see a lot of potential in the electric vehicle (EV) industry. With our laser-welding technology, we do good business on the precision welding of 0.5mm aluminium/steel for battery pack cooling systems. We now produce large machines making parts for electric vehicles e.g. cooling systems. We see this as a promising business."
