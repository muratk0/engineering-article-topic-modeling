# NO MORE EXCUSES

[TARİH: 01.04.2011 ISMR]

The Carvist Corporation

How an aluminium panel fabricator took on 3D scanning to dismiss field dimension errors and costly mistakes. By Leo Wegner

Throughout its 30-year history, The Carvist Corporation has pursued and incorporated technological advances. The latest innovation for Carvist, which specialises in high-end aluminum composite material (ACM) panels and glazing projects, is overcoming the inaccuracies often contained in relayed field dimensions.

Contractors can often be afflicted by poor field measurements at every phase of a project.

“Poor field dimensions can delay installations and slow shop production,” said Carvist co-owner, Bill O’Connell. “Our purchase of a 3D scanner, combined with our various CAD/CAM technology, creates a process that helps eliminates errors.”

Carvist combines the advanced 3D scanning camera by Leica 3D scanning software and existing 3D CAD to display the scan, and finally applies CAM software to serve its CNC machine shop.

Before purchasing the Leica Scan Station II, many Carvist projects required confirmed field measurements “often holding up projects until such data became available,” said Carvist Project Manager, Tim Egan. “Approved building plans are great as a map, but do little to provide exact measurements. Whether we are the supplier, manufacturer or installer we cannot foresee going back to the old way of hand-written field measurements. This new process is accurate to one-tenth of an inch at 900 feet.”

The Leica machine scans by rotating its head horizontally, canvassing a given structure with millions of points using a method known as LIDAR (LIght Detection And Ranging). LIDAR-like technology is also used in mapping, astronomy, meteorology and geology. Using calibration targets around the site, each Carvist scan is saved and later combined with Carvist’s 3D CAD software.

One recent Carvist cladding project required almost 200 aluminum composite panels, which were manufactured by Carvist but installed independently by another contractor. With few exceptions, mostly due to concrete being poured after their scan, Carvist was able to clad ACM around the ribs, bands and plywood portal with near-perfect precision for an ACM look that is becoming increasingly popular.

George Ness, Carvist’s co-founder, noted that the company’s first projects were mostly curtain walls designed using traditional methods. “We’ve come a long way from drafting boards and pencils,’’ he said.
