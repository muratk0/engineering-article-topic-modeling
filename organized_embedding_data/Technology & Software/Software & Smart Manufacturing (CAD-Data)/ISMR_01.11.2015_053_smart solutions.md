# SMART SOLUTIONS

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

Nowadays, EHRT tells ISMR, it’s no longer about a stand-alone machine or system but complete solutions and connected processes.

Above: EHRT's newly launched Smart Factory gantry storage system

Manufacturers producing equipment for the electrical industry (e.g. switchgear production) who are looking for efficient ways to cut, punch, and bend bus bars often use EHRT processing solutions. Based in Germany, EHRT’s over 50 years of experience brings the latest CNC technology to bus bar manufacturing. Line options include automation, MultiTool 6-in-1 and high speed punching machines.

At this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart, EHRT launched a new gantry system for fully integrated storage called the “Smart Factory”. It also focused on its PunchPRO production planning software for EHRT punching and bending machines. The nesting software avoids the waste of long scrap pieces. After the production process, it produces reports on production material usage and production times to users.

## Customised solutions

“We have been very busy over the last few weeks at several exhibitions (Blechexpo, FABTECH and TolExpo),” Matthias Winkelhoch, EHRT’s sales manager, told ISMR. “At this time of year, people want to place orders for next year. Over the last three or four years, we have grown from 30 to 40 employees and are on a continuous growth path. We are a family-owned company with a long-term view on the market, founded in 1963.”

At Blechexpo, EHRT showcased two connected punching and bending machines. A screen on the booth showed its Smart Factory solution, originally launched as a concept at EuroBLECH last year. The machines can process material of up to 20mm with durable tools especially manufactured to handle these thicknesses. Its PunchPro production planning software, to handle flat material bending as well as punching, was also on display.

“Our customers are processing with limited resources. The bulk of them work in the electrical industry, handling materials such as copper and aluminium, and have limited resources and labour so they really need to be as efficient, flexible and productive as possible. Batch sizes can often range from one to 100. Our machines can cope with low set-up times and we have a new tool-change system that is easy and quick to use for production ‘on demand’. Customised production is important and this fits the smart factory principle,” continued Winkelhoch.

“For example, we recently sold a high-end automated bending, punching and storage solution to customers in Switzerland, Scandinavia and the US. It features an automated production plan and can run ‘lights out’ 24 hours per day. Another similar solution was equipped with several new processes such as drilling and deburring units. These are all customised solutions.”

## Making connections

“We want to put a customer focus on costs and deliver tailored solutions. This requires strong collaboration with customers to develop customised solutions. We need to keep thinking about the complete value chain i.e. creating interfaces to other processes. It is not just about bending and punching but also grinding, drilling and everything around those processes. PunchPro is a good example of this – it was released at the end of last year but is still under development and we continue to keep adding features to it and incorporating customer ideas,” added Winkelhoch.

Nowadays, EHRT told ISMR, it’s no longer about a stand-alone machine or system but complete solutions and connected processes. The company has had to become more flexible to provide solutions to its company users. Some are more focused on the smart factory concept and EHRT has geared itself towards Industry 4.0.

“The Smart Factory is also about flexibility (of interfaces etc.), not just data exchange. EHRT offers a modular system. Every solution can be easily customised. This has meant a change in philosophy and a shift in thinking for us,” explained Winkelhoch.

“Software is becoming more important. There will be an increasing number of connections between processes and the Internet of Things is on the horizon. Making machines intelligent is what we do. However, although Industry 4.0 is a very big topic in the media, we shouldn’t forget that there are also smaller solutions that might fit better than a 4.0 concept. So we cover the complete range of solutions, standard as well as customised.”

As the only Austrian manufacturer of robots for arc welding, IGM Robotersysteme AG globally exports systems for the truck industry, the production of earthmoving machinery, railway construction, boilers production, shipbuilding and other industries. It sells a range of complex customised solutions for automated welding and cutting processes.

With over 40 years of experience in the development of welding robots, and in the planning of customer-specific welding production units, it also offers an extensive range of system modules. ISMR caught up with Martin Wihsbeck, IGM’s marketing manager, at this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

## 10-axis welding robot

At this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart, IGM presented its new, compact 10-axis welding robot system, Type RSX. This configuration consists of a vertical pillar with a rotary cantilever arm, mounted on the common base of the 2-axis manipulator. The RSX also features a 7-axis robot model, RTE497, mounted on the rotating arm for high manoeuvrability and access to welding joints, even in areas of a workpiece that are difficult to reach. The new RCE control system offers improved programming comfort using the K6 teaching pendant.

The robot is equipped with tandem welding equipment – the double wire drive is built into the robot’s main axis while all welding media is guided through the hollow shaft in the robot’s wrist joint. IGM also showcased its iCAM, a self-developed laser camera for online joint tracking and adaptive welding of variable groove geometries. Its range of compact laser cameras are designed for tracking different welding seam types, identifying and measuring detected gaps and for compensating volumes.

“Welding applications often involve heavy machinery,” Wihsbeck told ISMR. “This 7-axis robot on display (it can also be increased to 8 axes) is used in the heavy machinery field. We launched this robot and new controller last year and it can be used in most industries except automotive (construction, railway building, vessels etc.). We have a wide range of cameras (standard viewing range of 100mm but they range from 20 – 200mm), driven by applications from customers. These have been part of our product portfolio for six years now.

“Our customers are mainly in Europe but the biggest single market for us in China (20% of our turnover). Approximately 17% of our turnover is made in Germany. Our headquarters are near Vienna, Austria and we have a factory in Hungary, as well subsidiaries in the US, Europe and India."

The iCAM laser camera is mounted onto the wrist axis of the robot. It measures the position and volume of the weld groove online and adjusts the robot movement and the welding parameters accordingly. For welding in narrow workpiece ranges, the camera can be automatically deposited and re-adopted during the welding programme.

It can also be completely integrated into the robot control as programming is done with the IGM K6 teaching pendant without the need for an additional PC. A wide range of languages, European and Asian, are supported by the robot software. Using the logging function, the entire joint geometry can be displayed, indicating gaps and volumes. A live view of the measuring field is given by grey scale value images.

## The year in focus

“It has been a good year for us and we are satisfied with our financial results this year. We have also participated in some very interesting projects this year. The projects are becoming more complex – selling a standard machine is quite rare, these days. Increasingly, it tends to be more sophisticated applications for challenging and customised welding applications and software. Our market share has improved slightly – no peaks, no troughs,” Martin Wihsbeck told ISMR.

He also highlighted some of the trends prevalent in the welding industry today.

“In terms of trends, I see increased demands for complex welding robot installations with pick and place robots as well as FMS manufacturing lines, in combination with higher welding speeds and welding quality. We must fulfil these requirements for our customers and demonstrate our competence in engineering. We are strong in the sensor technology field and have developed our own laser camera (for MAG welding and cutting etc.). Demands for that system are increasing and we are one of the few producers of laser cameras which are also designed for welding applications (iCAM). A range of steel can be processed e.g. mild steel and stainless steel. I am also seeing increased requirements for aluminium welding as more aluminium is used in construction markets.”

Industry 4.0, Wihsbeck told ISMR, is a continuous process and its principles have long been part of IGM’s robot and system development practices.

“We have been incorporating Industry 4.0 processes into our product development for a while but the difference today is that better technologies are now available e.g. RFID. The big step is the continuous processing of data – this is not available yet. The common interface is also missing between robots, transportation systems etc.,” he explained.
