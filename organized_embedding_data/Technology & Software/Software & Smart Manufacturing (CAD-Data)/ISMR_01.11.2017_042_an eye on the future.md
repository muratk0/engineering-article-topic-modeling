# AN EYE ON THE FUTURE

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"Software is the key to meeting future bending market requirements"

ISMR sat down with Mario Traub, Senior Sales Manager, Biegemaster, to find out where the company is positioning itself for the future...

1978 saw the launch of the first manufacturing metal profiles. Under the Biegemaster brand, the folders quickly proliferated, designed for precision, reliability and simple operation. The long folders today feature robust construction and simple handling through intuitive control.

## On track for growth

"We have grown steadily over the last six years and are also on track for growth this year. We developed a range of new equipment for our folders in 2017, designed to make them faster, more efficient and easy to use. We have upgraded our software to multi-touch functionality. By continuously improving processes, we ensure that the latest technologies are implemented after extensive testing and development," explained Mario Traub.

"We are mainly in the roofing business, where customers tend to produce 20-off similar parts, then 10-off similar parts, then 200-off similar parts etc. We build folders ranging from 4m to 18m. Our most sold machines are 4m, 6m and 8m. Around 50% of our sales are to export markets (outside Germany)."

The company's export markets are split approximately 50% to Europe and 50% to outside Europe. It plans to exhibit at eight exhibitions in Europe next year, including the EuroBLECH 2018 sheet metal exhibition in Hannover, Germany.

## Upgraded software

Biegemaster's newly developed software, the BMS Multi-Touch Control, is now a feature on all the Biegemaster CNC folders with automatic back gauge. The software boasts several interesting functions and a new, modern design.

"The BMS Multi-Touch Control features a multi-touch screen, rather like those you see on an iPad. Using one finger on the screen, you can make profiles larger or smaller and angles wider or shorter in preparation for the bending process. Users can open existing profiles with one click. It is harder to find skilled workers now so it is important that the folder is easy to operate. The software is easy to use and operate, even for someone who has never used a folder before or bent a metal sheet," explained Mario Traub.

Other user-friendly and time-saving features have been designed into the software, such as a tapered back gauge feature with a 'stick together' function. This is helpful for operators who are bending, for example, several 4m sheets and wish to slide them together for faster installation. One side of the sheet is slightly smaller and fits into the wider side of the other profile, for significant time savings.

"A new laser-pointing solution for taper bending (which highlights the point where the sheet should be positioned) is another innovation. Our engineers devised this solution to ensure the accuracy of the process – users can see video of our double-folder with tapered back gauge on our YouTube channel (www.youtube.com/channel/UCmUvA ZZY3Y7aeEEtE76t5Q). There is also a loading position tool on the folder, to help load sheet into the folder. It might need two or three people, depending upon the length and weight of the sheet, to manually load material into the folder. With this solution, the bending beam rises to a 90-degree angle (like a table in front of the folder) so that it is easier to load the sheet. It is a simple innovation but it makes an operator's work much easier," added Traub.

## Trends and developments

"The business climate is very good right now and I believe that the global economic downturn is truly over. The banks are now willing to lend more money to customers for capital investment. The fear is gone, the recovery is underway...," Mario Traub told ISMR.

"In terms of market requirements, the real changes over the next couple of years will not be so much in the hardware, but rather improvements in the software. This feeds into Industry 4.0 principles. I also note that the speed of machines is becoming more important to customers."

Traub is optimistic for prospects in 2018 and has his eyes very firmly set on the future.
