# RISE OF THE MACHINES

[TARİH: 01.09.2014 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“The trend towards automation is fuelling further growth of industrial robotic systems”

Industrial robotics technology is used to automate a variety of manufacturing processes in a range of industries such as aerospace, automotive and general engineering. Processes such as welding, painting, assembly, inspection and testing often employ industrial robotic systems and their use has increased substantially, with more increases expected in the future.

## Global robotics industry

“In 2013, about 179,000 industrial robots were sold worldwide, again an all-time high and 12 per cent more than in 2012,” announced Arturo Baroncelli, IFR (International Robotics Federation) President in June at this year’s AUTOMATICA exhibition in Munich. “Incoming orders in the first four months of 2014 increased remarkably and requests from all customer industries are on the rise. Therefore, we expect that in 2014 growth of unit sales will continue at the same pace as 2013.”

According to the IFR, robot sales reached record levels in Asia/Australia, in the Americas and in Africa. Almost 100,000 new robots were installed in 2013 in Asia/Australia, 18 per cent more than in 2012. The European market increased by 5 per cent to more than 43,000 units, almost reaching the all-time-high of 2011.

Robot supplies to the Americas continued to increase by 8 per cent to more than 30,000 units. More than 700 industrial robots were sold in Africa, 87 per cent more than in 2012.

China is by far the biggest robot market in the world for annual sales and is also the fastest growing market worldwide. For the first time ever, the sales figures of Chinese robot suppliers have been included in the IFR statistics’ survey. Almost 37,000 industrial robots were sold in 2013 in China. Of these, Chinese robot suppliers installed about 9,000 units, according to information from the China Robot Industry Alliance (CRIA). Its sales volume was almost three times higher than in 2012. Foreign robot suppliers increased their sales by 20 per cent in China. Between 2008 and 2013, the total supply of industrial robots increased by about 36 per cent per year on average. In 2013, every fifth robot sold in the world was installed in China.

“Japan was the second largest market for annual sales but it still has, by far, the highest number of industrial robots in operation, more than 300,000 units. It is the most automated country in the world. Japan is the predominant robot manufacturing country. More than half of the global robot supply of 2013 was produced by Japanese companies. In 2013, sales of industrial robots decreased by 9 per cent to about 26,000 units due to reduced investments by the automotive and the electronics industries in Japan. However, exports of Japanese robots increased,” continued the IFR.

“Robot installations in the United States continued to increase by 6 per cent to the peak level of almost 24,000 units. Between 2008 and 2013, annual sales in the United Sates increased by 12 per cent on average per year. A driver of this growth was the ongoing trend to automate production to strengthen American industries on the global market and to keep manufacturing in the US and, in some cases, bringing back manufacturing that had previously been sent overseas.”

In 2013, robot sales to the Republic of Korea increased by 10 per cent to more than 21,000 units due to increased investments of the automotive industry. The electronics industry, which is the main customer for industrial robots in Korea, reduced its robot orders.

## Europe and Asia

“There was a considerable increase of robot installations in other Asian markets especially in Taiwan (Province of China), India and Indonesia. Also, important European markets such as Italy and Spain started to recover. Robot installations in Central and Eastern European countries as well as in Mexico and Canada continued to increase considerably. The robot market in Brazil lagged behind expectations,” explained the IFR.

According to the Federation, the automotive industry increased robot investments continuously and considerably between 2010 and 2013, by 22 per cent on average per year. The main countries involved were China, Germany and the United States. In 2013, robot sales to the automotive industry increased by 5 per cent. The metal and machinery industry enjoyed an average annual growth rate of 22 per cent in the same period. In 2013, robot sales to this industry were up by 17 per cent. The food industry as well as the pharmaceutical industry increased robot investments substantially in 2013. The electrical/electronics industry – which had reached a peak level in 2013 – increased robot orders by 9 per cent in 2014.

Robot sales to the German market were 4 per cent higher in 2013 compared to 2012 and reached more than 18,000 units, the second highest level ever recorded. The automotive industry was again the driver for the growth. Between 2008 and 2013, robot sales to Germany increased by 4 per cent on average.

These four markets represent 50 per cent of the sales volume in 2013, said the IFR.

## Drivers and applications

■ Energy-efficiency and new materials e.g. carbon-composites, requiring new production.

■ Global competiveness requiring increased productivity and higher quality.

■ Growing consumer markets requiring expansion of production capacities.

■ Decreasing life-cycles of products and the increasing variety of products requiring flexible automation.

■ Robots improving the quality of work by taking over dangerous, tedious and dirty jobs that are not possible or safe for humans to perform.
