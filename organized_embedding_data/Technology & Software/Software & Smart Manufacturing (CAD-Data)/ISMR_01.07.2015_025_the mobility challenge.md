# THE MOBILITY CHALLENGE

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 ISMR]

Bespoke Stairlifts invested in new tube bending machinery to create its own stairlift brand.

Huddersfield-based Bespoke Stairlifts, in the UK, was created by business partners Dave Burns and Gavin Finn with the sole aim of providing a cost-effective solution for people with limited mobility who lived in properties with curved or complicated staircases. Existing suppliers focused on straight staircases, with maybe a 90-degree turn at the top, at most.

Neither man was from a manufacturing background so their solution was to re-fabricate second-hand stairlifts to create a bespoke solution for every installation. Initially, the company targeted customers through its dealer network in the UK but, when the financial crisis hit, the challenge was to open up Europe for potential business. It did this with great success – so much so that it became a challenge to find used stairlifts to re-fabricate.

The obvious solution, therefore, was to develop its own brand of stairlift and begin manufacturing ‘from scratch’.

## Manufacturing ‘from scratch’

One of the obstacles in the way of this development was the lack of manufacturing experience at Bespoke Stairlifts, especially for tube bending the rails on which the stairlift operates.

“When it comes to re-engineering existing stairlifts we have no problem, as we have gained masses of experience over the eleven years that we have been in business. However, out and out manufacture was something completely new to us so we knew we would need to work with suppliers that were willing, and capable, of helping us along the way. This meant that we looked at a number of potential suppliers for our tube bending requirement, but everything kept bringing us back to BLM and its ELECT 80 Tube bending system, which we felt suited our needs perfectly,” said Simon Waddington, Bespoke Stairlift’s director of product development.

Bespoke Stairlift’s requirement was also unusual in the fact that it has a typical batch size of one-off, as every stairlift has a different configuration. This meant that whatever tube bending system they chose, it had to have a control system that would allow the company to quickly master the process of programming and setting the machine.

Further complicating the process was the fact that the rails featured complex helical bends that, in effect, require the machine to bend and twist the rail at the same time. Here, BLM provided the confidence through support and advice for Bespoke Stairlifts.

“In one respect, it was good that we had no experience of tube bending as that meant we didn’t realise how difficult it might be to create some of the shapes that we needed, so we just got on with it. We did this, however, in the knowledge that BLM were at the end of the telephone and their support was excellent. Not only did it include telephone support, but we also had back-up from their engineers who went through the software and programming for the more complex forms that we were producing,” explained Simon Waddington.

## Tube bending

The BLM ELECT 80, which forms part of a £1 million plus investment by Bespoke Stairlifts to create its own product range, is part of the ELECT range of fully electric CNC tube bending systems. These feature nine axes of movement, a bend head that features left- or right-hand bend sensing configuration, variable radius bending and a multi-stack tool mount (allowing up to eight tools to be used on the same tube in a single set-up). This facilitates multi-radii bends as well as bends with minimal straight tube between them plus compound bends.

The machine is controlled via a Siemens PC-based system that includes BLM’s own 3D visual graphic programming (VGP3D) software. The VGP3D software enables the user to complete real-time bend simulation and automatically select the shortest and most efficient bending cycle which, in turn, reduces cycle time and manufacturing cost. The system also highlights any possible collision points, and suggests practical alternative bend sequences.

Another factor that simplified the decision for Bespoke was BLM’s B-HELIX software option which was developed in conjunction with some of Europe’s largest stairlift and balustrade manufacturers. This software option enables Bespoke to input two pieces of data (staircase lift and radius) for the required stairlift into the CNC control, and the B-HELIX software, running via BLM’s VGP programming and control software, automatically calculates the helix and shape required for the stairlift rails. The rails are then produced using BLM’s variable radius bending technology on the ELECT 80.

“The decision to begin manufacturing our own product was a major decision and has involved significant financial input and time. It also brought with it a steep learning curve for all of us that were involved in the process. However, it is a decision that will allow Bespoke Stairlifts to continue to grow and we are already looking to employ additional engineering staff to help to manage the ongoing production process,” concluded Simon Waddington. ■

## A Stairlift for Curved Staircases

The stylish BS101 stairlift from Bespoke Stairlifts is designed specifically to go on curved or more complicated staircases. It negotiates bends, landings and corridors with ease and can be installed on either side of the stairs enabling the company to accommodate almost any staircase configuration.

The BS101's slim carriage and rail also makes it suitable for narrower staircases and, when folded away, leaves plenty of room for other stair users.

Operating the BS101 is simple – using push button controls or an optional joystick, it glides gently up and down stairs and features a swivel seat for ease of transfer onto the top landing. A slim twin tubular rail is fixed to stair treads and can be tailored to fit either side of the staircase.

The stairlift's battery-powered motor ensures a smooth gentle ride and, with automatic charging from the top and bottom of the rail, the stairlift is always ready to use, even in the event of a power cut.
