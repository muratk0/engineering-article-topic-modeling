Sensors use AI to assist robotic weld grinding

Kane Robotics has developed visual sensors for its cobot that use artificial intelligence to replicate the human eye. According to the company, the Grit Vision System can assess uneven surfaces and enable its cobot to make real-time adjustments in weld grinding operations.

A human operator sets up the cobot and attaches the necessary tools for the weld grinding process, then monitors and adjusts the process through a live custom interface. Using the camera, the vision system then "sees" uneven surfaces on a weld or seam.

The rapid-learning AI alerts the cobot’s robotic arm and steers it accordingly.

While originally developed for weld grinding, the system is compatible with various robotic components and customizable for diverse applications, from polishing aerospace parts to sanding furniture.

Wet deburring and finishing machine available with one or two heads

The 41 series Trident wet deburring and finishing machine has been introduced by Timesavers. Designed, engineered, and built in the U.S. for 24/7 operation, the 52-in.-wide machine comes with one or two heads with a combination of wide belt abrasive and barrel brush type heads.

As a wet machine, the system can run mixed materials with no need to change the abrasive media and completely clean out the machine.

Combination punch-laser machine delivers five fabricating options in small footprint

Prima Power offers the Combi punch-laser system, which offers two levels of high-productivity processing in a small footprint. Operated by one worker using one operating and programming system, the machine also offers the modularity to enable unattended production with automation equipment.

Features include a large tool capacity, a variety of special tools available, and fast and simple setup change, the company states. Delivering punching force up to 300 kN, speed up to 1,000 hits/min., and 6 kW of laser power, the machine performs punching, tapping, forming, marking, and laser cutting of complex shapes from 0.5 to 8 mm.

Its NC servo-electric axes provide high-speed operation, low maintenance requirements, energy efficiency, and lower production costs, reports the manufacturer.

Power feed beveler processes thick materials

Heck Industries/Woodward Fab has introduced a new power feed beveling machine for large beveling requirements.

The PRO-60G portable milling machine can bevel up to 2⅜ in. and can handle material up to 3½ in. thick. It features the ability to change the angle of bevel from 20 to 50 degrees. The tool can be used stationary or will walk along the plate.

The machine delivers 6 tons of air pressure to secure plate during beveling. Features include a variable-speed spindle, variable-speed feed rate, and a spring-loaded trolley.

Tank rolls offered in several types for various applications

BendmakUSA offers a variety of tank roll styles to meet different application and budget requirements.

Self-adjusting and conventional tank rolls are used to rotate circular parts such as tanks, pressure vessels, tower pipes, and fuel barrels. Units are available up to 300 tons, and machines up to 600 tons can be produced in special cases. The self-adjusting rolls are suitable for applications in which the diameter of the rotating workpieces is variable, such as ovals. The conventional rolls are designed for parts with diameters that are not variable.

Fit-up tank rolls have been developed to position the welding grooves of the parts being joined. Two circular parts can be brought into concentricity easily with this system, the company states. Comprising one fixed unit and one moveable unit on a rail, these rolls allow vertical and horizontal adjustment of the tank or pipe sections before rotating for seam welding operation.

SMAW machine also delivers TIG features

Lincoln Electric has introduced the Sprinter 180Si portable, lightweight welding machine with a maximum output of 180 amps for SMAW and 200 amps for TIG welding. The operator can weld up to 5/32-in. electrodes (6010/7018) with the unit or choose a dedicated 6010 mode for cellulosic stick electrodes. For TIG welding, the machine offers pulse mode with options from 0.1 to 100 pulses per second; a choice of high-frequency or lift start options; and an eight-pin connector for foot pedals, hand amptrols, and other devices

Weighing less than 20 lbs., the portable unit has dualinput voltage that allows the operator to plug into any common power supply.

AMRs available with lifter, roller modules

Omron has introduced the MD-650 and MD-900 mid-range autonomous mobile robots, available with lifter and roller modules and options from ROEQ.

The TML500/TML750 flat-top lifter enables the two AMR models to pick up, transport, and drop off pallets and cargo up to 1,120 and 1,680 lbs. respectively. A new PR750 pallet rack also is available with a multirack option. An optional cargo sensor kit provides additional control to ensure goods stay in place on the AMR.

The TR600/TR700 heavy-duty top roller allows the AMRs to pick up and deliver goods up to 1,490 lbs. between conveyor stations. It includes the GuardCom system, which uses sensors to provide additional safety and efficiency when transferring between the top roller and receiving station.

Both modules come with ROEQ Assist software that weaves the equipment and features together and configures it into one system.

40-V cordless hole puncher works on-site, anywhere

Hougen Manufacturing has introduced a new battery-powered Hougen-Ogura Punch-Pro electrohydraulic portable hole puncher. The 76004PR model can be operated on-site where electric power may not be available or readily accessible. Its 40-V battery can generate 17 tons of force to punch round or oblong holes up to 25/32 in. dia. in ⅜-in. mild steel in as little as 4 seconds, the company reports. With a throat depth of 19/16 in., the unit can punch flat bar, angle iron, H-steel, and channel.

The 24.9-lb. puncher is suitable for applications typically performed with electric hand-held twist drills. The handle can be rotated 360 degrees in either direction during operation to allow working in narrow or awkward positions. The punch can be jogged down to the center mark to confirm hole location accuracy.
