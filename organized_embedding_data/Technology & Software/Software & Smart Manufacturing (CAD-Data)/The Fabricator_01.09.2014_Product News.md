Electron beam additive manufacturing systems

Sciaky Inc., a subsidiary of Phillips Service Industries (PSI), offers electron beam additive manufacturing (EBAM) systems. Previously the company offered the process mainly as a service-only option to manufacturers that required assistance to produce large-scale prototypes and production parts up to 19 ft. long, made of titanium, tantalum, stainless steel, and INCONEL

®

alloy.

The process, which has been marketed as direct manufacturing, combines CAD, electron beam welding technology, and layer-additive processing. Starting with a 3-D model from a CAD program, the company’s fully articulated, moving electron beam welding gun deposits metal via wire feedstock, layer by layer, until the part reaches near-net shape. From there the near-net-shape part requires minor postproduction machining.

Deposition rates vary from 7 to 20 lbs./hr., depending on part geometry and the material selected. The build envelope can reach up to 19 by 4 by 4 ft., allowing manufacturers to produce very large parts and structures with almost no waste, the company reports.

GMAW wire feeder

EWM AG offers the drive 4X wire feeder for GMAW. Each wire feed roller is driven by its own cogwheel. The four cogwheels are interlocked with the drive shaft, causing the wire feed rollers to run synchronously.

The driving shafts of the four wire feed rollers, which are 1.46 in. dia., are mounted on two ball bearings for long life, the company reports. Two set screws can be used to adjust the contact pressure of the roller pair individually to match the material used.

The wire feed rollers are color-coded and available for wire diameters from 0.03 to 0.06 in. Rolls can be replaced without tools. The user unlocks and folds open the roller holder without removing it from the wire feeder. The rollers then are exchanged, and the roller holder is closed and locked.

Carbide circular cold saw

Behringer has added an automated inclined magazine to its HCS-90E carbide circular cold saw. The saw is designed for cutting ferrous material at 90 degrees in maximum-yield applications. High throughput performance is facilitated by the saw’s inclined magazine, which holds up to 3,500 lbs. of 12-ft.-long material. Once loaded with bundles, the machine runs without interruption, cutting preprogrammed lengths, ejecting trim and remnants, and channeling good cut pieces to a holding bin.

In this process, the back end of the material being cut travels past a light barrier in the machine.
