# UNIVERSAL TESTING MACHINES

[TARİH: 01.04.2012 ISMR]

FOR THE first time, Shimadzu UK Ltd is making the company’s portfolio of universal testing machines available directly to the UK market via the company’s customer evaluation facility in Milton Keynes, Buckinghamshire.

Founded in Japan, Shimadzu has more than 90 years of experience of design, manufacture and sales of high precision universal testing machines. The company offers UTM systems such as the Shimadzu Autograph AG-X. The AG-X series advances testing in three key areas – performance, operability and support. Standard equipment includes intuitive interfaces featuring a colour TFT touchpanel screen for PC-free operation and a smart controller.

Shimadzu’s extensive range of UTM system solutions includes both static and dynamic universal testing machines that can handle measurement capacities from mega Newton to micro Newton. The systems feature load cells with accuracy class 0.5 down to 1/1000 of load cell capacity. www.shimadzu.co.uk

## THE FINAL CUT

FICEP WILL be demonstrating two of its latest, high productivity machines from its range of CNC machines at MACH 2012. The successful Gemini machine is a system for profile cutting, drilling, machining and scribing and bevelling first launched at Mach 2010.

“To be launched at MACH 2012, the small footprint Excalibur 12 CNC automatic beam line for drilling profiles, plates and small fabricated components such as stair stringers, end plates and box sections, is the first mono-spindle gantry drilling line to use the patented scribing and milling options by means of an auxiliary axis system. This high productivity machine allows smaller fabricators to improve overall efficiency by scribing fabrication detail, drilling a hole pattern without moving material or machine and milling large diameter slots and holes all in one process,” said FICEP.

Gasparini rollforming systems are now represented by FICEP UK Ltd. Sales engineers will be available on the stand to discuss all application requirements in the rollforming industry for the first time at MACH. ■
