# Real-time calculation of sheet metal

[TARİH: 01.11.2023 ISMR]

Optimate, a specialist in AI-based part analyses in the cloud, exhibited its capabilities at this November's Blechexpo/ Schweisstec trade fair in Stuttgart. In addition to feasibility analysis and automated part optimisation, a new cost calculation feature was presented to show visitors.

"Contract manufacturers and design engineers in sheet metal processing were able to test our automated part analysis live at our booth in Hall 1," said Optimate CEO, Jonas Steiling.

The Stuttgart-based TRUMPF spinoff began as a start-up in 2020. It has now outgrown its infancy and become "a veritable companion for sheet metal processing." The cloud-based web app now covers much more than it did when it was launched three years ago.

"Our automatic part optimisation now generates 70 per cent more suggestions, recognizes more optimisation potential and can offer even more creative approaches to reducing costs," added Sebastian Beger, CSO, Optimate GmbH.

With the app, designers and sheet metal processors can identify defective parts in advance and adjust them, if necessary, before they enter production. According to Optimate, the feasibility analysis finds almost every part that cannot be finished and provides warnings. This means that manufacturers do not discover as late as in the programming stage, or even at the bending machine, that a part cannot be produced in this way.

## Flexible entry

There are three options for using the Optimate App: via the browser, via an API interface or via a CAD plug-in. It is possible to use the feasibility analysis, cost calculation and part optimisation directly in the browserbased web app without installation.

After registering, the user can start immediately. Users and web shops that wish to access Optimate's sheet metal knowledge across the board can do so via an API interface. Those who want to use the tool in their CAD system for design purposes can do so via a CAD plug-in for SolidWorks.

The development of further plug-ins for other CAD systems, for users in design and work preparation, is also planned.

## Cost calculations

Assembly analysis and cost calculation were also added as new features.

"In 2020, we started with feasibility analysis and optimisation and have now expanded these to include a cost calculation," explained CSO, Sebastian Beger. Users are now shown the three options for digital support as soon as they enter the app.

"If a user uploads his parts to the app via the browser, he sees feasibility warnings as well as optimisation potential on the screen within a few seconds," confirmed Beger.

"By using our cost calculation, designers of sheet metal parts can identify possible cost drivers already in the development process," added Jonas Steiling.

In addition to the analysis of individual parts, assemblies with up to 25 individual parts can also be checked for feasibility and optimisation potential. Users can upload a complete assembly to the Optimate app during design, which is then disassembled and checked for feasibility.

The app simultaneously determines the optimisation potential for the individual sheet metal parts and performs a cost calculation.
