## FOCUS ON CAD/CAM

## Web shops are now forming part of sheet metal manufacturers' digital strategies.

Processes are being converted into digital-focused web shops, based on RADAN sheet metal software.

RADAN's German reseller, 3D Concepts (who supplies innovative CAD/CAM solutions), has worked with several of its customers to analyse the digital change philosophy of Industry 4.0 and developed individual concepts.

Thomas Menholz, from 3D Concepts, says that digital building blocks (such as CAD/ CAM, MES, MRP and ERP) can all come together in SQL-based systems to form datadriven Smart factories.

“Every company needs to find its own unique path based on a dedicated digitalisation strategy. An important aspect of the digital trend is the introduction of a web shop for the procurement of sheet metal parts, where customers can request components online, be provided with an offer and place the order. Web shops operate autonomously and are accessible 24 hours per day."

The digital twin is created as a forerunner to the real product which comes later

## An automated process

As an increasing number of products are being developed in 3D CAD systems, web shops are based on 3D sheet metal parts provided in STEP and other similar files, along with 2D cuts from files such as DXF format.

“The digital twin is created as a forerunner to the real product which comes later. Also, it's likely that 3D data will replace 2D data in future, and that will simplify the processing without the need for further queries. At the moment, additional queries prevent seamless process handling, which is why this paradigm shift is very welcome for many suppliers and contract manufacturers in the sheet metal industry," added Thomas Menholz.

3D Concepts recently implemented a web shop for Trio Metall und Design GmbH, in Luhe-Wildenau (Germany). Trio is a contract producer of sheet metal components, providing a complete service from consulting, designing and planning, through to manufacturing 'ready-for-assembly' components, ranging from single parts and small batches, to large quantities. Using the latest laser technology, it also works with non-ferrous metals such as copper, brass and titanium.

The digital strategy began early last year, focused around digitising internal processes and creating a web shop, Trionline 24, for the procurement of sheet metal parts.

"At the same time, we linked these new processes to its existing ERP system, and controlled its new laser machine tools with RADAN," explained 3D Concepts. Trio's workshop runs an AMADA EML-3610 NT combination machine, two AMADA Ensis AJ 3015 laser cutting systems equipped with storage towers and several AMADA press brakes.

The web shop delivers the orders digitally to the ERP system, which transfers them to RADAN's Ordermanager MRP module for production processing. Completed orders are passed back from the MRP software to ERP, triggering the subsequent processes for shipping and invoicing.

"This means that the horizontal logistics are now digitally mapped, as well as vertical manufacturing operations," said Thomas Menholz.

Trio's key account manager, Christian Weinberg, confirms that the web shop automates its quotation preparation, which was time-consuming when carried out manually.

"We've also noticed that larger inquiries, such as for complete welding assemblies, are now sent directly to us via the web shop, which led to us investing in RADAN's offline quotation module, Radquote, for calculating complete assemblies. This almost halved the time taken in preparing quotes," he told ISMR.

Trio's managing director, Robert Käs, added: "To pursue a complete digital strategy, we invested in machine tools, software and new employees. The solution we now have puts us in excellent stead for the future. We're currently advertising the new web shop intensively and expect it to generate additional sustainable revenue."

Trio's RADAN-focused web shop was set up as TrioOnLine24 at www.trionline24.de/

## Web shops in focus

Schönberg Technikhandel + Service GmbH, based in Erfurt (Germany), has also set up a RADAN web shop, and sent 32,000 letters to customers introducing the digital service. Its managing director, Stefan Schönberg, confirmed that it already used RADAN to program its Mazak laser cutter and Dener Puma XL 30220 press brake for production of heating technology storage tanks, and felt a web shop would be ideal for filling capacity with contract manufacture.

Last year, the company invested in RADAN's web shop technology, and now processes orders for production using Ordermanager.

The Schönberg Technikhandel + Service web shop can be accessed at www.schoenberg-laserzentrum.de/

Frank Röhrle, managing director of Böbingen-based Röhrle GmbH, who watched how two major suppliers of sheet metal parts used their web shops, were encouraged to go down the same route. The company has many years' experience in sheet metal processing and plant construction, producing components with a unit weight of up to six tons.

RADAN drives Röhrle's TRUMPF TruLaser 5060 laser cutter and EHT VarioPress 400-40 Tandem press brake, so the software was its choice for the company's next digital step.

“As there are only a handful of functioning webshops in Germany, I regard our company as being one of the pioneers in the digital procurement of sheet metal parts," said Frank Röhrle.

Röhrle GmbH's marketing strategy was based on a new brand, and the web shop has now been introduced at: www.sheetmetal24.de/
