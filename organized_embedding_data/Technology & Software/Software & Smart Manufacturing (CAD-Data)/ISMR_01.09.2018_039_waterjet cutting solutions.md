"TPS/i lets users call up weld data at any time – not just locally, but also via the browser of a computer, tablet or smartphone. The power source is equipped with what is known as a SmartManager for this purpose. A secure connection protects sensitive information from unauthorised use. A user management system makes it possible to configure individual access rights so that employees can only use those TPS/i functions that they actually need," Fronius told ISMR.

Authorised users can view the jobs stored at the power source as well as details about the installed system components and characteristic curves. The Live Viewer also lets users track what is currently happening at the power source. This means that running processes can be monitored comprehensively with fine resolution. The SmartManager enables TPS/i to fulfil the requirements of the new DIN EN 1090 series of standards for steel and aluminium designs.

In addition, Fronius has developed a WeldCube data management solution which is software that enables better utilisation of the information acquired in the power source. It can be used to interconnect up to 50 welding systems. This makes it possible to merge and manage information generated by different devices and at different production sites.

"WeldCube thereby boosts transparency in the welding process and contributes toward making quality verifiable in production," concluded Fronius.

A NEW RANGE of waterjet cutting systems is now available in the UK from AXYZ International of Telford and Nottingham. It follows the recent acquisition by the parent company in Canada of WARDJet, a US-based waterjet cutting technology specialist manufacturer.

AXYZ International's UK managing director, Mark Sweeney, commented: "Whilst remaining a major supplier of traditional CNC routing and knife-cutting systems to the many disparate industries in which these remain the most appropriate for the materials processed, we can now provide waterjet cutting solutions to industries that use harder and thicker materials which these systems cannot effectively handle. Typically, these include stainless steel and other non-ferrous metals, glass, ceramic, marble and stone for which waterjet cutting is the most viable option."

UK-based Vice President (Market Development) at AXYZ International, Robert Marshall, added: "Since 1991, we have been a major global developer and supplier of CNC routing and knife-cutting systems. The decision by the parent company to add waterjet cutting systems to our extensive product portfolio emanated from the need to maximise the business potential that exists in a wider range of industries, including primarily the aerospace, automotive, metal fabrication and general engineering sectors."

Examples of the WARDJet waterjet cutting systems can now be viewed at the AXYZ International main UK facility in Telford. For more information, visit www.axyz.co.uk
