# AN EYE ON ACCURACY

[TARİH: 01.01.2022 ISMR]

InspecVision's managing director, Dr. Jan Antonis, shared his thoughts on industry challenges, artificial intelligence for metrology and new industrial trends with ISMR.

Award-winning InspecVision, based in Northern Ireland, specialises in the design and manufacture of computer vision-based measurement systems for industrial applications. Its inspection machinery can be used to carry out one-click, single-second field of view 2D, 2.5D and 3D measurements.

Manufacturers, particularly in the automotive and sheet metal industries, can use these systems to measure and obtain traceable results, without the need for hand tools or manual input. They can also link the system to computer-aided design (CAD) models to show users if a sheet metal part is in, or out of, tolerance with overlayed colour mapping.

Its managing director, Dr. Jan Antonis, shared his thoughts on industry challenges, artificial intelligence for metrology and new industrial trends with ISMR.

ISMR SAYS: "Recent new product launches include the AOI and Guidance and Verification systems, which both use AI to inspect sheet metal parts."

ISMR: Please give a brief background to InspecVision and highlight any notable successes and achievements over the past two-three years.

JA: InspecVision was founded in 2005 by my father, Mike Antonis, and one of his colleagues (one of the largest sheet metal manufacturers in Northern Ireland) to create inspection solutions for the sheet metal industry. They had become frustrated with the lack of available options to inspect sheet metal parts. I was doing research in computer vision systems at the time and so we all got together to solve a practical challenge and manufacture a 2D measurement solution, Planar, primarily for sheet metal part production.

The company was started by engineers and is still very heavily focused on R&D, which includes continuously streamlining and improving our products. We currently employ around ten people. In terms of achievements, we won the "2021 Queen's Award for Enterprise: International Trade", the 'Federation of Small Business Northern Ireland Winner of High Growth Business of the Year' and a marketing award from the Chartered Institute of Marketing, Ireland (in the last three years). We were also 'Highly Commended' in the global player category of the Northern Ireland Chamber of Commerce Awards 2021. These awards recognised our substantial growth, on a global level, over the last number of years.

We have also worked with TRUMPF to integrate our sheet metal inspection system into its TruTops software to help create a closed loop smart factory. We became a TRUMPF Partner in 2021 and were, for the first time, part of the TRUMPF stand at the Blechexpo 2021 exhibition in Stuttgart, Germany, recently. You can also see our machines in TRUMPF Smart Factories and showrooms across the UK, U.S. and Germany.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals? What about the climate in 2022?

JA: Our sheet metal manufacturer/ subcontractor customers are mainly in the U.S., China and then rest of world/Europe. Our products are largely made of sheet metal so our typical suppliers also tend to be our typical customers. We sell overseas through distributors and now we have a new sales distribution network through our partnership with TRUMPF (part of its TruTops smart factory Industry 4.0 initiative). This is great brand exposure for us.

We are very closely connected with our customers and keep a close eye on their production capabilities, challenges etc. so that we can continue meeting their needs. Our core market is the sheet metal industry with customers producing components for a wide range of applications, including the automotive and aerospace sectors. What we are hearing and experiencing from them is longer delivery times, rising prices and, most of all, trouble accessing experienced and skilled staff. This was a problem before the COVID-19 pandemic but the current situation has definitely made it worse.

Despite the levels of automation, there are still many manual steps that need to be taken in most sheet metal production. There can be a reluctance to take on jobs which require skilled manual operations, particularly for smaller production runs. Our customers are looking for practical automation to help with these issues.

In 2022, I imagine that things will improve although it is an evolving situation. People are finding ways of adjusting to the new business environments. Rising costs will affect prices but access to skilled staff in the sheet metal industry will continue to be a challenge.

ISMR: How has the COVID-19 crisis affected your operations and which steps have you taken in response?

JA: In terms of health and safety measures for our staff, we implemented a mix of working from home and social distancing in the factory. We've also experienced longer lead-times for materials and components and less choice of suitable suppliers. This meant that we had to find more suppliers in Europe and, specifically, more local Ireland-based suppliers.

A major impact of COVID-19 was the restrictions on travel and our ability to install machines and train operators. Pre-pandemic our engineers would be travelling abroad regularly to install machines and provide on-site training. Using a combination of video conferences, installation instructions in video format, online training and, most importantly, our ability to tap remotely into all our machines, we were able to work with our distributors and customers to complete installations.

In 2020, to address the issue of restrictions on both budgets and travel, we also launched a new low-cost, plug-and-play model (the P43.100) which does not require an on-site visit by an engineer to install.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical approach for the company?

JA: We believe that the shortage of skilled and experienced staff for manual tasks was a problem before COVID-19 and is likely to remain one afterwards. Many suppliers do small production runs for their customers; one example is welding. Even if a robotic welding system was available, the programming skill and time could not be justified for a small production run that may only be ordered a few times per year, possibly with revision changes.

We therefore developed a prototype machine learning system that can watch an operator perform a manual task in 2D/3D. As a task is performed, it learns each step of the task by scanning the work areas in 3D. Each step of the task can then be played back later to guide a new or less experienced operator. The system uses augmented reality projections to indicate the next step to take. Then, as the operator performs each step, the system verifies this step by scanning the work area to ensure that the step has been performed correctly.

The system is called 'Guidance and Verification' or GaV. With this product, geared towards manual operations, we are leveraging our technology expertise to create almost a production machine which guides and verifies manual operations to help generate accurate parts more quickly. It is more of a production-focused asset than a metrology tool, so it almost feels like a change in technical strategy for us.

For example, has the bracket been attached upside down or on the wrong side? Has a screw been missed etc.? If a manufacturer receives a repeat order for a low volume item, someone with no experience of the job can complete the order and the output can be verified at each step.

In terms of our strategic approach, we will continue to focus on growth by building on the foundations we've built over recent years, particularly in the North American market and through the formation of the partnership with TRUMPF.
