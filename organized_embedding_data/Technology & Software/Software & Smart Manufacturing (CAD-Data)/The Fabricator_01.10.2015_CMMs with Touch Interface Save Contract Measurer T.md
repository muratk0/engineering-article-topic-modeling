Applications

SITUATION

When Robert Howard formed Carolina Metrology in Duncan, S.C., a provider of measurement services for production and prototype parts for automotive OEMs, aerospace companies, appliance manufacturers, and power-generation manufacturers, he knew the level of core measuring capability that was required to compete as a contract dimensional measurement service. He has spent more than 15 years in the metrology, quality, and measurement fields managing a number of inspection shops.

"The key to success in contract measuring is to be able to program fast and have equipment that can run parts with a high degree of accuracy and repeatability," Howard said. "When you can provide highquality dimensional service at a competitive value, understand the inherent degrees of accuracy of the different types of equipment, and supply the scheduling and timing required to meet the customer’s requirements, you have mastered the fundamentals of CMM measuring."

When choosing CMM equipment for his company, Howard reflected on the traditional CMM software programs he had used previously in his career. With those systems, access to functionalities was through a complex system of toolbars, drop-down menus, and window selections. He wanted to find a system that would eliminate those numerous steps to save time both in measuring and operator training.

RESOLUTION

The company acquired two CMMS from Perceptron: a Coord3 Hera 10.7.7 with a Renishaw PH20 probe, and a Coord3 Hera 15.10.9 CMM with a Renishaw PH10MQ for large-part inspection and tactile and laser scanning. They offer a measuring range of 1,000 by 700 by 700 mm and 1,500 by 1,000 by 900 mm, respectively.

Carolina Metrology had been using three different software versions. The new machines allowed the company to continue to use its current software until operators became familiar with the Coord3 TouchDMIS™ software. This new software, with an intuitive touch interface, seamlessly integrates CAD data with the CMM programming and measurement process. According to Howard, the interface has minimized learning time to a few hours.
