# JETCAM FOR TOTAL AUTOMATION

[TARİH: 01.08.2019 ISMR]

JETCAM is based in Australia. Its core nesting software supports all punching and profiling sheet/roll cutting (punch, laser, combination, waterjet, oxy-fuel, plasma, routing and sonic knife cutting). Relocating to Europe in 1991, several machine tool manufacturers now offer JETCAM software alongside their machines, and JETCAM's dealer and customer network has grown to span 83 countries.

In 2002, JETCAM extended its reach into the aerospace routing and composite cutting markets, launching CrossTrack (in use by companies such as Bombardier Aerospace). ISMR spoke to its General Manager, Martin Bailey, to find out what's next for the software specialist...

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes, achievements or awards and the reasons for this?

MB: JETCAM has either been nominated, or won, over 25 awards due to its demonstrable return on investment. It was recently a finalist in two categories at the MCI (Motion Control Industry) awards and was outright winner in a third category. The win was due to an existing JETCAM user (I-Cherng Refrigeration Industrial Co, a customer since 1995) initially purchasing OEM software with a new AMADA laser, but dropping it after only a few weeks due to issues. It purchased a new post and software update from us and saw significant improvements, including 13.2% more throughput on the machine and an ROI of under six months.

Last year, we launched version 20 of JETCAM Expert, which featured major new laser functionality – JET-Cut. It offers fully automatic 'fly cutting'. This is where, instead of cutting each hole in an array singularly, the laser head instead 'snakes' across the cutting path, turning the beam on and off as it makes all of the horizontal cuts, placing a radius whenever the axis has to turn so as not to have to stop to change direction.

Afterwards, the vertical cuts are made with the same technique. This can drastically reduce the programming and machine runtime of components with internal holes. JET-Optimizer is an algorithm that calculates the optimum cutting path to avoid travelling over previously cut areas, thus avoiding potential head collisions.

About two years ago, we launched the JETCAM University. This is a free access online video tutorial library, covering all of our core products for existing customers. This has revolutionised support for both us and our dealers. Customers expect to be able to get answers immediately these days, and the University provides 24/7 access to a searchable database. Think of it as Netflix for CNC programmers! New videos are added every month – each one is only a few minutes long and focuses on just one feature. Customers can also request videos, which are generally added within seven days. Courses are broken down into categories, and the system remembers where users left off and starts on the next video. The site is written to work on desktop, tablet and mobile. Since going live, over 27,000 videos have been watched! One of the previously mentioned award finalist positions was due to this.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

MB: There is excitement and apprehension in equal measures for Industry 4.0 and the IIoT (Industrial Internet of Things). Smaller companies fear being left behind, thinking that automation is limited only to expensive hardware. This is something that’s worked to our advantage – the great thing about demonstrating automation with software is that it’s very quick to demonstrate!

Many companies, regardless of size, are concerned about the security of such systems, with cloud-hosted mission-critical apps being of prime concern. Although we can offer cloud-based hosting of our database-driven CrossTrack system, without exception no company has opted for that, citing security as the prime concern.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

MB: Connectivity is always one of the first questions we’re asked. ‘How do we talk to our ERP system?’ ‘Can we connect to load/unload or a material storage system?’ Our philosophy has always been to start with 100% automation and work backwards (in terms of interactivity), so data exchange and ensuring that our system ‘plays nicely’ with others has always been important. In fact, CrossTrack has, for example, five methods of data exchange when it comes to importing material stock.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

MB: It’s automation all the way, but it must be developed in such a way that it fits into the core product and allows the user to interact with the system to deal with real-world situations. Our ethos has always been to ensure that customers can run an off-the-shelf product, rather than having something bespoke. That way, when it comes to support and upgrades there are no headaches. It's much more cost-effective from our side to support a common product, which is a saving that can be passed onto the customer.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal CAD/CAM and cutting markets?

MB: We've seen an ongoing trend of 'best-in-class' selection when it comes to software. Companies are no longer interested in a ‘one-size-fits-all’ vendor that can supply everything, as invariably some elements do not perform as well as others. Instead, they prefer to work with companies that have 'best-in-class' products for each of their required stages, of course on the understanding that the systems will communicate reliably together.

ISMR: What is the focus of your booth presence at key manufacturing exhibitions this year?

MB: We're known historically for nesting but, for some years now, the focus has been on automation and connectivity. Companies that are investing in CNC machines not only need competent software to drive it but want to integrate with their existing systems to automate the flow and improve traceability. Over the last few years, we've partnered with companies that have products serving the same customer base – this works well, as customers get to see best-in-class solutions across a wider range of products that integrate well together.

ISMR: Have you or are you launching any new products this year?

MB: We’ve got new versions of all our key products around the corner:

JETCAM Expert (CADCAM/Nesting): Major improvements to JET-Cut and JET-Optimizer, covering automatic microjoints and common cutting on fly-cut parts, a new UI macro recorder and more.

CrossTrack automates and de-skills the nesting process, taking order lists from MRP, creating and scheduling nests, and also tracking material location (from the raw material through to cut parts). In the aerospace composite sector, this functionality extends to tracking material life, as pre-preg carbon fibre has to be stored in a freezer, with life tracked when frozen and at room temperature.

The latest version includes support for tracking material consumption based on quantity (sheets), length (rolls), weight, (screws/rivets, etc) and volume (liquids). Updated iOS/Android apps allow low-cost mobile devices to be used for transferring material or quickly getting information on the shop floor. We’ve got some very exciting integration projects 'on the go', with CrossTrack controlling complete manufacturing cells and integrating with some unique hardware.

ISMR: Are there any new initiatives (domestic or international) that you would like to discuss or highlight?

MB: We've seen very encouraging year-on-year growth over the last few years. Germany and Italy are really embracing automation, so we’ve been working closely with our distributors there on high-end solutions. We've recently seen an expansion in our founding country (Australia), with the sign-up last year of our new dealer, Eurotec. There have been successful user days in Czech Republic, Australia, UK, Germany and Switzerland – customers really appreciate these as they get to learn tips and tricks from both us and other customers, which often they don’t have time to research during the normal working week.
