# CAD/CAM AND ERP

[TARİH: 01.07.2010 ISMR]

You need to accurately track materials and inventory, communicate effectively with suppliers, schedule for increased profitability, and deliver products on-time to customer specifications.

“The first step in choosing an ERP package is to establish a software selection committee. Members of this committee should be decision makers in the organization representing all relevant departments.

“Next, you must define the business problems you want to solve. Many companies make the mistake of trying to solve business problems with software. Software is not a part of this step. Develop a list of what you want the company to achieve, not features of the software such as ‘create accurate job costing quotes,’ or ‘maintain a 98% accurate inventory.’

“The next step is to prioritize your list items. Select the top three to five items that your company needs to achieve. This does not yet include software, but is a focused vision for your company. By creating a vision, you have achieved something a vast majority of search teams have not: you have goals.

“Before beginning the search for a vendor, you need to make a few more decisions such as what type of database platform do you want to run your software on? Are you looking to run on an SQL Server database or an IBM database? You can avoid wasting time by looking only at software that runs on the platform you choose. How many users do you believe will be on the system? Licensing fees can be based on named users (the number of unique users that will use the system) or concurrent users (the maximum number of users on the system at one time). At this stage, you need only an estimated number of named and concurrent users.

“Define your business. Are you make-to-order? Make-to-stock? Mixed mode? Process? You need to look only at a package that fits your business model. What is your budget? There are plenty of solutions available at many different price points. Devise a ballpark budget that includes software and implementation services and avoid wasting your time investigating software that you can’t afford.

“Armed with your goals and some basic statistics on your business, you can now begin your search for an ERP vendor. The next step is to contact each ERP vendor on your list. Email is probably the best method of communication. Let them know that you are heading up a search for an ERP solution, then provide the information you have already defined ie your business model, the approximate number of users, the size of your business (revenue or number of employees), the software platform and the top three to five goals that the software needs to address.

ERP systems can help you control and plan inventory.

“Ask the vendor if it has a possible solution for you. If the response is negative, ask for a recommended solution. If the vendor recommends one that is not on your list, add the new vendor to your list and contact it. For any vendor claiming it has a solution for you, that vendor needs to provide 10 references. At this point, you need to determine whether the vendor’s reference list includes the kinds of organizations matching your profile.

“In addition, require each vendor to give you a ballpark price for software and implementation. The goal here isn’t to hold them to a price; it’s to find out whether, at a high level, you can afford what the vendor is selling.

“Select the top four to six vendors that have provided solutions to companies similar to yours. To be in control, avoid trying to control the process. Let the vendors who made your list know that they made the cut. Then ask them how they would like to proceed.

“Remember: observe, don’t direct. Here are some questions to ask yourself: Do the vendor staff want to visit your company and get a better insight into your issues, or do they just want to demo? Do your vendor staff ask for data to put into the presentation? When they do demo, are they addressing your top three to five business goals, or are they running through a canned demo? Do they understand your business issues, or do they just know their software? Are they addressing your goals, or are they showing you what they think is important?

“Take good notes at the demo. This is critical because after the passage of time, the different solutions you have seen will start to blend together. Now it’s time to make another cut. Try to limit your selection to no more than two vendors.

“While a vendor is preparing for the final demo, you can be checking references by phone, but do not plan a site visit yet. Then begin the final demo process and start the implementation process. Now it’s decision time. The two final steps before signing a contract are to conduct a site visit and finalise the price”.
