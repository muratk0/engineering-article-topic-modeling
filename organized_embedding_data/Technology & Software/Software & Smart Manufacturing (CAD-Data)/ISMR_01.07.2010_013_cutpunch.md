# CUT&PUNCH

[TARİH: 01.07.2010 ISMR]

ISMR: Please outline current trends, usage and applications of CAD/CAM systems for cutting/punching operations in sheet metal markets? Have you noticed any regional trends in different parts of the world?

CT: CAD/CAM systems are used to expedite four major areas of cutting and punching operations: nesting and material usage optimisation, NC toolpath creation and motion optimisation, MRP & ERP integration and revision control and, finally, programming time reduction. Nesting is just one part of the puzzle. CAD/CAM software should also be used to optimise the NC toolpath according to which the machine actually runs. The code that makes up the NC path can be used to control machine ramp-up/down times and speeds, lead-in/lead-out placements and can have a very dramatic effect on part quality. Manufacturers can no longer afford to scrap parts because of NC trial-and-error programming.

Some of the more recent industry trends involve fully automated and lights-out nesting and fabricating, which allows customers to create and run programs completely automatically with little or no programmer involvement. Machine capabilities are ever-increasing and nesting software is absolutely required to ensure those capabilities are fully utilised.

One of the major trends we’ve noticed among our customers is that they frequently utilise multiple types of machines to ensure that their parts are cut optimally. Punch machines are used to pre-punch holes, while laser cutting machines are used to cut intricate contours or custom parts. The flexibility that comes with using more than one machine gives manufacturers an edge over their competition.

I’m sure I don’t have to tell you that the ‘going green’ trend is in full swing around the world. Many companies are beginning to realise that truly green manufacturing actually results in less wasted capital. If you waste less material, that translates into very real money that you’re not throwing into the scrap bin at the end of each day.
