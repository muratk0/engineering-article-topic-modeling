Tooling with scan-in programming helps increase production by 60 percent

SITUATION

Recently a national customer of ALM Positioners needed tooling for a positioner it was purchasing for its assembly line.

The customer had been using a manual fixture to make 15 assemblies per day. The required unbolting, moving of the fixture, and rebolting onto a new fixture added time and labor to the process.

RESOLUTION

ALM Positioners referred the customer to Hawk Technology Ltd. Hawk designed and built tooling with a user-interface PLC that allowed the customer to scan in the required part information in about 15 seconds.

Since every assembly that comes through production is different, the programming has been very significant in increasing efficiencies. With the new tooling and programming, production has jumped to an average of 25 assemblies per day—an increase in production of more than 60 percent. The time to change out a tool has decreased by 15 minutes per assembly.

Hawk now is transitioning the ALM hydraulic positioners into servo positioners to help condense floor space. The firm also is implementing a new process for design and job flow for the customer, creating process sheets (design and job) and conducting and implementing safety studies.

Satellite dish assembly process tweaks result in labor savings, throughput increases for metal stamper

SITUATION

Highlands Diversified Services (HDS), London, Ky., is a provider of metal stamping, secondary operations, powder painting, and assembly serving the automotive, appliance, telecommunication, aerospace, and office equipment markets.

In the manufacture of a satellite dish assembly (dish, mounting base, and support arm), HDS’s production process was not as fast and effective as desired. Stamping press operators and paint-line workers were dependent on a forklift to remove their full totes and bring them parts, respectively. Operators placed stamped satellite dishes on paint racks that held only two dishes at a time. All of the components of the dish assembly were packaged on an entirely manual packaging line.

With these methods, the company completed 228 skids per shift.

RESOLUTION

HDS recently reviewed the entire process and made improvements across the board. First, it created a staging area dedicated to the satellite dish assembly only. Located close to the paint line, the staging area has reduced the time spent by the forklift driver handling associated totes, reducing forklift driver labor by 25 percent per shift.

The company redesigned the paint rack to hold four dishes rather than two. With this new configuration, the company paints an average of 6,000 dishes per shift—a throughput increase of 100 percent.

Two robots have been installed at the end of the line to place the assembly kits on pallets. A stretch wrapper has been added, eliminating manual wrapping of finished pallets. These packaging improvements have resulted in a labor reduction of 25 percent (three individuals from the packaging line). With the robots and stretch wrapping, workers can complete 240 skids per shift—a throughput increase of 5 percent.
