# LVD stormed into EuroBLECH 2018 with four new products, three of which were world premieres.

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“LVD takes ISMR through its latest innovations at EuroBLECH 2018"

LVD put its focus squarely on flexible automation solutions, cell manufacturing and process digitalisation for increased production efficiency. LVD's Industry 4.0-ready laser cutting, bending, punching and software products are designed for an easy and smooth transformation to a more competitive workshop, helping fabricators to "step into the digital reality" as it told ISMR.

Matt Fowles, LVD's group marketing manager, took ISMR around its booth to demonstrate its latest new innovations, all revolving around Industry 4.0 and its smart manufacturing philosophy.

## Bending in focus

"I am very proud of our continuous product development. LVD has innovation coursing through its veins and we love to challenge convention. We have been working on new innovations over the past two years and we chose EuroBLECH as one of the major international platforms for us to launch these new technologies. We are bringing four new products to EuroBLECH this year (three are world premieres). Our philosophy is to collaborate closely with our customers and bring out new products on the back of our discussions with them. Two examples of this in the bending arena are the new ToolCell XT and the Dyna-Cell, which are on our booth here." Matt Fowles told ISMR.

"The ToolCell XT automated tool-changing press brake, with extended tool storage capacity, holds 50% more tooling (compared to a standard ToolCell). This increases the flexibility that you get from the machine that changes the tools, but it also helps operators who are processing mild steel and stainless steel together (where they need separate tool sets). You can have duplicate tool sets to avoid this in a ToolCell XT – very useful for sectors which require no cross contamination (such as the pharmaceutical industry)."

EuroBLECH was the world premiere for LVD's ToolCell XT. The large capacity of tools housed within the press brake provides the flexibility to handle a range of bending jobs. The built-in Easy-Form®Laser adaptive bending system is designed to ensure part accuracy. ToolCell XT holds three rows of punches and seven rows of dies, with each row containing 12 toolboxes equally spread along the entire length of the machine. With its extended tooling capacity it offers the versatility to handle varying material types, organise tooling by material type and switch between stainless steel and mild steel applications. The latest generation ToolCell features LVD's Touch-B control.

The Hanover exhibition was also the launchpad for LVD's Dyna-Cell robotic bending cell. The cell combines the Dyna-Press Pro 40-ton Electric Drive press brake with 1500mm working length and five-axis back gauge with a Kuka industrial robot in a small footprint of 5m × 5m. The press brake and robot function as a compact, integrated unit. Dyna-Cell's design concept also allows for unmanned or manned operation, offering a solution for varying batch sizes. The robot features a gripper that was designed and patented by LVD. The gripper fits part sizes from 20mm x 100mm up to 300mm x 400mm. Users can make bends on three different sides of a part without regripping.

"With our Dyna-Cell robotic bending cell we take electric press brake automation to new levels for high-speed bending of small- to medium-sized parts in high volumes, at bending speeds of up to 25mm per second. There are four key USPs with this product. One is to ensure that the ratio of cost of the automation is not more than twice the cost of the stand-alone machine, which keeps the direct cost of the part at a sensible level. We have also introduced what we call a 10/10 rule (ten minutes for programming, ten minutes for set-up and first part production) so we can go from 'art to part' in 20 minutes."

LVD's Easy-Form 80/25 press brake was also displayed on its booth. Equipped with the Easy-Form® Laser in-process angle monitoring and correction system, this press brake adapts the ram position in real-time to ensure the desired angle from the first bending operation.

## Laser and tubecutting innovations

LVD also showcased its Electra FL-3015 fibre laser cutting system with new 10kW fibre laser at EuroBLECH 2018. It can process ferrous and non-ferrous materials (plate thicknesses of up to 30mm) with acceleration of 2G – or 20m/s². Electra uses a lightweight and rigid cast aluminium gantry, controlled by specially-tuned servo drives. At EuroBLECH, the Electra FL featured a 10-shelf Compact Tower (CT-L). The CT-L keeps pace with the Electra to load, unload and store parts and material.

"The Electra was built for speed – the rigidity of its frame means that you can maintain the rate of acceleration, even when working at really high feed rates with very high acceleration. We can even, by utilising the core power benefits of 10kW, maintain 2G acceleration whilst cutting. A part in 8mm material cut on a 10kW fibre increases productivity by 520% compared to a 6kW CO₂ laser," Fowles told /SMR.

EuroBLECH 2018 was also the European debut for LVD's TL 2665-FL tube laser-cutting machine. The TL 2665-FL processes round, rectangular, square or other standard tube profiles up to 7925mm long in a range of materials using a 2kW fibre laser. It is equipped with a standard seven-position, magazine-type automatic tube loader, automatic set-up and run, bow detection and twist compensation features which are designed to minimise downtime and increase tube cutting output.

"We are showing the tube laser for the first time, using a Fanuc fibre laser source, Fanuc control system and Fanuc motor drives. We want to increase the tube laser's brand presence in Europe. We are also positioning it in the market as an ideal first step into tube laser cutting for our customers," added Matt Fowles.

The Strippit PX-1530 punch press, equipped with the advanced Flexible Automation system (FA-P) at EuroBLECH was also on display on the booth. The punch press can punch, form, bend and tap to complete multiple processes, including complex, three-dimensional parts. With a capacity of up to 200 tools, the ability to use any tool at any angle anywhere on the sheet and the ability to form flanges up to 75mm high, the Strippit PX offers strong versatility. LVD launched an electric drive punch press at the FABTECH exhibition this November in Atlanta (USA).

## The big picture

As the trend toward digitalisation and smart manufacturing advances, LVD drives its vision of the factory of the future with its integration philosophy and CADMAN® software suite. CADMAN looks at the big picture and helps to streamline the complete fabrication process - from production control, communication, planning and management to punching, bending and laser-cutting – through integrated, database-driven software that gives real-time insights from the shop floor and instant control of production processes. Matt Fowles demonstrated the complete CADMAN Suite at EuroBLECH 2018.

"Here at the booth, we are presenting our latest view of the big picture, including the latest version of CADMAN which includes the integration of non-LVD machines. We have developed post-processors for non-LVD machines with a CADMAN-Job Client running alongside so that users can feed data back into the CADMAN-Job scheduling system. We are also looking at providing, within CADMAN-Job, the ability to schedule and manage the non-core fabricating-related processes, such as packaging," he told /SMR.

"When Industry 4.0 was given a name, many small- to medium-sized businesses thought 'this is just for the big companies'. However, we say to our smaller customers, 'if you have an LVD press brake with a centralised database, you have already been practising Industry 4.0 and Big Data (especially if you have been using feedback from the control in the pre- and post-production planning). The difference is that now a lot of small- to medium-sized companies understand that Industry 4.0 will make a difference to their business – they are not scared by it any more. We are showing them how it can benefit their businesses by using our systems and our vision of the future, rather than just preaching Industry 4.0 (what we called integrated sheet metalworking for many years or Art to Part-4.0). We are trying to demystify it all and show how it can add value. The lifeblood of our industry is small- to medium-sized businesses - they make up 65% of our customer base. These guys know how to make money and have had to transform their businesses, which are driven by service levels, webshops/digitilisation and lead-times."

## Looking to the future

"It has been a strong year for us – orders have been good. We have increased our global manufacturing capacity by expanding our facility in Slovakia (adding about 50% more capacity on that site). Business is fuelled by market demand and new technology development," explained Matt Fowles.

He pointed out the success of LVD's newly built XP (Experience) Centre (a five-million-euro investment) at its headquarters in Belgium, which has attracted hordes of visitors. It holds major technology events at the new Centre, to showcase its innovations, for its subsidiaries around the world as well as its agents.

"We have our eyes on new markets such as Vietnam. Our established markets are doing well. However, there is a worldwide shortage of skilled labour so user-friendly technology and software is crucial. We are also looking at technologies such as Augmented Reality (AR), Virtual Reality (VR) and Artificial Intelligence (AI). We are showing VR applications here at EuroBLECH with 360-degree demonstrations of our products so that you can immerse yourself in the machine," he continued.

"We are cautiously optimistic for business next year. There may be a slight slowdown but we are a global business, and manufacture across different continents, so we are well positioned to handle any fluctuations in the future."
