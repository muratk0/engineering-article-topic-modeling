Italian metalworking machinery manufacturers can attribute their success both to the high-tech value of their products as well as their business approach. Regardless of these companies’ sizes, their structure provides the flexibility and capability to respond quickly to customer needs. In sectors ranging from aerospace and automotive to appliances, leading global companies – from Boeing to General Motors – continue to choose Italian metalworking suppliers for the quality they need to maintain their competitive edge.

The objective of the Association of Italian Manufacturers of Machine Tools, Robots, Automation Systems and ancillary products (NC, tools, components, accessories), known as UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, is to protect the interests of the industry and to promote the growth and spread of an entrepreneurial culture. Members of the Association produce around 70% of the output of the industry.

## Market indicators

In 2011, Italian production in the sector grew to 5,019 million euros, an increase of 19.6% compared to the previous year. The result was achieved thanks to the growth in exports by 29.3% to 3,367 million euros. According to the UCIMU Studies Dept. data, in the first eight months of the year, the following countries were the outlet markets for Italian exports: China, Germany, United States, Brazil, France, India, Russia, Turkey, Poland and Spain.

According to the most recent UCIMU survey, sales grew in Germany (+62.9%) to 228 million, United States (+99.4%) to 170 million, Brazil (+84.1%) to 115 million, France (+23.2%) to 105 million, India (+19.7%) Russia (+15%), Turkey (+85.1%), Poland (+55.3%) and Spain (+14%). Sales experienced a countertrend (-0.4%) in China which, with 240 million euros, remains the primary outlet market for ‘Made in Italy’ products in this sector.

“2012 estimates, although positive, show ‘slowed growth’ in the sector. The level of production, above all, is expected to grow by 3.4% to reach 5,190 million euros. Compared to the positive trend in the overseas market, as shown by the export data that will grow by 4.8% to 3,530 million euros, the domestic market is expected to slow down. Consumption is expected to increase to 2,820 million euros, 2.1% more than in 2011. To alleviate the weakness of domestic demand, Italian manufacturers will intensify efforts towards exports, further increasing their share of production for overseas markets which will reach 68%,” the association told ISMR.

The trend observed in the domestic market is different. Consumption, although increasing by 11.9%, stood at 2,761 million euros, showing the weakness of Italian domestic demand. The modest increase in domestic demand reflects the slow recovery of manufacturers’ deliveries in the domestic market which did not exceed 1,652 million.

In the fourth quarter of 2011, the index of machine tool orders, worked out by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE Studies Dept., marked a fall of 12.4% in comparison to the same period in the previous year, for an absolute value of 95.1. This figure is, however, among the highest recorded in the last three years.

“Despite the slowdown at the end of the year, the 2011 average index of orders for Italian manufacturers registered an increase of 12.8% compared to 2010, for an absolute value of 93, the highest recorded in the last three-year period,” said the association.

“From a more exhaustive analysis of the data, an increasingly higher discrepancy between the data from the domestic market and that of overseas markets emerges.”

On an annual base, the index of the orders collected by the manufacturers domestically marks a fall of 11.9% (for an absolute value of 55.2). Conversely, the index of foreign orders recorded an increase of 20.9%, compared to the previous year (for an absolute value of 116.3).

For markets outside Italy, according to the last survey carried out by UCIMU Studies Dept. on the base of ISTAT data, for the period January-October 2011, the main markets for Italian machine tools were:

■ China (+11.5% compared to the first ten months of 2010)
■ Germany (+55.9%)

■ India (+13.5%)

“In the light of this data,” said Giancarlo Losma, UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE President, “two considerations are clearly emerging. First of all, we do not believe that the slowdown recorded in the last quarter of 2011 heralds a new period of difficulty. Currently there are no elements demonstrating an inversion in trend and therefore the start of a new negative cycle for the Italian machine tools, robots and automation industry. On the contrary, it shows the level of efficiency that characterises the industries of the sector: the index of production capacity that, in the fourth quarter of 2011, reached 80%.”

“Certainly,” continued Losma, “the still unstable context does not reassure us completely but the hope is that the measures adopted by the current

Government, with the Salva Italia (Save Italy) Decree and with the Cresci Italia (Grow Italy) Decree now adopted, will be fruitful. We are confident of the work of this Administration which is showing the determination and the desire to improve the destiny of the country by supporting the development of the economic system in its complexity.”

“The second consideration,” added the UCIMU President, “refers to the widening discrepancy between the results obtained by manufacturers for domestic and overseas customers. The continuous and chronic reduction in domestic orders is not directly connected to the reorganisation of domestic consumption which has, partially, simply changed its needs, favouring standard and medium-range products.”

“This is the reason why manufacturers decided to direct their complex, highly customised technology offerings abroad where margins are more rewarding against the necessity of increasing research and innovation activities, indispensable for keeping up with foreign competitors. In consideration of this, despite knowing that available resources are scarce, we have asked the government authorities to include concrete measures to support companies that choose innovation. On the other hand, growing activity in overseas markets requires us to push for the reconstitution of the Italian Institute for Foreign Trade.”

## Lamiera in view

The Lamiera 2012 exhibition (to be held from 9-12 May in Bologna, Italy) will provide an overview of developments in metal forming. A core focus of the show is to promote and provide a forum for what defines technically advanced solutions via the exchange of ideas between manufacturers and potential customers. The 2010 show attracted 441 exhibitors - 30% of whom were from outside Italy and featured in a 37,000 sq. m. exhibit area. Of the 18,000+ attendees, seven per cent came from outside of Italy.

The Italian machine tool industry saw a slight resurgence in manufacturing with machine tool orders doubling in the first quarter of 2011, a 19.1 per cent increase compared to the same period in the previous year, according to UCIMU-Sistemi per Produrre. While the numbers indicate a rebound, the association says that there is still a gap in growth between the domestic market, which is still weak, and the export market which is spurring the recovery.

It is noteworthy that Italian machine tool exports/sales to traditional markets such as Germany, the United States and France are declining but India, Russia, Iran, Brazil and other emerging markets show significant growth for Italian machine tool exports.

On 1 January 2011, UCIMU decreed that environmental sustainability of the production cycle will become a key criterion for Italian manufacturers to receive the UCIMU blue mark of quality in addition to commercial reliability, financial strength, attention to safety/function testing and customer care (monitored through Key Performance Indexes). ■
