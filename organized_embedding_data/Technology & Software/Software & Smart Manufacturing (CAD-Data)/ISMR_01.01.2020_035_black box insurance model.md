# Black box insurance model

[TARİH: 01.01.2020 ISMR]

EDINBURGH-BASED INROBIN has partnered with The University of Strathclyde’s Advanced Forming Research Centre (AFRC) to develop a new insurance model offering an Industry 4.0 approach aimed at achieving better pricing for manufacturers.

Based on the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT), the new model analyses real-time data from industrial machinery. This allows insurance companies to offer competitive premium rates based on individual usage of equipment, according to factors such as frequency of machine maintenance and operational efficiency.

“Manufacturers are set to benefit from a combined offering of risk prevention and tailored insurance, with in-depth data analysis also providing a deeper understanding of how to run machinery more efficiently. This new model will also bring additional benefits, helping manufacturers to improve maintenance schedules and prevent breakdowns, which can cause expensive downtime,” Inrobin told ISMR.

## Trialling the system

Inrobin worked with the AFRC’s bid writing team to secure Innovate UK funding. The firm’s data science experts will now work with the research centre to trial the system on its industrial machinery. It will also exploit the centre’s expertise in IIoT and connectivity, while tapping into its extensive list of manufacturing contacts to gather further information to guide the project.

Aiming to predict machinery life expectancy based on usage, the programme also helps to ensure that equipment is properly serviced and maintained, while flagging up unforeseen issues potentially affecting production.

“Insurance providers currently assume that two companies buying the same machine should pay the same premium based on the same life expectancy. However, the new model will offer a more tailored pricing approach – analysing each machine’s specific behaviour and machine maintenance in real-time to adjust rates,” said the two partners.

## Tailoring premiums to needs

Jose-Maria Guerra, chief executive officer at Inrobin, added: “Using IIoT, we can track life expectancy and failure expectations within the machine in real-time to offer manufacturers a premium that fits their needs, ensuring that they have the right type of coverage.

“Working with AFRC's cutting-edge equipment throughout the project allows us to build up an understanding of failure and test certain things that would not be possible within a manufacturing environment. We’ve already been speaking to global insurance companies that have registered interest and we are set to work on a pilot project with a major European provider.”

Danny McMahon, metrology and digital manufacturing team lead at the AFRC, concluded: “According to current insurance models, a manufacturer who uses a machine to its full capacity every day without regular maintenance might be on the same insurance premium as a company that is using it at 25% or 50% capacity, while also servicing their machinery each month. With the new model, insurance companies will offer a more competitive service while manufacturers will maintain their machinery more efficiently.

“Inrobin’s new model will create a more transparent offering for industrial machinery similar to the use of ‘black box insurance' for cars. In the way that sensible drivers who are looking after their vehicles are rewarded with a cheaper premium, manufacturers sensibly using their equipment can take out insurance that reflects their use, rather than an industry average.”

The AFRC will act as a transition to market for Inrobin. After helping with the initial bid, its machinery will be used to trial the system and offer machinery expertise to push exploitation forward and help the business to grow.

Located near Glasgow Airport, the AFRC is the only High Value Manufacturing Catapult centre in Scotland. It helps manufacturing firms to de-risk innovation so that they can seize new opportunities, access new markets and disrupt existing ones. A specialist centre within the new National Manufacturing Institute Scotland (NMIS), it is a global specialist in forming and forging research and advanced manufacturing technologies.
