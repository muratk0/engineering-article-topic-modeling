# INNOVATE TO ACCUMULATE

[TARİH: 01.11.2024 ISMR]

Dr. Jan Antonis, founder and managing director of InspecVision Ltd, outlined his strategic and technical vision, as well as the company's latest new innovations, to ISMR.

ISMR SAYS:

“Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Augmented Reality (AR) in product innovation were central tenets of InspecVision’s presence at EuroBLECH in Hanover this October.“

InspecVision Ltd. was set up in March 2003, in Northern Ireland, to develop a range of precision inspection machines for industrial applications. The founder of the company, Dr. Jan Antonis, carried out advanced research into computer vision systems at Queens University Belfast and saw a gap in the market for a system which could measure parts quickly and easily. Sales of the company's Planar inspection system, for quality control and reverse engineering applications, began in 2005. InspecVision products are now sold in over 30 countries worldwide.

In recent years, the company has earned several prestigious awards, including 'Exporter of the Year (Northern Ireland)' at the 2023 Federation of Small Business (FSB) Awards, the 'Queen's Award for Enterprise: International Trade' in 2021 and 'FSB High Growth Business of the Year (Northern Ireland)' in 2020.

Dr. Jan Antonis, founder and managing director of InspecVision Ltd, outlined his strategic and technical vision, as well as the company's latest new innovations, to ISMR. This included new achievements with Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Machine Learning (ML) in its latest products presented this October at the EuroBLECH trade show in Germany.

ISMR: Please outline any successes, achievements or awards this year?

JA: In the last year, InspecVision has continued to focus on product innovation and has made great progress in areas such as the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) and machine learning in our recently launched Guidance & Verification product. We are really excited about the range of applications for this and think that it is something new and exciting in manufacturing. The product was debuted at EuroBLECH this October and generated a lot of interest with multiple questions about the AI capabilities.

We are also very proud to have collaborated with one of the largest aerospace companies this year on a bespoke version of our Planar system. It has helped it to monitor and verify parts from a range of its sheet metal suppliers.

Our tailored solution met the demanding standards of this sector.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals? What about in 2025?

JA: Throughout 2024, we have seen sheetmetal professionals continue to manage the instability caused by high input costs, persistent inflation and political uncertainty. The labour market has continued to be very tight for our customers and I think this will continue into 2025.

This is the key driver to find greater efficiencies through automation and integration right across the manufacturing process. Companies need to retain their skilled staff and use their skills most effectively, so products such as our Opti Scan 3D and Planar 2D (which help sheet metal professionals to automate and speed up the inspection process) are well placed to optimise processes.

ISMR: Please explain your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

JA: We are focused on our strategic partnerships so that we can deliver integrated solutions to our customers. Integration and automation are key issues for our customers.

We are also investing heavily in R&D to keep our technology up to date, as well as making InspecVision systems more compatible, ensuring smooth data flow between machinery and other digital resources. This connectivity allows real-time data sharing and helps manufacturers to achieve integration.

We are excited to begin field testing our Guidance and Verification system and really dive into the innovations in AI. There is an incredible amount of potential for our customers who wish to improve efficiency in day-to-day operations.

Industrial metrology machines

InspecVision specialises in the design and manufacture of measurement systems for industrial applications that utilise computer vision-based technology, with roots that originated in the sheet metal industry. Its 2D and 3D non-contact optical metrology machines use modern camera technology for automatic quality inspection and reverse engineering of components for industries such as aerospace, automotive and electronics.

“Our precision metrology equipment captures large quantities of measurement data at high speed without touching the part, enabling faster rates of inspection, digitised reporting and continuous quality control at all stages of production," it told ISMR.

“Our Planar 2D system has been designed as a user-friendly, lowmaintenance optical 2D scanner for rapid, non-contact scanning. The Opti-Scan 3D scanner offers full 3D inspection and reverse engineering. It can be used alone or combined with the Planar 2D for a complete 2D/3D measurement solution. The Accuity scanner is for inspection of small components requiring accuracy in both 2D and 3D," it concluded.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal/inspection markets?

JA: Automation is a key issue, as is eliminating complex manual tasks from production. As automation increases, there is a growing demand for workers who are skilled in digital technologies, robotics and machine programming. This accelerates the need for user-friendly and easily operable inspection systems (such as Planar 2D and OptiScan3D) as companies prioritise equipment that requires minimal training.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

JA: Integrating AI and data analytics into traditional manufacturing environments is complex. Connecting inspection equipment with production lines, ERP systems and other digital tools requires technical expertise and engagement across the workforce.

We want to explore new markets, particularly in Japan and Korea. These markets can be difficult to enter without a physical presence, or cooperation with a company who can provide that, but we believe our technology would benefit a lot of customers there.

ISMR: Are there any products, technologies or software that you have released or are releasing this year (including at EuroBLECH) that you would like to discuss?

JA: Our new product at EuroBLECH was the AI-powered Guidance and Verification System (GAV). The system can see in 3D and watches an operator perform a task. It can then learn this task step-by-step. Once the system has been trained, it automatically generates work instructions. When an operator needs to perform this task again the system projects the work instructions for each step onto the work area using Augmented Reality (AR). Because the system can see in 3D, it can also verify that each step has been taken correctly. The entire production process can be recorded in 2D, 3D and 4D (3D with time).

The applications are basically any reasonably complex manual operation: it could be adding inserts to a part, welding a part, loading a robotic welding cell, assembling a PCB or even for goods inwards.

ISMR: What is your approach to technologies such as Artificial Intelligence, Augmented Reality, Digital Twins and Virtual Reality?

JA: Existing products, such as our SurfScan option for the Planar 2D inspection system, utilise Augmented Reality to enable operators to visually identify good and bad parts or to identify if particular features (such as holes) are missing. It is a very useful feature and our customers really like it. As I mentioned earlier, the Guidance & Verification system also utilises AI learning.

As engineers, we want to embrace new technologies but we need to do it in a way that does not compromise cyber security or make processes overly complex. Widespread adoption of these technologies will depend largely upon the cost of implementation.

ISMR: How is InspecVision handling sustainability in both its products and its organisation (including carbon footprint calculations etc.)?

JA: As long-term energy costs continue to be a concern, we have just installed a new solar system with battery storage at our premises in Northern Ireland. We hope that, even in the winter, we can become carbon neutral for at least a few days per week.

Our machines are inexpensive to run and durable, so they are built to last. They use energy-efficient LED lights.

We design our products to be modular so we can keep most of our older machines up to date. This helps our customers to achieve their sustainability goals.

ISMR: Is there anything else that you wish to add?

JA: Thanks to all our customers and partners who visited us at EuroBLECH this year. We look forward to working with you to meet the diverse requirements of the sheet metal industry!

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
