The global consumer electronics market is classified into consumer electronic devices, wearable devices and smart home devices. Consumer electronic devices are the devices which contain electronic circuit boards used by individuals in day-to-day life. It comprises a wide range of products, including smart-phones, digital cameras, televisions, DVDs, tablets, camcorders, printers and many more. Wearable devices are user friendly and autonomous in nature. They can be connected to the Internet wirelessly via a third party tool. Smart home device include intelligent security systems, HVAC systems and smart kitchen appliances that can be controlled via iOS or Android devices.

The electronics industry is subject to rapid, significant change that is both the result and the driver of innovation. Cloud computing, “big data,” social-networking, data analytics and machine-to-machine (M2M) communications are impacting both consumers and the way businesses interact with them.

According to Reportlinker, increasing disposable incomes, the introduction of technologically advanced devices and the decreasing prices of consumer electronic devices are the basic factors responsible for the growth of the consumer electronics market globally. Innovative connected TVs are driving the market for television in matured economies. The decreasing price of smart phones is fuelling its market growth and is expected to continue propelling market growth in the long term. Growing demand for interchangeable lens cameras, against standstill cameras, is boosting the demand for cameras. Growth in demand for high-end graphics games are anticipated to continue cementing market growth for gaming consoles.

## Market forecasts and trends

According to FMI, the global consumer electronic market was valued at US$ 1,224.8 billion in 2014 and is projected to reach US$ 2,976.1 billion by 2020, reflecting a CAGR of 15.4 per cent during the forecast period 2015-2020. Increasing disposable incomes, an expanding middle class population and growing internet penetration are some of the prominent factors driving the demand for global consumer electronics market growth. In addition, increasing government support especially in Asian countries such as China and India is expected to boost market growth, thereby increasing competitiveness in the market.

“The consumer electronics device segment dominated over half of the overall consumer electronic market share in 2014. However, the smart home device segment is expected to grow at the fastest CAGR over the forecast period, 2015-2020. The consumer electronics device segment has been further subsegmented into smartphone; feature phone; tablet; smart TVs; other TVs; set-top box; personal computer; digital camcorder and camera; digital media adapter; digital video recorder (DVR); game console; printer and other consumer electronics devices. Among all these consumer electronics device sub-segments, the smartphone sub-segment dominated the market and accounted for over 30 per cent market share in 2014, followed by the personal computer with over 15 per cent share of the global consumer electronics market. Additionally, the smartphone is expected to be the fastest growing sub-segment during the forecast period,” it explained.

FMI classified the global consumer electronics market into seven major regions which include North America, Latin America, Western Europe, Eastern Europe, Asia-Pacific excluding Japan (APEJ), Japan, and Middle East & Africa (MEA). Of all the regions, APEJ dominated the market with a share of over one third of the overall consumer electronics market share in 2014 and is expected to remain dominant by the end of 2020, said FMI. ## Industry 4.0 – the connected shop floor

According to a recent Frost & Sullivan research on the industrial services market, a new wave of influence is disrupting business dynamics between end-user and supplier. This change is founded on new service paradigms that are enabling end-users to achieve high degrees of cost optimisation and enhanced operational efficiency. For instance, end-users and supplier equations are currently being determined by service architectures founded on frameworks defined by advanced Information and Communication Technology (ICT). Services based on such advanced ICT concepts were found to hold more than 75 per cent of the global industrial services market in 2014.

While spare parts and maintenance still retain a major share of service revenue models, the growth of advanced services is expected to witness a CAGR of 20 per cent over the coming years.

“In order to design and deliver such advanced services, industrial suppliers are required to forge partnerships with cloud and data analytics vendors. In some end-use cases, even the most rudimentary solutions built on an integrated analytics package have enabled suppliers’ upsell and increase product prices by up to 10 per cent. It also helped achieve differentiation in a technology saturated market place,” noted Frost & Sullivan Practice Director, Industrial Automation & Process Control and Measurement & Instrumentation, Muthukumar Viswanathan.

Major structural revisions are also expected on the shop floor driven by the advent of M2M (machine-to-machine) communication. By 2020, nearly 12 billion devices in the industry are poised to be connected via advanced M2M technology.
