Sinker EDMs’ AI technology makes logical use of conditionmonitoring data

MC Machinery, a subsidiary of the Mitsubishi Corp., will showcase its latest EDM and precision milling technology, including wire and sinker EDMs, machining centers, and automation.

With artificial intelligence adaptive control technology, the new Mitsubishi SV12P and SG12 sinker EDMs help reduce electrical consumption and take the guesswork out of estimating machining time. The proprietary AI technology makes logical use of conditionmonitoring data. For example, these new machines can diagnose real-time problems within the burn and directly modify specific parameters for a stable and accurate output.

Because the AI technology analyzes current sensor data to determine optimal conditions, it reduces overall electrode wear and costs, reports the company.

Automated arc welding system made for high-mix, low-volume production

The TruArc Weld 1000, TRUMPF’s automated arc welding system, helps fabricators get started in automated welding. Designed for high-mix, low-volume production, the system includes a cobot that makes it easy to program and weld in minutes, the company states. First-time operators can program by simply guiding the cobot by hand.

Programming is simplified by the operating unit on the welding torch and factory settings for different materials and sheet thicknesses. With this rapid programming, fabricators have a costeffective means of tackling short production and single-part runs, says the manufacturer.

Overall, the system creates a process that is more efficient than manual welding and produces a high-quality weld seam.

Heavy-lifting cobot to be demonstrated

FANUC America will demonstrate its new CR-35iB collaborative robot, offering a 35-kg payload and a 1,831-mm reach. The cobot recently received a makeover that includes a more streamlined and lighter mechanical unit along with the company’s latest sensor technology for safety. The heavy-lifting cobot can work alongside operators, which helps minimize the risk of repetitive strain and other work-related injuries and reduces costs typically associated with lift-assist devices or other custom equipment, the company states.

The cobot will be demonstrated helping an operator assemble a bicycle. The operator will program various assembly positions with the company’s tablet TP, featuring a drag-and-drop user interface.

With a large work envelope and small installation footprint, the cobot is suitable for use in tight spaces. For programming, operators can use the hand-guidance option, leading the cobot through paths to teach a new task, or the tablet TP. It also is compatible with the manufacturer’s standard tech pendant.
