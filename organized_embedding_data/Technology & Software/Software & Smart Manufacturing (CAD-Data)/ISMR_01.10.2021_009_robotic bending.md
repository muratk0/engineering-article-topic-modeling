# Robotic bending

[TARİH: 01.10.2021 ISMR]

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals? What about the climate in 2022?

MF: I think it is hard to predict what will happen in 2022. I hope that the manufacturing industry will remain as resilient as it has been. I think there will still be challenges with material costs and availability. This increases the focus on the 'order to cash' cycle (working capital and cash flow) and the streamlining of this process. More people are now aware of the benefits of Industry 4.0 and the use of Big Data, which is so essential to speeding material flow.

LVD will demonstrate the capabilities of the new Ulti-Form robotic bending cell at Blechexpo in Stuttgart, Germany, from 26-29 October 2021 (Hall 1, Stand 1713). Ulti-Form handles both small batches and long production runs, maintaining in-process quality and bend angle consistency, using LVD's Easy-Form* Laser adaptive bending system.

It features a 135-ton automated tool-changing press brake integrated with an industrial robot. The press brake houses a built-in tooling warehouse and uses a gripper mechanism in the machine's back gauge to change tools. The press brake and robot work together to minimize changeover time, handling parts from 50 x 100mm up to 1200 x 800mm, weighing up to 25kg.

"Ulti-Form offers a fast ‘art to part’ process with our powerful programming wizard. Programming of both press brake and robot is handled by CADMAN*-SIM and no robot teaching is required. CADMAN* software automatically calculates the optimal bend program, imports all bending data into the robot software module and automatically calculates gripper positions, generating the fastest collision-free path for the robot," commented LVD.

2022 will bring more face-to-face contact with customers through normal sales and service channels. There will, however, still be pressure on short lead times and low inventory levels. The big question is: will something happen that affects market or consumer confidence, such as a new COVID variant etc.? That is key.

ISMR: How did the COVID-19 crisis affect LVD's operations and which steps have you taken in response?

MF: LVD has a counter-cyclical strategy which protected us when the COVID-19 crisis happened. We immediately made changes to our sales, service and manufacturing operations. This kept us dynamic and flexible. You need to react dynamically to the conditions that you face.

Despite the pandemic, we have done very good business in the last year with our online demonstrations, webinars and virtual events. Of course, if users wish to see a machine, they can visit a customer who has one or come to one of our Experience Centres. Business is not entirely online but we have relied more on digital sales and marketing technologies this year because of the pandemic. It was very tough, initially, going from face-to-face oriented to going virtual. We focused on creating great content, getting that out to salespeople and doing one-on-one online or live meetings with customers. However, at some point in the sales purchase, there needs to be physical contact. It is an essential part of the journey.

We have done a lot of virtual events (our Virtual Tech sessions) in the last year - between June 2020 and June 2021 we held 101 online events. We covered 13 topics in three different languages with attendees from 36 countries and 5580 registrations (with an average registration to attendee rate of 50%). Around 2790 people attended, in some way, one of our virtual Tech sessions. This has widened our reach considerably and kept awareness high.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus for the company?

MF: Innovate, innovate, innovate! This is one of our major strategic focuses. Just because something is done a certain way, does not mean there isn't a better way to do it. Automation is a classic example of this. For years, the way you had to teach a robot inside a robotised bending cell was always a restricting factor to the wider adoption of automation, as well as the cost. Now, this has changed dramatically with programming and software changes.

We will be showcasing several new products at the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart this October. This includes the Phoenix 20kW fibre laser machine, the Ulti-Form automated bending cell and the latest CADMAN 8.7 software version, which enables users to integrate ancillary operations into the sheet metal flow (such as deburring and tapping).

The powerful 20kW Phoenix FL cuts up to 2.5 times faster than a 10kW fibre laser with rapid processing of a wide range of ferrous and non-ferrous materials and material thicknesses up to 40mm. It features LVD's Touch-L user interface for easy operation, programming and nesting. The 20kW fibre laser source is available on Phoenix 3015, 4020 and 6020 models.

"It pierces and cuts thick materials at rates faster than most plasma cutting systems with straighter kerfs and the ability to cut holes half the thickness of the sheet, with the processing efficiency and accuracy of fibre laser cutting," said LVD. Additional options include a 10-station automatic nozzle changer; CADMAN-L programming software and MOVit modular automation systems from the MOVit Compact Tower to full tower and warehouse automation systems.

More recently, we added a new tube cutting machine, TL8525, to our product portfolio to process larger diameter tubes. In the past year, we also introduced a new Strippit PL combination punch-laser to the market as well as the new YSD LaserONE Cost-Efficient fibre laser cutting system. In early 2020, we introduced the new MOVit range of automation systems for laser and punching applications. So, the last couple of years have been quite busy for us.

We will continue to challenge conventional thinking and keep innovating, as our technical focus. That means continuing to develop new technologies, bending automation, higher laser powers, software solutions and automation systems over the coming year.

Software is a big part of what drives this. You want to make a 10% impact on 90% of the process, rather than a 10% impact on 10% of the process. Customers want to see us demonstrate how their processes can be improved in our Experience Centre. We will feature this at our next Smart Factory LIVE event from 23-25 November (see bit.ly/3DIno1W).

From an operations perspective, we will continue to bring the values of the company locally to customers by setting up new global subsidiaries such as LVD Mexico and LVD Vietnam. Our latest new subsidiary is LVD Vietnam and we have plans to open other subsidiaries in the near future.

One of the big challenges for our customers is keeping up with those lead times, plus material supply issues and material costs. Scrap or reworking erodes their margins. Process automation is the key.

There continues to be a global shortage of skilled sheet metal workers, even in countries with lower labour costs. Machines and software need to relieve this burden. If you can improve efficiencies, you can redeploy personnel to new value-add tasks or processes. Efficiency is in the material flow, even for small to medium-size batches.

It is important for customers to invest in the relevant technologies that enable them to accelerate the 'order to cash' cycle and protect their margins. In the last 18 months, the cost of shipping containers and surety of supply are now issues so I think more people will be receptive to the technologies that we have developed. Nowadays, people are more aware of Industry 4.0 and the flow efficiencies that can keep them competitive.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers right now and how are you addressing these issues for them?

MF: Customers have short lead times (as OEMs want to keep minimal inventories) so speed of process flow and getting parts right first time are becoming more and more important.

ISMR: How are you leveraging technologies such as Industry 4.0, artificial intelligence, Big Data, VR, robotics etc. for your business?

MF: Our customers are now asking about these technologies without being prompted. They know that they need Industry 4.0. We have been practising Industry 4.0 principles for many years now through our centralised CADMAN database (the hub) to help users manage processes, run times, feedback etc. We continue to develop the software - the latest version is CADMAN® 8.7, which allows us to add value to operations by enabling users to implement ancillary operations into the planning flow.

We already work with automation specialists, such as KUKA, on bending robotics.

We are co-developing Virtual Environment programming solutions, such as CADMAN®-SIM, which is the key element to re-imagining bending automation. With this unique software solution, we can get from 'Art to Part' in under 20 minutes including robot and press brake programming and even set-up and first part production.

However, the over-riding question with any new emerging technology is whether this adds value to customer processes. We are application-driven and want to understand the process. We will only bring a new technology to market when we can clearly see how it can add value. We understand Big Data and Industry 4.0, and understand that they add value to the entire process flow. It underpins our philosophy and all our machine development.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
