CAD/CAM nesting software automatically tools all single hits

SigmaTEK Systems has released SigmaNEST X1.4 CAD/CAM nesting software. This new version features a customizable ribbon toolbar, which allows users to create custom groups, display functions that are used most often and hide the ones that are not, add and remove functions from all groups, and rearrange all groups and functions within the toolbar.

The software also offers an HD rectangular nesting engine. This dynamic algorithm allows users to nest rectangular parts with an average 3 percent increase in yield compared to the SigmaNEST rectangular nesting engine.

The new Auto Single Hit function for punch users automatically tools all single hits quickly and efficiently, the company states, eliminating the need to manually click on each one. The user can select which types of tools will be used and whether to apply tooling to internal, external, or all contours.

Band saw cuts tube upward from support surface

Kasto offers the KASTOwin tube A 5.0 fully automatic band sawing machine for tubular material. It cuts round tubes up to 19.7 in., with a shortest possible cutting length of 0.39 in.

The direction of movement of the saw feed is rotated through 180 degrees, and sawing is performed upward from the support surface. This helps ensure precise sawing with improved chip flow and reduced cutting tool wear.

With the frequency-controlled drive, cutting speed can be adjusted continuously between 39.4 and 492.1 FPM. The carbide saw band is clamped in position hydraulically and cleaned using a replaceable, electrically driven chip-removal brush. The vise and saw feed are equipped with zero-play linear guides, and ball screw spindle drives help ensure controlled cutting and material feed movements.

GTAW torch allows electrode cooling to the tip

Fronius has developed ArcTig, a modified GTAW process for mechanized joint welding. The torch has an electrode clamping system that enables the electrode to be cooled to the tip, increasing the combined resistance and creating a high arc voltage.

Electron emission takes place over a small area but at an extremely high density. This makes the arc narrower and more targeted for improved weld seam quality, the company states. The cooling also prevents overheating of the electrode during welding.

Users can weld up to 0.4-in.-thick, high-alloy sheets and pipes without extensive seam preparation, quickly, and to a high-quality standard, according to the manufacturer.

Laser system available with nLIGHT light sources

Cincinnati Incorporated now offers its 900 series fiber lasers with nLIGHT alta light sources to give job shops and OEMs the ability to cut reflective red metals. The 8-kW fiber lasers power an array of industrial cutting applications.

Proprietary back reflection technology allows cutting of reflective metals such as polished stainless, aluminum, brass, and copper at full power and stability.

According to the company, systems with this fiber laser technology can absorb the reflection and continue running with no error messages or operator intervention.

Press brake features 4-axis, dual Y1 and Y2 ram

Kaast Machine Tools Inc. offers the HPB series press brake, featuring a 4-axis, dual Y1 and Y2 ram with glass scales and Delem graphical touchscreen controllers.

The machine has a rigid upper beam running on eight-point bearings with a precision of 0.0004 in. and a 2-axis backgauge system with 31 in. of travel and 0.0004-in. repeatability. Also available is a 6-axis ATF dual independent backgauge for complex bends.

Hoerbieger hydraulics provide synchronized working of chrome-coated cylinders with up to 2,000 tons of pressure across 4 to 20 ft. of bending length. The machine includes quick-release clamping, with Promecam-type top clamps to help ensure rapid and accurate bends, the company states. A motorized X-axis backgauge and manual R-axis are standard.

AM machine produces metal parts with technology based on electric arc welding

Gefertec GmbH has introduced GTarc machines for manufacturing metal parts. Their 3DMP

®

additive manufacturing (AM) technology is based on electric arc welding, so it uses wire as original material. The final part is formed by applying welding layer over welding layer.

The machines manufacture near-net-shape metal parts to high quality standards that are comparable to standard manufacturing methods, says the company. Only the finishing of the part is done using standard CNC milling.

The machines are available in three versions, with three or five axes. CAM software calculates the data out of the CAD model, thus enabling the CNC unit to position the welding head precisely. The machines produce the near-netshape part automatically.
