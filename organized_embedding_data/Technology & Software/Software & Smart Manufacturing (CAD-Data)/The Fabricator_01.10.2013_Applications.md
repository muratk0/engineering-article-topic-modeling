CAD/CAM software converts customer-supplied 3-D models quickly for fabricator

Situation

Seeco, based in Bedford, U.K., produces precision components and fabrications for a variety of industries, including telecommunications, lighting, medical, power supply, and printing. Its equipment includes a punch/laser combination machine with automated sheet loader, a CNC press brake with in-process laser angle checking, a fastener-inserting machine with automated bowl feed, and a milling machine.

After installing SolidWorks®, the company was receiving 3-D models from many of its customers, so it decided to research CAD/CAM software systems.

Resolution

Seeco chose Lantek Expert, which was recommended because of its seamless link to SolidWorks and the way it works with the solid models. The software takes 3 to 4 min. to convert a 3-D model to a nested CNC program.

“The software looks at our library of tools, and if there is one available, it automatically punches the hole,” said Garry Law, technical director. “Otherwise, it generates a laser path. The only decision we have to make is if we want to add microjoints for parts or scrap too big to go down the chute.”

The company also uses Lantek Flex3d SW, which deals with complete multipart assemblies from SolidWorks. It interrogates the assembly and identifies all of the parts that need to be flattened before processing.

Seeco uses an ERP system that enables it to coordinate all aspects of its business, from customer requirements through material allocation and production schedules to shipping. The CAD/CAM software uses this information to nest a mixture of parts in each sheet, even in large apertures that would otherwise be scrap, to help maximize material use and ensure delivery targets are met.

“We produce a variety of parts, from single items to 1,000+ production runs,” Law said. “The punch/ laser machine runs 24/7, processing any amount of different products in various materials and gauges. We are able to put up to 2 tons of material at a time on the loading table, which is then processed without any manual input. This flexibility allows us to meet the ever-increasing requirements of our customers.”

When the CAD/CAM software was installed, only one small change to the postprocessor was required to get the punch/laser up and running. Since then the company has needed to use the software support only occasionally for advice on difficult parts.

Dust collector keeps things clean indoors for outdoor equipment manufacturer

Situation

Keystone Ridge Designs in East Butler, Pa., supplies steel trash receptacles, benches, bike racks, planters, and other heavy outdoor furniture to parks, campuses, municipalities, housing developments, and shopping malls.

“After we sandblast our substrates to a white finish to remove all possible contaminants, we electrostatically apply a zinc-rich epoxy primer, followed by a coat of colored polyester powder,” said Paul Arnold, vice president of operations. “Both coats are oven-cured to help ensure an optimal finish.”

The production process yields a substantial amount of dust and fumes, particularly while workers sandblast the substrate material during two shifts per day. Workers sometimes couldn’t stay in the blasting booth for more than 20 min. because of the heat and dust generated. And the refuse pot filled quickly for frequent emptying.

The company used small dust collectors within its powder coat booth, as well as a larger unit to remove air contaminants from the overall fabricating facility.

“Our basic issue was the horizontal positioning of the filters within our old collector,” Arnold said. “The filters were literally on top of one another. That meant the top one would stay relatively clean, but would dump material, when purging, onto the filters below it, leading to what we thought was very premature filter failure throughout the system.”

Resolution

After conducting a search into collectors that offered nontraditional, vertically hanging filter placement, Arnold chose the CMAXX® compact collector from Imperial Systems.

The modular cartridge collector uses the company’s DeltaMAXX™ vertically hanging filters with nanofiber filtration technology, which incorporates a very fine surface layer of synthetic fibers to prevent particulates from embedding into the premier-quality media. As a result, the filters provide twice the service life of competitive models, while yielding a 15 ASHRAE MERV efficiency rating, according to the manufacturer.

In nine months since the filter installation, Keystone had not needed to change a single filter. “Our air is remarkably clean throughout the blasting area—and the entire shop, in fact. The only maintenance I’ve had to do is to apply some grease to a few screws around the compressor,” said Justin Pyle, shop manager. “And best of all, material from one filter doesn’t fall down onto another filter because of the vertical positioning.”

The unit’s pulse distribution technology delivers 100 percent even pulsation across the filter wall to help maximize performance and filter life.

“[It] has amazing suction power; it draws air from everywhere,” stated Arnold. “Its construction ensures that contaminants do not get back into the airstream and blown back at our workers. Plus, because there are no bolt holes, water cannot get into the unit to damage the filters.”
