From a product point of view, the latest software can interpret data to create a flat format of a 3D shape in a fraction of the time that it would take a more manual process to achieve. As well as the speed, it helps to free up an employee's time so that they are not laboured with manually intensive jobs that a smart tool can do, but instead prioritising projects that require that all-important human touch.

A fully automated warehouse system further enables a workforce to monitor a firm's storage – for example, how many sheets it has remaining, enabling critical stock control.

Additionally, businesses will be able to maintain precision via quality and welders can avoid repetitive, high-volume tasks because modern robots can always be 'on' and their work is consistently accurate. Painters are also given the knowledge to use a fully automated powder coating system that automatically sprays components and provides an efficient, streamlined process.

Alongside the efficiency, commercial advantage and competitive edge that smart machinery can provide, it should also go a long way towards promoting a sheet metal fabricating workshop that is motivated and happy because employees are focusing on the jobs they want to do, rather than those that they have to do.

With automation, an organisation's management can monitor all processes, even if they're not on the factory floor, and tap into a 24/7 service that encourages a modernised approach to manufacturing - allowing businesses to stay ahead of the curve and remain relevant in a saturated marketplace.

DIGITALISATION, individualisation and climate protection are megatrends which represent challenges for coating jobshops and companies with in-house painting operations. With roughly 500 exhibitors already registered at the end of December 2019, the forthcoming PaintExpo will offer a range of solutions from 21-24 April 2020 in Karlsruhe, Germany. The exhibition portfolio of this leading trade fair for industrial coating technologies covers all process sequences for liquid and UV painting, as well as powder coating and coil coating.

"Companies with in-house painting operations specialising in vehicle manufacturing, capital goods and consumer goods production, architectural applications and all other industry sectors are facing these challenges – regardless of whether components made of metals, plastics, wood and wooden materials, glass, composites or other materials need to be painted or coated," said the event organiser, FairFair GmbH.

Around 500 companies from 26 countries had already booked their booth floor space at the end of December 2019. With approximately 16,000 square metres of net exhibition floor space, they have already reserved more than all the exhibition floor space occupied at the event in 2018.

"Attention will, of course, also be focused on automation and digitalisation solutions for painting processes at PaintExpo. In the meantime, it's no longer just a question of automating individual coating steps, but rather of networking the process chain," continued the organiser.

Amongst other solutions, multi-axis robots specially designed for the coating industry and robot painting cells for liquid painting and powder coating will be presented. Efficient solutions for offline programming of robot motion sequences are also available in addition to programs for manual teach-in. The required application technology is adapted to the respective project. New spray gun controllers ensure precise application. All painting parameters can be accessed and adjusted accurately – in some cases even from a smartphone or a tablet. This also helps to limit material consumption and reduce operating costs.

"PaintExpo exhibitors will also present innovative solutions which make it possible for the operating companies to integrate their MES systems to implement automated monitoring of process parameters such as paint quantities, temperature and process air, right up to spray jet monitoring, and to log and evaluate the resulting data. Quality control systems for the coated surfaces can also be integrated into the process," added the organiser.

As a result of the trend towards greater individualisation, demand for multi-colour coatings is continuously on the rise. The goal is to apply coatings without any masking at all, or with significantly reduced masking work and less overspray. In addition to new developments for faster and easier masking, the use of electrostatic application systems, which inherently cause less overspray, is making its contribution. A new application solution which functions entirely without overspray is now being used in series production for two-tone vehicle roof painting.
