# Evolution of work instructions in metal fabricating

Digital connectivity makes it possible to interact with physical devices like PLCs, sensors

Digital work instructions provide shop floor employees with a visual guide to accompany written directions. Digital connectivity with devices like PLCs and sensors promises to turn this type of digital interface into an automated quality control tool for manual activities.

or those not keeping score, industry pundits suggest manufacturing is entering a period being billed as Industry 5.0—moving from a focus on digitalization and automation of processes using artificial intelligence and internet technologies (which happened to be Industry 4.0) to more of a broad view of how humans can enhance these systems. A good example of this scenario coming to fruition is in the latest release from VKS, a developer of a no-code, browser-based digital work instruction platform.

Readers of The Fabricator might recognize VKS from previous stories written over the last decade about the company. VKS started as an offshoot of CMP Advanced Mechanical Solutions, Chateauguay, Quebec, which had developed digital work instructions to help it reduce quality errors in its production process. Simply put, some employees struggled with drawings, such as converting a 2D image to 3D reality. Digital representations more accurately demonstrated what needed to happen, and that proved a useful tool in helping to reduce error rates. The digital platform also provided engineers a very quick way to update drawings, eliminating the need to chase down old routers that still might be found on the shop floor.

Over the years, VKS has added enhancements such as real-time tracking of worker performance and even traceability of parts. Now it’s entering an era that VKS officials think is a great example of the promise of Industry 5.0—embracing the strengths of the human worker to truly unlock the potential of advanced technologies.

The Power of Connection

In the latest release of the VKS software, IO Connect is included. It’s described as an add-on that allows digital work instructions to be connected with physical devices like PLCs, sensors, devices, and machines. This type of direct connection actually makes the work instructions a vital part of any process. Instead of just being a guide for an employee, the VKS platform is essential to quality efforts, literally not allowing an employee to proceed until a part is checked or a series of actions validated.

"It’s debatably one of the biggest things that we’ve released in a very long time," said Ryan Zimmermann, VKS’ director of business development. "It’s going to really change the way that connectivity is seen on the shop floor when we’re talking about operators doing manual processes."

This IO Connect enhancement comes on the heels of some other VKS digital connectivity achievements. DataConnect made it possible for the work instruction platform to communicate with databases and database-driven equipment to send and receive real-time data, giving manufacturing teams the vital information they need instantly. ToolConnect IoT integrated torque tools into the platform framework, allowing performance parameters to be sent out to the tools and capturing and validating torque values. Through the combination of integrated work instructions and real-time feedback from tools and equipment, VKS users can automatically track output, productivity, and live progress of manual activities.

While those tools are helpful in keeping manual processes in line, IO Connect is more of a guarantee of high-quality results when it comes to manual processes.

"We’re creating a fully automated workstation—so much so, you can now automate a manual process," said Kyle O’Reilly, VKS’ director of sales. "We can connect with a sensor that knows when the person showed up at the workstation. We know when he picks up a tool. We know that he’s inserted a screw, and he’s tightened it. We now can know if it’s the right screw and if it’s done correctly."

Potential Examples

To better understand how work instructions tied into physical devices might change a process, VKS officials offered up a couple of examples:

• IO Connect makes it possible for a visual inspection system to operate from within the work instruction platform. For example, a hardware insertion machine operator might lean on the work instructions for guidance in placing nuts on a sheet metal workpiece. When complete, an automated camera can take a picture of the workpiece and then determine if it meets specifications. If the part is as it should be, the work instructions continue. If not, the operator is notified of the problem. Additionally, VKS records and stores the pass/fail event along with associated serial numbers for quality control and assurance.

• Work instructions can communicate directly with sensors. Imagine that a metal fabricating company has an assembly area for electronics. Such environments usually require specific temperature, humidity, and sterility levels. So, before an assembler can begin a task, VKS communicates with room sensors to determine if the indoor climate is within accepted levels. If the environment meets production standards, the employee is able to proceed and use the VKS work instructions. If not, he or she has to take whatever actions are necessary to ensure the indoor environment meets those standards.

"Accurate data now can be collected on manual operations, just like automated operations," O’Reilly said.

Making It Happen

The VKS platform was designed so that a person with no coding experience could create work instructions. After a couple of hours of training, people should be able to create the digital guides using a drag-and-drop approach to the content creation.

While digital connectivity sounds intimidating for nonengineers, O’Reilly said a user doesn’t need coding experience or someone from IT to establish connections between the VKS software and shop floor devices. Identifying a few details, like a device’s IP address, is the first step, but then it’s just documenting the information flow within the platform.

"When initiating this, you output the signal, note the value that you want to receive in return, and set up the response you want once that value is received," O’Reilly said.

IO Connect is now available in the latest version of the VKS software, which was released in early October.
