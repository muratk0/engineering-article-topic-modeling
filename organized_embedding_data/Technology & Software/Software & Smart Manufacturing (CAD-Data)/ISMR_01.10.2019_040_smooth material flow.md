# SMOOTH MATERIAL FLOW

[TARİH: 01.10.2019 ISMR]

Horticulture and machine specialist, Nobels BV, relies upon a modular automation concept to increase its productivity and material flow.

T he horticulture and agricultural sectors recently experienced a major boom - to keep up with the competition, companies need to position themselves for the future and consistently respond to market demand.

The Nobels Group, which includes the companies Nobels, Javo, Agro Techniek and Javo International, is an innovative global provider of automation solutions for agriculture, horticulture and industry. Within this group, Nobels BV acts as a machine development and production location. As part of the reconstruction and reorganisation of its production hall, the company decided to automate its laser systems using modular C systems from Remmert.

By adopting a flexible retrieval strategy which was adapted to production requirements, Nobels was able to more than double its output with just one laser cutting system, instead of the two previously used, whilst significantly optimising laser utilisation.

## Increasing productivity and material flow

Nobels BV, which is based in the Netherlands and operates internationally, specialises in the production of machines for agriculture and horticulture. This includes small batches and large-scale production. The entire design and development process, which in some cases is customised, takes place at the company's headquarters in Noordwijkerhout, in the province of South Holland. Arnold van Nobelen started here in 1955 with the development of a sorting machine. Three years later, Nobels produced the first prototypes for a harvesting and planting machine, which went on sale in 1958. Today, Nobels focuses on the research, development and manufacture of potting machines, tray automation and soil and robot systems.

Nobels was able to more than double its output with just one laser cutting system, instead of the two previously used, whilst significantly optimising laser utilisation.

The company is continuously expanding its portfolio and production site. This, it told ISMR, goes 'hand-in-hand' with the modernisation and automation of its machines and factories. Rapid and efficient processes are vital. In this context, laser cutting systems presented a particular challenge in terms of automation.

"We now have 30 years of experience in laser cutting. That's why we knew exactly what we envisioned and wanted in this respect," explained Ber van Nobelen, Nobels Group's proprietor.

The main goal for the Nobels project was to increase productivity and optimise material flow. To achieve this, the company wanted to be able to work through the night shift in the new hall without reducing utilisation of the rapid fibre laser. To this end, it integrated the Remmert LaserFLEX, as an automation solution, into the process (in addition to a BASIC tower that acts as a raw sheet metal store). This is employed in combination with the belt conveyor and used as a second BASIC tower to buffer finished parts.

With 85 storage locations, the first BASIC Tower is used as a storage tower for 3015-format raw sheet metal. The second tower has 43 storage locations. The system, consisting of the LaserFLEX and belt conveyor, allows rapid manual sorting of lasercut sheet metal parts. It provides material from the finished parts tower for further processing on the belt conveyor, or via the station for outgoing material, to enable Nobels to design night shifts in a more efficient way with no disruptions during the day.

## 'Seamless’ material flow

"The raw sheet metal is initially provided via the station for incoming material and stored in the BASIC Tower. The tower then provides the material to the transfer unit that, in turn, relays it to the loading unit. With a material changeover time of around one minute, we believe that LaserFLEX is the fastest solution on the market for loading and unloading laser-cutting machines," Remmert told ISMR.

For material retrieval, two strategies are used: either the laser-cut sheet is provided for manual sorting immediately after laser cutting via the belt conveyor, or it is stored in the BASIC Tower II buffer storage solution. In turn, there are two variants. One option is to bring the sheet back in automatic mode to the belt conveyor for manual sorting. Another option is to provide the laser-cut sheet for further processing in a 'dead-man' operation via the outgoing station.

"The system can therefore retrieve material in different ways, so that we can be completely flexible in our choice of retrieval strategy and use the appropriate method for any situation. This has two major advantages: not only do we keep a close eye on the material flow in the Remmert system, but we can also organise our entire intralogistics operations a lot more effectively," explained van Nobelen.

Nobels is not only able to operate outgoing goods from the buffer storage system, but can also simultaneously or separately transport finished parts again on the conveyor belt.

"This allows the laser-cut sheet to be buffered at night in the second tower while the day shift provides the sheets for further processing," reported van Nobelen. "The Remmert system provides us with more efficiently organised production."

## Rapid automation and a modular approach

LaserFLEX features two components, loading and unloading units, which work independently. The process of feeding the laser machine can therefore be decoupled. While the loading unit can place new raw material on the laser, the unloading unit can, for example, provide finished parts on the belt conveyor. After the sheet metal has been laser-cut, the loading unit lifts the processed sheet from the laser shuttle table. At the same time, the loading unit picks up a new sheet from the connected BASIC Tower. As soon as the unloader returns the sheet to store it back into the BASIC Tower or provides it for other processes, the loader places a new sheet on the laser.

"LaserFLEX needs approximately one minute for this material change, which virtually eliminates downtime and significantly improves laser uptime," added Remmert.

The integrated Remmert automation solution can be integrated with a choice of cutting equipment from laser manufacturers, depending upon the user’s preference. The components are modular and can be easily connected to software.

"An example of this is in the laser cutting system planning at Nobels. The company initially wanted to use two laser cutting systems, but then decided to start with just one system. The LaserFLEX solution is flexible enough to accommodate expansions, changes and independent manufacturer changes – it can be modified quickly and easily and provides customisation options," added Remmert.

"Besides the hardware components, another key component of the modular system is SMART Control. This is an intuitive software and control system for the entire production process, which can be easily integrated into existing systems due to open interfaces. With self-explanatory pictograms and little text, it is possible to operate the connected Remmert components – LaserFLEX and BASIC tower – intuitively. Through error message visualisation, the user immediately has an overview of where the problem is in the system and can quickly eliminate downtime in this way.”

## An eye on efficiency

"One great advantage is undoubtedly that Remmert is a single-source provider," said van Nobelen. "We had very complex requirements for the working processes. In spite of this, in close collaboration with Remmert, we managed to bring these together into one needs-based concept for a customised complete solution. Remmert experts were there to provide professional advice and practical help at all stages of the project."

Matthias Remmert, Managing Director, Remmert GmbH, also highlighted the benefits of this modular approach.

"The modularity of our approach means that we are able to support each and every client on the basis of their production requirements and, thus, develop the best possible concept for the flow of materials. The modularity of our standard components also allows us to adapt the system to changing requirements over time," he told ISMR.

"In this way, we are also able to respond to new client requirements and expand or upgrade modules or processes as needed," added Jan Schimmelpfennig, Product Manager, Remmert GmbH.

Through its fibre laser cutting system and the deployment of LaserFLEX, Nobels can now go through twice as much material and the laser cutting system can be better utilised than before. Together with LaserFLEX, the output from one laser is now double the previous output from both lasers combined, it told ISMR.

"This 200 per cent increase in productivity is remarkable," summarised van Nobelen. "Thanks to the modularity of the system, a second laser can be easily added and is being planned. This increases both future-proofing and planning security. We are very satisfied with the solution, the sound and comprehensive advice and the successful partnership with Remmert and we are convinced that we have a stable and supportive partner for the future."
