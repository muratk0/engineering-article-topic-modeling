Boschert USA Embraces Industry 4.0 to Serve Customers Better

One of the basic tenets of Industry 4.0: when disruption knocks, opportunity is close behind. That proved to be true as fabrication evolved during COVID 19.

Before COVID, Boschert USA was already embracing definitive 4.0 characteristics—AI, automation, robotics, the Internet of Things and data analytics. The pandemic further impacted routine approaches to sales, service and training, while limiting prospects’ timetables/investments in networked technologies. But that didn’t stop innovation. Instead, Boschert USA stepped up strategies to advance its commitments to customers in both machinery and service.

Predictive Maintenance Controls

Boschert USA’s brand teams added controls that enhance machine learning, networked databases, automated loading and robotics. For example:

Boschert GmbH has controls that anticipate the growing demand for networked manufacturing. Usage data can be gathered on machines to schedule routine maintenance like oil and tooling changes. Machines even monitor performance data to prevent mechanical issues before they occur. "While only a few of our customers access these features," says Boschert USA’s President Greg Hoesly, we expect this to soon be a real growth area, even for smaller fabricators."

Picot has introduced second generation EasyRoll© so• ware, an AI-level database that stores customer’s production processes and applies 30 years of experience and bending data. EasyRoll© flawlessly guides bending procedures based on complex material properties.

With PC400 controls and automatic radius measuring, PBT profile bending machines can bend a range of extrusions into complex shapes, meeting exacting specifications and reducing waste—without skilled labor.

Customer Relationships

"We also adapted to meet customer engagement expectations," Hoesly continued. "Trade shows, road trips and in-shop training came to a halt. So we made better use of technology to support customers and meet prospects, ramping up digital communications, with a bigger commitment to video and virtual interaction:"

Boschert USA introduced HoloLens remote training, maintenance and service. Technicians work hands-on with customer teams in their shops. "Customers get great service, faster and with less downtime," says Hoesly.

Boschert is testing a virtual trade show booth at FABTECH. "We learned a lot when 2021 cancelled," says Hoesly. "In Atlanta, we’re showcasing our brands remotely—in operations across Europe and in our showroom in Wisconsin, delivering virtual demos and providing access to experts."

"We are proud of how we have adapted and embraced new opportunities," concluded Hoesly. "We are gratified by the success it has led to. And we are excited to test new approaches to meet changing customer needs."
