# Automation peace of mind via the internet

[TARİH: 01.10.2025 The Fabricator]

Tech Spotlight

An app helps an automation integrator troubleshoot cobot bending cells effectively and affordably

By

Dan Davis

Automated bending cells sold by Mid Atlantic Machinery Automation now come with an app that provides operational data on the cobot and video, both of which can be synced up for review. The app from Olis Robotics is designed to run on a smartphone or tablet.

Olis Robotics

I

t’s an exciting time to be a part of U.S. manufacturing, with reshoring activity increasing and laws being put into place to encourage extensive capital equipment investment. Simultaneously, however, some metal fabricating companies are dragging their feet when it comes to preparing for expanded opportunities in the future. Automation can be intimidating.

That’s the way Josh Mayse, vice president and co-founder of Mid Atlantic Machinery Automation, sees it. As an automation integrator, the company has seen a lot of interest in its AutoTend, a cobot-based press brake tending system, but it’s also seen some hesitancy from small to medium-sized shops about jumping into this type of automation for the first time.

"In these instances, they don’t think they have the right people," Mayse said. "They are intimidated by it, and they’re concerned that they’re not going to be able to get the support they want."

That type of support can come with on-site training and checkups, but that escalates the investment cost for such automation. After all, a cobot-focused cell is attractive because it doesn’t require the same investment level as an industrial robot.

Mayse has thought about this extensively, trying to find the right balance between good customer service and keeping costs in check. He thinks he might have found the answer in an app from Olis Robotics that can run on Android-based smartphones and tablets.

Direct Connection

The app turns these smart devices into gateways inside the operations of an automation cell. Mayse said he plans to include a tablet with the Olis Robotics app downloaded on it, a tripod to support the tablet, and a tablet mount with the purchase of every AutoTend cell. He’s also going to cover the first year’s $499 license.

"It’s exciting because it really doesn’t inflate the price of my product," Mayse said. "And it allows me to offer world-class support that I’ve been striving to give the end user."

The smart device is connected to the controller via an Ethernet cable. Once IP addresses are configured, the smart device can begin to collect diagnostic data.

This connection allows for access over the internet. Once connected, a Mid Atlantic Machinery Automation technician could access the inner workings of the cobot to see what is going on.

"We can see exactly what they’re seeing on the teach pendant. We can see the states of inputs and outputs. We can see the camera feed," he said. Mayse described a recent incident where a gripper on the cobot wasn’t working properly. A technician was able to access the cobot and compare operational indicators with the actual actions of the cobot. With both the diagnostics and the camera feed available for review, the technician only needed 15 minutes to determine that the fault wasn’t related to wiring. A bad valve caused the gripper to not perform as expected.

"So you might have somebody that doesn’t speak the same language as the control engineers on the other side of the phone, but now they’re seeing the same things," Mayse said. "A lot of that lost-in-translation stuff that you deal with is now gone."

As this digital representation of an AutoTend cell reveals, guarding is necessary because the force- and torque-limited cobot is running outside of collaborative speeds. The automated bending cells are set up for production-level work, making uptime very important.

Instantaneous Help

This type of digital connection with the metal fabricator goes beyond just troubleshooting. It can be helpful with training as well.

The Olis Robotics app supports screen sharing, so Mid Atlantic Machinery Automation personnel can show the fabricator how to do something on the cobot’s teach pendant. This quickly provides the metal fabricator with an answer that it might otherwise have to wait for until a qualified technician is available for a visit.

"This way they don’t become so dependent on having a person there to help them," Mayse said.

He added that he’s used the app himself to help with a recent installation where he was working on wiring and trying to fire some outputs to see what was going on. Trying to accomplish the two tasks was "like standing on your head," Mayse said.

He reached out to a colleague in Texas via the app to have him fire off the outputs while he watched the cobot’s PLC. Mayse said he was able to finish the job as if he and his colleague were in the same room.

A Tool for the Future

Mayse views the app not only as a tool for troubleshooting and training, but also as a productivity enhancer.

Fabricators can use the app to configure alerts so that when the robot stops running, someone can respond. This allows an engineer to focus on other duties and react should an alert go off.

In the future, Mayse said he would love to have this app feed production data to a web server, which managers could access for overall equipment effectiveness reports.

"This helps the bigger companies get into automated reporting and starts to give small companies the ability to use the tools that the large companies have been using for years," he said.

One alternative to this app is putting a small computer in the cabinet, which adds another processor to the system. This adds considerable expense and complexity.

Another alternative is flying technicians everywhere for offline cells, investigating troublesome processes, and answering questions about programming. That costs money and eats up a lot of time, which metal fabricators and technicians don’t have in abundance.

This is one example of an internet-enabled technology delivering on the promise of digital manufacturing.
