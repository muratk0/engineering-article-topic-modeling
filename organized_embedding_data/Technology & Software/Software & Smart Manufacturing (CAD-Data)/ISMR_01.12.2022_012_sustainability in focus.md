## Technical focus

There is a lack of qualified workers in the sheet metal industry so automation requirements for machine tools have been steadily rising. ARKU has seen this trend reflected in its machine tool sales, and particularly from requests from customers for more automation around ARKU machine tools. "One machine that we showed at Blechexpo, the EdgeBreaker® 6000, is a deburring machine for laser-cut parts. The machine can adjust and calibrate itself logically on minimal information which makes it very easy for operators, and also allows automation. We combine machine, tools, software and handling concepts to provide complete solutions to our customers. We also work with partners on automation options e.g. handling devices etc. and advise customers on the entire process," outlined Andreas Hellriegel.

"We are currently working with partners to fully automate the deburring process with a robot, where the robot needs no teaching or programming. We have already proved this concept with installations in Germany and it not only shortens set-up time considerably but can also be used with smaller part batches," he continued.

"Together with TRUMPF, we have introduced a new coil laser cutting system," commented Hellriegel. "As batches get smaller and smaller, the combination of coil and laser becomes more interesting i.e. material flow and less scrap, thanks to better nesting possibilities. The coil guarantees continuous material flow. We see interesting potential for it in the future."

## Digitalisation and Industry 4.0

Hellriegel also took ISMR through a two-year long project aimed at integrating ARKU machines into existing production environments. He pointed to a growing trend of more manufacturers wishing to integrate machines into larger production lines, rather than using one-off or stand-alone machines that process parts. Users may choose to put a deburring machine in front of a leveller on a line or connect it with other machines.

The company has developed a standard interface so that ARKU machines can interact with existing production systems/ environments, displayed in the TRUMPF smart factory in Ditzingen (Germany). "Our machines can now be set up for Industry 4.0 and for integration with other machines. It is becoming more important for customers to be able to track their machine data and production bottlenecks. They can learn from the data, ensure more production efficiencies and get the best use out of their machines. They can also remotely monitor the machines, carry out online surveys, check maintenance etc.," Hellriegel told ISMR.

"In January 2022, we will also start using new ERP software. This will be crucial for the efficiency of our production processes and will generally streamline all our processes. It also prepares us for the challenges of digitalisation and Industry 4.0.," he added.

The company is devoting a lot of its energy to digitalisation and service for its customers.

"We must follow these trends in the market. We therefore have to listen carefully to our customers. That will also be our focus for next year," concluded Andreas Hellriegel.
