AC inverters help improve energy input into welded material

Amada Weld Tech Inc. has released the MIB-300A and MIB-600A AC inverter welding power supplies. They feature secondary constant current control up to 20 pulses per weld, as well as the ability to set upslope, downslope, and weld interrupt. Up- and downslope help improve energy input into the welded material, while weld interrupt stops the flow of current if displacement breaches a set level, preventing overwelding.

The power supplies may be used with the same transformers and weld heads as standard AC welders. With maximum output currents of 20,000 and 40,000 amps, respectively, the units may be used for a variety of welding applications including sheet metal welding, wire compacting, and brazing.

Machine takes novel approach to metal folding

Roper Whitney, a Tennsmith company, announced a new partnership with EvoBend GmbH to bring the company’s folding technology to the North American market.

In conventional folding, clamping tools secure the sheet metal before a folding beam swings upward and (in bidirectional machines) downward to bend the flange. EvoBend takes a different approach. The machine’s upper and lower clamping tools are slanted to provide 300 degrees of bending clearance. If the first flange is bent in one direction 150 degrees, the second flange can be bent in the other direction by up to 300 degrees.

The machine has no swinging beam. Instead, flush with the slanted work clamps are upper and lower bending tools that slide toward the workpiece. A hooked tip on each bending tool gives it the ability to make bends past 90 degrees. The bending tool on the lower clamp slides up to make positive bends, while the bending tool on the upper clamp slides down to make negative bends.

Available in different lengths to accommodate 126-, 252-, and 317-in. folds, the servo-electric machine is suited for continuous part profiles and can fold up to 14-ga. mild steel, 0.125-in.-thick aluminum, and 18-ga. stainless steel.

System detects edge position in robotic welding

Fronius offers the WireSense system to assist with automated robotic welding. The patented technology works with the welding system to enable the robot to detect edge position and any air gaps between the sheets.

To do so, the wire electrode becomes a height sensor. A low sensor voltage is supplied to the wire as it scans the component with reversing wire movement. If it touches the component, an imperceptible short circuit occurs, which is cleared by raising the wire. This allows each geometrical change on the component to be registered with precision.

A defined threshold, which lies somewhat below the sheet edge height, is determined in advance. If the power source detects values above this threshold during the scanning process, this indicates that the edge of the sheet has been detected and the TPS/i power source outputs a digital touch signal and the determined height value. As a result, the robot knows where the edge of the sheet is and how high it is.

The robot controller can use this signal to save its current positional data and then correct the robot path by comparing it with the target data. Any component inaccuracies are thus recognized and compensated for. The robot then welds in the correct location.

As the sheet edge height also is transmitted with the digital touch signal, the system calculates air gaps between the sheets. Stored welding programs can be called up as required for the various air gaps, allowing the robot to respond appropriately and weld with the parameters that are suitable for the air gap dimension.

Cooling management systems keep coolant free of contaminants

Accustrip offers coolant management systems designed to minimize downtime by keeping coolant free of chips and impurities. The company supplies installations of all sizes, from small machine shops with a single CNC machine to large plants. The systems can transport tons of chips and coolant from far-flung CNC machines to a central system, then separate chips and particles from the coolant and transport the chips back to the foundry to be cast and machined again.

The systems remove the lubricant that inevitably ends up in the coolant without a need for slow processes in enormous tanks. This is done in the same flow, taking advantage of the oil’s natural propensity to aggregate, so after it is collected at a high speed in a patented capillary system, it can be routed out of the flow.

Impurities down to 15 microns are filtered off with no outside intervention. Filters are flushed clean with coolant and compressed air, and impurities are collected and discharged dried in a container.
