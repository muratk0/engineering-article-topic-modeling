Victor Manufacturing Ltd in Keighley, West Yorkshire (UK), has established itself as a leading supplier of equipment for the catering, hospitality and retail sectors. It specialises in both standard and custom equipment as well as counters to ensure food is at optimum temperature at the point of service. Its product range includes hot cupboards; hot plates; plate dispensers; banquet carts; tabletop servery units; heated drawer units; refrigerated salad wells; multi-decks and heated servery and carvery counters.

The company started life in central Bradford, manufacturing paint tins and dryers for tea towels back in 1947. Today, the business boasts a turnover approaching £12 million and is responsible for employing over 125 people. Throughout its long and prominent history, the brand has built a reputation for quality and flexibility, maintaining both over more than 75 years in business. Today, it supplies sheetmetal products to some of the UK's largest companies and groups in many market sectors including restaurant and hotel groups; retail chains; contract catering operators; education; offshore and UK Ministry of Defence installations.

“Our products are manufactured to a high quality and are designed to have a long lifespan. All products are supplied with a full two-year parts and labour warranty as standard. Where they are installed in retail, healthcare or educational establishments, it is not uncommon to find Victor counters or hot cupboards still in daily use some 20 years later," commented the company.

## Spreading its wings

In 2018, a management buy-out meant that the company is now owned by Phil Williams, with several of the Williams family working alongside him (including his wife and children). Before becoming owner and MD, Phil Williams held the post of managing director, and has over 22 years of service as a Victor employee.

In addition to his duties for Victor, Phil Williams serves as President of EFCEM (European Federation of Catering Equipment Manufacturers) following many years as an active member of the council of the FEA (Foodservice Equipment Association) UK trade association. This helps to enable Victor Manufacturing to steer alongside the direction of the industry and keep up to date with new trends and developments in this rapidly changing market.

The company relocated towards the end of 2020, moving 11 miles from Bradford to the outskirts of Keighley (Yorkshire, UK). The new factory site spans over 108,000 square feet, has enabled company expansion and offers a more practical layout of both factory and office block.

## Meeting new challenges

With pressure on fuel and transportation costs, as well as raw materials (including steel and glass) subject to extreme buying conditions, it has been difficult to minimise price increases across Victor's product portfolio. Suppliers are facing material shortages and increased lead times for components, so it has been a challenge acquiring some items from Europe and the Far East. Alternative parts are having to be sourced closer to home, at greater expense.

Taking these difficulties into account, one way to control costs is to maximise efficiency in all areas.

The sheet-metal fabrication division at Victor Manufacturing includes one Bystronic 6kW fibre laser cutting system which has been recently fitted with automation. This includes a storage tower with automatic loading and unloading, which feeds six Bystronic pressbrakes where laser-cut sheets are folded into shape.

"Upgrading the automation and software and standardising the Bystronic platform has allowed the night shift to run automatically, reducing the number of skilled operators required in this department. The overall capital upgrade has increased capacity dramatically over the past two years," Swiss sheet-metal specialist, Bystronic, told ISMR.

“Choosing us as a supplier has enabled easy control of software and automation and builds on Victor's experience with our team and its historical use of press brakes from the same stable," it added.

## COVID-19 response

During the COVID-19 pandemic, whilst many businesses either made the decision to, or were forced to, close, Victor Manufacturing committed to remaining open to satisfy demand for essential front-line care service, retail and education supplies.

One of the major projects it undertook during the first few months of the pandemic was winning the contract with a major high-street chain (specialising in selling coffee and baked goods) to provide COVID-19 screens made from glass with metal frames. This was to overcome a shortage of Perspex as the demand for that material caused supply issues and increased costs.

Contracts to supply equipment for Nightingale Hospitals were also of great importance as the UK responded to the rapid changes brought about during the pandemic. These are examples of the strategy of customer-led innovation that has enabled Victor Manufacturing to continue to develop products and offer complete solutions aimed at simplifying the supply chain for clients.

## Looking to the future

Introducing a robot to load one of Victor's press brakes will further automate the folding process for repetitive parts (sometimes 500 identical parts with four folds are required, which would require a dedicated operator to load and unload without a robot).

To further improve throughput of sheetmetal fabrication, one of the next steps is to bring powder coating capability in-house. Space is available on the new site, and this will improve the cost and lead time of currently outsourced coating and painting of sheet-metal work. Further investment in capital equipment will include a CNC router and an additional three-metre press brake to handle the larger folding requirements.

The introduction in March 2022 of a new range of refrigerated multidecks, specifically developed with large bakery chains as the target customer, has further improved Victor's product offering to its clients. It also recently established a purpose-built welding area to manufacture general fabrication work and enable back-of-house refurbishment of kitchens for clients, with Victor supplying all the equipment required to complete the refurbishment including custom carpentry, HVAC systems and lighting.

Expanding the capability of supply to the commercial catering equipment sector and using the latest in manufacturing equipment enables Victor Manufacturing to secure large contracts and deliver high-quality products which boast accurate control of temperature.

New opportunities in the food and drinks sector are also on the horizon to ensure the continued growth and success of this UK manufacturer. Trends in the industry towards "grab and go" and an increase in vending or selfservice environments create new demand in this important sector, which Victor Manufacturing has positioned itself to support.
