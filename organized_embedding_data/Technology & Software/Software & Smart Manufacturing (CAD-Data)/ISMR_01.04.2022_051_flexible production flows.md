# Flexible production flows

[TARİH: 01.04.2022 ISMR]

High-tech company TRUMPF will be presenting a new Oseon software package for production scheduling and control at its Digital Summit in-house trade fair on 6 April 2022. With its open interfaces, the software is also suitable for users who want to integrate machines from other suppliers into their fully networked production, in addition to TRUMPF machines. Oseon also allows users to fully automate the flow of materials on the shop floor, potentially eliminating the need to invest in a large-scale storage system.

"Manufacturers can use our new Oseon software to create significantly leaner production processes and make major gains in productivity. The software includes a range of new options for managing the transportation of materials in a flexible and automated fashion. Oseon also offers production workers a clear display of all relevant production data directly within their work environment," it told ISMR.

"As well as reducing downtime and nonproductive time, the software also makes better use of machine capacity and makes life easier for employees. The resulting productivity gains can be as high as 20 per cent," added TRUMPF product manager, Wolfgang Liertz. "Oseon lets users synchronise material flow with their production schedule so they can respond even faster and more flexibly to unexpected events such as rush orders, as well as processing more orders in less time."

The software also holds information on batches, material stocks, the locations of each cart and trolley and the parts required on the shop floor. The TRUMPF developers rely on a combination of barcodes and smart docking stations to efficiently manage the flow of materials. Alternatively, this journey can be made autonomously by an automated guided vehicle (AGV) controlled by Oseon.

Using a tablet, production staff they can see all the relevant information on a job without leaving their work environment. The software guides them through all forthcoming tasks.

TRUMPF also offers access to its new Condition Monitoring service. Its Predictive Service Centre receives up-to-date condition data from all the digitally connected machines on a customer's shop floor. Whenever an anomaly is detected that could mean a potential malfunction or downtime, it informs the customer proactively and sends them recommended solutions. This allows the customer to solve problems before they result in unexpected downtime or cause the machine to produce unusable parts.
