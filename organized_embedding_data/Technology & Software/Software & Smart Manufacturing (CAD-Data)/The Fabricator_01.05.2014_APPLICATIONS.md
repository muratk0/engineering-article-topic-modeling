Bridge-type CMM brings data collection in-house for precision stamper

Situation

Weiss-Aug Co. is a manufacturer of precision stampings, insert moldings, and interconnect equipment for the automotive, medical, and aerospace industries. Its component stampings go in various assemblies, including overlay molded assemblies for surgical devices.

In the past the company was producing the stampings and sending them out to a supplier for scanning, because there was an engineering demand for large quantities of data. That process took up to a week, and sometimes longer.

Resolution

Weiss-Aug acquired a Zeiss ACCURA® bridge-type CMM, which combines optical scanning and contact sensors, to collect the data in-house.

The company uses the line scan laser to collect very large quantities of data early in engineering development, when the part design isn’t finalized. The engineering team and the customer can view 100,000 data points on a part and see areas they might not have focused on before.

When the part gets closer to manufacturing, the company switches to the scanning probe technology for measuring a lot of profiles to the model.

Since the acquisition, the company now can collect the same amount of information for the engineers in less than 15 min. that used to take up to a week when outsourced. In addition, the system allows the company to perform model-based programming. Collected data can be compared to the model, with whisker plots showing the exact extent and location of the part’s deviations.

Contract manufacturer uses software to cut estimating time, improve costing accuracy

Situation

Sunset Metal Works is a contract manufacturer of painted metal weldments and fabrications to OEMs. It serves a variety of segments in agriculture and construction equipment, aerial work platforms, vertical transportation, oil and gas equipment, Department of Defense, and custom work. Its services include shape cutting of all ferrous and nonferrous materials, forming, machining, tube bending, materials joining, finishing, assembly, and reverse engineering.

The company has relied heavily on “tribal knowledge” and experience in its cost estimating efforts. In 2007 the company implemented a new ERP system and began to use that as its primary cost estimating tool as it created bills of materials for each project. The cost estimating process was thorough, but fairly lengthy. And the company was winning only 30 percent of the quotes it put together.

“We were slow and often missing quoting deadlines as we tried to balance all the new and existing customer opportunities we were quoting on,” said Laura Nicklas Sponseller, head of business development. And an increasing number of existing customers were looking for greater transparency in the cost process. This added pressure to document what were highly manual processes.

Resolution

The need to take action hit home in 2012 when Sponseller saw an article about another contract manufacturer that had deployed a product cost management platform from aPriori. The article talked about how “the first quote in often wins,” especially where there is an existing relationship.

Sunset decided to implement the software. While Sponseller had read that the software typically was used only at the OEM level, she and her colleagues saw an opportunity to differentiate Sunset by investing in a more in-depth product costing capability that would enable it to respond to customer RFQs more quickly and accurately, strengthening its ability to win more new business.

The flexible costing engine can determine instantly the cost of a part or product from a 3-D CAD model, based on the materials to be used and the manufacturing environment. With the click of a button, users can get a real-time cost assessment at any time during the development process. As changes are made, the software automatically reassesses and provides an updated product cost.

The software also allows users to use product cost data hosted in other enterprise applications to calculate costs and communicate product cost information among all functional organizations involved in product definition and delivery. This includes the import of complex engineering bills of materials from ERP, PLM, and other enterprise applications.

Today the software enables the company to produce detailed and accurate product cost data for its customers.
