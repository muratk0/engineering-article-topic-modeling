# ’Take That’ Amada

[TARİH: 01.03.2012 ISMR]

A LARGE proportion of the structural parts for Om, the giant robot taking centre-stage on band Take That’s record-breaking Progress Tour in 2011, were produced by UK-based Warren Services using Amada technology.

The internal skeleton and drive tug for the 60ft robotic Om featured a multitude of mild steel plate parts ranging in thickness from 5mm up to 20mm. With a lead-time of eight weeks between receipt of drawing and first rehearsals, the speed and flexibility of Warren’s automated laser and folding cell was fundamental to the success of the project.

Taller than the Angel of the North, Om is able to move forward and stand upright with outstretched arms, holding band members in his giant hands. The largest individual components profiled by Warren Services were some of the shoulder elements, which measured approximately 1.0m x 0.5m.

The centrepiece of the automated fabrication cell at Warren Services is an Amada LC3015 F1 4kW CNC laser profiling centre. There is also an Amada ASLUL automation tower that stores pallets of raw material and automatically loads and off-loads sheets. This enables the F1 to operate without manual set-up or intervention, allowing lights-out production if required. The final element of the cell is an Amada HFP 220-4 (220 tonne, 4m) CNC sevenaxis press brake. ■

## FANUC named a top global innovator

FANUC CORPORATION, a diversified manufacturer of CNC systems, robots and machine tools, was recently named by Thomson Reuters as a 2011 Top 100 Global Innovator. This new Thomson Reuters programme is an initiative that recognises the world’s most innovative organisations as a result of analysing patent data and related metrics in a proprietary methodology.

Thomson Reuters’ Top Global Innovators programme evaluates innovation performance of companies that invent on a significant scale and work on developments that are acknowledged as innovative by others around the world and whose inventions are globally protected due to their importance.

This is the second top innovation recognition that FANUC received in 2011. Forbes magazine also named FANUC one of the top 100 companies on its “World’s Most Innovative Companies” list. Today, FANUC has more than 600 engineers working in R&D and, with more than 2,200,000 CNCs and 220,000 robots installed worldwide, is a leading player in CNC and robotics. ■

## Remote bridge building

THE RECENTLY completed San Francisco-Oakland Bay Bridge US$ 350 million dollar project, led by the American Bridge-Fluor joint venture and sub-contracted to Shanghai Zhenhua Heavy Industries Co (ZPMC), has just been successfully completed in Shanghai and shipped to the USA.

During construction of the Bay Bridge on a small island close to Shanghai called Changxing, ZPMC (the contractor) experienced some welding-related issues that had to be resolved before the completed components could be shipped to America. American Bridge -Fluor JV and ZPMC invited ESAB China to propose a technical solution.

ESAB China’s welding engineers worked closely with ESAB global experts to run trials and subsequently help introduce new welding procedures and consumables to China that resolved the welding issues. ESAB China supplied 12 40’ containers (130 T) of specialised welding wire called ESAB DualShield 70 Ultra Plus.

ESAB China’s new marketing director based in Shanghai, Sarah Robinson, who had a long association with the project, says: “This is just the tip of the bridge-building iceberg. This is the start of a new manufacturing industry market sector called Remote Bridge Building.” ■

## Sustainable efficiency is the key to success

## SUSTAINABILITY AND

efficiency are increasingly at the centre of industrial development around the world. Assessments of companies’ performance no longer focus purely on commercial factors, but also take into account their use of energy and raw materials.

Hannover Messe 2012, which runs from 23 to 27 April, will provide an overview of all key resource-saving industrial technologies. Thousands of innovations will be unveiled in Hannover this year. They will span all sectors of industry, from industrial automation and IT, energy and environmental technologies, industrial supply, production technologies and services through to research and development.

“The official Hannover Messe 2012 motto is ‘greentelligence’. The exhibitors at all eight flagship fairs to be featured under the umbrella of Hannover Messe will demonstrate that an intelligent mix of efficient processes, ecofriendly materials and sustainable products is critical to survival in today’s highly competitive, dynamic international markets,” said Dr. Wolfram von Fritsch, Chairman of the Managing Board at Deutsche Messe.

Electric motors offer enormous scope for boosting energy efficiency. According to estimates by the Association of German Engineers (VDI), some 300 million electric motors are currently in use around the world – a number which the Association suggests is rising by 10 per cent each year. The VDI believes that energy consumption in industrial operations can be reduced by as much as 30 per cent through the use of intelligent energy automation systems, modern energy transmission technologies and the latest electrical engineering components.

According to the VDI, the demand-side savings will come from variable-speed drives. The latest expert analyses indicate that the use of these kinds of intelligent motor control systems can potentially save some 1.7 billion kWh of electricity worldwide, the equivalent of the annual output of 290 nuclear reactors.

Given the growing importance of sustainable efficiency, this year’s Hannover Messe will also see the debut of a new flagship fair that is dedicated to this issue, IndustrialGreenTec. Here, companies will present solutions, processes, machines and energy efficiency concepts that help industrial enterprises change over to “green production systems.” Eight flagship fairs are taking place under the umbrella of Hannover Messe 2012: Industrial Automation – Energy – MobiliTec – Digital Factory – Industrial Supply – CoilTechnica – IndustrialGreenTec – Research & Technology. ■

## Welding World chooses TRMG

THE ASSOCIATION of Welding Distribution (AWD) has announced that ‘Welding World’, the Association’s official magazine, has found a new home for production, distribution and advertising sales. The AWD has reached a publishing agreement with TRMG, a consumer and industrial event programme and magazine publisher with 21 years’ experience. Based in Hatfield, Hertfordshire (UK), its titles include International Sheet Metal Review, Scuba, Formula 1 and England Rugby magazines.

“TRMG’s combination of production and marketing skills will enhance the look and feel of the magazine with a view to increasing the number of issues per annum and, in conjunction with the AWD, the circulation of the only welding industry title in the UK,” said AWD.

Adrian Hawkins, Managing Director and National Chairman of AWD Ltd, said: “Welding World is the only UK title devoted to the welding industry and this agreement gives us the opportunity to increase the circulation, frequency of publication and professional look of the final product. TRMG are proven experts in this field looking after a number of both industrial and consumer titles for various organisations and they will add the necessary ingredients to ensure that Welding World becomes better known within our industry, whilst the AWD will continue to expand and develop the readership with industry manufacturers, distributors and end users.”

AWD will be in the Welding World Village at the forthcoming MACH 2012 exhibition to be held in Birmingham, UK from 16 – 21 April. www.awd.org.uk

## ALUMINIUM RIDES HIGH FOR NAVAL DESIGNS

MODERN ALUMINIUM alloys and their ability to provide strength, durability, protection and enhanced survivability – whilst offering less weight for increased speed, manoeuvrability and enhanced fuel efficiency – makes the material suitable for modern naval designs, according to a paper by Alcoa Defense presented recently at the American Society of Naval Engineers (ASNE) show.

Aluminium has been successfully used since its commercialisation as a material for ships. Most recently, the latest ships from the US Navy, such as the Joint High-Speed Vessel (JHSV) and Littoral Combat Ship (LCS,) utilise aluminium in hull and superstructure applications. The Independence sea-frame LCS features more than one million pounds of high-grade aluminium alloys in each ship, making it one of the single largest users of aluminium in the world.

Survivability is a key factor in any military application. Alcoa’s armour alloys have been battle-tested in Iraq and Afghanistan. A copy of the paper, presented by Kyle A. Crum, Technology Manager for Alcoa Defense at the Alcoa Technical Centre, can be found at http://www.alcoa.com/navaldesigns

## German machine tool orders

IN THE fourth quarter of 2011, order bookings in the German machine tool industry fell, for the first time in two years, by a total of 4 per cent. Domestic orders were 2 per cent up on the preceding year’s figure, whereas order bookings from outside Germany fell by 7 per cent.

“December is responsible for the decrease in the year’s fourth quarter, with a minus figure for the first time in 25 months,” explained Dr.Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association).The preceding year’s equivalent month, however, reached the highest figure since order booking statistics began to be recorded in the early 1960s.

Overall demand was up by 45 per cent in 2011. Demand from German users rose by 46 per cent and from overseas customers by 45 per cent compared to the preceding year. Production output of machine tools rose by one third in 2011, the highest increase ever recorded. Capacity utilisation averaged 93.8 per cent over the year as a whole.The order backlog was at 9.3 months. ■

## Automotive eco-friendly pre-treatment

HEALTH AND environmental protection, the trend towards lightweight construction and, as a result, the increasing metal mix in car bodies and components are the main reasons why the Mercedes-Benz plant in Gaggenau has intensively examined alternatives to zinc phosphating. In February 2011, pre-treatment was changed to the OXSILAN(r) technology developed by Chemetall GmbH in Frankfurt.

In the new pressing plant at the Mercedes-Benz site in Gaggenau, which is one of the oldest car factories in the world, each day more than 1,400 spare parts such as doors, trunk lids, fenders or engine hoods for all Mercedes-Benz models are manufactured, pre-treated and painted. Formerly, the components were mainly made from galvanized steel. But aluminium is increasingly making its way into automobiles. For zinc phosphating, this increase in material mixes makes it necessary to carefully explore technical limits.

“The trend to lightweight construction will further increase in the future. Mercedes-Benz therefore needed an alternative that was free from heavy metals and compatible with multi-metals,” explained Klaus Rudo, responsible for global process release at Daimler.

Each day, more than 1,400 components such as doors, trunk lids, fenders or engine hoods of all Mercedes-Benz models are pre-treated and coated in the new pressing plant at the Gaggenau site.

The new OXSILAN technology was adopted for use in series production in Spring 2011. For Sascha Malorny, responsible for pretreatment and painting at Mercedes-Benz in Gaggenau, much has become easier with the changeover to the OXSILAN(r) technology:

“It used to be that the phosphating zone had to be acid-rinsed every ten weeks, which took a whole weekend each time.

With OXSILAN(r), cleaning of the complete facility is completed after six to eight hours.” The zinc phosphating process also used to require weekly cleaning of the filter system, which meant a complete exchange of the filter mats. Today, it is enough to rinse the filter press once a month with water since the new pre-treatment technology is virtually sludge-free.

“The OXSILAN process operates at room temperature and does not require costintensive bath heating,” said the manufacturer. “In the Gaggenau plant, it is also now possible to reduce the temperature of the cathodic electro dip (CED) bath by around two degrees and the voltage by around ten per cent. The many advantages in terms of energy consumption, wastewater treatment, bath de-sludging and disposal as well as the reduced labour costs results in high process cost savings – without compromising quality in any way. In addition, the omission of the nickel-containing zinc phosphating offers a major safety advantage for employees.” www.chemetall.com
