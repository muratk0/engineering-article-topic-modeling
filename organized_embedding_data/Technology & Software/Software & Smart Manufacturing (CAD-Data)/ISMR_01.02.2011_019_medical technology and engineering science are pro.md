# Medical technology and engineering science are progressively converging, creating innovative applications.

[TARİH: 01.02.2011 ISMR]

The Institut für Mikrotechnik Mainz GmbH (IMM) creates brain probes that measure brain currents and can input energy for the purposes of electro-desiccation. The fifth-generation probes are up to 300 millimetres long with a diameter of just 0.5 millimetres. The tip houses 30 electrodes, connected to the integrated electronics. This means that surgeons can position their movements with exceptional precision.

Metrology is one of the IMM’s specialisms. One of its latest creations was designed for the medical technology sector. The job profile: for calibrating the accuracy of CT scanners, there had hitherto been only one test piece, featuring glued-in spheres, which could be used to precisely determine the position in space within a certain framework. Together with the German Institute of Physics and Technology (PTB) in Brunswick, the IMM created a new standard tester. This is a small titanium cube with an edge length of ten millimetres, on three sides of which the IMM has incorporated ultra-precise structures.

“We want to improve this standard tester still further in terms of the structures’ precision,” says IMM. “In future, we aim to see the manufacturers and users of milling and eroding machines using this test object as well.”

Delcam has just launched a healthcare and medical division and has a range of products in the healthcare field. One area is in the design of custom medical devices using 3D modelling, CAD and multi-axis milling. Applications in non-invasive and maxillofacial surgery have been presented as well as creating CAD textures and hole patterns to assist implant bone bonding.

The shape memory metal Nitinol has increased in use and popularity since its discovery in 1959. Today, there are Nitinol-based stents, guidewires, surgical instruments, scoliosis rods, embolic protection filters and a host of other medical applications. The next few years should see exciting Nitinol medical device developments in a number of areas. In the future, new designs for stents will also involve narrower tube diameters, thinner walls and more complex designs, as well as tighter material property specifications.

“Medical technology and engineering science are progressively converging, creating innovative applications, designed to ensure improved and more efficient health care for ill people,” explains Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association). “Without massive deployment of high-tech systems, the future challenges faced by the health care system cannot be met and mastered.” ■

## MANUFACTURING

How modern-day production technology contributes towards cost-efficient manufacture of top-quality medical technology equipment will be one of the topics discussed by experts from the fields of medical and production technology on 17 March 2011 at the “Metal meets Medical – Manufacturing Potentials” conference in Karlsruhe. It is being organised by the VDW in conjunction with the Institute for Production Management, Technology and Machine Tools PTW, Darmstadt, Germany.

The event will be kicked off by Rainer Bader, Professor of Medicine at the Polyclinic of Rostock University, and also an engineering graduate. He will be reporting on the current status of research work, and outlining from a clinical viewpoint the future requirements posed by medical technology for precision and perfect surfaces. The technical papers will be complemented by an overview of the rules and regulations governing medical technology.
