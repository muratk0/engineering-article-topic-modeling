# STAM's focus at EuroBLECH was on customised slitting lines and variable section profiles, as well as laser systems for blank shaping and automatic de-cambering systems

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

STAM (Italy), is a specialist in flexible roll forming lines as well as machines and equipment for cut-to-length and slitting lines for coil and lines for flexible manufacturing systems. The company employs 130 people and has additional facilities in Spain and Brazil. It has delivered and installed more than 1000 machines and lines worldwide.

Its focus at this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover was on customised slitting lines and variable section profiles, as well as laser systems for blank shaping and automatic de-cambering systems.

ISMR sat down with STAM's sales and project manager, Dr. Ing. Andrea Barazza, to discuss the Italian specialist's latest new developments and innovations.

"The other technology we are showcasing is roll forming for variable section profiles. The technology is relatively new although we have many of these lines in regular operation with customers. We see a lot of interest in this technology. When you operate a variable section roll forming machine, you need straight blanks and straight coils so we have developed a system to reduce the camber on the strip so that the blanks are shaped properly for cutting. It can detect camber and automatically adjust the system to reduce camber so that the blank is straight and narrow. We are presenting this solution to visitors here at EuroBLECH."

"Here, at EuroBLECH, we are showing a slitting head which was part of a slitting line and a cut-to-length line. Slitting is a very promising area for us – we are investing strongly in this technology. We will be delivering slitting lines to process material of up to 16mm thickness in 2017. Our cut-to-length lines can handle material which is over 20mm thick," Barazza told ISMR.

"Our roll forming lines are extraordinarily flexible, able to change in just a few minutes from the production of one type of profile (with a certain section and dimensions) to another without the need for manual intervention, enabling their use for large, as well as small, production batches," Barazza told ISMR.

## A technology showcase

One of these applications is a flexible roll forming line for U, C, Z and Sigma profiles. It can process profiles with thicknesses of up to 4mm, and variable widths of up to 400mm. Stam has developed a complete production line which can punch, roll form, cut and stack these profiles in automatic and flexible mode.

The roll forming machine can quickly change from one profile to another, within the range indicated, with no manual tool change needed. For instance, the passage from a C- profile 100/400/100, of 4mm thickness, to a Z- profile 50/200/50, 2mm thick, can be done in approximately six minutes. The prepunching unit of the strip is automatic and flexible, and is equipped with all the punches necessary for the different products.

"The cutting process can be done either before roll forming, to avoid the need for interchangeable cutting tools for the different profiles, or after roll forming, by flying cutting systems. The traditional limit of flare of the profiles has been solved by using a set of forming tools, which remove tension, flare and torsion effects, for production of high-quality profiles across the entire thickness range," explained Barazza.

"For the construction industry, the flexible roll forming lines for U,C,Z and Sigma profiles are complete production lines that can punch, roll form, make the middle and the end 'Tab' supports, cut-to-length and stack the various profiles necessary to construct a building, in a completely automatic and flexible mode. All the adjustments are made automatically, with no operator intervention."

STAM has also developed various flexible systems for its flexible rollforming lines to punch coil as well as integrated laser cutting systems for coil. For customers who wish to automate their lines, stacking systems (up to 18m high) are offered for the automatic stacking of profiles.

## Flexible roll forming lines

In 2013, STAM reached a new technological frontier. It designed and built 'the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses of up to 7mm'. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength. The success of the project, followed by the positive testing of the line, was a rewarding way to celebrate 50 years of STAM activities.

Barazza confirmed that STAM's flexible roll forming line, for the production of U, C, Z Sigma profiles, has been very successful this year. STAM delivered two fully automatic lines this year to European customers and are delivering more lines next year.

"It is appreciated by customers because of its flexibility and the quality of the profiles produced. These lines have been installed all over the world," he told ISMR. "The construction industry is moving ahead again with steel fabrication systems. More industrial and civil buildings now employ steel frames/fabrication systems. This is a good application for our roll forming technology, which is also used by the solar industry," Barazza told ISMR.

STAM roll forming technology (machines, tooling and presses) are also used to make components for rainwater systems e.g. gutters, downpipes, connections and corners etc. Another key industry for the technology is the automotive industry, where it is used by Tier 1 suppliers to produce components such as profiles for cars and bumpers, side crash bars etc.

"Automotive suppliers seem to increasingly prefer roll formed profiles to pressed parts, because of the quality of the profiles. This is a successful industry for us," explained Barazza.

"STAM is a world leader in the supply of rollforming lines for truck beams as well as flexible lines capable of rollforming U-beams of up to 16mm."

## Looking to the future

"We are happy with results this year. For 2017, the forecast is good and we expect more growth. We export all over the world. Europe is an important market for us. We have a factory in Brazil, supplying the North American and Mexican automotive markets, which are going well. The Russian market is slow but the market in China is strong. We also supply to other regions such as North Africa, Egypt etc.," Barazza told ISMR.
