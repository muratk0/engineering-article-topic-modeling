InspecVision, based in Northern Ireland, specialises in automated 2D and 3D measurement systems for the inspection and reverse engineering of sheet-metal parts at every stage of production (parts that are flat, folded, welded or assembled). Its inspection machinery can be used to carry out one-click, single-second field of view 2D, 2.5D and 3D measurements.

The award-winning TRUMPF partner will be bringing a host of new technology innovations for quality control, improved efficiency and waste reductions to this year's EuroBLECH and FABTECH exhibitions. R&D is a key focus for the company, which is on target to make record sales in 2022.

Managing director, Dr. Jan Antonis, outlined his view of sheet-metal markets, industry requirements, trends and advances in technology to ISMR.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to InspecVision and highlight any notable successes and achievements over the past two to three years.

JA: InspecVision was founded in 2003 when my father, Mike Antonis, was managing director of FG Wilson, Northern Ireland's largest sheet-metal manufacturer, and I was completing a PhD in computer vision systems at Queen's University Belfast. Mike knew that the time required for inspection and quality control of sheet-metal parts caused a regular bottleneck in the production process. Hightech laser-cutting machines could produce parts a lot faster than they could be inspected, and he and his colleagues had become frustrated with the lack of options available to inspect sheet-metal parts. We got together to solve a practical challenge, saw a gap in the market and developed the Planar 2D, the world's fastest measurement system for 2D parts.

The product range has further developed to include the Opti-Scan 3D for full 3D inspection and the Accuity, a system suitable for both 2D and 3D inspection of small intricate parts. As a company we continue to be very focused on R&D, which includes continuously streamlining and improving our products.

Achievements in the past two to three years include being awarded the prestigious Queen's Award for Enterprise in International Trade in 2021. Other notable awards in recent years include 'Northern Ireland Winner of the Federation of Small Business High Growth Business of the Year' and a marketing award from the Chartered Institute of Marketing Ireland. We were also 'Highly Commended' in the global player category of the Northern Ireland Chamber of Commerce Awards 2021.

In addition, we have collaborated with TRUMPF to become a TRUMPF Partner company and are the only inspection system that TRUMPF recommends to its customers in the sheet-metal industry.

These achievements highlight the substantial growth of InspecVision, on a global level, over the last few years.

ISMR:What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet-metal professionals? What about the climate in 2023?

JA: The war in Ukraine has had a huge impact on energy prices, particularly in Europe. This has driven up the price of raw materials and the running costs of the production machines. When sheet-metal parts cost more to produce, it becomes even more critical to minimize the production of bad parts. Inspection is one of the best ways to do that as any quality issues can be identified early in the production process. With the urgent push to carbonneutral energy, these pressures may be around for quite some time.

One of the biggest issues currently for businesses globally is accessing experienced and skilled staff. This was a problem before the COVID-19 pandemic but is an even bigger problem now. Despite sheet-metal professionals increasing the levels of automation in their businesses, there are still many manual steps in most sheet-metal production. There can be a reluctance to take on jobs which require skilled manual operations, particularly for smaller production runs.

Our customers are looking for practical ways to increase automation and reduce the skills required to do the tasks that cannot be automated. The demand for sheet-metal components continues to grow and our customers are seeking ways to grow without having to recruit additional staff.

ISMR: How has the COVID-19 crisis affected your operations and which steps have you taken in response?

JA: Like many manufacturing companies globally, the COVID-19 crisis impacted many aspects of our operations. This ranged from a substantial reduction in output due to many of our customers putting investment projects on hold, employees being on furlough/working from home and the difficulties of installing machines around the world due to travel restrictions globally. Thankfully, our working environment is almost back to normal now although we have continued with a more flexible way of working, with some employees choosing to continue working remotely.

In terms of sales and production, we are on target for a record year. The year 2019 was also a record year for us and sales in 2022 look set to exceed pre-COVID levels. However, as for everyone, the level of inflation is a major challenge.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus for the company? Please elaborate.

JA: Our company strategy continues to focus on growth and on building the InspecVision brand. Our goal is to be the 'top-of-mind' brand for sheet-metal businesses who are seeking to automate or improve their inspection and quality control processes. Our origins are in the sheet-metal industry and our mission is to use our expertise to provide the industry with technologies which will save time, reduce costs and improve processes. In terms of markets, North America and China continue to be key markets. However, we are also very focused on growing our market share in Europe. Our partnership with TRUMPF is an important element in our growth strategy.

We have an extremely full order book so optimizing our production processes and supply chain is a key focus internally. This will also help to offset some of the price increases caused by the high inflation we are all experiencing.

As previously mentioned, we have a strong focus on R&D. Our technical focus has concentrated on developing two new technologies to bring to market. The first is an extension of our Opti-Scan 3D product range. We have developed a 3D scanner with a larger two-metre field of view and turntable, the OS2000.35. This unit has a 4K projector and a 65MP monochromatic camera. Feedback from our distributors and customers globally indicated that a 2m-wide automated 3D inspection system will meet the needs of a much larger segment of the sheet-metal industry than our current 1m-wide unit.

Our other development is quite different to our current product range. It is a guidance and verification system (GAV). Many suppliers do small production runs for their customers. Welding is one example. Even if a robotic welding system was available, the programming skill and time could not be justified for a small production run that may only be ordered a few times a year, possibly with revision changes.

We have developed a machine-learning system that can watch an operator perform a manual task in 2D/3D. As the task is performed it learns each step of the task by scanning the work areas in 3D. Each step can then be played back later to guide a new or less experienced operator. The system uses augmented reality projections to indicate the next step to take. Then, as the operator performs each step, the system verifies this step by scanning the work area to ensure that the step has been performed correctly. For example, has the bracket been attached upside down or on the wrong side? Has a screw been missed etc.?

With this product, geared towards manual operations, we are leveraging our technology expertise to create more of a productionfocused asset than a metrology tool. This represents a change in our technical focus.

ISMR: What, in your view, are important industry trends, challenges and areas of growth/opportunities? How are you responding to these?

JA: Anything which can reduce scrap, automate production or at least reduce the skill levels required in production is of critical importance. Building the profile of the sheetmetal industry and manufacturing in general as an attractive career choice is also key.

We will continue to develop technology which helps sheet-metal manufacturers in these key areas, providing them with valuable products and services. We have also been involved in initiatives with local schools and universities to raise the profile of the manufacturing industry and bring it to the attention of younger generations.

ISMR: Are there any products/technologies/ services that you have released or are releasing this year (including for EuroBLECH 2022) that you would like to discuss?

JA: Apart from the OS2000 and GAV systems, we also recently released a new option for our Planar system called AOI (Area of Interest). This option uses lights above the part to see and verify the 2D dimensions of features that only appear on the object's surface. This could be the outer diameter of a counter sink or the location, diameter and presence of a pin or insert.

As the AOI option uses 2D images without the need for 3D scanning, it is still extremely rapid.

ISMR: How are you leveraging technologies such as artificial intelligence, Big Data, AR, VR, robotics etc. for your business?

JA: Our new Guidance and Verification technology is designed to learn how to perform a welding or assembly operation by watching a human perform the task. This requires machine learning. It then uses Augmented Reality (AR), rather than Virtual Reality (VR), to guide the next operator through the learned task. Augmented reality is a much more practical approach in a shopfloor environment than virtual reality. The system projects vital and pertinent information onto the part and work area. It does not require a headset or goggles.

Augmented reality has been a fairly important part of our products for a while. Anything that can help our users to understand the inspection results saves time and mistakes.

ISMR: How have you positioned InspecVision with respect to Industry-4.0?

JA: Our machines are all designed with Industry 4.0 connectivity in mind. One of the key advantages of our systems is that we can connect remotely and support a customer anywhere in the world.

Inspection is an often overlooked, but necessary, part of automation. We see our solutions as playing a vital role in delivering real-time information to our customers and helping them to develop a connected factory.

JA: We have had a busy year to date with exhibitions and, by working closely with our partners, we were able to exhibit at a wide range of trade fairs throughout 2022 including SteelFab in the United Arab Emirates; MACH in the UK; FABTECH Mexico; FIMAQH in Argentina; INTECH at TRUMPF Germany; Lamiera in Italy; Manufacturing Solutions Ireland; Australia Manufacturing Week and BIEMH in Spain.

We're really excited to be back at EuroBLECH in Germany in October, followed by FABTECH in the USA in November to finish off the year. EuroBLECH is the biggest event in our calendar and we're very much looking forward to that week in Hanover, meeting with customers and learning more about the most important issues and trends for the sheetmetal industry.

We will start 2023 at the SteelFab exhibition (Sharjah, UAE) in January.
