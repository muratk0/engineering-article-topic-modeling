# DESIGN FOR PRECISION

We highlight the factors to consider when choosing CAD/ CAM software to meet your needs, now and in the future

ISMR SAYS:

“A CAD/CAM software package offers the functionality of both CAD (computer aided design) and CAM (computer aided manufacturing) software."

Computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing (CAD/CAM) software is used to design and manufacture prototypes, finished products and production runs of products. CAD/CAM applications are used to both design a product and program manufacturing processes. A CAD/CAM software package offers the functionality of both CAD (computer aided design) and CAM (computer aided manufacturing) software. Consequently, rather than using two different software platforms to create and develop a design, an operator uses the same software.

Design processes for CAD and CAM are unique. Although they are part of the same manufacturing workflow, their process is quite different from each other. CAD design is used, for example, by architects, construction managers and engineers and has replaced manual drafting. It helps users to create designs in either 2D or 3D to visualise construction, and enables the development, modification and optimisation of the design process. 3D CAD also enables more efficient sheet-metal processing by ensuring rapid and reliable workflow that moves quickly from the design process into fabrication.

CAM software uses the models and assemblies created in CAD software to generate toolpaths that drive machine tools to turn designs into physical parts.

"CAD software is used to produce, analyse and alter two- or three-dimensional designs and models in a digital environment instead of employing traditional drafting methods. Computer-aided design software is frequently used by architects, engineers, commercial and industrial designers, and artists to create multi-dimensional drawings and models. It is commonly utilised due to the numerous advantages it has over manual drawing. CAD software is more efficient, provides more precise designs, interacts seamlessly with digital workflows and allows users to build a searchable and widely available library of designs and design knowledge," explained analyst, Emergen Research.

## CAD/CAM prototyping

Experienced metal fabrication companies will utilise CAD and CAM prototyping to create accurate prototypes with minimal waste.

"CAD prototyping accurately and precisely models a product before the metal fabrication can take place. Using CAD, designers can create vector-based drawings. These drawings display the products' size, shape and texture. Since CAD is done on a computer, the modifications are endless. You don't have to waste money and materials in prototypes. CAD files can also be multiplied. This ensures that you have backups of any prototype in case you want to go back to a prior version. The files can be shared with employees and the customer to ensure the product is modelled correctly before a single product is made," explained manufacturer, Meta Fab Inc.

"Once the model is created with CAD, CAM prototyping controls the machinery to achieve an accurate model. CAM prototyping is essential to bring the product to life successfully. The systems are automated and include a wide range of processes such as cutting; grinding; stamping; milling and routing," it added.

## Choosing CAD/ CAM software

"Many CAD/CAM software packages are available online, so choosing the right one can be an uphill task. Having discussed the best software for your CAD and CAM needs, here are factors to consider when choosing one," suggested manufacturer, Rapid Direct, which has listed various factors below.

## Cost

"Cost is often the limiting factor when choosing CAD/CAM software. The reason is that most CAD packages are very expensive. However, before making a financial commitment to software, ensure that you carry out proper research. The costly ones are not always the best, so look out for the features you need," advised Rapid Direct.

"It is also important to note that most software comes in versions, and later versions always perform better than earlier ones. Keep this in mind when buying software at a lifetime cost or when making a subscription-based payment," it added.

## Support for CNC techniques

Many CNC techniques are available (milling, laser, plasma etc.). However, not all software supports them. Before choosing software, you should know the CNC techniques that you need for manufacturing and only use software that supports them.

## Rich features

The number of features on software often determines the quality of work it would do. Always look out for software with rich functionality, as it considerably reduces the workload while improving the quality of work.

## Support for file formats

"There are a lot of file types (STEP; STL; IGES; DXF; X3D; Parasolid etc.) used for computerised numerical control processes. The file type usually depends upon the software used in generating your CAD file. It might be problematic if your software does not accept a wide range of files, both importable and exportable," cautioned Rapid Direct.

## Collaboration with other software

"Another factor that you should consider when choosing software is its ability to work with other software. This would help to eliminate the time spent on preparing and moving files for manufacture between systems. Always choose software that works and collaborates seamlessly with others," concluded Rapid Direct.

## Future-proofing your decision

Software developer Autodesk also recommends factoring future growth plans into any software purchasing decisions. Two common mistakes of CAM purchase decisions, it said, are buying too little software or too much..... "If you buy too little, you are typically later forced to upgrade to a different package. If you buy too much, you may overpay and not use most of the features in a package. One common way that someone may buy too much is purchasing five-axis simultaneous software when it turns out that five-axis positional is faster, easier and less expensive and cuts the parts just as well," it explained.

“The strength of a CAM product can be no greater than the strength of the developer. Users need to examine the developer and consider the following issues......

How many developers and support staff are there for the product?

■Does the developer have the resources to continue development?

■How often are updates made available?

■Does the developer have staff that understand sheet-metal machining or have machining experience?

“The quick tip here to purchase from a company that is knowledgeable in the product and will offer excellent support; one that has shown a long-term commitment to manufacturing and helping their customers," it concluded.

It also recommends performing a trial evaluation of the system and actually cutting metal on the machines in the shop.

When trialling CAM software, it told ISMR, take the following into consideration:

1. Try the software on the largest parts you may work on, to see how well the database handles larger data sets.

2. Try the software on the smallest and finest parts you may work on, and check the surface finish and quality.

3. If there were any parts from the past that were particularly difficult to program, try some of those.

4. Check if there are post processors already available.
