# Fabricator increases data center component consistency and throughput with fiber laser

SITUATION

Goshen Engineering didn’t begin as a fabrication company. It began as an engineering-driven organization focused on automation and system integration. The business expanded into engineered-to-order power distribution equipment, particularly for the growing data center market.

Data center infrastructure demands a level of precision and reliability that leaves little room for variability. Every fabricated component, whether part of a rack system, enclosure, or cooling structure, must integrate seamlessly into a larger system. Even small inconsistencies can create downstream challenges in assembly, airflow management, or long-term performance.

When fabrication processes are stable and repeatable, assembly becomes faster and more efficient, errors and rework are significantly reduced, project timelines become more reliable, and scaling production becomes a controlled process rather than a reactive one. And in a market where timelines are tight and expectations are high, this level of consistency becomes a competitive advantage.

Like many manufacturers entering high-growth sectors, Goshen faced a familiar set of constraints that began to compound over time:

Old equipment that limited throughput and required significant manual input

Long lead times tied to outsourced components

Increasing order volumes with greater variation in part design

Pressure to scale production without proportionally increasing labor

The company’s waterjet system, while effective for earlier stages of growth, became a bottleneck as demand increased. It required extensive operator involvement and couldn’t support the pace or volume required for Goshen’s evolving business model.

And relying on external suppliers for enclosures introduced delays, reduced flexibility, and limited the company’s ability to compete.

Goshen’s shift toward modular switchboards and data center applications demanded faster turnaround and greater consistency, two things the existing process struggled to deliver. To move forward, the company needed more than incremental improvement; it needed ownership of the process. That decision to bring enclosure fabrication in-house became a turning point.

RESOLUTION

Goshen Engineering acquired a Salvagnini fiber laser with loading and sorting automation, marking a fundamental change in how the company approached production.

What once required 50 to 60 hours per week on a waterjet could now be completed in just one to two days—a dramatic increase in throughput that immediately relieved pressure on the shop floor.

But speed alone wasn’t the full story. The real transformation came from the ability to scale output without scaling complexity:

Production capacity increased to support hundreds of enclosures over defined project timelines.

A compact fabrication team of 10 individuals now supports 40 to 50 assembly personnel.

Automation enabled extended production hours, including unattended or lights-out operations.

This shift allowed Goshen to meet growing demand while maintaining control over labor, cost, and quality—something that would have been difficult to achieve through manual expansion alone.

As the company continued to grow, it didn’t stop at increasing output but rethought how parts moved through the entire process. Instead of adapting production to fit existing designs, Goshen worked closely with Salvagnini’s team to begin designing products based on the capabilities of the equipment. Enclosures were engineered to be fully compatible with panel bending from the start. Programming became more streamlined, reducing setup time and operator dependency.

This shift toward flow-based thinking reduced interruptions, minimized rework, and created a more predictable production environment. It also reinforced a key advantage: the ability to handle complexity without introducing chaos.
