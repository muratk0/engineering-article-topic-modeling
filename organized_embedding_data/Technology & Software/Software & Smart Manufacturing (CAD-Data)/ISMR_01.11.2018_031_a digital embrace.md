# A DIGITAL EMBRACE

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

## KASTO is gaining strong recognition for its Industry 4.0 initiatives and is using new technologies to cut costs and time for its customers.

Sönke Krebber, Member of KASTO's Executive Board (R&D) at EuroBLECH 2018

KASTO showed its latest innovations and solutions from the world of sawing and storage technology at the EuroBLECH 2018 exhibition in Hanover this October. The new KASTOmiwin bandsaw series, more new models for the KASTOmicut workshop saw family and upgraded saws in the KASTOtec series were shown to ISMR on the booth. We also saw universal sawing machines in the KASTOwin family, the UNITOWER compact tower storage system with energy recovery and the KASTOspeed circular saw with robot connection.

ISMR SAYS:

“KASTO is using Augmented Reality and AI in its service solutions”

KASTO has embraced Industry 4.0 and new digital technologies and has received awards for its innovative approach to design and energy efficiency. Sönke Krebber, Member of KASTO's Executive Board (Research and Development) told ISMR more..

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes, achievements or awards?

SK: I am delighted to confirm that we won an award in the Industry 4.0 field. The Federal State of Baden-Württemberg in Germany awarded KASTO an Industry 4.0 award because of the very high integration of its storage systems with its sawing systems as well as with the sorting robots, deburring and centre boring tools that we offer. We provide standardised interfaces.

We also received another award from this same German state for our energy efficiency. Our innovative energy recovery and storage concept, using the UNITOWER storage system as an example, converts surplus kinetic energy into electricity which is stored temporarily for later use as needed. An intelligent controller charges and discharges the energy storage unit, depending upon the process currently running. In this way, users can reduce the connected load of the SRM operating gantry crane by an additional 50 per cent, and they can use smaller transformer stations in their planning. This reduces operating and investment costs. Existing KASTO storage systems can also be retrofitted with energy recovery and storage.

I can also confirm that we have been nominated for an MM award in the sawing category for our KASTOwin amc saw for additively manufactured parts. The part is printed on a metal base layer and has to be cut which can release small unhealthy particles of powder when sawing. Our machine is completely covered so that operators are not affected by particles and parts do not fall onto each other when we are sawing upside down.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

SK: The business climate has been very good this year – we are very active and very busy; so are our customers. A lot of our customers are facing the challenge of having to output more right now. To achieve this, they need solutions that can scale, according to their business needs. We are consequently seeing a lot of automation as automated systems can work several shifts and this scales well to customers' business needs.

We expect 2019 to be a busy year as our order book is filling up. We see strong opportunities ahead and do not anticipate a sharp drop in sales. We are very active in the European and U.S. markets. Most of our customers are in Europe but the U.S. market is growing right now. We recently opened a subsidiary in China and, a few years ago, one in Singapore. These markets are developing well and China needs more automation right now.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

SK: We are trying to standardise more of our products to get better scale for our customers. Delivery times are going up (as people are very busy) so more standardisation will help to cut our delivery times. This is also a trend in our customers' industries because they want to combine different solutions from other companies (such as drilling machines), so standardised interfaces will enable them to do this.

We are also focusing on software. Our KASTO warehouse management system, KASTOlogic, has evolved a lot over the last few years and we can now sell KASTOlogic without a KASTO crane system because we are able to organise manual storage areas for inventory. This means that we can constantly ensure strong material flow through the software interfaces (whether manual or automated material storage) and we can also organise material flow from sawing systems and bar handling systems, paint systems etc. So, we are expanding in the field of Industry 4.0.

With KASTO VisualAssistance, we are also offering an innovative solution for maintaining machines and systems remotely. The heart of the system is an interactive app for tablets, smartphones or smart glasses. Customers can use it to connect to service staff using video and audio streams. Users and engineers share the same field of view in real-time. This immensely simplifies mutual understanding and helps to quickly identify individual system components and possible faults. The app also enables KASTO experts to provide visual assistance and to superimpose markings, for example, on the live video.

The customer receives all necessary information directly on his display using Augmented Reality while he is carrying out maintenance or repair work on the saw or storage system on-site. If he is using smart glasses, his hands are also free, which also makes his job easier. KASTO service engineers have a virtual presence on-site and guide staff accordingly. Extensive training and expensive site visits are therefore superfluous. At the same time, users benefit from minimal downtimes as well as high availability and productivity of their machines and systems.

ISMR: Are there any new initiatives (domestic or international) that you would like to discuss or highlight?

SK: To follow the service example, we have also worked on another way of saving time and call-outs. Our robotics are 100% KASTO programmed at our facility in Germany. The typical way of programming a robot is to do a slow speed teach-in, instructing the robot, and then turning up the speed. As we do not know what the customer is sawing with his saw, we can't really do the teach-in as it means that a KASTO technician has to come out each time that a customer changes his methods. So, we now use an online programming algorithm which, for every cut case, determines which gripper to use for the robot. It calculates the movement of the robot online and thus saves a lot of time, call-outs etc.

I am delighted to say that we also held a very successful Open House a few months ago, with a fantastic reaction from visitors to our systems. We gave numerous presentations, as did our customers.
