Machine shop improves capacity, delivery performance with scheduling program

Situation

Using milling, turning, and grinding processes, Denmark-based Rynkeby Machine Shop produces large and small workpieces for customers in the brake and pump manufacturing, co-generation plant, agriculture, and wind energy industries. The company makes such products as transmissions, brake components, bar machined parts, pistons, screw compressor houses, and fast spindles from aluminum, cast iron, and steel.

The shop was using a manual production planning system—paper and scissors, primarily—that wasn’t meeting its needs. When an order came in by email, staff would prepare the order for production in a business management system. Then they put the calculated time for the order and all operations on pieces of carboard, cut them out, and placed them on a Gantt chart. This method took a long time, and when changes were needed, they had to move all the cardboard pieces that belonged to that order to new positions. Not only was this process time-consuming, but the company had a hard time providing customers with valid order confirmations.

The program shows the order’s progress in terms of production start and number of manufactured items.

Resolution

The shop decided to invest in the Rob-Ex Scheduler program, and it’s brought big changes. Now, staff members enter and process the order in the business management system, and the scheduler program takes into account the expected delivery date and schedules the order so that it does not delay other existing orders. The shop can see the schedule and send an order confirmation to the customer.

As the order moves through production, operators use screens on the shop floor to report how much work they have done. The program shows the order’s progress in terms of production start and number of manufactured items. The order finally ends up in the shipping department, where it is packed and given a delivery slip, and the program shows it as finished.

At this point, the scheduling program is updated via import from the business management system, and the production schedule and status on all orders are updated.

With this system, orders are scheduled with minimal waiting time so the machine shop can get more through production by optimizing the utilization of already-existing capacity, eliminating the need to add machines or employees.

"We have improved our ability to deliver because now we have a powerful tool for managing the production planning," said Rynkeby’s Hans-Christian Schmidt. "We can provide a valid delivery date, and if everything does not go as expected, we can inform the customer in due time and thus still be a reliable supplier. At the same time, there has been more time for follow-up on the shop floor because the scheduling in Rob-Ex saves time compared to the cardboard strips system."

Flexible cobots help metal fabricator boost productivity

Situation

More than a decade ago, Orrville, Ohio-based Last Arrow Mfg. started out as a contract manufacturer for welding projects with just seven employees. Today, the company specializes in metal fabrication processes including laser cutting, forming, and welding, employing more than 85 people.

Like many small shops, Last Arrow’s production rates are typically low-volume/high-mix runs with various materials that require constant part changeover, so flexibility is key. A combination of factors had the company’s President Matt Bowling searching for ways to continue to grow the business, meet customer needs, and improve production planning to keep his skilled workers focused on projects that required their expertise.

Bowling decided automation was the answer but felt there wasn’t a good automation option for small shops. He believed standard robots were designed for large manufacturers, and, therefore, were too expensive. Hard automation just didn’t have the flexibility he needed, so he decided to experiment with two different robot cells. However, they were not a good fit for a wide product mix.

"We needed a cost-effective solution that could handle low- or high-volume part runs; a big robot wasn’t going to give us that type of flexibility," he said.

Resolution

Then, at a meeting with The Lincoln Electric Co., someone showed him the FANUC CRX cobot. Bowling was impressed by the app-based programming that could be performed on a tablet. "I immediately said, ‘I want two of them.’

"The cobots were delivered, set up, and running right after lunch," added Bowling. "We literally had an hour of assembly and about a half day of training, and we were running two different part numbers that first day."

As a business owner, Bowling understands the risks and challenges of getting projects up and running, and the initial startup costs for automation equipment and employee training. He says the cobots allow the company to balance a heavier workload with increasing overhead costs while still retaining skilled workers. "I think of the cobot as a long-term strategy to increase our productivity," he continued. "[It] is compact and can work in any type of space. It’s also flexible and can be easily moved around to take on various tasks. Now we have the cobots handling the simple and more repetitive work, which frees up our skilled welders to do the more challenging jobs that require their high level of expertise. And our team sees the benefits firsthand because we’re able to get more product out the door."

Since implementing the cobots, the company has reduced the time needed to produce a weldment from two hours to just 17 minutes—an 85% improvement. On another project, the company was able to go from delivering 12 finished products a week to 20 in a very short time—a 67% increase in production. On yet another job, the company used a cobot for a tedious weld that was estimated to take a manual welder nine minutes per part. Now it’s running 10 parts on a fixture in just five minutes and 20 seconds—a 94% savings of time per part.

The cobots also have made it easier to schedule the percentages of a job that will be welded robotically or manually. And better scheduling is helping Last Arrow exceed its production goals, achieve a quick ROI, and take on new business. Most importantly, the automation has helped the company use its employees to their full potential, which makes them feel empowered to accomplish higher-level tasks. Today, less than one year after getting its first two cobots, the company has 12 cobots that process nearly 100 different part types. Bowling sees future growth opportunities and hopes to have 20 to 25 more cobots for welding and other tasks, such as metal finishing and machine tending.
