Software cuts quote setup time with AI-supported workflow

Paperless Parts has launched its new AI-supported quote setup workflow, designed to streamline the tasks associated with RFQ intake and prep work.

For most shops, the process of preparing to quote involves receiving the RFQ via email or from a supplier portal, logging the RFQ in a project-tracking spreadsheet, downloading files to the estimator’s hard drive, organizing them into line items on a shared drive, printing out files for markup and review, and then building out the bill of materials in spreadsheets or ERPs.

With the quote setup workflow tools, users can forward an RFQ from their email inbox to Paperless Parts to start a quote with all quote files attached. The software uses AI to review RFQ emails and associated files for part information, build line items, and auto-assign files to line items. There is a designated area for storing and organizing all files related to a quote, such as emails, part files, and vendor quotes.

Gas-reduction technology for fiber laser helps improve cut quality in mild steel

A patented gas- and burr-reduction technology is now available for the Mitsubishi GX-F Advanced series of AI-enabled fiber lasers to help improve cut quality while reducing gas consumption when cutting mild steel.

Mitsubishi Laser’s proprietary Agr-Mix nozzle technology does not require external blending tanks or high-pressure oxygen. Low-pressure air combined with nitrogen within the nozzle creates a blended gas that can be adjusted easily based on material type.

To develop the nozzle, the manufacturer built on its no-contact assist gas reduction nozzle technology, which reduces nitrogen-assist gas consumption by up to 75%. According to the company, the new nozzle technology can reduce it even further—up to 50% more.

Nitrogen is funneled to the center of the nozzle in an air shroud, while a separate chamber funnels some of that air into the nitrogen stream. This produces a mix gas that is made up of about 95% nitrogen and 5% oxygen. The wider kerf that the mix gas provides makes it easier to remove parts, especially thick ones, from skeletons.

Real-time machine status software updated

Cincinnati Incorporated has announced updates to its CIber-Dash software, which provides real-time status of the manufacturer’s machines anywhere from any device.

A newly designed dashboard and detailed log files provide users information for analyzing and optimizing their assets. They can schedule when, who, and how frequently they want to receive raw machine data or prebuilt reporting. Users can view up to six additional days of machine activity with the Expanded Time Frames feature.

Digital application simplifies weld traceability

ESAB has introduced InduSuite WeldCloud Assembly, a cloudbased digital application that provides welding professionals with a new level of production traceability. Key functions include the ability to record weld session data directly from a welding power source and immediately compare the weld to the weld procedure specification. If a weld is outside the WPS, the system can send an alert so that users can repair the weld right away and begin identifying the root cause of problems.

The app offers supervisors and managers a global dashboard that provides insight into four areas of project management: current production state of a project, percentage of welds completed, repair rate, and welds waiting. The app complements the current products in the InduSuite platform, including WeldCloud Notes and WeldCloud Productivity.

Instead of manually recording welding data, the app digitizes the process for speed and accuracy. With the universal connector or WeldCloud-enabled power source, welding data for nearly any brand of welding machine can be recorded and analyzed.

The app also identifies production bottlenecks by tracking weld stages and allows customers to track all projects with each subcontractor; print weld reports with NDT requirements; and get statistical measurements of assemblies, parts, welds, repair rates, audit logs, and full counts of all types of documents.

Hoists made for heavy-duty service center, steel fabrication applications

Engineered Material Handling has introduced its line of EM model HF engineered hoists. Designed for heavy-service, Class D applications such as steel service centers and heavy steel fabrication, the hoists offer traditional features such as double reeving (8/2 and 12/2) for true vertical lift and half-depth drum grooves to eliminate rope guides.

The hoists allow easy access to upper sheaves for simplified maintenance. They offer standard closed-loop, flux-vector VF hoist control, designed with Class F motor insulation. According to the company, users can expect 40,000 hours of B-10 life for hoist gearboxes and motors.

The motors and gearboxes have standard components so that spare parts can be obtained easily when needed for maintenance.

Pallet system conveyor can be customized with accessory modules

Dorner has introduced the Dual- Move pallet system conveyor. It uses twin-strand timing belts to place, orient, and transfer pallets to workstations for robotic or human manipulation.

The fully configurable conveyor has seven different pallet lengths and widths. It can carry a load of up to 100 lbs. per pallet, making it suitable for small to medium-sized appliances and electronics that require assembly and packaging processes to be completed.

Both regular and heavy load corners allow for seamless navigation of pallets, the company states. Available accessory modules include lift and transfer, lift and locate, and lift and rotate.

Features include an extruded aluminum frame and a T10 timing belt that allows for increased load capacity and strength. The conveyor can be equipped with automation sensors and modules to meet product requirements.

Backing and adhesive technologies help maximize ceramic abrasive life

Walter Surface Technologies has launched three new technologies designed to maximize the life of its ceramic abrasives.

SMART RESIN technology, found in bonded abrasives such as the ZIP XX and the XCAVATOR XX, works to expose fresh ceramic grains when needed in an application to achieve a productive balance between sharpness and life.

V-STRONG backing technology, found in the company’s ceramic sanding discs such as the XTRACUT and the COOLCUT XX, offers a strong cloth with 14% more vulcanized fiber than competitors, the manufacturer states. This helps improve wheel durability and toughness for longer-lasting sanding discs and fewer wheel changeovers.

MAX LIFE cold-curing glue helps ensure fresh abrasives on products such as the ENDURO-FLEX Turbo. Baking is not needed to bond the flaps to the backing, which prevents the discs from microcracking and drying out and can extend disc life by up to 25%, the company states.
