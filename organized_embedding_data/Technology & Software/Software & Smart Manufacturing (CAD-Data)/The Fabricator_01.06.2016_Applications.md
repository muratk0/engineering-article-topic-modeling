Software helps air cleaning product-maker streamline fabrication programming

SITUATION

Air Quality Eng. (AQE) manufactures a variety of air cleaning products at its Minneapolis facility—from industrial units for coolant mist or welding fumes to commercial kitchen-exhaust systems and tobacco-smoke and e-cig filtration systems.

The OEM employs 48 people at the plant, which houses two punch presses, a laser cutter, a turret punch press, and four press brakes. After acquiring the laser in 2014, the company reviewed the functionality of its CAM software, used to program the new laser and the existing punching machines.

RESOLUTION

AQE implemented Radan from Vero Software for the laser, and upon purchasing a new press brake in early 2016, it installed Radbend from Vero Software as well. Designed for the offline programming of press brakes and fully integrated with Radan, Radbend offers CAD packages SOLIDWORKS

®

and Autodesk

®

Inventor

®

and delivers 3-D simulation of the bending process.

With the use of either 2-D or 3-D geometry, the software programs press brakes to perform operations automatically that are traditionally performed manually in sheet metal manufacturing.

The offline programming helps maximize machine time; improve production repeatability of production; and eliminate simple, repetitive tasks. The automatic collision detection functions protect machines and tooling.

"Since Radbend and the addition of the new press brake, the idea is to have all of our parts programmed offline and have minimal adjustments that need to be made," said Jon Borgen, production manager.

Parts at AQE typically are designed in Inventor and imported into Radan. "There are a fair amount of parts created in Inventor, so we have the benefit of the 3-D model, but some of them are those old DXF files." Now the company is creating Radan and Radbend programs from the flat DXFs.

Jumping between the two programs can be done with the click of a button. "We’re able to add the bend lines in Radan, form it up, jump to Radbend, and program the press brake," Borgen said.

The press brake software examines the parts to be programmed and automatically processes a bending operation that takes into account factors such as part geometry and best machinery practices.

The company also maximizes nesting capabilities within Radan. Used by the laser operator to build nests, the nesting engine helps reduce error while saving time and materials. To build nests, the software takes into account the accurate shape and material thicknesses of all the components within a batch. It automatically sorts and separates the parts to build nests from full sheets, as well as from off-cuts and remnants.

Fan manufacturer works around overhead crane to provide facilitywide airflow to metal building-maker

SITUATION

BlueScope Buildings is a supplier of pre-engineered metal buildings, producing components in 18 manufacturing plants on four continents. At its plant in Laurinburg, N.C., the company fabricates long-bay trusses and other structural components from hot-rolled steel.

In summer workers at the plant coped with stifling heat, but the sheer size of the 280,000-sq.-ft. facility made air-conditioning too costly to consider, and small floor fans didn’t provide nearly enough airflow to keep all the workers cool.

The company wanted to try high-volume, low-speed fans, but the building’s overhead bridge crane left a gap of only about 6 ft. between the ceiling and the top of the crane.

The company also faced the challenge of metal halide light fixtures in the facility, which used twice as much energy as the most efficient modern lights and burned out quickly. The firm wanted to improve both comfort and visibility.

RESOLUTION

BlueScope enlisted the help of Big Ass Solutions, the parent company of Big Ass Fans and Big Ass Light, to provide facilitywide comfort and solve the lighting problem too.

The fan manufacturer was able to maneuver around the bridge crane to install four 18-ft. Powerfoil X2.0 fans for air circulation, eliminating the need for the ineffective floor fans. The fans were mounted directly by the yoke to the bar joists, saving about a foot of space and allowing room for the crane to pass underneath.

"Employees and visitors say [the fans] make a huge difference," said David Volk, plant manager. "They give people the feeling it’s much cooler during the long summer months."

The company also installed LED fixtures in three buildings at the site. Designed to be maintenance-free, the fixtures have a rated life of up to 150,000 hours.
