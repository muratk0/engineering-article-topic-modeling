# The umati team updates us on the status of its new machine tool interface standard for the international machine tool industry.

[TARİH: 01.09.2019 ISMR]

## ABM: Why did companies and the VDW initiate umati?

Görisch: Following a workshop in the VDW themed around the issues of Industry 4.0, held in early 2017, it clearly emerged that things were definitely moving in terms of standardisation. After some market research, however, it also became clear that, for the machine tool sector, none of the proposals involved was usable. On occasion, standards had already been agreed between machine tool manufacturers and customers from the automotive industry, but only on a bilateral basis. This meant that very extensive resources in companies were channelled into developing and maintaining the various customer implementations concerned. The intention is for the umati global interface to replace these, thus also creating capacities for developing new functions with customer benefits.

## ABM: Why has OPC UA been chosen as the communication standard?

Görisch: This issue was thoroughly examined during the initial months of the project in 2017. In the past two to three years, OPC UA has been experiencing a boom and is tacitly acknowledged as the standard in industrial communication. OPC UA utilises internet technologies and protocols. Basically, it specifies how communication is handled. At the same time, sectoral-specific expertise enables users to define, in what are called Companion specifications, the details of what is communicated. This means that the specifications provide a kind of sectoral-specific dictionary.

## ABM: Why is standardisation not being done by other organisations, such as ISO or IEC?

Görisch: OPC UA, as a fundamental specification, is already an IEC Standard (IEC-62541), meaning a standard published by the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC). The Companion specifications are at present still so development-intensive, and are so urgently required, that traditional standardisation procedures are not fit for purpose here. As soon as the Companion specifications have stabilised, the long-term aim is to adopt them in IEC/ISO Standards.

The VDW is already actively involved in the preparatory work in ISO/TC 184 – a body at the ISO (International Standardisation Organisation) that draws up standards in Automation Systems and Integration. In the shape of OPC UA, the basic implementations with the corresponding tools are already available, whereas in a normal standardisation process lasting three to five years you may still not have any implementations, with the corresponding tools, at the end of that period.

## ABM: How will the umati showcase at EMO Hannover function?

Görisch: The umati showcase at EMO Hannover 2019 will demonstrate that machines from different manufacturers, featuring umati, can be securely, seamlessly and effortlessly connected to the customer’s IT systems. For this purpose, we have published a simplified version of the umati Companion specification’s draft, with the aim of reducing the amount of implementation work by the individual participants involved.

The machines are connected to an aggregation server, which is typically installed on the shop-floor level. On this server, the data from all machines are grouped together and made available to an OPC UA client in the destination application. Since we, as the VDW, cannot build a demonstration showcase of this kind at EMO Hannover all by ourselves, we are being assisted by T-Systems. At the same time, many clients (applications) will link up to this server, a special feature, since most participants also possess a digital product for utilising the data. In addition, we have brought on board relevant added-value service providers, such as Adamos or Symmedia, to participate.

## ABM: How extensive is the showcase’s data record?

Görisch: For the demonstration scenario, we took the draft version of the Companion specification, and defined a typical machine-tool. This reduces the implementation work involved for the manufacturers concerned, and the scenario contains everything that has already been defined in the standardisation work. The data enable most of the ten usecases specified for the first version to be focused on the machine chosen for the showcase.

## ABM: When is the standard scheduled for completion and publication?

Görisch: If it were up to me, before the end of this year. However, we still have a lot of consultation work ahead of us. After submitting the release candidate to the OPC Foundation, we have deadlines to comply with for comments and objections. So, a realistic date for publication is early 2020. This doesn't mean that initial implementations can't already be deployed. However, modifications can and must be made.

## ABM: When can actual products be anticipated?

Görisch: I am confident that machine tool manufacturers at EMO Hannover will be discussing initial specific pilot implementations with their development customers. The delivery times involved, however, will indubitably extend into next year. At the same time, the requisite testing and certification preconditions have to be put in place by the umati project team – with a view to dependable quality. And finally, the umati product for machine tools and software products has to be given its final description. Against this background, I would assume we'll be seeing the first products next year.

## ABM: How are your companies contributing to the umati showcase at EMO Hannover?

Zapf: Heller will be providing a total of four data suppliers, featuring the EMO umati data model: one five-axis machine on the Heller stand, two training machines (of which one will be exhibited on the VDW’s stand) and another five-axis machine that is installed at Heller’s production facility in Nürtingen. These machines will provide the data for the showcase. In addition, Heller is a member of the VDW’s Core Group for designing and trialling the umati interface and for liaising with Siemens.

Wohlfeld: TRUMPF will be connecting individual machines to the data hub as a showcase for the fair. We are, for instance, providing the VDW with a connected marking laser. Besides the machines in the showcase, we are also supporting the VDW's umatithemed events in our role as a member of the VDW's core group and the modelling group for the Joint Working Group. It was important to the umati group to opt for a technology that offers maximised benefits for the new interface. Thanks to semantic self-description of the data in the information models, the data are not only structured, but provided with meaning, and therefore significantly upgraded. OPC UA is the standard chosen for communication in the framework architecture for Industry 4.0 (RAMI4.0).

## ABM: What is the status of the standardisation work for your focus?

Zapf: The standardisation work is very elaborate, complex and time-consuming. Feedback has to be obtained from different companies. Basically, what’s needed is abundant willingness to compromise on the part of everyone involved. An up-to-the-future data record is being developed, which takes time and experience. From Heller's viewpoint, the current modelling status covers about 90- per cent of our requirements.

Bernd Zapf is responsible for Development New Business & Technology at Gebr. Heller Maschinenfabrik GmbH in Nürtingen. Photo: Heller

Wohlfeld: TRUMPF has had, for several years now, its own in-house standard in terms of OPC UA. We see umati as the next logical step on the path leading to the crossmanufacturer smart factory. We’re working hard on this. Approval of the companion specification by OPC Foundation Products is the next major objective. We should then also be able to put products on the market. Among the vendors, the standard will be successively disseminated. The tempo here essentially depends upon how quickly they integrate the standard in their products.

## ABM: Which specific pilot projects can be demonstrated at EMO Hannover?

Zapf: Heller will be demonstrating at EMO Hannover, with connected machines, the entire EMO umati data record. In our estimation, this already covers about 40 percent of the final data record. We supply this data record with MDA/PDA signals from our present MDA/PDA interface and, in addition, have more signals available which we have so far not been able to deploy. These will be contributed by the umati interface. We will therefore, in the future, be creating a link from the present MDA/PDA interface on our machines to new and future requirements. This can, for example, be the acquisition of machine status conditions.

Wohlfeld: At EMO Hannover, we shall be exhibiting a pilot, as part of the umati showcase, with TRUMPF machine apps which, among other things, display the machine status.
