# FACE HMI interface wins 2020 German Design Award

[TARİH: 01.03.2020 ISMR]

IN RECENT YEARS, the world of industrial automation has seen a small yet great revolution: the urge to place humans at the centre of technological development.

Various design and development methods and practices, typical of other sectors, have been introduced into the industrial field. The aim is to facilitate and guide innovation processes, which see technology as a tool for generating added value in the relationship between people and the products and services that they use every day. Think user-centred design or human-centred design. Contrary to other innovation processes, these methods place recipients and their needs at the centre of the design and develop their paths in reverse order: by mapping the users' interaction with the products, they identify any weaknesses to be eliminated as well as all possible strengths to be conserved. This is why innovative machine tool manufacturers have begun to tackle renewal projects.

Salvagnini, a specialist in the design and production of sheet metal processing machinery and systems, has been renewing the software accompanying its products for several years now. It has done this in partnership with NiEW, a strategic consulting firm. In early 2017, Salvagnini and NiEW began to revise the machine tool control system. With a lean, cooperative and measurable approach, they were able to build a new FACE human-machine interface, adopted by the entire range of Salvagnini Group's products from September 2019. FACE has now won prestigious design awards including the 2019 Red Dot Award and now the 2020 German Design Award.

"Precisely because of its ability to enhance the characteristic elements of production technology, in the contemporary industrial world the human-machine interface plays a significant role in the commercial success of a machine tool. But it is also one of the main points of contact between the user and the manufacturer of a technology and can consolidate the perceived value of the product," commented Salvagnini.

Tecno 3FP in Cerea (Verona, Italy) has been using the FACE HMI technology.

"Over the years I have used many different brands, models and versions of bending presses and their related software: believe me when I say that, in terms of software and interface, simple and intuitive use is a great help. FACE makes everything immediately clear and available. Other interfaces are completely different, you have to stop what you're doing and go and find what you need to complete the bending in different places, sub-menu after sub-menu. FACE, which is functional and intuitive, simply asks you to read: everything is available on the monitor, in just a few clicks. I now work with just a touchscreen. With one hand, I manage the drawings and with the other, I work on the console," explained Roberto Anselmi, head of Techno's press brake department, to ISMR.

Implementation and learning times are also another decisive factor in the steps to adopt a new human-machine interface.
