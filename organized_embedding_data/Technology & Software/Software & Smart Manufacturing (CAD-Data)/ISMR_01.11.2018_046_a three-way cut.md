# A THREE-WAY CUT

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

ISMR sat down with Mark Ringgenberg, Director Global Product Portfolio, Messer Cutting Systems, at EuroBLECH to see how the company is approaching the challenges of digitalisation.

Messer is a supplier of oxyfuel, plasma and laser cutting systems, from hand guided machines up to special machines for shipbuilding as well as machines and equipment for oxyfuel welding, cutting, brazing, soldering and heating. It is active in more than 50 countries, with over 800 employees in five main locations with production sites.

It has concentrated on the digitalisation of its products and processes, with in-house software solutions developed to optimise business and production processes. It shares its expertise with selected technology partners to develop intelligent customer solutions, for example in automation.

At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition, Messer Cutting Systems launched its new MetalMaster Xcel oxyfuel, plasma and laser cutting system on the European market. It features options such as advanced marking technology, bevel cutting, a self-cleaning cutting table and the ability to combine cutting processes on one plan or even on one part. It also presented a new product family of modular software solutions which connects all systems together for the simplifying and automation of document exchange during order processing and work scheduling.

“At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition, Messer Cutting Systems launched its new MetalMaster Xcel oxyfuel plasma and laser cutting system on the European market”

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements?

MR: This year, we are celebrating our 120-year anniversary. Messer was founded in 1898 by Adolf Messer in Frankfurt, Germany. Over the years, we have focused on providing customer value through continued product innovation across oxyfuel, plasma and both CO₂ and fibre lasers. This complete portfolio allows us to choose the right technology for our customer's application.

We have 50 years of experience in laser technology. Our first CO₂ laser machine, launched in 1968, was used to cut automobile covering parts. We developed the first heavy gantry machine in 1997 and Messer Cutting China unveiled the first high-power fibre laser cutting machine in the world in 2008. We recently introduced the FiberBlade V lasercutting system and MetalMaster Xcel machines with new customer-valued capabilities. The MetalMaster Xcel machine has been sold in North America and China and we are launching the system here in Europe, at EuroBLECH. This one is a 6kW laser, but we can support 8kW. It features plasma (straight or bevelcutting), along with fibre laser. The machine was designed from the ground up as a lasercutting machine that could also support plasma compared to a plasma machine that can support laser. This is a unique distinction in the market.

With the MetalMaster Xcel machine, we are targeting users who process 4-5mm and above material thicknesses. It is designed as a large format fibre laser machine that can also support plasma cutting so it is very flexible and versatile. Users can process thin sheet of up to 50mm mild steel on this machine. This flexibility is key for job shops and steel services centres or large OEMs who want to get parts off the machines faster. Traditionally, they were buying huge machines (two plates side by side or end-to-end with multiple plasma torches on each), but they are now finding that cutting one plate very quickly, taking the parts off and moving them downstream, then loading another plate gives them better flow through their shop, than loading up a huge table with multiple plates. This is a trend we see more in the industry.

ISMR: What is your approach to Industry 4.0 as a company?

MR: We see a general trend in our industry towards the integration of our machines into customers' business processes in work preparation, production planning scheduling and analytics. Automation with material handling is gaining importance and is the primary focus area of our Industry 4.0 strategy. IoT plays a key role in enabling these areas and providing the foundation for systematic data collection that will allow our customers to gain insight into machine operation and predictive maintenance.

As an integrated solutions provider, Messer will bring the OmniFab® 2018 software product family to market at the end of this year. It provides an integrated and modular set of applications and services powered by IoT, with the aim of providing insight into production thereby increasing productivity and streamlining the user experience.

We have been data collecting and sharing this data with our customers for ten years now. The Internet is finally on the shop floor. The challenge now is trying to understand what they should do with all this data. It will play a key role in areas such as planning, predictive maintenance and efficient operations.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal plasma and laser-cutting markets?

MR: Certainly, I see that adding even more multi-process capabilities on plasma and oxyfuel machines continues to be a trend. Many customers continue to see the advantages of doing multiple operations on a part in one location, rather than cutting parts on one machine and performing secondary operations somewhere else. Drilling was a trend a few years ago and continues today. The MetalMaster Xcel machine continues down this path by combining fine feature cutting with a fibre laser and plasma-bevel capability on the same machine.

Secondly, we are being challenged to reduce cycle times and ensure faster processing (thereby increasing productivity and reducing costs). For Messer, this begins with order processing all the way through to parts offloading. Our new OmniFab® suite now includes features to streamline the process leading up to the cutting machine, while getting data back from the CNC to further improve the flow and reduce costs. Positioning speeds of plasma machines will increase but, more importantly, the acceleration rates will also improve to not only reduce cycle times but to take advantage of the newer plasma systems. Our new MetalMaster Xcel machine can go faster (up to 76m/min.). But it's not just the speed, it's

the acceleration that's different. This half a G of acceleration allows us to get in and out of corners quickly which improves part quality and reduces secondary operations.

ISMR: Please can you highlight any particular strategic or technical initiatives?

MR: We are executing a number of initiatives to streamline internally and increase our global business. One of our strategic initiatives is to grow our laser business.

This year has been very successful for us. It is one of the first years where, globally, the economy has been good everywhere. As one of the last true global manufacturers of plasma, oxyfuel and laser cutting machines, we see market growth in all regions. Our backlog going into next year is quite good globally.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.

Dr. Michael Schnick, MD, Oscar PLT, and Volker Krink, Head of Development, Kjellberg Finsterwalde, outlined the company's approach to Industry 4.0 and networked systems, as well as its new technologies, to ISMR at EuroBLECH.

Volker Krink (left) and Dr. Michael Schnick with the new Q power source

Kjellberg Finsterwalde offers products and technologies for thermal separating, joining and altering. As one of the longest established manufacturers of plasma cutting techniques in the market, it offers its customers various plasma and laser-cutting techniques for diverse tasks in automated, mechanised or manual cutting.

For more than 20 years, Kjellberg Finsterwalde has developed and manufactured special plasma torches for bevel cutting. At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, it launched a new direct diode (DDL) laser, ProFocus, for laser deposition welding, coating and additive manufacturing applications. It also presented its Silent Cut plasma cutting technology for the plasma power sources in its HiFocus series, at the show, and stormed into the exhibition with a new generation of power sources, the Q-series.

Dr. Michael Schnick, MD, Oscar PLT, and Volker Krink, Head of Development, Kjellberg Finsterwalde, outlined the company's approach to Industry 4.0 and networked systems, as well as its new technologies, to ISMR at the show.

## New DDL launch

"3D generating or additive manufacturing has the potential to revolutionise present design guidelines and manufacturing processes. OSCAR PLT GmbH has made an important contribution to this change with its new development of the coaxial direct diode laser, ProFocus. At EuroBLECH 2018, this new laser deposition welding innovation is being premiered to experts in the field," Dr. Michael Schnick, MD, Oscar PLT, told ISMR.

OSCAR PLT GmbH is the Kjellberg Foundation's research company. Its aim is to research and develop new and innovative plasma and laser technologies for cutting, welding and coating of materials as well as to test and further develop new materials and component requirements. It made its trade show debut on the Kjellberg Finsterwalde booth at the EuroBLECH exhibition.

With laser power of 1kW and six single laser beams, which are arranged coaxially around the material feed, the ProFocus can be used flexibly for deposition welding with powder or wire. The beam generation and optics of the direct diode laser are positioned in a processing head.

"In contrast to powder bed-based generative technologies, this system makes it possible to further process any existing semi-finished product and to add geometry elements of any size. Due to its coaxial structure, processing that is completely independent of direction is guaranteed. Ultra-HDR cameras ensure reliable process monitoring. Compared to other generative processes, ProFocus allows users the freedom to decide flexibly on the type of the filler material and to change it within a few seconds. In some cases, different materials and material forms can be used on one component. By using hot-wire technology, the productivity of the laser can sometimes be doubled (approx. 1kg/h) while processing constantly filigree structures of between one and 2mm. Further advantages are very high powder efficiency as well as excellent inert gas shielding," added Dr. Schnick.

The ProFocus diode laser is used for additive manufacturing, repair welding and coating applications. Among the first successful projects are devices for the welding repair of running wheels and for the refurbishment of tools such as shovels and straw cutters.

"This new 1kW DDL laser is our first step into this technology. It is the first head on the market that can deal with wire, powder or hot wire. It is very flexible, as every process has its own advantages. It offers greater productivity and quality benefits. For medium-sized companies, this is a very viable tool with good gas shield quality and powder efficiency," concluded Dr. Schnick.

## People come first, despite digitalisation

This October's EuroBLECH exhibition had its finger on the pulse of digitalisation. Kjellberg Finsterwalde also demonstrated this capability by showing how specific solutions for new and existing machines put people at the centre of the circle.

The company's Silent Cut technology can now also be applied to the plasma power sources in its HiFocus series. Live demonstrations with two HiFocus power sources showed the difference between plasma cutting with Contour Cut technology and the new Silent Cut process. The differences in sound pressure levels reached up to 15dB(A) during mild steel cutting. Both technologies ensure the same cut quality and users can choose their preferred technology.

"The result, with Silent Cut, is a considerable reduction of the noise level thus making an important contribution to safety at work. With Silent Cut, the sound pressure drops partly below 85 dB(A), depending upon the material thickness. From this value onwards, preventive measures to protect hearing are usually required," Volker Krink, Head of Development, Kjellberg Finsterwalde, told ISMR.

Dr. Michael Schnick also explained how Smart Factory and eServices will be implemented in the future by using its new Q series plasma cutting machines as an example. Kjellberg Finsterwalde announced its new Q series for 2019 at the exhibition.

"We have decided to run a special event around the Q source next year. We are sure that this will be a real revolution over the next few years. It is a totally new design, with new digitisation functionality such as remote support, a new dashboard visualisation for data such as maintenance and process improvements etc. to assist operators and help them improve the plasma cutting process," he told ISMR.

The power sources' process communication takes place via EtherCAT and thus enables efficient communication of the power source with the guiding system and all other components of the production process. Updates and diagnoses can be carried out by the manufacturer as remote service via a VPN connection. In addition, users will be provided with tools for visualisation, operator support and troubleshooting regardless of the device used. Further flexibility is created by the modular design of the power sources which makes it possible to extend operationally the cutting capacity, as required.

## An eye on Industry 4.0

"Since the last EuroBLECH exhibition in 2016, the greatest change is that digitilisation is now meeting sheet metal applications. We are Industry 4.0-ready and IoT-ready. It will change everything. Data is all-important. I expect to see the first business models on this at the next EuroBLECH exhibition as it is a revolution that will take time. We are seeing a lot of technical innovations, data handling and interconnectivity - this is just the beginning of the process," explained Dr. Schnick.
