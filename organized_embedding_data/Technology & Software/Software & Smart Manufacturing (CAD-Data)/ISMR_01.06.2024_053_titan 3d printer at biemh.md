# TITAN 3D printer at BIEMH

[TARİH: 01.06.2024 ISMR]

The Tekniker technology centre, a member of the Basque Research and Technology Alliance (BRTA), was at the International Machine Tool Biennial Exhibition (BIEMH) this June in Bilbao. It showcased the most recent developments in its main R&D areas relating to advanced manufacturing, surfaces, materials and ICTs for production.

At this year's BIEMH exhibition (from 3-7 June 2024) at BEC in Barakaldo (Bizkaia, Spain), the technology centre focused on the TITAN machine, a sizeable 6100mm x 3100mm x 3420mm 3D printer that will be assembled on the stand itself. Standing over three metres tall, TITAN is used to manufacture very large metallic structures characterised by complex geometries.

To do so, the equipment (designed and developed by Tekniker) uses laser technology and, more specifically, a Direct-Energy Deposition (DED) technique that deposits energy directly in the form of a metal wire.

The machine features a controlled atmosphere enclosure to simulate different environmental conditions. This feature, explained Tekniker, makes the TITAN suitable for sectors such as aeronautics, shipbuilding or space missions for operations to be carried out in extreme climates.

Carlos Soriano, a Tekniker researcher, added: “TITAN is innovative and advanced manufacturing equipment fully developed by Tekniker to produce large parts that incorporate a range of metal alloys to withstand extreme operating conditions. Consequently, we showcased a rocket engine nozzle currently being manufactured on a machine that simulates environmental conditions on the planet.”

## Collaborative robotics, AR and surfaces

During BIEMH 2024, Tekniker also used its stand to present a collaborative and flexible robotic cell performing a 'bin-picking' process. The robot picked up parts on a lateral loading tray in complete disarray that, once detected, were placed in an orderly pattern and as close as possible to the point of exit.

The solution features several technologies developed by Tekniker that enable natural human-robot interactions in different production environments. One of the key features is the SmartPicking software that uses 3D vision to guide the robot and pick up the right parts. Another is the "Proximity Detector" software that sets the distance for operators to guarantee their personal safety.

The demonstrator also showcased HoloLens augmented reality (AR) goggles to simulate the robot's working environment and monitor its movements. Digital twin technology will make it possible to compare the robot physically with its projection on the device.

The solutions presented by Tekniker at BIEMH were complemented by the organisation's capabilities in terms of the surfaces and materials produced for parts employing the DED metal wire technique. Users saw parts with different coatings and surface finishes.

“It was possible to see how advanced tribological materials have been developed by combining excellent properties that enhance tribological and mechanical performance and generate energy density,” concluded Tekniker.

Visitors to the technology centre's stand not only saw the machine itself but also several components produced by the DED metal wire technique (such as propellers or spheres).

Tekniker is a technology centre specialising in advanced manufacturing, surface and product engineering, as well as ICTs for manufacturing. It is based at Gipuzkoa, Spain, and was established as a non-profitmaking foundation in 1995.

The current Governing Board is made up of 22 members: eight institutions and 14 companies.
