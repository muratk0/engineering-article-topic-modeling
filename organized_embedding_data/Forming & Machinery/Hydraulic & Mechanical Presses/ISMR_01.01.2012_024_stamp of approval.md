# STAMP OF APPROVAL

[TARİH: 01.01.2012 ISMR]

Press manufacturer AIDA outlines its key strategies for growth to ISMR

AIDA is well known as a global manufacturer of metal stamping presses. Worldwide, it boasts approximately 2 million square feet of manufacturing space, 1,500 associates and a press manufacturing capacity exceeding 2,000 presses per year making AIDA, in units produced, one of the largest press manufacturers in the world. Its presses are used to manufacture a broad range of items including automobile components, appliance parts, electric and electronic components, connectors and terminals, and motor laminations.

“AIDA’s new Medium-Term Management plan defines its key policies of creating customers and increasing value-added content, forming the basis for its growth strategies going forward”

AIDA manufactures a complete line of metalforming presses, including gap, straightside, progressive die, transfer, high speed, cold forging and servo-driven mechanical presses. It also offers complete turnkey solutions with feeders and automation. Currently, AIDA has more than 80,000 presses installed worldwide, with capacities ranging from 35 to 4,000 metric tons. AIDA's flexible infrastructure includes manufacturing facilities in the United States, Europe, China, Japan and Malaysia, as well as sales, service and support offices in some 36 cities across 18 countries.

It continues to develop new and innovative metalforming solutions, building on a nearly century-long tradition of product quality and innovation established by parent company, AIDA Engineering.

## Global and local

“The challenge of AIDA group has been, over the past years, to move from a well known Japanese leader supporting the growth of its Japanese customers by selling and servicing locally and worldwide, to a Japanese-owned company with a global presence, selling and servicing to any customer worldwide. This change of mentality, which also corresponds to our business model, continues today as we open new branches worldwide. The most recently opened offices are in Morocco and Russia,” Arnauld Guillet, AIDA’s sales and marketing manager in Europe, told ISMR.

AIDA’s key policies include focusing on growing markets and the upgrade and expansion of its range of innovative products. Its performance target, under the new medium-term management plan, is for net sales to exceed 600 million euros by 31 March 2014 (its fiscal year-end). The new Medium-Term Management plan defines its key policies of creating customers and increasing value-added content, forming the basis for its growth strategies going forward.

Under its policy of increasing value-added content, there are two main initiatives being implemented: focusing on strong products (high market share, high profitability) and high-income operations and pursuing improved productivity (improved inventory asset turnover, higher overseas production ratios). Strong products and high-income operations refer to general-purpose presses and high-speed precision presses, where it holds domestic market shares of 50% and 80%, respectively, as well as its service businesses.

ISMR SAYS:

According to AIDA’s 2012 Annual Report, orders were up 21.1% year-on-year to ¥58.0 billion, and consolidated net sales rose 27.4%, to ¥52.2 billion. This was due to significantly higher orders from manufacturers in automotive industries in emerging countries, including those from first-time users. Thanks to higher sales, operating income increased 52.0%, to ¥2.2 billion, and net income was up 131.5% to ¥2.8 billion. As a company that derives two-thirds of its product sales from the automotive industry, this proved to be a favourable situation for the AIDA Engineering Group.

“Our forecast for the next couple of years is quite conservative as we are forecasting some difficulties. Indeed, after two years of massive investment in capital goods (our market), driven mostly by countries like Germany, USA and the BRICs, we tend to see a contraction. However, we believe that, while we’re still reinforcing the quality and seriousness of our offer, this difficult market climate will also affect some competitors, thus generating potential market share for AIDA. The mid-term projection (2014) expresses this thinking,” said Guillet.

He also points out that a distinction should be made between emerging markets/ countries where local companies are growing, and emerging markets/countries where numerous foreign companies are growing locally. This, he says, mainly affects technical sensitivities as well as the overall decision-making process.

“However, we acknowledge that all the BRIC countries represent a potential market for AIDA and we already have a presence in all of them. South-East Asia, India, Brazil and Russia are particularly interesting for our group,” he told ISMR. “On the other hand, due to our recent history in Europe and the recognition of AIDA products in the market, Europe has some of the strongest potential for new markets and customers.”

Automobile-related capital investment has been rising in Asian countries, such as Indonesia, Thailand and India, as well as in Mexico, Brazil, and Russia. Accordingly, as confirmed in its 2012 Annual Report, AIDA intends to conduct new order-acquisition activities targeting these countries in the future whilst continuing its advancement in China, currently the largest market for automotive products.

## Stamping trends

Guillet also underlines how AIDA is responding to press user needs and sheet metal market requirements which have gradually changed over the years.

“Recent major developments in the sheet metal forming market (such as AIDA's servo- driven press in 1996 with sales initiating in 2002 and over 1000 running today) have generated important changes in customer perception of press forming. The new solutions offered by our group are driving new types of press sales – highly technical, highly automated (less operator input) and with much higher performance, leading to lower operating costs. This new generation of lines, fully integrated, is forecasted to grow,” he explained.

In tandem, adds Guillet, these new generations of line require particular attention on service and maintenance support, thus creating a need for staff training and 24/7 service availability, 365 days per year.

In addition to its initiative centred on growing markets, the Group will also focus on upgrading and expanding innovative products with efforts centred on servo presses and the peripheral equipment that supports them, a series of which is currently in development. In its 2012 fiscal year, it developed and commercialised a new series of servo presses suitable for high-precision, multistep progressive forming processes, while also taking other steps to bolster its servo press line-up. The number of orders for general-purpose servo press lines rose 20%, accounting for more than half of all orders on a monetary basis.

“Servo press manufacturers are currently locked in a fierce race to develop new products. Amid this intense competition, we believe that strengthening our ability to create forming systems incorporating presses with peripheral equipment will be key to differentiating ourselves from the competition,” AIDA told ISMR.

Ever-increasing demand for the use of light and strong high-strength steel in automotive body frame parts has spurred AIDA to develop presses that can form this type of material. The material is hard and, much like a spring, has a tendency to revert to its original shape which makes it a very difficult material to form. Included in such materials is 100kg-class high-strength steel, which is referred to as super-high-strength steel. Forming 100kg-class high-strength material requires a press system that is completely different from a conventional press system. Accordingly, in late 2010, AIDA developed its SMX-10000(LD) dedicated high-strength steel forming press (capacity: 10,000kN). The system features a dedicated high-strength steel forming press, an existing transfer press and a press-to-press high-speed conveyance system.

Leveraging its breadth of knowledge in forming systems, AIDA’s stamping solutions incorporate research on new raw materials, the development of new forming methodologies, reviews of forming processes and the design and manufacture of dies.

## FOCUS ON STAMPING

## Continuing innovation

“Recently, we have been focusing on the high value-added service area we have dubbed ‘modernisation services’. In this area, we offer consulting services that propose improvements such as upgrading mechanical presses to servodriven presses to improve productivity and installing optional servo die cushions to boost formability,” AIDA explained.

AIDA continues to expand its range of servo presses. It holds a dominant market share for general-purpose presses and high-speed automatic presses.

“The EuroBLECH exhibition has been identified as one of the biggest exhibitions in the world for our business. As such, AIDA has decided to invest there and show its latest servo-driven press developments. Three different lines will be running during the exhibition, all of them showcasing one or more innovations,” Guillet told ISMR.

In August 2012, AIDA Europe supplied the world’s largest servo transfer press to a North American customer. The transfer press, with a capacity of 3000 tons and a bolster size of 6,000 x 2,500mm (236 x 98 in.) is the largest of its type in the world. The press is driven by four AIDA high-performance servo motors providing 30spm in normal operation and 35spm in ’pendulum mode’. In pendulum mode, the slide does not perform the whole stroke but just the portion necessary for the operation, thus allowing a higher output than a similarly-configured mechanical press.

“The energy management and buffering systems are also unique. Despite an installed capacity of over 2 Megawatts, in normal working mode this system requires less power than a mechanical press of the same size. Thanks to an intelligent energy management system utilising capacitors, the power requirement from the electrical grid is reduced to a minimum. This system is used mainly for high-strength body parts for the latest generation of German-designed cars manufactured in the USA. The increased model variety and reduced lot size are supported by a die change of less than 5 minutes,” AIDA told ISMR.

At the end of last year, the AIDA Global Group introduced a new line of straightside stamping presses, the MCX Series. The MCX, modular configuration series presses, offer stampers a wide array of press configurations and specifications. Options include single or double gear, 6.3mm or 12.7mm rating points five drive options (including mechanical or servo), a wide variety of stroke lengths and die height choices and capacities ranging from 315 tons to 1250 tons.

Currently, approximately 30% of the Group’s production is conducted overseas. However, as overseas demand is expected to grow more than ever before, it has set the goal of raising this level above 50% and is pushing forward with the development of a global network that will allow procurement and production to be conducted in optimal locations.
