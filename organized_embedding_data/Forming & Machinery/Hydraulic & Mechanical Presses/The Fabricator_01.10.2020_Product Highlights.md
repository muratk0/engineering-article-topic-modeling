Wearable exoskeleton helps reduce worker strain, fatigue

Hilti North America has developed the EXO-O1 wearable exoskeleton. According to the company, the wearable system can help reduce strain and fatigue for experienced and novice users.

To create the exoskeleton, the company partnered with Ottobock, a provider of prosthetics, orthotics, and exoskeletons that help people increase and retain their physical independence. The product currently is in real-world job site testing, and the manufacturer expects to bring it to market in late 2020.

The exoskeleton initially is focused on overhead and shoulder height and above applications because this type of motion is so physically intensive and fatiguing. To help customers embrace technology transformation, the company also will offer on-site implementation, training, and support services.

Portable magnetic drill updated with safety features, smaller footprint

Hougen Mfg. has introduced the upgraded HMD904 portable magnetic drill with numerous new features in a lightweight, small-footprint package.

The motor cord now is incorporated into the drill’s motor casing and housing to help increase safety and reduce maintenance. Overall height has been reduced by 1½ in. to allow the drill to fit into tighter areas. A pilot light has been built into the magnet to help the operator line up the pilot with the hole’s center in low-light conditions.

Powering the drill is a proprietary Hougen motor with an aluminum casing to help improve durability. The drill also includes a two-stage magnet that increases magnetic holding power by 30% when the drill motor is turned on. The arbor system uses a ⅝-in. slot drive for more rigidity, hole accuracy, positive slug ejection, and quicker use of accessories such as drill chucks and tapping attachments, the company states. The 30-lb. unit provides drilling capacities from 7/16 to 1½ in. dia. and 2-in. depth of cut.

Fiber laser cutting machine handles small-, medium-diameter tubes

Mazak Optonics Corp. has introduced the FT-150 Fiber laser cutting machine for processing small- to medium-diameter tubes.

Features include a proprietary agile U-axis, a 2.5D cutting head with focus detection, and a programmable B-axis angle. It offers extrusion tapping, which uses a rotary spindle and eight tool positions. Any tool position can be deployed for direct tapping or combined with extrusion, providing proper thread depth.

Options include a spatter guard, part sorting system with dual belt conveyors, and weld seam detection camera.

Video chat feature aids remote diagnostics for machine repair

The UP! Service Network has launched a new feature, UP! Video Chat, for industrial field service.

The service network is accessible through desktop, tablet, or phone, giving machinists a rapid connection to a machine service provider. The requester provides details on the machine and outlines the current problem. The tech then has the necessary information to diagnose the issue and begin to determine the needed maintenance or repair. With the new feature, the service tech can provide guidance on how to make the repair via video and often can provide remote diagnostics.

The video chat feature also helps refine the estimating process, as the requester can take a phone or tablet to the machine and show the service provider the problem in real time to review repair options. With its ability to seamlessly switch from a horizontal to a vertical view, video chat lets the requester get into the machine and move around to get the view the service provider needs to diagnose the issue.
