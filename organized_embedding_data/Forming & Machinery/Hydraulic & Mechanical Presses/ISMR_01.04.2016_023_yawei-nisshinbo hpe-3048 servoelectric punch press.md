SPOTLIGHT ON ITALY ISMR SAYS: “ The average age of production machinery has grown in Italy by almost three years, standing at around 12 years and eight months”

2015 closed very positively for the Italian machine tool industry, which registered double figure increases for almost all main economic indicators.

Italy, the eurozone’s third-largest economy and second-largest manufacturer, finally emerged from a bruising triple-dip recession in 2015 when it recorded 0.7 per growth. According to BMI Research, it has a comparative advantage in a number of areas, including industrial design and innovation. However, there has been a lack of significant structural reforms to address Italy’s decline in productivity growth and external competitiveness.

However, its tentative recovery was highlighted as 2015 closed very positively for the Italian machine tool industry, which registered double-figure increases for almost all main economic indicators. In 2015, production registered a 12.2% rise against the previous year, according to UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robots and automation systems.

Remarkable growth was reported in Italian machine tool consumption, recording a 31.3% increase compared with the previous year. This reawakening in demand for production systems rewarded both manufacturers’ deliveries and imports (imports increased by 45.5%). After the setbacks suffered, in 2015 exports started to grow again strongly, recording a 7.9% upturn.

## Proposed measures

“Certainly,” stated Luigi Galdabini, the association’s President, in February this year, “the machine tool order index results achieved in Italy in 2015 are satisfactory and foster a positive outlook for these early months of 2016”. However, despite the tentative recovery in Italy, he warned that lost ground had yet to be recovered (when measured against pre-global economic crisis results in Italy).

On the other hand, according to the last survey conducted by UCIMU on the pool of machinery installed in Italian metal and mechanical enterprises and presented recently at the Chamber of Deputies of the Italian Parliament, it emerged that the average age of production machinery had grown by almost three years, standing at around 12 years and eight months: the oldest ever registered in forty years. About 30% of machinery installed is over twenty years old, lower by 25% than in 2005.

Galdabini suggested preventing machine tools from ageing by adopting a system of incentives to voluntarily replace obsolete machinery with new technologies designed and constructed in compliance with new production requirements, energy saving and compliance with the current EU regulations on work safety.

Smaller and lighter components, increased versatility of use and sealing against the elements are just some of the benefits of the adhesive bonding process.

In recent years, the use of modern adhesive technologies has become increasingly prominent in the industry. The property of bonding all kinds of materials quickly and securely is one of the reasons why adhesives are now regarded as an established part of many production processes. Almost all types of metals – conventional standard steels and stainless steel and aluminium – as well as synthetic and fibre composite materials and glass or ceramics can be combined adhesively with professional application. In this way, specific material properties can be optimally utilised for a component in product manufacture.

The manufacturing of domestic appliances, building facades, filters and wind turbines are many examples of the wide range of industrial applications. In truck construction, nearly all modern box bodies are now bonded and sealed at the same time. The sandwich design of modern wall and ceiling elements enables lightweight structures that can only be fitted by means of bonding.

Epoxy adhesives are mainly used for high load transmission to replace common mechanical joining methods. Two parts bonded with such an adhesive could be considered as structurally linked. Mechanical characteristics such as high strength, high modulus and high adhesion have proved to be effective for customer applications in demanding industries such as aerospace and automotive.

## Process benefits

As far as cosmetic appearances are concerned when bonding, there are no protruding parts such as rivets, bolts or screws – and there is no heat distortion or discolouration caused by welding techniques. These considerations are important when it comes to finished products that rely on appearance as a selling point.

On the other hand, strength is a vital issue that affects most manufactured goods and, here, adhesives can provide some important advantages. Because the bond created by the adhesive is continuous, there is a uniform distribution of stresses over the areas in question in a correctly designed joint. This is in contrast to local concentrations of stresses that cannot be avoided with mechanical fasteners or welds.

Similarly, the presence of a continuous bond produces a stiffer structure. Where stiffness is not a question, the weight of the materials being bonded could possibly be reduced, resulting in potential cost savings. Either way, bonded components generally offer longer life under loading conditions. The continuous bond also prevents leaks, thus reducing the chances of corrosion.

But the advantages do not end there. Adhesives can readily join materials that are dissimilar in composition, have different coefficients of expansion and are of varying thicknesses. Adhesives also tend to absorb vibration and sound – providing valuable dampening properties.

To achieve optimum results when using adhesive, it is vital to ensure that all surfaces to be bonded are clean, dry and free from oil or grease. ■

## Advantages of structural bonding and sealing methods

- More uniform stress distribution over the entire bond face: This has a very positive effect on the static and dynamic strength achieved. Where welding and riveting result in localised stress peaks, adhesive bonding achieves uniform distribution and absorption of stress loads.

- No change in surface and texture of the joined materials: Welding temperatures may change the texture and, therefore, the mechanical properties of materials. In addition, welding, riveting and fastening all affect the visual appearance of the parts.

- Weight saving: Adhesives are particularly popular for lightweight constructions, where thin walled parts (wall thickness < 0.5mm) must be joined.

- Sealed joints: Adhesives also act as sealants, preventing loss of pressure or liquids, blocking the penetration of condensation water and protecting against corrosion. Liquid sealants offer flexibility and fit the requirements for most applications.

- Joining dissimilar materials and reducing the risk of corrosion: The adhesive forms an insulating film to prevent contact corrosion when different types of metals are joined. It also acts as an electrical and thermal insulator.

## Future outlook

UK caravan company, Elddis, has radically changed its manufacturing methods, favouring a fully bonded structure over one using mechanical fasteners. Out went approximately 800 screws, and in came adhesives for structural and exterior bonding. The outcome has been revolutionary, creating a shell that is stronger, lighter and drier than before.

Novelis, a specialist in automotive aluminium sheet, and Henkel Adhesive Technologies signed a long-term agreement in 2015 to collaborate on the development of advanced bonding technologies for the use of aluminium in high-volume vehicles. The first product to launch, as a result of this partnership, was BONDERITE® M-NT 8453, an aluminium surface pre-treatment and adhesive bonding system for demanding vehicle requirements.

“It is important to consider the stresses to which the joint will be subjected during its life. These include a combination of shear, compressive, tensile or peel stresses. It is worth remembering, therefore, that adhesives are strongest in shear, compression and tension. Conversely, they perform with less efficiency where peel and cleavage loading are involved. With that in mind, to achieve maximum strength (and therefore, reliability), adhesive joints should be designed so that cleavage and peel stresses are, wherever possible, avoided. In addition, the substrate material in question, its thickness and the type of adhesive should be evaluated to establish the optimum area of overlap,” commented Henkel Adhesives.

In view of the prevailing trend towards lightweight design and the targets of high productivity and economic efficiency in manufacture, the use of structural adhesives opens up attractive future prospects for small and large firms. They will come increasingly to the fore, as resourceconserving lightweight design often incorporates adhesives. There is a clear trend towards composite materials and alloys.

Future materials and material combinations, as well as hybrid solutions, will offer streamlining and resource potential through the use of new adhesives whether you are joining precision sheet metal components with cold-curing, twocomponent adhesives or substituting soldering with conductive adhesives or even bonding hybrid materials made of aluminium and steel or different plastics.

In future, the economic success of a metalprocessing firm will increasingly depend on the extent to which high innovation potential is utilised in the manufacture of composite and metallic materials. In this context, structural bonding is an important factor, which is why it is constantly referred to as a key technology of the 21st century.
