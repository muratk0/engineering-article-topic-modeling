# The introduction of new plasma cutting machines has slashed lead times, eliminated production bottlenecks and saved costs at Ezystak.

[TARİH: 01.05.2016 ISMR]

If you have ever driven past a construction or heavy industrial site, you have probably, at some point, seen an Ezystak conveyor system in action. With over 20 products in its range, the County Tyrone (Northern Ireland) company has recently invested in new plant and equipment to keep abreast of its remarkable growth levels.

## Keeping up with demand

The family-owned Dungannon business manufactures a range of tracked and wheeled conveyors, bin feeders, trommels, screener and incinerator products. It has enjoyed turnover growth of 300% and its staff levels have increased by 580% over the last five years. It now exports to over 15 countries. Keeping up with these growth levels is a key issue for the company so Kerf Developments recently supplied one Kerf RUR 3000 CNC UltraSharp plasma cutting machine together with an RUR 3500 machine for profiling wider material sheets.

Ezystak managing director, Seamus McGrath, explained: “We wanted to eliminate bottlenecks created by suppliers delivering parts to excessive lead times, as this was putting pressure on other departments in our business. We also wanted to reduce production costs. We needed a reliable machine with excellent cut quality and, after reviewing the market, believed that Kerf had the best solution for our business.”

The Kerf plasma machine was installed with a large press brake and the project proved so successful that the decision was made to order a second RUR plasma machine to bring all work in-house. The first machine arrived in Summer 2015 and the second was delivered in September 2015.

“We were immediately impressed. The Kerf machine helped us to take two weeks off our lead-times and bring all our cutting and folding in-house,” said Mr McGrath.

## Quantifying the benefits

Ezystak has witnessed a range of benefits including significant cost reductions in producing its parts and respective machinery. Subcontract suppliers were providing a unit cost per part to Ezystak and, by making the parts in-house, this cost has been slashed. By doing the work internally, EzyStak is achieving maximum material utilisation through the Lantek Expert CAD/CAM and nesting system supplied with the Kerf RURs.

From a lead time perspective, Ezystak specified the RUR machines with a 10m bed, so that it could cut 5m x 3m sheets on one end of the bed whilst loading or unloading another sheet at the other end. This pendulum loading system increases the output from the machine by up to 70%. The speed and precision of the UltraSharp plasma system to cut mild steel from 3mm to 35mm and the combination of ‘pendulum’ loading has reduced lead times by at least two weeks. This is significant when the overall production and construction of a machine takes from five to seven weeks, depending upon the size and model of the machine.

“Conveyor manufacturer, Ezystak, has enjoyed turnover growth of 300% and its staff levels have increased by 580% over the last five years.”

According to Ezystak, one of the main benefits has been the elimination of subcontract cutting costs.

“We were spending upwards of £12,000 per week on subcontract cutting - this has been eliminated and we now provide other companies with our own subcontract cutting services. The Kerf machines have been so influential to our business they will pay for themselves in the coming months on direct cost savings alone,” concluded Seamus McGrath. ■
