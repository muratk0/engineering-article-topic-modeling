# PACKING A PUNCH

[TARİH: 01.01.2012 ISMR]

Steinel has over sixty years of experience in punching and bending. ISMR spoke to its head of sales and marketing on its plans for the future and strategy for success.

ISMR SAYS:

“Steinel has evolved to become a ‘one-stop-shop’ for all aspects revolving around the punching and bending tool”

Steinel has a long and innovative history. In 1927, toolmaker Bernhard Steinel developed the world's first multi-spindle drilling machine. Since 1948, the company has been developing and producing standard punch parts at its factory in the Black Forest, Germany. Today, it is a specialist in punching and bending technology from high precision guiding elements, gas pressure and system springs, active elements, tank and composite panels to the latest generation of thread moulders and expert toolmaking services. Christian Pape, Head of Sales and Marketing, discusses his views on punching and bending markets as well as customised tooling requirements in sheet metal markets with ISMR.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate?

Our traditional core markets are Germany, Austria and we currently forecast fairly stable economic development. We generally see the market for punching and bending tools in a rather stagnant phase at the moment, when the figures are adjusted to accommodate typical seasonal influences.

As a company, we have been able to reach a double-digit growth rate over the past few years. We mainly attribute our growth rate to two factors: the expansion of our product portfolio and our increased presence outside traditional markets.

## Please outline any recent successes

CP: For many decades, Steinel has been well known as a supplier of high quality standard parts. Over the past few years, we have been steadily expanding our product portfolio and today see ourselves as a ‘one-stop-shop’ for all aspects revolving around the punching and bending tool. We are currently in the middle of an exciting development process which will evolve our company into a system provider, able to deliver customised solutions such as composite plate systems or the award winning S-former threadforming unit which are both significantly contributing to our current growth rate and success.

## What are the main drivers for your products and why?

We see three pillars as essential to support trends rather than geographical aspects:

• Our particular expertise is meeting high quality, tough requirements placed on the service life (lifecycle) of the tools. Intelligent and successful companies place more emphasis on the ‘total cost of ownership’ than on attaining cheap individual prices for tool components.

• Tools are becoming increasingly complex because more and more process steps are being handled by a single tool. As a result, the requirements for accuracy and durability of components are also increasing and so is, therefore, our competitive edge.

• We are very competitive when providing customer-specific components, such as customised guide units. For Steinel, ‘Made in Germany’ is not just a label but a practising corporate philosophy. And, because we manufacture the products (from start to finish) in our highly flexible manufacturing plant, we are able to manufacture customer-specific parts quickly and economically.

## What is your strategic outlook for the next few years? Are there any aims, objectives or initiatives that you can discuss?

CP: As a partner to our customers, we aim to provide any service related to the provision of quality tools – from the finest standard product all the way up to a perfectly adapted individual component, and from technical tool consultation to an intelligent and turnkey subsystem. It is no coincidence that three years ago we founded a technology centre with Steinel active elements for tool manufacturing.

The world is getting smaller and smaller so we are no longer aiming to define our borders geographically. Our vision is that, if someone is in the process of manufacturing a punching and bending tool to the highest degree of quality no matter where in the world, they initially think of Steinel.

In the future, Steinel components (such as guide units) will increasingly be utilised in machine tool manufacturing for handling machines and industrial robots as well as for other demanding applications.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal markets and where do you see the greatest opportunities for your products?

CP: Tools are becoming increasingly complex, effective and efficient. If, for example, in the past a finished punched part only received its thread during the second manufacturing stage, today this is done with the Steinel S-Former directly in the punching & bending tool. If there are particularly rigorous requirements for a tool’s operational service life, it is equipped with a composite plate system from Steinel and therefore requires less maintenance and ultimately – even if the cost is higher - will emerge as the most economical solution. As part of our product and quality philosophy, we regard the combination of all of these aspects as a great opportunity.

Christian Pape is head of sales and marketing.

ISMR: Who are your customers? Can you segment them by activity or sector? How many do you have around the world?

CP: We do not carry out segmentation according to sector or industry, but rather according to respective demand and quality. Everywhere that bending or punching is performed and the requirements for a tool are particularly high, you will find Steinel components. And we have many thousands of satisfied customers from a broad range of industrial sectors including automotive, white goods, fastening systems etc.
