# DSF-NE2 servo press

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

In 2021, AIDA launched its new DSF-NE2, a monolithic two-point servo press that combines the high rigidity of the monoblock frame with the flexibility of the freely programmable servo movement of AIDA DSF® technology.

“It enables users to control press speed and stroke length, as well as dwell time, restrike operations and auxiliary function timing. Stroke profile programming is made easier with a manual step feed and teach function. Slide position can be dialled in with 0.1mm accuracy, with full torque and energy capability for testing die and forming processes. The manual step feed helps to eliminate inaccurate inching. The adjustable slide stroke length of a Direct Servo Former can be optimised by using the forward/reverse rotation function (pendulum motion) of the servo motor," explained AIDA.

“Our DSF-NE2 presses are equipped with our own low RPM, high-torque servo motor for metalforming applications. Each frame component is designed and verified with FEM systems and manufactured to reduce deflection under load below 0.1mm/m. The DSF-NE2 press series guarantees low-noise, low-vibration, multiple varied-length forming, optimising all manufacturing and maintenance operations as well as ensuring flexible applications and long die life spans. This series of presses can reach speeds of 120 strokes per minute," it told ISMR.

“The DSF-NE2 press series received an award at the EuroBLECH Awards as the most innovative product in the 'Metalforming and punching (Stanzund Umformtechnik)' category," it added.

"We are experiencing extremely high demand for our presses dedicated to electric motor core production. This is really a booming sector right now. We have doubled our production of these presses. We were lucky to introduce our latest press range, with the right bed size to manufacture components for electric powertrains, just before the market boom. This has significantly increased our worldwide sales. China is a booming region for this sector and we have sold lots of press equipment there," explained Archenti.

“EV is a good business for us, but we need to protect our manufacturing capacity to cover existing market demand. The numbers are huge. If electric cars keep growing, then the battery business and plate business will grow exponentially too so there is great market potential," he continued.

To respond to these high levels of demand, AIDA has had to make some internal changes and realignments to stay on track.

"We are setting up more facilities to respond to EV market demand. We are extending our dedicated factories in Japan and converting their production to serve these particular product lines. We are also starting to implement partial production processes for these presses in Europe. That is a challenge because, like many other manufacturers, there are supply-chain challenges for components/ castings etc. because of factors like the pandemic and Ukraine invasion," outlined Archenti.

He also pointed out the impact of supply-chain pressures on delivery times and the challenge that this presents when setting up new manufacturing facilities or relocating specific manufacturing processes to better serve customers.

"Internally, we are also increasing machine capacity and our equipment assembly capacity in our factories. We need to be able to easily switch from one product to another. This means significant investment in our production," he added.

"Right now, the strategic regional focus for us is the German market. The German economy is the locomotive of the automotive industry in Europe and a key barometer of the European market. So, we want to do more in this market. That is one of the reasons that we are glad to be back at EuroBLECH in Hanover again," confirmed Dr. Archenti.

## In conclusion

Whilst global factors (such as the war in Ukraine and rising inflation levels) make it difficult for Archenti to forecast what 2023 may bring for AIDA, he is confident that growth is on the horizon this year for the press specialist.

"Compared to last year, I think we will see double-digit growth this year (at around 10%)," he told ISMR.

“We have just finalised our half-year figures. This year, we have a higher target to beat than last year but, despite our conservative projections, I still believe that we will do better than last year. We are chasing a lot of big projects at the moment, but I never believe in putting all your eggs in one basket....." he smiled.
