# Five press hardening lines for Fiat

[TARİH: 01.08.2013 ISMR]

AN INCREASING number of vehicle manufacturers are deciding to use press hardened parts in their cars as a means of reducing weight and improving safety. Fiat installed its first press hardening line at its production facility in Cassino, Italy four years ago. It now has five press hardening lines, all of them from AP&T.

The factory in Cassino is one of Fiat’s five Italian facilities with approximately 4,000 employees and a production capacity of 1400 cars per day. Amongst others, Fiat Bravo, Alfa Giulietta and Lancia Delta are produced there. Since the Cassino factory is the only Fiat facility that manufactures press hardened parts, it is also an important cog in the entire Group’s industrial structure.

“We produce press hardened parts in Cassino not only for the car models that are produced here, but also for those that are manufactured at our other facilities in Italy,” confirmed Aldo Balsamo, manager of the Cassino factory’s press shop.

The first order for a press hardening line from AP&T was signed in 2007. The first line was deployed in 2009 in a 20,000 m² industrial building built for the purpose. Since then, AP&T has served as a partner for an additional four installations, the most recent of which was deployed in 2012. Production solutions include presses, automation, furnaces, robots and tools. Service and maintenance are handled by Sofir, AP&T’s Italian service partner.

The equipment has a total production capacity of 15,000 parts a day and is used, for example, to manufacture A-pillars, B-pillars and door and floor reinforcements. The share of press hardened parts in Fiat, Alfa Romeo and Lancia different car models is between 10 and 15 per cent of the body’s weight (BIW), which is a high percentage compared to the industry average.

“The press hardened parts’ combination of low weight and high strength enable lower vehicle weight – and thus lower fuel consumption and emissions – at the same time as the level of safety is maintained or improved. Press hardening also makes the material very dimensionally stable,” explained AP&T.

“For the car models that are manufactured in Cassino, we have managed to lower body weight by an impressive 22 per cent by replacing cold formed parts with press hardened parts. And all of our car models that contain press hardened parts have five stars in Euro NCAP,” added Aldo Balsamo of Fiat.

Press hardening also makes it easier and less expensive to integrate materials with different properties, for example to reinforce some parts of the body. The Cassino factory will start manufacturing press hardened parts for two additional models at the end of 2013 and will probably add new ones in the future. The development of materials will determine whether the proportion of press hardened parts will increase further.

“There are always new challenges when a new technology is introduced. On the whole, however, we are enthusiastic about the possibilities afforded by press hardening, and each experience helps us understand what can be improved. This is a journey that we – Fiat, AP&T and Sofir – have embarked upon together,” concluded Aldo Balsamo.
