# Packing a punch

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

QUICK PROCESSES require highperformance tool elements. STEINEL, a manufacturer of punching tool components, manufactures high-end guide units and nitrogen systems.

“The tools for machining sheet metal have to be even more precise than the products which they make. Because time is money when it comes to punching and bending technology, it's all about high speeds and long lifetimes. In addition to active elements, guides and spring systems are among the tool components subject to the most stress. We offer precise and extremely durable roller and sliding guides as well as nitrogen systems which, when working in concert, ensure high production speeds and low wear,” the tooling specialist told ISMR.

When as many parts as possible must be produced as quickly as possible, profitability is the combination of speed, process reliability and wear. The quality of the tool's movable parts is key in this regard. Guide units and springs, particularly, must absorb great forces and minimise friction. At the same time, they mechanical and tool engineering comes to the fore here. Its product range includes guide pillars as well as the corresponding sliding, ball and roller guides made from various materials. The standardised products cover lengths from 30 to 560 millimetres and diameters of between three and 160 millimetres. Beyond these dimensions, STEINEL also offers customer and application-specific customised guide units with diameters of up to 200 millimetres and lengths of up to 3,000 millimetres.

With its newly developed guide bushes with adhesive grooves, STEINEL also offers an alternative to conventional fitting processes.

“It is simple and quick to assemble. Guide bushes with adhesive grooves are particularly well suited for high-precision tools and guides with smaller diameters. They are suitable for ball guides, as well as for the higher loadbearing roller guides, and are completely dimensionally compatible,” STEINEL told ISMR.

## Maximum force in small spaces

STEINEL nitrogen cylinders also offer a wide variety of sizes, forces and areas of application. With sliding speeds of up to 2.5 metres per second, they are an alternative to conventional mechanical springs – particularly for high blankholder forces.

“The nitrogen cylinders require less installation space and a lower installation height, while maintaining the same stroke. Their flat force curve, which remains constant across their entire lifetime, is easy on the tool and allows for consistently high production quality,” explained the manufacturer.

“Multiple connected nitrogen cylinders, or composite tube systems, ensure a flatter force increase while using a slightly higher nitrogen volume. The permanently balanced force ratio between the springs avoids lopsided loads and high lateral forces in the tool. This leads to longer lifetimes and greater process reliability. Composite plate systems offer the same advantages while featuring a more robust and compact design. The systems, which are made according to customer specifications, connect multiple nitrogen cylinders and a control panel via boreholes in the composite plate,” it added.

For particularly complex production tasks, STEINEL produces customer-specific manifold plates with volume boreholes as integrated nitrogen buffers, space-saving manifold cylinders and control panels. These manifold plates allow a filling pressure of up to 150 bar while featuring ‘minimal pressure increase’. These values are designed to ensure a flexible and tool-friendly production process as well as low operating temperatures. The result, said STEINEL, is extremely long lifetimes of up to 10 million strokes.

In recent years, STEINEL has worked on the miniaturisation of its manifold plate systems. The result is high-speed manifold plates that generate maximum forces on small spaces using high packing density. They are primarily used in the production of large batches when challenging materials must be processed into high-precision punched parts.

STEINEL was founded as a mechanical engineering company in Schwenningen, Germany, in 1925. Since 1948, the company has focused on the production of standard parts for the tool shop. Assembly groups and custom tool systems for the punching and bending technology sector were added later. Nowadays, the company develops, designs and produces about 25,000 different products at two locations in Germany.
