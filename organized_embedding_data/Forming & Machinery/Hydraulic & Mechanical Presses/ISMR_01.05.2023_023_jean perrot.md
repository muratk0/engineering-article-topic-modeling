ISMR SAYS:

JEAN PERROT has developed a novel robotic bending cell concept, in manual or automatic mode, for one-off, small batch and serial production.

PINETTE P.E.I. industrial engineering group, designs, manufactures and supplies sheet metal processing equipment. This includes press brakes; automated robotic bending cells; shears; tube/profile benders as well as rolling, notching and punching machines. Its portfolio ranges from standard machines to turnkey automatic production lines, including XXL heavy duty machines and custom tools.

The company has a history of developing award-winning products such as its Maneo Premium LCS press brake, with laser control system, which won the TolExpo sheet metal award. It has successfully developed robotic bending cells and tandem press brake lines for customers in Europe for many years. It has also pursued key partnerships with suppliers and customers to further its development.

Jean Perrot will celebrate its 60th anniversary on 22 September 2023, part of the 160-year-old PINETTE P.E.I. industrial engineering group. Sharing staff and resources across the brands, Jean Perrot can call on 130 employees in France and a subsidiary in Germany with ten people. In 2022, it purchased GADE, a press brake manufacturer in Ferrara (Italy) with 32 staff.

## A new bending concept

“We didn't suffer too much during the pandemic as we found new customers and developed new innovations (mainly in robotics and automation) which netted us a double-digit increase in turnover for 2021 and 2022. We work across a lot of sheet metal industry sectors so if one suffers, we can switch to others. For example, the agriculture sector remained strong throughout the COVID-19 pandemic and we delivered a lot of robotic bending cells for large 5-6m long parts, weighing up to 130kg," Erick Rousseau, Director, JEAN PERROT, and Deputy CEO, PINETTE PEI Group (as well as GADE Board Member), told ISMR.

Jean Perrot specialises in developing robotic cells for large sheet metal parts.

"We operate in a field which is risky because parts are large and heavy and are not easy to bend. It is a very niche market and, to be efficient in this market, you need a lot of engineering and automation/robotic technology expertise. So, we are a one-stop shop for customers with lots of capability in-house. We do not work, for example, with robotic integrators or HMI software partners but provide this expertise ourselves. Our own HMI software can link to a customer's ERP system for overall supervision of manufacturer operations," he added.

The GADE robotic cell that will be at LAMIERA is the result of Group synergies.

Over the past two/three years, JEAN PERROT has enjoyed huge success selling a lot of automated and robotic press brake cells. It is continuing to innovate in this area as it has found a new way to use the robotic cell, in either manual or automatic mode, for either one-off or serial production. This new bending cell concept, with Fanuc robots, envisages manufacturers using their press brake operators during the day for unique, one-off or small batch production and at night, when workers have gone home, switching the cell into automatic mode for serial production.

“We worked on a lot of safety issues to obtain CE certification for both modes. The cell enables us to mix these two modes and extend production during the night shift. The cell will be customised and adapted to the needs of the customers, in manual or automatic mode. The next step is for more flexible cells that can be put in front of the press so that users can switch between the cell and manual press brakes. I want to change mindsets so that robot cells can be used for different types of production and batch activities, especially in a climate of global labour shortages. The technology, with Industry 4.0, HMI and robot programming, also appeals to younger generations," explained Erick Rousseau.

## Strategic focus

The company is receiving a lot of requests primarily for robotic cells for specific industrial projects linked to new business development for customers or new strategic visions for customer company management (e.g. improving overall productivity, starting lean manufacturing operations or trying to overcome resource issues).

"In terms of strategic focus, we bought GADE (an Italian press brake manufacturer) in 2022. We wish to explore more international markets and have a base in Italy. We also want to run more development activity and sell more robotic cells overseas. GADE has a fabrication workshop so we can use that to produce/manufacture the main parts for JP/ PINETTE press brake frames. We would also like, with GADE, to develop international sales with two brands as GADE already offers hybrid technology (its own hybrid press brake with hydraulics). This means that we can now offer hydraulic, electric and robotic press brakes to customers going forward," outlined Erick Rousseau.

GADE will continue to operate as an individual company but is now part of the PINETTE group. Erick Rousseau intends to focus on the synergy between the JEAN PERROT and GADE brands.

"We are trying to develop new innovations together, using common suppliers, so we will be showcasing our first GADE robotic cell at the LAMIERA exhibition from 10-13 May 2023 in Milan. We encourage visitors to LAMIERA to come to our booth (E26 in Hall 15) at LAMIERA to see this technology in action," Erick Rousseau told ISMR.

The global PINETTE group is also focused on sustainability, trying to reduce the energy consumption of its machine tools and tapping into decarbonisation. The majority of JEAN PERROT's customers are French and European. Erick Rousseau's ultimate aim is to attract customers outside Europe, especially in the USA and Canada.

"Steady, supported growth is the way to go. We continue to grow, step by step," he smiled.

## Continuous innovation

At JEAN PERROT's anniversary event this September, it will showcase a new robotic cell innovation. It is aimed at supporting pressbrake operators, improving useability and productivity and securing production (reducing the time for production changeovers).

At the Global Industrie exhibition in Lyon (France) this March, it showcased its largest ever press brake (a MANEO Premium model of 540 tons and 3m length). It focused on heavy press brake and ancillary technologies/ accessories at the show.

“We designed the bed; RAM; special die clamping technology; tool slides etc. We have also developed an active hydraulic crowning system with gauges on the frame of the press, which was on display at Global Industrie. This compensation system adjusts the curvature of the lower beam automatically during the bending phase for perfect bending results (equal and consistent angles) along the length of the bend," Erick Rousseau told ISMR at the exhibition in Lyon.

JEAN PERROT has also developed logistical accessories around the press brake to support the operator, improve productivity and reduce the risk of accidents and muscular injuries. For example, it has developed new motorised sheet followers on press brakes (part of its 'green' initiative) to reduce set-up time and simplify machine operators' work.

These bending aids provide valuable practical assistance (including automatic height adjustment, according to workpiece size) for operators, especially for heavy or large parts. They are easy to move and active/inactive modes can be controlled directly on the sheet follower to avoid safety risks (e.g. LED light on the sheet follower).

The Toolbox was also on display at Global Industrie in Lyon. This manual toolbox enables the tool line to be changed in less than five minutes. Tool change can be a heavy and demanding job for press brake operators. To avoid this, the Toolbox is installed on the side of the press brake so that tools/dies can slide onto the guide and new tools/dies can be installed. Sliding, rather than lifting, protects both the operator and the tool.

## Industry 4.0 in focus

JEAN PERROT has fully embraced opportunities around Industry 4.0, with its own software engineers in-house and innovations such as its HMI software connectivity to ERP systems. Around four years ago, it launched augmented reality (AR) and virtual reality (VR) products.

"They were originally dedicated to technical service support but now we have some maintenance contracts (delivering AR glasses to customers so that they become our eyes and hands, and we can support them). We developed our own software and applications and now Augmented Reality is included in our 4.0 maintenance contracts. It works extremely well and we used AR technology/glasses to install presses in China and Australia during the COVID-19 pandemic when there was no travel allowed. We also developed our own program working with Hololens, sending back images etc.," explained Erick Rousseau.

JEAN PERROT uses Virtual Reality (VR) technology for concept reviews with its customers. When the design phase is complete, it invites customers to its facility and transfers the 3D design into its new VR format so that customers can 'see' the real production line using the VR mask.

"The customer can virtually 'walk' inside his line and check every facet of the line e.g. maintenance access; ergonomics; check for conformity etc. We try to anticipate any future issues and involve the customer in the design validation phase with these new VR tools. It is an impressive and productive tool to improve the quality of our designs. We developed these tools ourselves in-house," added Erick Rousseau.

## Sheet Metal Village

JEAN PERROT has taken a partnership approach to success in various industry sectors. It believes that this collaborative approach breeds success for customers and suppliers. ISMR met the company and its partners on its Sheet Metal Village recently at the Global Industrie exhibition in Lyon, France. Erick Rousseau designed the Sheet Metal Village with this partnership principle in mind, inviting key partners (with complementary technologies) to join JEAN PERROT on the Sheet Metal Village stand.

"When you work alone, you have less power than if you are working with friends or colleagues. It's like being part of a football team. So, the initial idea was to play collectively. If a customer wishes to come to see a press brake, he may have other sheet metal technology needs and, if we can showcase other types of partner technologies in the village, there are more opportunities to sell," Erick Rousseau told /SMR.

“So, in the Sheet Metal Village we decided to focus on the activities and technologies that would be of interest to our sheet metal customers. The partners introduced and endorsed each other to customers. We attracted new customers to the Sheet Metal Village. Having the technologies close by and on show was timely and important. At the centre of the Village was a bar so that people could properly network and relax with each other," he added.

The Sheet Metal Village idea goes back to an Open Day event held by JEAN PERROT in France in March 2021, during the pandemic.

“During the pandemic at the end of 2020, it was very hard to travel and visit customers so we decided to hold an Open Day event at our factory and invite customers. I had received a lot of orders for machines but could not deliver them, so there was a lot of technology on the shop floor to showcase. However, to make it more attractive to customers, I also decided to showcase other partner technologies at the event and encouraged our ten partners to invite their customers. Our partners (including Mazak, GYS and Hexagon) agreed with this concept and we therefore ran the event in March 2021," explained Erick Rousseau.

“We attracted over 150 companies and the day was a real success. In doing so, we created a movement or philosophy, a new trend, and we were asked to repeat it. So, we are doing it again this year on 21 September at our facility in France, just before our anniversary, with ten partners," he added.

Feedback on the Sheet Metal Village at Global Industrie has been very positive, Rousseau highlighted, "especially from customers who liked the idea that new partners had been endorsed, which minimises the risk of taking on a new supplier." He aims to run the Sheet Metal Village again at Global Industrie in Paris in 2024.

"We are open to welcoming additional partners but with a highlight on specific technologies such as cutting and welding as well as a strong appetite for safety, ecological transformation and full customer satisfaction," he told /SMR.■
