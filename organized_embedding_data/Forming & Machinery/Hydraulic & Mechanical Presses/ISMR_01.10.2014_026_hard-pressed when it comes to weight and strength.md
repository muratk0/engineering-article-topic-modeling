TWENTY FIRST CENTURY SOLUTIONS
ISMR SAYS: “We highlight Amada’s new product developments, R&D and technology initiatives”

In the second of this two-part series, ISMR toured Amada’s R&D and demonstration centres at Isehara as well as its factory at Fujinomiya, Japan

In May this year, ISMR was invited by Japanese machine tool specialist Amada Group to meet its President and CEO, Mitsuo Okamoto, at its headquarters in Isehara, view its R&D and demonstration centres, visit its factory at Fujinomiya and meet several of its customers.

In the September issue of ISMR, Mitsuo Okamoto outlined Amada’s objectives, new initiatives and machine tool philosophy for sheet metal markets worldwide to ISMR. In this article, we highlight Amada’s new product developments, R&D and technology initiatives and focus on continuous innovation – with a key remit to promote competitiveness and eco-efficiency for customers.

In Japan, Amada has three factories (Fujinomiya Works for development and manufacturing, Toki Works and Ono Plant, for bandsaw production). It also has other factories in France, China and the US.

## Amada Innovation Fair

Amada now operates through 81 subsidiaries on five continents. In addition to manufacturing machines in four areas – sheet metal working (blanking, welding and bending machines), stamping press machining (Amada presses), cutting (band sawing and drilling machines) and structural steel plus machine tools (grinding machines, turning machines etc.) – it offers solutions ranging from control software and peripheral equipment to dies and maintenance services. In Japan, it has three factories (Fujinomiya Works for development and manufacturing, Toki Works and Ono Plant, for bandsaw production).

Amada’s eco focus for its products extends to its own internal practices, taking into account the overall life of the machine beyond its operational phase, material recycling, CO emissions from its factories and other sites and reducing industrial waste. It aims to reduce CO emissions from its factories and new machines by 25 per cent by 2020.

Products made using Amada machine tools include air conditioners, mobile phones, vending machines, office furniture, elevators, computers, cash registers and ATM machines. Under its blanking machine portfolio come a variety of laser, combination and punching machines. Other categories include bending machines, welding machines and general fabrication machines (for marking etc.)

ISMR was invited to Amada’s Innovation Fair 2014 (AIF2014) at the company’s HQ Solutions Centre in Isehara (Kanagawa Prefecture).

Fifteen new sheet metal solutions and technologies were on display, including the SheetCentre LASBEND-AJ system, five fiber laser cutting machines, three fiber laser welders, five punch/laser combination machines, 13 bending machines and the Amada Miyachi welding portfolio.

Particular highlights included the ENSIS-3015AJ 2kW fiber laser cutting machine. The 2kW oscillator corresponds to 4kW of cutting performance (capable of cutting mild steel sheets up to 25mm thick) and ensures stable and continuous processing across a wide range of material thicknesses, from thin to thick without a lens change.

The secret behind this innovative development lies in the ability to modulate the laser beam (flexibly) as a function of sheet thickness. In particular, when used in combination with a high performance automation system, the ENSIS offers noticeable results in terms of both economy and ecology.

The first appearance of Amada’s LC-2515C1 AJ punch/laser combination machine, equipped with fibre laser, was also a highlight. It is designed to be highly productive, energy and cost-saving. It features a light-shielded open table and provides greater differentiation in the blanking process.

Integration is a key theme for Amada this year, which in short means combining multiple processes within a single system solution. Its LC-2515C1 AJ brings together processes such as punching, fiber laser machining, automation and tool management.

Robotic bending was also on the agenda with the addition of a robot to Amada’s HG-ATC bending machine – the new HG-1003AR featured a high-end, fully automatic bending system.

The HG machine is the flagship model of the Amada press brake range. Utilising electric servo driven hydraulics, the patented Hybrid Drive system has a positioning accuracy of 0.001mm, providing users with increased accuracy and repeatability along with reduced running costs using 50 per cent less power and 60 per cent less oil. Accurate bending is also achieved using an automatic material thickness detection system and new active lower beam which automatically adjusts during part processing.

The EG-6013 AR fully automatic bending system comprises an Amada 60 tonne servo-driven electric press brake, seven-axis robot, tool station, loading device, a pair of unload containers and an unload conveyor. The cell also includes Amada’s BiS automatic angle sensors and an automatic material thickness detection system which corrects the angle during the bend cycle.

“Offering a surprisingly small footprint, the cell uses a high speed, high accuracy press brake with the world’s first dual servo press drive mechanism. Here, a low-powered, fast-acting motor is used to power rapid beam approach and retract, while a higher power motor is used for the forming operation. This concept helps to reduce overall power consumption,” explained Amada.

In combination with the seven-axis robot, which features a new gripper design with both path parallel to the machine, also reaches through the press brake beam to complete previously difficult parts. The automated process means that small parts that involve hazardous operations when handled manually can be bent safely and quickly.

Amada’s latest virtual prototyping system VPSS 3i was also on display at the Innovation Fair as well as its new FLW-4000MR fiber laser welding system. VPSS 3i is an intelligent, integrated and interactive software environment that acts as the ‘centre’ for the entire production process. The dimensioning of the final assembly is the starting point, after which the system anticipates all blanking, bending and welding operations, thus avoiding mistakes or quality issues on the final product.

“The AIF (Amada Innovation Fair) is a very large private fair – our Japanese and Asian customers come here to the show in Japan every May. Another important show for us is EuroBLECH in Germany which will take place this year in October. The third important event for us is FABTECH. We aim to release new products at each of these shows,” Mitsuo Okamoto, Amada’s President and CEO, told ISMR.

“This year’s Amada Innovation Fair is our first attempt to take customer drawings and process them on our machines and show the results to the customer, supported by the actual data. This is a trial and a new proposal style for our customers. In our Show Plaza, we also had an inspection machine provided by Mitsutoyo, as an example. We are trying this kind of process for the first time so, after welding the finished product, we can measure the parts in front of the customer. In our Show Plaza, we also explain to customers how we create the fiber laser beam, how to control those beams and how to utilise them for cutting or welding.”

The new automated Fiber Laser Welding (FLW) cell from Amada has been designed to achieve high speed and high quality welding far beyond the capability of conventional welding systems. The new system is supported by dedicated offline programming and welding simulation that allows production to continue without interruption.

FLW innovation includes a beam weaving and filler wire system for welding large gaps. Here, a gyrating lens is used to rotate the laser beam, while an automated filler system allows the user to specify when the filler is to be used. This patented beam weaving feature facilitates the welding of gaps of 30 per cent of the material thickness, up to a maximum of 2mm, therefore overcoming one of the challenges associated with laser welding.

The system is also capable of welding a multitude of difficult materials that would not normally be possible using standard welding processes. This is because, compared with conventional lamp-pumped Nd:YAG lasers, fiber lasers can achieve a small focus diameter. The result is high power density at the workpiece, reduced heat input and reduced heat-affected zone. FLW functionality includes NC-controlled, automatic focus of the laser from within each processing condition. This aids quick and easy switching between various types of weld.

The Amada Solutions Centre at Isehara is also where Amada offers solutions to sheet metal challenges for companies engaged in manufacturing and to solve customer issues. The Centre has multiple functions as an exhibition site to display various products, as a site for process verification (through which Amada finds issues and proposes solutions) and for the verification of these proposed solutions. It uses a Virtual Prototype Simulation System (VPSS) to check all manufacturing aspects before making the part.

Amada’s Fujinomiya Works at the foot of Mount Fuji

With an eye on quality, Amada runs an annual precision sheet metal technology fair to select award winners for the most innovative sheet metal designs made on Amada machines – punch presses, lasers, press brakes etc. It is supported by national and local Japanese government organisations and local Japanese sheet metal associations and attracts entries from around the world. ISMR viewed previous entries that were exhibited at the Amada Solution Centre at Isehara in Japan. The Solution Centre also identifies problems and proposes solutions to sheet metal challenges for companies engaged in manufacturing.

Amada support services to customers include IT service vehicles, remote support, plant safety proposals, a Call Centre, 24-hour parts centre for global parts supply, maintenance support and service support from over 1000 trained service engineers worldwide.

In 1978, the company opened the ‘Amada School’ – Japan’s first vocational training school specialising in metalworking machinery. In 1987, the Amada Foundation for Metal Work Technology was established by Isamu Amada, using his own funds. The company also gives support to 25 Japanese sheet metal associations.

## Fujinomiya Works

“While outsourcers require more complex parts and ever-higher quality with shorter deadlines, the batch sizes become smaller and prices become more competitive,” Amada told ISMR. “One solution is therefore to consider the overall product manufacturing process, moving towards excellence (‘Monozukuri’) and, of course, the latest new technologies.”

Fujinomiya Works is located at the foot of Mount Fuji and boasts a site area of 757,000m2. It consists of a first and second factory plus a factory dedicated to laser cutting machine tool production (production capacity of 140 machines), a development centre and a parts centre. ISMR toured the factory which has become Amada’s central base of innovative product development.

The development centre focuses on problemsolving and the early release of new products using 3D CAD systems. There are four innovation rooms where the latest design and video equipment is installed so that customers and Amada staff can use the rooms as creative space for developing new metalworking machines using 3D CAD designs. Amada’s new front-loading machine developments have shortened time-to-market of its new products.

“Fujinomiya Works is an environmentallyfriendly, energy-saving and highly efficient factory built on the concept of ‘Circulation’,” Amada told ISMR. “Here, products are developed and manufactured based on the 3S + E concept (Safety, Security, Surroundings and Energy). We use a production control system, AM-HITs, that is unique to Amada and tells us the factory status at a glance.”

The first factory at Fujinomiya Works handles the processing, welding and coating of frames, processing of turrets and assembly of turret disc modules based on the ‘Table Manner’ method. The second factory produces punching machines and bending machines using the booth-stand production system. The machines are designed in modules based on a front-loading development concept. They are then manufactured using parts that have been procured ‘Just in Time’ at booth stands, where all the relevant tools are located and where production takes place. Each booth stand can also be used by customers to check product specification, quality etc.

“This factory was built 27 years ago, operates around the clock,” said Morio Yanaka (Europe Business Department), “and is the largest development and manufacturing base in the Amada Group. We have other manufacturing facilities in France, China and the US. We employ energy-efficient practices here at the factory.”

ISMR was also shown the parts centre at the factory which can hold 1.3 million parts. To ensure prompt service delivery to customers, automated picking carts with IC tags have been adapted to eliminate the delivery of wrong parts. Critical components, such as circuit boards, are delivered in a returnable box with IC tags to ensure reliability. The four warehouse processes of ‘inbound’, ‘storage’, ‘picking’ and ‘outbound’ are completely automated.

“Seamless information-sharing between the LMS (logistics management system) and WMS (warehouse management system) enables us to reduce the lead time from parts order to delivery while improving the QCD of parts supply so that we can deliver a prompt service to our customers. We will reinforce our transportation network and reduce transportation time through partnerships with leading logistics companies,” Amada explained.
