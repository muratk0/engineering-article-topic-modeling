# TIMESAVERS burst into EuroBLECH with a host of new deburring and edge-rounding innovations.

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Paul Hartendorp, MD, Timesavers

When Arie van der Linden started a repair shop for woodworking machinery in Rotterdam (The Netherlands), Timesavers today specialises in a broad selection of machines for grinding, finishing, precision grinding and deburring metal plates. Operating from three facilities in the USA, the Netherlands and Taiwan, the organisation has placed a strong focus on innovation and R&D. In 2016, it won the prestigious Red Dot award for industrial design for its 42-series Rotary Brush (RB) machine.

At the EuroBLECH trade fair in Hanover this October, Timesavers presented a combination of its latest rotary 42 series HWRBW rotary brush machine, with an Add-on machine.

The company also presented its new aggregate, the Hammer Head™, designed to remove heavy slag on larger steel products. This new pre-treatment aggregate will lower the tooling costs for its machines and was showcased on the 42-series HWRB machine on the Timesavers' booth at EuroBLECH. It also presented its smaller 12 series machines with a new design style. ISMR caught up with its managing director, Paul Hartendorp at EuroBLECH to find out more..

## A versatile move

"On the booth, we are showing how users can increase the multi-functionality of their Timesaver machines (processing small parts large parts and all kinds of materials with different applications). We are also displaying our new Hammer Head™ on the 42 series rotary brush machine - it collects all the scrap and takes it away so that the product can move through the machine. It is simple functionality, but very useful, which we developed with a brush supplier. We have already sold two smaller 42 series machines with this feature. We sold the machine you see here at the show to a customer in Sweden," Hartendorp told ISMR.

"We have already sold 12 machines here at EuroBLECH - a combination of the 32 series smaller hybrid version that we launched last year, very successfully, and the 42 series machine. It has the same features as a 42 and can process multiple materials, with edge rounding, finishing and deburring capacity.

The new Add-on machine attracted a lot of interest at the booth. Before the workpieces are deburred or edge-rounded in the main machine, they pass a dedicated conveyor belt zone. The Add-on zone deburrs the bottom of the workpieces, giving them a rounded edge. With this combination, time is saved, since the workpieces no longer have to go through the 42 series WWRBW machine twice - they are simply loaded and unloaded.

Timesaver's 10 series Manual Grinder was also on display on the booth. With its manually changeable operation mode between abrasive disc and brush unit, it is designed to be a costsaving alternative to conventional machines for small batch production.

## Anticipating new trends

"EuroBLECH has been very successful for us. 2018, in fact, has also been a great year for us. Over the last two years, business has grown enormously but this has unfortunately meant that lead times are going up. This seems to a common issue across the industry as the market has grown. I think the introduction of the fibre laser, which leaves sharp edges on material, has greatly impacted metal finishing operations. The market has changed, over the last couple of years, from deburring and edge rounding, to stopping sharp corners," added Hartendorp.

"Customers are moving more towards brushing, rather than widebelt grinding machines. A brush generates a lot of dust in the machine so you need to address how to remove dust from the machine. You also need to fix workpieces in the machine and we have been adapting our machines to these challenges. Our next step is to adapt the machine to process small and large parts and we are addressing that with the new technologies that we have introduced here at EuroBLECH. The brush market is continuously developing and growing."

Timesavers has a strong history of diversifying both its geographical customer base and its product portfolio. With strong roots in the woodworking, and later metalworking, industry, it is also involved in the calibrating market for the machining industry. Hartendorp is always looking for opportunities to transfer that rapidly-growing technology to the sheet metal market. He believes that this industry alone has the potential to double the size of the company over the next five years.

"We have been growing 15% or more per year over the last five years, so we have almost doubled in size over this time. We make deburring and edge rounding machines, but we are also one of the few manufacturers in the world who makes stainless steel finishing machines," he explained.

## Industry 4.0 in focus

Hartendorp has a firm eye on Industry 4.0 and the potential it offers to manufacturers for networked production.

"Industry 4.0 is a big issue now and we are on board with this. It is why we equip all our standard machines with HMI and PLCs so that they are open platforms, giving users access to all the parameters in the machine for future connectivity in the production line. In the last couple of years, we have moved over to Siemens controls on all our machines, as Siemens is an Industry 4.0 leader and we want to be ready to interface with other equipment in this way," he explained.

He has also positioned Timesavers to take advantage of future automated deburring technology, a project that has occupied him for a while. He presented a machine with a robot at EuroBLECH in Hanover last year - a combination of its 42 series WRBW rotary brush machine and a robot. The workpieces, to be deburred or edge-rounded, passed the machine in a dedicated zone on the conveyor belt; the robot identified the parts, turned them and, for the final brushing operation, placed them on the opposite zone of the conveyor belt.

Although manufacturers can integrate their own robots with deburring and edge rounding machines, the automated deburring concept is a vision that Hartendorp believes can be taken to the next level. However, the key to realising this concept, says Hartendorp, is developing vision technology that will enable users to identify sheet metal parts once they are inside the machine.

"I still believe that there is a place for robot vision technology, to be able to see parts in the machines, but it is an expensive process and may only be of immediate interest to large companies doing mass production. However, we are now ready with our control interfaces for when customers are ready to take this step," he told ISMR.
