## WHAT IS FLOWFORMING?

Flowforming is a rotationally symmetrical forming process, in which the volume of a hollow body is suppressed in three dimensions: in radial, axial and tangential directions.

The clamped hollow body is pushed onto a driven mandrel by the forming rollers. The mandrel and the part are driven in axial directions through no less than two forming rollers that apply pressure to the part. The forming rollers remain radial and perpendicular to the rotational axis of the hollow body that has a set position. The radial positions of the rollers are determined so as to achieve a defined reduction in the wall thickness of the hollow body while being axially driven.

During the wall thickness reduction process, the pressure on the material between the forming roller and mandrel is so high that the material actually flows. In this pressure area, the material flows in three dimensions so that high friction between the material and the mandrel is imminent because there is relatively aggressive sliding movement between the mandrel and the workpiece. The result is that the formed material will, among other things, heat-up significantly.

This problem can only be reduced when the relative movement between the material and the mandrel is controlled. This is now possible with a new process from Repkon that has been dubbed ‘free flowforming’.

## “FREE” FLOWFORMING

The term “free flowforming” implies the essentially free flow of material during the flowforming process. The principle is simple: the pre-form will be centred on the mandrel; while one end of the pre-form is connected with a tappet on the main spindle, the opposite end will be in front of the forming rollers. The mandrel is only centred and guided in the centre of the rotation axis of the main spindle. The mandrel is not connected with the main spindle, which means the mandrel is free in axial and tangential directions.
