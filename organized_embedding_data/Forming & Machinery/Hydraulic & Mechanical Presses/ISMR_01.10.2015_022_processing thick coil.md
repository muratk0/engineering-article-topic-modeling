# PROCESSING THICK COIL

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

## BPW Bergische Achsen KG produces parts from high strength steel with up to 12mm gauges using Schuler presses and thick coil lines

Around one million brake shoes and half a million supports for the Wiehl near Cologne, Germany, every year. The innovative mobility partner and system partner for the transport industry uses steel coil with gauges of up to 12mm – or thick coil – in its production operations.

To produce such rigid parts from mostly high strength materials, the family-run company has long relied upon two 1,600-metric ton presses, together with coil lines and levellers, supplied by Schuler.

Headquartered in Wiehl, Germany, BPW is an international mobility and system partner for the transport industry and offers innovative solutions from a single source. Its portfolio ranges from axles, suspension systems, brake technologies, closure systems, body technology, lighting systems and plastic technologies through to user-friendly telematics applications for trucks and trailers. The BPW Group’s technologies and services create transparency in loading and transport processes and enable efficient fleet management. The owner-managed group has around 5,800 employees worldwide.

## An efficient production strategy

“The new production concept we introduced some years ago was a giant leap forward for us,” recalled Hartmut Schmidt, Director of Operations, BPW. “We previously used presses with forces of 400 to 630 metric tons. The challenge was to find an economic way of producing the parts and to integrate it into our production flow. The old machines weren’t able to achieve this with the necessary quality and output levels.

“In 2006, we therefore decided to set up a completely new press centre. As a one-stop shop for both press and automation equipment, Schuler was an obvious front-runner.”

This concept has also been embraced by BPW itself. As a system partner for vehicle manufacturers, the company can provide its customers with complete systems and extensive services, as well as individual components such as brake shoes and supports, from a single source.

The thick coil sector for such products in Germany and Europe is not exactly large.

“Most suppliers produce for the automotive or domestic appliance industries where thick coil already starts at 3-4mm,” said Schmidt. Consequently, equipment which can straighten, blank and form coil of up to 12mm is quite rare.

## Joint development of leveller

When using such thick materials, BPW attaches great importance to keeping waste to a minimum as scrap costs for thick coil are correspondingly high. The nesting option i.e. placing numerous parts with the smallest possible gaps between them on the coil and thus using the coil’s full width, is absolutely essential.

“To ensure that the main product and subsidiary products drop out of the press in perfect condition, we not only need high blanking forces but also good guidance of the coil along the line,” explained Schmidt. Whether at the beginning, end or middle of the coil, thick coil stock must always enter the die in the correct position. The task of the leveller is therefore to balance out any bends and cambers.

“There were no solutions on the market and so we decided to develop one with Schuler,” added the engineering graduate.

According to Schmidt, the new equipment not only enabled BPW to raise output but also machine availability.

“This is equally important as our production flow is highly integrated and we produce ‘just in time’. We would need huge storage and inventory levels to compensate for a line which doesn't meet our requirements. We therefore looked for a reliable line with high availability and wanted to work with a company which could solve any technical problems we might have.”

BPW’s new press centre helped to take the company’s productivity to a new dimension.

“We more than doubled stroking speeds and the higher tonnage meant that output volumes were more than quadrupled. In a good shift, we used to produce 3,500 to 4,000 mass-produced parts – today, the figure is more like 16,000,” reported Schmidt. At the same time, the accuracy, and thus quality, of the parts has improved as tolerances have been reduced from 3mm to 0.5mm.

“This means that we no longer have to check dimensions and re-work or reject parts. Because of the specific machine configuration and consistent development of the interplay between die and machine, we also gained extra capacity. As a result, we no longer just produce parts for our own products but can take on outside orders, including finishing work such as KTL coating.”

And how does Hartmut Schmidt himself judge the production line’s performance after several years of working with it?

“There are always teething problems with projects like this and especially with new developments. The important thing, however, is that you have a partner who can always help you solve such problems. This is what we ourselves promise as a reliable partner for the transport industry – finding solutions together with our clients. Schuler really provided great support in all situations. This partnership is ultimately what helped us achieve such success," he concluded.
