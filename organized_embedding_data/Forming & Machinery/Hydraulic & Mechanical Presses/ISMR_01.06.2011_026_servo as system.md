# SERVO AS SYSTEM

[TARİH: 01.06.2011 ISMR]

SINCE KOMATSU Industries Corporation introduced its hydraulic servo press, HAF, in 1994, it has advocated the concept of the free motion press. This concept has been adopted from its initial hydraulic press to the HCP 3000 AC servo press, the H2F hybrid AC servo press, the H4F series and the H1F series of hybrid AC servo presses in 2002. Komatsu also applies the free motion concept in the forging press segment.

Instead of just replacing the flywheel with a large servomotor, Komatsu designed its servo press drive as a system incorporating standard AC servo motors and various torque multiplying designs that vary according to the application.

“For servo presses, the length of the stroke is changeable so that only the stroke length that is actually required for production is used,” the company told ISMR. “By setting the optimum slide motion, which is determined by the type of production and dies used, productivity can also be improved. The slide position fluctuation at the bottom dead centre and the slide parallelism fluctuation by the eccentric load between points are limited to +/- 10 μm. This accuracy is not dependent on the press size.”

The noise signature of Komatsu’s servo press is also small. Blanking noise can be further reduced by carrying out ‘two-step blanking’ where the punch is stopped halfway through the blanking process and then started again.

“The level of blanking noise can be decreased by more than 10dB in comparison with conventional blanking,” Komatsu explained.

Servo presses do not employ flywheels or clutch brakes, and they feature a lower number of gears than conventional mechanical presses which enhances maintainability.

“Because there are no elements such as clutch or brake, little press lubricant is needed,” added Komatsu.

At present, Komatsu servo presses use four types of driving mechanisms:

Top: Komatsu's H2W is an eccentric servo press suitable for progressive, transfer or manual die operations. It features an independent servo drive system with auto die height adjustment. H2W tonnage is 200 – 300 tonnes

Above: The H1F model is 60 – 200 tonnes

Type 1 – this type of press employs an eccentric servo mechanism. For example, Komatsu's H2W is a two-point eccentric servo press suitable for progressive, transfer or manual die operations. It features an independent servo drive system with auto die height adjustment.

Type 2 – this driving mechanism is used for Komatsu’s H1F series one-point servo press. It is a hybrid mechanism, where the drive shaft of the link press is driven by the servo motor through the timing belt. H1F is suitable for deep drawing and continuous operations at high speed.

Type 3 – this is a hybrid mechanism used in the H2F and H4F series. The rotation of the servo motor is transmitted to the ball screw by the timing belt. This type is more suited for larger press capacities whilst featuring the same level of flexibility as Type 4.

Type 4 – this type is used in Komatsu’s initially developed HCP AC servo press. The rotation of the servo motor is transmitted to the ball screw by the timing belt and the slide is moved up and down by the reciprocating motion of the ball screw.

Komatsu servo presses include a plunger guide system which absorbs thrust load from the eccentric motion. A size-specific plunger guide is engineered for each different press model.
