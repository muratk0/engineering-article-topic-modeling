# KEEPING CONTROL

[TARİH: 01.09.2013 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“We highlight a selection of the latest new servo press developments in the global market”

With increasingly stiff competition, the differentiating factor for automotive manufacturers seeking dominance is the creation of body designs that attract consumers, lighter vehicles that boost mileage and stronger vehicles that enhance crash safety. These trends are resulting in new challenges for the press industry. Increasingly sophisticated forming technologies are becoming necessary for both the forming of exterior panels for body designs and the forming of lighter and stronger frame members that reduce vehicle weight and improve crash safety.

Achieving the three-dimensional outer body panel designs that automotive manufacturers seek requires unprecedentedly high-level drawforming technologies. Servo presses, where the forming speeds can be precisely controlled, answer this requirement. A servo press can provide positional control that is not possible using a conventional press and this has enabled controls that offer precise synchronisation between the press and the transfer equipment. A servo motor gives a press slide motion flexibility in terms of accurate speed, motion and position control. This flexibility produces infinite slide motion variations that may improve part quality and operations such as painting and assembly during one press stroke.

Servo-driven presses enable the slide position curves, speed and stroke to be programmed so that different manufacturing processes can be carried out with just one press, including jobs that could not be done with mechanical presses until now.

## Servo press developments

“Servo presses are being employed increasingly by manufacturers, a trend that is most prominent among European automobile manufacturers,” press manufacturer, AIDA, told ISMR. “Servo press manufacturers are currently locked in a fierce race to develop new products. Amidst this intense competition, we believe that strengthening our ability to create forming systems incorporating presses with peripheral equipment will be key to differentiating ourselves from the competition.”

AIDA is a specialist in servo-driven forming presses, with a yearly capacity of over 2000 presses and more than 70000 presses installed worldwide. AIDA presses are used to manufacture a broad range of items including automobile components, appliance parts, electric and electronic components, connectors and terminals, and motor laminations.

It delivered a modern servo press to the Institute for Metal Forming Technology (IFU) of the University of Stuttgart for research in developing deep-draw and stretch-forming technologies for modern sheet metal materials for the automotive and aerospace industries. This press boasts a nominal capacity of 630 metric tons and a die cushion with a robust energy management system which stores and subsequently uses the recoverable energy from the motion of the slide and cushion on the basis of the load.

Long term research goals can be pursued in relation to more highly efficient drive systems for metal forming machines, allowing for a significant contribution to the energy savings of components formed in metal stamping presses. The press is driven by a proprietary AIDA high-performance servo motor.

AIDA’s line of straightside stamping presses, the MCX Series, offer stampers a wide array of press configurations and specifications. Options include single or double gear; ¼- or ½- inch rating points; five drive options including mechanical or servo; a wide variety of stroke lengths and die height choices; and capacities ranging from 315 tons to 1250 tons.

Swedish manufacturer AP&T designs, manufactures and markets automation, presses, tools and complete production lines for the sheet metal and tube forming industry. Its HNC is a control system mainly for servo-based hydroforming applications with the functionality of a NC-system i.e. quick response and precise regulation of position, force and speed during the entire work cycle.

Benteler Aluminium Systems in Tönder, Denmark manufactures structural parts based on extruded aluminum profiles for several leading European car manufacturers. The company recently invested in new equipment from AP&T. The delivery included a new 315 metric ton ODEN press with standard bolster and an extra side-mounted servo axis/automation for specialised tool usage.
