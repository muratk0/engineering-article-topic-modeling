# A DIGITAL PATHWAY

[TARİH: 01.09.2023 ISMR]

Example of coil-processed metal.

Produtech's CEO, Fabrizio Mologni, outlined its latest technology/R&D developments, recent investments and digitalisation philosophy to ISMR.

Produtech Srl, based in Albano Sant'Alessandro in the province of Bergamo (Italy), produces customised sheet-metal processing (punching and laser cutting) machines and systems which feed material directly from coil. Systems also include combined punch-laser systems and special equipment, such as punching lines for processing metal bars and beading machines.

Its latest innovations include an optical fibre, coil-fed laser system, its EffiCOIL coil-fed combination fibre laser cutting and punching machine and a new AutoCOIL automation project. Produtech's CEO, Fabrizio Mologni, outlined its latest technology/R&D developments, recent investments and digitalisation philosophy to ISMR.

ISMR: Please highlight any successes and achievements, as well as any significant targets reached, over the past two years.

FM: Produtech has set as its main objective, and not just in recent years, the production of machine tools and systems that offer technological product and process innovation. This primarily involves the design and construction of laser and punching machines for sheet-metal processing which are fed directly from coil. To support these innovative activities, research and development has always been an important investment item in the company budget.

Today, our machines include systems covered by international patents. We create tailor-made solutions for specific production requirements and have customers all over the world. Our latest innovations have given a significant boost to the growth of the Produtech brand. We have received numerous awards from Italian government agencies, such as Invitalia, for the enhancement and development of our patents. We have also received awards from the European Community under the Horizon 2020 programme, with several certificates of excellence awarded to us for the development and implementation of machines and innovative solutions.

In recent years, we have worked on the design, construction and marketing of our 'EffiCOIL' production line for punching and forming (using mechanical tools) and cutting (with an optical fibre laser). This innovative and unique machine enables two production technologies to interact over a single work area, fed directly from coil.

In the current market, production from coil (on machines and systems that are coil-fed, to produce finished products with no need for further or different production processes) is still relatively little used. This method offers great production benefits and substantial cost, space and manpower savings (eliminating all preliminary processes).

ISMR: What are your views on current global markets for coil-processing professionals?

FM: Market sectors that produce metal components (such as the household appliances, or white goods; air conditioning; cleanrooms; automotive; grain silos; metal furniture and shelving sectors) have already expressed a strong interest in lines and machines that produce from coil. They see many opportunities in this production method linked to the flexibility offered by machines that are coil-fed and can automatically perform complex cutting and forming processes on a single machine, with no restarts or intermediate tooling needed.

We are therefore working to expand the range of our machines and peripheral equipment so that we can offer fully automated solutions for the coil-fed production of metal components. It would also be useful, as highlighted by many of our customers, for governments to offer investment incentives (aligned with, for example, Industry-4.0 investments) and support for the economy to become more structural and support market development.

ISMR: Do you project any growth in turnover for this year, compared to last year?

FM: For several years now (except for 2020 due to COVID-19), we have embarked upon a path of continuous turnover growth. We grew our turnover in the last year and figures to date suggest, with a high degree of probability, that this growth path will continue this year and next year.

We believe that the market for machines that work directly from coil is destined to expand and that there are excellent growth possibilities for coil processing.

ISMR: Which investments have you made over the past two-three years (machine tools, factory, staff etc.)?

FM: In recent years, Produtech has massively grown its turnover. This is because of substantial investments made in company organisation, expansion of its machinery, increasing personnel levels with the inclusion of new technical staff and enhancing investments in Research and Development. New equipment and machine tools were purchased, as well as a 3D printer. The technical design office was strengthened as well as various production departments.

We also made important investments in the design, implementation and integration of software, systems, platforms and applications customised to our specific needs. This includes the management and coordination of design, purchasing, production and all company functions that complete the production process. This powerful production management system is making an important contribution to Produtech's digital transition.

ISMR: What percentage of your turnover do you spend on R&D? Which R&D areas are you focusing on now?

FM: Produtech commits about 6% of its turnover to Research and Development, considering this activity essential for its growth. We are developing new versions of our innovative machines, continuing the development of systems for metal component production from coil.

Our R&D commitment is to industrialise this production method, expanding the range of machines and peripheral equipment, to allow the most diverse processes. This includes refining the coil-fed production process and increasing opportunities for using coil-fed systems. Having developed the coil-fed punching, optical fibre laser and combined machines, our R&D efforts are now focused on making the coil-change process rapid, easy and safe. This means that metal component producers will be able to replace the coil, automatically and easily, when working with different materials.

ISMR: What is Productech's approach to Industry 4.0 and enabling technologies?

FM: Produtech's approach to Industry 4.0 has been significant as all our machines and systems comply with the parameters of Industry 4.0 and contribute to our customers' digital transitions. That is one of the reasons why we have invested in digitalisation, digitising the entire production process.

ISMR: Are there any products/technologies that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

FM: We are currently working on a new project called "AutoCOIL". This project, which will be covered by new patents, aims to complete the automation of operating cycles which, from coil, produce finished components on a single line. Project implementation will allow metal component manufacturers to use systems that feed directly from coil, as well as a system for coil change and automated production. They will benefit from the production flexibility of direct coil feeding.

The "AutoCOIL" system will consist of a compact magazine to house the various coils and a shuttle that picks up the coils from the magazine and loads them automatically onto the expandable shaft of the decoiling machine. The cycle for replacing a coil will be simple and safe, as well as automatic.

ISMR: How has Produtech embraced sustainability in its products, processes and operations?

FM: By designing and manufacturing innovative machines and systems, Produtech has not only contributed to improving production processes but has greatly contributed to sustainability in the ways highlighted here:

1. Significant energy savings during the sheet-metal working processes thanks to the direct feed from coil which avoids numerous ancillary operations for sheet cutting, packaging, logistics and the use of additional machines.

2. Saving CO₂ emissions through simplification of the production process and by using fibre optic lasers to replace CO₂ lasers.

3. Reduction of the use of raw materials through production on continuous sheet metal strip which eliminates head and tail sheet-metal scrap.

4. Lower production of scrap metal enables further energy savings by reducing the costly processes required to regenerate the scrap metal.

5. The use of direct-drive motors (linear and torque motors) eliminate mechanical transmissions and therefore lubrication with oils that are sprayed into the environment.

6. Reduction of noise pollution through mechatronic drives that control speed and the impact phase of the tools on the sheet.

7. Increased safety in the workplace by eliminating handling operations for sheet-metal sections and manual control of various production operations which could necessitate high-risk interventions.
