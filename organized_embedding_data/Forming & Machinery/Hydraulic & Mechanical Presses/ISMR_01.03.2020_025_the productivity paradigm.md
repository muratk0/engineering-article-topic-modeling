# THE PRODUCTIVITY PARADIGM

[TARİH: 01.03.2020 ISMR]

JML Engineering made a strategic decision to switch from hydraulically-operated benders to all-electric ones for greater productivity

ISMR SAYS: “All-electric benders from BLM are driving efficiency for JML Engineering"

Twenty years ago, Alistair Bailey became disillusioned with working long hours for the benefit of someone else's business, so he made the break and created JML Engineering Ltd. in the UK to specialise in tube bending and tubular fabrications. The business developed through investment in several used hydraulic tube bending machines, with key customers providing steady orders for security (bollards and crash protection) and safety (hand railings and cages) products.

The old hydraulic machines served the business well but, as with all things, age started to tell and manufacturer support for them began to drop off. Recognising that it was time to invest further in the business to improve productivity, Alistair researched the market and identified that a switch from hydraulically-operated benders to all-electric was the way to go.

This decision led to the arrival of his first BLM all-electric multi-stack tube bender, an Elect 52.

## An eye on accuracy

“Breakdowns on our hydraulic machines were becoming an increasing issue and the decision to invest was made for us because of this. The arrival of the BLM Elect 52 certainly eased the demands placed on production, as business grew rapidly, and the Elect 52 handled the bending of smaller handrails with ease," explained Alistair Bailey.

Key advantages of the BLM All-electric Elect machines is the speed at which they can be set up and the manufacturer's guarantee that bends will be consistent, without any need for adjustment from the start of a batch to the last tube being finished. The CNC control on the Elect machines manages all the clamping and slide pressures to ensure consistency of the bend. Once the tools have been changed, the operator calls up the program and the control handles the bending process.

"Our hydraulic machines, on which we were very well versed, could take 45 minutes to achieve a first-off, after which there would be a degree of trial and error before we got the bend just as we wanted it. Now, we can complete the set-up in less than 15 minutes and know that the first tube will be within specification. Scrap is a thing of the past, thanks to the uniformity of the process, and the software in the control is a major benefit. For example, the B-Tools software helps to eliminate the requirement for manual adjustments, which were always needed on our hydraulic machines," Alistair Bailey told ISMR.

## A smooth flow

B-Tools software is part of the standard VGP3D 3D graphic programming software contained within the BLM control. It functions by taking the theoretical data of a part to be bent and automatically applying corrections from a database of materials and bending experience. A database of prior bending experience in similar materials is also available.

Overall the VGP3D programming software allows the user to either create a part program at the machine or import IGES and STEP files and automatically convert those into a bending program. Collision detection is also part of the system, with VGP3D checking the feasibility of a part program to ensure that there is no risk of damage to the part, machine or tools.

"Health and safety has also improved as operators have no reason to manually interact with the machine to make any adjustments and the safety features of the light guards and fencing offer further protection during production. The machine's multi-stack tooling system allows up to three sets of bending tools to be on the machine at any one time, further reducing set-up times. The Push Bend capability also allows flexibility in production which enables small and large radii to be bent on the same piece of tube, something that wasn't possible with JML Engineering's hydraulic machines," added BLM.

## Continued investment

The extra capacity has given us the potential to unlock new opportunities and expand our customer base.

The initial success of the BLM Elect 52 was such that Alistair quickly followed it with a second machine, this time the larger Elect 80 with its capability to bend up to 80mm diameter tube.

"At the time we had no requirement to bend tube at that diameter, but the extra capacity has given us the potential to unlock new opportunities and expand our customer base. With the state-of-the-art nature of these two machines, we are in a good place to meet existing and future demands from customers. We know that we are now far more efficient, both in production and for the environment as the all-electric machines use less power and the working environment is a nicer place to be without the constant noise from hydraulic pumps (and the associated oil leaks that went with them)," added Alistair Bailey.

These two All-Electric Elect bending machines have now replaced four older hydraulic machines.

“Finding people with the experience and knowledge to operate hydraulic benders efficiently is extremely difficult. We had minimal training on the BLM machines and I am now confident that just about anyone could operate them and produce high-quality parts. We have built our reputation on the supply of high-quality parts. The control and software are identical on both machines, so there is no problem switching from one to the other," concluded Alistair Bailey. ■
