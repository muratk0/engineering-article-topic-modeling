# SAY IT WITH COIL

[TARİH: 01.11.2019 ISMR]

ISMR sat down with Ottavio Albini, Asservimenti Presse's sales manager, at Blechexpo in Stuttgart to find out what customers are looking for in today's coil processing lines.

Italian manufacturer, Asservimenti Presse, produces equipment for press automation (decoilers, straighteners, electronic feeders, handling systems, robotics etc.) as well as cut-to-length and transfer lines etc. for sheet metal working. With over thirty years of experience, it currently exports its technology to over 20 countries.

It produces mainly two types of feeding lines. Its traditional lines feature three machines (decoiler, straightener and feeder), used with high-speed presses (100–300 strokes/min.) to process delicate materials (stainless steel, pre-painted strip etc.). Its compact lines include decoiler and electronic straightener-feeder-units for material thicknesses of up to 14mm and widths from 200 up to 2000mm, complete with systems for automatic strip insertion. The compact lines are suitable for customers who have limited space in their workshops.

Its electronic straightener-feeder-units, with open upper head to enable cleaning of the rolls, make material introduction easier and allows material release for the pilot centring, which is especially suitable for coil lines processing higher material thicknesses. ISMR sat down with Ottavio Albini, the company's sales manager, at the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart this November to find out how coil processing lines in different markets have performed this year.

Ottavio Albini, Asservimenti Presse's sales manager

## Lightweight material processing

"We specialise in press automation and produce feeding lines with decoilers of up to 25-ton capacity and external diameters of 2000mm, which can work with steel strip of up to 2000mm in width and 14mm thickness," Albini told ISMR.

Above: Coil feeding line incorporating servo press technology for material thicknesses from 0.5mm up to 12mm

"We focus primarily on markets in Europe and started this year with new distributors in Germany. We have an extensive distributor network across Europe. Our 2000 square-metre factory is based in Italy. Most of our orders come from countries such as Italy, Germany, Poland – regions where automotive production is strong," he added.

On its booth at Blechexpo, Asservimenti Presse was showcasing a double-decoiler and straightener. It supplies coil processing lines for a range of industries, including automotive. The move to electric and hybrid systems in the automotive market sector, as well as the prevalence of individual electric motors in cars, has seen the company's business grow. More of its lines are producing components for electric motors in cars for its customers.

"We find ourselves working more now on coil lines to process lightweight materials (such as high-strength steels and aluminium) with quick changeover times. Our customers are asking us for this. They also ask us for more coil lines to process copper (for electric car production)," Albini told ISMR.

"Customers want short changeover times for greater efficiency and productivity. I also see more requirements for automation in lines, as well as a drive towards Industry 4.0. In Italy, the government is offering a tax incentive (around 30% discount) for customers to buy capital equipment or equipment with Industry 4.0 capability. This has been very good for our business over the last two years and we expect this government programme to continue well into 2021."

## Trends and requirements

Energy saving has been a key focus for Asservimenti Presse over the years. Its electronic straightener-feeder-units feature an electronic pilot release that releases material through a system of numerical control. This machine enables a reduction of time for head opening & closing; a reduction of the roll impact speed on the strip; reduced overall energy and reduced use of lubricating oils.

Asservimenti Presse's speciality is customised lines – it works with OEMs, press and equipment manufacturers to equip complete coil lines. These lines may incorporate a range of equipment, such as decoilers, straighteners, feeders, presses etc. Albini has seen the increasing adoption of servo technology in many of these lines, as customers opt for more control and better productivity on their lines.

"Customers want more capable technology, but they always keep an eye on price,' explained Albini. "We see increasing requirements for lines to process material up to 17mm. Over the last three years, we have worked on supplying lines to process greater material thicknesses and higher tensile strength materials.

"The last five years have been good for our business – we have a number of ongoing projects in production and our order book is healthy. However, the German economy has been suffering a little – but markets such as Poland, Romania and Spain have remained strong for us this year," he told ISMR.

Next year, he admitted, looks a little more uncertain in the wake of issues such as Brexit, geopolitical trade tensions, tariff conflicts etc. As he pointed out to ISMR, 'the business climate and economy must support investment, not discourage it....

"The biggest challenge will be the economy next year. To counteract this, we need to stay in constant contact with the customers and keep collaborating closely with them. This means deepening our relationship with them on key projects," he concluded.
