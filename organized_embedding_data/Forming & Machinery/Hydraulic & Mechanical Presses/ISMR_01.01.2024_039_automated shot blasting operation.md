# Automated shot blasting operation

[TARİH: 01.01.2024 ISMR]

A Dutch manufacturer of industrial furniture (ML Constructie) decided to bring its coating operation in-house. It also did the paint preparation in-house by shot blasting with an automatic shot blasting machine from Rösler.

Ben Luizink, co-owner of ML Constructie, reported: "To better control the blasting and coating operations, we created our own inhouse coating department."

## The coating process

ML Coating was therefore started and has since grown into a coating company that shot blasts and powder coats its own products and provides blasting and coating services to other companies. One of the first purchases by the newly created company was a spinner hanger blast machine from Rösler.

"A pre-condition for achieving a homogeneous, high-quality coating is that the components are thoroughly and precisely shot blasted. Most companies in our area still do this manually. However, right from the beginning we decided to automate the entire shot blasting operation," explained Mike Luizink.

With the Rösler blast machine, employees place the workpieces onto the respective fixtures and remove them after the blast process. Noise is considerably lower than the noise from manual blast rooms. The automated shot blast process, said Rösler, is eight to ten times faster. While in manual blast rooms, media quantities of about 6kg are dispensed per minute; each of the four blast turbines in the spinner hanger machine throws 150kg of media at the workpieces every minute.

The spinner hanger blast machine is equipped with two rails in a “Y" design. This allows the operator to place workpieces onto the fixture at one rail branch, while the workpieces from the other rail branch are being blasted. This helps to eliminate unproductive idle equipment times.

"In the automated shot blast process, the blast media is getting into even the tiniest work piece corners. This is nearly impossible with manual shot blasting operations," commented Ben Luizink. “The Rösler shot blast machine is very tight and emits practically no dust. Since we are coating the workpieces in the same hall, this is a huge benefit."

## Cost and energy savings

ML Coating also achieved significant cost savings in other areas.

"After ten blast cycles, the sand used in manual shot blast operations becomes dust and is, therefore, no longer usable. This produces a lot of waste material," outlined Ben Luizink. "Another drawback of manual blast operations is that the sand remains in tight corners of the workpieces and is, therefore, lost. In automated shot blasting machines, the entire quantity of thrown blast media is collected in the bottom and transported to the media cleaning system with special augers. This ensures much longer usable life of the blast media. Significantly harder blast media can also be used," he added.

In manual blast rooms, the compressed air needed for the blast operation is generated by compressors requiring high energy input. In the new Rösler shot blast machine, the blast media is accelerated by direct-drive electric turbines.

Another important energy-saving feature in the Rösler machine are the Rösler Gamma G turbines with their curved throwing blades. Because of the curved blade design, the blast media leaves the throwing blades at a higher speed without additional energy requirements. This results in higher impact velocity of the media on the workpieces. The energy consumption can be further reduced by intelligent adjustment of the turbine RPM.
