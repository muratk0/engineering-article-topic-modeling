Software offers automatic importation of standard bevels from 3D models

Lantek has added automatic importation of standard bevels from 3D models to its Assembly to Nesting (A2N) function. This new feature, available in Lantek version 44, is designed to help streamline workflows for users working with bevel-edged sheet metal parts typically used in welding preparation.

A2N can detect standard bevel types directly from the 3D geometry and automatically assign the appropriate technology during the import process. This eliminates the need for manual editing in the drawing module and helps reduce preparation time and increase consistency and precision.

Support for imperial units, bend deduction, and k-factor tables in the interface also have been added.

Solid MIG wire designed for easy slag removal

ESAB has launched OK Autrod 42 LSW, a copper-coated ER70S-9 solid wire with a chemistry engineered to create minimal slag and silica islands on the weld bead. The wire is available in diameters of 0.035, 0.040, and 0.045 in. and comes in standard spool weights of 39 lbs. or the Marathon Pac bulk wire system (551 lbs.).

According to the manufacturer, the few slag islands produced by the wire are manganese and titanium oxides, which helps make cleanup easier before the weldment moves to galvanizing or painting.

The company reports it developed the wire especially for automative, yellow goods, and similar single-pass welding applications in which paint and corrosion protection are applied after welding.

Compact vertical band saw designed for metalworking

Grizzly Industrial has released the G0994 10-in. variable-speed vertical band saw for making angled cuts in metal. The precision-ground cast-iron table tilts up to 45 degrees for bevel cuts. Laser welding system requires no laser protection cell

Scansonic has introduced the SCW (Safe Component Welding) laser welding optic that meets Class 1 laser requirements, eliminating the need for a laser protection cell. A form nozzle encloses the welding area, preventing the escape of laser radiation. It is customized to the geometry of each workpiece to help ensure protection for operators without additional housing. The new welding optic is based on the company’s FCW system. An integrated image recognition system covers a working area of 5 by 25 mm, allowing users to determine the welding position using a teach-in procedure.
