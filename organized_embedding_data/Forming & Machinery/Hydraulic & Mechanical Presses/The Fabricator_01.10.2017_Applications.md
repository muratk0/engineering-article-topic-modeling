Feeder control changes help roll former achieve production requirements

SITUATION

Omco, a custom roll former based in Wickliffe, Ohio, produces 100 million to 120 million lbs. of processed steel per year. The company uses 50 roll forming lines and more than 50 stamping presses at four U.S. locations to manufacture highly engineered shapes for a variety of markets.

Omco faced a challenge with its feeder control. Designed by Coe Press Equipment, a manufacturer of machinery for progressive die and stamping applications, the control was suited to stamping operations, which typically use a single feed link and repeat the same pattern over and over.

"Because of this, the feeder control was limited to 99 progressions, as engineers never envisioned a need for anything beyond this by way of standard controls," said Roger Reed, corporate plant engineer. "But we had situations where we needed 100."

There also was an issue associated with putting material through a roll former and having it stretch to different lengths depending on yield strength. "Instead of 50 in., it might come through at 49.85 in., so there was some slight variation," said Reed.

RESOLUTION

To overcome these hurdles, Omco asked Coe to add a feature to its controls to handle more than 100 progressions. In addition, as part of the controls package, Coe developed a stretch compensation program so that when something is slightly out of tolerance, machine operators can simply input a single percentage that will automatically adjust the program.

Now the roll forming controls can handle more than 100 feed lengths in one part with single-touch stretch compensation that automatically adjusts for out-of-tolerance material.

In addition, because of the need for increasingly higher levels of precision, Omco requires technology to help it achieve those tolerances quickly. Most recently Coe added the ability to program a part via absolute positioning to help the roll former produce difficult parts consistently and achieve on-time delivery.

Pallet rack storage manufacturer achieves cost savings with filtration system

SITUATION

North East, Pa.-based Ridg-U-Rak produces more than 100 million lbs. of pallet rack, high-density storage rack, and specialized rack systems every year. As part of the production process, the company uses steel roll forming, and this machinery requires chemical coolant to ensure smooth and efficient operation.

Despite the use of this lubricant, fine metal particles 0 to 50 microns in size came off during the process and contaminated the coolant. As the coolant was being returned to the open fluid tank, the particulate built up in the tank, requiring it to be regularly vacuumed out. Employees had to remove the contamination and clean the tank every three months, at a cost of $3,000 each time.

Not only did the fines build up in the tank, but they also damaged the roll form tooling. Very small ferrous particulates in the coolant adhered to the surface of the rolls and built up over time, until the rolls in high-pressure areas had to be resurfaced or replaced.

RESOLUTION

Ridg-U-Rak chose an Auto-Mag Skid magnetic filtration system from Eclipse Magnetics. The unit, which has a self-contained filtration and fluid recovery system, is fully automated to minimize fluid loss during cleaning.

It removes magnetic and paramagnetic contamination, down to submicron size, from coolants and lubricants and holds the contaminants until they are released during the automated purge process, which briefly diverts the filter’s purged output so that fluid carries all the contamination to the buffer tank. The buffer tank then feeds the contaminated fluid to the high-intensity magnetic coolant roller, which removes the contamination from the fluid and puts clean fluid back into circulation. The contamination is extracted in a form ready for disposal or recycling.

The fluid tank now needs to be cleaned out only once a year, compared to four times a year before the filter was installed. Ridg-U-Rak estimates the savings are about $5,000 in tanker expenses alone. Quality of the product also has improved as a result of cleaner fluid and thus cleaner tooling. Previously tooling lasted for about two years. The magnetic filtration system has been installed for 18 months with tooling in constant use, and there has been no need to regrind to remove pressureadhered contamination on the tool profile.

Ridg-U-Rak estimates its total savings to be near $20,000 per year without any tooling savings factored in.
