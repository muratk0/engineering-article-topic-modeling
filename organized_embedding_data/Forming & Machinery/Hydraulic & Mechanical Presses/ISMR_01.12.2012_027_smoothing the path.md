# SMOOTHING THE PATH

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

LARGER PARTS on the same press: this is the benefit offered by Schuler’s innovative transfer system unveiled at the EuroBLECH fair in Hanover, Germany, from 23 – 27 October 2012. Dubbed the Intra Trans, it conveys parts along the x-axis – i.e. the feed direction – using movable carriages, whose servo drives are integrated into the transfer rails. The parts are handled by grippers mounted on the carriages. The distance between stations is generally dictated by the space requirements of the largest forming stage.

“As each carriage can be moved individually, this even allows variable distances between stations,” explained Rolf Cisar, Head of Transfer Systems at Schuler Automation in Hessdorf. “This means that the number of stations can be increased or that a smaller press can be used.”

The new transfer system also means material can be fed from both sides enabling, for example, the central joining of parts: “It opens up the possibility for completely new manufacturing processes,” added Rolf Cisar.

Its drive solution ensures that the transfer rails no longer work through the press window – as with conventional solutions – but between the press uprights in the feed direction. This means that the full width of the window is available for material feeding and is no longer restricted by the transfer rails. A major benefit of the Intra Trans’ compact design is that larger parts can be produced on the same press – a key argument when purchasing or modernising a press line.

“We are currently observing a trend away from progressive die and towards transfer technology,” explained Cisar. “In contrast to conventional transfer systems, progressive die enables the use of the complete width of the press window for material feeding. The downside is that it involves more scrap as parts are transported from station to station by the coil material itself. This means that additional material has to be subsequently removed from the finished part as scrap. In view of rising metal prices and the growing use of expensive high-strength steels, efficient coil processing is becoming increasingly important.”

In the past, however, changing from progressive die to transfer technology generally meant that users could only produce much smaller parts – as the rails of conventional transfers work through the press side window – or that production had to be moved to a new, larger press which offered enough space for the transfer but incurred higher purchase and manufacturing costs.

“The compact design of the new Intra Trans now makes changing to transfer technology much easier,” explained Cisar.

For the operator, the die can be accessed just as easily as with conventional transfer solutions. During die changes, the transfer rails are placed on brackets and moved out of the press together with the die on a moving bolster or die change plate. The new transfer solution features an active vibration dampening (AVD) system for exact and controlled part transport.
