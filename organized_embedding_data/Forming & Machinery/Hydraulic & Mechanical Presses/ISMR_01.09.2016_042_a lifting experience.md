# A lifting experience

[TARİH: 01.09.2016 ISMR]

DT ENGINEERING NW recently installed a Goliath crane on top of an overhead electric travelling crane in a car plant's press shop, without taking off roof parts. This enables essential repair and maintenance work to be carried out on the flywheels that form part of the press.

The crane manufacturer and lifting equipment specialist, which is based in the North West of England, had occasionally worked with German business Schuler, which manufactures and maintains automotive presses, including those used by the car manufacturer.

Tom Coyle, managing director at DT Engineering NW, explained: “Two companies that had previously quoted explained that the project could simply not be carried out without sections of the roof coming off and two mobile cranes doing the lifts there. For the client, cost and time were the main concern. We were invited, two weeks before the start date, to quote.”

Coyle explained that the four flywheels that form part of the automotive press needed to be removed for maintenance and the only access point for these was at the top of the machine.

“It was agreed that our job could be done. We were saying ‘yes, it can be done,' when the others said 'no'. The fact that we had worked with Schuler before ensured the client had that extra confidence in us. The main concerns were timelines given by our suppliers. We needed a 15t wire electric hoist with HOL (Height of Lift) of 15m—and fast. So we got on with stripping the press and positioned the Street Crane manufactured overhead crane to give us space to pull out the fly wheels, which are around 3m wide diameter and weighed 13t. As clearance out of the access hatch was only 3 or 4mm, precision was key."

DT Engineering NW employs 21 staff and has over 30 years of experience in the manufacture, service and maintenance of mechanical handling equipment, including factory cranes, jib cranes, wire rope and chain hoists on a day-to-day basis. Coyle added that there was an inevitable variety of safety concerns to keep in mind.

“The installation period lasted approximately two-and-a-half days, and we were happy with this," said Coyle.

The successful project took place during August 2016 with the Goliath due for decommissioning at the end of that month. For further details, see www.dtengineering.org.uk

## Feel the force

GOUDSMIT Magnetic Design BV recently started supplying special flexible magnetic belts and magnetic systems from strong Nd-Fe-B (Neodymium) magnetic material. These systems have been designed to allow objects to be fixed to steel ceilings and walls in spaces including hygiene areas in hospitals, clean rooms, rooms with climate ceilings, offices and shops.

"Gaps and holes in surfaces located in these areas are prohibited, ruling out the option of drilling and screwing. For this, magnets offer the ideal solution. Neodymium magnetic material is five times stronger than standard ferrite magnetic material. Flexible magnetic belts ensure strong, secure fixing of objects such as smoke detectors, curtain rails and cable holders. For heavier objects we offer a magnetic system with a rubber seal, which helps to prevent scratches on ceilings and walls," explained the manufacturer.

The flexible magnetic belt is magnetised on one side with multiple poles. The nonmagnetic side features a strong double-sided self-adhesive. Rubber-encased Neodymium magnetic systems contain a double-sided self-adhesive or screw hole for easy fixing (available from 31mm to 8mm diameter). See www.goudsmitmagnets.com
