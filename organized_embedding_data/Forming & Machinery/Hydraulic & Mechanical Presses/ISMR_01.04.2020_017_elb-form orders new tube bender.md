# ELB-Form orders new tube bender

[TARİH: 01.04.2020 ISMR]

FOR AUSTRIAN COMPANY ELB-Form, a simple order for a new machine from Schwarze-Robitec ended in a huge increase in productivity and efficiency, it told ISMR. The fully electric multi-radius tube bending machine, CNC 80 E TB MR, offers extremely short cycle times for the specialists in cold and hot forming.

“The key to this productivity gain is the intelligent NxG control system. Production time is reduced by 20 to 40 per cent, depending on the component and the desired tube geometry. It ensures extremely tight tolerances, even with complex tube geometries for confined spaces, for the production of exhaust tubes and systems for ELB-Form," Schwarze-Robitec explained.

ELB-Form, an Austrian company based in Vandans (Vorarlberg), serves customers in automotive and aviation industries, among others. It is currently using a tube bending machine with NxG control for the first time.

“For each bending sequence, the system automatically checks the interaction between all axes and ensures the movement sequences are optimally coordinated. This means that whilst a bending process is ongoing, the axes are already being prepared for the next sequences. The production time is reduced by 20 to 40 per cent depending on the component and the desired tube geometry – the more complex the geometry of the tube to be bent, the more time is saved. The factory acceptance test carried out for the machine demonstrated the benefits of ELB-Form. For one particular application, the time required for a single bending process was reduced from 35 to 16 seconds. This is a major advantage for suppliers in the automotive industry, where turnover times are key," added Schwarze-Robitec.

The new machine is also helping ELB-Form to reduce production time with faster toolchanging times using the Quick-Tool-Unlock system for quick and easy assembly and disassembly with no additional equipment. Users no longer having to adjust the tensions bars themselves – e.g. to make them tighter or looser – which increases the level of repeatability and enhances process security. Schwarze-Robitec implemented a client specific log-in system, seamlessly linking the machine to other internal company processes such as clocking in and out of work.

ELB-Form is now planning to retrofit the NxG control system on an additional machine in its inventory. See www.schwarze-robitec.com

## 3D printing

3D printers are now being used in manufacturing. They are revolutionising spare parts management and opening the door to new designs for complex components. Lasers are increasingly being used in places where objects were previously forged, milled or cast. Laser beam welding creates objects layer by layer. It is also now possible to 3D-print a wide variety of metal parts in aluminium, high-grade steel, titanium as well as nickel and cobalt alloys.

3D printing has been around since the 1980s. Originally, only rapid-hardening plastic was used for the process, which was ideal for making prototype parts that would later be mass-produced by conventional stamping or injection moulding machines.

"What does a 3D printer do? There are two major technologies within 3D printing; they are selective laser sintering and plastic extrusion. Plastic extrusion technology consists of a plastic filament that runs through a hot-end and heats it up and melts plastic while a stepper motor pushes that plastic through a nozzle. This is an ideal way to print plastic products. Selective laser sintering shoots a laser into a pile of powder that fuses it into a solid shape creating the desired product. Selective laser sintering is often referred to as SLS. Commonly, SLS is used more in industrial and commercial cases, while plastic extrusion is more limited to plastic use," explained Technavio.

The global 3D printing industry forecast is promising, the market is moderately fragmented with 3D printing companies market share currently occupied by several key players. ## Metal AM outlook

Bound metal additive manufacturing technologies have gained significant interest and investment dollars over the last three years and are being developed by the likes of HP; Desktop Metal; GE Additive; ExOne and more. They are now expected to achieve close to twice the rate of growth compared to the overall metal additive manufacturing market over the next ten years, according to the latest from SmarTech Analysis.

“Steels are expected to be the primary material of choice, later augmented by nickel alloys, titanium and potentially aluminium. Steels are currently the most popular material in this early stage, in part due to the high level of sintering expertise possessed in manufacturing communities for these parts and their general cost-effectiveness. Aluminium AM solutions are in high demand across technology segments, and players in bound metal technologies stand to gain significant opportunity if reliable aluminium printing and sintering solutions can be brought to market to compete with metal powder bed fusion," explained SmarTech Analysis.
