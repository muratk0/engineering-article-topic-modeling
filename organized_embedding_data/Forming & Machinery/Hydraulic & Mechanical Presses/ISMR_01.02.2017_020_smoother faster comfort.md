# SMOOTHER, FASTER COMFORT

[TARİH: 01.02.2017 ISMR]

Japanese expertise is supplying engineered components for the Shinkansen ‘bullet train' and the UK's Intercity Express Programme

E7 Series bullet (high-speed or Shinkansen) train. This train is operated by JR East and JR West services as Kagayaki (Shine) for the Hokuriku Shinkansen line (Tokyo - Kanazawa route).

Japan's main islands of Honshu, Kyushu and Hokkaido are served by a network of high-speed train lines which connect Tokyo with most of the country's major cities. Japan's high-speed trains (bullet trains) are called Shinkansen (新幹線) and are operated by Japan Railways (JR). The world's first high-speed commercial train line is now over 50 years old. The term "Shinkansen" literally means "new trunk line".

The new Tokyo terminal for the latest incarnation of the bullet train – the Maglev, or Chuo ("central") Shinkansen - is due to connect Tokyo to Nagoya by 2027 and is being built 40m underground. The Maglev is the next technological stage in the evolution of high-speed rail travel in Japan.

The Shinkansen network consists of multiple lines, amongst which the Tokaido Shinkansen (Tokyo - Nagoya - Kyoto - Osaka) is the oldest and most popular. All Shinkansen lines (except the Akita and Yamagata Shinkansen) run on tracks that are exclusively built for and used by Shinkansen trains. Most lines are served by multiple train categories, ranging from the fastest category, which stops only at major stations, to the slowest category that stops at every station along the way.

## Shinkansen interior equipment

Running at speeds of up to 320km/h, the Shinkansen is known for punctuality (most trains depart on time to the second), comfort (relatively silent cars with spacious, always forward-facing seats), safety (no fatal accidents in its history) and efficiency. Using the Japan Rail Pass, the Shinkansen can also be a very cost-effective means of travel.

Hiromoku Giken Co., Ltd. manufactures train car components, unit assemblies and fittings for the passenger transportation industry. The company was founded in 1950 and is based in Kudamatsu-shi, Japan. It manufactures parts used in the interior of the Shinkansen train carriage, such as the corner panels of the entrance vestibule of the Shinkansen and limited express service trains, switch boards on the platform, equipment room units, inner access doors and ceiling and drift panels in the Gran passenger class.

"When it comes to manufacturing train parts, we receive orders from five of the main train manufacturers in Japan to produce the interior panels that are used in the vestibule of a Shinkansen carriage. The passenger transportation industry strives to achieve energy conservation and low noise while ensuring great comfort. As part of the industry, our company devotes our entire energy to manufacturing high quality products using the technology that we have acquired over the years and the latest equipment," explained Yoshiaki Hironaka, President of Hiromoku Giken Co., Ltd.

"We can expect the demand for railway trains in Japan to remain strong for the time being. The global rail market is said to generate 20 trillion yen every year, but the markets that can be expected to have high growth are Europe, which is in the lead with a 50% market share, North America and emerging countries such as China and Southeast Asia."

R-shaped corner panels for the vestibule of new trains are manufactured by Hiromoku Giken Co., Ltd. The processing work for the corner panel has become increasingly difficult, reported Hironaka, as the panel uses a core of aluminium alloy in a four- or six-sided honeycomb structure, which is sandwiched between two thin aluminium panels, measuring 0.5mm.

"Our strength is in manufacturing various R-shaped corner panels, from processing the panel material for the honeycomb to its assembly. The manufacture of train parts has a lead time of three to four months. There is always work in progress in the factory, including the sheet metal process. We installed five machines for the blanking and bending process in sheet metal processing including a punch and laser combination machine, EML-TK, and the latest bending machine, HG-1703," added Hironaka.

Hiromoku Giken equipped its new AMADA EML-TK with a take-out loader (TK) and inkjet printer (IJP) on the two joint shelves. Materials are blanked, based on their quality and thickness, and processed into sheets, which are then sorted into different products and transported out of the machine via the TK. The car type and working diagram number are printed on the sheet material in advance, using the IJP based on the blanking information, making it easy to identify the sheets by product after they are sorted.

"We will continue to add and update our equipment while meeting the growing demand for railway trains. We are also interested in installing a fibre laser machine for cutting and welding," added Hironaka.

## Japanese expertise for UK IEP project

Hironaka's main customer, Hitachi, Ltd., won the order for the large IEP (Intercity Express Programme) project in the UK to produce trains and the operation system, as well as provide maintenance service for 27.5 years. Hitachi has built a rail vehicle manufacturing facility in the UK and acquired a railway-related company in Italy, further accelerating its overseas business.

“We contributed to the production of the three trains in the initial batch manufactured in Japan for IEP. In the future, we plan to continue manufacturing the switch board unit and other parts that are procured from Japan,” added Hironaka.

As a key player in the development of the Shinkansen bullet train vehicles in Japan, Hitachi has taken part in all Shinkansen rolling stock projects. In 2015, Hitachi, Ltd. made remarkable efforts to expand its railway system business globally. In January 2015, the company began shipment of trains manufactured in Kasado Works in Kudamatsu City, Yamaguchi Prefecture, for the UK Intercity Express Programme (IEP). Hitachi won the order for the large-scale IEP project worth approximately 1 trillion yen in July 2012, and received additional orders in July 2013.

Under the contract, the company will build a total of 866 carriages (122 trains) and provide maintenance service for 27.5 years. 76 carriages were manufactured in Kasado Works, while production for the remaining 790 was started in November 2015 in a factory in County Durham, UK. For this important step change in train travel in the UK, Hitachi Rail Europe will provide new electric and bi-mode trains for both the Great Western Main Line and the East Coast Main Line.

“Our trains are a combination of fully electric and bi-mode trains - the latter have diesel and electric propulsion. The trains can travel at up to 125mph, in various formations from five to ten cars. IEP will provide an efficient means for passengers to travel on electrified and non-electrified routes, as well as enhancing the customer experience with more modern trains, better interiors and seating arrangements, and of course, faster journey times,” said Hitachi.

Kentaro Masai, Vice President and Executive Officer and COO of Railway Systems Business Unit from Hitachi, Ltd., explained his part procurement philosophy for trains that are produced in factories in the UK and Italy.

“In principle, we will follow the concepts of ‘local production for local consumption’ and ‘material procurement from the most suitable place’. We choose the best parts based on a variety of factors, including the reliability and price of the parts. For the parts, we follow the local production for local consumption method essentially to either ‘create parts in the most suitable location’ or ‘procure the best material regardless of the location (price)’. To that end, we also have different policies based on the type and characteristic of the parts when it comes to material procurement. What we basically do is select and use parts with the highest quality and most reasonable price to the customer,” he explained.
