# PACKING A PUNCH What are the parameters that influence the cost of a punched part?

[TARİH: 01.11.2013 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“You can save time and money by applying the right punching tool concept”

BY: Gerhard Krammel, Product Manager, Punching Tool Construction, Meusburger GmbH & Co KG

Many punch toolmakers make their tools by always applying the same concept. Even if the parameters (which depend upon the quantity produced each year) change, the same punching tool concept is maintained. But what are the parameters that influence the cost of a punched part, depending upon the quantity produced?

In punching, speed and precision are key issues. It is important that the cost per individual part is as low as possible to be competitive, particularly for large quantities. The following criteria encapsulate the performance-related parameters:-

Number of strokes: The larger the quantity produced, the more important is it to select the optimum number of strokes. Attention should be paid to the fact that the highest number of strokes is not always the best; a higher speed increases the risk of tool breakage and damage. The priority objective is to maximise the parts output per shift at the required quality standard.

Automation: The larger the quantity produced per year, the more important is a quick coil change. This can be achieved by welding together the strip ends using strip welders. It is also important to relieve the machine operators of their workload by automating peripheral devices. The time saved can be used to inspect the produced parts.

Positioning systems: Positioning systems are very important when the tool is changed frequently. The set-up time can be reduced by preparing the equipment that is required for changing the tool beforehand, while the machine is still in operation. Positioning systems help to avoid costly machine downtime, even if not many tool changes are required.

Use of standard parts: Standard parts are very important, in many ways, for producing small and large quantities.

They ensure easy exchangeability.

They can be quickly replaced.

There is a price advantage when producing large quantities.

Tool costs: The influence of the tool costs per punched part is reduced, with increasing annual quantities, because tool costs can be divided by the number of parts produced.

Depending upon the quantity, different tool concepts should be used to achieve the optimum production cost/tooling cost ratio. The following criteria help users to select the optimum tool concept:-

Geometry of the parts: The complexity of the parts’ geometry determines the number of process sequences and, consequently, the tools’ dimensions.

Punching machine: The size of the table and the tonnage are crucial factors for deciding if one or more punching processes are required for producing the part.

Strength and thickness of the material: This determines which material is used for the active components such as cutting and bending punches.

Accuracy of the components: The use of ball or sliding guide bushes may influence the components’ accuracy. This can also be increased by using an SP precision die set where the guide pillars are fixed in the centre.

Criteria which help users to select the optimum tool concept

## Punching and bending operations:

Depending upon the complexity of the punching and bending operations, slides or other additional components are required.

Quantity per year: The quantity of the parts produced per year determines if a simple tool can be used or if an optimised tool concept is required.

Frequency of set-up processes: For frequent set-up, it makes sense to invest more time and money in optimising the work stages to reduce fitting time e.g. by preparing the equipment that is required for changing the tool beforehand while the machine is still in operation.

To aid understanding, we can explain the criteria by taking the specific example of a progressive die (second column from the left). In this specific case, the available machine, with a table of 400mm x 650mm, could be used. Because of the material’s thickness (0.6mm) and the accuracy of the components, a ball guiding system was used. An SP precision die set, where the guide pillars are fixed in the centre, was also chosen. Because of the low complexity of the part’s geometry and the low number of process sequences required, it was possible to use a simple progressive die.

The small quantity, in combination with a high number of small jobs, made it necessary to use the right positioning systems such as a tool centring device which helped to reduce clamping time. Keeping the same strip inlet height increased the efficiency because more than one tool could be used on the same machine.

In summary, selecting the right tool concept is decisive for achieving competitive advantage as early as the tender stage. In the final analysis, the decision on the best tooling concept depends, above all, on the products and the customer’s requirements. ■
