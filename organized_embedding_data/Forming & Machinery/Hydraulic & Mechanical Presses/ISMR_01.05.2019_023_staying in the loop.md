# STAYING IN THE LOOP

[TARİH: 01.05.2019 ISMR]

Servopresse has made innovation the backbone of its business, creating a new servo laser line that combines the benefits of fibre laser cutting with the productivity of coil production systems.

Servopresse Srl, a family-run coil processing specialist in Italy with over 48 years of experience, offers a complete range of coil lines. Established in 1970 to design and produce equipment to process coil material for presses, shears, forming machines and machines that use sheet metal coils, today it is one of the leading European manufacturers in this sector.

The company's production range covers coil up to a maximum capacity of 20 tons, widths of up to 2000mm and thicknesses of up to 20mm. It has placed a keen focus on line customisation, automation and processing of high-strength materials, in line with its customers' requirements and the trends it has identified in coil processing markets.

Servopresse can build single machines, compact lines and special lines featuring decoilers (single or double, with or without motors) and hydraulic self-centering expansion; straighteners and electronic rollfeeders. Its traditional lines feature decoilers and straighteners used with an independent feeder. Its compact lines include integrated decoilers and feeder straighteners. Special lines can be built to meet sophisticated working requirements such as zig-zag feeding lines; straightener cradles; complete cutting lines; micro-perforation and embossing coil lines as well as automatic multi-disc cutting lines from coil.

## Aims and objectives

"We want to keep growing and producing as many machines as possible, whilst staying connected to the market's needs and requirements. Last year, we made three million euros in turnover. We hope to make a 5-10% increase on turnover this year. Every year, since the global economic downturn, our turnover has been better than the year before and we have emerged from the downturn with much stronger and durable relationships with our customers," explained Servopresse's marketing manager, Lara Cecchi

Staying connected is a key theme for Servopresse, which quickly identified a growing need for line automation and greater software connectivity amongst its customers some time ago. It uses Siemens control technology in all its lines, benefitting from Siemens' encompassing approach to Industry-4.0 principles for industrial control technology.

## Orders and deliveries

Around 90% of Servopresse's production is sold outside Italy through a network of technically-qualified distributors able to offer customers installation services and post sales assistance/support.

"Our customers tend to stay with us for a long time and stick with our machines. Some 85-90% of our turnover goes to export, with around 5-10% staying in the Italian market. Our best market in Europe right now is Poland, which is attracting a lot of investment. We have made many deliveries to this country. Last December, we also delivered a compact machine for heavy-duty material to ZF in Mexico," Lara Cecchi told ISMR.

Servopresse is currently delivering a comprehensive line to a roll forming manufacturer. This is a compact line for feeding and welding band head – tail. The coil handling equipment has been designed to reduce time and cost on the cutting and profiling lines. It features decoiling, feeding and straightening equipment, with the welding head-tail positioned at the end of the feeder-straightener.

"The head-tail welding system has numerous benefits, especially for complicated progressive moulds and transfer systems. This is because, by using this unit, the stoppage, re-start and coil change time in the production line can be drastically reduced," added Cecchi.

## New servo laser line

"A company's ability to stay on the market is measured by its ability to renew its offer by incorporating input from suppliers and customers, but above all by the strength to innovate, creating opportunities that were not there before and industrialising a production process that allows it to customise solutions based on the needs of individual customers."

Servopresse has made the above philosophy the backbone of its business, creating its new servo laser line that combines the benefits of fibre laser cutting with the productivity of coil production systems. The manufacturer wanted to create a solution for large trimmings in moderate quantities. The servo laser can be used for prototypes, pre-production batches or the production of niche vehicle components.

"It is important to understand what the market needs. In my opinion, productivity in coil processing and flexible production are key. Material costs keep rising and quantity batches keep reducing, so a machine is needed that offers the right production flexibility to customers. That is why we have launched our servo laser system. It is the first time that we have incorporated a laser feeder in our lines," Lara Cecchi told ISMR.

"Working from coil is like working with a sheet of infinite length, as well as offering the advantage of a range of production that is no longer limited by the capacity of the sheet metal press. To enable users to do this, we have developed our new coil-cut servolaser machine.

"The servo laser line has been designed to offer flexibility and productivity to companies wishing to increase their production capacity. Working from coil means that you remove an important constraint for the nesting algorithm and can reduce material scrap rates by up to 10%. A precision tape guide avoids trimming, so the width of the tape can be matched to the size of the pieces to be produced. A further saving on the raw material costs can be made by purchasing coil rather than sheet," she added.

With the new servo laser, the 'dead time spent loading the sheet is eliminated as the coil is only changed when it is necessary to change the type of material.

"Coil laser technology reduces energy consumption, compared to alternative systems, and is more efficient and flexible. The fibre laser source offers excellent cutting quality and a significant reduction in operating costs. The laser system is able to cut detailed workpieces to the desired lengths using the feeder (which enables pieces to be cut automatically and continuously) so that greater efficiency is achieved in the production process. This also ensures raw material savings, consistent operational working patterns and cost savings," explained Lara Cecchi.

"This machine, therefore, enhances the concept of strip processing by cutting faster than a press and with considerable production autonomy, thanks to continuous feeding and optimal management of cut piece unloading.

Fiber laser, by its nature, is simple to manage and does not require an optical path for the beam transport, which simplifies assembly and means lower maintenance costs (as there are no focal lenses to get dirty or deteriorate). This translates to cost savings, as well as significantly lower energy consumption than that expended by CO₂ lasers.

"There are also less well-known operating advantages such as the fact that, on low material thicknesses (typical of the insulation and sheet metal sectors), the fibre laser triggers the cut better and works more quickly, as well as being able to cut different materials. Bands of variable length (generally from 500mm) can be cut," explained Cecchi.

The Servopresse servo laser can also be used as a cut-to-length line. It is therefore possible to produce sheets with the dimensions required by other machines. It is suitable for processing strip from 0.3mm to 4mm thick, without length limitations. It can also cut a range of different materials such as mild steel; high-strength steel; aluminum and copper.

"The servo laser line is particularly effective on long parts with non-straight contours or complex pieces. It lends itself well to the production of records. It is ideal for OEMs and operators in the cold forging sector. With this line, subcontractors can also find more efficient solutions while maintaining the flexibility inherent in their profession," concluded Cecchi.

Servopresse will be showcasing the servolaser line on its booth D98 (hall 13) at the forthcoming LAMIERA sheet metal exhibition in Milan from 15-18 May 2019. It will feature a compact coil line of 1000mm wide, able to handle coils of 8 tons with a range of thicknesses from 0.6 to 6mm (specialised in working with stainless steel). This will be connected to a laser feeder cutting line – the 1kW fibre laser on the version at LAMIERA is able to cut 1.5mm thickness. Together, they make up the servo laser line. It will also be displaying a compact line for press stamping – a 400 x4mm-wide full optional version of the servo laser line (with 4-ton decoiler) which can process 0.6-6mm thicknesses at LAMIERA.
