# The grinding equation

[TARİH: 01.06.2016 ISMR]

THE VOLLMER stand at the recent MACH 2016 exhibition in the UK was a hive of activity with a new machine receiving its UK exhibition debut. The Vgrind 160 was launched to offer greater capacity and flexibility and was showcased alongside the Vollmer CHC840 grinding machine and the Loroch K850-T for processing circular saw blades.

The new Vgrind 160 received its world premiere at the Grindtec exhibition in March and was flown fresh from its global premiere to MACH 2016. The Vgrind 160 was sold on the second day of MACH to a UK cutting tool manufacturer.

"Compared to its predecessor, the new Vgrind 160 can now process tools of up to 50mm diameter and also has an upgraded automation system. The previous Vgrind machine had a capacity for two pallets whereas the new configuration with a Fanuc robot can more than triple capacity. This impressive automation system will also change the collets in the machine for the automatic unmanned processing of multiple diameter tools," Vollmer explained.

"In addition, the new Vgrind has an 8-wheel tool-changer system and a tool length capacity of up to 265mm (200mm grinding length) with a steady support. The previous model offered a 6-wheel tool-changer system and a maximum tool length of 160mm. It is these new additions, along with the option of motorised grinding spindles for processing PCD tool flanks, that sold the machine off the stand."

EWM'S FIRST "Innovation Day" was reportedly a great success for Germany's largest arc welding technology manufacturer. On 12 April 2016, some 100 delegates visited Handwerkskammer Koblenz to find out about the new "forceArc puls" welding process, in particular, and other EWM offerings. They were shown five presentations and several welding demonstrations.

Under the spotlight was the new "forceArc puls" welding process, which combines the strengths of the "forceArc" process with the benefits of pulsed arc. This EWM innovation enables wide applicability, low heat input and higher welding speeds. "There are substantial benefits for customers," stated Susanne Szczesny-Oßing, President of EWM AG's Supervisory Board,"because 'forceArc puls'both protects resources and generates significant cost savings."

Opportunities to reduce costs were also the main focus of the other Innovation Day talks. Boyan Ivanov, Head of Process Engineering, EWM, reported on the commercial benefits of the new welding process and the potential savings from fillet welding with deep penetration under DIN EN 1090. His colleague Hassan Lakhnati, director of the Technology and Mechanisation Centre in Weinheim, talked about the efficiency benefits of EWM's technology when applied in mechanised welding in equipment and pipe construction. And Benedict Menningen, responsible for EWM's software service, showed how modern software can be used to implement continuous quality assurance during welding.
