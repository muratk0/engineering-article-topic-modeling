# INVEST FOR SUCCESS

[TARİH: 01.06.2019 ISMR]

"Gregory Wilfred, YSM's head of marketing and business development, unveils its latest strategic initiatives and new technology launches"

YSM has made significant investments in new product development, including a new technology and demonstration centre in India and an expanded presence in Europe.

Yih Shen Machinery Co., LTD. (YSM) was set up in 1979 to manufacture machines for cutting, coiling and forming metal springs. Today, it is a global specialist in forming machinery, including stamping and bending lines.

At the TIMTOS exhibition in Taiwan earlier this year, the company showcased its YSM "NC series" (Servo) cam-less, servo-driven stamping and bending machine. It is capable of speeds of up to 250 strokes/min. with slides that can be aligned in either radial or linear arrangements.

ISMR spoke with Gregory Wilfred, YSM's head of marketing and business development, to uncover its latest strategic initiatives and technology launches.

ISMR: Please give us a brief background to Yih Shen Machinery (YSM)?

GW: YSM has been in business for forty years. We started as a spring forming machine tool manufacturer and then moved onto making specialised stamping and bending lines for customers. We have now progressed from making machines to produce sheet metal parts to specialised modules for integrating welding, tapping/ threading as additional in-line operations. Our machines have therefore evolved to fully fledged manufacturing lines which can offer secondary operations, as well as automation, to customers.

We have a three-tier structure with a dedicated manufacturing and R&D/design facility in Taichung, Taiwan, with about 50 employees. We also have a large office in Pune (India), with over 20 employees. This is also where we have built a new technology and demonstration centre for the Indian, south east Asian and Middle Eastern markets. We also have an office in Indonesia and, by the end of the year, we aim to have dedicated offices in Europe.

Our global group turnover is in the region of US$ 14-15 million and we produce around 200- 220 machines per year.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements and the reasons for this?

GW: There are two main achievements that I would like to highlight. One is our entry into the European market where we have begun dedicated service, sales and technical support for our European customers. We will be delivering end-to-end solutions with the support of teams locally and in Taiwan.

Secondly, we have developed two new machines especially for the European market which I will describe later. We are still operating through our European partner companies in Italy and in Austria. By the end of this year, we will have a dedicated support team in Europe for the European market and we aim to set up offices and representatives for sales, service, support and technical assistance in Europe.

A recent new success for us is the building of a new Technology Centre in Pune, India, which will be officially opened at the end of October this year and will serve Indian and Asian markets. It will feature showcases for machine assembly and maintenance, tooling, training and design.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

GW: Over the last two years, we have seen steady 20% growth year-on-year. This growth has been consistent over the past five years. In terms of the sheet metal business climate, I would say that it slowed down towards the end of last year, but we are now seeing growth from our Tier One and OEM customers in the switchgear, electric and automotive industries. These sectors are picking up now and we expect to see further growth this year and over the next three years.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers and how are you addressing these issues for them?

GW: Two things are of key importance for our customers – one is the speed of delivery and the second is the accuracy and quality of the parts that they can produce.

To help our customers, we do not wait for them to order a machine and then build it after receipt of order. Instead, we have a base inventory that we manufacture every year and keep ready where machines (around 60% finished) are kept in stock. We can then do the final integrations and modifications that the customer requires on these machines in stock. We stock a wide variety of machines that cater for their needs. We work on a modular design principle, so we can put the correct modules together to meet customer requirements in the final stages of machine assembly and integration. This reduces overall time to market and gives the customer 'a jump start' for their production operations.

In terms of part quality, we build our machines to current accepted Japanese and European CE standards. For example, our machines can produce parts for circuit breakers. We are already delivering machines to European automotive and electrical customers.

By the end of this year, we will have a dedicated support team in Europe for the European market.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

GW: Over the next three years, our goal is to further improve our R&D and new product development activities. The ultimate objective is to ensure multiple processes/operations on a single machine to enable our customers to carry out all necessary operations on a single manufacturing line.

We consistently aim to grow our turnover year-on-year, by around 20%. We have managed that over the last three years and intend to continue in that vein this year. We aim to do this by finding new customers, entering new markets and investing continuously in new product development.

ISMR: What is your approach to Industry 4.0?

GW: Industry 4.0 has been a great gamechanger. Over the last two years, we have been working towards implementing an Industry 4.0 approach. To us, it primarily means automation and the removal of manual and external processes, integrating and automating these processes into the manufacturing line.

In a nutshell, Industry 4.0 is about simplifying processes and providing better output, as well as better quality, for customers. We work with control software suppliers on these issues. Our machines are all IoT-enabled.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal stamping and bending markets?

GW: In the sheet metal stamping and bending market, we have noticed two key trends over the last year (which we have accordingly designed into our machines) - the customer need for high-speed processes in manufacturing, as well as the need for multiple integrated operations.

In response to this need, we have launched flexible new machines that can process a wide range of materials (brass, copper, stainless steel etc.).

ISMR: Which international exhibitions are you attending this year?

GW: We will be at Blechexpo in Stuttgart this November to serve the European market. We are also planning to attend three major exhibitions in India between January and March next year. We will also be at Auto Expo in India next year, as well as Elecrama in India next year. You can also find us at Wire and Tube in Düsseldorf (Germany) next April and, of course, the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition in Hanover next October.

ISMR: Have you launched any new products this year?

GW: We have just introduced a new YSM 36T high-speed, small mechanical stamping and bending machine line (able to run at a speed of 220-250 strokes per minute for single or multiple part production). We aim to launch this over the next 45 days.

We have also launched a new model that is a combination of a servo mechanical stamping and bending line (linear servo-driven and radial mechanical-driven bending station, for complex parts, along with a stamping line). Both these machines will enjoy their European premieres at Blechexpo in Stuttgart this November.

Some of the key features of these manufacturing lines are faster set-up times; suitability for small batch quantities; independently-driven servo slides and a servo stamping press.

ISMR: Are there any new initiatives (domestic or international) that you would like to discuss or highlight?

GW: We recently launched a new project for a customer in the switchgear industry – a combination of stamping, bending, welding and tapping processes into a single machine. We are very proud of this customised combination.
