## Pressing in focus

Taiwanese manufacturer Chin Fong’s new single point servo presses (iLS/DLS series) ranging from 80 – 300 tons; its double-point SDS series from 400 up to 1000 tonnes; the compound link LF and KW series for stampingforging composite processes; the long stroke knuckle joint presses KL and warm-hot forging WF series have facilitated its slogan: “Intelligence of Forming, Vision of Forging”.

Chin Fong now has offices in Taiwan, China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and the USA as well as representatives in numerous countries around the world. Its A0 tandem press line for the automotive industry includes stages such as deep drawing, following by trimming, piercing etc. The basic A0 line weighs 2400 tons with 800 tons of weight for the rest of the machines. To gain ground in production efficiency, Chin Fong replaced the conventional hydraulic press with a mechanical press featuring a link drive and long stroke.

Chin Fong’s straightside double crank power press, with 400-ton capacity, has a bed area of 2.5m x 1m and can produce metal stampings from material measuring up to 400mm wide and 3.5mm thick. It has a variable speed of between 30-50 strokes per minute and a maximum shut height of 450mm.

DEES Hydraulic has long been committed to integrating new technologies and has successfully developed a closed loop, hydraulic, servo-controlled die spotting press. With microprecision control and energy efficiency at the top of its R&D goals, the servo system’s fixedflow control as the core technology used in the hydraulic spotting press, can further improve the precision of the conventional design or overcome the ball-screw’s wear and tear to reduce overall maintenance costs.

“The energy-efficient servo motor can save up to 30% energy. Adopting a pulse wave signal handwheel jog down for micro precision of 0.01mm can prevent damage when spotting the punch and die. The closed loop, servo-hydraulic system can reach high slide repeatability levels of +/- 0.02mm,” explained the manufacturer.

Interlaken Technology, a manufacturer of production press equipment for hydroforming, elevated temperature forming and hot stamping systems, has introduced a new four-column hydroforming press system for running highvolume production while reducing cycle time. Interlaken offers this new hydroforming press system in clamp forces ranging from 250 to 5000 tons.

Hydroformed parts are stronger and weigh less due to structural integrity and fewer welds or add-on pieces. In addition, costs are reduced and time is saved by eliminating secondary operations, reducing scrap, lowering material and manufacturing costs, and increasing design flexibility.

“At room temperature, aluminium and magnesium perform poorly in challenging forming applications. Warm forming of aluminium and magnesium allows for tighter formed radii, better elongation and deep draws that cannot be achieved at room temperature. In some cases, aluminium elongations can be more than 100%,” said the company.

Founded in 1940, Arisa manufacturers a complete line of mechanical and servo presses, along with related coil line automation systems and technical services. “This acquisition provides Minster with a larger and more competitive global footprint in the machinery, automation and services marketplace,” Winch added. “Arisa’s products are a good fit for us since there is little overlap with Nidec Minster’s products. At its Waghäusel site recently, German manufacturer Schuler presented its new PCHflex technology, a further development of the “pressure controlled hardening” process. It is designed to enable flexible and economical production of hot stamped parts with high output performance and consistently high quality combined with process reliability and availability.

“More performance pays off,” said Schuler managing director, Dr. Martin Habert. “In this way, costs and energy consumption per part are reduced.” PCHflex uses rapid hydraulic presses with Dynamic Force Control and RingValve technology. With four parts per stroke (4-out mode), up to four million parts per year can be produced on one line.

By controlling pressure during hardening, the necessary press force can be distributed evenly over the part. The flexible bed cushion ensures – within the part and distributed over several parts – a uniformly high contact pressure, resulting in faster cooling. Cooling time, said Schuler, is only half that of conventional methods, while productivity and part quality are increased.

Often compared to sheet hydroforming presses, Flexform fluid cell presses employ ultra-high pressure to form a wide variety of complex shapes.

The new PCHflex technology can also use existing conventional press hardening dies on these lines. Conversely, dies designed for PCHflex technology can also be used on.

The Minster Group includes a diverse and versatile line-up of material forming solutions, including Minster mechanical and servo presses; high speed Kyori presses, MMC presses and servo presses from Nidec-Shimpo. Coil line and turnkey system products in The Minster Group are available from Minster Automation and Dynamic Products. All products are supported by Nidec Minster’s global customer service organisation.

“With PCHflex, automobile manufacturers and suppliers are more independent of die and material fluctuations for process reasons; different manufacturing tolerances and sheet thicknesses can be compensated for more easily. This reduces scrap as well as downtime and idle losses that would otherwise occur due to the reworking of dies, for instance,” added Schuler.

A US car manufacturer recently placed an order with Schuler for four production lines to manufacture lightweight parts using PCHflex technology. In addition, options have been agreed for four further lines.

Schuler is a specialist in press hardening equipment. Due to growing requirements for passenger protection and the need to reduce CO2 emissions, demand is expected to grow in the coming years. A Hot Stamping TechCentre will therefore be put into operation at the Group’s main site in Göppingen, Germany, in early 2016. It will serve as a demonstration centre as well as a location for training and research on the topic of press hardening.

Schuler AG is streamlining its production in Germany and will only be manufacturing at four facilities in future, instead of the seven at present. New machine manufacturing will be pooled in Erfurt and Göppingen. At the same time, production of presses and new machine components will be discontinued at the company’s sites in Netphen (by the end of 2016), Waghäusel (by the end of 2017) and Weingarten (by the end of 2017). External assembly, commissioning and service will be maintained at all three sites and engineering and support activities will be continued. The planned changes will affect around 450 jobs in Germany in the medium term.
