Integrated, automated bending system works quickly

Amada’s HG 1003 ARs is a fully integrated robotic bending system with a 6-axis robot, automatic gripper changer (AGC), and automatic tool changer (ATC) with patented tooling. Each stage of the bending process, including tool loading, gripper exchange, and robotic bending, is performed at fast speeds to maintain high levels of unattended machine productivity, the company reports.

With this system, fabricators can bend a variety of parts accurately and automatically. It supports workpieces up to 39.3 by 31.4 in. and has a weight capacity of 44 lbs.

The ATC’s four tool manipulators load tooling from 15 punch stockers and 18 die stockers. Each stocker can hold 31.5 in. in tooling, which brings the ATC’s tool capacity to more than 86 ft. Rotating stockers can install punches in reverse orientation. The robot performs all part loading, bending, and unloading operations. An optional integrated loading camera system compensates for misalignment during workpiece stacking to ensure parts are picked up precisely by the robot. During bending, a potentiometer backgauge system with additional side gauge also compensates for any deviation when the workpiece is placed on the die.

Yaskawa Motoman’s MotoFit™ is a 6-axis force-sensing package for high-speed precision assembly applications. It alters the robot’s position based on the force encountered to align or assemble parts.

Blast turbines feature curved throwing blades for increased throwing speed

Walther Trowal has introduced a new generation of blast turbines for THM type troughed-belt, continuous-feed shotblast machines. These turbines feature curved throwing blades, which increase the throwing speed and reduce cycle times for shotblasting of light metals such as aluminum and magnesium.

These materials require softer aluminum blast media that has a lower bulk density, so its impact energy is lower than other media such as steel shot. The higher throwing speeds help to increase the effectiveness of the lighter-weight blast media.

The throwing blades are curved on both sides, so the turbines can be run in two directions. For applications requiring steel and stainless steel blast media, the blades are available in wear-resistant tool steel.

Automated tool change added to robotic joining system

Coldwater Machine Co. offers the Spot-Meld™ robotic joining system with new automated tool change capability. Suitable for volumes required for high-production environments, the system enables the joining of aluminum (1000 to 7000 series), magnesium, nonferrous, and dissimilar sheet materials.

The system can be mounted on any industrial robot or retrofit for replacement of existing processes such as resistance spot welding, laser welding, and riveting.

The process is based on refill friction stir spot welding technology. It uses rotational friction to heat the materials along with pressure to forge together the parts using wear-resistant tooling to join two or more surfaces. It joins lightweight materials, forms solidstate joints that are near original material strength, creates a small HAZ, offers consistent weld duplication, and delivers fast weld times. In addition, the process involves no filler material, spatter, smoke, radiation, or shield gases.

The tool doesn’t fully penetrate through the bottom sheet, leaving a smooth surface with potential for use on exterior body panels.

Oil mist collector’s packed-bed filters capture tiny oil droplets

RoboVent has introduced RoboVent Clarion™, an oil mist collector that clears oil- and particulate-laden air in demanding manufacturing environments. It uses packed-bed filters that capture tiny oil droplets and let them coalesce into larger droplets. When enough liquid accumulates, it drains off of the filter media into the collection area; often it can be returned and recycled to the process. The filter cleans itself as liquid flows off the surface, carrying solid particulate with it.

The filters approach 99.5 percent collection efficiency on 0.3-micron particle sizes, making them suitable for both mechanically and thermally generated oil mists. They continue to work even when fully saturated, offering a life up to two or three years, the company reports.

Features include an all-in-one blower and motor combination and the optional eDrive

®

automatic VFD, which automatically adjusts the motor RPM to compensate for filter loading. The unit is suitable for all types of oil, including petroleum-based, vegetable-based, and soluble oils; synthetics; and semisynthetics.

Air monitoring system controls network of room ventilation systems

Kemper has introduced the Kemper Connect concept, based on automatic communication between the AirWatch air monitoring system and room ventilation systems or extraction systems using a decentralized wireless network.

The air monitoring system controls room ventilation systems automatically using a specifically developed, autonomous wireless network. If several room ventilation systems dial into the network, the range of the network increases. The radio network establishes itself automatically, and the plants network wirelessly without further configuration.

The air monitoring system continuously measures the quality of the hall air and controls the room ventilation systems on the basis of recorded values.
