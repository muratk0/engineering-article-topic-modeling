## Auto clamping head

MANUFACTURERS INVOLVED in automation are increasingly under pressure to produce smaller batch sizes, increasing part variants and less time to get parts out of the door. To resolve these issues, workholding specialist Hainbuch has developed an automation solution designed to make the end-user more productive, more independent and more flexible.

When investigating smart, minimalist automation solutions that would make work easier, Hainbuch created a stocking and changing station placed on the workpiece feeder belt of a vertical lathe. To set up the system, the spindle passes, with the chuck, over the combination of clamping head and end-stop to be changed and is swapped within seconds by a pneumatic pulse. The workpiece to be clamped is then directly taken from the next changing station and clamped. The design needs no additional automation.

“The system makes the machine tool amazingly flexible. Using a clamp check with air sensing, efficiency can be further increased. This simple, practical solution is suitable for a variety of machines. This is because the changing station can be directly installed besides an industrial robot or gantry. This new automation solution is suitable for lower batch sizes or multiple part variants and can enable operatorfree production over three shifts,” explained Hainbuch. See www.hainbuch.com

FOR THE FIRST TIME in its almost 130-year history, welding supplier Foster Industrial is fixing its name firmly in front of customers with a range of own-label welding accessories. Foster Industrial is one of the largest suppliers of industrial gases, welding, cutting and abrasive products. It supplies over 7000 stock products.

The team at Foster Industrial, led by operations director Anthony Ashford, has branded a concise collection of its most popular premium welding supplies including an auto-darkening welding helmet; CE-marked MIG welding wire; an Argon gas regulator; acetylene and propane gas cutting nozzles and Thoriated, Zirconiated and Lanthium TIG welding tungstens.

The family-run business, established in 1886, is based on a 15,000- sq. ft. site in Shepshed near Loughborough (UK). Its product range includes used welding machines as well as supply and support for leading international brands Miller, EWM, Speedglas, Tyrolit & Sia Abrasives. See www.fosterindustrial.co.uk
