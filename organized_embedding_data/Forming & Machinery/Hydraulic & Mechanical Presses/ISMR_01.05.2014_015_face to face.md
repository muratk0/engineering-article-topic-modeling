# FACE TO FACE

[TARİH: 01.05.2014 ISMR]

LCG-3105 series offers a choice of new AF3500i-C 3.5kW oscillator with improved cutting performance using pinpoint beam focus, and the 2kW AJ fiber laser source with faster processing of thin materials and low running costs.

Amada’s EG-AR automated bending cell can process high-mix, small-batch parts to low-mix, high volume batches. The EG-AR uses a high speed press brake with, as Amada terms it, the ‘world’s first dual servo press drive mechanism’. The press brake is combined with a robot that is designed for high speed bending of small complex components. The cell also includes Amada’s BIS automatic angle sensors and an automatic material thickness detection system which corrects the angle during the bend cycle. The new system offers quick changeover between jobs to minimise downtime and boost efficiency. Tooling set-up time is greatly reduced with automated tool changing and accurate positioning. Up to 10 tool stations are able to accommodate a variety of individual tool lengths up to 100mm. The loading device

Amada’s second generation of its HFE press brake offers improved user friendly operation and energy-saving features. The range consists of models with press forces from 50 to 220 tonnes and bending beam lengths of 1250mm to 4000mm. The features of the HFE-M2 series include large open height, large throat depth, narrow lower beam and larger distance between side frames. The HFE-M2 series is also equipped with Amada’s patented instant reactive beam which ensures constant parallel deflection of both the upper and lower beams. A constant bend angle along the full length of the machine can be achieved.

“HFE 3i series and HG series 1003 are new press brake models. They feature the AMNC 3i control, multi-touch screen interface and ToolNavi – quick and easy tool set-up guidance – as well as BendNavi. DXF files can be imported and, along with the hybrid drive system, a thickness detection system is also available,” explained Amada.

The FLW fiber laser automated welding cell, demonstrated to ISMR, enables high speed, high quality welding using dedicated offline programming with FLW CAM software (efficient welding simulation without interrupting machine production) for increased welding efficiency. The FLW cell features a two-axis positioner table, repositioning robot carriage, six-axis welding robot, fibre laser welding head and 4kW fibre laser engine.

Amada’s new EMZ 3612 MII is a twin-servo electric punching system for lower power consumption. It can process 3m sheet with no re-positioning needed. The multi-purpose turret has four dedicated tapping stations. The machine also features ID tooling capability to track every tool in a turret. The result is faster, more productive and higher-grade sheet metal fabrication.

The new FabriVISION 3Di Laser 3-dimensional laser inspection system can measure heights of up to 8˝. Form features, such as emboss and bridge lance, are easily measured using 3Di. Edge-to-edge measurement at variable heights and hole-to-hole measurement is possible. Users can examine any opaque flat part including plexi-glass, gasket materials and other rigid parts.

Since FabriVISON is fully integrated with AP100US, inspections can be performed by the machine operator at the production station. A full scan is completed in seconds while comparing each measurement to CAD specifications. This reduces the time between CAD drawing and first article inspection by up to 96%, according to Amada.

Amada UK recently demonstrated the company’s commitment to the next generation of engineers by celebrating its third year as lead sponsor of the Sheet Metal Technology competition at the Skills Show 2013. It showcased the latest F1 linear drive 4kW laser profiling centre along with two HFE II press brakes, operated and maintained by Amada’s own apprentices during the competition. They taught visitors how to fold the Amada biplane, allowing them to keep the fruits of their efforts as a future reminder of the show, the technology and a potential career in manufacturing. ■
