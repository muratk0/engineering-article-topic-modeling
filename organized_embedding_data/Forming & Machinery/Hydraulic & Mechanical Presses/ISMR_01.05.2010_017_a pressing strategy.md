# A PRESSING STRATEGY

[TARİH: 01.05.2010 ISMR]

ISMR analyses recent developments in the stamping sector and lists general pressing 'parlance' for today's stampers

In recent years, stampers have seen major changes to ways of doing business, particularly for high precision micro parts which must be stamped in large numbers (mainly for the electronic watch making and automotive industries). The materials to be stamped have become thinner, the speed pitches shorter, required precision is greater than ever before and the pricing pressure is much stronger. Furthermore, production following the stamping process (eg the assembly of components or the over-moulding of punched parts) requires greater accuracy and, above all, maximum repeatability.

Press manufacturers have had to respond to these increasing requirements with changes in their product portfolios. Couple these demanding requirements with one of the deepest and most damaging economic downturns in recent times and it has been a difficult year for manufacturing.

## TURNING A CORNER

Stampers, however, are feeling more optimistic about the market in 2010, although the effects of the economic and financial crisis will continue to dominate fiscal year 2009/10. Certainly, press manufacturer Schuler believes it has turned the corner. The press manufacturer sees signs of an upturn in new orders and expects a slow recovery.

"New orders in the first quarter of 2009/10 (1 October to 31 December 2009) amount to € 151.7 million," the manufacturer told ISMR. "Compared with the preceding quarters, new orders are now more stable. The lowest level was reached in the second quarter of fiscal year 2008/09 with new orders worth around € 113 million. As expected, consolidated sales were down to € 146.8 million in the first quarter of 2009/10, compared with the prioryear figure of € 228.4 million. Order backlog amounted to € 512.1 million, following € 694.7 million in the previous year."

In the period under review, the Group generated sales of € 55.9 million in Germany, compared with € 97.5 million in the same prior-year period, while sales in the rest of Europe reached € 22.3 million, compared with a prior-year figure of € 45.8 million. In the US, sales in the first quarter of 2009/10 totaled € 25.6 million, following € 48.7 million in the previous year, while in Asia sales reached € 42.7 million, compared with € 36.1 million. 61.9% of consolidated sales were generated outside Germany, compared with 57.3% in the previous year.

In the past fiscal year 2008/09, Schuler launched a comprehensive restructuring programme in response to the effects of the global economic crisis. As a result, the company was able to achieve significant cost savings and efficiency enhancements. The German Engineering Federation (Verband Deutscher Maschinen- und Anlagenbau – VDMA) recently announced that the first green shoots of recovery are now visible, after 13 months of decline for the sector. ## ALLIANCES AND NEW DIVISIONS

Hydraulic press manufacturer PJ Hare has linked up with German counterpart Röcher GmbH to forge an alliance that will help expand its activities across the continent and the rest of the world. With Röcher specialising in larger presses from 100 to 1,600 tonnes, this logical tie-up marks a big step forward for both companies. P J Hare can now supply large hydraulic presses for a much wider range of applications and capacities, with customers benefiting from a single point of purchase.
