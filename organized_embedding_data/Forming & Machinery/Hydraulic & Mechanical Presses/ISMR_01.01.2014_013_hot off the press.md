# HOT OFF THE PRESS

[TARİH: 01.01.2014 ISMR]

ISMR speaks to Joachim Beyer, CTO of Schuler, on his outlook on markets, technology trends and new initiatives in 2014

Schuler’s 175th anniversary begins in 2014. The company was founded in 1839 by Louis Schuler in Göppingen, Germany and it still has its headquarters there. As a technological and global market leader in metalforming, it supplies presses, automation solutions, dies, process technology and services to the metal-working industry and the lightweight construction of vehicles. Its clients include car manufacturers and their suppliers, as well as companies in the forging, household equipment, packaging, energy and electrical industries. Schuler is also the market leader in coin minting technology.

Schuler showcased its latest presses and automation components for manufacturing lightweight parts at the recent Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart. High-strength steels, for example, represent a particular challenge for the forming machine. According to Schuler, mechanical presses with ServoDirect Technology are ideally suited for such tasks as the slide movement can be perfectly adapted to the respective material, dies and process. ISMR caught up with Schuler’s Chief Technology Officer, Joachim Beyer, at the Blechexpo exhibition to find out his perspectives on markets, technology trends and new initiatives in 2014.

## The Asian equation

Strong figures have made Schuler confident for 2014. Earnings in 2012/13 were up 19 per cent, with sales at the prior year’s level, and it has further expanded its global presence. It recently announced sales of €1.19 billion on a par with the previous year and new orders of €1.16 billion with a further slight improvement in Ebitda margin to 10.4 per cent. Consolidated sales in fiscal year 2014 are expected to reach about €1.1 billion.

“It was a brilliant year for Schuler – we are profitable and growing. Our capital situation is stable, we have Andritz as a new investor and we have used the opportunity to re-engineer our technologies and develop new machines for future growth,” Joachim Beyer told ISMR at the Blechexpo exhibition.

China plays a central part in its strategy. Schuler has invested €16 million in expanding its new site in Dalian which has also led to the creation of new jobs. In total, more than 350 people work for Schuler in China.

“One of our main markets is Asia (30 per cent of sales) – China takes up a large part of that. Around 17 per cent of our sales are to America and 37 per cent are to Germany. We also have a large facility in Brazil (supporting the South American market and supplying equipment to North America). We have been active in China for over 20 years and are very experienced in that market. Our technology in the automotive industry is well established and this has greatly helped our success in China.

“We now have a second facility in China (Dalian) tripling our capacity in China to 16,000 square metres. Our first Chinese facility is in Shanghai. There has been a strong commitment throughout our organisation to see China as our second home market. Looking at the different forecasts and predictions for the next few decades, nobody can neglect China. It is the largest and most competitive market and our customers expect us to produce parts there,” explained Beyer.

“Apart from Shanghai, Beijing, Jinan and Tianjin, we will open further service offices in China to support our customers and business. We want to gain more business in China as this market is growing so we are trying to expand our product portfolio as this is strategically necessary and opens a wider customer range. So, we are quite optimistic for the future.”

## Technology trends

To date, Schuler has sold about 340 presses with ServoDirect technology. Now, the smallest machine in this series, the MSD 250 with a press force of 250 metric tons, has been completely revised. The Group has also developed a drive for hydraulic presses (“Efficient Hydraulic Forming”) which reduces energy consumption by 20 to 60 per cent compared to conventional lines.

Schuler recently also launched a new laser blanking line from coil which can cut material into shapes and save a lot of material. The award-winning Laser Blanking Line can now cut blanks directly from a moving sheet metal coil using laser beams. The process is called “DynamicFlow Technology” and involves the use of three coordinated cutting heads. In a single, continuous process, the sheet metal is decoiled, straightened, cut and stacked as blanks. The flexibility of the laser cutting process means that any shape can be cut, without the need for dedicated dies. Changes to the respective contours or even completely new blanks can be quickly and easily implemented.

“This system is very attractive for the production of parts for niche cars – you don’t need a die (the laser is the die) and you can cut any shape (it is freely programmable). I see this as a step towards Industry 4.0 because you can adjust the shape of the blank according to the forming process. This opens up new ideas for closed loop systems, albeit at a very early stage,” explained Beyer.

“Some trends in sheet metal stamping stem from the requirement to reduce the weight of car body parts in automotive production. This leads to the development of different materials and processes and I believe this trend will continue for many years.”

There is growing demand from car manufacturers and their suppliers for press hardened parts, for which hydraulic presses are used. By greatly increasing the contact pressure and optimising the die, Schuler has succeeded in drastically reducing cycle times (Pressure Controlled Hardening or PCH).

“One of the other trends we see is press hardening,” continued Beyer. “It is becoming more popular in Asia and has good potential in that market. We have sold over 60 press hardening lines over the last 10/15 years. Another key driver is energy saving and we believe that our servo direct technology is the right step forward for this. We reduce the stroke rate to reduce energy consumption.”

## Restructuring for growth

The Schuler group recently restructured and consolidated aspects of its organisation in Germany.

“Internally, instead of technology fields, we now have six divisions to make the structure less complex,” said Beyer. “Generally speaking, the restructure is mainly about concentrating competencies in Germany and our facilities there as well as centralising our production activities. We will close the foundry in Goppingen because it is not profitable and no potential buyer was to guarantee its continued existence. In-house commissioning will be centralised in Goppingen. There will also be a new engineering centre in Goppingen – building will start next year and be finished by 2016. This is a 40-million euro investment and there will be space for 750 people.”

Schuler is optimising its structures as part of its group-wide “Growing Together 2.0” programme to become more efficient and global. In addition to the now completed downsizing of the Board of Management and second-tier management level, several German subsidiaries are to be merged. The concept also involves streamlining manufacturing operations and centralising administrative tasks. This will affect 350 jobs in Germany over the medium term.

“The reorganisation will streamline the company, to make decisions faster. It is the right time to do this and we want to avoid the organisation standing still and keep moving forward, make some changes and adjust capacity to react to increasing requirements from China,” added Beyer. “Most of our capacity is still in Europe (ie Germany) but, to supply to Asia, we want to produce and source more in China. The automotive industry is the main driver for our business – in China, it touches 20 million cars per year,” concluded Beyer.
