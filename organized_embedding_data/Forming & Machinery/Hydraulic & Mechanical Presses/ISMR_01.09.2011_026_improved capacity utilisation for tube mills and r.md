# IMPROVED CAPACITY UTILISATION FOR TUBE MILLS AND ROLL FORMING LINES

[TARİH: 01.09.2011 ISMR]

New technologies provide a quantum leap for roll forming applications.

A global oversupply of goods is forcing many producers of consumer goods to produce an increasing number of new products for any possible target group.

As a consequence, their suppliers have to deal with an increasing number of product variants and often drastically reduced lead times. Many tube and profile producers are not sufficiently prepared to manage this challenge. Today’s roll tool change-over techniques require a lot of time. At the same time, the companies are forced to reduce their inventory costs to meet cost targets.

Recently, Dreistern in Germany developed a number of technologies which allow for an efficient counter strategy. These are targeted mainly in two directions:

1 Reduction of the required time, to a very few minutes, for a product change to enable the producer to supply (just-in-sequence and just-in-time) even small batch sizes without the need for intermediate storing.

2 Enhanced capability to produce completely different products on one production line. This means that tube manufacturers can produce round tubes as well as complex welded and open sections.

“We provide a range of options to accelerate product change-over. An easy and rapid way is a change-over analysis. Our experience shows that usually a 50 per cent reduction in change-over time can be achieved at relatively low cost. We have developed a successful and proven procedure based on video recording,” Dreistern explained.

The automatic adjustment of roll tools is another efficient option to accelerate product change-over. Many operators know how much time and material could be saved when adjustment of tools after production start-up could be avoided. The new TMA System from Dreistern targets this problem. It enables automatic measuring and adjustment of all roll tool parameters before production starts. It ensures that all tool parameters are set and the new product can be produced within the required tolerances and without the need for additional adjustments.

“Our most important leap towards a SMED (single minute exchange of die) strategy is a new, highly flexible production concept. A complete product change-over, including change of cross-section and punch pattern, can be executed in 3 to 15 minutes. The capability of such a flexible production line to switch between completely different products in a very short time enables the economical production of very small batch sizes. This is opening up new business opportunities for the roll forming industry which, as a result, will bring better capacity utilisation,” Dreistern told ISMR. ■

## A GLOBAL FORUM

EMO is a common initiative of European machine tool builders dating back to 1950. The first European Machine Tools Exhibitions was launched in 1951 and shortly after became the world’s biggest venue of that kind. The first EMO, as a fair open to international exhibitors, took place in Paris in 1975 and has been increasing its worldwide significance ever since. The international recognition and importance of EMO is attributed to the traditionally dominant role of Europe in the machine tool world. Moreover, as developing countries have emerged as significant producers and consumers of machine tools, EMO has evolved into a global forum and a meeting point for the international machine tool industry.

EMO’s principal focus is on cutting and forming machine tools, production systems, high-precision tools, automated material flows, computer technology, industrial electronics and accessories. Trade visitors to EMO come from all major industrial sectors such as machinery and plant construction, the automotive industry and its component suppliers, aerospace technologies, precision mechanics and optics, shipbuilding, medical technology, tool and mould building, steel and lightweight engineering.
