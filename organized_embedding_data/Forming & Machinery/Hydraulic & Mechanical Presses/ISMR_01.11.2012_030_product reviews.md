PRODUCT MIX flexibility, the ability to produce different products on the same production line, is one of the critical decision factors when investing in new equipment. Shorter innovation cycles and growing product diversity make this issue even more important. In recent years, many remarkable developments have advanced the flexibility of roll forming lines. However, this can also present an increasing risk to the profile producers and this flexibility can result in much higher equipment costs.

The trend towards larger roll forming machines with more and more forming stations can be seen as an example of this, rollforming specialist Dreistern told ISMR. It recently supplied a machine with more than 60 forming stations.

“What if there aren’t enough complex section orders to fully utilise the machine’s potential? Run simple sections too? Simple sections require fewer forming stations, the 60- pass machine would be far too big and too expensive to run for such a jobs,” Dreistern explained. “The situation is similar when punching and embossing operations are integrated into the roll forming process. Take an example from the automotive industry. Roll-formed longitudinal reinforcement beams require pre- and post-punching operations, which can easily double the investment cost. The same is true for the footprint of the machine, which can double as well. The next product run on this line will most likely require different, possibly with fewer, punching operations. Again, the machine would be too big and too expensive to operate.”

This was the starting point for Dreistern to develop a new machine concept, offering true product mix flexibility. A radical break from existing concepts, based on single-purpose line components such as roll forming machine, punch press or cutting machine, that are only capable of fulfilling their assigned function, made this possible.

Dreistern’s concept of a multi-functional roll forming machine takes an entirely different approach. The key requirement – operations such as roll forming, punching or welding – can be executed at any position on the machine only by exchanging components (within a time period usually required for a simple tool change).

“A multi-functional machine can be configured precisely and rapidly to produce the next part. Compared to conventional technology, the footprint of such a machine can be reduced by 30% to 50%. No redundant line components will increase the machine’s hourly rate. This was our key development objective: more flexibility with lower cost and less floor space,” said Dreistern.

The guiding model for this Dreistern development was the quick-change roll-tool cassette. With the aid of standardised interfaces, tools can be exchanged easily and quickly. In the future, complete line components such as flying punching units or welding devices will be exchanged in an equivalent manner, it explained. Consequently, a 60-station machine could manufacture a highly complex section today but tomorrow it could run a simpler 18-pass section but also include comprehensive pre- and postoperations. The space of 42 forming passes would now be occupied by a number of punching units, a welding station, or units for placing clinch nuts or bolt rivets. www.dreistern.com
