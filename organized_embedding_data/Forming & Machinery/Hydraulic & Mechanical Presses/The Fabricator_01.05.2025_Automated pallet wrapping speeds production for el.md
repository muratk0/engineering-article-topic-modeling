# Automated pallet wrapping speeds production for electrical enclosure fabricator

[TARİH: 01.05.2025 The Fabricator]

Applications

Automating the pallet wrapping process has saved Saginaw Control & Engineering hundreds of thousands of dollars per year in labor costs and forklift utilization.

Situation

Saginaw Control & Engineering designs and manufactures standard and custom electrical enclosures in two facilities in Saginaw, Mich., using automated forming, fiber optic laser cutting and welding, and robotic press brakes. Powder-coated carbon steel, galvanized steel, and stainless steel housings are offered to suit any environment or application.

Stocking thousands of products at six warehouses, the company employs a staff of nearly 500. But facing ongoing labor shortage issues, Chris Sims, product distribution manager, continues to investigate automating key tasks to address the issue.

When Sims joined Saginaw in 2006, the company had just invested in seven turntable stretch wrapping machines to automate the formerly manual pallet wrapping process. Over the years, Sims noted the turntable wrappers would knock unstable loads off the pallet during the wrapping process, and sometimes the wrap would come loose during transit and cause freight damage.

"This was always very upsetting and one of my biggest reasons we started looking for a more modern, automated approach to pallet wrapping," he explained.

Resolution

Sims read about the TAB Wrapper Tornado orbital stretch wrapper in The Fabricator and then saw it in action at the TAB Industries booth at FABTECH in Chicago.

"I immediately liked how the forklift driver could operate the TAB Wrapper without leaving the seat of the forklift," said Sims.

He bought the Smart Controls model and had it up and running in the Saginaw East manufacturing facility in a few months. Its 100-in. wrapping ring accommodates products up to 60 in. tall on 72- by 72-in. pallets, representing nearly 75% of the company’s electrical enclosures.

The unit automatically wraps plastic film 360 degrees around and under the pallet and load while it is raised on a forklift to create a stable, unitized load. A single forklift driver can manage the pallet wrapping process— which takes 30 seconds or less—without leaving the forklift or contacting the machine.

The forklift driver raises the pallet load, drives it into the center of the wrapping ring, and presses "start" on the wireless remote control. The wrapper automatically encircles the pallet load in layers of plastic stretch film as the driver gradually moves forward or back until the wrapping process is finished. When the driver presses "stop" on the remote control, the built-in automated cut and wrap device automatically cuts the end of the plastic and readies it for the next pallet load.

Operator training took only 15 minutes, and response in the plant was immediate. "Everyone loved it," said Sims. "It wraps fast with consistently tight tension, it’s quiet, and our drivers really appreciate being able to control the wrapping process without getting on and off the forklift."

Sims purchased seven more of the Smart Controls models over the next four years for all the company’s distribution centers. He also purchased three of the fully automated Perfect Storm systems for the Saginaw manufacturing facilities. With these systems, the forklift driver places the pallet load on a roller conveyor, then leaves to go about other tasks while the machine feeds the pallet load into the wrapping ring, applies and cuts the stretch wrap, and discharges the wrapped pallet load down a second roller conveyor to an accumulation area.

"The annual savings in labor costs and forklift utilization from the Perfect Storm systems alone probably tops $100,000 per year," said Sims. The packaging team at Saginaw now stretch-wraps upwards of 900 pallet loads per day, a five-fold increase in volume.
