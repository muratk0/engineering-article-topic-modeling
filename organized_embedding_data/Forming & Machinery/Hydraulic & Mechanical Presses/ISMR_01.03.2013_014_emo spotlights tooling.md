# EMO spotlights tooling

[TARİH: 01.03.2013 ISMR]

WHAT ROLE does tooling play in Industry 4.0, the new manufacturing concept with web-based networking? A report from the 2012 Tool Conference in Schmalkalden, the meeting point for insiders from the metalcutting sector, may hold the answer.

The controversial issue of Industry 4.0 was not on the agenda at the 10th Tool Conference in Schmalkalden. Nevertheless, the manufacturers and users of metal-cutting tools are not indifferent to the issue: when you take a closer look, some tools have already been prepared for Industry 4.0 as delegates saw during the presentations and a tour of the test bay and laboratories of the GFE – Gesellschaft für Fertigungstechnik und Entwicklung Schmalkalden e.V. (Society of Production Technology and Development) – who organised the symposium themed around high-precision tools.

The experts in Thuringia unveiled a mechatronic tool, for example, designed for retrograde machining of large boreholes, which uses telemetry to acquire the ongoing status of the tool during the metal-cutting operation. This tool, which acquires and transmits measured data, fits neatly with the new concept known as the “Internet of Things”, in which all participants communicate with each other just like on the conventional web.

The BMBF’s (German Federal Ministry of Education and Research) joint project Sensomikrosys goes one step further. It has created extremely small sensors that monitor in real-time machines and tool components exposed to highly dynamic loads. These microsystems, for example, measure the forces acting in tools and clamping systems. For this purpose, the GFE showcased a test rig for dynamic load testing of tool clamping systems in machine spindles at the Tool Conference in Schmalkalden. Sensors of this kind can be integrated into hand-held tools.
