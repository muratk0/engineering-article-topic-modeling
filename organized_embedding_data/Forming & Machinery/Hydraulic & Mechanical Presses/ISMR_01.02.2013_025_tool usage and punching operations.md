# Tool usage and punching operations

[TARİH: 01.02.2013 ISMR]

The challenge for NES Architecture was to punch the aluminium sheets with rows of punched holes in irregular wave lines across the entire panel surface. The holes were punched in six different sizes and were unconventional in that they had a "dimple" around the perimeter edge to produce different visual effects in different light conditions.

To achieve these features, the wave lines of holes were punched in a Safan Challenger turret press using custom CNC programming and Mate tooling that included a special countersink embossing tool to create the hole dimples. The special application tooling has a major diameter of 35mm and a top-to-top of 6mm. It is used in a 3.5-inch Strippit station in the Safan press. The tooling provided consistent hole embossing to achieve the desired design.

Close-up of the countersunk embossed holes on one side of the sheet (inset right) shows how the hole sizes vary in size and position and make possible the showing side of the sheet with its flowing wave of holes.

“To create the right visual effect, we had to punch 1100 holes on each panel utilising six hole sizes randomly positioned but following the design artist’s vector line on the panel surfaces,” reported Mr. Holt. There were a total of twelve CNC programmes utilised, six per side covering all six hole sizes – 18, 20, 22, 24, 26 and 28mm – so that each was used 200 times on every panel. Programming for the Safan Challenger press used Radan CAD/CAM software which, according to Holt, slashed programming time by a third.

“The panel material used was J57S aluminium, 3mm thick and in two panel sizes, 3.6 x 2m and 2.4 x 2m," reported Mr. Holt. "We use a lot of J57S aluminium because it is specifically designed for modern architectural applications for interior and exterior cladding. It punched very well with Mate tooling producing all of the different size holes, each with its own distinctive countersunk embossed hole."

Punched holes with dimple-type features require more additional steps than “normal” holes. The building application had additional complexity because the holes had to have both distinctive appearing countersunk embossed hole and variations in size, appearance and position.

The punching process required randomly positioned embossed dimples around each of the punched holes, with the holes not necessarily centred in the middle of the dimple. Spacing of the holes was such that some dimples overlapped.

“Some of the countersunk embossed holes overlapped each other which put extra stress on the embossing tool but the robust tool design stood up well to this type of punching without problems or maintenance," reported Mr Holt.

To accomplish the hole pattern, all holes were punched firstly into the face of the panel so slugs fell free of the panel using Mate's Slug Free® dies. The panel was then turned over so that the dimples were "up-formed" from the reverse side into the punched holes. This method created the desired visual effect for the rows of dimpled holes while keeping the panel surface flat and scratch-free.

The flat, mark-free surface was essential to obtaining a defect-free, bright and shiny finish after the aluminium panels were anodized following punching. The waves of holes show off spectacularly. Punching time per panel averaged 1-1/2 hours, including the time required to re-position the panels on the machine to achieve the desired hole pattern and hole finish.

"Consistency of the embossing, not speed, was key to the success of this project," reported Holt. "We operated the press well under top speed to ensure sheet flatness for the best visual appearance and so that sheets lined up perfectly when affixed to the building. Our past experience with punching architectural components came into play. We operated the punch press and Mate tooling at the optimum speed – but not too fast to create distortion and scrap – to achieve top quality results." ■
