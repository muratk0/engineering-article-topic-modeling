Servo-electric turret punch press fits in compact spaces

Prima Power offers the E5x Compact Express servo-electric turret punch press. Designed to offer versatile capacity, it features a new machine control and user interface software with touchscreen panel.

Compact load/unload automation, which uses the space above and below the machine to maintain a small footprint, allows unattended operation. Several combinations of loading and unloading can be used, including automatic loading/unloading, automatic loading and manual unloading, manual loading and automatic unloading, or manual loading/unloading.

The equipment is integrated close to the machine, but manual operations can be performed on the free side of the machine. Pallets can be loaded and components removed from them while the machine operates. In addition, the machine can be installed against a wall or in a corner.

The system handles 48- by 96-in. sheet without repositioning. Punching speed is up to 700 HPM with 0.04 in. between holes. Punching forces of 19.1, 22, or 25.3 tons are available.

Hybrid welding process welds thick metal sheets quickly

Laser Zentrum Hannover e.V. has developed a hybrid welding process that allows the welding of thick pipelines and metal sheets made of aluminum alloys or steel at speeds of 19.69 and 4.92 FPM, respectively.

The process can be used to make single-sided, zero-defect welds on aluminum sheets up to a thickness of 0.47 in. It combines a laser beam with two GMAW torches. A scanner mirror can be used to oscillate the laser beam lengthwise or crosswise to the feed direction.

The joining process can reach feed speeds of 16.40 to 19.69 FPM, bridging gaps up to 0.02 in. and edge misalignment up to 0.08 in. It also can weld steel sheets up to 0.91 in. thick at speeds up to 4.92 FPM for single-layer welds.

The hybrid process requires two to three filler layers. The first layer is made using the hybrid laser-GMAW process. Subsequently, the sheets are subject to one or two backing runs with the GMAW process. In this way, the process can be used to weld a 4.92-ft.-long component in one minute.

Compact cutting systems equipped with CO or fiber laser

Knuth has introduced the Laser-Jet 1512 and 2512 laser cutting machines. The compact cutting systems can be equipped with either a CO

2

or fiber laser. Parts can be produced individually and unloaded directly at the machine. In addition, a sheet of material that has been used for a single part can easily be set aside and later reloaded.

Both laser types are equipped with a PC-based control. They handle material sheet sizes up to 8 by 4 ft. (2512) or 5 by 4 ft. (1512).

The CO

2

laser source delivers high-power cutting of nonalloy and low-alloy structural steel plate up to 0.31 in. thick. It is available in 600, 1,000, or 1,500 W. Laser fusion cutting is possible using nitrogen under high pressure as the cutting gas. High-alloy stainless steel and aluminum materials can be cut with high quality in plate thicknesses up to 0.12 in., the company states.

The fiber laser source generates a 1.6-µm wavelength to make the beam more focusable. It can cut stainless steel, aluminum, and brass in plate thicknesses up to 0.24 in. with very high quality, the company reports. Much thicker plates can be cut using the 4,000-W laser source. For nonalloy and low-alloy structural steels, it can cut plates more than 0.47 in. thick.

Sheet marking system fits into turret punch press

Wilson Tool Intl. has released SmartMark®, which the company says is the first ink-jet sheet marking system that fits into a turret punch press.

The ink-jet printer can be loaded directly into the C station of most thick-turret machines. Using a combination of the company’s SmartMark Writer software and CNC programming, fabricators can program the printer to mark parts with alphanumeric text, lines, time stamps, or bar codes. Users can mark part numbers, descriptions, and assembly and part handling instructions to help reduce production errors.

Each marking kit includes a printer, the software, a wireless router, one ink cartridge, two batteries, and a battery charger.

Multifunctional grinder accesses hard-to-reach areas

CS Unitec offers the GLADIUS® 1800 grinder. It grinds, deburrs, blends, and polishes ferrous and nonferrous metals. The narrow arm makes it suitable for corners, angles, and tight spaces.

The grinder features infinitely adjustable speed control from 1,000 to 3,800 RPM, with a tachogenerator that provides constant speed, even under load. The tool’s design allows it to use the full length of both sides of the arm.

An assortment of abrasive belts and polishing fleece is available for the grinder. Sanding belts can be changed without tools. Belts are interchangeable among the PIPE-SNIPER®, GLADIUS, and the company’s PIPE-MAX pipe-belt grinder. An integrated duct extraction port enables dust-free sanding when connected to an industrial vacuum. A rubberized, bow-shaped handle helps ensure slip-proof operation.

Parts leveler’s new control simplified for novice users

Arku’s new control for the FlatMaster® parts leveler enables novice users to produce dead-flat, stress-free metal parts on the first try nearly every time by entering as few as two values: the material type and thickness. An operator interface of touchscreen icons uses a database of material properties, part thicknesses, operating parameters, and plausibility checks that allows new users to run the machine like experts with as little as 90 minutes of training, according to the manufacturer.

The new control accesses all of the leveler’s functions with icons. When the user enters basic information on the workpiece, the control calculates optimal leveling parameters for the first part. These parameters can be fine-tuned by the operator and saved as a part program for future use.

The new control supports all of the machine’s leveling capabilities, including two-way leveling, reverse operation, and series leveling. A plausibility check evaluates operator entries and provides an explanation of incorrect values to aid in learning.
