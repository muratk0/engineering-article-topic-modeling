# Portal grinding system

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 ISMR]

KUHLMEYER delivered the second fully automated portal grinding system with ROBOTEC handling technology to finish large, complex stainless steel structures after forming and welding - eliminating all manual labour.

“In Spring 2014, one of our longstanding customers from the USA, a producer of high-end kitchen appliances–after an extensive period of detailed trials and research–decided to integrate the first-ever delivered Kuhlmeyer PZM – ROBOTEC into his production. By automating the process of finishing formed and welded components, the customer no longer needed manual labour for this task,” Kuhlmeyer told ISMR.

“The customer already had plenty of experience with the quality of a Kuhlmeyer machine. Over the past ten years, he had accumulated twelve conventional long-belt sanding TBS machines in several facilities and sub-suppliers. While these machines delivered excellent results, there was a need to optimize ergonomics, quality and throughput for larger kitchen hoods and stove fronts due to the difficulty of staffing the finishing operation of the business. This requires skilled individuals doing repetitive work over extensive periods of time. With this in mind, along with improving safety, the planning and selection of the final configuration began. The results ensured great consistency and repeatability of the process.”

Kuhlmeyer, a mid-sized German company in Bad Oeynhausen, has extensive experience in polishing, grinding and deburring for metal and wood applications and is able to customise with practical solutions. The R&D team at Kuhlmeyer began designing and testing several different approaches to automation and handling systems on long-belt stroke sanding machines three years ago. The preliminary prototype was based on its standard Twin Belt Machine (TBS). After the customer’s first trial run, the necessary improvements were made and Kuhlmeyer achieved the results specified at the beginning of the project.

“Based on the size of the parts and the acceleration requirements of the individual axis drives, it was obvious that the standard TBS solution would not meet the specifications. The concept switched from the TBS base machine to the Portal Twin Belt configuration (PZM). This offered better access in a significantly larger working range. Necessary movement in the vertical Z axis direction was made possible by mounting a much stronger handling unit on a fixed rail with the grinding unit. Now the machine could fully rotate parts of up to 1680mm (66”) under the belt, allowing for work on the short sides of long components,” explained Kuhlmeyer.

The machine was successfully finished in October 2014 at Kuhlmeyer’s German factory, and subsequently subjected to a detailed acceptance test. All test results were satisfactory, and a punch list with minor changes was worked out before the machine was shipped to the USA.

Kuhlmeyer’s US factory representative MetalFinish LLC coordinated shipping and import, and installed the machine at the customer’s facility in Wisconsin, USA. Start-up, training and final acceptance then followed.

A subsequent order for a second system shortly followed and shipment for the second ROBOTEC was scheduled for Winter 2015. This machine was exhibited at FABTECH 2015 in Chicago, before installation at the customer’s site.

“Cycle times were cut by more than half as the output per machine was improved by a factor of 2.7. The quality of the finished product is both improved and of greater consistency by automating the grinding process. The machine operator no longer has to make repetitive movements for extended periods of time, and now has ample time to prepare the workpiece for the next automated grinding cycle. Set-up and pre-finishing steps are now completed while the cycle is running and therefore don’t reduce output any further,” concluded Kuhlmeyer.

The PZM ROBOTEC integrates ten motorized axes carrying a maximum payload of 110lbs for workpieces of up to 80“ x 20“ x 28“ long. The control, a Simatic S7, has been extended with a second operating panel outside the guarded hazardous zone for safety and operator convenience. All part programs are programmed and stored directly in the control. All programs can be backed up and called up for execution at any time.

A number of parameters such as feed rates, limits and grinding pressure, are vital to a successful application. Actual values are displayed on the screen and can be adjusted on the fly while observing the process. The machine commands two separate grinding belts for the first coarse material removal and the final finish, reducing belt change times and increasing uptime. Automatic tool motion can be switched off for manual mode, increasing the flexibility of the system. The operator, for example, can equalize uneven welds or remove unexpected spots and scratches from a surface.
