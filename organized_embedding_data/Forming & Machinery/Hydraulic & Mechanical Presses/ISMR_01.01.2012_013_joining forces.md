# JOINING FORCES

[TARİH: 01.01.2012 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“2012 has been a breakthrough year for Rosenberger and Unison, with the companies enjoying their most successful business year ever.”

Two innovative manufacturers of tube bending machines, Unison and Rosenberger, have entered into a joint sales and marketing venture to create a new international force in the metalworking machinery market.

The joint venture greatly extends each company’s product range and application expertise, and the international reach of their sales and support networks. Their combined product ranges are designed to provide manufacturing organisations with an extensive choice of all-electric tube bending and processing automation.

## An eye on tube

Both companies specialise in all-electric, servomotor-controlled, tube bending machinery. Rosenberger operates mainly in the smaller end of the market for tube diameters of up to around 30mm and is particularly strong in the automotive sector. It also offers numerous all-electric endforming machines and has a strong automation engineering team to design and build turnkey work cells. The company sells mainly into the German-speaking region and in mainland Europe.

Unison’s tube bending machine range is broader, offering the capability of processing tube diameters up to 200+mm. It is involved in a much wider range of applications, from aerospace and shipbuilding to furniture production and subcontract metalworking fabrication. Most of the company’s sales activity is in English-speaking markets.

Together, the two companies employ over 130 people and have annual sales approaching 20 million euros. Immediately, the cooperative agreement will expand the global footprints of both organisations in Europe and the Americas. Over time, the companies hope to cooperate to improve penetration into Asia-Pacific markets.

The CEO of Rosenberger, Gerhard Rosenberger, explained: “We have admired each other’s products and work over many years, and believe that this relationship creates a strong synergy that will expand the tube processing solutions we are able to offer to users and the global support.”

Alan Pickering of Unison added: “This partnership also has significant potential in terms of technology development. Both companies have innovation-oriented cultures, and the development possibilities opened up by our combined intellectual property and R&D resources in areas from machine control software to robotics and handling is genuinely far-reaching.”

Commenting on the joint venture, Unison directors Peter Wilkinson and Julian Kidger said: “We are delighted to have found such a business partner as Rosenberger and believe this agreement could be a defining moment in the histories of our companies. It gives us a platform to dramatically improve our offer to customers and drive our businesses forward much more rapidly than we could achieve by operating independently.”

2012 has been a breakthrough year for Rosenberger and Unison, with the companies enjoying their most successful business year ever. Rosenberger is currently celebrating a significant multi-million euro contract in the European automotive sector. Unison has been expanding rapidly in North American markets, thanks to a string of recent orders including a multimillion dollar contract in the shipbuilding sector.

The combined all-electric tube bending machines available from Unison and Rosenberger following this agreement now span tube diameters from 16 to 200mm. The choice of machine configurations is broad and includes single bend head machines; versatile machines with left- and right-hand bending capability or a rotating head; twin-head machines for rapid fabrication of symmetrical parts; machines with integrated robotic handling arms, with integrated cutting heads plus a wide range of tube end formers and related finishing capabilities.

Rosenberger is headquartered in Simonswald in Southwest Germany, and has three further regional sales and support offices in Germany and France. Unison is headquartered in Scarborough, UK, with a sales and distribution network in the USA and Germany.

## Precision for aerospace

The benefits of all-electric tube bending machines for precision aerospace manufacturing applications were highlighted by Unison recently at the 2012 Farnborough International Airshow. Unison machines feature a bending system based on precision digitallycontrolled servomotors. This all-electric architecture provides significant performance benefits in terms of rapid and repeatable software-based set-up and lower energy consumption and noise, compared with traditional hydraulically-powered machinery.

Unison’s machines are supporting fabrication processes that must manufacture ultra-precise parts in very small batches - typically without generating any scrap; bending complex part shapes in a single stage; bending expensive exotic alloy tubing such as titanium with wall thicknesses as fine as 0.9mm and eliminating jig-based inspection processes. One of Unison’s most recent users in the aerospace manufacturing sector cites the enhanced ability to bend exotic alloys and reduced process stages on complex shapes as key reasons for purchase. Another key advantage for the high precision bending required for aerospace components is Unison’s laser-controlled spring-back correction system. By eliminating scrap wastage associated with conventional hydraulically-powered bending machines, the system helps manufacturers address the spiralling cost of raw materials, and is especially beneficial for companies working with exotic alloys. The correction system can also be used for edge and feature detection to facilitate more stable process control of pre-laser-cut parts.

At the Airshow, Unison highlighted an innovative quick-change tooling system that dramatically speeds tooling changes on its allelectric tube bending machines. The system covers all of the tooling components on the machine, and includes barcode identification to verify the changeover process.

“Aerospace is one of the most demanding applications for tube bending and, over time, the manufacturing tolerances are tending to become even tighter,” said Alan Pickering, CEO of Unison Ltd. “We are able to show that all-electric bending automation has helped aerospace manufacturers to evolve component production processes that are at the vanguard of what is technically possible in terms of precision, shape creation and cost effectiveness.”
