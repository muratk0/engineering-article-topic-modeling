# Slimming solutions for cars

[TARİH: 01.06.2012 ISMR]

The hot forging process makes the metal far more rigid (Schuler)

THE MORE rigid a material, the less is needed to achieve the desired level of stability – thus reducing weight. This applies to high-strength steels. With the aid of hot and cold forging processes, these materials enable manufacturers to produce much thinner panels without sacrificing any of the part’s rigidity.

“The car body accounts for 40 per cent of total weight. The potential savings are therefore correspondingly large,” explained Schuler General Manager, Manfred Wischnewski.

Schuler leverages the benefits of its ServoDirect Technology, as such presses can be tailored specifically to special requirements for cold forging high-strength steels. As the high rigidity of such steels offers suitable crash behaviour properties, they can also be used for safety components – such as bumper supports or B-pillar reinforcements.

Press hardening (also called Pressure Controlled Hardening or PCH for short) goes one step further: parts are formed in a heated state and simultaneously cooled down. The material used in this process can reach even higher levels of rigidity and can be processed using lower press forces. The process also offers greater flexibility for part design than cold forging.

“Aluminium has almost become a classic for lightweight construction. If every car body part were produced using this lightweight material, it would save up to 40kg. The hydroforming process is used to produce exhaust systems and special structural parts, such as side members. In general, a tube is pressed from the inside against a die by pumping in a water-oil emulsion. As the wall thickness can be influenced, there are also possibilities for saving weight,” said Schuler.

A number of vehicle concepts are currently being developed in the USA, which involve the use of hydroformed parts made from high-strength materials in the passenger cell. Schuler has a dedicated competence centre for hydroforming based in Detroit.

“We’ve already received several orders from car manufacturers and their suppliers for lines based on the supporting pressure method – a further development of hydroforming,” added Wischnewski. www.schulergroup.com
