# THE STAMPING EQUATION

[TARİH: 01.02.2014 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest worldwide stamping and press developments

Press manufacturers are constantly researching, upgrading and launching new models to comply with ever increasing requirements for efficiency, energy saving, greater control and better material flexibility.

ISMR SAYS:

“Stamping and pressing operations are focusing more on cost savings, energy efficiency and flexible material processing”

Over the last year, ISMR has seen a number of innovative new developments in this sector so we have highlighted a selection here of the latest worldwide stamping and press developments.

## Keeping control

AIDA presses are used to manufacture a broad range of items including automobile components, appliance parts, electric and electronic components, connectors and terminals as well as motor laminations. Currently AIDA has more than 4,600 presses installed in North America and 80,000 worldwide, with capacities ranging from 35 to 4,000 metric tons. It manufactures a complete line of metalforming presses, including gap, straightside, progressive die, transfer, high speed, cold forging and servo-driven mechanical presses.

AIDA-America recently showcased three Direct Drive servo formers (DSF Series) at the Fabtech exhibition in Chicago at the end of last year. Each of the AIDA DSF Series servo presses featured a different demonstration including tooling and parts production. One of the demonstrations featured an AIDA DSF-N2- 3000, 300-ton, straightside servo press, recently purchased by, and courtesy of, Jagemann Stamping, Manitowoc, WI.

In cooperation with Formtek/CWP and Pax Products, with material provided by Heidtman Steel Products, the DSF-N2-3000 servo press produced a pipe flange part from 4.5mm thick, 120mm wide material via a progressive die operation. Additionally, AIDA, in cooperation with Hyson Products and making use of its servo die cushion, demonstrated a DSF Series servo press for a difficult drawing application. AIDA-America also displayed an HSLA blanking operation highlighting the ability of a DSF Series servo press to dramatically reduce reverse tonnage.

At the beginning of 2014, Swedish press specialist AP&T delivered an additional hydroforming press to VW-FAW in Changchun, China.

“Interest in hydroforming in China is growing, and we are receiving an increasing number of inquiries from manufacturers of vehicles and vehicle parts,” explained Peter Robisch, product manager for hydroforming at AP&T.

The hydroforming press with 3500 tons of press force will be used to manufacture suspension parts. Installation will take place over the spring of 2014 and deployment of the equipment is planned for May. VW-FAW in Changchun has almost 10,000 employees and production capacity of 600,000 cars. The facilities are jointly owned by Volkswagen and Chinese FAW.

AP&T is also deepening cooperation with Oerlikon Balzers for the development and testing of different types of coating for tool steel for press hardening and manufacturing of parts for ventilation and roof drainage. By optimising the surface coating of tool steel to ensure that the surface obtains the right properties for the purpose, friction and wear are reduced and the tool’s durability and lifetime increase.

AP&T has also introduced a new energy-efficient furnace with automation for press hardening. The new system – Multilayer Feeder Furnace – reduces heat loss by two-thirds compared to conventional roller hearth furnaces, it told ISMR.

Dr. Martin Skrikerud, Vice Chief Technical Officer at AP&T explained “to achieve a high level of reliability and low maintenance costs, we have simple and robust furnace modules that can be used independently of each other. Thus, production can continue even if one of the furnace modules is not in operation.”

Spanish press manufacturer, ARISA SA, places a special focus on mechanical presses and their main accessories using its own technology. The first patent on clutch-brake mechanisms and stroke change were registered by the company in 1957. At present, the Arisa R&D team includes specialists in mechanics, hydraulics and automation areas.

It collaborates with the Mechanical Engineering Department of the Engineering School of La Rioja University on calculations for “Kinematic Synthesis” and “Dynamic Synthesis” press command mechanisms.

The Automation unit has effectively contributed to the modernisation of the ARISA product range, designing various levels of easy-to-use autoset systems, which reduce set-up and die-change times, avoid possible operator errors and offer a diagnosis of malfunctions as well as remote assistance via modem from the Arisa Technical Service Centre. The projects developed by the R&D team have been incorporated into machines manufactured by ARISA, introducing several important technical and functional improvements to the machines.

## An eye on innovation

BRUDERER UK will be celebrating a £500,000 deal at the MACH 2014 exhibition in April 2014 to deliver a ‘world first’. The company will be supplying a high-performance automatic stamping press to electronic component specialist, Harwin. The press will help the company increase efficiencies and attack new opportunities in aerospace, instrumentation and industrial markets.

The new press, the BSTA 280-75 with a BSV-75 high-speed servo feeder, represents a world first in that it integrates a planetary gearbox into the shaft of the main motor. This will allow Harwin’s engineers to have full control of tooling development to produce test runs of new products.

Chin Fong Machine Industrial Co., Ltd, established since 1948, is a significant manufacturer of mechanical power presses in Taiwan. Its product portfolio includes servo presses, straightside presses, C-type presses, transfer presses, automated tandem press lines and heavy stamping presses along with a range of associated automation. It has accumulated a number of sales records for tandem press lines in the automotive industry since 1986 with Toyota. “Over years of innovation and technical cooperation with Europe and Japan, we now manufacture our A0 line up to 54000KN with full automation. Customers include Toyota, Ford, Honda, Nissan etc,” the company told ISMR.
