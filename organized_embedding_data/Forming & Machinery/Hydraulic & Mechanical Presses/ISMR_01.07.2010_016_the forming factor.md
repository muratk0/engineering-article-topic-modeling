# THE FORMING FACTOR

Above: Twin rollformer

UK-based Formit is a specialist in rollforming technology, supplying rollforming and coil processing equipment including design, installation, project management and maintenance (around 80% of its business). In addition to rollformers, Formit can also supply press feed equipment from specialist manufacturers, including coil holders, straighteners and servo roll feeders.

If there is a special requirement for a customised line, then it will make its own equipment (special recoilers for tube, for example). A range of flying shears have been designed which, it says, are suitable for any application. Transfer systems, piercing, bending and welding can all be integrated into the final fully automated production line.

The company also offers a complete refurbishment service to bring machinery back to ‘as good as new’ standard. Exclusive partnership agreements with leading manufacturers, such as Elite (Taiwan), ensures that it can supply complete process lines including decoilers, pre-pierce systems, welding, flying cut-off presses and saws together with bending, pick and place stackers etc.

ISMR spoke to Formit’s founder and managing director, Jim Bain, to gauge his views on trends in the market, new customer requirements and the company’s near-term targets for growth.

## MARKET TRENDS AND REQUIREMENTS

“Customer requirements are definitely changing now – instead of just roll forming and then doing secondary operations to a roll formed section, they now want piercing, bending and welding in the line so that the product comes off the line as near ‘finished’ as possible,” he told ISMR.

“A lot of the lines we build now are used for products in the construction industry – purlins, cladding, roofing, windows, racking, shelving etc. – as well as steel furniture. Now, our lines will generally include piercing, notching, welding etc. capability to meet our customers’ requirements. The last few years have seen an increase in CNC controlled multiple-operation lines that include ancillary operations.

“Everyone also wants high speed now (from 50 up to 100m/minute) on their roll forming lines – it used to be 10-20m/minute. We are designing a lot of lines that can deliver two products at a time using in-line slitting in the rollformer which doubles the line output straight away. We have produced three of those lines in the last few months.”

In terms of regional trends, he points out that a lot of his customers’ production has moved to eastern Europe and Formit has accordingly produced lines for factories in Romania and the Czech Republic. The attraction of eastern Europe, he says, for a lot of his customers is the fact that goods can easily be delivered by road back into western Europe.

The global economic downturn meant that Formit sales were static in 2009. The company managed to avoid short time and were fortunate enough to pick up some large contracts in mid-2009 that enabled it to maintain full production and, in some cases, work overtime.
