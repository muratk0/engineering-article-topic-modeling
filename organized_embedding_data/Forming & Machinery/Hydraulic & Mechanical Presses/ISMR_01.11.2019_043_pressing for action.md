## A focus on servo

ISMR sat down with Javier Martinez-Aldama, Director General/CEO, Nidec Arisa (part of Nidec Press & Automation) at this year's Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart, where the new GS2 series servo press was on display.

With developmental input from Nidec Press & Automation engineers on three continents, the newly configured GS2 press series features integrated automation and manufacturing support. Nidec Arisa was one of the earliest innovators of servo press technology, with a significant installation base on five continents. The GS2 Series boasts a capacity range of 3150 to 8000kN and has been configured for manufacturing in North America, Europe and China. The product is backed by Nidec Press & Automation's global Customer Service team with 13 Technical Service Centre locations around the world.

"We have also launched a new EV press series to produce rotor stator components for electrical motors. The new global GS2 servo press (315-800 tons) can also be adapted for hot forming. It is a very flexible solution and we provide a range of accessories for it. Our modular approach reduces lead-time and is more affordable for customers," Javier Martinez-Aldama, Director General/CEO, Nidec Arisa, told ISMR.

"We are highlighting the real synergy of the NPA brands on the EV press that we are also promoting at this show – the press is made by Minster and the automation is developed by Vamco or SYS. We are very proud of the brand synergy behind our products. The global GS2 servo press demonstrates this and is a joint programme by the engineering teams in all the group companies."

He confirmed that the Group is working on new solutions to reduce noise levels by over 20 decibels. Its strategic focus has moved more towards servo presses to highlight issues such as energy management, better control etc.

"This means, ultimately, that customers can choose whether they want to reduce their electrical consumption," he told ISMR.

## Significant expansion

With new and expanded customer service centres on three continents, Nidec Press & Automation (NPA) is now able to expand its offering of press and component remanufacturing with shorter lead times. Presses can also be adapted for retrofit so that they can respond to future market needs.

"We have developed a global technical service to be closer to our customers over the last two-three years – particularly in Asia, North America, Europe etc.," outlined Javier Martinez-Aldama. "We have increased our presence in Asia, China, Germany and Japan and have also built dedicated technical service centres in Mexico and the United States.

"We now offer a 24/7 service for spare parts and technical assistance/service to customers anywhere in the world. We have also developed Industry 4.0 solutions to help highlight and optimise, for example, consumption and productivity etc. and are working on remote solutions for this."

Much NPA's facilities growth has been targeted toward the expansion of remanufacturing capabilities. With more than 120 years of manufacturing experience, Nidec Minster has been remanufacturing presses and press components for more than 70 years. Construction is underway on a 25,000 sq. ft. addition to Minster's dedicated service, repair parts, machining and remanufacturing facility at its world headquarters in Minster, Ohio (USA). The NPA Service Centre in Queretaro, Mexico, is also expanding with a new facility that will not only increase its warehouse space for common repair parts but will also greatly increase capabilities for remanufacturing larger presses on-site.

## FACE TO FACE

Nidec Arisa recently moved into a new US$ 30 million production facility in Navarrete, Spain, while the manufacturing operations for Kyori presses in Pinghu, China, are also undergoing major expansion. The facility improvements in Spain and China will not only boost new press production but will also provide the resources for remanufacturing all the NPA machinery brands on location.

NPA remanufactures all makes of presses, specialising in technology upgrades, new controls and safety requirements. All NPA remanufactured presses carry a two-year warranty, while rebuilt components come with a one-year warranty.

## Strategic and technical objectives

"It has been a very good year for the Group. I expect to see double-digit growth this year, compared to our turnover last year, and a lot of growth from new acquisitions," Martinez-Aldama told ISMR.

"For next year, we do see some uncertainties in the market. We have a strong order backlog to sustain us over next year, but the general feeling in the market is that everyone is waiting for new projects to be confirmed and launched with requirements for new materials and new processes. We need to be conservative in terms of expectations for the coming year."

The acquisition of SYS-DPA, earlier this year, allows the Group to offer a wider array of products and services, increase its global presence and combine the experience and synergies of group companies.

"We want to become more capable, in terms of services and total solutions to customers, to solve their requirements and offer new processes and new technologies. We are launching new presses and automation solutions that can also process materials such as high-strength steels, martensitic steels etc. We have also ensured that our presses can handle processes such as hot forming, for example," explained Martinez-Aldama.

"In terms of technical developments, we offer high-speed small presses made by Kyori and medium-sized, medium-speed presses by Minster. We also offer large/major installations and transfer presses, tandem lines etc. produced by Arisa. Each brand has its own engineering capability and benefits from the combined technical expertise of the other brands."

## An eye on the future

Martinez-Aldama also highlighted the importance of technologies such as AI (Artificial Intelligence) and Industry 4.0 to keep customers apprised, for example, of machine status updates, press data and connectivity.

"Customers always want strong technical and service support. They also want the latest technical solutions as well as new technologies, such as remote assistance and Industry 4.0, to optimise processes/ productivity, as well as, of course, to reduce costs. They want to be at the forefront of technological development and be more competitive," he told ISMR.

"Customers also want more flexibility and we have tried to adapt our products to meet not just their current, but also future, requirements. Industry 4.0 is a big umbrella. We view this, primarily, as remote diagnosis and assistance, machine self-learning systems and systems to help identify machine status and optimise productivity etc. Self-learning processes help machines/presses to adapt to changing conditions and production parameters. Presses must be able to do this automatically. This is a key area of focus for us and we will launch further solutions for this in the future.'

At FABTECH 2018, NPA launched its Field-Hawk, an IloT mobile application for remote production monitoring, as well as Optiservo, software developed by Nidec Arisa which matches the movements of a servo press to transfer tasks to achieve increased machine performance. NPA develops its own software with different partners e.g. Siemens. In terms of Augmented Reality, it already uses the technology in its sales systems but, explained Martinez-Aldama, this could also be adapted for training or service.

"We aim to keep investing in new technologies, such as Industry 4.0, but these also need to be affordable for customers. The size of the investments that we are making in this area may means a change to the dynamics of the service that we offer to customers, as well as to our relationships with suppliers etc.,' he explained.

"Our main challenge is to adapt our products to new customer requirements, new materials etc. To do this, we need to keep working closely with our customers to provide solutions to help them meet the challenges that they face now and in the future. We wil keep developing and maintaining very close collaborative partnerships with them."
