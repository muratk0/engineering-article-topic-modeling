Modern stamping and forming technologies offer manufacturers more sophisticated and capable options in the battle to form ever lighter, stronger and more flexible parts.

Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes, such as punching, blanking, embossing, bending, flanging and coining. This could be a single stage operation, where every stroke of the press produces the desired form on the sheet metal part, or could occur through a series of stages.

## Sheet metal solutions

Japanese press specialist, AIDA, is constantly investing in products, technology, manufacturing and service facilities to meet its customers’ changing production requirements. It pioneered servo press technology by independently developing and manufacturing its proprietary compact low-speed high-torque servo motors, suitable for press metalforming applications. AIDA was the first company in the world to develop and market direct-drive servo presses, delivering to date over 1000 servo presses worldwide.

AIDA Direct Servo Former (DSF) presses are AIDA’s new forming systems. AIDA-America showcased three Direct Drive Servo Formers (DSF Series) at the last FABTECH exhibition in the US.

With a new project organisation, AP&T has strengthened its ability to deliver complete sheet metal forming solutions with a focus on reduced cost of ownership for the customer. It has been delivering high quality equipment for sheet metal forming for fifty years. Today, the company is a global player and a majority of the projects are complete production lines for advanced forming where the single machine is part of a complex solution that delivers a function to its customers.

AP&T designs, manufactures and markets automation, presses, tools and complete production lines for the sheet metal and tube forming industry. It specialises in forming technology and automated processes, delivering complete production systems where it assumes complete supplier responsibility – a concept which it calls One Responsible Partner.

Press hardening technology experienced its major breakthrough ten years ago. AP&T produced functioning solutions early on, and thus got a head start when the technology made a commercial breakthrough. AP&T is also introducing new stop/start energy-saving technology into its hydraulic presses. The “Frequency Drive System” is similar to the start/stop function found in modern vehicles.

Above: Avure’s new isostatic pressing application centre in Västerås, Sweden

In September 2014, AP&T inaugurated a new development and production facility in Ulricehamn, Sweden. The company’s global headquarters is now located there.

Avure Technologies, a leading Swedish manufacturer of ultra-high pressure systems, recently established an Application Centre to assist fabricators utilising advanced materials in the development of hot and cold isostatic pressing applications. The facility, next to the Avure manufacturing plant, is equipped with two Hot Isostatic Presses (HIPs) and one Cold Isostatic Press (CIP).

“Traditionally, HIP has been used for densification, but the trend is now to combine densification with solution treatment, quenching, ageing, and/or other heat treatment processes in the same cycle,” explained Avure’s Peter Henning, Business Unit Director-AMD. “This leads to significant savings in lead-time, improved product quality and lower costs and energy savings resulting from fewer transports and re-heating.”
