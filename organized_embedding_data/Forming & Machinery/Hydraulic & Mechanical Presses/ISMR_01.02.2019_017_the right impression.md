# THE RIGHT IMPRESSION

“New stamping techniques, concepts and press models come under the spotlight in this article.”

Stamping is the use of a moving ram to transmit force, or an amount of tonnage, to specific tooling to achieve a product's final shape, often with little or no scrap and, whenever possible, with minimal operator intervention. A wide variety of parts can be formed in different press sizes and types. Systems include C-frame presses; automatic blanking presses; knuckle joint presses; servo presses; hydroforming presses or conventional mechanical transfer/progressive die presses. Greater flexibility, shorter cycle times and a focus on energy conservation are just some of the hallmarks of the latest technologies.

A stamping press shapes or cuts metal by deforming it with a die. Stamping (also known as pressing) is the process of placing flat sheet metal, in either blank or coil form, into a stamping press where a tool and die surface forms the metal into a net shape. Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes such as blanking; punching; piercing; bend/draw forming; embossing; flanging and coining. This could be a single-stage operation, where every stroke of the press produces the desired form on the sheet metal part or could occur through a 'progressive' series of stages.

A wide range of materials, including stainless and cold/hot-rolled steel; hardened and tempered steel; aluminium; copper; titanium and specialty alloys, can be processed. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing innovations.

## Forming in focus

AIDA is a global specialist in the design, manufacture, sale, service and support, refurbishment and modernisation of metal stamping presses and metal forming automation equipment. Its servo and mechanical stamping presses range in capacity from 35 to 4,000 tons, from 1 to 1,500 strokes per minute, from gap frame to straight-side, high-speed to cold forging, transfer and progressive die, material handling and coil-feeding equipment.

At the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover (October 2018), it showcased its MSP high-speed stamping presses for the production of motors such as EV motors, HEV motors and energy-efficient home appliances motors. Dynamic accuracy has been significantly improved with their compact design and multiple suspension point architecture. The presses, which feature Adjust in Motion functionality, are suitable for production of parts with thicknesses of less than 0.20mm.

AIDA-America recently released the next generation of its Allen-Bradley-based DSF Series servo press control for servo presses from 315-3,500 tons. This control utilises an Allen-Bradley Compact Logix PLC for press operations, diagnostics and safety on a 19-inch Allen-Bradley PC touch-screen OIT.

"Infinitely programmable stroke and velocity profiles allow the user to achieve higher productivity than a mechanical press and better precision than a hydraulic press," said the press specialist.
