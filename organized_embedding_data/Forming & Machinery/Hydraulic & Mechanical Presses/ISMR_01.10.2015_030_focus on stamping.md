# FOCUS ON STAMPING

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

Lien Chieh Machinery Co. Ltd. (LCM), a Taiwanese press specialist and family business, was established in 1947. Its product portfolio includes a range of presses for die spotting; die tryout; hydroforming; laminating; deep drawing; hemming and forging. Every press it makes is custom-tailored to suit user requirements.

The Taiwanese pressmaker has sold hydraulic presses directly to automotive OEMs such as Ford, Toyota and Honda. It is currently turning its attention to a range of new hot forming and mechanical press developments.

Its R&D department has increased by around 7% over the last few years as its engineering expertise increases in line with higher demands for more sophisticated press design. It has also increased its total workforce by around 10-12%.

Selling into a range of markets such as automotive, aerospace, marine and electronics, its factory is based in Yang-Mei (35 minutes south of Taipei’s international airport and 15 minutes away from Taipei City). All its hydraulic presses, from 50 – 20,000 tonnes, are assembled in-house.

Nowadays, customers want entire production lines, automation and complex working parts to process different materials, not just single presses.

## Made in Taiwan

“From the very beginning we have continued to focus on our ability to provide hydraulic presses with optimum features emphasizing performance, quality, reliability and safety. Our 'after-market' support services are critically important to us and we maintain dedicated resources for this sole purpose,” explained Patrick Wang, its GM, to ISMR.

LCM’s new products include a 7500-ton hydraulic bulldozer press designed for mining equipment companies located in the United States and a 12,000-ton single cylinder hydraulic hot forging press, with a bolster area of 2700mm x 2500mm, used to manufacture aluminium alloy rims for commercial vehicles in Taiwan. The company also produces a fully automated highspeed hydraulic tandem line. Another revenue stream is complete project management of press lines for a customer’s press shop.

LCM presses contain features such as slide safety locks, safety light curtains, fault monitoring, customisable HMI screen and sound levels below 83dB when operating at full tonnage. Its main markets are North America, China, North and South Asia.

“Asia is a booming market for us,” continued Patrick Wang. “We primarily supply automotive OEM customers from the US whose factories are in China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Japan, India and the US. Ford, for example, has sourced presses from us for its factories in India. Some of our customers are Japanese automotive manufacturers. We want to start supplying more customers in Europe. China is the strongest market for us. Our second biggest market is North America.

“LCM is the only Taiwanese press maker to sell hydraulic presses directly to Ford Motor, Toyota Motor and Honda Motor directly and not through a Tier One supplier. We sold eleven hydraulic presses to Ford in September (2500 tonnes). We also sell hydraulic presses to GEELY, China directly. We have been very successful at exporting our products – approximately 95% of our production is exported to around 1000 customers around the world. We want to increase our European market share and already have customers in the UK and Poland.”

He believes that 2015 will be a steady year for the company with no startling highs or lows. He has progressively increased the marketing of LCM hot stamping presses in the Chinese and Indian markets. These are the two largest markets in Asia for LCM and he believes that there is great potential for this technology for carmakers in those markets. In India, carmakers produce small, compact cars (1-1.5 litre engines) and hot stamping technology is well suited to this type of automotive production.

LCM is continuously making process improvements to its hot stamping press technology – engineers examine issues such as reducing the time spent making components, overall process improvements etc. The hot stamping process involves high-temperature sheet metal forming, followed by immediate cold quenching of the sheet metal inside the tool. The process ensures high mechanical strength properties of the metal as well as significant weight savings (30% or so) so that lighter, stronger cars can be manufactured.

Another of Wang’s priorities is the development of large mechanical stamping presses, which he intends to launch at the end of this year. These presses will range from 1000 to 2500 tons. He has also started introducing servo motor technology into smaller LCM presses.

## Trends and objectives

“Nowadays, customers want entire production lines, automation and complex working parts to process different materials, not just single presses. They want a solution. We have a lot of experience to help them to produce complex parts. If they give us a spec., we will figure out how to produce this for them. We also work with a range of automation partners on a press line so that our customers will have a complete solution,” explained Patrick Wang.

“The overall trend I see is towards more lightweight, stronger and more complex part production. Hot stamping has become more popular for manufacturing A and B pillars. Our presses are used extensively in the automotive industry but they are also used to produce, for example, aerospace wing mounts, rice cookers, sinks and bathtubs etc. A customer from Egypt makes light covers using our presses. Our hydroforming presses are used to produce mufflers, for example. Most manufacturers are now looking for higher strength materials, safety and the best possible production/manufacturing processes for their requirements.”
