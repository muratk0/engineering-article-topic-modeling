# A SMOOTH FINISH

[TARİH: 01.09.2024 ISMR]

As an investment in future-ready crane production, Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH has introduced a new automated deburring line for cut parts at its factory in Biberach, Germany. Its line partners are ARKU, Rösler and Teqram.

As a new global première for machinery developed by ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH, Rösler Oberflächentechnik GmbH and Teqram B.V, Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH has introduced a new automated deburring line for cut parts at its production site at Biberach, Germany.

Working with specialists from ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH (Germany), Rösler Oberflächentechnik GmbH (Germany) and Teqram B.V. (The Netherlands), Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH has automated the edge rounding of plasma, oxyfuel and laser-cut metal parts. The machinery will aid Liebherr's production of high-quality tower cranes and mobile construction cranes.

Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH is one of the largest manufacturers of tower cranes, with a workforce of around 1,600 employees at the Biberach plant producing a range of lifting technologies for construction sites.

More than seven decades of experience have made Liebherr a recognised specialist in lifting technology for all types of construction sites. The range of Liebherr tower cranes encompasses an extensive selection of high-quality tower cranes that are used worldwide. This includes fast-erecting, top-slewing, luffing jibs and special-purpose cranes as well as mobile construction cranes. Liebherr also offers a wide range of services that complete the company's portfolio: Tower Crane Solutions, the Tower Crane Centre and Tower Crane Customer Service.

## Opting for automation

The new deburring line plays a key part in the production of high-quality tower cranes and mobile construction cranes.

"Removing slag, descaling, deburring and rounding edges: the new system does it all. This means that metal parts weighing up to 200kg can be optimally prepared for subsequent production steps. The parts are then turned into components for crane jibs and tower sections. Robots with the latest camera technology position the parts, which are up to 1,250mm wide and 100mm thick, and remove them again after processing," surface finishing specialist, Liebherr, told SMR.

"Until now, our edge-rounding work has always been done manually," explained Robert Piesche, Production Technology Engineer, Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH. Despite high safety standards being in place, there was still a certain risk of accidents happening with this job and its use of angle grinders.

"Automating processes means that we can free employees from monotonous and physically demanding work. They are also therefore exposed to less dirt and noise and we have been able to significantly increase occupational safety," he continued.

Liebherr brought together three experienced partners in metal processing and robot technology, ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH, Rösler Oberflächentechnik GmbH and Teqram B.V., with a view to fully automating the work processes.

"This set-up, as it exists at our plant, is the first to bring together components from this trio of companies," explained Dominic Kreutle, Head of Production Technology, Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH. One of the greatest challenges was smoothly integrating the various system interfaces.

The robot systems with various grippers are supplied by Dutch specialist, Teqram B.V. Equipped with intelligent cameras, the 'EasyBots' can recognise parts in their work area independently and regardless of their position on the pallet. This means that the usual time-consuming teaching or programming of each robot is no longer necessary. The robots also have the option of automated processing for parts that are too large for the line. With access to various tools, such as chisels and angle grinders as well as advanced camera and sensor technology, they are designed to finish parts with precision.
