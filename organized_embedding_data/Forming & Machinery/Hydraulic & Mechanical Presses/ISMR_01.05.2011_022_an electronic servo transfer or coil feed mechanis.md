# An electronic servo transfer or coil feed mechanism can deliver increased production speeds and greater efficiency

[TARİH: 01.05.2011 ISMR]

AIDA, Schuler, SEYI and Minster/Amada, make use of servo technology in their presses.

AIDA is a leader in Direct Drive Servo technology. Its ServoPro features high torque/low RPM servo motors designed and built by AIDA specifically for stamping press applications. Connected directly to the crank, these servo motors provide precise accuracy and reliability. The result is improvement in almost every critical area of press performance. Presses featuring ServoPro, like the NC1-D Gap Frame Servo Press, have the same torque and energy characteristics as conventional mechanical presses.

AIDA’s ServoPro servoforming technology allows customers to select short stroke options without sacrificing speed. With the ability to program up to 20 different velocities for a single stroke, ServoPro makes it possible for one press to do the job of several presses. AIDA was involved in the development of the ‘world’s fastest servo press line’ with HONDA last year. Both companies developed a revolutionary servo press line that enables the deep draw-forming of automotive body panels at the tremendously high productivity levels (max. of 18 strokes per minute – SPM) that was not possible using conventional press lines, and installed it in the HONDA Motor Company’s Suzuka Plant.

Currently AIDA has more than 2,000 presses operating in the United States, 400 in Canada, and 80,000 worldwide, with capacities ranging from 20 to 4,000 metric tons. It manufactures a complete line of metalforming presses, including gap, straightside, progressive die, transfer, high speed, cold forging and servo-driven mechanical presses.

It recently launched a high-strength steel forming press where, at the first forming stage, the shape of the 100kg high-strength steel is set, thereby achieving powerful and accurate forming that was impossible using conventional methods. The second and subsequent stages utilise AIDA’s high-speed transfer technology to effectively leverage the existing equipment to enable efficient production.

Founded in 1962, SEYI (ShiehYih Machinery) in Taiwan has established a strong position in the press building industry over the past 48 years. Press products include servo presses, single/double crank presses, straightside presses, high speed presses and a variety of press ancillaries. Current joint ventures with Japanese press maker Amino, H & F (Hitachi Zosen Fukui Corp.) & LEM in developing hydraulic, straightside and precision high speed presses add to SEYI’s technical abilities.

SEYI has launched a new generation 200-tonne servo press with the latest servo motor and control technology specifically for stamping applications.

“The servo motor offers three times higher torque than other market servo motors and 30 per cent torque higher than other direct-drive servo presses,” SEYI told ISMR. “The servo direct drive technology is connected directly to the crank and provides the highest flexibility of stroke motion programming such as pendulum strokes, multiple hits in one stroke and longer dwell time. The energy storage system eliminates the issue of higher power requirements for installation and saves more energy cost than other servo presses. By the third quarter of 2011, the servo press product line (from 110-400 tonnes) will be ready for market.

“Servo presses have become more popular in the past five years. The press combines the advantages of hydraulic presses, fully programmable slide stroke function, means control tonnage, speed and dwell at any point of the entire stroke and the speed of a mechanical press. This enables users to form more complicated geometric parts, in exotic materials, and eliminates many secondary operations.”

Taping in the die also gets easier with servo presses. Dwell profiles help stampers to eliminate material spring-back and make higher quality parts. Fast ram speed at die-closing and die-opening segments, and slow ram velocity segment at the impact point of cutting, blanking or forming in one stroke, achieves higher production rates. Reduced die reverse loads, prolonged die maintenance cycle time and less stamping noise/vibration are other key benefits for the production of high tensile material components. The other major advantage of the servo press is the high energy saving generated compared to conventional presses.

“Consumers are paying more and more attention to energy saving and carbon reduction,” said Cyrus Kuo, General Manager at SEYI. “The future development of commercial products has been a trend toward lighter, thinner, shorter, smaller parts with greater energy savings and an accent on environmental protection. Based on this, we believe more metalforming applications for servo presses will be applied and this is just the beginning….”

This sentiment is echoed by SEYI’s CEO. “We will continue to develop the servo technology on our presses,” Claire Kuo, SEYI CEO, told ISMR. “Asia is still very much a volume market using a lot of traditional mechanical presses but customers there are starting to look seriously at servo technology now.”
