# WATERJET

[TARİH: 01.12.2010 ISMR]

## NEW WATERJET ADVANCES

Since being commercialised in the late 1970s, waterjet machining has leapfrogged from merely a niche cutting tool to a mainstream precision tool competing on an equal footing with other machine tools. There are inherent merits of waterjet machining such as material independence, multimode machining capability and preservation of structural integrity of machined parts etc. The cost effectiveness, fast turnaround and low capital costs have attracted users from small and large businesses.

“Manufacturers have discovered that waterjet machining can complete most parts from design to finish in two to three days or sooner. Such fast turnaround is perfect for the ‘just-in-time’ practice as a key element of lean manufacturing cannot be accomplished by outsourcing the same parts abroad. As a result, waterjet machine tools have emerged as the fastest growing segment of the general machine tool industry in the last decade. Yet, there is considerable room for this emerging technology to grow as the market share of waterjet machine tools is less than US$ 1B out of the US$ 50B machine tool industry. A survey by Frost and Sullivan has also indicated that the lack of awareness of this technology is one of the key impediments preventing it from growing even faster,” waterjet manufacturer, OMAX, told ISMR.

As the role of waterjet machining expands, its market share is expected to increase proportionally.

Flow International Corporation conducted live demonstrations of the Mach 4 system with Dynamic Waterjet XD at FABTECH in Atlanta recently.

The Mach 4 is the flagship of the Mach series and combines Flow’s HyperJet® pump, rated at 94,000 psi, and Dynamic Waterjet XD, high-precision flat stock and 3D cutting technology.

“Just as increased wattage allows lasers to increase cut speed, waterjets equipped with HyperPressure systems cut as much as 50% faster or more than conventional waterjets. Parts are cut faster, with a lower cost per part, and production runs are shorter,” Flow told ISMR.

Also during FABTECH, Jet Edge introduced the new energy-efficient, servo-driven ECUBE 6200 intelligent waterjet pump and the Idro line 5-axis water jet cutting system. Jet Edge also demonstrated its 90,000 psi (6,200 bar) X-Stream waterjet intensifier pump technology and Mid Rail Gantry precision water jet cutting machine.

KMT Waterjet Systems launched the KMT Streamline PRO® 100,000 psi/7.000 bar waterjet pump at Fabtech.

“In addition to increasing linear cutting by more than 100%, the second generation of the KMT Streamline PRO® Series of Ultra High Pressure Pumps cuts non-linear aluminum, steel, titanium, and stainless steel up to 75% faster than standard waterjet systems cutting at 60,000 psi/4.137 bar,” KMT told ISMR.

MDC Max Daetwyler AG and specialist water jet company Waterjet AG, in collaboration with NUM, have developed an innovative CNC-based water jet cutter capable of cutting materials to within ±1 micron.

OMAX Corporation, which specializes in direct drive waterjet pump technology, has now developed its new EnduroMAX pump for its JetMachining® Centers, which provides double the operating life of previous pumps and makes for faster part processing, lower operating costs and easier maintenance.

OMAX also released Intelli-ETCH, a new software tool that allows the company’s abrasive waterjet machining centres to create 3D patterns in a wide array of materials, and the new A-Jet (articulated jet).

Water Jet Sweden machines are capable of cutting virtually any sheet or plate material to extremely small tolerances, up to a maximum of 300mm thick.

Linear drives now come as standard on all WJS models from August 2010. The advantage of the programmable positioning of independent cutting heads, that comes with having linear drives, allows users to increase the scope and speed of production.
