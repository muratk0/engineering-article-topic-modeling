Deepening its hold into the Chinese market and its requirements for cars, modern home appliances and construction hardware, it has invested USD 45 million in its new 150,000 sq. m. plant in Huai’an. It has also concluded strategic alliances with the Japanese IHI group and Yamada Dobby to offer advanced technologies on super large tandem presses line and ultra-high speed stamping presses.

Fagor Arrasate designs and manufactures presses and stamping systems, supplying presses from 630KN up to 150,000KN, using mechanical, hydraulic or updated servo-driven technology depending on the needs of users. It has developed a new range of servo-driven mechanical presses. The main feature of servodriven presses is the elimination of the inertia flywheel and its replacement with direct coupling between the main drive motor of the press and the slide movement transmission.

Komatsu's H2w is an eccentric servo press suitable for progressive, transfer or manual die operations. It features an independent servo drive system with auto die height adjustment. H2w tonnage is 200–300 tonnes.

The noise signature of Komatsu’s servo press is also small. Blanking noise can be further reduced by carrying out ‘two-step blanking’ where the punch is stopped halfway through the blanking process and then started again. Servo presses do not employ flywheels or clutch brakes, and they feature a lower number of gears than conventional mechanical presses which enhances maintainability.

“Because there are no elements such as clutch or brake, little press lubricant is needed,” added Komatsu. At present, Komatsu servo presses use four types of driving mechanisms. They also include a plunger guide system which absorbs thrust load from the eccentric motion. A size-specific plunger guide is engineered for each different press model.

The Minster P2H-FX Series Servo press line, from Nidec-Minster, is designed for applications requiring alternative slide motion. The Minster P2H-FX combines the characteristics of a traditional mechanically-driven press with those of a servo drive. The presses are available in 63-, 100- and 160- ton sizes.

The new MSD 250 servo press from Schuler offers greater performance with the same press force and reduced energy consumption. To date, Schuler has sold about 340 presses with ServoDirect technology. Monoblock machines with a welded press structure are particularly popular, and can be delivered in one piece. Now, the smallest machine in this series, the MSD 250 with a press force of 250 metric tons, has been completely revised.

The new MSD 250 can be installed directly at floor level and has a height of 4400mm, making it more than one metre lower than the previous model which was 5430mm tall. This is a consequence of reducing the slide stroke from a maximum of 200mm to 160mm. The die shut height has also been reduced from 600mm to 550mm. The height of the bolster plate above floor level has been reduced from 1480mm to 1200mm. There is also a positive side-effect – a press operator can look into the die much more easily if required.

The new length of the bolster, 2000mm, with the same depth means that not only the height but also the footprint of the new MSD 250 is smaller. The compact design also has positive effects on the rigidity of the overall system - it is 30% higher, and consequently the accuracy of the produced components is greater. At the same time, die wear and the cutting impact are reduced. The small servo press can also score points when it comes to speed. Whereas the maximum stroke rate at maximum stroke height used to be 70 strokes per minute, 90 are now possible – even 160 (formerly 140) in pendular stroke mode. Power consumption in pendular stroke mode is more than 20% lower – in full stroke mode even more than 30% – despite the fact that the new development no longer has an energy accumulator.

New Schuler presses have been successfully using ServoDirect technology for several years now. With the aid of Schuler’s patented Dualdrive technology, it is now possible to retrofit conventional flywheel presses with this new technology. Such upgrades can boost the productivity of existing presses by up to 50 per cent.

The Göppingen-based press manufacturer also launched a completely new drive concept: TwinServo technology (TST). This further development of the company’s ServoDirect technology (SDT) features two de-centralised servo motors in the press bed. This new design makes it possible to combine properties and characteristics which used to be regarded as contradictory. The result: benefits for the user which cannot be achieved with conventional press designs.

In the TST design, the slide is no longer pushed downward but pulled downward: “This makes the force flow much more direct and thus reduces deflection by around 30%,” explained Thomas Spiesshofer, Technical Manager, Automotive Press Technology at Schuler. “Favourable leverage effects with force application points toward the outer edges result in geometrically enhanced conditions and offcentre force applications.” This improves tilting resistance by up to 400% compared to conventional machines.

As the force application points on the slide are located further toward the edge, the entire clamping surface can now be used: “This means we can achieve both greater eccentricities as well as greater eccentric loads in absolute terms which is particularly important for multi-station presses,” explained Frank Viola, Sales Manager, Automotive Press Technology at Schuler.

SEYI products have been sold to customers in over 40 countries around the world and the company is a dominant foreign supplier to Mainland China, India, South East Asia and the Americas in terms of volume. With its advanced research & development capabilities, it has developed high precision presses for the 3C and small components industries as well as large panel presses for automotive applications. With subsidiaries in the US, China and now Europe, the Taiwanese press manufacturer is expanding its reach globally.

At EuroBLECH, SEYI launched its new line of single-point and double-point servo presses in Europe. Offered from 80T – 400T, they are equipped with up-to-date direct drive servo motor and control technology, designed specifically for servo-forming applications.

“The servo direct drive technology eliminates down-gearing, belts and unnecessary linkages and has very short distance of transmission, helping to maximise torque distribution. A user-friendly Point-On-Demand (POD) controller provides 8 pre-set stroke profiles such as pendulum strokes, multiple hits in one stroke, and extended dwell time, as well as free-hand programmability. Our energy-saving storage system eliminates the issue of higher power requirements and power surges,” SEYI explained.

SEYI servo press frames are engineered with a ribbed frame construction, offering more rigidity - especially during longer dwells - and less deflection. The hydraulic overload has been removed and replaced with an electronic overload, providing more stable protection when working closer to rated tonnage for long periods of time. The electronic overload is also programmable.
