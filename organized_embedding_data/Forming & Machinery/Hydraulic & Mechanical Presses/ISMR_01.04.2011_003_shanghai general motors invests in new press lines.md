# SHANGHAI GENERAL MOTORS INVESTS IN NEW PRESS LINES

[TARİH: 01.04.2011 ISMR]

THE CAR PLANT of the future is expected to run more and more like clockwork. This is perfectly illustrated when new factories are built and the complete manufacturing process has to be precisely coordinated right from the planning stage. The latest example is a new press shop for Shanghai General Motors (SGM) in Shenyang, north-east China, to be constructed on a greenfield site. Production of stamped parts for two car models is due to start as soon as mid-2012.

The forming experts at Schuler have been chosen as partners for the project. They will be installing two new press lines for the manufacturing of around 40 different body panel parts. This represents the second round of a successful partnership with SGM: technology supplied by the Schuler group is already being successfully employed at a different facility. The decisive factor for SGM were the specifications of the lines, which fit seamlessly with the company’s declared aim of establishing ‘perfect manufacturing logistics’ in Shenyang.

At the moment, SGM’s facility in Shenyang does not have its own press shop: parts have to be brought in from a group facility in the north-eastern city of Yantai, 600 kilometres away. The two identical press lines will feature a draw press with 20,000kN of press force and three follow-on presses with 10,000kN of force each. Both press lines will be equipped with automation systems which can transport even large body panels between presses, as well as handling part discharge, part repositioning and part placement into the subsequent forming station. The lines produce at a rate of 14 strokes per minute.

At the same time, both press lines can be quickly and flexibly set up for different parts several times per shift. Following a fully automatic die change, it takes less than three minutes for the lines to start producing again. This is especially important for SGM, as the company produces mainly lean batch sizes. With such a fast and flexible system, the company can optimise valuable production time and thus save costs.

Major parts of the two press lines will be finally assembled at the Schuler group’s Chinese facility in Dalian. Key components such as the press bed and crown will be supplied from Germany, while the uprights will be manufactured at Schuler’s facility in Shanghai. This means that the design and final assembly of the lines can be discussed in detail in China and closely coordinated with the various planning stages for the new press shop.

SHEET METAL EVENTS GATHER PACE

FOLLOWING THE SUCCESS of BLECH Russia 2011 (15 – 17 March 2011, at St Petersburg’s Lenexpo Exhibition Centre), where exhibitors from 20 countries presented their products and services to the Russian sheet metal working industry, the second BLECH India exhibition will take place from 14 – 17 April 2011 at the Bombay Exhibition Centre in Mumbai.

“The show is more than three times bigger than last time. The huge demand for stand space at this trade exhibition is due to the continuously booming industry on the Indian subcontinent, in particular in the automotive and construction sector. However, the Indian industry still largely depends on technology imports to be able to cover the growing demand of India’s consumers for goods such as small cars. Forming machines, in particular, are in need.

“Although India’s machine tool industry expects an increase of 35 – 40 per cent for 2010 / 2011 compared with the previous year, it still cannot cover the domestic demand for sheet metal working machines,” the organiser, Mack Brooks, told ISMR.

With BLECH Nordic, Mack Brooks Exhibitions offers a new trade exhibition geared towards the Northern European sheet metal working industry. The first BLECH Nordic Exhibition for Sheet Metal Working will be held from 4 – 7 October 2011 at Stockholmsmässan in Stockholm, Sweden.

BLECH Nordic 2011 will take place alongside the Scandinavian Technical Fair, the renowned trade exhibition for the Scandinavian manufacturing industry, featuring some 300 exhibitors and around 25,000 trade visitors. FOR MORE INFORMATION, SEE www.blechevents.com
