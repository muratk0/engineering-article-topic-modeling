Pneumatic clamps designed for robotic applications

Elesa offers Misati pneumatic clamps for workholding and transport of assemblies in manufacturing operations. Designed for robotic use, the clamps feature simple power and control connections that enable rapid installation and setup. Applications include warehousing, welding, framing and assembly, jigs and fixture manufacturing, and die- and moldmaking.

The clamping action features mechanical interlock at closure with an integral nonreversible ramp mechanism that takes over when the jaws are closed. This helps ensure that peak loads are transferred directly to the body structure of the clamp and not reliant on the pneumatic pressure.

Zirconia grinding discs reduce heat buildup

Superior Abrasives LLC has introduced SHUR-KUT Zirconia Grinding Aid Quick Change Discs. Their longlasting, durable grains and sharp cutting action allow for extended stock removal with low heat generation, the company reports. The cooler cut reduces friction and premature breakdown of grains and backing to help lengthen disc life.

A hybrid grain exposes a fresh cutting edge during use for aggressive grinding on stainless steel and exotic alloys, as well as ferrous and nonferrous materials. The discs are suitable for general-purpose grinding and stock removal, deburring or parting line removal on cast parts, spot weld removal and blending, mold and cavity grinding and refurbishing, and grinding and preparing hot castings.

They are available for type S or type R, in 1½-, 2-, and 3-in. dia. for use with right-angle die grinders. Grits are available from 36 to 120, with optimal speeds of 7,000 to 13,000 RPM and maximum speeds from 20,000 to 30,000 RPM.

Adapter aids machining with long milling tools

Walter has introduced the Accure·tec AC001 adapter, a patented damping system for vibration-free milling when using long tools. It is suitable for deep-pocket milling in operations at depths of up to 4xD.

The vibration damping is provided by the axially and radially flexible positioned damper element that is preset at the factory so tools can be used immediately without fine-tuning.

Applications include components with deep cavities in aircra construction and the aerospace industry in general, as well as the automotive, energy, die/mold, and general metalworking industries.

Pipe cutting saw and track cuts square with no HAZ

Esco Tool has introduced the APS-438 air-powered saw and WrapTrack trolley system for cutting stainless and carbon steel pipe from 6 to 60 in. dia. The trolley guides the saw around the circumference of the pipe without wobbling to produce perfectly square cuts, the company states. The saw also produces no HAZ, leaving a smooth finish for end prepping or joining pipes with couplings.

The system is offered in 28 sizes of stainless steel bands that clamp onto the pipe and accept the trolleymounted saw. The operator maintains the fiberglass-reinforced blade’s leading edge on the pipe to produce a cut with ±1/16-in. accuracy.

Circular cold saws use low-maintenance electromechanical components

Linsinger has released the new Raptor line of circular cold saws. Hydraulics have been eliminated, so components such as pumps, pipelines, hoses, and accumulators have been removed. This contributes to quiet operation and a reduction in the cost of consumables such as hydraulic oil, seals, and filters.

The line uses low-maintenance electromechanical components with high energy efficiency. These components help improve positioning and repetition accuracy, and when paired with thin-cut circular saw blades, they help ensure optimum usage of the material over the entire length.

Depending on the material dimension, the clamping jaws of the machine can be prepositioned simply into an optimized initial position, which helps save time on each sawing cycle. The clamping force can be varied depending on the material or wall thickness of pipes and profiles, helping to prevent deformation.

Picosecond black marking designed for medical devices

Amada Weld Tech Inc. offers black, corrosion-resistant marking capability for the medical device manufacturing industry. It is generated using a picosecond laser. The ultrashort pulse durations of the picosecond laser allow it to impart energy to a material surface with almost no thermal effect.

The mark is an extremely high-contrast periodic nanostructure with antireflective properties that make it appear deep black against its surroundings. Since it is restructured surface material rather than an oxide layer, the mark resists bacterial growth, passivation, corrosion, and autoclaving.

Dew point sensor helps eliminate porosity during welding

Huntingdon Fusion Techniques HFT has developed the PurgeNet Dew Point Purging Gas Moisture Sensor for measuring the dew point for critical welds. It predicts when water will form on metal surfaces, which can cause porosity and create cracking in the weld joint.

The sensor is designed to fit any of the Argweld Weld Purge Monitors that read down to 1 PPM. Data is displayed on the monitor’s screen in split format, so that both oxygen and moisture content are shown as PPM value.

Robotic bending system features automated toolchanging press brake

LVD Strippit has introduced the Ulti-Form robotic bending system, which incorporates an automated tool-changing press brake to handle small batches and long production runs with minimal changeover time, unattended.

The system features a 135-ton press brake designed using the ToolCell platform, integrated with an industrial robot. The press brake houses a built-in tooling warehouse and uses a gripper mechanism in the machine’s backgauge fingers to change tools. As the robot picks the first workpiece from the input stack and centers it, the press brake completes the tool change.

The system handles parts from 1.9 by 3.9 in. to 47 by 31 in. weighing up to 55 lbs.
