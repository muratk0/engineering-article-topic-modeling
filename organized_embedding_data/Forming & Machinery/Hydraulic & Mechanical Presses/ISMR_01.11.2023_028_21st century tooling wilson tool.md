# 21ST CENTURY TOOLING WILSON TOOL

[TARİH: 01.11.2023 ISMR]

ISMR met Wilson Tool's CEO, Paul Johnson, and its Global Vice President of Sales, Ron Palick, at Blechexpo to discuss the company's latest innovations and market views.

Wilson Tool is an independent manufacturer of tooling systems for punch presses, press brakes and punch and die components for the stamping and tableting industries. Since 1966, it has been a leading provider of tooling solutions designed to enhance the performance of sheet-metal fabricating machinery worldwide. The company celebrated 55 years in business in 2021.

Products are manufactured at Wilson Tool's global headquarters in White Bear Lake, Minn., USA, as well as at manufacturing facilities in England; Canada; Brazil and Italy. Wilson Tool's network of sales engineers and international distributors operate in most industrialised countries.

The company showcased a range of sheetmetal tooling innovations (including bending and punching tools, plus accessories) on its stand at this November's Blechexpo/Schweisstec exhibition in Stuttgart, Germany. ISMR caught up with Wilson Tool's CEO, Paul Johnson, and its Global Vice President of Sales, Ron Palick, at the exhibition to discuss the company's latest innovations and market views.

## New product launches

Innovative tooling solutions exhibited on Wilson Tool's stand at Blechexpo/Schweisstec 2023 included EXP® Punch technology for a longer-lasting tool, reducing the frequency of sharpening and machine downtime. Clamping systems for new and old press brakes, designed to speed up tool changeovers and reduce downtime, as well as custom press brake tooling designed to reduce the number of set-ups and improve part quality were also on display. Visitors to the exhibition could also see in-machine tapping tools that eliminate the need for hand tapping threads and reduce part handling for operators.

L-R: Wilson Tool's Global Vice President of Sales, Ron Palick, and its CEO, Paul Johnson, at the exhibition.

"We are showcasing our new E (electric) clamp here in Stuttgart; we also displayed it at the FABTECH exhibition in Chicago. There is nothing in the market quite like it right now. Most clamping systems on press brakes today are hydraulic with parts that wear out at the worst times. The advent of electric means that if you have a failure on one part, customers can take it out and put in another one. There is no need for a service technician and the system is designed to make bending faster and more efficient (doing away with wear parts on clamping systems)," explained Ron Palick, Global Vice President of Sales, Wilson Tool.

"We feel that this will be a real gamechanger. The feedback has been great so far and we are confident that it will do well. Early Q3 2024 is our delivery target and we are taking orders from OEMs right now. It's all about giving customers an edge and an environmental advantage," he continued.

From the first computer grinder patented before the company experienced significant growth, Wilson Tool has gone on to receive patents for the first "shimless" punch assembly; the first punch press tooling with interchangeable punch tips; forming tools that rolled on, rather than penetrated the sheet, and many others. To date, the company has been granted 91 patents.

"If you look at our history, innovation has been one of the keys to our success. An example of this is the new products we are launching such as the electric clamp and a B-station tapping tool that helps customers to free up the C- and D-stations. We have also just developed a shimless die for the TRUMPF product line. These are three great new products. We have a lot of products in the hopper that we are working on continuously, including smart tooling. We invest continuously in R&D projects. We are successful if our customers are successful," outlined Paul Johnson, CEO, Wilson Tool.

Examples of the company's innovations also include the new Brake Partner® that enables operators to use new or existing punch press tooling to make forms in the press brake that would otherwise need to be made on a punch press. Brake Partner offers fabricators the capability to perform punch press operations in-house, even when no punching machine is available, and/or to further expand the capabilities of their existing press brakes.

The company has also been spearheading smart tooling innovations as well as identification (data matrix) coding on tools. It has patented several smart tooling innovations and continues to develop this area of its business.

"We are trying to find ways to incorporate artificial intelligence into our tooling to benefit our customers, as well as to use it internally to help provide better services to our customers. There is strong opportunity here and things are changing quickly," added Paul Johnson.

"If you look at the last two years, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic, I am very proud of how we got through this period. We did an amazing job servicing our customers and delivering what they needed. That includes investing in our people (education, training, etc). This has made us a better and stronger company for the future," Paul Johnson told ISMR.

"However, it is the employees that really make our company. Some people have been with us for 40/45 years and their in-house knowledge/expertise is fantastic. People don't leave Wilson Tool very often. We have developed a real family environment in the organisation and dedication to continuous innovation, providing value to our customers. Innovation is still a big part of what we do. It's a competitive market and you must change with the times," he added.

Wilson Tool's growth has allowed it to develop further services for customers such as lean manufacturing seminars, webinars, application training and industry fora. These all allow it to connect with customers in meaningful ways. Collaboration with customers is a key pillar of its growth.

"We make a lot of customised special tooling for the market in different tooling styles. There isn't a day that goes by that we don't build something different to meet a customer's needs. Wilson Tool has been around for a long time and has a very wide customer base. We always find ways to grow through different opportunities, innovation, technology etc.," explained Ron Palick.

## Global market trends

Palick also pointed out several trends in today's global sheet metal and tooling markets that feed into the development work undertaken by Wilson Tool.

Wilson Tool shimless die adaptor.

"Automation is really pushing this industry. It is hard finding labour so you see more load/ unloading systems and automation on and around machine tools. The labour problem is not going away. We have embraced automation with products like the E clamp and we continue to observe developments at machine-tool manufacturers," he said.

"The skills and labour shortages also mean that you must design and build useability and simplicity into products today. When you develop a new product, you must make it easy to use and to maintain. For example, our tapping tool can be maintained by customers (and be more efficient). Our punching tools feature simple click adjustments so that there is no need for a ruler or caliper. We will see more of that," he continued.

He also emphasized the lack of young people coming into the global sheet metal industry.

Wilson Tool's Swing Ear tool (open).

"We contact technical schools to hire people and the graduating classes are a fraction of what they used to be. That is a concern. We run apprenticeships and training programmes but all of us need to make this industry more attractive to younger people," he underlined.

Paul Johnson emphasised the challenges of a global ageing manufacturing workforce, with skilled engineers retiring and new, younger hires needing internal training and focused, specialised programmes. He also offered a 'big picture' view of how market movements and trends factor in Wilson Tool's philosophy and future planning.

"Customers need to do more with less these days. It's about how you can process parts faster, quicker and more efficiently. Artificial intelligence, automation and other technologies feed into this. I think the world will change much more quickly in the next ten years than it has in the last thirty years. I lived through the CNC revolution but just looking at the huge amount of technological change just in the last 25 years has been amazing. We are gearing up for what we believe may happen in the next 25 years," he told ISMR.

## Looking ahead

Global market factors, such as increasing inflation and interest rates along with regional conflicts, present challenges to manufacturers across the world.

"The supply shortage, which ties into inflation, is also a challenge for us. We carry a lot of products and a lot of inventory to service our customers. Hopefully, this situation will settle. We have a global footprint which helps us with supply chain issues, and even labour shortage issues, but there is a tremendous amount of uncertainty right now in the market. Factors such as increasing inflation and interest rates mean that we have made changes internally to help us cope with these," Johnson highlighted.

"We are going through our global business plans now for next year. We have locations all over the world and it is quite different depending upon where you are based. The U.S. seems to be fairly stable right now while Europe is going through a downturn so stability is key for us. Our turnover this year will be higher than last year, not by huge percentage points but definitely a better year than last year," he added.

Ron Palick agrees with this assessment and is cautiously optimistic about the future.

"We have not yet seen a recession in the U.S. but we are concerned about Europe and Germany. Some of the energy costs are a huge burden for small businesses right now. We are hopeful that this will stabilise, as it has been going on for about a year now. For 2024, we are planning for success and will re-budget if we need to. We are optimistic about the future; cycles in this industry are nothing new," he said.

## Sustainable and smart

Wilson Tool has also placed an increasing focus on sustainability in its product design and processes. This includes the coolants and oils that it produces as well as energy efficiency savings in its tooling products. Its insert-style tooling consumes an estimated 40-81% less tool steel with less packaging required and lighter weights/smaller volumes. Its plastic packaging is 100% recycled and it employs 100% renewable electricity at its UK factory at Swindon.

In recent years, Wilson Tool has incorporated additive manufacturing and 3D printing into its manufacturing operations. For example, it 3D-prints the tank for its new tapping tool.

"Additive manufacturing is interesting and we find that we are using the 3D printer more on our product lines these days. However, our R&D division is focused on punching, stamping and bending innovations.

Punching multi-tools.

The XSharp™ uses an automated height detection feature to locate the top of the tool before grinding.

We also make tools for robotic press brakes as well as customised special tooling. We are aiming to bring some exciting new bending products to market next year. We will be able to say more about that early next year," outlined Ron Palick.

"As tooling moves forward, more and more bending and punching tools will bear data matrix codes so they can be scanned by customers. Information in the codes can include size, dimensions, set-up instructions etc. to make tooling easier to re-order. This is happening now with our tooling but will gain momentum in the future. There will be a tooling standard at some point; I think the industry will want that to happen," he concluded.
