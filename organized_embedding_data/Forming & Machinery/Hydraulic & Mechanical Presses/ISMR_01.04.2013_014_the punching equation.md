# THE PUNCHING EQUATION

[TARİH: 01.04.2013 ISMR]

“Speed, energy efficiency and minimal tool set-up are just a few of the features needed on today’s punch presses…”

Today’s punch presses have to keep an eye on speed, workshop flexibility and good practice as well as overall running costs. Features such as noise, heat and energy reduction, minimal tool set-up and intelligent use of sheet materials are all indicators of current customer requirements in the pursuit of increased productivity.

Here, ISMR has summarised a selection of the latest new punch press/tooling technology offerings and model introductions on the market today to help you with punching choice.

Amada’s comprehensive punch press tooling range includes features such as Airblow, APH (Amada Powder High Speed Steel), quick adjustment NEX Canisters and Alpha guides. Both thick and thin turret tooling is available.

Haco Atlantic now offers the latest version of the Q5 (punching, nibbling, louvering, rolling and bending), 22- or 30-ton turret punch press with an automatic tool changer (12 to 20 stations with indexable tools) to punch up to 6.5mm steel thickness providing up to 1200 hits per minute. “The external tool turret (punch and die holder) changes the tools automatically in the punching head avoiding excessive wear of the punch holder that occurs in classical systems. Continuous improvement 
Murata Machinery USA, Inc. has a long history of revolutionary manufacturing for process integration. The story dates back to 1934 when Theodore Wiedemann designed, manufactured and marketed the Wiedematic, the first turret punch press without a centre post. Through the years, many innovations to the original turret punch press design have evolved.

With Murata’s purchase of the Wiedemann line in 1989, a new era of sheet metal fabrication machines was introduced including the first electric punch drive, the first automatic turret changing punch press system and pick and place loading/sorting automation.

In 1994, Murata Machinery changed the evolution of the turret punch with the introduction of the first electric award-winning servo punch drive — the Motorum 2000 series — which has since become a standard in the industry. Murata also launched revolutionary changes in productivity with the introduction of the first turret punch featuring a turret changer, the NPS-01, and the Motorum 2558 Hybrid, which combines electric turret punch capabilities with laser contour cutting.

Murata Machinery USA, Inc. unveiled powerful new automated turret punch presses at FABTECH in November 2012. Murata’s new Motorum 2048ST turret punch press features a larger table and turret in a reinforced J frame design. The unit’s nextgeneration, energy-efficient ram drive is less complex and more rigid, resulting in higher punch speeds and greater productivity. The efficient design of the Motorum 2048ST is suitable for economical production of parts requiring punching, forming, tapping and deburring.

Also introduced was the Motorum 3048. The machine’s new 33-ton servo-driven ram boasts 510 hits per minute on one-inch centres with a blistering 5,039 inches per minute traverse speed. The Motorum 3048 uses large turrets for greater on-demand tool availability coupled with indexable multi-tools for flexibility. The 3048 also features the latest Fanuc control with an enhanced operator interface for ease of use.

Murata Machinery’s other displays at FABTECH 2012 featured automated load/unload systems including the new SL-1250 and the FG-1250. Muratec automated loaders can potentially double the production of a stand-alone machine for unattended operation.

At the 2012 EuroBLECH exhibition – for the first time ever – the new TRUMPF SheetMaster Compact in combination with the TruPunch 3000 was displayed. This spacesaving automation unit loads the punch and removes both scrap skeleton and micro-joint panels. In the latter, the partially punched parts are retained in the sheet by means of tabs.

“The automation components are located next to the machine and some of them are placed below the machine’s worktable. The SheetMaster Compact loads medium-sized sheets and blanks quickly and cleanly. Once machining is completed, it discharges micro-joint panels or scrap skeletons onto a removal carriage. Since, with the TruPunch 3000, the completed parts are discharged directly, either through a parts chute or joined by micro-joints, the SheetMaster Compact enables entirely unattended operation,” explained TRUMPF.

The blanks and sheets are loaded by a frame fitted with suction cups. While the TruPunch 3000 is doing its work, the SheetMaster Compact separates and positions the next sheet on the loading station behind the machine’s table. Here it checks to make sure that sheets are not doubled. As soon as machining is complete, the SheetMaster Compact positions itself above the machine table. There, it uses a rake to sweep the scrap skeleton or the micro-joint panel off the table and into a removal cart positioned in front of the machine. Then the suction frame locates the new sheet in the clamping jaws.
