# New Taurus FL

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

LVD’S NEW Taurus FL delivers the flexibility to process extra-large sheets or multiple sheets, accommodating workpieces up to 3.3 metres wide and up to 30mm thick. It is offered with a 6-, 8- or 10kW fibre laser source.

It is designed for easy access. Only the gantry features an enclosure, not the complete machine. The operator has two touch-panels for convenient access to the control at all times, and a handheld control unit. Taurus FL also provides the option of bevel cutting or 2.5D cutting up to 45°. The Taurus FL bevel head uses two direct-drive motors. The bevel head can cut on a horizontal surface as well as cut shapes in pre-formed parts or cut under angle within the limits of the Z-axis and +/- 45°.

An optional automatic nozzlechanger increases machine uptime and throughput. The unit has storage for 30 nozzles in a turret-style holder positioned close to the cutting head. The nozzle changer features an integrated camera that checks nozzle alignment, size and condition. It offers automatic calibration of the capacitive height sensing and nozzle cleaning after a pre-set number of piercings. Optional CADMAN®-L software includes automated functions to simplify programming.

## Ulti-Form bending

LVD HAS LAUNCHED the new Ulti-Form robotic bending system, featuring an automated tool-changing press brake. Ulti-Form incorporates an automated tool-changing press brake to keep bending productivity at its peak, handling both small, medium and larger batches with minimal changeover time.

Ulti-Form features a 135-ton press brake designed using the ToolCell platform, integrated with an industrial robot. The press brake houses a built-in tooling warehouse and uses a gripper mechanism in the machine’s back gauge fingers to quickly change tools. Ulti-Form handles parts from 50 x 100mm up to 1200 x 800mm, weighing up to 15kg.

Programming of both the press brake and robot is handled offline and no robot teaching is required. CADMAN®-B software automatically calculates the optimal bend and robot program. The Ulti-Form robot gripper is an auto-adapting design engineered by LVD (patent pending). It can accommodate several part geometries, automatically adjusting to the workpiece size. A series of different part geometries can be processed without the need for a gripper change, keeping production continuous and uninterrupted.

ISMR: Which issues are of key importance to your customers now and how are you supporting them in this?

MF: Our strategy has always been to be there for our customers – for sales, service etc. The lockdown created a few challenges. A lot of people were working from home with key workers supporting the economy (e.g. manufacturers making ventilators in the fight against COVID etc.). We were proud to be able to support them in their efforts.

Manufacturers still need to keep their businesses resilient and moving forward. Many of them have adapted their production during this period (e.g. making hospital beds, hand sanitisers etc.). We have also tried to adapt to the new environment. To support customers, at the beginning of the lockdown, we developed (with a local technical college) a door-opening device. This bracket fits over the door handle to enable users to open a door without touching it with their hands. We then shared the design with everyone – putting it on our website, on social media etc. – so that customers could use the design and start making it.

As well as running webinars and online events to contact and support our customers, we have also used the events to highlight and help them with issues and challenges for their business. For example, the ‘order to cash‘ cycle is important for customers – it all comes down to the most efficient way of manufacturing the part (the ‘art to part’ process). It means that they can leverage their cash, and their organisation, in the leanest way that they can. Software and Big Data are key, as is Industry 4.0 which gives them the ability to make an informed choice. Customers really are looking at how they can evolve their processes.

The labour and manufacturing skills’ shortage in the industry also means that manufacturers need to look at how their employees add value. This has sparked greater interest in the benefits of machine automation and the reallocation of human resources to areas where they can add greater value to a company. Automation also brings its own benefits for material handling and rapid throughput etc. We have responded to this need with systems such as our automated bending systems, the Dyna-Cell and Ulti-Form. We have now also developed a rapid programming system for bending automation – from ‘art to part’ in 20 minutes – which is a game-changer in terms of robotics/ automation. It is about putting the intelligence and the intuitive aspects into the controllers and into the software.
