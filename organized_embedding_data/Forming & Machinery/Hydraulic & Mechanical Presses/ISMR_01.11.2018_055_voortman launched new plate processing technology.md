# Voortman launched new plate processing technology at this October's EuroBLECH.

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Voortman has designed and developed machines and solutions for the steel fabrication and manufacturing industries for approximately 50 years. With a wide range of products for beam, plate, flat and angle processing, as well as painting and shotblasting, Voortman has installed numerous systems worldwide through its network of international subsidiaries.

The machines marketed by Voortman are segmented into two markets; the steel fabrication and the steel manufacturing markets. Its line of plate processing machines is relatively new in the steel manufacturing market and, in the last few years, its plate product portfolio has increased to seven plate processing machines.

At this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Voortman launched its new advanced plate processing machine, the V310. Besides the V310, Voortman launched the new V631 drilling and milling machine. Voortman also presented new technology for heavy-plate processing and beams at the exhibition, along with an interactive 3D experience into its machinery on the booth. This technology is used to visualise actual customer production and find the right customer solutions.

ISMR met Gideon Ebbers, Voortman's marketing coordinator, on its booth at EuroBLECH to find out more.

The company showcased its new V310 plate processing machine for cutting, drilling and marking plate products at EuroBLECH. Equipped with a drill spindle, holes of up to 40mm can be carbide drilled with a heavy SK40 spindle. A heavy steel bridge ensures a higher allowable feed rate and provides extended consumable life with minimal vibration during drilling operations. The toolchanger moves along with the bridge for instant tool changes (up to 10 tools).

"The Voortman V310 uses the fastest height control in the market, a proven technique on our other plate cutting machines. Combined with the reduced cut-to-cut cycle time of our Instant Cut Technology, users can gain a 30% processing time improvement for each product," explained Gideon Ebbers.

"When processing thick material, the V310 uses a revolutionary oxy-fuel torch. This new torch equips standard GCE nozzles and can be easily exchanged with a bayonet mount for minimum downtime. The ignition and automatic height control systems are integrated within the torch, creating one solid torch for the operator."

Voortman's new V631 drilling and milling machine was also on display at the booth. Featuring direct drive motors, it adds increased milling capabilities to its automated beam drilling machines. Capable of simultaneous drilling, marking and milling on all sides, the V631 provides high-speed processing to accelerate production performance.

Users can drill, mark and mill all sides of the material simultaneously without needing extra handling or turning of the profile. With optimised milling paths, robust bearings and short distance to profile, the milling process can be finished in one-pass.

With all functionality integrated inside the machine, it is equipped with new VACAM 4.0 software which gives graphical representations of the production process, making it fit for potential in-line system interpretation. Voortman's V631 offers a choice between a fast feeder truck and fast feeder rolls.

The visible chip conveyor, direct drive motors and 24-station tool changer increase the time that the V631 can run autonomously, said Gideon Ebbers, increasing production efficiency and decreasing labour costs.

The high level of autonomy speeds up part production, reduces machine downtime and enables 24/7 manufacturing.

## An eye on automation

"Automation is one of our USPs. Many of our customers want to run their machines faster, so automation is key for them. I am proud to say that we have developed our own Multi System Integration solution which means that all our machines can work simultaneously. Automation is our key focus," Ebbers told ISMR.

The high level of autonomy speeds up part production, reduces machine downtime and enables 24/7 manufacturing.

"Customers continuously request more automation. Labour costs, as well as finding well-trained employees, are issues for them, as are production speeds. There is a shortage of experienced operators and welders in the market, so machine quality and ease of use becomes very important. It is very hard to find good welders now. A solution to this is the Voortman Fabricator, a completely automated beam welding machine. This also leads to reduction of set-up times and increasing productivity for our customers. Our strategic pillars are automation, maximum output and customer service 'value for life' costs," added Gideon Ebbers.

## New initiatives

Voortman Steel Group registered a turnover of more than one hundred million euros. With a total of approximately 500 employees, its production and assembly facilities, as well as its sister steel construction company, are based in Rijssen (The Netherlands) where, says Ebbers, it can 'focus on quality and process'.

"Both companies, Steel Construction and Steel Machinery, benefit from each other's presence in the same group. As each other's most strategic supplier and customer, synergy is achieved at all levels on level playing fields such as innovation, testing, data-analysis etc. Voortman's theme at EuroBLECH 2018 was 'Accelerating Performance' to express our results-driven approach," explained Ebbers.

"We will also be launching a new website at the beginning of next year, focused on fabricating as well as manufacturing. It will also include product information, as well as detailed benefits for the customer, enabling them to easily see why and how Voortman offers the systems they need," he concluded.

INVEST FOR SUCCESS ISMR SAYS: “Artificial Intelligence optimises bending results in EVO's control system”

HAEUSLER launched its newly developed EVO four-roll plate bending machine at EuroBLECH.

Haeusler is one of the leading companies in metal forming. With over 80 years of experience, it is a pioneer in the development of innovative bending machines. What once started as a small locksmith's shop in 1936 is now a future-oriented family business in metal bending, metal forming and general assembly technologies. These include universal plate bending machines (including three- and four-roll bending systems), special plate-bending machines and section plate benders. Dishing presses, flanging machines, plate-straightening machines, tube-end sizing machines, pipe mills and shrinking machines are also included in its portfolio.

With around 140 employees in total, it has two production facilities - its headquarters in Switzerland and a manufacturing facility in Germany (20 minutes from each other, over the border, by car). It specialises in customised and turnkey bending solutions, with a strong focus on R&D. ISMR caught up with Mario Maier, Haeusler's deputy director of sales, at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, as he launched its newly developed EVO four-roll plate bending machine.

## On a roll

"We have designed the EVO four-roll plate bending machine to generate the lowest TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) for our customers and it is, we believe, technically superior to other machines on the market. Eighteen months of research and development, over 23,000 hours of project work and many hundreds of test hours have been invested in this project. We started with a blank sheet of paper, which allowed us to pursue completely new approaches," explained Dipl.-Ing. Mario Maier, Haeusler's deputy director of sales and one of the two project managers for the new machine.

"In Spring 2017 we started with a vision to create a revolutionary bending machine which could guarantee perfect bending results, even for operators who had no experience of rolling metal, and that could halve operating costs. Today, you can see the result – our EVO four-roller plate bending machine fulfils these goals and is the first bending machine worldwide, we believe, to integrate Artificial Intelligence into its redesigned control."

HAEUSLER chose the EuroBLECH 2018 exhibition in Hanover to launch the EVO, screening the launch to all its employees in Switzerland and Germany. THE EVO is packed with several innovative and patented features designed to increase production range and productivity. Its patented and intuitive BENDTRONIC control system, using Artificial Intelligence to optimise bending results, will be a standard on all EVO machines. New and patent-pending VSA® technology allows the operator to bend either very heavy wall thicknesses or very small diameters by adjusting the bending geometry.

"I believe that EVO is the only four-roll plate bender with this feature – for our clients, it means that they are now in a position to get a machine with up to 85% more bending movement compared to conventional bending machines. Additionally, they can use super-small interchangeable top rolls for bending tight diameters. By using two, instead of just one, pivot point for each side roll swing arm fixation, the machine can be adjusted either to create a wide bending geometry for heavy wall thicknesses or tight geometry if small diameters need to be bent," clarified Maier.

BENDtronic® controls not just the movements of the machine but also the bending result itself. The graphically supported software enables single-pass, as well as multi-pass, plate rolling and can be used for any kind of product shape (such as cylinders and pipes, ovals, tanks or other more complex geometries up to S-shaped workpieces). BENDtronic® contains various auxiliary tools to support the operator.

Eighteen months of research and development, over 23,000 hours of project work and many hundreds of test hours have been invested in this project.

The HAEUSLER Hybrid Drive System HHDS® features electrical motors in combination with planetary gear boxes, said the company, to save up to 50% of the running costs. The Hybrid Drive System also enables step-less adjustable rotation speed from 0-8 m/min for high productivity.

"With this new system, we are taking a further step towards Industry 4.0. We have worked on interfaces on the EVO to other machines, so that they can share information. Artificial Intelligence supports the machine operation. The newly upgraded software includes Artificial Intelligence to help operators understand what to do and optimise their bending results – with every piece that you roll, the machine gets smarter," explained Maier.

"We wanted to make it modular and easy to assemble, which also means that we can build it faster. HAEUSLER is well known for making large and heavy machines to process material with heavy wall thicknesses but, with EVO, we can now process thinner plate thicknesses (10-70mm)."

## An eye on growth

"This year was a great year for us, with some very high-value orders. We sold one of the biggest four-roll plate bending machines we have ever built to a government-owned company in China (in marine manufacturing) – this was delivered a few weeks ago. We also sold our biggest three-roll bending machine in Europe to a company which produces gearboxes in Holland – this will be delivered in December. The pace of work has kept us busy – some of our teams are working three shifts per day to keep up with demand," commented Mario Maier.

He is optimistic for sales in 2019 (especially for the company's large machines) and believes that its new smaller EVO offers more market potential for the company with customers who bend thinner sheet and plate.

"We grew last year by 10% (by employees). Our customers are worldwide. The largest markets for us right now are Russia, China and Europe (Germany, Netherlands, UK etc.). We also sell to Australia and Brazil. We sell between 40 and 50 machines each year, but they are high-value machines. We are always looking for distributors worldwide," he told ISMR.

## Streamlined production

All of the company's machines have been customised, up until now. However, with the advent of the EVO as a modular system, HAEUSLER is now changing to serial production of the machine at its German factory. This, it believes, will bring down prices and is a key technical target which will greatly affect its own production operations. It has already substantially invested in this area and is spending between seven to ten million euros on new equipment to adapt its production and assembly lines for this type of manufacturing. Next year, it plans to invest in a new ERP system for its production line.

"Steel grades are getting higher, so machines are getting bigger. Wall thicknesses may also be coming down. Rolling is like art - it is hard to get good results. The real gamechanger is to find software which makes it easy for unskilled operators to get perfect bending results every time and not generate scrap (which can be very expensive on large machines). We believe that our software offers this capability. Machines are becoming ever more capable, flexible and versatile," concluded Maier.
