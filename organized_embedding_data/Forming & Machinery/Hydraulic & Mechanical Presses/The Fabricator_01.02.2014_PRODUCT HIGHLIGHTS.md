Waterjet robot allows 6-axis cutting

WARDJet, 180 South Ave., Tallmadge, OH 44278, 330-677-9100,

www.wardjet.com

WARDJet has introduced the multipurpose waterjet robot. It integrates waterjet cutting with end-of-arm tooling to achieve 6-axis cutting. A seventh axis is possible with the addition of a headstock/tailstock fixture that rotates the part while the robot cuts around it.

The robot is a collaboration between the manufacturer and Motoman. The HP20D-A80 robot arm is designed for use in cutting and dispensing applications. Fitted with a waterjet cutting head, the robot can achieve accuracies within 0.1 mm, depending on the application.

The slim base, waist, and arm of the robot allow the cutting tables and fixtures to be placed close to the machine to help minimize floor space requirements. The compact design allows the robot to be operated alone, or it can work with up to eight other robots without concern for collision.

The robot can cut either with pure water or a mixture of water and abrasive.

Product lets welders hand-guide robotic welding machine

Robotiq, 966, chemin Olivier, Suite 325, St-Nicolas, QC, G7A 2N1, Canada, 888-762-6847,

www.robotiq.com

Robotiq, in partnership with Yaskawa Motoman, has released Kinetiq Teaching™, a new product that allows operators to task robotic welding machines without requiring in-depth programming knowledge.

The product enables operators to move the robotic welding machine with their hands and program welding paths intuitively by using a dedicated teach pendant interface. No in-depth programming knowledge is required. According to the company, the product opens the door to robotic welding at small and medium-sized manufacturers with high-mix and low-volume welding.

Once the welding point is reached, the welder determines the welding parameters through a touchscreen interface. Once all the points are recorded, the welder can play back the programmed trajectory and modify it on-the-fly.

Laser blanking line cuts aluminum, steel blanks from continuously fed coil stock

Schuler Inc., 7145 Commerce Blvd., Canton, MI 48187, 734-207-7200,

www.schulergroup.com

Schuler has developed a laser blanking line that can cut blanks directly from a moving sheet metal coil with the aid of laser beams. The process, called DynamicFlow Technology, involves the use of three coordinated cutting heads.

In a single, continuous process, the sheet metal is decoiled, straightened, cut, and stacked as blanks. Any shape can be cut without the need for dedicated dies. Changes to the respective contours or even completely new blanks can be implemented quickly.

In the coil feeding device, the sheet metal first is wound off the coil by the decoiler and fed into the straightener at a constant speed. Coil-tension and coil-center regulation devices help ensure smooth, precise feeding. To reduce coil changeover times, double decoilers or fully automatic cassette-changing devices can be used for the straightener.

Three 4-kW fiber lasers cut the blanks in parallel from the continuously transported sheet metal coil. Cutting speeds of more than 328 FPM can be achieved depending on the material.

Fiber laser supplements plasma cutting technique for thin sheets

Kjellberg Finsterwalde Plasma und Maschinen GmbH, Oscar-Kjellberg-Str. 20, D-03238 Finsterwalde, Germany, 49-3531-500-0,

www.kjellberg.de

Kjellberg Finsterwalde offers the XFocus 500 fiber laser system for cutting and marking of thin alloyed and unalloyed steels, as well as aluminum. It can be used in connection with all common guiding systems, either individually or alternating with a plasma cutting unit.

The compact system, with a laser performance of 500 W, is suited for processing mild steel from 0.5 to 5 mm thick, stainless steel from 0.3 to 5 mm, and aluminum from 1 to 3 mm. Nine different cutting speeds are available for each type of material and thickness. The focus position of the laser head and the gas pressure are adjusted automatically via the database. The system comprises an integrated cooling unit, an axis with height control, and a laser head with motor-controlled focus position adjustment, as well as an automatic gas supply.

Hybrid press brake delivers high power with low energy

Ermak USA Inc., 1260 Landmeier Road, Elk Grove Village, IL 60007, 847-640-7765,

www.ermakusa.com

Ermak USA Inc. has introduced the new-generation Evolution II hybrid press brake series. The machine uses servomotors and a high-efficiency pump to operate at speeds up to 0.78 IPS. Bending accuracy is ±0.0003 in.

According to the company, the press brake uses an average 65 percent less energy than standard press brakes. No pressure is lost through proportional valves, so bending efficiency remains high.

The pumping system does not run when the machine is idle. This prevents the oil from heating up, so the oil is more durable. Oil changes are required once every five years. The system’s Delem DA66 T controller presents a 3-D simulation of applications before bending. It allows a maximum of 25 steps in a program and offers 99-step repeatability.

Coil feed allows unattended laser cutting

The Dimeco Group, 2, rue du Chêne, ZI, F25480 Pirey, France, 33-3-81-48-38-00,

www.dimeco.com

Dimeco Alipresse has developed the LINACUT® line of machines that combine laser cutting with coil-fed processes. Focusing on processing strips up to 0.16 in. thick, the line comprises a coil handling section, a LINACUT continuous laser cutting module, and an optional automatic robotic stacking cell.

The line complements 2-D laser cutting tables and CNC turret punches. Using coils up to 15 tons, the system extends cutting capacities so it can work more than eight hours without operator intervention.
