Machining center processes wind turbine workpieces

Starrag has developed the Heckert HEC 1600 machining center, suitable for manufacturing large, heavy workpieces, such as those used in wind turbine production. It can process workpieces with edge lengths of 11 ft. and up to 13 tons in weight. Users can choose between a horizontal main spindle with integrated extendible NC quill, or a swiveling horizontal/vertical head for five-sided machining.

The NC quill has stepless adjustment up to 30 in. for long travels and the machining of deep-lying form elements. It guides the tool into the gearbox houses to work safely without requiring special tools with excessive lengths. The NC quill also provides a significant surge in machining large gearbox housings, torque support, and planetary gear carriers in large components, the company reports. The quill and its bearing are cooled with a separate cooling unit to provide automatic temperature compensation for thermal influences and thermal stability.

Handling robot designed for hazardous environments

The high-speed, 6-axis MHP45L handling robot from Yaskawa Motoman is Factory Mutual (FM) approved for Class 1, Division 1 use in hazardous environments. It offers a wide motion range for transferring large workpieces while avoiding interference with paint robots and other peripheral equipment. The robot can streamline automated paint line layouts by eliminating the need for dedicated part transfer.

The handling robot offers a 45-kg payload capacity, as well as high moment and inertia ratings, for accommodating large, heavy workpieces such as automotive bodies and components. It features a 2,850-mm horizontal reach, 5,095-mm vertical reach, and ±0.07-mm repeatability for processing multiple parts in various sizes.

SMAW machine helps set optimal parameters

Kemppi has introduced the Master 315 SMAW machine for workshop and on-site use. The lightweight, compact machine is made of injection-molded plastic with impact bridge protection structures for durability.

With the HR45 Bluetooth hand remote unit, users can control the welding power and select memory channels from a distance of up to 100 m. Buttons and a multifunction control knob help users find optimal welding parameters for every job. They can select settings from the panel manually or allow Weld Assist to select them automatically. Pulsed SMAW can be selected for lower heat input and better control over the welding arc.

Portable bevelers handle plates up to 50 mm thick

CEVISA offers portable beveling machines that work on the rotary shear principle, offering bevel speeds of 1.7 to 2.6 MPM. The machines can bevel plates 6 to 50 mm thick for uniform V, K, and X weld seam preparation.

The machines chamfer carbon steels, alloy steels, stainless steels, and aluminum. The mechanical chamfering does not create a HAZ and causes no thermal distortions.

The beveler can be carried to work and is self-propelled to follow the edge of the panel while chamfering without operator assistance. The suspension trolley enables the machine to be operated on uneven ground. The top and bottom of the panels can be chamfered with two machines at the same time.

Software helps increase production safety in tube processing

Transfluid has further developed its t project software with two versions, basic and professional, for different areas of tube processing.

It connects CAD, MES, and ERP systems to bending machines and bending robots so that bending techniques can be tested in advance to help eliminate collisions. Various add-on modules contain the data for complete processes to add control over the process and to help manage the forming techniques.

Tube geometries that are produced or changed with the software now can be saved in an IGES format. This makes it possible to transfer these tube geometries back into a CAD system after a necessary change or prototype development, such as an adjustment to prevent a collision.

For testing large quantities of bending geometries, the user can make a list to test their feasibility sequentially. In a fully automatic process, the tube data is imported from the CAD systems, necessary adjustments are calculated, and a collision test is performed.

Software assists in sharing work instructions with suppliers

VKS has introduced the Digital Ecosystem package as an addition to its VKS Lite, Pro, or Enterprise software. This package helps users share interactive work instructions with suppliers and business partners.

The software’s approval work flow feature helps ensure suppliers’ work instructions, control plans, and processes meet the user’s needs. The system allows instant feedback within the supply chain to address issues and share knowledge in real time.

The Smart Forms feature helps create digital checklists to ensure suppliers are capturing data at specific steps during the process. Live job tracking helps decrease paperwork and offers real-time visibility of the shop floor.
