# Seven Schuler presses in five years

[TARİH: 01.04.2020 ISMR]

IN APRIL 2014, Schuler delivered the first 400-ton MSD 400 servo press to Alzner Automotive in the town of Grafenau, near Stuttgart in Germany. An order for an MSD 250 came under one year later, followed by another MSD 400, an MSD 630 and a 500-ton hydraulic HPX press ordered in the meantime. A second 630-ton machine was ordered in July 2019, and an order for a second HPX 500 in late 2019 brought the total to seven Schuler presses in a span of just five and half years.

Alzner Automotive is equipping the presses with the IIoT connector from Schuler. This allows the operating status of the lines to be called up from anywhere and at any time. The password-protected portal “mySchuler" also provides information about the running die, the current stroke rate, the press force and the lubrication and cooling circuits, among other things. In this way, possible deviations can be detected early and eliminated quickly. The automotive supplier is also considering the purchase of Schuler's camera-supported monitoring system for dies ("Visual Die Protection").

“At the time, both of us were looking for someone, and we managed to find each other," said Alzner Automotive's managing director, Hans-Martin Tekeser, as he recalled the early days of his relationship with Schuler. “Since then, we haven't run into any noticeable problems with the presses. They also provide a great cost-benefit ratio for us. We can't afford a situation where a machine sits idle for three or four weeks at a time.

"We're still producing right up to our limits, and we don't have much extra capacity," added Tekeser, highlighting the fact that Alzner Automotive was able to double its production volume using a special die for the 630-ton transfer press.

## The move to electric

The thriving business at Alzner is being fuelled by the shift towards electromobility. The company is generating new orders in this area.

“We're getting a lot of business in electromobility, both from our existing customers and from new ones. Customers are focusing more and more on price. We also talk to auto manufacturers about how parts can be designed more efficiently, for example by using sheet metal instead of tube as starting material. This is where our physical proximity and practical approach to getting the job done are extremely beneficial," added Tekeser.

To handle the volume and level of precision demanded, Tekeser hired additional employees and invested in presses and automation, noting: “You can't meet the demand with manpower alone." The company is currently in the process of implementing an automated high-bay storage system for dies.

Over the past five years, Alzner Automotive has doubled its physical shop capacity to a total of two hectares. When the most recent plans for expansion encountered delays, Schuler placed Alzner's first 630-ton press into storage for six months until local authorities in Grafenau finally gave the green light. Another 7,500 square metres of space was added in autumn 2019. See www.alzner-automotive.de/en and www.schulergroup.com ■
