With hydroforming, most punches are made from cast iron as opposed to the hardened tool steels used for match die drawing punches. Hydroforming tools are also easily mounted and aligned, making set-ups fast and efficient.

Development Costs: Prototyping is often a necessary step in the manufacturing process. Changes in material type or wall thickness specifications can typically be accommodated with hydroforming without creating a need for new tooling.

Reduced Press Time: Complex parts requiring multiple press cycles in matched die operations can be drawn in a single hydroforming cycle. Hydroforming presses frequently achieve reductions of 60-70%.

Finishing Costs: Aerospace, medical and commercial cookware applications often demand parts with outstanding surface finishes. Unlike matched die metal forming, which can leave scratches and stretch lines, the flexible diaphragm utilised in hydroforming eliminates surface blemishes, reducing the need for costly finishing processes such as buffing.

## STEP INTO THE LIGHT

Designplan's first lighting products, designed using Solid Edge CAD software, went into production nine months after implementation.

Portfolio includes an extensive range of energy-efficient solutions for clients requiring environmentally friendly or low maintenance fittings.

Designplan currently has more than 140 employees, and sales in more than twenty countries worldwide. Project applications range from rail and metro stations, social housing complexes, custodial environments, secure healthcare facilities and urban areas where robust lighting is demanded. For half a century, Designplan Lighting has been making weather and vandal-resistant luminaires that have tough bodies, tightly sealed diffusers and tamperproof screws and can be found in airports, train stations, housing estates and secure health care units.

Electrical equipment sometimes needs to withstand extreme conditions and deliberate attack, as well as normal wear and tear. One indicator is the level of protection against mechanical impact. In Europe, this is measured in terms of the impact protection (IK) ratings. For lighting products, the standard requirement is a rating of ten. Designplan's most robust products are rated much higher, at 16 IK, which protects against an impact of 150 Joules, or 10 kilos being dropped from a height of 1.5 metres.

A drive to improve efficiency

Designplan makes both standard and customised lighting products, and it was the particular challenge of making customised products that led Designplan to review its use of technology in the development process.

Tony Croke, design team manager, explained: "Over the years, the company had acquired multiple Computer Aided Design (CAD) platforms so when we had to quote for a new product, we faced a whole series of questions. Which existing model was closest to what was required, which package had it been designed in and who was most familiar with that software? Our vision was to consolidate on one package and improve our efficiency."

A demonstration from Siemens PLM Software solution partner, Prion Cutting Edge (PCE), convinced the Designplan team that Solid Edge software with synchronous technology was the way forward.

## An organic approach

After six months, Designplan had created master models for three product sets and the team was reviewing customer requests and anticipating demand for different sizes.

Amy Deeprose, design engineer at Designplan, noted the benefits of the new approach: "In particular, synchronous technology allows us to work organically. We can explore options because there is no sketch to build, as in an ordered modelling environment. When we have a Solid Edge assembly, we can do model explosions and gain visual help to cross-reference quickly against the master BOM (Bill of Materials)."

Synchronous technology allows us to work organically.

All Solid Edge files are kept together in one folder as Designplan builds a library of master and variant components within product families. The data management features of Solid Edge support basic workflows for file release, storage and retrieval. The team has also been creating Building Information Modelling (BIM) files in line with government legislation.

"Solid Edge is fully compliant with BIM regulations and produces widely recognized standard BIM files," explained Deeprose. "There is no extra step required. If a BIM file is requested at 8am, we can usually produce it by midday."

The first products designed using Solid Edge went into production nine months after implementation.

"Now that we have started to work from master models, any changes are taking minutes rather than hours," said Deeprose. "There is also the option to use the simulation capability within Solid Edge to assess structural integrity. We can cut further time in production if we identify any weak points through simulation."

And Croke added: "We have improved our products and reduced manufacturing errors. Production stoppages for bespoke products used to average between ten and 18 per week. Since we began to use Solid Edge, they have dropped to just four."
