# At EuroBLECH in Hanover this October, SafanDarley's CEO, Gert Dekker, outlined a bending E-volution that embraces digitalisation, robotisation and the Smart Factory.

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

Intelligent 'lights-out' factory solutions were the focus of SafanDarley's stand at this October's EuroBLECH sheet-metal exhibition in Hanover, Germany. The bending and shearing specialist, inventor of the servo-electronic E-brake bending system, has heralded an 'E-volution' in sheet-metal processing by moving increasingly towards the automatically and digitally controlled factory.

Its automated, modular and software solutions on display at EuroBLECH have been developed for the future production environment and are designed to contribute to lower energy consumption, higher productivity and cost savings.

SafanDarley develops and builds press brakes and shears for thin and thick sheet metal, from R&D and engineering up to manufacture; assembly; sales/marketing; training and service. It has two production plants, a head office in Lochem (the Netherlands) and a branch in Eijsden, and employs approximately 200 members of staff. It has existed for over 90 years.

SafanDarley's CEO, Gert Dekker, outlined the company's bending E-volution that embraces digitalisation, robotisation and the Smart Factory to ISMR at EuroBLECH in Hanover.

## Digitalisation in focus

"The huge market trend that we see right now is towards robotisation and digitalisation, and I feel that we are just at the beginning of this cycle. We are also working on Big Data projects with some university colleagues,"

Dekker told ISMR. "We are therefore highlighting a range of automation solutions at EuroBLECH this year."

SafanDarley demonstrated its E-Brake Mini Cell and R-Brake sheet-metal bending systems at EuroBLECH in a separate area of its booth targeted at the production environment of the future. Both systems were supplied with material using Kumatech's new Pallet Jack AGV (automated ground vehicle which also removed pallets loaded with completed products). Booth visitors could see that the bending process on the R-Brake and the Mini Cell was 100% automated. During the exhibition, the R-Brake 130T produced parts for Meijer Handling Solutions BV, a manufacturer of KOOI®Reachforks for forklift trucks.

“With the R-Brake, the press brake and the robot are integrated into one modular system. The automatic tool and gripper change are linked to the machine and, together with the product, are programmed completely offline using our RoboBend offline simulation software. A unique feature of the R-Brake is that the robot can be traversed horizontally along a gantry of six metres above the press brake, leaving the floor in front of the machine completely free for supply and unloading stations, which saves a lot of space," explained Dekker.

SafanDarley also showcased its modular E-Brake Mini Cell for the automatic production of small complex products. The version at EuroBLECH featured a sophisticated Mini Cell, equipped with a discharge belt for the scratchfree disposal of products outside the cell. The Mini Cell also boasts a pallet management system for the automatic supply and removal of pallets.

## Tool-changing solutions

In addition to its robotic press brake range, SafanDarley also showcased the E-Brake C 200T with Tool Mate. The E-Brake C 200T has a C-frame and is equipped with a Tool Mate, an external automatic and intelligent tool-changer. This combination is designed to offer significant time savings when producing small batches.

"We are particularly proud of the Tool Mate; this tool-changer is a big step for us. It is the result of a successful collaboration with our partner in Italy. We can also do the same with an H-brake hydraulic press brake and create combinations i.e. a Tool Mate in the centre with two press brakes flanking on either side. It is the first E-brake in the world with a C-frame, our 'light version'. We are developing a next-generation Mini Brake with a C-frame that will be ready in the next two years, along with other versions," explained Gert Dekker.

The Tool Mate is very intuitive and, Dekker told ISMR, does not require extensive training. The SafanDarley E-Control manages, with software from the Toolmate, operation of the press brake as well as tool change. Tools from the previous tool set-up can be re-used. This means considerably shorter changeover times. The latest model in the hybrid range, the H-Brake Hybrid+ 230T Ultra, was also on display at EuroBLECH. The Hybrid+ series is an extension of the existing Hybrid series and offers other advantages, with a dramatic reduction in energy consumption.

SafanDarley also showcased machines from its standard product range, such as the H-Brake Hybrid 110-1600 and the E-Brake 130T Ultra equipped with an intelligent bending aid, the I-Mate.

"Our bending aids control the metal sheet in the press brake. Our intelligent bending aid, the I-Mate, will be available from Q1 2023. Using the I-Mate increases overall production efficiency and reduces the requirement for additional operators when processing larger sheet metal parts," explained Gert Dekker. "At the end of this year, we will also be ready to release our new ASC Control software which connects the press brake and robot. This software will be very important for us in terms of sales around the world, particularly in industrialised countries (such as the Netherlands, North America, Australia). It will enable more robotisation options i.e. standalone gantries for every press brake.

"We are very happy with our visitor attendance at EuroBLECH; it has been a great show for us. It has been terrific to look at new technologies and network with our suppliers, customers and competitors here at the exhibition," Dekker added.

Adapting to the exigencies of the COVID-19 pandemic, SafanDarley has adopted a hybrid working model (combining work-from-home with office working) which suits its workforce and allows it added flexibility. It has also continued to offer online demonstrations of its technology and online training/meetings.

"We are also inviting young people to special 'job market' days at our factory where they can see our technologies and meet our personnel. We also support our younger workers with their studies and professional development," he added.

## An eye on software

SafanDarley's subsidiary in Sweden, FCC, also displayed AutoPOL 3D offline bending simulation software on the booth which is used in all the company's machines as an important part of the automation process. The 3D sheet-metal parts to be processed can be unfolded, after which the bending sequence can be determined and simulated fully automatically. This external (offline) programming of bending programs minimises downtime on the press brake. It is also possible to program the E-Bend L angle measuring system offline with AutoPOL.

“We do not intend to develop a complete sheet-metal portfolio i.e. laser cutting, stamping etc. Our aim is to be the best bending supplier in a digitalised world. We have a long and rich history and heritage in bending and shear technology. A key part of this is our open software. Our software is open and independent, so we can connect with any machine in the world, regardless of brand, which our customers like and which we see as the future. Not all our competitors do this," continued Gert Dekker.

“Our other software partners are working on projects such as managing a laser, punchpress etc. and the connection to AutoPOL. This means that we can integrate bending into other processes done by our customers, which is another important element of the Smart Factory," explained Gert Dekker, who envisions integrated manufacturing solutions playing an increasingly important role, mainly around the integration of machine control, automation and communication.

Gert Dekker has a strong vision for SafanDarley, which is centred on expanding its expertise as a bending and shearing specialist as well as increasing its focus on robotisation and digitalisation.

## A strong vision

"In terms of regions, we focus on Europe, North America and Australia but also other countries that are highly industrialised and use high-tech equipment. We have a factory in Lochem where we build standard machines on assembly lines and special lines for E-brakes. Our subsidiary in Eisen builds customised H-brakes and hybrid technology, so we have a mix of production," he told ISMR.

"Our target areas for growth are countries in Europe that offer capital equipment subsidies like Germany; Italy; Poland and the UK. We are also focusing on the United States; Canada; Mexico and India. Our business is split between 30% North America, 30% Europe and then the 'Rest of the World'. Over the last few years, we have sold machines in over 50 countries," he added.

In a difficult market, where capital investments have been postponed and concerns abound over energy costs, the Russia/Ukraine war and spiralling inflation in Europe, he emphasised that SafanDarley is a global business.

"We do business all over the world – around nine per cent of our turnover is in our home market of the Netherlands. North America and certain regions in Europe are strong markets right now that have been relatively unaffected by the issues plaguing Eastern European markets. I believe that the European market will be uncertain over the next few years (apart from those countries offering subsidies for capital equipment/Industry 4.0 investments). However, it is difficult to forecast as some European customers are also very busy," he explained.

He confirmed that supply-chain issues had affected the company, like many manufacturers, since May 2021 (for deliveries of microchips; motors; drives; filters etc.).

“However, the situation has eased and we can now try to shorten our delivery times," he continued. “Remote servicing and maintenance of machines has also become more important since the pandemic. Some countries are further along than others in this respect."

He confirmed that SafanDarley is still developing future technologies such as Augmented Reality (AR).

"We have experimented with AR glasses in the past (the SafanDarley Eye, using the Hololens) and we are still developing this technology but with artificial intelligence (AI) and cameras. We are also working on technologies such as Virtual Reality, simulation and Digital Twin. Virtual Reality is used in our robotisation software, specifically for the robot arms. We have our eyes firmly fixed on the future," he smiled.
