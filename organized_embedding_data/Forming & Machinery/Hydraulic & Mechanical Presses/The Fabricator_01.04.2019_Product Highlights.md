Rotary tools finish ID, OD of tube ends in one operation

Severance Tool has developed a line of tube end finishing rotary tools that can deburr, form, or chamfer. The tools are designed to finish both the ID and OD of tubing ends in a single operation.

Available features include adjustable, two-piece construction to accommodate work on same-size tubing with varying wall thicknesses; Chatter-less™ tooth geometry to alternate tooth-cutting angles and eliminate resonant vibration from the tool and workpiece; and Grayhone™ finish that provides cutting edges ready to work with no break-in period required.

Tube deburring tools are available in high-speed steel or carbide. Designed for light duty, they break sharp edges off the ID and OD simultaneously. The tools operate from 50 to 200 RPM.

Adjustable tube end chamfer mills are designed for heavy-duty cutting to machine 45-degree-OD and 30-degree-ID chamfers. They are available for pipe and tubes from 3/16 to 2½ in. OD.

Tube end forming cutters machine both an ID and OD radius, establishing a smooth, rounded end that is suitable for applications in which tube may be exposed. They are available in high-speed steel and carbide for tubes from ⅛ to 2½ in. OD.

Flap wheels grind stainless steel

Saint-Gobain Abrasives has introduced a line of Norton Merit Pink R928 flap wheels and specialties for grinding stainless steel. This abrasive material provides faster cutting and longer life compared to traditional aluminum-oxide products, according to the company. The products have a poly-cotton backing for increased life, and they offer a grinding aid for cool cutting on hard-to-grind materials.

To support these abrasives, the company offers the Norton hand-held pneumatic die grinder. Its double-bearing spindle helps reduce vibrations. Adjustment of the speed regulator gives the operator control of the surface finish. The small, lightweight grinder features a nonslip grip. It offers a free speed of 25,000 RPM for deburring, light to medium surface cleaning and stock removal, welding preparation, and finishing.

Welding tractor can be used horizontally, vertically

Abicor Binzel has introduced the Abi-Car welding tractor. It is available in three models, including one with an oscillator feature.

Its strong magnet base lets the tractor be used for horizontal and vertical welding positions. It comes with a variety of cross support and clamping options for user customization.

Standard weld cell serves as entry point into automated welding

Acieta has introduced FastARC standard weld cells. They are pre-engineered with integrated utilities and a one-piece platform for fast delivery and startup, says the company.

The ready-to-use package is designed to meet the industry’s most common robotic welding needs. Four standard cells are available with attachments and upgrade options. They come standard with Lincoln Electric or Miller weld equipment.

The Single Zone Cell offers a large work zone and automated steel popup door for flash protection and operator safety. The Dual Zone Cell allows for load/unload while the robot is welding, as well as the ability to produce different parts simultaneously.

3D CAD software simplifies imported and native models

IronCAD has released its 20th anniversary edition, IronCAD Design Collaboration Suite 2019, for large assembly designs and work flows common among machinery manufacturers.

The new release extends the software’s Shrinkwrap capability to simplify imported and native models and gives users the ability to dynamically load the simplified or full versions based on their preference. Users also employ this capability to hide selective parts, nonvisible parts, and hole and pocket regions while forcing critical components to remain.

Web-connected technology helps simplify dust, fume collection management

Donaldson Co. Inc. has introduced web-connected technology that monitors industrial dust, fume, and mist collectors and notifies owners to take early, cost-saving maintenance actions.

The smart technology uses sensors and a controller to gather real-time data from a collector, apply analytics in the cloud, and relay insights back to the operator through a web-based dashboard and email or text alerts.

Proper maintenance such as timely filter changes and earlier hopper cleaning can help prevent larger problems, assist in optimizing performance, and aid in reducing total dust and fume management costs over time, the company reports.

The system can be installed on existing or new industrial dust and fume equipment.
