# At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Germany, Asservimenti Presse placed a firm focus on coil-processing lines that help to offset high material and energy costs.

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

Italian manufacturer, Asservimenti Presse Srl, produces equipment for press automation (decoilers; straighteners; cut-to-length lines; transfer and handling systems; robotics etc.) for sheet-metal working. With more than thirty years of experience, it currently exports its technology to over 20 countries.

It has completed over 2500 installations globally and specialises in press automation, aiming to optimise the production cycles of its customers. The manufacturer produces feeding lines with decoilers (up to 25-ton capacity with external diameter of 2000mm) which can process steel strip of up to 2000mm wide and 14mm in thickness.

At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Germany, it placed a firm focus on coil-processing lines that help to offset high material and energy costs. These are designed to counter the challenges that manufacturers face from the impact of the COVID-19 pandemic, supply-chain disruption and rising inflation/energy prices.

## New lines and projects

"Here in Hanover, we are highlighting our ability to produce equipment that saves on material and energy costs. Energy price rises are a real concern for manufacturers. For some years now, we have focused our R&D on these areas by, for example, changing from hydraulic to electric drives in our lines," explained Ottavio Albini, the company's sales and marketing manager.

“For example, our straightener-feeder unit with electronic pilot release is designed to reach 110 strokes/min. with a step of 300mm. It offers energy savings of 30-40% compared to traditional machine types (hydraulic, mechanical etc.). It can process material from a minimum width of 200mm to a maximum width of 2000mm," he added.

The company's latest new coil-cutting lines can process aluminium and high-tensile steel. It has installed many of these lines across Europe in countries such as Poland, Germany and Italy. Its latest developments also incorporate automatic coil-change on its lines and the new SMART HMI control panel.

"Next year, we will be starting a major project for a new coil-cutting line processing aluminium and steel. Over the last two years of the COVID-19 pandemic, we have been producing more standardised lines to meet customer requirements. However, for this project, we are focusing on our specialty area: more customised lines in collaborative projects with our customers," confirmed Albini.

Asservimenti Presse's stand at EuroBLECH in Hanover, Germany.

Asservimenti Presse works with OEMs, press and equipment manufacturers to equip complete coil lines. These lines may incorporate a range of equipment such as decoilers; straighteners; feeders; presses etc. Albini has seen the increasing adoption of servo technology in many of these lines, as customers opt for more control and better productivity on their lines.

"We completed a project recently to make a new straightener to process 5mm-thick aluminium for electric vehicle production. This was for a Tier 1 customer's lines for automotive parts in Europe and USA. His three coil-stamping lines (using mechanical presses) produce aluminium battery cases (3-5mm thick)," Albini also told ISMR.

## Market movements

The company's key market sector is the automotive industry, where Albini has noted the marked trend towards electric vehicles. The move to electric and hybrid systems in the automotive market sector, as well as the prevalence of individual electric motors in cars, has seen the company's business grow. More of its lines are producing components for electric motors in cars for its customers.

"I think coil-processing trends, in general, are pointing towards smaller, relatively uncomplicated lines that guarantee rapid changeovers. A lot of part production has now come back to Europe, but this calls for a new type of production and line philosophy. These need to be planned and coordinated with customers and close collaboration is required," he explained.

“We find ourselves working more now on coil lines to process lightweight materials (such as high-strength steels and aluminium) with quick changeover times. Our customers are asking us for this. They also ask us for more coil lines to process copper (for electric car production)," Albini told ISMR.

Short changeover times help to ensure greater efficiency and productivity. Albini also sees more requirements for automation in lines, as well as a drive towards Industry 4.0. This has been encouraged in some European countries (such as Italy) where government tax incentives encourage the purchase of capital equipment with Industry-4.0 capability.

"All our automotive customers are currently based in Europe. However, next year, our customer profile will change slightly and 80% will be in Europe, with the remaining 20% in central America," confirmed Albini.

"Business has been very good this year and our turnover will be better than last year. However, we (like many other manufacturers) have been affected by supply-chain part shortages. I am optimistic in my outlook for next year, but there are lots of geopolitical factors that could affect the market (China, Europe, the war in Ukraine). It is very hard to predict what will happen," he concluded.
