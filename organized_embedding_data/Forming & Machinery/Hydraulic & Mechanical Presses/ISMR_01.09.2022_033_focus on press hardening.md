# FOCUS ON PRESS HARDENING

[TARİH: 01.09.2022 ISMR]

## Two innovations on the same line

The greatest technological challenge was integrating two new innovations, AP&T's servohydraulic press and TemperBox®, into the same line for a process that had never been used commercially before. Organisationally, it was a challenge to put together a dedicated project team with the right expertise and right attitude toward the project. During recruitment, experience of the automotive industry was vital, as were excellent cooperation and listening skills.

"It was crucial that everyone, from the project team to R&D, really understood the expectations that our customer and their customers had for the line. The goal was to manufacture, on an industrial scale, products that had tailored, repeatable attributes with short cycle times, high availability and optimised energy consumption. That was our aim," outlined Theander.

To improve efficiency, GEDIA also put together a project team with corresponding expertise under the lead of Björn Müller, who participated in the installation of the lines at Nowa Sol and was responsible for work on the try-out line in Attendorn.

"I already had positive experiences working with AP&T, and these were confirmed when we embarked on a new project together. During this journey, we developed a close relationship based on trust, which meant we could solve tasks very smoothly. Nothing was left to chance," said Björn Müller.

Jörgen Theander also highlighted the importance of close cooperation.

"Throughout the project, we worked closely together and spoke on a regular basis, often in person at GEDIA in Germany. Doing so made it easier to catch problems and tackle challenges efficiently. We worked together like one big, close team, all striving for the same target," he told ISMR.

The project's starting point was documentation from the existing production lines and the try-out line installed in Attendorn. Issues, such as which proven design solutions could be used in the new line, what needed to be improved and what would be replaced by the new technology, were tackled. Using this analysis, a requirement specification was put together and initiatives to build the new line were started in earnest. In Autumn 2019, the line was installed and commissioned at GEDIA's Attendorn production facility.

"Since it was the first commercial presshardening line of its kind, it was important to maintain a presence for some time to ensure that it was functioning properly, tweak the equipment and be able to rectify any problems that might arise," Theander cautioned.

Before the first line installation was complete, AP&T received an order for an identical line for GEDIA's Spanish production facility outside Barcelona, Spain.

“We were able to draw on our experiences from the first line at the very beginning for working on the second line. So, just 17 months later, we were able to deliver a solution optimised for the Spanish facility together with GEDIA's German organisation," added Theander.

In January 2020, GEDIA ordered a third production line of this type. This time, it was for its Dalton, Georgia, factory in the U.S. The line was to be installed in Autumn 2021.

## The partnership principle

"Together with GEDIA, we developed what is probably the world's most advanced production line for manufacturing presshardened components for the automotive industry. By collaborating, we not only solved technological challenges and gained valuable experience, but we also managed to meet high expectations," explained Theander.

"Through professional, trusted and forwardlooking collaboration, we jointly developed and industrialised a new technology, with a business-strategic focus. But it is all about people. They must be dedicated to the task, work well together and understand and trust one another. Not only is this how our relationship with AP&T is characterised, but it is also key to our successful efforts with TemperBox®,"said Vogt.

So how does the result compare to earlier AP&T press-hardening lines?

“First and foremost, the new lines are considerably more energy-efficient mainly because the servo-hydraulic press consumes up to 70% less energy than conventional hydraulic presses. The latest generation of multi-layer furnaces, consuming 40% less energy than its predecessors, also makes an important contribution," said AP&T. "In addition, the new lines make it possible to shorten manufacturing cycle times of certain products by up to 20%, freeing up time for more production."

In the design process, increased availability and a reduced need for maintenance were key aims. The multi-layer furnaces posed several challenges, which the teams resolved. Further collaboration efforts will continue to try to significantly increase annual production hours.

"We have also developed a method of verifying the CQl-9 process repeatability of the furnaces so that it is significantly faster than before. This makes room for more valuable production time," added Theander.

For Stephan Gante, GEDIA's group director of machine and building management, the original requirement specification has been practically applied three times over. What does he think of the results?

"We now have a user-friendly, spaceefficient solution with a high degree of process reliability and excellent energy efficiency. Aside from TemperBox®itself, AP&T's servo-hydraulic press, with its considerably lower cooling requirements and energy consumption per stroke compared to conventional hydraulic presses, plays a central role in the result. We are very satisfied," he told ISMR.

Björn Müller is also pleased. He confirmed that visitors are impressed when they catch their first glimpse of the new, highly automated lines. He also emphasised that the developmental journey with AP&T is continuing.

“Every phase of this project has given us new experience and insight, which we took with us to the next phase. It is a continuous improvement project that is still ongoing," he said.

But perhaps the most important result is the experience gained from this type of collaboration. Or, in the words of Jörgen Theander: "We usually talk about how we act as One Responsible Partner® in many of our customer collaborations. But in this joint effort, there have been two responsible partners who jointly created a world-class solution through a clear common goal; an open, curious approach and a deep sense of dedication."■
