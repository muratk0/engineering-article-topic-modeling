# AUTOMATING THE PROCESS

[TARİH: 01.03.2016 ISMR]

Lara Cecchi, a Servopresse director, gives ISMR her views on developing marketplaces, new customer requirements and ongoing technology trends in coil processing markets

Italian coil processing specialist, Servopresse, was established in 1970 with the aim of designing and producing equipment to process coil material for presses, shears, forming machines and machines that use sheet metal coils. Today, the mediumsized, family-run company is one of the leading European manufacturers in this sector with factories, covering 5000 sq. m., in Milan (Italy) and Nice (France).

Servopresse celebrated its 45th anniversary last year. Around 95 per cent of its production is sold outside Italy through a network of technically-qualified distributors able to offer customers installation services and post sales assistance/support. Experience acquired in collaboration with companies producing sheetforming machines enables Servopresse to produce a complete range of coil lines for use in all types of machine tools such as presses, shears, bending presses, punching machines, profile-forming machines etc.

As the company prepares to exhibit at this year’s MACH (UK), LAMIERA (Italy) and

EuroBLECH (Germany) exhibitions, ISMR spoke with Lara Cecchi, a Servopresse director, for her views on developing marketplaces, new customer requirements and ongoing technology trends.

## Meeting new requirements

“Since my father, Silvano, and uncle Umberto set up Servopresse in the 1970s, we have delivered over 5000 coil lines to customers worldwide,” Lara Cecchi told ISMR. “Through a period of global financial downturn that affected most of the world across almost all sectors, we have emerged stronger with a close-on threefold increase in production. I am very optimistic for this year. Our forward order book is full up to mid-2017. We have been steadily increasing our turnover (four million euros last year) as well as our number of employees and have also recently purchased new machines for our factory. One of our financial targets is to achieve a turnover of six million euros in 2016.”

The company’s production range covers all coils up to a maximum capacity of 20 tons, widths of up to 2000mm and thicknesses of up to 20mm. It has placed a keen emphasis on line customisation, automation and the processing of high-strength materials, in line with its customers’ requirements and the trends it has identified in coil processing markets.

“We see increasing demands for machines which can process new, high-strength materials or aluminium,” Cecchi told ISMR. “For our customers, it is important that our machines are very capable and more automated. The machine must be able to work on its own. Most of our customers are big companies wrestling with shortages in skilled operators – the machines need to be automated to support their operations. In the last year, our production has changed from semi-automatic to fully automatic line production.

“Customers also want machines to be faster – our machines will work for up to 20 years so customers want a guarantee that you will be there to continue to support the machines over this lifecycle. We are now focused on producing bigger machines and lines (three tonnes plus), with more automation, and no longer make small decoilers/machines.”

## An eye on automation

Servopresse can build single machines, compact lines and special lines comprising:-

■ Decoilers: single or double, with or without motor, and hydraulic self-centring expansion.

■ Straighteners: normal or feeding straighteners, comprising a cast iron drafting head and hardened and ground steel straightening rolls.

■ Electronic roll feeders: easy to use and suitable for automated lines.

■ Traditional lines: these feature decoilers and straighteners to be used with an independent feeder.

■ Compact lines: these are composed of integrated decoilers and feeder straighteners.

■ Special lines: to satisfy most sophisticated working requirements such as zig-zag feeding lines; straightener cradles; bar feeders; complete cutting lines; micro-perforation and embossing coil lines as well as automatic multi-disc cutting lines from coil.

Importantly, the company also produces its own software which is a key feature of the automation process. Lara’s brother and fellow director, Dario, is a software engineer who drives Servopresse’s IT in-house.

The family’s second generation, Dario, Lara and Paolo Cecchi are, respectively, the technical, commercial and financial managers of the company.

“When it comes to coil lines, our major customers generally request that the decoiling procedure be automated as much as possible. We have therefore developed new automatic adjustment systems for coil feeding equipment which enables us to offer complete decoiling/ feeding/straightening lines able to automate the set-up phase of the machine,” explained Cecchi.

“Once the operator chooses the stored working program, the system automatically sets the decoiler; the pressure arms; the container cones; the self-centring guides on the width in addition to automatically adjust the straightening rolls in relation to the thickness and the pass line height. This system is suitable for all companies who have multi-production runs within one shift, since only one operator is needed.“

With her eye firmly on line automation, Cecchi confirmed that Servopresse is working on a special project this year for automated coil loading on a line.

“We can now produce automated coil processing lines but one of the features that still remains to be automated is the coil loading process. This is still a manual process but we are focusing, this year, on automated coil loading from the dock. Servopresse PLC software will manage the coil car, find the coil, take it and load it into the machine. This is our new project, with a customer, this year and hopefully we will be announcing its success next year. There will be no further need for cranes for coil cars etc.,” she explained.

“The secret of our success is that we are a manufacturer, not just a simple assembler. Around 85 per cent of mechanical are manufactured in-house and we are able to offer special ‘made-to-measure’ machines which can satisfy even the most sophisticated, diverse and complex manufacturing requirements.

"The name Servopresse comes from a play on words in Italian on ‘servo’ , in the sense of ‘aid to press’. This name was conceived by my mother, 46 years ago when the company was founded and then later when the brand was filed in 1975. We do not make or manufacture servo presses but we hold the trademark on this name. Because of that, we have received several takeover offers from Asian manufacturers wishing to expand their activities in Europe!"

## Markets in focus

“Our biggest customers are the automotive stampers but we also supply to sectors such as home appliances, furniture and rollforming,” confirmed Cecchi.

“With 95 per cent of our production sent outside Italy, the international market is key for us. The global market is getting better. The UK is the most important market for us right now and we deliver to a lot of customers there. In recent years, we have delivered almost 15 large lines (ranging from 1000 to 1600mm/10-15 ton coil width) to one of our best customers, Sertec, an OEM for Jaguar-Land Rover (JLR) and many others. As result, we have decided to participate for the first time at the MACH show in April 2016 on our stand 4361 in Hall 4.

“Sertec has multi-production runs so automation is very important for them. It wanted compact lines which are very small and space-saving. Line safety is a key prerequisite for us – for example, we fix the protective barrier as an integral part of the machine and not as an add-on,” added Cecchi.

Servopresse has customers in Poland, Hungary and Scandinavia as well as Italy. It has also delivered lines to customers in the US, Mexico and Brazil. It is currently in the process of delivering a machine to a rollforming manufacturer in India and is also working on a project for a customer in Russia. The company also sold several specialised machine lines, in 2014 and 2015, to a New Zealand customer with factories in the US and Hungary.

“We delivered two cut-to-length lines to our customer, Fletcher Building. They will be used for roofing production and feature a corrugator system and a sliding unit with three blades and embossing units. Another interesting machine we delivered was a coil line for microperforation and embossing of aluminium to a company in the metalforming sector making heatshields and acoustic screens,” said Cecchi.

In February 2015, Servopresse delivered a fully automated line to DENSO in the US which featured a double decoiler. The line is processing aluminium material which is 600mm wide using a coil car for a 6-tonne coil. In December 2015, it also delivered an automated and customised line to ELSA (USA).

“South Africa and Argentina are very interesting markets,” continued Cecchi. “We also have our eye on India. Lots of European companies are moving their factories there so we are already delivering lines there. Recently, we delivered a heavy duty compact line, able to handle HLE material DP600, to MW South Africa as we have already delivered two similar lines to its factory in Poland. The Italian market is still a challenge but also an opportunity. We will be exhibiting at the LAMIERA exhibition in Bologna this year and I am hopeful that the business climate in Italy this year will be more positive.”
