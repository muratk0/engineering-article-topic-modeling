Low-manganese-emission welding wire

ESAB Welding & Cutting Products has introduced Coreweld C6 LF, a new low-manganese-emission, high-efficiency, metal-cored welding wire developed in response to new EPA regulations and guidelines from American Conference of Government Industrial Hygienists (ACGIH) for manganese exposure limits.

Compared to standard metalcored electrodes of the same classification, the wire has less than half the manganese content and reduces the manganese levels in the welding fumes. According to the company, the wire also provides consistent arc stability, very low spatter, good bead shape, and minimum cleanup. Its low diffusible hydrogen level helps prevent hydrogen-induced cold cracking in the welding of high-strength steel.

The wire is suitable for both handheld and robotic or automated applications in the automotive industry, as well as for civil construction, mobile and heavy equipment, shipbuilding, railcar manufacturing, and general fabrication.

Weld overlay cladding

TIGer technology from Polysoude is a variant of hot-wire GTAW. The basic principle involves the juxtaposition of two GTAW arcs, organized and controlled to combine into one single arc with the calorific value of the combination of both powers, but with characteristics that are unusual for such intensities, the company states.

The wire is preheated using the Joule effect via the control of a third current source. This enables a special profile of the weld pool to be used that helps improve efficiency. Depending on the requirements, it is possible to optimize layer thickness to between 0.06 and 0.14 in. This flexibility enables the quantity of weld metal to be adjusted to strict requirements, resulting in savings of filler products. Optimized welding speeds are 27.56 to 35.43 IPM, with deposition rates of 5.5 to 13.23 lbs./hr.—about three times more than hot-wire GTAW, the company states.
