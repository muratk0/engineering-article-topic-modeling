Largest AIDA servo stamping press

NOT ONLY did AIDA-America display its largest servo stamping press ever at a FABTECH exhibition recently, but it also showcased two more AIDA servo presses stamping parts at the Chicago show.

Across the aisle from the mammoth DSF-M2-4000 400-ton straightside servo stamping press (part of a complete production line, under power and stamping parts), on its booth was an AIDA DSF-C1-1500, 150-ton gap frame servo press. This press demonstrated an HSLA blanking operation and AIDA's "silent blanking" stroke profile.

AIDA's next generation DSF series servo press control system, featuring Allen Bradley Control Logix, significantly improves the performance of the AIDA DSF series (Direct Drive Servo Former) servo presses. See www.aida-global.com ■

FIPA HAS expanded its extensive suction cup portfolio with the addition of two new series of oval suction cups that enable the dynamic, slip-free handling of dry and oily metal sheets, as well as ridged, curved, elongated and cylindrical workpieces (such as rails, tubes and bolts) in a variety of automotive, metal processing and mechanical and plant engineering applications. These were on display at the FABTECH exhibition in Chicago recently.

“The new oval cup design of the SM-OF and SM-OB Series is especially ideal for use in constricted spaces. The robust new design utilises multi-part, anti-slip cleats together with anti-slip supporting ribs that provide a high degree of slip resistance, effectively absorb shear forces even when handling oily sheets, and prevent the deformation of thin sheets which is critical in applications such as automotive body manufacturing,” explained FIPA.

“Oval cups deliver significantly greater suction force than round cups of equal width, and the flat SM-OF and bellows SM-OB cups are also wear-resistant to provide long lifetime performance, which contributes to significant savings in operating costs over time. These suction cups are PWIS- and silicone-free, and feature a flexible sealing lip that provides leakfree suction on ridged, curved, elongated and cylindrical workpieces with minimal surface area such as rails, tubes and bolts.”

Rated for working temperatures spanning -22°F to +194°F, FIPA’s new oval NBR suction cups are available in different sizes with six different styles of robust aluminium connection threads. See www.fipa.com ■
