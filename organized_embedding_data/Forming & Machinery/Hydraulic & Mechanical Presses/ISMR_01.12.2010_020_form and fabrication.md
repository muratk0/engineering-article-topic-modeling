## THE BASICS OF TUBE BENDING

Bent tubes are required for the transportation of all kinds of fluids or gases and are also used as construction elements. They are just as widespread in the automotive, aviation and shipbuilding industries as they are in the chemical, refrigeration/HVAC, furniture and steel construction industries.

In general, all materials which can be used for forming in cold condition are suitable for tube bending e.g. steel, stainless steel, copper, brass, titanium, aluminium or CuNiFe. Not only circular cross sections, but also square and oval tubes, flat and mono-block material as well as profiles with all kinds of cross-sectional shapes can be processed with a bending machine. Different methods can be used for bending tubes, depending upon the material in use and the required finishing precision. Rotary draw bending is among the most common procedures, as is 3-roll bending, compression bending and ram bending (press bending).

## TUBE BENDING SOLUTIONS

TT’s tube and pipe working technology division develops and manufactures innovative products and efficient solutions for bending and fabricating tubes and pipes. Its product range includes CNC and semi-automatic tube and pipe bending machines, end working machines and measuring systems as well as customer-specific system solutions and software solutions for the pipeshop.

“In comparison with welding, bending technology has several advantages. For example, by saving expensive fittings, not only material costs but also the expenses for acquisition and storage can be lowered immensely,” TT told ISMR.

“Production time and costs for bending are also clearly lower than those for welding connections (including preparation of the welding seam and finishing works). Production failures, which often occur when welding, are completely avoided by using the bending method.

The CNC-controlled mandrel bending machines of TT’s Tubotron line are designed for serial production as well as for flexible single production of tube and pipe bending parts. All functions are automated and the speed of all axes can be programmed freely.

Depending upon the model, tubes and pipes with an outside diameter of up to 170mm can be bent. The small benders are equipped with a servo-electric bending drive. In contrast, the larger machines are driven hydraulically with proportional valve regulation.

Tracto-Technik's latest development is an all-electric line called Tubotron Vario. Based on a common main frame, this machine type can be configured as right-hand bender, left-hand bender or combined right-and-left-hand bender with up to three tool levels for each bend direction. Equipped with forming tools for rotary-draw bending as well as a roll bending tool for free-form bending, the Tubotron Vario is able to bend tubes with small and large bending radii in one clamping.

For mass production, the company has developed various system solutions which can be flexibly adapted to the specific requirements of customers. Separation from a tube stack, weld seam detection, tube alignment, feeding, unloading and depositing of the tubes can be easily integrated into the bending machine’s control or executed by a robot.

"In contrast, our Tubobend line automates the bending function while tube feed and tube rotation have to be carried out manually. These semiautomatic machines are primarily used for single or small series production as well as for repair works on jobsites. Depending upon the model, tubes of up to 140mm outside diameter can be processed,” TT explained.

A range of bending and assembly machines for hydraulic tubing is also on offer. The Tubomat mobile and flexible working centre for tubes (from 6 to 42mm outside diameters) can process operations such as sawing over deburring, cutting-ring assembling and flaring up to chamfering of the tube ends. Ram bending machines are also produced - ram bending is suitable for simple bending applications and thickwalled pipes, because the pipe cannot be supported from within by a mandrel.

## A TRUE MEASURE

If you want to measure sample or bent tubes, process-attending or stand-alone, twodimensional or three-dimensional, TT offers a range of tube measuring systems. 3D measuring of bent tubes can be done with the Tuboscan systems, which differ in hardware components, measuring method, measuring range and accuracy.

Besides several portable CMMs, which can be equipped with contact probes, infrared tube measuring fork or line laser scanner, TT's latest development is an optical tube measuring cell, which operates under photogrammetric procedure. The tube under measurement is applied to a light-transmissive surface and consecutively illuminated by several LEDs, which are installed at defined positions in the measuring cell. The shadow images from this are taken by a high-resolution CCD camera and evaluated by TeZetCAD tube software. In this way, the complete 3D model of the tube is generated within the blink of an eye.

With the Scopelink isometric system and the Robofix handling system, TT offers an automated solution for fast and precise production of template pipes, which is successfully in use by several shipyards. Isometric data of planned pipe courses (e.g. between two given flanges) are measured onsite in minutes using the Scopelink system. The data is transferred to the pipeshop and used by the Robofix device to precisely replicate the correct alignment of the flange planes as well as the rotation angles of the flanges (as measured on-site) in the pipeshop.

"Thus the most complicated precision-fit pipe constructions between two given connecting flanges can be produced quickly and easily. Employing this system solution considerably reduces the working times and costs for template pipe production," TT explained.

TT also develops software solutions and IT-supported systems for the organisation and optimisation of production processes in the piping industry. Its PipeFAB software pack is a comprehensive business solution for planning and organising efficient production processes and work-flow for the piping industry.
