"Our machines are not just waterjets – they can be customized for different operations," WardJet explained to ISMR. "If a customer wants drilling or tapping or reaming etc. capability, we can look at the application and see how we can add this option to the machine for greater productivity. Our 5-axis cutting head can potentially tilt up to 90 degrees from vertical to cut more complex parts, which is also attractive for customers. We design our own software and have a range of consumables and options to add to our machines."

Last year, Wightman Stewart supplied a turnkey waterjet system to CML Alloys that includes an Accustream AS Series intensifier and diamond cutting heads, a WardJet waterjet cutting table and twin-screen CNC controller. The AccuStream AS 60 100hp intensifier and the WARDJet ZX-2543 waterjet cutting table give CML Alloys the ability to cut nickel alloys and titanium with a high degree of accuracy and with minimal waste material.

The company has also improved lead times and further expanded its range of services using the waterjet cutting process to provide fully machined parts to exacting customer specifications.

Water Jet Sweden's Beveljet profile opened up the market to a range of new waterjet applications. Work that was previously considered to be beyond the scope of waterjet profiling, especially weld preparation for thick metal sheet, could now be readily accomplished. The Beveljet is well suited, said WJS, to producing profiles and holes with a minimum of taper and no secondary operation.

The system has already demonstrated its 360º capabilities by producing large diameter beveled holes in thick steel base plates into which tubular stanchions can be welded. Another extension of the role of the waterjet process is its ability to work with formed or pressed sheet, thereby freeing the process from the earlier perception of being suitable only for profiling flat material. ■
