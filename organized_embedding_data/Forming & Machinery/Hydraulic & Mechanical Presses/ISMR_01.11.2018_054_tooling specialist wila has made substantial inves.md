# Tooling specialist, WILA, has made substantial investments in new capacity and smart tooling for the future.

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

At this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover that were perfectly aligned with the Industry 4.0 initiative and designed to maximise productivity for its customers. Many of them are based on its Tooling Identification and Positioning System (TIPS), an intelligent system that can be incorporated in the clamping system of press brakes. The WILA Tool Advisor is intelligent online software which helps users to design the best possible tools for specific bending applications. If a standard tool is not available to form the sheet part, users can immediately create a special tool and order it online.

Along with a smart tooling app, new gripper technology and intelligent tooling developments, the Dutch tooling specialist had a lot to showcase at EuroBLECH 2018. ISMR met Michiel Wensing, WILA's director of sales and marketing, to find out more...

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements?

MW: We are very proud to have expanded our position in the Chinese market. Two years ago, we started to hire our first employees and now we have a team of eight sales people in China. We are preparing for tooling production in Summer 2019 in China (our facility is an hour from Shanghai). We have been very active in that market and are positive about the future there.

Another success for us moving forward is that we have bought a new property in Lochem, The Netherlands (where we have our HQ) and will be building a new factory which is about 1km from our existing 11,000 sq. metre HQ. We needed to expand our production and we needed more space with a second plant (17,000 square metre property). This more than doubles our current production capacity.

Two years ago, we introduced our webshop and Tool Advisor so that our customers could design a customised tool online and get the right quotation on the spot. This has been very successful for us and many of our customers now prefer to buy their tools online (whether a special or standard tool) because it saves them a lot of time, workload and administration.

ISMR: What is your approach to Industry 4.0 as a company?

MW: We are moving forward strongly in this area. Here, at EuroBLECH, we can confirm that every one of our premium tools will have a unique ID. We are preparing for tooling production in Summer 2019 in China.

We have already launched a smart tooling app, so that users can scan the tool or packaging to upload all the details of the tools in the packaging either to the cloud, PC etc. on tool data can be streamed to a newly launched (here at EuroBLECH) Bluetooth clamping system. This clamping system communicates via a DIN PC with the press brake control software. You can also stream tool data to a smartphone via Bluetooth, which automatically uploads all the tooling data into the press brake's software controller. There is no need to do anything yourself – it is very easy for an operator and saves time. There has been a great deal of interest in this at EuroBLECH.

We see this as a two-step process – smart tooling which is also linked to the TIPS infrastructure that we launched some time ago. Having ID tags in your tool and the ability to communicate with the software on the position of the tool makes the press brake bending process even smarter. Even if the operator makes a small mistake, the software will automatically recognise that a tool is in the wrong position in the press brake. If an operator has an automated system for tool change, you need an accurate and faster clamping system as well as tooling for 24/7 operation. TIPS technology means no mistakes – the software stops the bending process if the tool position is incorrect.

We are also showing our new gripper on the booth here at EuroBLECH. It is seventh generation and has been upgraded with the latest data from our customers. It can be mounted on a robot for tool change. This year, OEMs and integrators have asked us to help turn press brakes into a cell where automatic tool change and bending is done by robot. This has supported our growth and our Industry 4.0 products.

We have been integrating these new TIPS technologies into independent software controls from other suppliers so if a manufacturer has a machine with those software controls he can just buy the clamping systems and tooling from us and there is no need for any further integration.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

MW: We have been very happy with our overall growth levels. We have seen a lot of growth in New Standard tooling and toolholders, which are key products for us. Our turnover increase this year will be around 20% (it was 24% last year), so our turnover is increasing and we are investing in more capacity. We have around 340 people at our office in Lochem, 15 people in the U.S. and eight in China (although that will increase). We want to serve our customers in Europe, Asia and the U.S. Many of our customers, especially the large manufacturers of press brakes, have a similar strategy so we want to support that and be more local.

For 2019, we are trying to grow our capacity to deal with future demand. The market still seems to be strong and our customers seem to feel that 2019 may grow at a similar pace. We will continue to invest, even if the market slows down. This way, we will be better able to deal with market fluctuations and establish spare capacity to feed future growth.

We have grown our market share geographically (in Asia, Europe and the U.S.) and technologically (with smart tooling and new concepts for Industry 4.0, designed to make things easier for the customer, including service).

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers and how are you addressing these issues for them?

MW: Automation is taking off seriously now for customers. They want ATC cells with automatic tool change. The trend is towards more complex products, with customers more sensitive to material marking and always needing higher quality. Many of the end customers want smaller and smaller batches (one-offs etc.) i.e. individual product batches and they need to keep changing tooling set-up for more complex products. So, tool set-up times are becoming crucial – they need to be reduced so that customers can work as quickly as possible.

Productivity is a key issue, and intelligent tooling is a strong enabler for increased productivity.

ISMR: Can you describe your customer base and your R&D strategy?

MW: Our customer base is firstly the machine builders, the OEMs. Europe is still the biggest market for us (Europe 55%, USA 30%, Asia and rest of world 15%). It is important to maintain communication with the end-user too, via our web shops. Tooling is still a very local business so the aftermarket in tooling is done by local organisations and partners.

We want to strengthen our distribution network in Europe. We have a strong distribution network in the U.S. In China, we are still aiming at OEMs as well as distributors and we want to educate endusers in China about increasing productivity.

We have 18 people in R&D at our HQ in The Netherlands working on external product development as well as our own factory processes. We apply the same principles and vision internally as we do externally - we want to increase productivity and a lack of skilled labour is also an issue for us. We are also spending a lot of investment on automated inspection tools to consistently measure our quality levels.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

MW: We will continue to focus on geographical and smart tooling growth, plus automatic tool change solutions. Smart toolholders and smart tooling will remain our focus for years to come and we will expand this concept. We made the first step with TIPS and now we have added the second step with smart tooling.

We think toolholders can become smarter - we have the communications infrastructure and electronics to do this. This can be an enabler for OEMs to create their own new concepts on this platform i.e. adding pressure measurement or more data on when to use the tooling, in which positions etc. Customers will become more self-sufficient in using data to continue optimising the production process for their customers. Data can give you the analytics and the right arguments for new product designs to solve customer challenges.

The connection and functionality between the different platforms and the web shop can be better integrated so that it is a more synchronised and integrated operation. We want to maintain our focus in the right areas and expand our current strategy manufacturing on three continents. We will continue to invest in smart solutions.

ISMR: What is your view of additive manufacturing?

MW: We think that additive manufacturing offers an interesting application for tooling manufacture from metal. We do not see it as a threat, but as an opportunity. We have done some testing and R&D and find it interesting. It is too expensive to be competitive at the moment, compared to what we are offering today, but we will watch to see how it develops. We see other competitors going into additive manufacturing for more plastic/carbon fibre tool for prototype or temporary/limited use as a quick solution. We have not yet decided if this is for us or not.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in tooling markets?

MW: Tolerances are becoming more important – the parts being made are more complex; often flange lengths are smaller so there will be smaller V openings. We therefore calibrate the system to ensure that top and bottom parallelism is synchronised so that we don't have problems with tolerance.

You really need smart tooling to keep up with changes on press brakes, as press brakes become smarter. This is one of the key trends in the tooling industry.

In the end, four things need to work to optimise the bending process: the machine, the material, the tooling and the operator. All these need to be balanced to achieve the best result.
