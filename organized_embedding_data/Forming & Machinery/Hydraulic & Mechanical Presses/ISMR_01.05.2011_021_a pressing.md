# A PRESSING

Auto part suppliers face considerable challenges at present: shorter model cycles of car manufacturers on the one hand, and increasingly complex requirements for parts and the use of high-strength materials on the other. Trends in stamping markets have become more pronounced in recent years, particularly for high precision micro parts which must be stamped in large numbers. The materials to be stamped have become thinner, the speed pitches shorter, the required precision is greater than ever before and the pricing pressure is much stronger.

Production following the stamping process (eg. the assembly of components or the overmoulding of punched parts) requires greater accuracy and, above all, maximum repeatability. Press manufacturers have had to respond to these increasing requirements with changes in their product portfolios. A number of press manufacturers have concentrated on new press and automation equipment innovations over the past few years. Advances in servo technology, variable speed drives and projects to reduce overall energy consumption are just some of the initiatives undertaken by manufacturers.

## AN EYE ON SERVOTECHNOLOGY

A servo press is a mechanical press whose flywheel, clutch/brake and main drive motor has been replaced by a servo motor drive system to provide total programmability of the press stroke, speed and slide motion profile. A servo transfer or coil feed mechanism gives a press the ability to change the motion profile for the transfer pitch, the transfer bar clamp motion and the lift motion or with a coil feed the progression as well as the acceleration and deceleration of the material in the feed cycle. An electronic servo transfer or coil feed mechanism can deliver increased production speeds and greater efficiency because all feed profiles can be adjusted and re-timed for each part or each job to achieve optimum system operating speed.

Generally speaking, servo presses combine the advantages of mechanical presses, such as high productivity and comparably low energy consumption, with the high flexibility of hydraulic presses throughout the entire stroke rate range. In a hydraulic press, for example, the travel/time and speed/time profiles can be controlled specifically using direct drives.
