# SKELETON-FREE PUNCHING

[TARİH: 01.02.2011 ISMR]

WHILST BEING A natural by-product of sheet metal punching, profiling and forming, the sheet skeleton can compromise productivity. Parts can get caught on it and unloading the skeleton is another working process. Either the machine operator has to remove the unwieldy leftovers into a container or it becomes a job for a mechanical unloader. A workpiece skeleton increases costs in terms of production time and material wastage.

TRUMPF has now introduced an alternative. Skeleton-free processing is a new feature on the TruPunch 5000 and the new generation TruPunch 3000 machines.

“This new processing method relies on common slit cuts that are achieved without increased programming as every possible geometric configuration is resident in the software. This enables the sheet layout to be optimised and prepares for the necessary ‘release strokes’. The parts are initially processed in sequence and then freely punched before discharge and removal,” TRUMPF explained.

Parts or scrap are rotated using a separating tool with a bi-level stripper for ejection via the parts chute. The remaining strips are then crushed. The processing time per part remains the same as before but the development can greatly enhance process reliability and cost-efficiency.

“A key benefit of skeleton-free processing is an increase in material usage by 10 per cent. It results in energy savings too,” added TRUMPF. “The development is also space-saving because peripheral equipment responsible for scrap cutting and disposal is no longer required. Any scrap is shredded directly on the machine and collected with any metal slugs on the scrap conveyor for disposal in a single bin.”
