In May this year, ISMR was invited by machine tool specialist Amada Group to meet its President and CEO, Mitsuo Okamoto, at its headquarters in Isehara, Japan. It also viewed its Solutions Centre and the Amada Innovation Fair at Isehara, visited its factory at Fujinomiya Works and met several of its customers. It was a very informative and interesting trip, and gave ISMR a glimpse into Amada’s new product developments, R&D and technology initiatives and focus on continuous innovation – with a key remit to promote competitiveness and eco-efficiency for customers.

Founded in 1946 as a bandsaw manufacturer, Amada now operates through 87 subsidiaries on five continents. In addition to manufacturing machine tools in four areas – sheet metal working (blanking, welding and bending machines), stamping press machining (Amada presses), cutting (band sawing and drilling machines) and structural steel plus machine tools (grinding machines, turning machines etc.) – it offers solutions ranging from control software and peripheral equipment to dies and maintenance services. In Japan, it has three factories (Fujinomiya Works for development and manufacturing, Toki Works and Ono Plant, for bandsaw production) with other factories in France, Austria, China and the US.

In March this year, the Amada Group registered sales of 1831 million euros and profit of 147 million euros. In the first of this two-part series, Amada’s President and CEO, Mr Mitsuo Okamoto, outlined Amada’s objectives, new initiatives and machine tool philosophy for sheet metal markets worldwide to ISMR.

## A strong focus

“We have announced our intention to achieve a sales volume/turnover of 300 billion yen (2.3 billion euros) and operating profit of 15 per cent in 2015. Amongst our mid-term targets, the European portion is about 17 per cent. Competition is very tough in Europe. How do we win against our competitors in Europe? There is a wide range of strategies available to us to achieve this but I would firstly like to speak about our products and the market,” explained Mr Okamoto.

He uses Amada’s recent initiatives on fiber laser oscillator sources as an example of maintaining competitive strength in the sheet metal market. Laser is a flexible technology, open to many applications (cutting, welding etc.) and has attractive energy-saving implications for customers.

“Fiber lasers offer significant productivity increases as well as significant energy savings for customers. So the trend is obviously towards fiber laser technology and the question we asked ourselves was how do we maintain competitive strength in this field? The answer was to develop our own fiber laser oscillator in collaboration with US manufacturer, JDSU. Amada now has its own laser resonator oscillator. The other machine tool manufacturers producing fiber laser machines employ mainly IPG oscillators, not their own. So, to differentiate ourselves and maintain competitive strength in the market, we developed our own oscillator,” added Mr Okamoto.

According to data based on laser usage technology that he quoted, the laser applications market is estimated to be worth about 438 billion yen (3.5 billion euros) of which metal cutting applications take 66 per cent, marking applications take 10 per cent and medical equipment is apportioned 10 per cent. Welding comes in at 8 per cent and very fine processing takes 7 per cent of the market.

“We are trying to expand our fiber laser business so we need to expand our product line-up. At the same time, we have to expand our application areas. For this reason, last year we took over a famous Japanese laser applications company called MIYACHI TECHNOS. We changed the company name recently to Amada MIYACHI. Its yearly sales turnover is about 20 billion yen. Amada MIYACHI has very specific expertise in laser welding,” he outlined.

At its Show Plaza at Isehara, Amada explains to customers how it creates the fiber laser beam, how to control the beams and how to utilise them for cutting or welding.

“Fiber laser technology is easy to apply to the machine because the fibre beam can be delivered through fiber cable. A manufacturer can easily assemble a laser cutting machine using a dedicated source but creating high quality beams and controlling them for cutting and welding is very complex and this differentiates us from other manufacturers,” Mr Okamoto told ISMR.

Amada recently launched its new ENSIS 3015AJ fiber laser cutter and automatic nozzle changer. The 2kW oscillator corresponds to 4kW of cutting performance (for cutting mild steel sheets up to 25mm thick) and ensures stable and continuous processing across a wide range of material thicknesses, from thin to thick, without a lens change.

Amada has also turned its focus towards meeting customer requirements for flexibility, versatility, variable batch sizes and greater productivity. “We offer direct sales and service and often discuss issues with our customers at our technical centres around the world. Customers will bring their own part drawings and together we will process these on Amada machines to improve a customer’s production efficiency. Labour costs are increasing, even for emerging countries now, so automation for sheet metal processing is a very important factor for Amada and its customers. ## Automation and sustainability

Automation for sheet metal processing has been a key factor for Amada and its customers. Historically, Japan does not employ a high percentage of foreign workers so labour shortages can be severe. As the population ages, and young people opt for more officebased jobs, finding machine operators with the right skills is an ongoing issue (and not just in Japan but all over the world). Intelligent automation/robotics has compensated for this and it is no accident that Japan has, by far, the highest number of industrial robots in operation (more than 300,000 units). It is also the most automated country in the world and more than half of the global robot supply in 2013 was produced by Japanese companies.

“We employ automation/robotics in our machines mainly for loading and unloading purposes,” explained Mr Okamoto. “But, for some countries (particularly developed countries), labour costs are an issue. Variable batch size is very important and is an important industry trend. One of our new products designed to process variable batch sizes efficiently is our LASBEND AJ. This is an ultimate combination machine for flat sheet which offers processes like fiber laser cutting up to 3D forming. Our sheet metal fabricator customers often produce prototypes to show to their customers but they also have their own regular production lines. If they produce a prototype on their regular production line, it disturbs production so prototyping is an important issue for them. On the LASBEND AJ, you can produce one, ten or a hundred pieces so it can easily perform prototyping operations.”

Energy efficiency and sustainability is very important for Amada customers, he told ISMR. Japanese customers have seen their electricity running costs increase by more than 10 per cent compared to two years ago.

“We try to seek alternative power generation methods as electricity supply is a big issue for customers. One Japanese customer who installed a fiber laser has reduced his power consumption and running costs considerably. My dream is that, in the morning, when a machine tool operator switches on his machine, it comes up with maintenance instructions and then a productivity result (e.g. how many tonnes of material or scrap was used last week) as well as indicating electricity usage and costs, with a profit statement highlighting how profitable the machine is for the customer. That is my dream….

Amada’s eco focus for its products extends to its own internal practices, taking into account the overall life of the machine beyond its operational phase, material recycling, CO2 emissions from its factories and other sites and reducing industrial waste. It aims to reduce CO2 emissions by 25 per cent from its factories and new machines by 2020.

Mr Okamoto also sees a number of new trends in sheet metal markets, including requirements for processing new types of materials.

“Up until now, sheet metal fabrication has been mainly using mild or stainless steel. Another trend now is for new material processing ie titanium, high tensile strength steel and lots of lightweight new materials. The processing times for these new materials will be very important.”
