# MAKING ITS SCHULER MARK

[TARİH: 01.05.2012 ISMR]

ISMR profiles metalforming specialist, Schuler, and its strategy for future development

T he Schuler Group can trace its roots back to a locksmith shop founded in Göppingen, Germany, by Louis Schuler in 1839. The company has produced metalworking machines since 1852. Stamping and cutting systems from Schuler are used successfully in a wide range of industries. They enable sheet metal parts to be produced flexibly and economically. Its products range from C-frame presses, automatic blanking presses, knuckle-joint presses and servo presses to conventional mechanical transfer/progressive die presses.

Schuler is also a recognised name in coin minting technology. The company employs around 5,200 people and is represented by its own facilities and sales offices in 40 countries. In Germany, there are production facilities in eight different locations. International production facilities are in China (Shanghai and Dalian), the United States of America (Canton, Michigan), Brazil (São Paulo), Switzerland (Gettnau) and the Czech Republic (Mořkov).

## Expanding the portfolio

The demand for large pipes for the construction of pipelines is currently growing. Schuler AG recently reacted to this trend and expanded its product spectrum to include turnkey systems solutions for the economic production of large pipes. This was enabled by the company’s acquisition of ATIS GmbH, an engineering firm specialising in this field based in Deggenhausertal, near Lake Constance, Germany. Schuler and ATIS made a joint announcement of their plans at the opening of the “Tube” exhibition in Düsseldorf, Germany, on 26 March this year.

During their manufacture, pipes are carefully scrutinised using ultrasonic devices, x-rays and water pressure (with the “hydrotester”). There are two basic methods for the production process itself:

“Large pipes are either welded together as spirals from a long metal coil or bent to an ‘o-shape’ with a longitudinal weld,” explained Manfred Wischnewski, Managing Director of Schuler SMG GmbH & Co. KG.

Spiral-shaped pipes can be manufactured in lengths of up to 24 metres with diameters of 450 to 3,500mm and wall thicknesses of 6 to 25mm. The forming and welding stages can directly follow each other (“online process”). Longitudinal weld pipes with diameters of up to 1,625mm and wall thicknesses of up to 65mm are produced using either crimping presses, u-forming and o-forming presses, or equipment for the step forming process – such as Schuler’s Linear Feeding J-Press (LFJ press). Longitudinal weld pipes are usually produced in lengths of 12 or 18 metres. The new LFJ press can also produce lengths of up to 24 metres.

Schuler sees great potential in its new product developments – at least ten of which are expected to be launched in 2012

A lower-priced alternative to stainless steel pipes – needed for the transport of aggressive or sensitive substances – are lined pipes: a combination of thin-walled stainless steel and conventional carrier pipes. They are produced using the hydroforming process during which the pipes are also sized. They can be produced in lengths of up to 12 metres and reach diameters of up to 530mm.

## Award-winning systems

Schuler’s Icon V-Drive is a press for manufacturing up to 250 aerosol cans per minute. It features extremely low material consumption – as much as 40 per cent less than conventional methods – and can also process 100 per cent recyclable materials, such as aluminium alloys and tinplate.

In March this year, Schuler announced that it is to receive the coveted red dot design award in the “Industry and Crafts” category for its Icon V-Drive packaging machine. Past winners include such companies as computer producer Apple, carmaker BMW and watch designer Maurice Lacroix.

“Customers are not only impressed by the performance and technical specifications of our presses, but increasingly also by their design,” stated Schuler’s CEO, Stefan Klebert. “It is a visible representation of the innovation leadership we have achieved with machines like the Icon V-Drive.”

Recently, Schuler also won the BMW Supplier Innovation Award 2011 in the Productivity category for the world’s fastest press line.
