# INVEST FOR SUCCESS

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"WEBER presented its latest deburring technology at Blechexpo"

At Blechexpo, Hans Weber Maschinenfabrik GmbH emphasised its focus on investment, automation and quality.

Hans Weber Maschinenfabrik GmbH (WEBER) presented its latest deburring technology. Four separate product ranges of compact grinding and brushing machines for surface finishing, deburring, rounding and scale removal were on display. In the standard version, up to four machining stations can be set up in a space-saving, customised arrangement.

For over 100 years, WEBER has been developing and producing grinding technology to meet stringent demands, including technology for thin sheet processing. WEBER builds grinding machines for deburring, rounding and surface grinding of metal sheet and sheet metal parts. In addition to the classic technologies such as roll grinding and brushing, WEBER's STC bolt grinding beam and DR planetary head are also in use. The grinding procedures can be used for dry and wet grinding. For surface grinding and brushing, WEBER's product range includes a modular series for multiple grinding and brushing rollers, built with an operating width of up to two metres.

At this year's Blechexpo in Stuttgart, ISMR caught up with Weber's CEO, Georg Weber, to review the company's latest new initiatives and developments.

## A focus on investment

"We have received so many orders that we are currently enlarging our capacity (another 3000m² of manufacturing/final assembly space at our factory in Kronach, Germany) to help with this. We are active in various areas of sheet metal working such as general laser cutting operations, the automotive sector for mass parts production (which is growing quickly) and the stainless steel industry (surface finishing and 'linishing' operations)," Georg Weber told ISMR.

"We are a small family-run business with 450 people and saw 30% growth levels last year so, this year, we have reorganised the entire business (corporate structure, management, production etc.) to support these growth levels. We constantly push new technology development, coming up with new innovations (such as our brushing systems, new tooling etc.). Here, at Blechexpo, we are presenting our latest deburring technology."

## The move to automation

This year, WEBER has focused on fine-tuning its machines controls, to make them more user-friendly and intuitive. It is also working on integrating robots with its machines for scanning and handling, as well as loading/unloading, parts. The aim is that robot systems, with vision technology, will be able to 'see inside' a machine to identify part location on a conveyor to facilitate loading and unloading.

"We see a trend towards more automation to cut labour costs etc. Robot costs have come down and there is no reason why they cannot be used for finishing and loading/unloading operations, as they are currently used for press brakes etc. We are putting robots into our own production as well as demonstrating the robot systems to customers here, to gauge market reaction to the idea," explained Georg Weber.

"Increased connectivity and processes around the machine are becoming more important so I see no huge leap in deburring and grinding demand, but more a movement towards handling, special tooling, camera solutions for positioning and more robots/ automation. That is our key focus for deburring and grinding operations. The dream is Industry 4.0 but there is a gap before that which needs to be filled..."

WEBER is already preparing for the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition in Hannover next year and intends to promote the experiences of its customers who are already using WEBER machines with robots. In the meantime, WEBER has placed camera systems into its robotically-assisted machines to check the process inside the machine, as well as part location and what is coming out. The camera systems can be used to control the robot system. WEBER develops its own software but also works very closely with Siemens, a company which has closely embraced the ideals of Industry 4.0.

## An eye on the future

"As well as the robot demonstrations on our booth, our main focus is to find new customers and new leads here at Blechexpo. We have had some great discussions with users at this show, discussed new trends and decided on how to approach EuroBLECH next year," Georg Weber told ISMR.

"We intend to keep focusing on service, maintain and build on the good relationships we have with customers and ensure that their machine uptime and operations run for as long as possible."
