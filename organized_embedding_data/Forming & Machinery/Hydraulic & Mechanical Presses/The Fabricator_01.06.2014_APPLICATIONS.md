Large section bending machine brings new jobs to structural fabricator

Situation

Tromba Maurizio Snc, Verona, Italy, specializes in the bending of extremely large sections for big construction projects. Established in 1967 for the fabrication of flanges used for closing heater hatches, the company has responded over the years to market demand for fabricating larger and larger elements. Today its work can be seen in the Bergisel ski jump in Innsbruck, Austria; FNB Stadium in Johannesburg, South Africa; New Mestre Hospital in Venice, Italy; the thermo-electric power station in Civitavecchia, Italy; and the backbone of Italy’s Genova-Ventimiglia railway tunnel.

After years of continued investments, the company had acquired seven bending machines manufactured by MG Srl, including its largest-capacity machine, a model AR 360.

However, even with that machine in its arsenal, “we could not satisfy some of the new market requests demanding larger sections bending, due to the machine’s physical limits,” said Maurizio Tromba, co-owner.

Resolution

The company now has acquired an AR 530 from MG. The giant machine—which the manufacturer says is the largest of its type in the world—is 9 by 9 by 8 m, weighs 340 tons, and delivers 220 HP of total power.

Twelve trucks were required to transport the machine. Two trucks transported two modules weighing 54 and 64 tons, while four flat racks and six long trucks moved more than 60 parts.

It can bend all standard sections and profiles; specials; bars hard way; square, round, and rectangular tubing; angles; and many kinds of structural profiles. The biggest beams on the market—1,000-mm HEM—typically are cut in the middle the long way, bent, and then welded to form a part. With the new machine, the company can bend the profile the hard way, eliminating the other processes. Traction Tooling technology allows the core to remain straight and intact for the whole length.

The three double prebending motorized rolls are keyed to 540-mm-dia. shafts, allowing the assembly of dies up to 1,100 mm dia. The force of the two side shafts is 950 tons each, generating a total thrust of 1,900 tons on the piece to be deformed.

The planetary approach of the two side shafts toward the main shaft allows excellent bending and shorter flat edges, even on hard pieces. The hydraulic side guides have tridimensional movement so the forming rolls adapt to the different bending radii. A geometrical laser control system allows the CNC to read the radius in three points, permitting necessary corrections online.

“Now we will be able to compete on a new market segment,” said Tromba, bending beam sizes beyond the capacity of other fabricators.

Roll former allows door manufacturer to improve framing system

Situation

Premier Products Inc. of Monroe, La., manufactures commercial hollow metal steel doors and frames, as well as a complete line of building doors, frames, and aluminum windows for metal building applications. President Joey Meggs was in the market to offer his customers a new door frame—a kerf frame profile with a groove that would simplify the application of weather stripping.

“This wasn’t an idea new to the industry, but it was new to us,” Meggs said. “We initially worked with another company, but they were unable to deliver a roll former that met our expectations.”

The company required a one-of-a-kind, customized machine, and it had to be durable and dependable. Meggs investigated four or five different roll former manufacturers to find the machine.

Resolution

Samco Machinery’s engineering team designed a roll forming machine that would fit the parts being produced by Premier and maintain the tolerances required for door framing headers and jambs in varying widths and heights.

With experience producing machines that manufacture passage and garage doors as well as framing for them, Samco was able to build a machine to help the company offer the new door frame to its customers.

“We went up to Toronto and ran sample parts up there to make sure the unit was working properly, and we tested again upon delivery and installation to make sure it was still doing what it was supposed to do after it was transported here,” said Meggs. “We are very pleased thus far, and we believe our operators will have a hard time keeping up with how fast this machine really is.”
