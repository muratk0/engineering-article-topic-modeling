Modular, automated washer handles high-volume parts cleaning

Ransohoff, a division of the Cleaning Technologies Group LLC, has introduced a new, modular automated cleaning system. The RBS Modular System is an automated immersion cleaning system that can be expanded as needed to meet process requirements. The dedicated inline process includes wash, rinse, ultrasonic wash, ultrasonic rinse, heated blowoff, and final vacuum drying.

The system is set up to process parts in a basket or on a fixture. It can handle parts up to 1,100 lbs. at up to 15 cycles per hour. It cleans using a washing and rinsing process of agitation, spray impingement, hydraulic purging through immersion and rotation, ultrasonics, and heated blowoff drying.

The triple-action batch cleaning process was developed to process challenging parts, especially those with internal bores and passages and complex geometries.

Rotary oxyfuel bevel head allows 3D processing of thick steels

MicroStep has developed the MicroStep rotator, which integrates an oxyfuel torch into the company’s existing rotary bevel head design for cutting complex contours with multiple bevel cuts in thick materials.

The 3D bevel head offers endless rotation and a tilt up to 52 degrees (for plasma torches). Featuring a torch pivot point aligned with its rotary axis, it is suitable for cutting a variety of bevel shapes, including internal contours, variable bevels, and multiple bevel contours Y, X, or K.

For oxyfuel, the head has been modified to include an endless gas transmitter; a tactile height measurement system; and a 67-degree torch tilt, which allows the head to make use of the generally narrower and pointier oxyfuel torch for cutting material at bigger angles.

The rotary oxyfuel bevel head handles direct beveling of materials more than 4.75 in. thick (up to 7.85 in., depending on application and bevel angle). It offers a cut angle range of 0 to 65 degrees. The AC geometry of the bevel head allows it to cut small-radii holes and inner contours, such as for thick pipe intersections.

The oxyfuel rotator with an integrated laser scanner can use the company’s Additional Beveling Process (ABP), which greatly expands the possibilities of beveling thick sheets on a cutting machine. The principle of this technology is that the part is first cut straight, then removed from the sheet, placed on a free spot on the cutting table, and scanned for exact contour/position, and bevel cuts then are added to the contour. For oxyfuel, this means that parts from 9.85 to 11.8 in. thick can be produced with clean bevels with angles from 60 to 65 degrees while saving gas and material.

Programming system helps destroy slugs created in laser, punching operations

The Lantek Expert V2021 programming system for 2D cutting and punching machines from Lantek Sheetmetal Solutions USA helps destroy slugs created during laser cutting operations. By tackling the problem at the programming stage, slug destruct can be addressed in the beginning of the process rather than at the end, minimizing the risk of machine shutdowns caused by collisions and damage to the cutting head.

Slug destruct is the cutting of one large slug into smaller pieces that can easily fall through cut slats. It is done so that large internal contours do not tip up and get caught on the slats or crash onto the laser head. The system features several approaches, each of which provides more control over the operation, allows customization, and helps to create a more predictable process.

The programmer can assign tabs or wire joints that will hold the internal contour to the whole part, preventing tip-ups. However, when tabs are assigned, usually for punch cutting, denesting is necessary. The system provides the flexibility to choose Slug Destruct or Assign Tabs, depending on the application.

The spiral cutting function for holes destroys the residual material left behind in the holes so that it falls into the machine bed. The intelligent collision avoidance algorithm has been developed to minimize potential collisions between the heads of fiber laser cutting machines and the unstable contours of raw sheet metal during the cutting process.

Brand-agnostic workflow software designed for robotic, welding, cutting operations

ESAB Corp. has launched InduSuite, a brand-agnostic portfolio of software applications designed to help fabricators and manufacturers improve operational efficiency. According to the company, the software offers mixed-fleet customers simplicity for accessing data and documents with an easy-to-use interface; real-time insight into performance, traceability, and workforce accountability; and control over metal fabrication operations.

Cloud-based applications include WeldCloud Productivity, WeldCloud Fleet, WeldCloud Notes, and CutCloud for cutting data analytics. Installed applications include Columbus CAD/CAM nesting software for automated cutting and Octopuz for offline robot programming.

These apps help users develop welding and cutting procedures in accordance with standards and regulations; program robot, welding, and cutting operations offline; track and analyze welding and cutting productivity, quality, and consumables usage; maintain fleet equipment; and oversee all key welding and cutting activities.

The software is suitable for operations of all sizes, from small fabrication customers to large companies with fleets of equipment spanning multiple countries. This collection of cloud-based and installed software applications connects equipment and data across all brand platforms to help companies take total control of their operations.

Laser power sensor made for dusty, dirty production operations

MKS Instruments Inc. has introduced the Ophir IPM-10KW, a high-power, modular laser power sensor for use in rough production environments. It measures powers from 100 W to 11 kW for wavelengths from 900 to 1,100 nm and 10.6 μm.

The sensor is designed for manufacturers of laser-based machines for cutting and welding, as well as users who produce parts using these machines. Its modular format allows users to mix and match components based on application needs. An optional automated shutter with a field-replaceable window protects the sensor against dust and debris.

Optional communication modules provide Profinet or EtherNet/IP connectivity. These modules include two communication ports for daisy chain or ring networks, as well as two power connectors for power chaining. Measurements provide realtime visibility into the process and alarm triggering for corrective actions, enabling predictive maintenance.
