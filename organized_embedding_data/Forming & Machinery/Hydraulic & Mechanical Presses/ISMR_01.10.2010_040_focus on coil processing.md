## RAPID PROCESSING

FPB is a more universal system than an FMS, says Dalla Pace, and is designed for wider applications. For example, the line can produce lighting and firefighting equipment; electrical cabinets; elevator parts; supermarket equipment; shelving; hospital equipment; automotive parts; heating/boiler parts; vending machine parts; doors/window frames; domestic appliance parts etc.

“The customer can manufacture, for example, metal furniture parts (a cupboard’s side panels, door, back panel etc.) using just one coil width,” he told ISMR. “The line can punch and cut to length as required. A full range of parts can therefore be produced from just one coil – a very flexible process. This reduces scrap levels and increases productivity and efficiency on the line.

IRON has also developed two new CAD/CAM controllers – one for punching, one for bending – on the line for extra flexibility.

“The bending functionality features four electronic servo axes. Bending is much faster than that offered by an electronic press brake. The sheet remains fixed and is passed through four synchronized bending stations, so there is no downtime. We can count three seconds for transport, three seconds for bending and three seconds for unloading – a total of nine seconds for the finished part. This is extremely rapid bending in very short cycle times. There is no downtime or part turning time – it makes the punch-bending process so much more flexible with no bottlenecks.”

IRON SpA is currently building three lines for customers in South America, Germany and Russia.

ISMR SAYS:

“Flowforming is a rotationally symmetrical forming process, in which the volume of a hollow body is suppressed in three dimensions”

Flowforming, a decades-old forming technology, was traditionally considered to be confined, to ‘strategic’ sectors such as aerospace, nuclear and defence due to its cost limitations for process challenges overshadowing merits such as high precision and material savings. Friction remained at the core of these challenges, producing extensive heat and demanding excessive use of coolants, whilst requiring extreme forces to deal with resistance issues.

However, a new patented revolutionary approach from Turkish manufacturer, Repkon, has reduced friction intensity to negligible levels. Using a “floating” mandrel, friction is greatly reduced, allowing for speeds that are around three times faster than has previously been possible with the traditional use of this technology. Power requirements are significantly reduced when forming. The inner, as well as outer, surface quality now eliminates almost entirely any subsequent machining requirements.
