# Swedish Steel Prize finalists

[TARİH: 01.05.2023 ISMR]

The jury has nominated four applications who stand to win the Swedish Steel Prize 2023.

“The nominated applications are innovative examples of cutting-edge technology, embracing the possibilities provided by the SSAB premium steel product range. The winner will be announced on 11 May 2023 at a ceremony in Stockholm, Sweden," Swedish steel specialist, SSAB, explained.

The Swedish Steel Prize finalists are:

DigJim from Norway (ultra-fast maintenance system). This user-driven innovation is a patented system designed for quick exchange of bucket protection parts on-site, lower cost of ownership and increased productivity.

Gestamp from Spain (front lower control arm) is pushing material properties for strength and formability. This vital part of the chassis is crucial to everyday operation as well as to safety performance. Utilising advanced forming technology, this front lower control arm has an optimised singleshell design, made possible by introducing a new material strength level.

Green Buffers from Sweden (newgeneration train coupling technology). Green Buffers is introducing highstrength steel for energy absorption between rail wagons for increased train safety. This new system supports its vision of zero uncontrolled derailments related to collisions.

Levistor from the UK and ZOOz from Israel (mechanical battery for fast charging). These high-power mechanical batteries, using a new flywheel steel design, are sustainable solutions for a more sustainable world. Mechanical batteries offer increased service life, no degradation and a smaller material footprint compared to chemical batteries. Safer operation is also achieved by replacing traditional flywheel designs with layers of highstrength steel, isolating failure and limiting damage.

"The Swedish Steel Prize celebrates engineering, cooperation and steel innovations that lead to a better and more sustainable world. It inspires engineers, designers and inventors to further explore the unlimited potential of steel," concluded SSAB.
