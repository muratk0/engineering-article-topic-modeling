# PACKING A PUNCH

[TARİH: 01.05.2012 ISMR]

“Multiple operations on specialised lines, including punching, are IRON's speciality”

IRON is building upgraded demo lines for punching, bending and coil feeding/laser cutting

IRON, the Italian specialist coil processing manufacturer, is moving ahead with upgraded demonstration lines to showcase their latest new features. It is currently building a new demonstration line for punching and bending at its Mareno di Piave headquarters in Italy for a European customer.

"This system is in test right now with full servo bending – it is now faster, more reliable and more flexible. The line offers full panel bending – mostly for metal furniture, refrigerator panel doors etc. We are assembling one in the building and testing it right now for a German customer that produces metal furniture and enclosures. This will be delivered in two weeks to the customer," Massimo Dalla Pace (IRON export manager) told ISMR.

This year, the coil processing company has sold various cut-to-length lines to customers in Germany as well as laser coil feeding lines to clients in Italy, Germany and Finland. A major focus for the company in recent years has been markets in Russia, India and China and it has now set its sights on the US market. Multiple operations on specialised lines, including punching, are IRON's speciality.

## New and upgraded lines

Under a recently signed agreement, IRON's four main products – its cut-to-length lines, coil feeding lines for mechanical presses, punching and bending line from coil and laser cutting from coil line (dubbed TEMNOS) – will be sold through the Chicago Slitter portfolio in the US. Chicago Slitter (part of the RDI group) is a leading designer and builder of high performance heavy duty coil slitting lines and slit coil packaging lines with more than 1200 successful installations worldwide. It will now manufacture and sell IRON coil processing products in the US (apart from its slitting lines) and share product and market expertise as well as development and marketing costs. The two companies share similar philosophies, design styles and a strong focus on coil processing and high-end slitting operations.

"Chicago Slitter is showing customers our punching and bending systems and has generated a lot of enquiries in the US," Massimo Dalla Pace explained. "By working this way, we can give better technical service support around the clock and share our product design knowledge to produce even more customised products for this market. I think the US market is improving and there are good opportunities there for our systems and equipment. The European market is slower right now but we are using the time to continuously improve our product quality and customer service, as well as trying to develop new customers and support existing customers with new technologies and upgraded equipment."

IRON retrofitting activities include electronic and mechanical improvements to the line as well as faster speeds, better controls and higher quality tolerances. IRON has also equipped its machines to process higher strength materials and new generation materials.

"Under the US agreement, the punching and bending line will be demonstrated in Italy and tested by customers and, in the meantime, we are shipping our 4th generation of coil feed laser line (TEMNOS) for demonstration in the US. This has been designed in cooperation with Chicago Slitter – the laser/linear motors/rollfeeder designed by us and the stacker/linear motors designed by Chicago Slitter. The 2m wide line (handling up to 3.2mm thick material), mainly for automotive applications, will be built and installed in the US.

"The entry feed line (10-tonne decoiler/levelling) is done by IRON and shipped to the US. The levelling operation uses 19 rolls with 6-high back-up rolls, then loop compensation. The demonstration line will be ready in July for testing by US customers. Cameras will be mounted on the line so that all operations can be viewed whilst testing," added Dalla Pace.

## Culture of continuous improvement

IRON continues to develop its TEMNOS laser cutting from coil line as well as its punching and bending line from coil with a view to injecting more flexibility and efficiency into manufacturing operations. The punching and bending line is a flexible modular line that incorporates a punch press (with thick turret tooling), a part-turning device (after punching), positive and negative bends full servo on the punched parts and can handle part dimensions ranging from 650mm width x 1500mm length to 1500mm width x 3600mm in length.

"The strip holder and bending process is completely automated – using servo drives. We have developed a CAD/CAM system that works directly from a customer's drawing. The line itself is very complex, with different stations – coil handling; a punching unit; a shear; a buffer; a tilting device and four bending stations – and is designed for high production volumes. Each station has four servo drive motors (16 in all), which guarantees faster operation and better, cleaner process control," Dalla Pace told ISMR.

Other design elements promote high yield output such as tolerance quality and accuracy in both operations – punching and bending – as well as the ability to hold tight strip tolerances, which can potentially increase production by 100 per cent, says IRON.

The bending functionality features four electronic servo axes. The sheet remains fixed and is passed through four synchronised bending stations, so there is no downtime. IRON counts three seconds for transport, three seconds for bending and three seconds for unloading – a total of nine seconds for the finished part. This is extremely rapid bending in very short cycle times. There is no downtime or part turning time – it makes the punch-bending process much more flexible with no bottlenecks, IRON told ISMR.

"We are continually looking at new product and machine tool development to complement our slitting and cut-to-length lines," Dalla Pace explained. "The main advantages of using coil over sheet is that you don't need to cut to length, there is no need to nest (hence less scrap) and no downtime as you do not have to wait for blanks to be loaded onto the machine. There are substantial time and cost advantages to using coil over blanks with less material used overall. It is a more efficient process and certainly less costly.

This reduces scrap levels and increases productivity and efficiency on the line." ■
