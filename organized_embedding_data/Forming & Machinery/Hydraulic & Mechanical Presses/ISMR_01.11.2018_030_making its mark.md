# MAKING ITS MARK

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Taiwanese press specialist, Shieh Yih Machinery Co., Ltd. (SEYI), has established a strong position in the metalforming industry. It manufactures mechanical presses, ranging in size from 25 to 2400 tons, and servo presses from 80 to 2000 tons. SEYI products have been sold to customers in over 40 countries and have received numerous quality awards from around the world. It doubled its sales in Japan and India this year. SEYI Europe has made strong headway in Europe, since its inception seven years ago, and registered growth this year of 150%.

SEYI's three pillars for development are servo technology, automation integration and smart manufacturing, Steven Lee, SEYI's VP Sales & Service Division, told ISMR at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Germany. The company has placed a strong focus on servo press development, using an energy-saving concept that features a small but efficient servo motor, compact mechanical design and an electrical design to lower power consumption. It highlighted the benefits of this technology at EuroBLECH in Hanover.

ISMR met Steven Lee at SEYI's booth at EuroBLECH to discuss the company's latest servo-technology development, new initiatives and future strategic direction.

ISMR met SEYI at EuroBLECH to discuss the company's latest servo-technology developments, new initiatives and future strategic direction.

## An eye on servo technology

"As lightweight design becomes the trend, auto-makers are looking for solutions to perfectly form parts made of advanced high tensile steel, aluminium or multimaterials. We are exhibiting our servo press and demonstrating lightweight material stamping, via its powerful and controllable servo system, here at our booth. The stamping profiles can be customised, based upon different requirements. The press's energy-efficient servo motor can achieve low speed with high torque, lessening the springback effect, guaranteeing high-quality components and reducing tool wear rate, whether in hot stamping or cold forming processes," Steven Lee told ISMR.

## SD2 press

"With increasing trends towards electric vehicles and lightweight materials, the most challenging issue for auto manufacturers is how to form parts made of new composite materials, alloys or high-strength steel. Our servo presses can overcome the limitations of traditional presses via a flexible and controllable servo system. Press hardening, cold forming, single-stage or multi-stage processes, progressive stamping and automation integration are all possible using SEYI servo presses," he continued.

He cited the example of the Toyota Camry model, which 'has reduced its metal weight by 250kg over the past few years and is even safer now' by using high-tensile steel, which is formed by servo press technology.

"A lot of our customers use hydraulic presses for hot forming/stamping. However, if you use servo technology instead, you can produce seven more pieces per minute. That is a significant cost saving - from 30%-50%- for customers," explained Steven Lee.

He also highlighted the continuous programme of improvement and innovation that SEYI employs on its presses, as it clearly sees this technology as the future for metal stampers.

"We have managed to reduce noise levels on our servo engine from 100dB to 50-75dB. That is a massive improvement for an operator's working environment. In terms of efficiency, the press can guarantee another 30-50% additional output compared to traditional press technologies. Thirdly, it can digitally connect to external automation devices such as robots, peripheral equipment and ERP systems, along Industry 4.0 principles, for better connectivity in the factory," Lee told ISMR.

SEYI's servo motor features low revolution speed, high torque, quick acceleration/ deceleration rates and low power consumption. The press slide can run, said the manufacturer, at any position at different speeds and different slide motions can be selected. For instance, Hold Motion is suitable for special materials to dwell at BDC (Bottom Dead Centre) for extended times (BDC accuracy of 0.01mm).

All of this, says Lee, translates into value for the customer, which is one of SEYI's drivers.

"We have invested considerable resources into our servo press technology over many years. In this industry, we don't compare horsepower, we compare torque. To get the best results, you need to generate torque with low speed. We were one of the first to do this by using direct drives. To put this into perspective, if a customer employs ten steps on a progressive tooling press to form a part, then he can reduce the number of steps to three or less with a servo press and produce identical products even faster, because one machine can replace seven tooling stages and ten presses. So, it is not just about the cost of the press but its value to the customer (higher output, safer working environment and ease of operation)," he underlined.

## A step change in connectivity

Industry 4.0 is not just a technology differentiator, Lee told ISMR, but it also signals a step change in lifestyle, including the way we operate and maintain the equipment used in a workshop.

"Why do we need so many people on the shop floor? One person can do the work of 50 if you are working smartly. Industry 4.0 is not just about automation – it is really changing people's lives and the way that we live today. We are not just delivering presses with Industry 4.0 connectivity, we are also changing the way we produce, diagnose, maintain and troubleshoot the press. We have completely changed the process and flow so that all the shop floor systems feed automatically into the ERP and alert users in different countries if there are any issues. All you need is five people on the night shift to take care of any unexpected failures – most issues can be fixed remotely via cell phone. If we do not adopt this smart way of working and manufacturing ourselves, how can we convince our customers that our networked product can help them? Industry 4.0 is in our blood already," he told ISMR.

This also filters down to the way that SEYI presents and demonstrates its press technology.

"A few months ago, we tried something different. Rather than shipping a press to an exhibition booth, we decided to hold technical seminars remotely in different cities via video streaming. The results and feedback were excellent. Customers could see the press working on the screen in minute detail. We even did the remote diagnostics on the press remotely and finished all the maintenance checks in one hour, with no personnel anywhere near the press. That's the way to do business. We want to make it safer, easier and more affordable for customers – in other words, smarter. If you can keep people away from the press, you also mitigate the noise and safety issues. Users have tablets with IoT connectivity to maintain and operate the press. Industry 4.0 therefore permeates everything we do..." he explained.

This philosophy also addresses the enduring issues around shortages of skilled engineers and operators in the sheet metal industry. Designing presses to be simple to operate, even for those with limited experience, is key to maintaining productivity levels. If one university leaver, with just three months of training on a press, can reach the same standard as a qualified and experienced operator by 'letting the press take the strain' then, says Lee, this is a huge benefit for employers.

## Creating value

Ultimately, says Lee, the key is all about creating value for customers.

"It's all about what kind of value we can bring to customers. That is our goal. We work with lots of international customers and our aim is to help them make money – this, in turn, makes us successful. We are not a machinebuilder but a solutions provider and we aim to develop very close partnerships with our customers, changing their mindset on the best way to produce and design products to increase their margins and productivity," he concluded.
