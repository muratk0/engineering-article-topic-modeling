# Pressing for action

[TARİH: 01.02.2020 ISMR]

UTE SCHLIEDER METALLWARENFABRIK GMBH metalworks in Zöblitz, a town district of Marienberg near the German city of Chemnitz, has been forming metal for 140 years. Now, the company is equipping itself for the future and has purchased a fully networked MSP 400 servo press from Schuler as part of the process.

“Our dies kept getting bigger, and we were starting to run out of room,” recalled company President, Frank Schlieder. “We'd reached our limits in the production of our more sophisticated parts. That’s why we purchased this machine.”

The servo press was delivered by Schuler one year ago to the Schlieder facility in Olbernhau, located 12km away, where the other equipment is now also in service.

“The machine has been up and running in two shifts for six months now,” said Frank’s son Ben, who serves as the company’s CTO. “And we’re really quite pleased. The servo technology was new for us, since we’d previously only used presses with conventional drives at our company. But we’ve been able to make a successful transition.”

“In a press shop, the main driver is reducing set-up times and getting the most out of both the machine and the parts,” said Frank Schlieder. “Every day, you have to look at where you can improve.”

One useful tool in this regard was the advanced operator training provided by Schuler, which also included explanations of the machine’s intelligent functions.

“For us, it was obviously a major change with all of the different sensors,” the younger Schlieder admitted. “But we got used to that very quickly. We’re now monitoring a lot more parameters than before; we keep an eye on the main drives and the temperature and press force data. The operators use the Assistant to optimise the movement curves, for example.”

For processes such as stamping, forming, blanking, embossing, bending or drawing, operators can also choose from preprogrammed curves.

Ben Schlieder noted that the training has yielded “major progress” and added: “With the servo technology, we were able to increase stroke rates by 15 to 20 per cent in some areas, even though the part is formed over the same interval of time. Clearly, that’s a big plus.”

Schuler also supplied the coil line, which is able to keep pace with the high production rate.

Highly dynamic torque motors on the press can be programmed so that the slide speed remains constant during forming and only increases when the parts are being transported.

“We don’t make mass-produced parts, we manufacture high-quality items for the furniture and automotive industries,” noted Ben’s father, Frank. “That’s why we opted for a servo press, which also helps us to meet strict quality requirements. As an added benefit, the press’s controlled forming process extends the service life of the dies.”

Mechanical benefits, such as the gearless torque motors and external pressure points which enable eccentric loads, also factored into the company’s purchasing decision.

Ute Schlieder Metallwarenfabrik GmbH was established in 1880 in the small town of Zöblitz (near the German city of Chemnitz) as a maker of tin toys. Today, the company has approx. 200 employees who produce high-quality sheetmetal parts for the automotive and furniture industries. It also provides surface coating and component assembly services. The first automatic blanking press purchased by the company in 1993 was a 125-ton machine from Schuler. It is still in service today.
