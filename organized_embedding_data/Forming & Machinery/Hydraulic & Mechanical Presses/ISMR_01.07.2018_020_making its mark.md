# MAKING ITS MARK

[TARİH: 01.08.2018 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"DEES SPC has placed its focus on continuous quality improvements and the building of a global distribution network"

The DEES team at the TIMTOS 2017 exhibition. From left, Ale Lee (Sales Manager); Susan Huang (Overseas Sector Manager); Stephen Huang (Deputy General Manager); Mr. H H Huang (President); Rolern Lee (Vice President) and Tina Chang (Domestic Sector Manager)

Taiwanese hydraulic press manufacturer, DEES SPC, unveils its latest growth initiatives including a new factory complex to ISMR.

DEES SPC is a manufacturer of hydraulic C-Frame; 4-post; straightside; transfer; deep drawing; triple action; servo-controlled; tryout; die spotting; hot forming; tandem line and custom presses in Taiwan. The company continuously invests in developing and enhancing its hydraulic press technology in-house. It supplies end-users such as Honda; TOYOTA; Audi; Volkswagen; Volvo; Electrolux; GE Home Appliances; Foxconn and more.

Established by its President, Mr. Huang, in 1976, DEES has been developing hydraulic press technology for 42 years. As the company embarks on a substantial new investment programme, including a new factory complex in Taiwan, ISMR spoke with Ale Lee, its sales manager, to discover its latest new initiatives.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements and the reasons for this?

AL: Our new 10,700m² factory in Taiwan (almost double the area of our current factory in Taiwan) will be ready at the end of this year and go into operation next year. This will substantially increase our production capacity and sales volumes next year and means that we will be able to upgrade and modernise our production, increase our productivity and shorten delivery times to customers. We have also purchased new precision equipment for this factory, such as a front-access machining centre.

The new factory is about five minutes by car from our headquarters in Taiwan, much closer than our current factory.

We intend to build all our current press models and transfer all current production to the new factory. We have seen very high levels of growth, with annual revenue of US$ 31,000,000 in 2017. Turnover in 2017 registered almost 20% growth over 2016 levels.

We address safety, quality and user-friendliness in hydraulic press technology. To be certified by TOYOTA as an official system supplier in 2012 was a great success and a huge milestone for DEES. As people know, the Japanese standard of safety is extremely stringent. We have also developed key relationships with customers such as Bosch Group, Siemens, GE and more to research new developments.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal stamping professionals?

AL: The market in Asia is very strong right now. This year, the market seems to be coming back strongly. We are receiving more calls from customers and I have strong expectations for this market in 2018.

Our largest market is still China, where we have a 37,000m² factory in Kunshan in the Jiangsu Province – around 70% of our turnover is for the Chinese market. We supply our press technology to all of the main Chinese automotive manufacturer OEMs, as well as to Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers (with tandem lines, for example). However, there is strong competition from Chinese press manufacturers so that is one of the reasons we have invested so strongly in our new factory and our press technologies.

Around 70% of our turnover is for the Chinese market.

ISMR: Please explain your strategic and technical mission and objectives?

AL: From a technical point of view, we try to continuously improve our hydraulic press technology to give us an edge over our competitors. Most of our customers are in the automotive industry, with very stringent quality requirements. Over the past two years, for example, we have been focusing our efforts on building presses with higher speeds, reducing oil leakages and fitting accumulators and servo feeds for energy saving. We are also developing the capability to produce different moulds on the same presses. Most of the press parts are made in DEES factories and every part is monitored by our QC engineers.

Our mission is to produce high-precision, rapid and quality presses. Options such as moving bolsters; 180-degree tilting slide tables; safety devices; servo valves and servo motors are just some of the options we can offer on hydraulic presses. We own many patents and spend a great deal on R&D to further develop our press technology.

Strategically, we want to build our presence globally so that we can provide full service and support to our customers. We have therefore developed sales partnerships in several key countries and markets such as Russia, Indonesia, Brazil and Mexico. The U.S., South America and Europe are important markets and we wish to develop more sales partners/agents in these regions. We were recently at the MACH Tool exhibition to try to find agents in Poland. Our long-term objective is to construct a global distribution service network. We want to find reliable agents and provide good service to customers in our markets.

We export to almost 80 countries and try to find our markets in highly industrialised countries. You will find us at most of the major sheet metal exhibitions globally, such as EuroBLECH in Hanover (stand B135, hall 27) and FABTECH (stand B4921) in Atlanta.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

AL: The most important issue for our customers is service. We focus on improving our customer service and support packages as value-adds. User-friendly systems make operation or maintenance easier. Ethernet link technology in electrical systems enables our technicians to monitor press operation or fix software programs in real-time to shorten downtime. Fast service and efficient responses are our main goals. It is very important for us to establish trust with our customers.

Many of our customers are turning away from cheaper machine tools and placing their focus on stability and quality. With our focus on continuous R&D, we are ideally placed to meet these demands. Generally, customers want faster speed, greater control and more accuracy, as well as higher productivity, from their presses. Good after-service is also a priority for customers and a key focus for us.

Industry 4.0 and increasing levels of automation are also important issues for our customers. DEES has built many tandem lines with full automation. Many clients require a complete solution for feeding systems, hydraulic presses, transfer systems and robotic arms.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in global stamping/pressing processing markets?

AL: Sheet metal forming is a key process for the automotive industry. The move to lightweight material production is a very important trend in the automotive sector. New car designs will require new materials which, in turn, require new stamping and tooling processes. For example, we built a new 2500-tonne hot forming press at the beginning of this year for sheet moulding compound, a soft material. I foresee that this type of demand for stronger and lighter materials will increase in the future.

Hot forming is an important focus. Key sectors, such as electric car production within the automotive sector, have been especially profitable for us.

In terms of orders this year, we supplied a 300-tonne die spotting press technology to a tooling development centre in Mexico at the beginning of this year. We also received a repeat order from a customer in Argentina – a third order (it already has 12 presses from us) for 600- and 300-ton deep drawing presses.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

AL: Competition and price in a lot of markets (such as China, India, Asia and Vietnam) are key challenges right now. Duties and tariffs in some countries can make our products less competitive.

However, in terms of opportunities, more and more customers are asking us for solutions, and bringing drawings of a part to show us. More of them want us to plan their entire production process for specific parts and offer entire production solutions for them as a one-stop shop, with no other partners needed. I see this as an important opportunity for us in the future, along with the trend for more lightweight, higher-strength parts in the automotive industry

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
