# We outline a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing developments

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 ISMR]

“Press hardening, hydroforming, servos and hot stamping are highlighted in our stamping review”

As manufacturers seek to meet the challenge of forming lighter, stronger and more flexible parts, today’s pressing and stamping manufacturers have evolved sophisticated production systems to meet these requirements. In this review, ISMR highlights a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing developments to illustrate this.

AIDA-America showcased three Direct Drive servo formers (DSF Series) at the FABTECH exhibition in the US. Each of the AIDA DSF Series servo presses featured a different demonstration including tooling and parts production. One of the demonstrations featured an AIDA DSF-N2-3000, 300-ton, straightside servo press, recently purchased by, and courtesy of, Jagemann Stamping (Manitowoc, WI).

In cooperation with Formtek/CWP and Pax Products, with material provided by Heidtman Steel Products, the DSF-N2-3000 servo press produced a pipe flange part from 4.5mm thick, 120mm wide material via a progressive die operation at the show. Additionally, AIDA, in cooperation with Hyson Products and making use of its servo die cushion, showcased the advantages of using a DSF Series servo press for a difficult drawing application. AIDA-America also displayed an HSLA blanking operation highlighting the ability of a DSF Series servo press to dramatically reduce reverse tonnage.

AP&T designs, manufactures and markets automation, presses, tools and complete production lines for the sheet metal and tube forming industry. It specialises in forming technology and automated processes, delivering complete production systems where it assumes complete supplier responsibility – a concept which it calls One Responsible Partner.

In early 2014, it delivered an additional hydroforming press to VW-FAW in Changchun, China. VW-FAW in Changchun has almost 10,000 employees and production capacity of 600,000 cars. The facilities are jointly owned by Volkswagen and Chinese FAW. The hydroforming press, with 3500 tons of press force, will be used to manufacture suspension parts. Installation took place over the spring of 2014.

An increasing number of vehicle manufacturers are deciding to use press hardened parts in their cars as a means to reduce weight and improve safety. Fiat is one of them – it installed its first press hardening line at its production facility in Cassino, Italy four years ago. The company currently has five press hardening lines – all of them supplied by AP&T.

AP&T is also introducing new stop/start energy-saving technology into its hydraulic presses. The “Frequency Drive System” is similar to the start/stop function found in modern vehicles.

Avure released a new family of hydroforming deep-draw Flexform presses at its Open House in early February 2014. Visitors had the chance to see one system in operation during the workshop tour.

“The new deep-draw presses enable users to produce high quality sheet metal components with great accuracy, repeatability and at low cost. Material draw ratios of up to 3:1 are achievable, eliminating several forming operations, intermediate heat treatments and operator dependencies. The hydroforming process requires just one rigid tool half, the other being a flexible rubber diaphragm, leading to significant tool cost savings,” the Swedish manufacturer told ISMR.

Ingemar Nygren, technical development engineer at Avure, demonstrated the versatility of the new hydroforming press, allowing four different tool configurations in the same press, namely punch forming, cavity die forming, block tool forming and expansion tool forming. All processes used one single rigid tool half only and a flexible rubber diaphragm, backed up by high-pressure oil. The press, now in operation, was installed at a new Turkish facility (part of a Kale Pratt & Whitney joint venture partnership).

Another relatively new product, the fluid cell press type QFC 0.7x1.8-800, with a table size of 0.7 x 1.8m (27.5 x 72 inches), was also demonstrated at the event. With a capacity of up to one million parts per year, a rapid, 60-second process cycle and reportedly low-running costs, a space-saving small footprint and easy installation directly at floor level, this press is suitable for lower tier suppliers to the aerospace industry.

Avure Technologies has expanded opportunities for US-based Jones Metal Products Company (JMP) for economical partforming solutions to customers in demanding industries (e.g. aerospace, defence, medical, rail, lighting and power generation).

The Avure model QFC 0.7x1.8-800 fluid cell press features dual rectangular trays measuring 27.5” x 72” (700 x 1850 mm), a size that will enable Jones to expand applications in existing and new markets. The press operates at 11,600 psi (800 bar). According to Dan Erb, President and CEO of Jones Metal Products, in West Lafayette, Ohio, the Avure press gives the company better part tolerances and lower perpart production costs.

After visiting Avure’s manufacturing facility in Västerås, Sweden, the company decided to introduce new forming methods utilising high pressure as the next step in its planned growth trajectory. In addition to the press purchase, Avure and JMP have agreed to collaborate in the promotion of Hydroform/Flexform technology awareness in the market.

WWS Metallformen recently bought AP&T presses to manufacture different parts for passenger car exhaust systems – an ODEN-FT with 800 tons of press force and an ODEN-WP with 250 tons of press force. Both are multi-purpose presses for drawing, embossing and punching.
