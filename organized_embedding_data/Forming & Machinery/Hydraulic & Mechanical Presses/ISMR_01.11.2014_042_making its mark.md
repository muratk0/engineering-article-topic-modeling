Press manufacturer Hydraulico places its focus on continuous R&D and a flexible production strategy

Hydraulico A/S was founded in Denmark in 1946. It manufactures large straightside, hydraulic presses for deep drawing from sheet metal of stainless and mild steel as well as aluminium. Other operations include cold and hot forging, blanking and stamping, R&D etc. The product range is extensive, from automatic production lines for switches for the railway industry to the manufacture of (for example) heat exchanger plates; stainless steel sinks; bathtubs; automotive parts; Harley-Davidson tanks; SodaStream carbonators etc.

Press capacities range from 100 to 5000 tonnes and presses are all tailor-made to customer specifications. In addition, Hydraulico offers complete press upgrades and preventative press maintenance for any press brand.

The press manufacturer performs after-sales service and upgrades of its own presses as well as many other different press brands, and has developed Electronic Shimming™ (patented worldwide), a system integrated with the dies and the press to control the deep drawing to give higher output and higher quality, but at lower cost. It can also be used to draw a complex component, symmetrically or asymmetrically, in one draw without annealing.

To date, Hydraulico has delivered more than 4,000 presses worldwide. Its booth at EuroBLECH this year featured a simplified design for a heat exchanger press line, recently designed for a customer.

## R&D and flexible production

“We are working on a new deep drawing press development which we still need to industrialise. This should be ready by the middle of next year,” Torkild Salling, MD of Hydraulico, told ISMR. “We have already developed a forging line that is state of the art in the railway business – we will try to bring out a smaller line for the medium-volume segment. We have now covered the top segment of high volume producers in the railway sector.”

Hydraulico sets great store in continuous R&D for product innovation. Despite three ‘very good years’, Torkild Salling has diversified the business to supply press line technology across a number of different sectors.

“I learned the hard way in 1992, when the downturn hit,” he explained. “When the major part of your turnover is capital equipment, economic downturns hit hard and new investments are restricted. We therefore changed our strategy in 1992 to become a press technology leader across different segments, because there will always be someone in a sector who is buying. If you have the leading technology in that sector, you can cushion yourself. R&D is therefore key for us to maintain a culture of continuous innovation and product development.”

Hydraulico, interestingly, is also flexible in terms of its capacity. It has built up the organisation with self-employed engineering experts and can treble the workforce by hiring in experts for projects, if needed, to meet fluctuating capacity levels. This flexible strategy keeps expertise levels firmly in-house as well as controlling capacity and keeping down costs.

“When you hit periods of low investment, you have to adjust your capacity levels – this strategy gives us very flexible capacity. It also means that experienced guys stay with us, we can keep our customers happy and control our costs,” concluded Torkild Salling.

The Prima Power Group offers a complete range of equipment for the sheet metal industry. With over 35 years of experience, it has installed over 10,000 machines in more than 70 countries. It has manufacturing facilities in Italy, Finland, the USA and China and offers sales and service through a worldwide network of Group companies and distributors. Its range of machinery covers bending (panel benders and press brakes), punching, punch-shear combi, laser 2D and 3D, punch-laser combi and FMS.

“We are showcasing a lot of our technologies here at EuroBLECH, including our 2D laser cutting machines (5kW fibre laser). We are also introducing our new 3D laser cutting machine, Laser Next, for cutting High Strength Steel (HSS) components. It has been designed specifically for that purpose,” explained Claudio Banchi, Exec VP Sales EU, South America & 3D to ISMR at the Prima Power booth.

## Laser in focus

The Laser Next is designed to be a rapid, compact and efficient 3D laser for high volume automotive production applications. Over the last ten years, Prima Power has been steadily increasing the performance of its 3D laser machines for automotive applications. The Laser Next represents a step change in this development cycle. Productivity on a typical benchmark component (B-pillar) has been raised by 25%. This means that four Laser Next machines can produce the same as five of the previous model.

Laser Next boasts 208m/minute trajectory speed and 2.1g acceleration. Some of the advances in machine design include direct drive motors and transducers for the main axes and focusing head, and synthetic granite machine frame with optimised geometric design. It has a work volume of 3,050 x 1,530 x 612mm and is equipped with 3kW or 4kW high brilliance fiber laser. Its compact focusing head features direct drive motors, double protection SIPS, fully metallic sensor and Focal Position Control. Banchi confirmed that it has already been sold to Tier 1 automotive subcontractors and OEMs.

Prima Power’s latest Platino® Fiber laser machine has higher power and enhanced performance and delivers improved cutting quality, shorter cycle times, better process control and new automatic functions for unmanned operation. Also on display at the booth at EuroBLECH was the LASERDYNE® 430 for precision laser processing, including drilling and welding, of metal and non-metal components for many industries.

## Punching and shearing

“We are also presenting our new PSBB line (FMS including punching, shearing, buffering and bending) which incorporates the new SBe (Shear Brilliance punch-shear combination machine with linear drives)," continued Banchi.

The exhibition was also the world premiere of the Shear Brilliance cell which features linear drives, composite construction and servo-electric punching and shearing. The concept saves material with higher material utilisation, high quality parts with no nibble marks, automatic loading and fast sheet positioning with table lengths of up to 4,070mm and 3,100mm punching-shearing without repositioning.

By combining punching and shearing in one machine, Prima Power has set new standards in productivity and efficiency. Utilising servo electric motors which result in very low power consumption and low noise levels, compared with hydraulic machines, the concept is to operate without human intervention from the blank to the automated sorting of the finished parts.

The SBe enables the mixing of different components on one sheet. For largely rectangular parts, the result can be zero or virtually zero material wastage as the right angle shearing can split parts in a single stroke without the need for a skeleton. The shearing operation results in a high quality edge without any nibbling marks.

Prima Power also displayed, inside the FMS line, an updated version of its panel bender EBe5 with 2650mm of bending length and new NC control. It is a fully electrical panel bender. Close to the panel bender was its 50-ton, 2m electrical press brake. All its machines are electrically-driven (no hydraulics) to reduce electricity consumption and maintenance as well as to be more environmentally sustainable (lack of oil etc.).

“This is the biggest booth we have ever had at EuroBLECH, which is a very important exhibition for us. We can comfortably meet our customers (existing and new) here at the booth. It is also an opportunity for us to show the Prima brand to the market and showcase some of our new technologies and products,” added Banchi.

## The complete solution

As well as these innovative new machines, Prima Power showcased its new Tulus® Power Processing manufacturing execution system. This controls the whole production process from order management to finished product and reporting in real-time alongside Prima’s NC Express CAD/CAM software.

“In terms of order acquisition, at the end of September this year, our order acquisition compared to last year was consistently up. My region (EMEA, Russia, Turkey and South America) showed stronger growth compared to last year. I am optimistic for next year, especially as we tend to get a boost in orders after EuroBLECH,” Claudio Banchi told ISMR.

“Some countries in Europe are doing better than others and political situations can affect orders (like in Russia). The worry is that import duties there may be imposed which could affect our machine tool and spare part exports to Russia. Central Europe (Poland, Czech Republic, Slovakia) has been a good market for us this year – as EU funding can be interesting for decision-makers in some of these countries (up to 40% of the value of their machine tool investment). However, we have noticed a recovery, despite the political situation, in Italy as well as in Finland, the Baltics region and Spain.

“The Italian market has suffered a long period of depression for the past six years but it has got to the point where investors have to make their investment purchase decisions now as machine tools are getting old and they must stay competitive. This year, we have noticed a ramp-up of machine tool investments which I hope bodes well for next year.”

Plasma cutting specialist, Kjellberg Finsterwalde, has extended its expertise to new fiber laser and welding solutions

Carsten Trentau, MD, Kjellberg Vertrieb GmbH

Kjellberg Finsterwalde is the oldest manufacturer of plasma cutting techniques in the market and is one of the global market leaders in this area today. The companies in the Kjellberg Finsterwalde Group have developed and produced welding techniques and filler materials since 1922 and plasma cutting techniques since 1959. Today, they offer a large variety of products and technologies for thermal separating, joining and processing. Market sectors covered include automotive, shipbuilding and aerospace.

Over 200 square metres of exhibition space at EuroBLECH 2014, Kjellberg presented its products and solutions for cutting, welding and wear protection. Its main focus was on the presentation of a new Smart Focus product series for plasma cutting and its HiFocus neo plasma cutting technology.

ISMR witnessed live demonstrations of its plasma cutting units, and InFocus welding techniques, during the exhibition, and was also shown its new XFocus 2000 fiber laser cutting system for cutting and marking of mild steel up to 15mm, stainless steel up to 8mm and aluminium up to 6mm. Various approaches for surface welding and wear protection were also presented by Kjellberg under the brand name “Fidur”.

## Plasma in focus

“We are launching our brand new Smart Focus plasma cutting range here at EuroBLECH (for material thicknesses of between 0.5mm up to 80mm),” Carsten Trentau, MD, Kjellberg Vertrieb GmbH, told ISMR . “Customer feedback to Smart Focus has been great here, especially from our partners looking for this kind of product. Soon after the show, we expect to receive first orders.... Smart Focus can cut a wide range of materials such as mild steel, stainless, aluminium, copper, high strength steels and brass.”

## …Kjellberg also presented its 2kW fiber laser system, XFocus 2000…

For better gas control, the plasma power sources in the Smart Focus series are equipped with newly developed gas consoles, either manual (PGE 300) or automatic (PGV 300). Improved gas control enables high precision cut results. High cutting speeds reduce the costs per cutting metre. The new torches, PerCut 2000 and 4000, also contribute to the profitability of the cutting process.

Kjellberg’s patented Contour Cut and Contour Cut Speed technologies will continue to be used to rapidly cut contours, webs and holes. For cutting stainless steel, Kjellberg continues to rely on its Ar/H2 technology where single gases can be mixed for each specific job.

“Our results this year are much better than forecast. We are optimistic about next year as long as political situations in some countries remain stable. We are sure that, with our new range of equipment, we can be more competitive in overseas markets such as India, Indonesia and South/North America. We are very strong in Asia and South America. Europe is our home market but the second most important market for us is Asia.

“We recently opened a new research division working on R&D technologies for new product development, talking to customers and searching for new future technologies. We are growing year by year and our organisation now employs over 400 people,” added Carsten Trentau.

At EuroBlech, Kjellberg also presented its 2kW fiber laser system, XFocus 2000, as an all-in-one solution with matched components. It is ready to use quickly and easily and expands the power range of the existing XFocus series. The XFocus fiber laser systems cut fine contours with high precision and speed. The results are parallel cuts without dross as well as narrow kerfs. The system has been designed as a ‘plug and play’ fiber laser cutter especially for small and medium-sized enterprises with no previous experience of fiber laser cutting.

“About four years ago, we started to work on laser technology and sold our first compact laser systems last year. Our new 2kW fiber laser is a complete package for customers who do not have the time, manpower and money to learn about laser cutting. They get a complete package from us – a database, a cutting ‘black box’ and can literally ‘plug and play’… Laser requires more attention to cutting data and small companies may need to spend a lot of time and material on this. Our laser package helps them with this. Boschert, for example, has combined a laser with a punching machine (using our system for this) here at EuroBLECH,” Carsten Trentau explained.

“One trend we see is that customers are looking for higher speeds, more efficiency and less finishing work for plasma cutting and welding. In terms of laser, fiber and diode lasers are the new trends and we are watching these technologies in the market.”

## New welding process

At EuroBLECH, Kjellberg showcased welding of thin sheets made of high alloy materials with a robot-guided system as well as keyhole welding of medium-sized mild steel plates.

To conclude its EuroBLECH booth tour, Dr. Ing. Michael Schnick, MD, Oscar PLT GmbH Germany (a Kjellberg company) demonstrated a special welding process it has developed to ISMR. Its high power TIG welding technique, InFocus, sets new standards for the fast and automated welding of high quality seams. InFocus, Dr. Schnick explained, offers, with its laserlike welding speed, an economic alternative to laser welding and is suitable for processing thin sheets and foils, non-ferrous metals or for keyhole welding.

“The welding torch, which is the technology, influences the temperature distribution inside the cathode. This enables us to focus the arc at the tip of the cathode and get a very sharp focus to the arc, high energy density and high arc pressure for rapid and better welding. We can produce very fast welds on thicker material such as 10mm stainless steel and a perfect root. Stainless steel is very comfortable for keyhole welding but the real advantage here is welding on mild steel because the liquidity of the weld pool on mild steel is much higher than on stainless steel.

Schnick demonstrated welding of a 6mm thick mild steel sheet but, as he explained, 8mm and 10mm material welding are also possible.

“Keyhole welding of aluminium can be very difficult – even with laser – but we can melt material up to 5/6mm. For aluminium, it is very important to have a good seam. One of our most impressive achievements is the welding of copper. Welding copper is very difficult – we have succeeded in welding a huge structure with a 6-metre diameter (very thick pre-heated material). We welded it using an automated process in the workshop without pre-heating the material," he told ISMR.

InFocus offers the potential to increase the efficiency of existing applications considerably, especially as an alternative to conventional TIG welding, plasma or laser welding. It also extends the range of thicknesses which are typically covered by TIG welding, for foils and thin sheets as well as thick sheets, and enables welding speeds similar to laser.

Salvagnini’s panel bending product lines fully embrace the principles of Industry 4.0

Salvagnini designs, manufactures, sells and services flexible systems and machines for processing sheet metal. These solutions currently comprise panel benders such as the P4 P2 and P1 automatic panel benders, in a range of 14 models; FMS S4+P4 automatic panel production line and the new FlexCell flexible sheet metal processing cell. It was also one of the first manufacturers to produce a fiber laser cutting system and showcased its L3 and L5 fiber laser cutting machines at EuroBLECH.

It also produces the S4Xe punching and shearing centre; SL4 integrated punching and fiber laser cutting system; E3, B2 and B3 KinEtic press brakes and ROBOformER robotic bending cell. To date, it boasts 5300 installations in over 75 countries around the globe, covering 160 different fields of application.

## Bending in focus

With its FlexCell at EuroBLECH, Salvagnini presented, for the first time in the market, a new concept of cell-based manufacturing to which it has applied the same philosophy underlying the operation of its S4+P4 line, albeit in different ways. It is a solution that starts with bending and can be extended upstream to cutting, to optimise the workflow management of a cell comprising two of more stand-alone machines controlled entirely by Salvagnini’s proprietary OPS-FlexCell software.

“Our new FlexCell on this booth is a combination of panel bender and press brake technologies. The idea came from recent years of watching our customers working with production systems organized into cells in which various panel benders had been installed, some working in conjunction with press brakes, to complete all kind of bends.

“With its wide options in terms of the forms that can be produced and its inherent flexibility, the panel bender helps the cell to achieve high levels of efficiency, reducing payback periods. Our analysis revealed that these levels of efficiency, while undeniably positive and satisfactory, could nonetheless be improved upon. So we determined that the area requiring our attention was supervision – we had to come up with a solution, FlexCell software, that could provide the best possible flexible management of production flow between the machines in the cell, fully exploiting the specific features of each,” commented Pierandrea Bello, Salvagnini product manager.

Salvagnini’s new P2lean is essentially a sheet metal processing centre capable of producing a wide range of items, featuring proprietary MAC2.0 technology that allows the machine to adapt in-process to the material flow and the CLA SIM device for automatic auxiliary bending blade composition. The bending tool profile has also been revised so that it is possible to make return bends up to 55mm; the blades enabling bending of greater thicknesses of up to 3.2mm for steel, 2.5mm for stainless steel and 4mm for aluminium.

Customers need parts to be right first time

"Thanks to MAC2.0 technology, our new P2 Lean panel bending system can, after the first bend, determine the resistance/strength of the material being bent and compensate for this. These days, batch size is decreasing. Customers need parts to be right first time. Our panel benders are flexible and accurate enough to meet this need,” commented Bello.

“We are the global leader in panel benders with 80% global market share. We will continue developing panel bender technologies with new ways of production as well as combining machine tool technologies. Our expertise is in machine tool and line integration, with machines working together, to meet customer demands.”

The Salvagnini S4+P4 panel bending line presented in Hanover included some major new features designed to work with efficient flows: not only does the line let you work on the just-in-time principle, it also does away with the need to hold work-in-process and the intermediate handling that this entails, thus drastically reducing the machine set-up times. Helping achieve these objectives are the Salvagnini multi-press head and the ABA automatic blankholder adjuster fitted on the S4 and P4 respectively.

Salvagnini’s B3 press brake was also showcased, for the first time at EuroBLECH, with an automatic bending tool setting device. The device, Automatic Tool Adjuster, allows the machine to change and adjust tool length automatically, making up time and consequently improving production efficiency in the bending of both “batch one” and parametric parts. While the machine turns to pick up a new sheet to bend, the ATA system – practically in masked time – automatically sets the length of the tools and width of the variable V-shaped groove.

A number of its new products and processes in Hanover, such as the FlexCell and S4+P4 line, fully embrace Industry 4.0 guidelines.

## Timesavers presented re-designed and new surface finishing technologies at EuroBLECH

Dutch specialist Timesavers provides comprehensive solutions for sheet metal deburring, edge radiussing, calibration, finishing and surface finishing on stainless steel sheets and coils. It has amassed over 70 years of experience in the field.

“EuroBLECH is proving to be very successful for us. We received many compliments on the re-design of our machine tool line and the implementation of our new ‘RB’ and 'TB' model designs. Our new stand design also attracted attention and confirmed that we are on the right track,” Timesavers told ISMR. “Our booth design at EuroBLECH portrays who we are and what we are – a progressive and reliable partner in surface technology.”

Various technologies on display at its booth included its multi-rotating brush machine (RB) for pre-grinding, deburring/edge rounding and finishing for sheet metal. It is designed for the uniform deburring and edge rounding of laser/plasma cut, punched and machined parts. Pre-grinding of heavy burrs and final surface finishing can be made in one pass. The rotary brush head carousel, with four clockwise and four counter-clockwise rotating brushes, ensures an even finishing of the edges of the work piece. Tooling costs of the RB machine are still around €1.60 per hour, confirmed the company.

Timesavers also showcased its sheet metal top and bottom deburring and rounding machine (TB) for deburring the top and bottom faces of punched, laser cut, waterjet cut and flame cut parts in just one pass. By using the pre-grinding head and the finishing head, the machine is able to remove heavy burrs from the top face, deburr both top and bottom faces with the slashed abrasives and finish the top face of the product in one pass. The intuitive controls on the machine minimise set-up time and maximise output. The pre-setup programs in the PLC control standardise the deburring process.

This patent-pending machine is the first top bottom grinder in the Timesavers range and will join its family of deburring machines. It is the result of a collaboration between Timesavers, the TU in Eindhoven and a design office in Amsterdam.

“An easy exchange of aggregates is therefore possible. To change from pre-grinding to edge rounding takes seconds,” explained the company.

The head can be moved on the work piece by the stabilized arm, resulting in a uniformly rounded edge. A nonslip surface prevents movement of small parts. Because of the high friction table cover, even smaller parts can be processed.

“Over the years, we have also succeeded in inventing and patenting many innovations, including oscillating sanding pads, twin-pad orbital technology and the rotating disc. Another highlight is the Timesavers’ vacuum conveyor system, for material hold-down and feeding,” the company added.

Timesavers is based in Goes, The Netherlands, with regional offices around the globe in Shanghai (China), Taichung (Taiwan), Kuala Lumpur (Malaysia) and Timesavers Inc. in Minneapolis (USA). Worldwide, the company has more than 200 employees supported by a network of local dealers and partners.
