# With subsidiaries in the US, China and now Europe, Taiwanese press manufacturer SEYI is expanding its reach globally

[TARİH: 01.11.2012 ISMR]

Founded in 1962, Shieh Yih Machinery Co., Ltd. (SEYI), with headquarters in Taiwan, is one of the world’s largest press manufacturers in the world. Today, it supplies customers in industry sectors such as automotive/transportation, 3C (consumer electronics, computers and communications), home appliances, metal hardware, architecture and medical instruments. It employs 550 people (45% in Taiwan, 55% in China) and boasts total factory space of 34,500 square metres (12,500 in Taiwan/ 22,000 in China).

SEYI manufactures mechanical presses (straightside and servo presses) ranging in size from 25 to 2400 tons, at facilities located in Taiwan and the People’s Republic of China. At its original location in Taoyuan, Taiwan, SEYI operates a 12,500-square metre facility and, in 2003, began production in Mainland China at an 11,000-square metre facility in Kunshan, Jiangsu Province. Combined production capacity at the Taoyuan and Kunshan plants approaches 4,000 presses annually. Construction of a second 11,000-square metre facility in Kunshan was completed in 2009 and began production in 2011. The new facility in Kunshan can manufacture up to 300 presses annually, ranging in size from 300 to 4,000 tons. On 14 August this year, SEYI commenced construction of a new office facility in China.

SEYI products have been sold to customers in over 40 countries around the world and the company is a dominant foreign supplier to Mainland China, India, South East Asia and the

Americas in terms of volume. With its advanced research & development capabilities, it has developed high precision presses for the 3C and small components industries as well as large panel presses for automotive applications. With subsidiaries in the US, China and now Europe, the Taiwanese press manufacturer is expanding its reach globally.

## Stronger profit levels

At the end of August, SEYI released its audited financial results for the six months ending 30 June 2012 and the establishment of a legal presence in Frankfurt, Germany. For the first six months, SEYI reported net sales of TWD 1.9 billion (US $61.9 million), a 10% decline from net sales of TWD 2.1 billion (US$ 68.9 million) recorded in the same period last year. Continuing economic uncertainty in Europe, a tentative economic recovery in the United States and slowing economies in China, Brazil and other emerging markets have adversely impacted sales in 2012.

Despite strong sales headwinds, SEYI reported increased profits and margins. Net income for the six months ending 30 June 2012 was TWD 92.1 million (US$ 3.1 million), a 6.7% increase from last year’s net income of TWD 86.3 million (US$ 2.9 million). Earnings Per Share were TWD 0.64, compared to TWD 0.60 during the same period last year.

SEYI also recently announced that the company had established a German subsidiary in Frankfurt and hired Michael Juhl as General Manager. A graduate of the Copenhagen Business School, Mr. Juhl has more than twenty-five years of sales and management experience in Europe, including more than ten years with various divisions of Robert Bosch GmbH. The European subsidiary will enable SEYI to better support its customer base in Europe and further grow the company’s sales in the region.

“While European economies are now going through a difficult period,” Mr. Juhl said, “Europe as a whole is one of the world’s largest markets. When the region recovers, SEYI will be well positioned to capitalise with an expanded sales presence.”

In commenting on SEYI’s first half results, Claire Kuo, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, said: “We are, of course, disappointed with the slow growth of the global economy but we are pleased that we have been able to improve profits and margins in this very difficult economic environment. In addition to the operational improvements we have made, further commitment to China, the incorporation of our new subsidiary in Frankfurt and the addition of Michael Juhl to our management team.”

As the company completes its first fifty years in business, Ms. Kuo added, “SEYI is better positioned and financially stronger than it has ever been in its long and distinguished history.”

## An eye on productivity

Almost 40% of SEYI’s production is exported to China, 26% to Southeast Asia and 10% to India. The company shortens its production cycle by streamlining its manufacturing processes and constantly reducing inventory thus increasing productivity. By effectively controlling operating costs, greatly increasing production capabilities and dramatically reducing non-essential manufacturing operations, SEYI has greatly increased its competitiveness. SEYI created an STPS production management model in 2011 that reduced assembling scheduling by two-thirds, and engaged an IT consultant to upgrade its SAP enterprise management system.

With its mantra of ‘Global Service, Local Support’, SEYI has serviced a range of international customers with straight side and servo presses. Last year, it introduced a new line of energy-efficient servo presses that operate at low noise levels. The company’s single-point direct drive servo press received the 2012 Taiwan Award for Excellence and its straight side double crank press (SAG Series) received the Taiwan Silver Award for Excellence in 2011.

With a strategy of maintaining growth through the introduction of new products and technologies and letting long term goals guide the growth and structure of SEYI, the company has also formed joint ventures with other international high technology companies to improve its existing knowledge base and expand its product line.

The Taiwan Excellence is delivered by the Taiwanese Ministry of Economic Affairs (MOEA) and Taiwan External Trade Development Council (TAITRA) to encourage Taiwan industries to upgrade and incorporate “innovalue” into their products, a combination of innovation and value-added. First established in 1992, the Taiwan Excellence award aims to strengthen global awareness of high-quality products that are 100% designed, developed and produced by Taiwanese manufacturers.

“Helping our customers to operate more efficiently and successfully has contributed to our success and we want to move forward with them as a reliable partner – offering valueadded processes help them to optimise their margins. As an example of this, we displayed our new 200-ton direct drive servo press SD1- 200, our latest technology, at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover this October – the first time we have displayed this technology in Europe. The new servo model enables customers to invest in smaller tonnage machine tools that can handle and combine greater and more sophisticated workloads. This enables them to clearly optimise production processes, reduce expensive energy consumption as well as increase productivity through higher production speeds with fewer different press operations needed. Efficiency and cost reductions are the key features of a SEYI servo press,” Michael Juhl told ISMR.

“SEYI has strong R&D capabilities and has focused a lot of effort on continuous technology development, as various awards have highlighted, so that we can offer our customers cost-effective and energy-saving solutions to help improve their bottom lines. We recently created a new metal forming division at our headquarters in Taiwan to continue optimising the design and development of SEYI presses – new tooling systems, new frame designs, energy savings etc. – and take them to the next level.”

## Trends and markets

“Markets such as China, Thailand, Europe and the US offers strong growth opportunities,” continued Michael Juhl. “Our 2011 order book reflected 30% growth on our annual turnover – the SEYI Group in total made some 136 million euros – a lot of that growth was picked up in China. With our new production facility in China, we will be able to produce more than 5000 units per year and supply the growth we expect from markets such as these.”

“2012 has been a challenging year as the global economy recovers but we still see growth levels for this year and our margins are set to improve. We are continually improving our efficiency and this is reflected in our results – it’s not just about growing turnover. Our customers need to stay competitive by investing in new technology, particularly in challenging economic climates, and we can provide the modern solutions they need. I believe that the real spearhead of our growth will be the new technology developments we offer to our customers.”

As well as increasing sales in Europe, Juhl is focused on building strong sales and aftersales support networks for customers in Europe. Now that the European office has been set up in Germany (Europe’s current economic powerhouse) he will be focusing primarily on sales in Germany, Switzerland and Austria. He sees good opportunities in the UK for updating press technology with more modern presses – SEYI has already sold over 200 presses into the UK.

## A EuroBLECH Premiere

SEYI Europe GmbH exhibited its 200-ton direct drive servo press SD1-200, its latest and most updated technology, at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover this October. With its focus on environmental protection and cost saving efficiency, SEYI Europe GmbH unveiled new production methods for stamping, bending, drawing and coining from one single workstation via the SEYI direct-drive servo press SDseries. The line can be manufactured with 80-500 ton press forces.

During EuroBLECH, a fully working production line was showcased highlighting future possibilities for working with high tensile steel types like AHTSS, using the SEYI direct-drive servo press. The innovative compact design of the press frame is motor design – a specially developed, low speed servomotor with high torque and low energy consumption.

Combined with an easy-to-operate, free programmable control system, it is possible to achieve dramatically reduced power consumption, improved productivity, extended tool life (by 5-10 times) and reduced tool noise (up to 20%), Michael Juhl told ISMR. Improved workshop logistics mean fewer operations needed to finish part and, hence, fewer tools, he added.

“In Scandinavia, there is a strong culture of sheet metal manufacturing and Southern Sweden and Denmark export a lot to Germany so we will also be aiming to support these markets. Countries that are close to Germany, where German companies are investing heavily (such as Poland, Czech Republic and Hungary), will also be a focus for us. We have already sold a lot of machinery into Poland and the Czech Republic so we need to make these customers aware of our new updated technology. Our global philosophy is to provide solutions that optimise our customer’s productivity, savings levels, energy usage etc.”

He also stresses that South America has been a booming market for SEYI. Many automotive OEMs have set up production facilities in the region and it has been largely unaffected by the last recession to hit global markets.

“Manufacturers need to improve their margins and save as much cost as possible. That means improving workflow and productivity to enable them to compete effectively in the market,” Juhl explained. “Companies are fighting over the same volumes every year which is a spur to become even more efficient – greater efficiency levels will become even more important in the future.”
