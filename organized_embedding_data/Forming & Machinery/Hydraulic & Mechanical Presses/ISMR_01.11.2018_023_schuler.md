"At EuroBLECH, we demonstrate that major automakers, mediumsized suppliers and smaller sub-suppliers use the Smart Press Shop for more efficient production and fewer rejected parts," said Domenico lacovelli, Schuler's CEO since April 2018. "This means that we can give them the competitive edge they need – true to our motto for this year's event: 'Ready to Win Together'."

ISMR sat down with Domenico lacovelli on Schuler's booth at EuroBLECH to discover how he has placed digitisation at the core of Schuler's technical and strategic development.

## A digital focus

Schuler presented the MSP 400, one of the group of MSP servo press series which are available in a range from 200 up to 1250 tons at the EuroBLECH trade fair in Hanover for the first time. The 400-ton press, which is suitable for both progressive and transfer mode, can travel at an oscillating stroke of up to 70 strokes per minute using highly dynamic servo drives. Schuler designed the control of the machine in the style of an intuitive smartphone app; operators can select from predefined movement profiles or program them freely. It features intelligent software applications such as the Smart Assist and the Optimizer.

'Smart Assist' software guides the operator step-by-step through the set-up process, supported by small videos and text modules. The electronic assistant optimises the transfer and slide profiles to maximum output depending upon the clearance profiles – a complex process that used to take a lot of time. The process monitor integrated in the control unit offers extensive monitoring options. This ensures overload protection across the entire course of the press force profile and the entire movement profile; a minimum and maximum force can be defined for effective protection of the die

The integration of additional sensors – e.g. for acceleration, oscillation or pressure – enables comprehensive condition monitoring of the system, which can be displayed in the control system's visualisation.

"We are promoting digitisation here at EuroBLECH and I am proud to say that we are ready for Industry 4.0 and showing it here, along with Virtual Reality/Augmented Reality applications, on our booth. This is the first MSP 400 servo press with our new Servo Direct technology and the first press to incorporate our Industry 4.0 digital technologies i.e. our MMS machine monitoring system. We have also just sold an MSP 800 servo press mainly because of these technologies," lacovelli told /SMR.

"Everybody is talking about digitisation and Industry 4.0. They are no longer slogans - they are reality. We have products here that offer customer-specific Industry 4.0 benefits from our Smart Press Shop. We can apply these benefits to existing presses, not just new ones. Instead of taking eight months to ramp up a customer's press, which can cost him millions of euros, digital tools can offer virtual ramp-up and start-up of the machine in just four months, which can halve his costs. The benefits of digitisation include higher productivity and greater cost savings for customers."

Iacovelli has made a lot of organisational changes at Schuler, since he became CEO, with a clear focus on the growing importance of software and digital technologies, with Industry 4.0 in mind.

The "Smart Assist" software guides the operator step-by-step through the set-up process, supported by videos and text.

"We have now reorganised product development, so digitisation has been placed within the new Technologies division which reports directly to me. I have taken personal responsibility for digitisation because I believe that it needs to have its own special focus in our organisation. Software is growing in importance. This means a great technical and cultural change for us. We have also put the responsibility for product development back to the divisions. The divisions are now driving product development because I am convinced that product development must be done close to the market, so that the customer is an integral part of this process, and we can reduce time to market," added Iacovelli.

Schuler's new MSP 400 is designed to offer many smart functions, high productivity, process reliability and intuitive operability.

## An eye on markets

It has been a busy year for Schuler, with lots of reorganisation, new ideas and new personnel. It has also had to adjust to geopolitical challenges (such as Brexit) and adverse market conditions (such as the new CO₂ regulations in Europe).

"The market outlook looks quite good and we are on track to achieve our long-term goals, especially for 2019 and beyond. I cannot remember the last time that the global economic situation has been so positive, over such a long period, as it has been in 2018 and preceding years. Order intake is back on track in our core business and increasing for the first time since 2012. I am very confident for the future and, if everything stays stable, then I believe that 2019 will be a good year for Schuler," predicted Iacovelli.

"I still believe that we can do more in China and increase our 40% share of local content of some of our most important products in the country. China will be the main market worldwide for the next few decades. I believe that the European market will also grow, alongside trends such as electro-mobility, but I feel that we need to place a particular focus on Chinese and south-east Asian markets. The U.S. and South American markets are also important for us. 'Local for local' will be the key to our future success."

Iacovelli highlighted other market trends, alongside digitisation, such as electromobility, steel prices and the increasing use of aluminium, although he emphasised that steel has remained a central pillar of the manufacturing industry. He still sees steel as the dominant material for body-in-white followed by aluminium, for mass production in the future.

## A strategic and technical vision

Iacovelli has set a clear product development strategy over the next few years, alongside a global localisation strategy.

"On the product side, we have developed a faster servo press line for OEMs but we are also looking at developing a mid-range servo press line, the servo 16 line, for our mid-range customers (in China and Asia, for example). We will also offer a mechanical press line to customers. We are a premium manufacturer of presses but we understand that customers also want simple presses. We have been highly focused on the premium sector up to now but have decided to expand our range, including mechanical presses, to offer our customers a wider choice. We have a wide customer base, not just OEMs but also mid- and small-size companies, including steel service centres," said Iacovelli.

He confirmed that Schuler would be extending its MSP servopress line from 800 tonnes to 1250 tonnes. Across the same tonnage range, it will also offer mechanical presses and laser blanking lines. It has just developed the two-head version of the laser blanking system for tier customers, service centres etc.

"The second part of our strategy concerns globalisation. We have a global set-up with manufacturing sites in Germany, Brazil, USA and China so we are in a better position to deal with geopolitical changes worldwide. This will become more important in the future. We are also focusing on localisation by trying to develop and produce products in the local market. We can transfer expertise from Germany around the world. We need to use local expertise to adapt products to local markets. The third part of our strategy is digitisation, as we have discussed. So, our aim to be best in class in every segment, our global footprint and digitisation are the three central pillars of our strategy," confirmed Iacovelli.

"However, it is important to stay agile and keep refining our strategy. You can no longer work on 10-year market outlooks, you have to be extremely flexible and be prepared to adjust and refine your strategy in line with market developments."

Schuler's headquarters in Germany — the company is almost 180 years old.

## The future is now

So, what is the big picture for Schuler?

"Our industry is changing rapidly in terms of culture, technology and society. Software has become so important and it calls for a step change in traditional philosophies and a move towards more futureoriented thinking. Our hardest challenge is to change our own structures and environments to accommodate these changes and there may be occasions when we need external help. We are always studying technological developments and are in discussions with potential technology partners and start-ups, for example in IT security and Augmented Reality," concluded Iacovelli.

It is clear that digitisation underpins the future that Iacovelli has mapped out for Schuler and the press specialist is positioning itself for further growth in this area.
