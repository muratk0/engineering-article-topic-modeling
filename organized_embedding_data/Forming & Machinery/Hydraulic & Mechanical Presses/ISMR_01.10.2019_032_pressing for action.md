# PRESSING FOR ACTION

[TARİH: 01.10.2019 ISMR]

Established by its President, Mr. Huang, in 1976, DEES has been developing hydraulic press technology for 43 years. The company has now moved into a new factory complex in Taiwan and made major strides into hotforming, as part of a substantial investment programme. Its sales manager, Ale Lee, outlined its strategy and objectives, amidst changing market conditions, to /SMR.

ISMR: Please give us a brief background of DEES?

Taiwanese hydraulic press-maker (from solutions to design and manufacture) whose products include C-Frame; 4-post; straightside; transfer; deep drawing; triple action; servo-controlled; tryout; die spotting; hot forming; tandem line and custom presses. The company continuously invests in developing and enhancing its hydraulic press technology in-house. It supplies end-users such as GM; Jaguar Land Rover; Ford; BMW; Mercedes Benz; Honda; TOYOTA; Audi; Volkswagen; Volvo; Electrolux; GE Home Appliances; Haier; Foxconn and others.

AL: DEES owns 53,000 square metres overall of land which is used for our factories, R&D design centres and offices in Taiwan and China. Our head office is in Taiwan, from where we export all our press technology globally. Our factory in China mainly supplies the local market. In Taiwan, DEES has supplied more than 4,500 machines in 85 countries.

ISMR: Would you like to highlight any particular achievements over the past few years?

AL: We expanded into our new 11,200 square metre factory in Taiwan this May. Although production will be fully up to speed from the beginning of next year, this was a major investment for us, aimed at substantially increasing our production capacity and sales volumes. It also enabled us to upgrade and modernise our internal equipment and machining shops. Next year, we also expect to shorten our delivery times to customers. We have also purchased new precision equipment for this factory, such as a front-access machining centre.

Another highlight for us was our level of rapid sales growth, with annual revenue of US$ 29,000,000 in 2018.

Our European orders have also been promising. Last year, we received some major orders from Europe (an important market for us). For example, a Turkish customer with a factory in the UK (G-Tech) and one in Slovakia, bought two 1000-ton tryout presses (one for each factory) from us and we delivered these presses in April this year.

I am pleased to say that we also picked up new business. This included selling two large tryout presses (1250 tons each) to an Italian company who wish to build a company in the U.S. – it will be stamping parts for BMW. We also sold a 300-ton die spotting press to a customer in Hungary. All in all, our business in Europe is progressing well.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal stamping professionals?

AL: In 2019, growth has been much slower for us and this was affected by factors that included the general slowdown of worldwide markets. These have, unfortunately, been negatively affected by the effects of the U.S.- China trade war. Our Chinese customers have been particularly hit, with the pull-out of foreign investors in China and the reshoring of U.S. manufacturers. We have seen a significant slowdown of orders both in Taiwan and China now and some projects have even stopped or paused. However, the U.S. and Europe have still brought several new projects this year.

We are working on our backlog of orders right now but our sales revenue this year has dropped as customers delay projects, in response to the tariff escalations. This trade war also has an impact on other countries but we are looking forward to a better year in 2020, when our new factory is fully up to speed.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus/ vision over the next few years?

AL: China is one of our major markets and we have a factory there. We supply our press technology to all of the main Chinese and foreign investor automotive manufacturer OEMs, as well as to Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers (with tandem lines, for example). However, there is strong competition from Chinese press manufacturers so that is one of the reasons we have invested so strongly in our new factory and our press technologies.

The Vietnamese market is also important and we see strong potential growth there.
