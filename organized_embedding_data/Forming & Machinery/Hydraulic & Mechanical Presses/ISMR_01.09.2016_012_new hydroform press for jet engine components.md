# New hydroform press for jet engine components

[TARİH: 01.09.2016 ISMR]

QUINTUS TECHNOLOGIES has delivered a hydroform deep-draw press to Electro-Methods, Inc. (EMI), a US supplier of turbine engine components. In the face of robust demand from the jet engine industry, the new 4,000 ton press-force press will expand EMI capabilities, further enabling the production of intricate shaped sheet metal parts and assemblies.

"We have a long history in the aerospace fabrication business and, with this new investment, we are able offer additional capabilities to our customers, primarily General Electric and Honeywell," said EMI President, Randy Fries. "We are used to tough fabrication demands from our customers and are now well positioned and keen to support the expected ramp-up requirement in the aerospace industry."

"Along with strong forming capabilities, the Quintus press requires a single rigid tool half, significantly reducing tool costs. The other half of the tool is a flexible rubber diaphragm under uniform hydrostatic pressure," added Quintus Technologies.

Material draw ratios of up to 3:1 eliminate several forming operations, intermediate heat treatments and operator dependencies. The high forming pressure ensures close-tolerance parts direct from the press, with little or no secondary hand work required.

"Electro-Methods has received many awards for its diverse capabilities, which enable the manufacture of the most complex fabrications and assemblies, including flightsafety-critical turbine engine components," concluded Jan Söderström, CEO of Quintus Technologies in Sweden.

## GAC Motor completes phase II factory

CHINESE AUTOMAKER, GAC Motor, hosted the completion ceremony of the company's new phase II factory in Panyu, Guangzhou on 28 June 2016 and launched SUV GS8 and the plug-in hybrid sedan GA3S PHEV, taking a step towards further expanded production capacity with much improved efficiency. The previous month, a newly launched black GA8 sedan marked the 600,000th vehicle produced by GAC Motor since its launch in September 2010.

The completion of the phase II factory marked a new start for GAC Motor's independent research and development as a comprehensive production system integration and the use of intelligent technologies are enabling the company to achieve an annual production capacity of 650,000 vehicles. The phase II factory was constructed in 15 months following the principle of 'simple, slim and compact'. It was expected to reach a daily production capacity of 740 vehicles by the end of August and to produce 150,000 vehicles every year. GAC Motor's R&D efforts have reportedly significantly lowered production costs for the new factory.

In addition to producing the independently developed flagship car models such as GS4, GA8 GS5 Super and more, the R&D team also designed a new, innovative 90m-long engine assembly line with production speeds of 52 seconds per engine. The assembly line can produce over 200 models of engines, bringing annual production to 700,000 engines.

The company's future plan is set to achieve an annual production capacity of one million vehicles by 2020, after establishing the global research and development network supported by the GAC Automotive Engineering Institute.
