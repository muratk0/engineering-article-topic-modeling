# Micro cutting in focus

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

TO CUT NARROW incisions in hard materials with high precision calls for a specially developed waterjet cutting process. FAWJ stands for Fine Abrasive Water Jet (microwaterjet, finecut) and is a process launched in 2008 by Water Jet Sweden (WJS). Since then, the technique has been applied to specially developed precision machinery, designed to manufacture micro-components. Now, WJS has broadened the possibilities and launched its FAWJ Micro Head Package to its ordinary machine models. It is designed for customers who wish to cut fine parts with narrow incisions and high precision, without the need for the most extreme micro part tolerances.

FAWJ technique requires a special cutting head and is a waterjet cutting process suitable for very fine abrasives so that metals and other hard materials can be processed with incisions as thin as 0.2mm. The cutting process is simple and versatile, with a very smooth surface that requires no or very little post-processing, and there is no influence of the material constituent. FAWJ technology also allows you to cut quite thick materials. The thickness varies depends upon the type of material," the Swedish manufacturer told ISMR.

Water Jet Sweden's machine model "Micro" (NCM 10) is specially developed for the FAWJ-process and is used for the manufacturing of micro components. Micro waterjet cutting is an alternative to Micro-EDM and Laser. The Micro is a machine system based on Water Jet Sweden's patented Gantry design and has several features for cutting with tolerances of less than ± 0.01mm.

The FAWJ cutting process can cut as narrow as 0.2mm, compared to traditional waterjet for which the incision normally is 0.8-1.0mm. The upgrade package, FAWJ Micro Head Package, can be used on WJS's regular machine models, X-series and Premium, which both have the size of cutting tables (1 x 1m to 4 x 2m) and a CNC control system that can handle delicate sand feeding. The FAWJ Micro Head Package can also be used as an upgrade for existing similar size machines," added WJS.
