# THE FUTURE IS FIBRE

[TARİH: 01.01.2014 ISMR]

## Fibre laser cutting can now be extended from thin to thicker sheet materials

A recent innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin sheet metal has been the introduction of the fibre laser which can cut not just steel materials but also nonferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium. Linear drives enable rapid positioning and cutting speeds to be achieved.

The use of fibres eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized. Fibre lasers can comfortably be used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials and fine feature cutting.

Fibre laser light is created by banks of diodes. The light is channelled and amplified through fibre optic cable similar to that used for data transfer. The amplified light, on exiting the fibre cable, is collimated or straightened and then focused by a lens onto the material to be cut. Not only is creation of the light 200 per cent more efficient than via a traditional CO2 laser, but delivery is also far simpler, with no expensive optical mirrors. The focusing lens is, unlike on a conventional CO2 laser machine, sealed in the cutting head and is not a consumable item.

## Making a difference

“Fibre laser cutting systems have several advantages over CO2 laser systems as they require virtually no maintenance, are more energy-efficient and take up less space. The power supply is much smaller than that of a CO2 laser, yet fibre delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables,” explained fibre laser machine manufacturer, Hypertherm, which launched its HyIntensity™ Fiber Laser.

Amada launched no less than 12 products globally at the EuroBLECH exhibition in October 2013. Amongst these were innovative laser products such as the FLC 3015 AJ linear drive fibre laser cutting system and the latest addition to Amada’s punch/laser combination machines range, the ACIES 2515T.

BLM Group’s LT9 is designed for steel sheet cutting (mild steel up to 20mm thick, copper and brass up to 5mm thick with high speed cutting of aluminium and stainless steel) using linear motors and can be equipped with either CO2 or new fiber sources. Its modular structure allows it to be configured in the most convenient and profitable manner for a manufacturer. It can also process large format steel sheets with configurations up to 3000 x 9000.

Turkish manufacturers Baykal (with its BLS-F Fiber cutting system) and Ermaksan (with its Fibermak) have also invested heavily in fibre laser technology.

At Bystronic UK’s recent Open House, visitors were able to view the Swiss company’s 3kW BySprint Fiber with full automation. The 3kW BySprint Fiber 3015 has a compact footprint of slightly over 10 x 6 metres, a 3,048 x 1,524mm cutting area and a maximum simultaneous positioning speed of 140 m/min, with axis acceleration of up to 12 m/s2. According to David Larcombe, managing director of Bystronic UK, the benefits of a true fibre laser source include:

■ No moving parts or mirrors in the light generating source, unlike a conventional CO2 resonator or disk laser. This has a distinct advantage in terms of reducing maintenance requirements and operating costs.

■ Much higher electrical efficiency, resulting in considerably lower running costs. A 3kW Fiber machine uses one third of the power of a 4kW CO2 machine of average across-the-board performance.

■ Higher speeds when cutting thin material. Again, compared with the same 4kW CO2 machine, the Fiber is three times quicker in a straight line cutting 1mm mild, galvanised or stainless steel and twice as fast when cutting 2mm.

■ An ability to cut reflective materials without fear of back reflections damaging the machine. This allows copper, brass and aluminium to be cut without problems.

■ 50 per cent longer servicing intervals and 50 per cent lower servicing costs.

“The disadvantages of fibre compared with CO2 mainly relate to the cutting speed when processing thicker materials, typically above 5mm, where the CO2 machine is faster in a straight line and also has much faster piercing times at the start of the cut. There are also advantages of a smoother surface finish with the CO2 machine when cutting thicker materials,” added Mr Larcombe.

Bystronic UK’s experience so far is that the companies most likely to adopt the new fibre technology are either sub-contractors which specialise in cutting thin metal, such as for point-of-sale sheet metal work and white goods, or subcontractors using a Fiber machine’s strengths to complement CO2 laser machines cutting thicker grades of plate.

Larcombe does not see the death of CO2 laser cutting machines as a result of the introduction of fibre laser technology, as was predicted. The two systems both have their advantages and disadvantages, he told ISMR, and Bystronic works with customers to ensure that the best machine is proposed for individual requirements.

## Extending the range

With the new fibre laser system XFocus 500, Kjellberg Finsterwalde has extended its range of products for laser cutting and marking of alloyed and unalloyed steels as well as aluminium. With a laser performance of 500W, it is suitable for the marking and cutting of mild steel with a thickness from 0.5mm to 5mm, stainless steel with a thickness from 0.3mm to 5mm and aluminium with a thickness from 1mm to 3mm. The XFocus fibre laser systems can be used in connection with all common guiding systems, either individually or alternating with a plasma cutting unit, and are thus an interesting addition for plasma cutting of thin sheets.

In 2012, LVD launched its fibre laser, the Electra FL, and re-designed the Orion Plus laser cutting system, adding the Touch-L interface. With a comprehensive family of CO2 laser cutting machines such as its Orion, Axel, Sirius and Impuls ranges, and a range of automation and loading/unloading options, the company has a strong laser product line.

Some manufacturers, such as Prima Power, have also introduced punch-fibre laser combination machines. Prima’s Combi range of integrated manufacturing cells is a new generation of punching – fibre laser cutting. Its series LPe6f with high-end punching technology and a 2- or 3kW fibre laser source can run complex production orders with versatility and speed.

Prima Power Laserdyne recently announced the introduction of its new LASERDYNE 430 series of systems for cutting, welding and drilling of 2D & 3D drawn, thermoformed, hydroformed, punched, stamped and spun metal parts. This new laser system can also integrate LASERDYNE’S BeamDirector® motion and process control capabilities in a platform coupled with a fibre laser. The 430 BeamDirector is also capable of drilling shaped holes and welding a wide range of materials ranging from copper to stainless steel and to titanium.

At the recent Blechexpo show in Stuttgart, ROFIN presented two high-power lasers: the compact 3kW fibre laser and the well-established CO2 Slab laser. The ROFIN FL 030 C fibre laser has been specially developed for direct integration into existing machine concepts. With an output power of 3000W, the FL 030 C is available as a multi-mode laser with a 50µm or 100µm fibre.

Italian manufacturer Salvagnini’s L5 laser cutter, launched at the FABTECH exhibition recently, is not a converted CO2 laser but ‘the first laser designed specifically around fibre optic technology and the only one to incorporate the patented, rotary motor-driven, carbon fibre Compass head positioner’. With no mirrors to align, and no laser gas required, the result is a machine with a payback that is typically only 70 per cent that of a CO2 laser.

## TRUMPF 5030 Fiber

“We were the first to develop and market a laser using both a fibre optic resonator and fibre optic beam transmission to achieve superior results vis a vis CO2 lasers. Costs per part are slashed by more than 50 per cent and energy consumption is reduced by over 70 per cent. Running costs are only one third that of a 4kW CO2 laser with chiller. There is virtually zero maintenance,” Salvagnini told ISMR.

German manufacturer TRUMPF has gone one step further by extending fibre laser’s suitability for thin sheet cutting to thicker sheet processing. From mild steel to stainless steel through to highly reflective materials such as copper and aluminium; from 1mm to 25mm sheet thickness – all components can now be produced on one single laser machine. Its new TruLaser 5030 fiber with BrightLine fiber function is a breakthrough in 2D laser cutting giving manufacturers maximum productivity and excellent quality in cutting thin sheet and exactly the same attributes for thick sheet.

“This combination achieves quality and productivity in material of between 15mm and 25mm that is market leading. Even stainless steel and aluminium, up to 25mm thick, can be cut cleanly and very fine holes and contours applied; previously they could only be drilled,” TRUMPF told ISMR.

The most significant achievement of the TruLaser 5030 Fiber with BrightLine fiber is the edge quality of thicker materials. Although the TruLaser 5030 Fiber with solid-state laser was capable of cutting stainless steel up to 20mm thick and mild steel up to 25mm thick, secondary finishing was sometimes required. This problem has been completely eliminated with the introduction of BrightLine fiber. Edge quality and process reliability are assured. And additionally, the machine’s cutting capacity for aluminium and stainless steel has increased to 25mm.

Used in conjunction with BrightLine fiber, a new multi-stage piercing process offers additional advantages when cutting stainless steel. It prevents slag from forming when the laser beam pierces the sheet. This means that the machine no longer needs to give the cutting point a wide berth and can therefore execute even finer contours.

The TruLaser 5030 Fiber with BrightLine fiber not only processes thick and thin sheet with equal quality but is also suitable for cutting nonferrous metals such as copper and brass up to 10mm thick.

The new TruLaser 5030 is now TRUMPF’s CO2 flagship, all-purpose cutting machine. It cuts mild steel of up to 25mm, stainless steel up to 50mm and aluminium of up to 25mm with excellent edge quality throughout the whole spectrum. The TruLaser 5040, also introduced at Blechexpo recently, extends these benefits to large sheet processing; its capacity is 4m x 2m. The energy-efficient 6- or 8kW TruFlow CO2 laser, a new feature of these machines, further reduces operating costs thanks to an innovative high temperature cooling system.

TRUMPF’s new MobileControl app transfers the standard, control panel user interface on any model in the new TruLaser 5000 Series to an iPad touch screen. The facility gives operators complete freedom when monitoring and controlling the 2D laser machine. ■
