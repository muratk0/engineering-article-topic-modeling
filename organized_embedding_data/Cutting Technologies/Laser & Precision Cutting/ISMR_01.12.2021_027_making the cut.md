# MAKING THE CUT

[TARİH: 01.01.2021 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new global fibre laser cutting innovations in the market.

ISMR SAYS:

“Fibre lasers can be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials, as well as fine feature cutting.”

The fibre laser is a relatively new innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin, and now thicker, sheet metal (including non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium). Fibre lasers work with low amplitude noise, are extremely resistant to heavy environments and offer low maintenance costs. The technology has quickly been embraced by subcontractors and job shops as its extended cutting capabilities have increased their reach into new markets and new customers. A range of manufacturers have therefore invested heavily in fibre laser offerings to the global market.

Due to their excellent beam quality, fibre lasers are a key choice for diverse applications in laser precision cutting and welding, as well as in micro-materials processing and laser marking. In laser precision welding, fibre lasers enable users to create slim seams and achieve narrow kerfs in laser precision cutting – even with wide working distances. In thin materials, the high level of irradiance on the workpiece can be converted directly into a high process speed.

The use of fibres eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized. This also makes fibre lasers very stable and easy to use. The beam quality is high and continuously becoming more powerful. Fibre lasers are also very efficient and easy to cool, converting around 70-80% of the power from the pump source. This is better for power consumption, but also means that less power is being converted to heat. Fibre lasers can be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials as well as fine feature cutting. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

## Fibre in focus

Winner of the MM Award at EuroBLECH 2018 for ‘Cutting Technology’, AMADA launched its next-generation fibre laser cutting machine in the UK in September 2019. The AMADA VENTIS-3015AJ fibre laser cutter with AS LUL3015 tower uses a single diode module 4kW oscillator and proprietary LBC (Locus Beam Control) technology.

Available with 3-, 6- and 9kW fibre laser engines developed by AMADA using 3kW diode modules, the ENSIS-AJ range considerably increases production capacity. The 6kW and 9kW versions introduce AMADA’s Auto Collimation system, which allows the machine to automatically adjust the beam diameter for different materials and thicknesses with a single cutting lens.

The ENSIS-Rotary Index (RI) fibre laser is for flat, tube, channel and angle profile processing. The 3kW LCG-AJ boasts a carriage with low centre of gravity and comprises a single module and no combiner. AMADA also offers a range of punch/ laser combination machines for sheetmetal processing, plus its V-factory system providing real-time data from AMADA machines with AMNC control, anytime, anywhere, using any device with an internet connection.

Turkish manufacturer, Baykal, is promoting its fibre laser cutting system, BLS-1530. The BLS Series fibre laser cutting systems cover a wide range of industrial applications. Manufactured with an “All-in-One Box” design structure, the BLS has a built-in resonator and a chiller with dual motorised tables. A range of fibre power sources, from 500-2500W, are available. The BLS uses laser source or sheet, enabling high-speed, high-accuracy cutting and dual full-size pallet maximum production throughput. Machines have a simple and user-friendly operator interface, as well as an extensive cutting database,” the company told ISMR.

Bystronic’s 15kW ByStar Fiber offers sheet metal processing companies even better support in an increasingly competitive environment. The new power level is available for the ByStar Fiber 3015 and the ByStar Fiber 4020. To optimally integrate the higher laser power into the cutting process, Bystronic has enhanced the design of the ByStar Fiber’s cutting head to process a wide range of materials. The new ByStar Fiber cuts steel, aluminium and stainless steel in thicknesses of between one and 30mm, and brass and copper in thicknesses up to 20mm. The 15-kilowatt laser output now enables extended applications in steel and aluminium of up to 50mm.

Bystronic introduced its new “BeamShaper” function for flame cutting at Blechexpo in November 2019. The “BeamShaper” enables the cutting of steel sheets up to 30mm as well as adjustment of the cutting process to adapt to the diverse characteristics of changing sheet metal qualities. It has also expanded the ByStar Fiber laser cutting system with rotary axis for tube processing. The new function enables users to switch between flatbed laser cutting and tube processing in a few simple steps.

Bystronic now also offers the BySmart Fiber laser cutting system with more powerful laser outputs of 8- or 10 kilowatts. In combination with a laser cutting system, the new ByTrans Modular loading and unloading automation solution is for both large series and small batches. Thanks to its automated cassette changing and small footprint, the ByTower Compact automation solution opens up lightly manned or even unmanned production for any business size.

CYLASER has been pioneering the application of solid-state fibre optic laser technology for industrial cutting applications since 2004. Today, and hundreds of installations later, it has refined and improved its original vision of a robust, precise, efficient and productive fibre laser cutting system with two laser cutting processes, Vortex and Vega (designed to offer excellent cut quality). It showcased a new machine with linear motors at the LAMIERA exhibition in Milan in 2019 (linear motors give users more control over the cutting speed). It is also in the process of developing a new bevel head to increase cutting possibilities.

The technology has quickly been embraced by subcontractors and job shops as its extended cutting capabilities have increased their reach into new markets and new customers.

The Vega cutting process exploits laser beam diameter and a Programmable Shape laser beam enables users to automatically select the laser spot dimension with the same optics set-up. It is designed to increase the cutting quality for any metal, substantially reduce the cutting roughness on stainless steel and increase the quality of the cut on thicker materials. This includes enhanced cut quality on stainless steel and aluminium. Vortex is a process that is designed to enable users to obtain higher cutting quality on >8mm material thicknesses. With the Vortex feature, the user can take full advantage of fibre laser’s potential through beam emission frequency modulation and in-kerf gas flow management. Benefits include lower roughness on stainless steel cutting edge; reduced gas consumption in comparison to standard parameters; lower material heating (so that most complicated patterns can be cut) and easier part extraction from scrap.

CYLASER’s wide fibre laser cutting product portfolio includes 2D laser cutting machines (some with tube processing capabilities); tube laser cutting systems and 3D laser cutting machines. Machines range from 2.5m x 1.25m to 9m x 3m. All CYLASER machines are powered with up to 12kW fibre optic generators. The company also offers a variety of Load Unload Automation Systems and Material Storage Towers with automatic material retrieval, loading and unloading functions for lights-out operation.

Turkish manufacturer, DURMA, showcased its new HDFL 6G fibre laser cutting machine (with 10kW power source and high acceleration for faster speeds) at Blechexpo in Stuttgart in November 2019. The laser machine’s design, it told ISMR, had been completely updated.

“Our 3D laser cutting machine cuts through metal alloy parts with thicknesses of between 0.5mm-8mm. It is mainly used in the automotive industry for drilling and perimeter cutting of pre-formed materials which cannot be punched,” its sales and marketing director, Kemal Ileri, explained.

DURMA was a founder member of the Industry 4.0 group in Turkey and started developing its own Industry 4.0 software and developments in concert with this group. Now, all of its machines use these systems and it has also developed its own cloud systems to store data etc. DURMA also produces its HD-TC pipe and profile fibre laser cutting system.
