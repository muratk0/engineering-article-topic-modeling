# FOCUS ON FIBRE LASERS

[TARİH: 01.03.2016 ISMR]

“3D tilt cutting is no longer an issue regarding the type of laser technology to choose (CO2 or fibre), the tube size (diameters of up to 610mm /24”) or the tube geometry (straight or curved). The ability to tilt cut satisfies the need to prepare parts for welding and for applications requiring non-orthogonal cuts,” BLM added.

Its LT-FREE five-axis laser cutting system is designed for flexibility and simplicity of use for cutting 3D parts of different shapes; bent tubes, flat or stamped sheet, hydroformed components and welded assemblies can be pierced and trimmed.

Polish innovator Eckert launched an awardwinning innovative Piercing Control System (PCS) for its Diamond Fiber Laser 2 cutting machine which substantially reduces piercing time from 10 seconds to two seconds, as well as cutting gas/energy costs.

Turkish manufacturer, Ermaksan, unveiled the Fibermak Momentum Gen-3 G-Force fibre laser at Blechexpo in November 2015. It is designed for ultra-low energy consumption and very rapid cutting capability with low maintenance costs.

“The new machine features an advanced servo system where acceleration speeds can be increased up to 2.5G. There is a need for this in the market. We are also providing this upgrade for new and existing customers. along with free software upgrades. We produce our opensource software in-house, which gives us more control,” commented Ermaksan’s sales manager, Ersin Yildiz.

Fraunhofer ILT’s simulation work is aimed at optimising the process parameters when cutting with a fibre laser. This is part of an EU project called FILCO, which aims to build a 1μm laser head capable of cutting 15mm thick stainless steel with a cut quality similar to CO2 systems.

HIGHYAG Lasertechnologie GmbH (HIGHYAG), a subsidiary of II-VI Incorporated, is a leading supplier in the laser material processing industry. Its laser processing heads and fibre beam delivery systems are used for laser cutting, welding and brazing in the advanced manufacturing industry. The BIMO-FSC laser cutting head can cut various sheet thicknesses and is fully controlled by the cutting machine without manual intervention. The company also produces laser light cables with the new LLK-Q connector which is QBH-compatible and designed for dynamic use in cutting machines and high-brilliance lasers.

“Fibre laser cutting systems have several advantages over CO2 laser systems as they require virtually no maintenance, are more energy-efficient and take up less space. The power supply is much smaller than that of a CO2 laser, yet fibre delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables,” explained fibre laser machine manufacturer, Hypertherm, which has produced the HyIntensity™ Fiber Laser.

Hypertherm, a manufacturer of plasma, laser and waterjet cutting systems, is transitioning its Integrated Cutting Solutions (ICS) brand to SureCut™ Technology. SureCut is designed to maximize cutting performance by automatically applying optimised cutting process parameters to simplify cutting and reduce operator intervention. SureCut is delivered through a combination of Hypertherm products such as automated systems, CNCs and CAM software.

Kjellberg’s XFocus 1000 is a compact system with a 1,000 Watt fibre laser source for cutting and marking thin sheets of mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium with all common guiding systems that are suitable for plasma cutting. The laser system can be operated on the same guiding system with a plasma cutting unit, either in change mode or separately.

The Phoenix FL now completes LVD’s fibre laser line-up. The Electra was introduced in 2013 as a high- efficiency, ultra-fast fibre laser system and the Lynx in 2015 as an entry into the world of fibre laser cutting.

“The Phoenix design includes a welded steel frame construction that minimizes deformation caused by high acceleration, thus improving overall machine accuracy. Powered by a high efficiency solid-state doped fibre laser source, it provides fast, accurate processing of various sheet thicknesses and types. The Phoenix FL features a lightweight, high rigidity beam delivery system to allow for highly dynamic processing. It maximizes uptime with an integrated automatic shuttle table system that allows one table to be loaded while the machine is cutting on the other table. Table change time is only 30 seconds,” LVD told ISMR.

LVD’s Lynx FL features a Precitec “Light-Cutter” for high cutting speed with cutting quality and incorporates a protective glass cartridge change, temperature and constant distance control and a crash-protection system.

As part of the Newport Corporation, Ophir’s optics group is a leading manufacturer of infrared optics and optics for high-power lasers. In June 2015, it demonstrated its Fiberlens™, a series of innovative collimating lenses, focusing lenses and protective windows for use with high-power fibre lasers at the LASER World of PHOTONICS in Munich.

“We see that fibre laser in gaining more of a foothold is the laser cutting market. At the moment, we are looking to increase power on our fibre laser machines. There are a lot of manufacturers in this area. We expect the fibre laser market to segment and we want to be present in all segments, but especially towards the top end of the market,” Claudio Banchi, Prima Power’s executive VP of sales, told ISMR.

At Blechexpo 2015, Prima Power launched a new 2D fibre laser machine, Genius, with linear drives and a higher range with greater power and performance than its Platinum machine (6kW). At the same time, it also presented its new CombiGenius combination machine – punching and fibre laser – with a very compact design and patent-pending intelligent ram system.

The new Laser Genius – the spearhead of Prima Power 2D fibre laser cutting technology – features high dynamic linear motors for a 15% increase in productivity compared with conventional drive systems. The machine has a comprehensive range of automation modules and CNC proprietary management guarantees accuracy in cutting and head positioning.

The Prima Power fibre cutting head employs a single lens strategy, an impact protection system and a highly dynamic focal axis. The lens drawer has a quick alignment system (OPC) and protection glass drawer for easy inspection. A series of options are dedicated to different production needs, Prima told ISMR – SMART Cut for rapid cutting of thin sheets (up to 5mm) allows a reduction of cycle times by up to 30%, MAX Cut for the fast cutting of medium-thick gauge sheets, reducing processing times by up to 40%, and NIGHT Cut, for intensive production and higher piercing and cutting process safety.

At Blechexpo, Prima Power also introduced the punching unit of its new Combi Genius punching – laser cutting cell.

Based on the Platino® Fiber laser platform, the new version of Prima Power’s high powered Platino® Fiber laser is available in versions of up to 5kW. The machine delivers piercing time reductions of up to 90%, near-zero lens set-up times and cutting speeds of up to 5m/min on stainless steel, which is on average 20% faster than the 4kW machine. It also has an option for a Laser Piercing Monitor, to give real-time feedback and check when piercing is completed, and Prima Power Fiber Plasma Monitoring (detecting problems with the cutting and formation of plasma when cutting is lost).

Costs per part are slashed by more than 50% and energy consumption is reduced by over 70%

“We were the first to develop and market a laser using both a fibre optic resonator and fibre optic beam transmission to achieve superior results vis a vis CO2 lasers. Costs per part are slashed by more than 50% and energy consumption is reduced by over 70%. Running costs are only one third that of a 4kW CO2 laser with chiller. There is virtually zero maintenance,” Italian manufacturer Salvagnini told ISMR, highlighting its L3 and L5 fibre laser cutting systems. The company recently launched its AVS system (artificial vision for laser cutting systems), featuring a high-definition camera installed near the cutting head.

At Blechexpo 2015, it also showcased the latest version of its L5 laser cutting system featuring a new “compass” with advanced kinematics and a cutting head with the Dry Cooling functionality for improved cutting precision, higher levels of reliability and lower consumption.

German manufacturer TRUMPF has extended fibre laser’s suitability for thin sheet cutting to thicker sheet processing. From mild steel to stainless steel through to highly reflective materials such as copper and aluminium; from 1mm to 25mm sheet thickness – all components can be produced on one single laser machine. Its TruLaser 5030 fiber with ‘BrightLine fiber’ function enables manufacturers to cut thin sheet with exactly the same attributes for thick sheet.

“ ‘Brightline fiber’ turns the solid-state laser into a truly universal tool since it can process a broad range of sheet thicknesses and materials flexibly and at high quality. This option also makes for more stable processes, even smaller contours and significantly simplified removal of the finished parts. Now with 8kw TruDisk solid-state laser, the TruLaser 5030 fiber cuts stainless steel of up to 40mm thick, mild steel and aluminium of up to 25mm as well as copper and brass up to 10mm,” said TRUMPF.

TRUMPF’s TruLaser 2030 fiber is the latest addition to the TruLaser fibre portfolio. Combining the advantages of a compact 1030 fiber and the performance and power of a 3030 fiber machine, it features a 3- or 4kW TruDisk solid-state laser, cuts stainless steel and mild steel up to 20mm, aluminium up to 16mm plus copper and brass up to 8mm thick.

The second generation of the TruLaser Cell 8030 is for laser cutting hot-formed, 3D designs and press-hardened parts.

Yamazaki Mazak’s Optiflex Fiber range utilizes high dynamics configuration and a two-pallet changer design with a helical rack and pinion positioning system for high cutting speeds and high throughput. Intelligent multifunction torch and nozzle changer technology directly increases productivity by allowing the machine to optimise the torch set-up automatically per program. ■
