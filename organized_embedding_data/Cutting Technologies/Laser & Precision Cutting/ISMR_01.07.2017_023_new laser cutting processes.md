# New laser cutting processes

[TARİH: 01.08.2017 ISMR]

HIGHER CUTTING SPEED = higher laser power. This formula has been more than just a rule of thumb. But TRUMPF engineers have now severed the correlation between speed and kilowatts. The fruits of their labours? The Highspeed and Highspeed Eco cutting processes. A newly designed nozzle boosts the feed rate by up to 100 per cent for solid-state laser machines that employ fusion cutting with nitrogen.

Laser power does not need to be increased. Not only is the feedrate now faster, but so is the piercing process. These new cutting processes also enable almost a twofold increase in sheet throughput compared to standard cutting. What's more, less cutting gas is used because of the nozzle's innovative design. The Highspeed process requires 40 per cent less nitrogen on average (the Highspeed Eco requires 70 per cent less nitrogen). These new processes mean that we have achieved another milestone on the road to lower part costs, TRUMPF told /SMR

In fusion cutting, gas under relatively high pressure blows molten material out of the kerf; this entails high operating costs. Flame cutting using oxygen has generally been used for mild steel, especially for relatively thick sheets. The advantage of low gas costs is offset by oxidised cut edges, which often need to be reworked. The new Highspeed and Highspeed Eco processes, in contrast, are faster and use less gas, which is designed to increase the cost-efficiency of fusion cutting mild steel with nitrogen. The scope of application is also now broader for eight-kilowatt lasers used in fusion cutting. The laser can now cut sheets as thick as twelve millimetres – instead of just ten millimetres, as in the past.

The Highspeed process features a bi-flow nozzle. Some of the cutting gas passes through the centre of this nozzle, as does the laser beam. The rest forms a secondary flow around the principal flow to concentrate it onto the kerf, expelling molten material more efficiently.

A newly designed nozzle reduces nitrogen consumption by 70 per cent on average in the Highspeed Eco process. As with the Highspeed process, feed rates and sheet throughput increase by as much as 100 per cent.

The Highspeed cutting process can nearly double sheet throughput compared to standard cutting. A bi-flow nozzle reduces gas consumption by 40 per cent on average. The Highspeed Eco cutting process relies on a patented nozzle that is fitted with a sleeve.

The Highspeed Eco process relies on a patented nozzle fitted with a sleeve that forces the gas directly into the kerf, ensuring that little or no gas flows off to the side. While this moving sleeve glides across the material during fusion cutting, the nozzle remains 1.5 millimetres from the sheet surface. According to TRUMPF, this ensures that the nozzle can withstand any chips generated during piercing, which accelerates piercing and minimises the risk of damage.

Highspeed and Highspeed Eco can be used for fusion cutting of mild steel and stainless steel sheets of at least four millimetres thick. They can now be used on machines in the TruLaser Series 5000 equipped with an eight-kilowatt solid-state laser. Soon it will be available for use with six-kilowatt solid-state lasers. The Highspeed process is featured on machines in the TruLaser Series 3000 fitted with a six-kilowatt solid-state laser. Many relatively new machines can be retrofitted with these processes.
