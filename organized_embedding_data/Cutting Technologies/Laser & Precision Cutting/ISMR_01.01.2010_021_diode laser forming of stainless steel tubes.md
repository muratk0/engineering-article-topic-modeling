In this study, complex tube bending operations were carried out by laser forming. Bending of slotted tubes and the enlargement of one tube end is discussed. Experimental tests were also performed on AISI 304 stainless steel tubes with a thickness of 1mm and outer diameter of 20mm. A high power diode laser (HPDL) was used.

To design the new processes, a finite element (FE) model was developed which allowed tube deformation under laser processing. The numerical model was experimentally validated and used to study the physical phenomena during the forming process. The thermo-mechanical problem was decoupled and solved sequentially in two phases: a transient thermal analysis to obtain temperature distribution that was input as body load for the static analysis. ## INTRODUCTION

Laser forming is a non-contact technique for sheet metal and tube forming: bending is obtained because of the difference between the rapid heating rate (under laser exposure) and the subsequent slow cooling rate after the laser removal. In the last 15 years, laser forming has been investigated more closely due to the varied interesting applications in manufacturing.

Laser sources have many advantages in terms of compactness, energy efficiency, life time and running cost. In 2000, Li discussed the application of high-power diode lasers in material processing such as soldering, surface treatment, welding, marking and sheet metal bending. Among these applications, laser forming is a valid technique due to the high degree of control over the energy transfer, its accuracy and reliability and the very high flexibility and non-contact nature of the process. In particular, laser bending of thin sheet is achieved by plastic deformation induced by thermal stresses, which results from rapid material heating and cooling. Due to the noncontact nature of the process, hard tooling is not necessary and small batch production runs and rapid prototyping are very effective applications.

Several scientific studies deal with laser bending of tubes and related physical mechanisms. The current study deals with an innovation in laser tube bending in terms of complexity of the shaped tubes. Slotted tubes were considered as well as circumferential scanning paths. A numerical model was also defined to study the physical mechanisms at the basis of the proposed results and to design innovative applications.

A stainless steel tube with a thin cross section was used for the experiment and laser power was kept at 150 W.

## NUMERICAL MODELLING

A finite element model (FEM) was defined to investigate the tube forming under different process conditions. The model was built in ANSYS 9 using the parametric design language (APDL) to reduce modelling and solution times.

## Fig. 1. FEM model for laser forming

To calibrate the numerical procedure, only one tube configuration was involved, whereas in the following experimental section three different configurations are discussed. In particular, an AISI 304 tube with a complex slot was modelled (Figure 1). The tube was 50mm in length, 20mm in outer diameter and 1mm in wall thickness. The Ushaped slot was 15mm long and 10mm wide. The tube geometry was divided into three parts. The internal part, where the spot acted, was mapped meshed with a fine mesh. The two external parts were also mapped meshed but with a coarse mesh. There were three element divisions in each part of the tube along the tube thickness.

The laser heating was modelled using a thermal load as a heat flux over a 3.8 x 1.2mm2 rectangular spot as shown in Figure 1. 150 W was used for the laser beam. After the calibration procedure, the absorption coefficient was set to 0.5 and the heat transfer coefficient was 21 W/(m2K).
