In an industry where higher efficiency and productivity at reduced costs are crucial, fibre laser cutting systems must raise the bar with benefits such as higher powers, peak-level motion dynamics and material handling automation.

Eagle Lasers is a well-known laser cutting machine specialist. It was one of the first to launch 6kW, 8kW, 10kW, 12kW and 20kW laser cutting systems and, in mid-2021, premiered its 30kW fiber laser cutter.

However, it told /SMR, laser power alone is not enough. Motion dynamics, and more specifically acceleration parameters, also have to rise to the occasion.

"If you double the acceleration, you can technically halve the time it takes to reach the programmed speed. The rate at which the machine can decelerate into corners or arcs, and then accelerate out of them, has a high impact on cycle time. The shorter the cycle, the more parts produced per hour and the higher the profit," the manufacturer explained.

Eagle iNspire machines are designed for accurate processing and deliver 6G acceleration, cutting speeds of up to 150m/minute and a 350m/minute positioning speed.

## The crucial role of automation

Camillo Brena, Eagle's marketing director, highlighted its focus on a seamless, efficient process.

"During the machine development process, we understood that true efficiency can only be attained when every step of the manufacturing process cycle is automated and controlled. Transporting the metal sheet from storage to machine, loading and unloading material, as well as sorting and storing cut parts, are processes that need to work seamlessly. isolated action, but the swiftness of the entire process and fully automated systems clearly contribute to this. They enhance productivity by reducing costs and downtime while increasing worker safety, precision, speed and control over the whole operation,"

he explained.

A pivotal point is that a powerful fibre laser cutter is extremely fast and requires material handling solutions that can keep pace with such rapid cutting speed.

"Swift material handling is crucial in achieving high production volumes over shorter work cycles. Eagle's nine-second pallet exchange is critical in this process. However, automated loading/unloading and storage systems are equally important, Brena added.

## Space-saving solutions

Every manufacturer knows the value of their production floor area. With this in mind, Eagle Laser's automation solutions have been intelligently designed to make the most of vertical space.

"Fibre laser machines are significantly more compact and efficient than earlier technology cutters, where a single highpowered laser cutter can do the work of three to five machines with older technologies. If, on top of this, we add vertical systems for material handling, loading and storage (which are also modular and adaptable), we considerably increase productivity per square metre," Eagle Lasers told /SMR.

Brena detailed Eagle's automated material handling solutions.

"The CraneMaster simultaneously loads and unloads material while the eTower storage systems can be equipped with various numbers of towers and shelves, optimising space and stock control to suit each client's needs. These automated solutions are prepared for full integration with the manufacturer's ERP systems and work in sync. to constantly feed the machine while unloading and storing cut parts for smooth, uninterrupted work cycles," he outlined.

## Higher power benefits

Using material handling automation, a high-powered machine can perform at full cutting capacity and process larger volumes in less time. This has an enormous impact on overall productivity and efficiency. This is not just because it reduces downtime but, by cutting more parts in less time, it makes more efficient use of energy, gas and all consumables, resulting in reduced operational expenses and a lower cost per part. If the goal is continuous production, these solutions can line up cutting programs to keep the machine up and running unsupervised, even during night shifts or weekends.

The benefits of automation, however, do not end with material handling. Automated laser cutting machine systems boast extensive data processing and analysis capabilities.

"Built-in machine sensors transfer data which, through reporting systems like Eagle's MyeRS, is translated into dynamic reports on key indicators such as technician performance rates, energy and gas consumption, and program execution. These real-time reports are available anywhere in the world through cloud-based technology, enabling better control, speedier decisionmaking and the development of best practices to keep efficiency at peak levels 24/7,' concluded Camillo.

"When motion dynamics, automated material handling and remote monitoring systems work hand-in-hand, higher laser powers will definitely bring higher profits through unprecedented productivity levels," he added.■
