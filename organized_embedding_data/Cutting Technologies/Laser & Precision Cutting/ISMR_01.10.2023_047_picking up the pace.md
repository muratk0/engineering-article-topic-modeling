Mazak machining and laser cutting systems, with Jean Perrot bending technology, make light work of heavy weights at Watson Gym Equipment in the UK.

The moment he made his decision to buy his first laser processing machine: "I used to have an office that overlooked the workshop, and I could see all our welders spending most of their time cutting steel and drilling holes, rather than welding. I knew then that we needed to get our skilled people doing what they are paid to do," he said.

Simon is the owner and director of Watson Gym Equipment, a premium supplier of exercise machines, dumbbells, barbells and weight benches. The UK company, which was set up in rural Somerset in 1999, has evolved to become one of the most well-known brands in the personal fitness industry.

ANIMAL dual-stack chest press manufactured by Watson Gym Equipment.

Today, it supplies standard and customised gym equipment to high-end customers, commercial gyms, smaller personal gyms and celebrities. Around 78 people work for Watson Gym Equipment. Its two main factories in Frome (near Bath) are joined by a bridge and cover a total area of 40,000 square feet. It also has a separate upholstering factory for its gym products.

## Moving machining in-house

"I've always had a twin passion for both fitness and engineering," explained Simon. "I was originally a welder by trade, so I started making some quite simple gym equipment in my spare time. All I had was an abrasive wheel, cut-off saw, a grinder, my welder and a sewing machine for doing the upholstery. I would take the finished equipment to local gyms and offer them the equipment for free for a week with an option to buy."

While the first few years of operation were challenging, Simon soon identified a gap in the market for producing high-end specialist equipment, made bespoke to a customer's requirements. This proved to be a pivotal turning point. As the company evolved, Simon (who still designs all the products himself) began to introduce more product lines into the Watson Gym portfolio. However, this approach was not without its own growing pains.

Simon continued: "Our biggest challenge is the sheer diversity of our portfolio. We manufacture over 250 different products, many of which are available in different variations. To cater for this high-mix, lowvolume approach, we initially subcontracted a lot of the machining to third parties. However, retaining control of our inventory became increasingly difficult so we decided to bring all the machining in-house."

The company's first machine tool was a Mazak 250 QUICKTURN NEXUS 250-II MS turning centre, followed by a Mazak VCN-530C vertical machining centre, purchased in 2010, which was used to machine weight stack plates and stems. It then invested in another QUICK TURN centre. However, it was not long before the VCN-530C was operating at maximum capacity, so Simon Watson invested in a further Mazak lathe with a larger second spindle.
