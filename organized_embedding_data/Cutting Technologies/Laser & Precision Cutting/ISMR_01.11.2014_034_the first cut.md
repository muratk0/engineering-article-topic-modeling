# THE FIRST CUT

[TARİH: 01.11.2014 ISMR]

takes a tour around HIGHYAG’s booth to see its latest laser innovations

IGHYAG Lasertechnologie GmbH displayed its range of laser processing heads at EuroBLECH 2014. A subsidiary of II-VI Incorporated, it is a leading supplier in the laser material processing industry. Its laser processing heads and fibre beam delivery systems are used for laser cutting and welding as well as for brazing in the advanced manufacturing industry.

One of the highlights at its EuroBLECH booth was its BIMO-FSC laser cutting head. This processing head can adjust focus position and focus diameter automatically using the CNC control unit. This enables the cutting of different sheet thicknesses with no manual intervention for high productivity levels. HIGHYAG also showcased its current laser processing heads for welding and brazing, such as the RLSK remote laser welding head. Using scanner technology, the RLSK can freely position the focus in a three-dimensional workspace and offers the possibility of ‘welding-on-the-fly‘.

## A clear focus

“In general, EuroBLECH is one of our most important exhibitions. It clearly addresses our major markets, metal processing (core market for us), and we are showing laser cutting, laser welding and laser brazing products for these markets here,” explained Dr. Robert Kuba, Managing Director, HIGHYAG. “For example our laser cutting head, BIMO-FSC, gives our customers a wide choice of cutting opportunities. The product starts at 2kW and above for processing thin and thick materials (up to 25mm) and is designed to provide a high quality cutting process. Quality, uptime and robustness of the product, for high productivity levels, are important considerations for our customers.”

The company has made several upgrades to its products and improvements to product quality e.g. higher precision and higher automation degrees. The cutting process itself is very delicate – a two-step process of piercing the material and then actual cutting – this requires complex changes in laser beam diameter and focus position. HIGHYAG has simplified these processes with a product design which enables user-friendly operation and higher productivity.

“Not just for cutting, but also for welding, productivity and quality are major, continuous trends in the market," confirmed Dr. Kuba. “It is about controlling the quality of the cut/weld seam and laser beam control is part of this."

ISMR viewed an interesting remote welding demonstration on HIGHYAG’s booth, showcasing a typical automotive application for laser welding doors. Using complex optics, with the laser beam controlled by scanner mirrors, the welding beam moves to the correct positioning.

“There are not as many players in the remote laser welding field and competition is limited,” explained Dr. Kuba. "It is a very high productivity application as beam positioning is constant during the robot's movement. Therefore, time between weld seams can be minimised and laser utilisation increased. Maximum productivity can therefore be achieved – far in excess of 'conventional' laser welding. Remote welding is a great niche – software control and optics expertise are important here. There is a large customer base of Tier 1 manufacturers worldwide using our systems in this field.”

## Preparing for growth

“In general, this is a growth industry,” confirmed Dr. Kuba. “We are prepared for the next step in growth by our move in January into a new building, with a new footprint, new cleanrooms etc. Our next step is growth in volume and further product development so we have hired new people in R&D and production. We see synergies in other technologies for our product development activity.

“We now have a much larger team talking to customers every day to glean their requirements and develop our products accordingly. So, the next 12 months for us will be full of major product development activities so that we can grow our production capabilities and launch new products.

“We have seen significant double-digit growth this year. Growing above the market average is always a challenge but we see sound opportunities."

## Heinrich Weber, Dreistern’s director of sales, discusses its multifunctional roll forming breakthroughs at this year’s EuroBLECH

Dreistern’s multifunctional roll forming technology, showcased at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, offers a new way to meet changing customer requirements. A multifunctional machine can be configured within a few minutes for completely different parts with different process requirements by placing specialised work modules, like a flying punch or a laser welding module, onto the roll forming machine just like an ordinary roll forming tool. Every work module has its own mobile control which can freely be moved. This enables the exchange of work modules even between different production lines. Different lines can share work modules which cannot be fully utilised by one line.

“The benefits of multifunctional roll forming include 30% to 50% reduced foot print, 10% to 30% reduced investment cost for complex parts and expandable from an ordinary rollformer to a highly flexible sheet metal production cell at any time,” explained Heinrich Weber, Director of Sales, Dreistern, to ISMR at its booth at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition.

“At EuroBLECH, our focus is on multifunctional roll forming. We are running a machine here that highlights the benefits of the process to customers. It demonstrates a part designed for pre-punching which originally gave us the idea for multifunctional roll forming – in the past, we had to spend a lot of time and money modifying a machine for an intermediate punching operation. Now it takes just ten minutes to move the punch from one end of the machine to the other, so we can quickly change the position of the punch.

Heinrich Weber, Director of Sales, Dreistern

Multifunctional roll forming is already starting to become a success in the German market – we have sold two machines to customers in Germany. We have also sold our first multifunctional roll forming machine to China.”

“2015 has been a successful year for Dreistern in regions like China, USA and Mexico. China is a very important market region for us, especially the automotive market there and the requirement for steel automotive parts such as bumpers. We are constantly developing new technologies for these types of applications. For example, we just demonstrated a new CNC bending process for producing bumpers (in-line bending of variable radii within the roll former with high precision tolerances and quality).”

A multifunctional machine can be configured within a few minutes for completely different parts with different process requirements

In most cases today, a profile or a tube is a semi-manufactured product which has to undergo further processing until it becomes the final product. Roll forming itself is only one part of a comprehensive process chain. The concept of multifunctional roll forming is based on an integration of down-stream processes into the machine. Both part manufacturer and his client can benefit from this development. A reduction of the number of processes in a process chain always means cost reduction. The capability to supply value-added tubes opens up new market opportunities for manufacturers of roll formed products. Multifunctional roll forming means integrating additional processes like piercing, notching, embossing, fixing nuts or bolts and many other processes into roll forming machines and tube mills.

## An eye on innovation

The Dreistern Innovation Forum concluded in October this year, with the next event scheduled for next year. It is open for anyone to attend and includes presentations from different companies, including Dreistern, on the latest innovations in the market connected to roll forming.

“This year’s Forum was focused on multifunctional rollforming with demonstrations on how to change the punch on one machine in ten minutes and also change it from one machine to another. This can save a lot of investment on parts as you can share modules between machines (ie lasers, punches etc.). They are completely interchangeable,” added Heinrich Weber.

“Our standard machine design is now multifunctional, which means that every Dreistern machine can now integrate multifunctional work modules at a later stage. This is tremendous value-add for our customers if they want to add an additional process, such as punching or welding, at a later stage to their manufacturing operations. We believe that our roll forming machines will therefore become universal sheet metal processing centres with the addition of these extra processing modules.”

Dreistern is also continuing to innovate, with a new control system under development for Dreistern machines which will make programming easier for operators (who can just enter parameters and adjust the control for specific tasks). The manufacturer is also aiming to shorten delivery lead times for its machines, by pre-fabricating modules in advance.

“We are aiming to shorten our lead times from 10-12 months to 6-7 months for the bigger machines. We are also trying to reduce manufacturing costs – we have managed to reduce costs by 20% to date. At the moment, we are thinking of extending this cost-reducing exercise to our machines. Dreistern is well known for cutting-edge technology," concluded Heinrich Weber.
