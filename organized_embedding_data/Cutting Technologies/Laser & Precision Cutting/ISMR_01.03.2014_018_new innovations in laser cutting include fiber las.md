# New innovations in laser cutting include fiber lasers for thin sheet cutting and new punch/laser combinations

[TARİH: 01.03.2014 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Sheet metal laser cutting has undergone a transformation. The last few years have seen an explosion in new laser model launches, particularly with the advent of the fiber laser. We chronicle some of the latest new global innovations here for you.

## A flexible friend

Metal cutting with laser has a variety of benefits compared to other cutting methods on the market. For instance, higher levels of accuracy when cutting thin sheet; rectangular cutting edges and strong automation possibilities. It can also be used to cut very small holes; narrow webs; acute-angled geometric shapes and very complex contours. It is a process which boasts very low heat input, materials are not warped and are minimally affected by heat during the cut. It also features a very small cutting kerf (0.2mm to 0.4mm) and high cutting speeds can be reached when cutting thin sheet (up to approx. 4mm).

However, investment and operating costs can be higher, when compared to other processes (such as punching), and it may not always be an economical process for small batch sizes. Precise height control between the torch and the work piece is also necessary. Lasers, however, add significantly to manufacturing flexibility for materials with thickness ranges of up to 25mm.

CO2 lasers are suitable as robust all-purpose tools for stainless steel, aluminium and mild steel in all sheet thicknesses. If stainless steel and thick sheet metal are the priority, CO2 lasers are strong tools.

8kW CO2 laser cutting solutions are now offered as alternatives to plasma or waterjet cutting – they are particularly suited to the production of laser-cut blanks. It is much more cost-effective than milling the part from bar material, a process that is very wasteful by comparison. The ability to use thick sheet instead also introduces the potential for nesting; saving material, machining time and therefore cost.

Laser cutting has become an industrially accepted production process mainly because of the flexibility of the technique and the high quality cuts which can be made. Laser systems, from manufacturers such as IRON SpA and Schuler, have also been used to cut from coil. Exciting innovations are now also being made in pulse laser technology and new ‘digital photonic production’ technologies for next generation products.

## An eye on fiber

A recent innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin sheet metal has been the introduction of the fiber laser which can cut not just steel materials but also non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium. Fiber lasers are able to cut these highly reflective and thermally conductive materials that CO2 lasers are not able to process without damaging the optics. Linear drives enable rapid positioning and cutting speeds to be achieved. The use of fibers eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized.

Other advantages of using fiber laser technology include the fact that there is no need for expensive laser gases and there is lower electricity consumption, which is of ecological benefit and results in lower running costs. Crucially, its capabilities have also now been extended to cutting thicker materials.

“The most significant achievement of our TruLaser 5030 Fiber with BrightLine fiber is the edge quality of thicker materials. Although the TruLaser 5030 Fiber with solid-state laser was capable of cutting stainless steel up to 20mm thick and mild steel up to 25mm thick, secondary finishing was sometimes required. This problem has been completely eliminated with the introduction of BrightLine fiber. Edge quality and process reliability are assured. The machine’s cutting capacity for aluminium and stainless steel has also increased to 25mm,” German specialist TRUMPF told ISMR.

Manufacturers such as AMADA; Baykal, BLM Group; TRUMPF; Bystronic; Ermaksan; Kjellberg Finsterwalde; Hypertherm; LVD, Thalmann and Salvagnini have all launched fiber laser cutting systems into the market.

Subcontractors and job shops have quickly found that its extended cutting capabilities have extended their reach into new markets and new customers.

“We are cutting up to 50 per cent quicker when using the Bystronic fiber laser on stainless steel, which is on the machine for about 70 per cent of the time, 24 hours per day. The material is ideal for extracting maximum benefit from fiber lasers, so the machine will pay for itself quickly. For the other 30 per cent of its time, the BySprint laser machine is available for cutting reflective metals that we could not previously quote on. The fiber laser is already bringing in new business of this type, which we had to turn down before,” commented Gareth Taylor of subcontractor WEC, which is now cutting aircraft parts on its fiber laser.

Fiber laser light is created by banks of diodes. The light is channelled and amplified through fiber optic cable similar to that used for data transfer. The amplified light, on exiting the fiber cable, is collimated or straightened and then focused by a lens onto the material to be cut. Not only is creation of the light 200 per cent more efficient than via a traditional CO2 laser, but delivery is also far simpler, with no expensive optical mirrors. The focusing lens is, unlike on a conventional CO2 laser machine, sealed in the cutting head and thus not a consumable item.

“They have several advantages over CO2 laser systems as they require virtually no maintenance, are more energy-efficient and take up less space. The power supply is much smaller than that of a CO2 laser, yet fiber delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables,” explained fiber laser machine manufacturer, Hypertherm. ■
