WHEN IT comes to increasing efficiency in the production of parts or innovative tooling solutions, then Swiss manufacturer Agathon is a contender. At EuroBLECH, the family company will present backlash-free, round fine centering units used for the centering of parting plates/mould halves in tool construction (patent pending). Thanks to the integrated cage-retaining system, the preload can be exerted at every stroke movement.

These centering units are used with great success in mould construction as an alternative to conventional guide blocks, respectively sliding systems. The preloaded backlash-free round fine centering made of bearing steel provides high precision. The mould halves are closed without clearance and unnecessary burr/offset is avoided. Four standard sizes are available from stock. ■

FROM 21-25 OCTOBER 2014 at EuroBLECH, Jenoptik’s Lasers & Material Processing division will present its new laser system, JENOPTIK-VOTAN® BIM (Beam in Motion), for cutting and welding metals. The robot-based laser machine is a highly flexible, rapid solution for the processing of metals. It is primarily used in the automotive industry to cut highly complex 3D car body parts and structural parts as well as hydroformed tubes e.g. for exhaust systems.

“The heart of the JENOPTIK-VOTAN® BIM laser cutting system is a laser robot arm in which the laser beam delivery is fully integrated. The laser beam is coupled in through the robot foot so that the transport fiber does not need to be moved within the working space. In this way, not only are the costs of maintenance and renewal minimised, but the system itself is minimised as well. The small laser cutting head that weighs only 5kg can easily access even the narrowest areas. This is the only way to achieve the high path accuracy and very high level of system dynamics necessary for 3D processing.

“With a repeat accuracy of ±50µm this laser machine ranks among the most precise systems of its class. The axis movements of the Jenoptik laser arm are up to 60 per cent faster than current standards, allowing for a significantly shorter cycle time compared to other robot systems,” the company explained.

One key objective in the continuous further development of the JENOPTIK-VOTAN® BIM is to increase capacity while minimising the size of the machine. The aim is to simplify its integration into production lines or combined processing units such as cutting/welding systems. The components have a modular structure so that they can be flexibly arranged within the space to suit the processing task. As an alternative, they can also be mounted on a platform measuring about 12m² and accommodating the robot, the laser, the switch cabinet and a turntable that supports manoeuvrable sequences and continuous cutting.
