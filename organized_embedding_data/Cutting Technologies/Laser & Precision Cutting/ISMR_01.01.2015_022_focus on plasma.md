ESAB also introduced SGX, a compact, automated cutting machine for plasma and oxyfuel cutting, at EuroBLECH 2014. It carries up to three tool stations, including up to two oxy-fuel torches and one plasma station that can be equipped with ESAB’s m3- IGC Precision Plasma System. Advanced features include the Vision® T5 touch-screen CNC with built-in automatic nesting, as well as automatic gas controls and plasma automation powered by m3 Plasma technology.

Hypertherm®, a manufacturer of advanced cutting systems, expanded its line-up of Powermax air plasma torches in 2014 with the introduction of Duramax Hyamp torches. The new offerings include “long” torches for extended reach cutting and a number of robotic torches.

It also recently announced a major version upgrade to its ProNest® advanced CAD/CAM nesting software for automated cutting. ProNest 2015 contains a number of improvements designed to make the software more efficient and easier to use.

The company’s True Bevel technology for HPRXD plasma systems is a performance application for mild steel that simplifies the bevel-cutting process. Parameter values for several processes are available. Output values for angle compensation, kerf, cut height, cut speed and arc voltage are set which makes set-up for new jobs fast and results accurate. With Rapid Part technology, Hypertherm products integrate seamlessly to significantly increase productivity, without operator intervention.

Kjellberg Finsterwalde is the oldest manufacturer of plasma cutting techniques in the market and is one of the global market leaders in this area today. At the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover last year, its main focus was on the presentation of a new Smart Focus product series for plasma cutting (for material thicknesses of between 0.5mm up to 80mm) and its HiFocus neo plasma cutting technology.

ISMR witnessed live demonstrations of its plasma cutting units, and InFocus welding techniques, during the exhibition. For better gas control, the plasma power sources in the Smart Focus series are equipped with newly developed gas consoles, either manual (PGE 300) or automatic (PGV 300). Improved gas control enables high precision cut results.

High cutting speeds reduce the costs per cutting metre. The new torches, PerCut 2000 and 4000, also contribute to the profitability of the cutting process.

Kjellberg’s patented Contour Cut and Contour Cut Speed technologies will continue to be used to rapidly cut contours, webs and holes. For cutting stainless steel, Kjellberg continues to rely on its Ar/H2 technology, where single gases can be mixed for each specific job.

In September 2014, Victor Technologies announced the release of the VCM-200 portable cutting machine that is CE approved

for the European market. This tractor-type machine offers constant speed (from 2.5 to 150 cm per minute) across a wide range of temperatures. A high speed version is available up to 300cm per minute. A cone disc drive and AC induction motor provide strong cutting results, while dual drive controls and a direct speed readout allow the operator to quickly and easily adjust parameters. An optional adaptor provides compatibility with the Victor® Thermal Dynamics® SL100 plasma torch, supplied with Cutmaster® systems and also supplied as a retrofit torch for many other plasma systems, allowing fabricators to produce high quality cuts on stainless steel and aluminium.

At the EuroBLECH exhibition in Germany, Voortman Steel Machinery developed a solution to bevel without the use of corner loops, which does not increase the waste rate and still creates high quality corners. During the motion of the first bevel, the torch is already turning into a right angle for the next bevel. In a situation that requires two V-bevels, the torch will protrude the plasma arc during the first V-bevel and then drag the plasma arc during the second V-bevel.

Because corner loops are no longer needed, the total cutting length per nesting is shorter which is equivalent to a decrease in costs. Based on test results, waste reductions of 20% are possible with Voortman’s new bevel solution. ■
