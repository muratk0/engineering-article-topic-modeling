# NETWORKED,AUTOMATED ANDTRANSPARENT

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"Bystronic presented the networked world of sheet metal processing at the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart"

Bystronic CEO, Alex Waser, explains where integrated automation and new software solutions for laser cutting and bending will lead.

ISMR: In your view, which are currently the most interesting technology trends in the sheet metal processing sector?

AW: One trend is the integration of automation solutions and digital networking into the user's process steps, in combination with Industry 4.0-driven initiatives. I am thinking specifically of modular material handling solutions for laser cutting i.e. compact systems which adapt to customer requirements, with increasing laser output. For bending, flexible automation systems enable rapid transition between automated and manual manufacturing

Thanks to the possibilities offered by digital networking, all these systems can be integrated into a transparent and optimised manufacturing flow. They are controlled by high-performance software solutions which are simple to use.

ISMR: What impact will these trends have on users?

AW: Digital solutions and automation enable users to master fluctuating order situations using flexible systems. What is more, the manufacturing process will become more transparent. Using software solutions, such as the Bystronic MES, users will be able to create offers more rapidly, plan their production processes in an efficient manner and make the best possible use of their resources.

Live monitoring systems, such as our ByCockpit app, represent an additional building block. They provide users with real-time information about the running processing steps from their production environment. All this will result in an improvement of costs and processes. And this, in turn, is the prerequisite for sustainable competitive success.

ISMR: What role will service play in the sheet metal processing business of tomorrow?

AW: Optimised process solutions still require a reliable partner to provide support with a comprehensive range of services. Service means proximity to the customer and to the customer's requirements and challenges. Even the best processes are no use to customers without the corresponding knowledge of technology experts, fullytrained service technicians and a wellnetworked infrastructure.

We will therefore continue to focus on offering consulting and training in our modern training centres, with the latest equipment. On request, we also carry out training courses onsite at our customers' business locations.

ISMR: To what extend will digitalisation change the range of services in the future?

AW: Our customers can already access digital service solutions. In the future, these services will provide even more far-reaching support for their process steps. They will also expand the performance spectrum of integrated machine systems. Depending upon the configuration, this will result in more versatile or faster processes.

Predictive maintenance will also continue to progress. Digitalisation will allow us to integrate intelligent functions into our range of services. This provides customers with information about the condition of their machines and about the remaining operating life of components which are subject to wear. This allows them to schedule maintenance at an early stage and we can quickly be on-site with the requisite spare parts.

ISMR: What did visitors see on the Bystronic booth at Blechexpo?

AW: Visitors to Bystronic's exhibition booth experienced first-hand what their production environment could look like in the future; networked, automated and transparent.

THE INTELLIGENT TOOL ISMR SAYS: "Wilson Tool launched a range of new tooling ovations at Blechexpo in Stuttgart

ISMR sat down with Klaus Neumann, sales director of Wilson Tool's European operations, for his views on the future of tooling in the age of Industry 4.0.

Wilson Tool provides tool solutions for bending, punching, stamping and tableting customers around the globe. For over 50 years, it has provided customised tooling solutions to help customers resolve challenging sheet metal manufacturing issues. It manufactures a full selection of European and WILA/TRUMPF-Style precision press brake tooling and clamping systems that are designed to increase its customers' productivity and efficiency.

Wilson Tool was at Blechexpo in Stuttgart this November with a complete range of punching and bending tools for all major brands of punch press and press brake, along with the European launch of its new range of press brake clamping systems. ISMR sat down with Klaus Neumann, sales director of Wilson Tool's European operations, for his views on the future of tooling in the age of Industry 4.0.

ISMR: What is the focus on your booth at Blechexpo in Stuttgart this November? Which products are you highlighting at the exhibition?

KN: I am delighted to confirm that this show is the European launchpad for our i-Series punch press tooling – we are also launching it at the FABTECH show in Chicago (USA). The tool is marked with a unique ID number so that a fully automated punch press can choose the appropriate tooling and functionality for a job simply by reading the QR code on the tool (which gives full information on the tool, including when it is time to order a new tool).

This new line of thick turret tooling is designed to be fully compatible with the Amada ID Tooling System (AITS™) tool management system. AMADA originally patented the tool, but the patent period is over now, so we are now launching this tool and have successfully managed to bring delivery times down. This tool plays an important part in the march towards 'intelligent tooling', a small but necessary part of Industry 4.0.

Laser-marked with a data matrix code on each compatible tool, the new Series tooling line brings sheet metal fabricators more thick turret tooling choices for their AITS machines.

There are a lot of other companies working on tooling ID systems, which are the first step into Industry 4.0. Some people use an RFID chip in the tool, but this would not really work in the punching environment because of the shocks sustained on the tool.

Another step in our tooling ID portfolio is the Smart X CAP 4.0. Extra is our product line of accessories for sheet metal companies. We are showing the prototype here (which is still in development) of our intelligent tool cabinet so that a customer can see what kind of tooling he is using. He can see where the individual tool is located or how much he has used it, as well as on which machine it is mounted (perfect for controlling his inventory, with the click of a mouse). It can even generate an automatic email to advise whether new or replacement tooling is needed. You can scan in tools or type in their numbers manually. It can also connect to existing MRP or SAP systems, using open source software. I believe this system is unique in the market and is, again, another step towards intelligent tooling. We are looking for further opportunities with automation and Industry 4.0.

Another new product launch for us is our QuickTap™ tapping tool. Tapping is a common application that is best done in the punch press to prevent time-consuming secondary operations. The QuickTap™ can tap up to 200 threaded holes per minute. We are showing it here for the first time.

ISMR: What is the next step for intelligent tooling?

KN: That's an interesting question. We work with a lot of OEMs and regularly ask them what they need to make their machine tools 'bullet-proof' in terms of Industry 4.0. I think automation is a key focus, as are rapid toolchanging times.

We have to make it easier for the customer to use our tools. So, we try to develop tools where you need no tooling to assemble or disassemble the tools – the operator can do it by hand, for example.

We also supply bending tooling for press brakes. We are growing this market steadily and developed a new hydraulic clamping system, and introduced a new hydraulic crowning system, about three months ago to the European market.

ISMR: Are there any particular successes or achievements that you would like to highlight?

KN: We have seen double-digit growth this year in punch tooling – there is, of course, some organic growth but also a lot of new customers buying punching machines. Large numbers of punch presses are still being sold – they are strong workhorses for sheet metal shops and offer great flexibility. There is still, in our view, a strong future for punching. If you can dream about it and draw it, we can probably punch it.

Bending takes up about 25-30% of our business now – the growth is there. This year, we have sold more bending tooling. We are 'new kids on the block' in this area but are making decent numbers now in this market.

We have increased growth in every European market this year, compared to last year. Even last year, we saw fantastic growth levels from the middle to the end of 2016. That growth has carried on into this year. We have also seen an improvement in the Russian market since Summer.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

KN: We see good growth this year for punch press tooling, despite the prevalence of lasercutting systems. We believe that this is because it is easier to integrate automation onto punching machines, rather than on laser systems. It is easier, for example, to control the goods coming off the punch etc. Punches are also less expensive than laser and can offer forming operations on the sheet, unlike laser. Automation is also becoming more important now.

I think everyone is positive – our customers are certainly very busy. We are also optimistic for next year, especially for the first six months of 2018, in all European markets.
