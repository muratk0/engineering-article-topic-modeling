Drop-probe device protects welder operators’ fingers from injuries

The Rockford Systems RKC-500 Detect-A-Finger drop-probe device prevents serious injuries to welder operators by halting welding machinery from starting its stroke if a finger or hand is detected in the point-of-operation area. To block welding splash, additional lightweight, clear plastic shielding can also be attached to the device’s rod.

The key components of the device are a sensing probe module with a rod shaped to fit over or around the workpiece and a control module. When the operator initiates a stroke, the probe is released and drops by gravity. If it detects anything more than the material, it averts cycling. However, if an operator’s fingers or hands have not entered the point-of-operation area, the probe will drop into its preset position, and the control unit will allow the welder to operate.

Push/pull GMAW reduces risk of deforming aluminum wires

ESAB brand Tweco has launched the PullMaster2 push/pull GMAW pistolgrip gun. Its angled cable-to-gun connection and ball-and-socket strain relief help enhance balance and reduce perceived cable weight. The short-stroke trigger provides easy activation, while the aluminum unibody reduces gun weight.

Because improper drive roll tension deforms soft aluminum wires and leads to feeding problems, the gun features an internal preset drive roll tension mechanism that reduces the risk of deforming the wire while helping to ensure positive feeding performance. The drive rolls are electrically insulated to prevent microarcing, a common source of performance problems.

The gun feeds wire diameters from 0.030 to 0.052 in., has a rated duty cycle of 60% using mixed gas, is available with 20- or 33-ft. cable length, and uses standard Tweco 16APS standard contact tips.

Coil feed lines address needs of tier suppliers

Automatic Feed Co. has developed the new Pro series coil processing lines. These new press feed lines are aimed at tier automotive suppliers that process UHSS, AHSS, and aluminum for applications such as inner body panels and stamped parts, which are not surface-sensitive or surface-critical. Each line comprises a single reel and coil cart, peeler, straightener, threading table, and press feed.

The systems are available in three sizes (Pro 40, Pro 60, and Pro 80), and each can process coil stock from 4 to 40, 60, or 80 in., depending on the model. All three sizes can handle material thicknesses from 0.02 to 0.25 in. at up to 160 FPM. They can process aluminum with yield strengths up to 350 MPa, and up to 1,200 MPa for steel.

Fiber laser delivers high-precision processing of thin-to-thick materials

The ENSIS 3015 AJ series from Amada provides continuous processing of thin materials and thick plate without a cutting lens change or manual setup. This is made possible by a resonator that automatically changes the beam mode to accommodate whatever material and thickness is being processed.

An advanced collimation system controls beam diameter and beam configuration, allowing for infinite mode and diameter control combinations. According to the company, the machine achieves up to a 66% reduction in processing time when compared to conventional fiber lasers at the same wattage and achieves 1-sec. clean pierce in 1-in. plate.

The system is suitable for ferrous steels, as well as nonferrous metals such as stainless steel, aluminum, copper, brass, and titanium.
