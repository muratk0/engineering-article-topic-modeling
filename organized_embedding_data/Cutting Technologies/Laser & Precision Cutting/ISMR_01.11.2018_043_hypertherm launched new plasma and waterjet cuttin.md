# Hypertherm launched new plasma and waterjet cutting solutions at EuroBLECH 2018

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Hypertherm, a manufacturer of cutting systems and software, introduced two new systems at the EuroBLECH 2018 international sheet metal exhibition in Hanover this October. The XPR170 plasma cutting system, which joins the XPR300 as the only two X-Definition™ capable systems in the world, and what the company dubs 'the industry's first predictive maintenance waterjet pumps', HyPrecision Predictive. These new pumps are designed to substantially reduce maintenance costs and disruption caused by non-planned service.

Hypertherm designs and manufactures industrial cutting products for a variety of industries such as shipbuilding, manufacturing and automotive repair. Its product line includes cutting systems, in addition to CNC motion and height controls, CAM nesting software, robotic software and consumables. The company's reputation for cutting innovation dates back 50 years to 1968, with its invention of water injection plasma cutting. The 100 percent associate-owned company has more than 1,400 associates along with operations and partner representation worldwide.

ISMR sat down with Evan A Smith, CEO, and Marco Mostert, Regional Director Europe, for Hypertherm at EuroBLECH to discover how they see the future of sheet metal cutting.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes or achievements?

Evan Smith (ES): We are celebrating our 50th anniversary as a company and are also celebrating five years of being 100% employee-owned. Hypertherm takes social responsibility very seriously. We offer all our associates four paid days per year for social volunteering. After 15 years of this programme, we have now donated over 150,000 hours of paid time into volunteer communities. Most associates volunteer as teams. We were seventh on the Forbes list as a great place to work. I think that our customers feel the outcome of that in the quality of interactions they have with Hypertherm associates.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

ES: We tend to track industrial production — 2017 was a strong recovery year, globally. 2018 has continued in that vein – we expect double-digit growth this year. There is some growing uncertainty for next year, mostly related to global trade/tariff unrest, geopolitical risk and growing currency volatility. Our customers are a little cautious going into 2019 but we still see continued investment, where it makes sense. The borrowing environment for investment is still good.

We feel that our customers will be more discriminating in 2019 and we think we are very well positioned with our new technologies to improve their Return on Investment on cutting operations.

ISMR: Please take us through your new products here at EuroBLECH in Hanover.

Marco Mostert (MM): The theme this year for us at the booth is 'Shaping Possibility', highlighting our new technologies. Here on the booth, you can see our new generation waterjet product, the HyPrecision Predictive. We launched this product globally here at EuroBLECH.

HyPrecision Predictive waterjets are equipped with features such as Advanced Intensifier Technology and new patented technologies that adjust for pressure and temperature, oil viscosity and hydraulic system wear parts, along with technology that enables customers to use seals up to 40 per cent longer. The pumps are designed with ease of service in mind. Features such as a colour-coded junction box, electrical cable harnesses, quick-disconnect fittings and an easy access bleed-down valve all make maintenance faster and less expensive. Safety is also considered. A clear window cover lets the operator see the intensifier and attenuator. Optional electrical interlocks prevent unauthorised access and can automatically stop the pump when opened. Finally, Seal Maintenance Technology™ helps to keep the top deck and shop floor free of oil and water.

Our new XPR170 170-amp plasma cutting system joins the XPR300 as the only two X-Definition-capable systems in the world. It can cut mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium. Laboratory testing shows ISO-9013 Range 2 cut quality on thin mild steel and extended ISO Range 3 cuts on thicker metals.

Hypertherm has traditionally been well known for mild steel cutting and, with these new systems, we have stepped up our non-ferrous cutting capability. With our new XPR plasma cutting systems, we can offer near laser-type cutting qualities. The fundamental R&D that we undertook on this (20% fewer components used in the machine after a fundamental re-design), took five years and we are now a major player in this field.

All our machine builder partners have been working hard to integrate the new XPR platform. It is a fundamentally different product with a lot of new codes. With it, we have moved from analogue to digital – all our devices are now IoT-and cloud-ready with Industry 4.0 in mind. This helps our customers to bring down the cost of their operations and cut for longer at the same quality. It's a huge step forward.

ES: The first member (the 300) of the new XPR family came out one and a half years ago and it revamped all the key dimensions of plasma performance, such as a significant increase in cutting speed per amp, another 50% reduction in operating cost and a key upgrade in non-ferrous material cut quality. But it required all our machine builder partners to revisit their control integration, and their drive/motor capabilities. With the introduction of the XPR170, all of that work has now been done and partners can immediately reap the benefits.

We adopted a modular product design approach so that all subsequent models that we produce will leverage that same investment by our partners. Feedback here at EuroBLECH has been excellent, with 30 or more partners displaying and demonstrating our product.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

MM: From a technical perspective, in the next couple of years, we will expand these new technologies and the XPR family. We want a deeper understanding of what is happening with end-users. We created a group of subject matter experts to visit key account end-users to gain a better understanding of the value stream map, to find out what is important for them and to unlock opportunities. We have brought that learning to our channel partners. The speed of change is getting faster and we need to be ahead of the curve and get the best customer insight and ideas to help us develop products that can help them.

Another new initiative is to ask 2000 end-users what they like about the XPR family. We also asked them about the service they get from us to help us become the best partner to an end-user. We also need focus on service provision, with our channel partners. It is a journey we will continue for the next couple of years.

Our objectives are to improve our service levels, to consult directly with customers to discover their requirements on the ground and to continue informing our R&D efforts and future technical developments. Educating our customers is also a key strategic objective.

ES: There are four high-level corporate areas where we are focusing and investing. 1) The waterjet investment. 2) End-user experience (ensuring we are driving plasma adoption by a deeper understanding of top application opportunities, working directly with strategic end-users to develop those application spaces and then working with our partners to execute on those. We also want to make sure that every customer has a high level of service – the service experience really drives technology adoption). 3) Recognising software as a key application solutions driver, so we are heavily investing in this. 4) For all our products, including the product launches this year, the key theme is 'ease of use'. We are really simplifying and error-proofing products, making it easier for operators and making the products digital and self-optimising.

We are also looking at Virtual Reality (VR), Augmented Reality (AR) and Artificial Intelligence (AI) as an advanced technology initiative, although they are still nascent technologies, trying to find a niche and platform. AI is very suitable for ease of use, augmenting human capability (imagine servicing a machine and being able to visualise what you are looking at and which order to check items with VR-type aids). We also believe in safety, using advanced sensors to allow operators to quickly diagnose unsafe practices in the working environment. There are many dimensions of AI and machine learning that we are working on, but it is at an early stage. The key question is how you can simplify systems and make things easier for operators.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in plasma cutting markets?

ES: There has been a significant trend in recent years towards fibre laser cutting. We now find that the pendulum is swinging back a little. Now that fibre laser cutting technology is fairly well established and better understood, customers are beginning to ask questions such as 'What do I really need?' and 'Which technologies can I best apply to my applications?'.

Plasma cutting in the new 170-amp range is really targeted at 1-20mm or even 15mm cutting, which has a heavy overlap with laser cutting applications. We are able to cut in ISO Range 2 in plasma, which is the same tolerance as laser cutting, but we cannot produce the same very fine features (e.g. very small holes and very intricate patterns) although we can reproduce the total dimensional tolerance capability. The entry investment cost is much lower with plasma and, in some cases, material range diversity is greater with plasma (certainly with different quality grades of materials). That is a key trend for those customers with lower volume cutting and higher mix requirements. Many customers are finding their way back, in those applications, to plasma cutting.

We entered the waterjet space five years ago, through acquisition, as it is a faster growing technology than either laser or plasma cutting, because of the increased diversity of new materials (carbon fibre, advanced plastics, rubber, glass etc.). It is a very versatile technology and introduces no heat to the cutting process.

One of our early discoveries was that waterjet maintenance and repair can be unpredictable and costly. We took a fresh look at that - we found that sometimes customers operated their machines with side panels removed so that they can watch for leaks. So, with our new pump, we developed leak detection and predictive maintenance (the system itself can tell you when it is beginning to degrade and allow you to plan your maintenance). Avoiding costly downtime is key. We believe this is a major breakthrough and are investing very heavily in that part of the business, both through internal and planned acquisition growth.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.

## An eye on manufacturing

“Metal cutting machine tools find wide use in almost all manufacturing industries that are flourishing in the region. Recent expansion in manufacturing and industrial production, increasing efficiency parameters and the quest to innovate are also key factors that fuel a hike in demand for tools that cut and shape rigid materials and make machines,” the event organiser told ISMR.

"The economic diversification push has given an added impetus for the metalworking industry to acquire the latest tools for both metalcutting and metalforming, including automation and robotics, tooling systems, CAD/CAM and other technologies essential for manufacturing."

Metal cutting machine tools has become a key segment of SteelFab. Steel fabricators are also making the change from labour-intensive material handling processes to automated material handling processes. The Middle East fabricator faces a gamut of difficulties involving labour-intensive processes. Whilst the cost of labour is going up, associated costs such as government fees, visa costs, accommodation costs and retention costs are on the rise. Added to that are the costs of training and replacement.

Over time, the steel fabrication and overall manufacturing industry has seen a trend towards automating its material handling processes whether through cranes, magnetic lifting, positioners or robotic solutions.

"SteelFab has reflected this change in the industry here," added Mr. Saif Al Midfa, CEO, Expo Centre Sharjah. "We have noticed this change on our show floor, where the exhibits have evolved over the years. We now have more representation in material handling robots, overhead cranes, magnetic lifting and vacuum lifting," he added.

## THE PARTNERSHIP PRINCIPLE ISMR SAYS: “Bystronic showcased its Smart Factory vision at EuroBLECH 2018'

Johan Elster outlines the path that Bystronic is taking to move from a single machine manufacturer to a solutions provider for machines, services and data flow.

of solutions for the sheet metal processing business. Its focus lies on the automation of the complete material and data flow of the cutting and bending process chain. Its portfolio includes laser cutting systems, press brakes and associated automation and software solutions as well as services.

ISMR caught up with Johan Elster, President Business Unit Markets and a Member of Bystronic's Management Committee, at this October's EuroBLECH to discuss its vision of the future.

ISMR: What are you promoting on your booth at EuroBLECH? What is your message to EuroBLECH visitors?

JE: When it comes to solutions, we are displaying our industry vision here on the booth. This vision has been developed with some of our leading customers and tries to identify the future direction of our industry On our booth, you can see showcases that illustrate where the industry is going. Our aim is to be a partner to our customers on the entire journey, from single machines to the final vision (fully automated, integrated, Industry 4.0-ready production systems).

Our message is to sell this future, moving step by step with our customers in this direction. Of course, not all our customers will buy these integrated systems – a lot of our customers are not Industry 4.0-ready yet and we want to make this journey with them, to reach that final stage and work together with them on this. They need to decide who their partner will be on this journey. Our approach is to move forward with them on this path

ISMR: How do you see Industry 4.0 progressing?

JE: There is so much digitalisation going on in this industry, which is unlocking new possibilities for users. We are showing digital services here on the booth that we can provide today, as well as what will be available in the future (based on Big Data). The industry is now more software-reliant and there is a need for customers to be able to interface with different suppliers.

I have been in this industry for many years and I believe that it will take several years for Industry 4.0 to come into more widespread use on the shop floor. Of course, the frontrunners will get there more quickly but most of our job shop customers, who do not have the capital to make these huge investments, will have to move more slowly towards this goal. Some industry sectors/ markets also develop at different paces. We are there to develop a partnership principle with customers on this journey.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal markets?

JE: The semi-conductor business has been good, with servo farms being built all over the world to handle Big Data (particularly in South Korea). There are tonnes of sheet metal in cabinets, shelving and cooling devices etc. Electric cars represent another trend. Some of our customers supply transformers to charge electric car batteries. This is just the beginning. Some countries, such as Germany, are investing in electric infrastructures to charge cars – these are huge investments and very important for sheet metal.

On the material side, fibre lasers can now cut thicker material much faster. We are showing a 12kW fibre laser model at EuroBLECH that can cut thicker material and have placed our focus on cutting and bending technology. We definitely see a trend towards much more automation as it is hard to handle such fast machines manually. We were the first with a 10kW machine two years ago, and this has been a great success for our customers. People really understand now that automation is needed with such a fast machine - you don't want the machine waiting for the operators to finish.

In parallel, service has become more crucial than ever to eliminate any downtime issues. Remote service and predictive maintenance in the Digital 4.0 world becomes more important and we are working hard in this area as we believe that service will become even more crucial. This includes technologies such as Augmented Reality, VR and AI and we are working on projects with, for example, AI and Google glasses for remote support. The trend is for better support and assistance, particularly for higher automation levels, and this will be a key differentiator for customers.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for the sheet metal industry in Europe?

JE: This year has been the best year ever for us. The economy has been great for the last two years. We see some clouds in China with the US tariff situation and also in Turkey.

For 2019, we predict high-level consolidation, rather than rapid growth.

However, in general, the economy in Europe is still very strong. Brexit is a challenge – we have been successful in the UK, but Brexit brings uncertainty. A lot of our UK customers produce for the domestic market, which provides a cushion, fortunately, for them.

ISMR: Who are your customers? Where are your markets?

JE: Our largest market is still in Europe. Last year, Europe accounted for about 50% of our turnover (20% Americas and 30% Asia). I expect a slightly lower market share in Europe at the end of the year as our strategy has been to grow faster in the other two continents. We are a European company – many years ago, 72% of our turnover was in Europe. Now it is 50%.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

JE: I don't think that our customers' needs have changed much over the last few years. On the machine side, we hear that they want to automate more (if the economy is strong, customers always look for the next step i.e. if they have a laser and load/unload system, they look at sorting etc.). A general trend is towards more automation but also flexible automated solutions. For smaller batches, flexible automation systems can be a challenge (as they are generally built for large batches). We are showing this capability here on our booth on the Production Line – we are sorting different parts with different material flows that have been cut on the same cutting plan. The automation is flexible.

On the data side, our larger customers want the data flow and data planning to be automated. Some of our smaller customers may not have ERP systems but larger customers with ERP systems want to manage their stock, optimise their purchasing and planning etc. We also showcase this on the booth and have developed our own machine control software, production planning software, shop floor control and our MES software that can also tie into other processes. On the Production Line, you can see how our shop floor control system manages parts produced in machines so that the machines know what to do. So, we have a complete software portfolio to digitalise a customer's operations.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next two years?

JE: In general, we are transforming ourselves from a single machine manufacturer to a solutions provider on all sides (machines, services and data flow). A year ago, we started to change our internal organisation to start adapting to this vision. We have Competence Centres for each technology (laser cutting, bending, automation, tube processing and software). We also recently acquired TTM Laser (a tube and profile processing specialist).

On the automation side, we now have an entire portfolio of products after acquiring Antil and FMG. We have made automation a core competence within Bystronic with its own Competence Centre. On top of that we have a Solutions Centre where all the competences come together to create the solution for the customer.

Overall, there are three pillars in our market strategy. We want to be in the market in several segments, not just the high-tech level. We are also expanding geographically into new markets and provide better coverage into existing markets. The third pillar is to move from selling one single machine to selling solutions. This will happen, of course, incrementally.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
