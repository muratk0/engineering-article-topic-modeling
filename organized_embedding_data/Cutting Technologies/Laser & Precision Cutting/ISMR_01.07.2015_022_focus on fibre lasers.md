Amada has also introduced a new LC-2515 C1 AJ 2kW punch-laser combination machine. With a compact footprint, this is equipped with a fibre laser and multi-purpose tool turret for faster processing speeds and fewer secondary operations. On the company’s FOL3015 AJ 4kW fibre laser, material processing is rapid thanks to 340mm/min. linear drives and 5G acceleration, while users can expect to save around 70% in energy running costs in comparison with a CO2 machine, Amada told ISMR.

“The over-riding reason for purchasing a fibre laser, according to our customers, was the speed of cutting thin (1 to 2mm and up to 3mm) materials. An important secondary reason was the lower running cost and the lower power consumption, which was particularly important in companies at the top end of the electrical supply limit in their workshops,” Bystronic told ISMR.

Bystronic equips its BySprint Fiber laser cutting system with a 6 kilowatt laser source to increase the BySprint Fiber's cutting speed e.g. by up to 70 per cent compared to the 4 kilowatt fibre laser when cutting 3mm thick stainless steel. The speed advantage is even more pronounced when compared to the 6 kilowatt CO2 laser – in 3mm thick stainless steel, the 6 kilowatt fiber laser cuts three times faster. With the ByOptimizer, Bystronic is now also offering an online service that generates optimized cutting plans.

Both flat sheet and tube section can be processed on BLM’s new Adige LC5 laser cutting system. Capacity of the LC5 includes the ability to process flat sheet measuring up to 1500mm x 3000mm and round, square, rectangular and oval section tube up to a maximum diameter of 120mm. The user has the option of choosing either a 4.5kW CO2 laser source or a 3kW fibre laser which opens up the potential to process reflective metals such as copper, brass and aluminium.

Polish innovator Eckert recently launched an award-winning innovative Piercing Control System (PCS) for its Diamond Fiber Laser 2 cutting machine which substantially reduces piercing time from 10 seconds to two seconds, as well as cutting gas/energy costs.

Fraunhofer ILT’s simulation work is aimed at optimising the process parameters when cutting with a fibre laser. This is part of an EU project called FILCO, which aims to build a 1μm laser head capable of cutting 15mm thick stainless steel with a cut quality similar to CO2 systems.

HIGHYAG Lasertechnologie GmbH (HIGHYAG), a subsidiary of II-VI Incorporated, is one of the world's leading suppliers in the laser material processing industry. Its innovative laser processing heads and fibre beam delivery systems are used for laser cutting, welding and brazing in the advanced manufacturing industry.

Its BIMO-FSC laser cutting head can cut various sheet thicknesses and is fully controlled by the cutting machine without manual intervention. In combination with the BIMO-FSC’s rapid focus movement on the workpiece, this guarantees efficient flat sheet cutting and hence high productivity in manufacturing. The company also produces laser light cables with the new LLK-Q connector which is QBH-compatible and designed for dynamic use in cutting machines and high-brilliance lasers.

“Fibre laser cutting systems have several advantages over CO2 laser systems as they require virtually no maintenance, are more energy-efficient and take up less space. The power supply is much smaller than that of a CO2 laser, yet fibre delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables,” explained fibre laser machine manufacturer, Hypertherm, which has produced the HyIntensity™ Fiber Laser.

Kjellberg’s XFocus 1000 is a compact system with a 1,000 Watt fibre laser source for cutting and marking thin sheets of mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium with all common guiding systems that are suitable for plasma cutting. The laser system can be operated on the same guiding system with a plasma cutting unit either in change mode or separately.

LVD Company nv has expanded its fibre laser cutting systems product line with the introduction of the Lynx FL. As a part of the Newport Corporation, Ophir’s optics group is a leading manufacturer of infrared optics and optics for high-power lasers. Optics for CO2 lasers have been produced since 1995. In 2011, Ophir started production of optics for fibre and solid-state lasers in a new facility in Romania. In June 2015, it demonstrated its Fiberlens™, a series of innovative collimating lenses, focusing lenses and protective windows for use with high-power fibre lasers at the LASER World of PHOTONICS in Munich. It also demonstrated its High Reflection (HR) laser mirror coating, which was developed for use in high-power CO2 laser resonators and beam delivery systems.

Based on the successful Platino® Fiber laser platform, with more than 1500 installations worldwide, the new version of Prima Power’s high powered Platino® Fiber laser is available in versions of up to 5kW. The new machine delivers piercing time reductions of up to 90%, near-zero lens set-up times and cutting speeds of up to 5m/min on stainless steel, which is on average 20% faster than the 4kW machine. It also has an option for a Laser Piercing Monitor, to give real-time feedback and check when piercing is completed, and Prima Power Fiber Plasma Monitoring (detecting problems with the cutting and formation of plasma when cutting is lost).

With its new ROFIN FL 080, ROFIN has launched a fibre laser with an output power of 8kW for welding and rapid cutting. Depending upon requirements, beam quality can be adapted to the specific application task. It has also introduced the FL 040 C compact 4kW fibre laser. Both machines are equipped with the “ROFIN control unit” (RCU) which, in addition to diverse monitoring functionalities, also offers e-service capability and scannerbased applications. Its StarFiber laser sources offer rated outputs of between 100W and 600W. With its variable pulse width, ROFIN's PowerLine F 20 Varia delivers best marking results for large-scale metal removal at high speed through to fine processing.

“We were the first to develop and market a laser using both a fibre optic resonator and fibre optic beam transmission to achieve superior results vis a vis CO2 lasers. Costs per part are slashed by more than 50% and energy consumption is reduced by over 70%. Running costs are only one third that of a 4kW CO2 laser with chiller. There is virtually zero maintenance,” Italian manufacturer Salvagnini told ISMR, highlighting its L3 and L5 fibre laser cutting systems. The company recently launched its AVS system (artificial vision for laser cutting systems), featuring a high-definition camera installed near the cutting head.

German manufacturer TRUMPF has extended fibre laser’s suitability for thin sheet cutting to thicker sheet processing. From mild steel to stainless steel through to highly reflective materials such as copper and aluminium; from 1mm to 25mm sheet thickness – all components can be produced on one single laser machine. Its TruLaser 5030 fiber with ‘BrightLine fiber’ function enables manufacturers to cut thin sheet with exactly the same attributes for thick sheet.

“This combination achieves quality and productivity in material between 15 and 25mm. Even stainless steel and aluminium, up to 25mm thick, can be cut cleanly and very fine holes and contours applied; previously, they could only be drilled,” TRUMPF told ISMR.

‘BrightLine fiber’ turns the solid-state laser into a truly universal tool since it can process a broad range of sheet thicknesses and materials flexibly and at high quality. At the same time, this option makes for more stable processes, even smaller contours and significantly simplified removal of the finished parts.

The second generation of the TruLaser Cell 8030, which TRUMPF officially sent to market last October, celebrated its global debut at Intech. The main application is for laser cutting hot-formed 3D designs and press-hardened parts. TRUMPF also recently developed a pulsed, green laser for efficient welding of copper with the new TruDisk 421 pulse. The disk laser, in the pulsed mode, operates at a mean power of 400 watts, generating laser light at 515 nanometers. Light in the green spectrum solves the problems found when using infrared lasers to weld copper.

The laser manufacturer SPI Lasers, a member of the TRUMPF Group, acquired the company JK Lasers 15 April 2015. With almost 100 employees and annual sales of 17.6 million euros, JK Lasers is one of the world's leading manufacturers of high power fibre lasers for industrial use. ■
