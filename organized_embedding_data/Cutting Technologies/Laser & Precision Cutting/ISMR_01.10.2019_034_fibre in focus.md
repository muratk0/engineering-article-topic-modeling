# FIBRE IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.10.2019 ISMR]

With a steady plan for growth and the continuous development of new and more innovative fibre laser models, the future looks promising for CYLASER.

ISMR SAYS:

“We visited CYLASER's facilities in northeast Italy to see its fibre laser manufacturing and engineering operations and subsidiary offices in the U.S. and India, CYLASER has focused its efforts on fibre laser technology (for sheet and tube cutting), as well as welding technology. It has been pioneering the application of solid-state fibre optic laser technology for industrial cutting applications since 2004 and continues to build its expertise in the technology. It showcased two new laser cutting systems, Vortex and Vega, at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover last year as well as a new machine with linear motors at the LAMIERA exhibition in Milan in 2019.

CYLASER's wide fibre laser cutting product portfolio includes 2D laser cutting machines (some with tube processing capabilities); tube laser cutting systems. Machines range from 2.5m x 1.25m to 13m x 3m. A double configuration is also offered for medium-sized machines (a long version and a side version for companies with space issues). All CYLASER machines are powered with up to 12kW fibre optic generators. The company also offers a variety of Load Unload Automation Systems and Material Storage Towers with automatic material retrieval, loading and unloading functions for lights-out operation.

ISMR was invited to view its two facilities (for manufacturing and R&D) at Schio in Italy, and explore CYLASER's vision for the future with its President, Federico Campana, and its area manager, Frank Vanin, during the visit.

## A focus on fibre

CYLASER has been a true pioneer in the development of fibre laser cutting technology. In 2004, its founder (a welding specialist) developed a fibre laser welding machine in collaboration with laser expert, IPG. He then saw the potential of fibre laser technology for metal cutting at a time when the market was solely focused on CO₂ laser cutting. Partnering with IPG on this project, CYLASER developed a 1.5kW fibre laser cutting machine from scratch. It even developed a proprietary cutting head – now in its fifth generation.

"In 2004, nobody thought that fibre laser would be suitable for cutting thicker materials and that CO₂ would be a better laser cutting technology. However, we proved otherwise and have swiftly developed quicker and more capable fibre laser cutting systems over the years. I would recommend fibre laser cutting for material of up to 25mm thickness for a guaranteed quality cut. Our focus has always been on fibre laser technology and this is a key strength. Federico Campana and his sister Martina run the company now," Frank Vanin, CYLASER's area sales manager, told ISMR.

Operating from a 4500-square metre manufacturing facility in Schio (north-east Italy), with a separate smaller R&D/engineering facility a few kilometres away (which ISMR also toured), CYLASER has facilities in Italy, the United States and India. It has grown its technical and service departments and is now developing its marketing and is in the throes of launching a new website. It also makes use of a coordinated supply chain in the highly industrialised region of Schio, which includes a lot of specialist companies.

"In 2015/6, our turnover increased by 80% year-on-year. In the last couple of years we are keeping the average build at 40-45 units per year. We are very competitive on price for 6m and above. We also work with around seven local partners on customised automation for our machines. Growth is important, but it must be sustainable and steady growth that can be properly managed. We have seen an increase in demand for automation and we will always try to meet customers' requirements, if technically feasible. We aim to become a true partner, fully understanding our customers' needs and offering them the right machine for their requirements as well as the right level of aftersales service," added Federico Campana, CYLASER's President.

## New technologies and launches

Innovative features on CYLASER machines include their unusual 'aeroplane' structure. The cutting head runs on a beam that is mounted to the main structure, which is independent from the table. It is not a gantry system. Being independent from the table means that the system is not subject to vibrations, helping to control cutting quality stability in the long term. The cutting head is also at a higher level than a gantry, so it is not subject to thermal drift.

The company aims to bring at least one new product to market every year, and 2019 was no exception. It is in the process of developing a new bevel head to increase cutting possibilities. It also launched a new fibre laser cutting model at the LAMIERA exhibition in Milan this year that boasted linear motors, for greater cutting speed control.

"Our machines can be built as one aeroplane structure (with no migration on the cutting head) or they can incorporate a gantry system. This new model was presented at the LAMIERA exhibition in Milan this year. If a customer wants speed, the gantry system is more suitable. For those customers who want flexibility and versatility, then the aeroplane structure is ideal. It is slower than the gantry system but can easily switch cutting from thin to thick materials, whereas the gantry system is better to cut thin to medium material thicknesses," he explained.

"The new model has linear motors but we are also implementing a rack and pinion system (which offers higher speed than the aeroplane structure but slower, linear motors as a less expensive machine range). Linear motors give users more control over the cutting speed. This is the first machine where we have integrated linear motors and the reaction to its launch, by Italian manufacturers at LAMIERA, was very promising. It is primarily aimed at customers who wish to cut thin material at high speeds.

CYLASER is also introducing an affordable line for customers with lower budgets. The machine structure build is subcontracted out and CYLASER then fits the rest of the machine.

"This is still a 100% CYLASER machine, with the relevant CE certificate, but it unlocks an opportunity for people to use our technology at a lower budget and migrate up.... So, we have the entry level machine, the gantry rack and pinion, the aeroplane structure and a wide range of production models (small to large) that cover up to 99% of the market. Power ranges go up to 12kW," said Frank Vanin.

## Addressing new markets

CYLASER also opened a new branch office in India (near Mumbai) this January. It believes that this price-conscious market has strong potential.

"We have already picked up three orders for delivery at the end of the year in India and we visited fifteen further customers in India this year who are interested in our technology. Our name is gaining recognition in India," continued Frank Vanin.
