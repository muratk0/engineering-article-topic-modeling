# THE WATER MARGIN

Waterjet cutting is referred to as a "cold cutting" process because it requires no extra heat. In fact, as only high-pressure water and abrasives are used, waterjet cutting generates very little heat, even from friction, so there is no risk of burning, melting or affecting the material in any way (no heat-affected zone). Cold cutting also reduces overall production time because there is little or no need for clamping or bracing materials during set-up. Operators do not have to wait for materials to cool down before moving onto the next step in production.

Cold cutting also offers a high level of precision. As pressurised water is flowing through a very small area, users have greater control over the cuts being made. Cold cutting is often used for more detailed cuts, such as geometric shapes or designs. It can also be used to cut a wider variety of materials and more irregular surfaces.

From mass producing parts out of sheet metal to carving intricate 3D designs in marble, the waterjet has numerous applications and is one of the most versatile cutting systems in the manufacturing industry today. The introduction of 5-axis cutting heads has triggered massive growth in waterjet cutting. No longer confined to cutting out flat objects, waterjets are now able to produce continuous bevels, revolve around the workpiece and cut at angles from 0-90 degrees.
