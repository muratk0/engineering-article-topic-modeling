Dust collector reduces laser table dust problems

Situation

Maintenance Manager Mel Waldner was frustrated by the amount of time and money he was spending tending to the leaky dust collector in the Starland Metals manufacturing facility near rural Gibbon, Minn. He knew that the company’s ability to produce top-quality metal manufacturing and parts fabrication depended on the effective collection of dust generated by its laser tables. The sensitivity of optics on laser tables makes proper capture and removal of contaminant crucial in laser cutting applications.

The leaking, vertically hung cartridge collector created a dusty environment in the utility room, necessitating frequent replacement of the collector’s filters, as well as those in the compressor and chiller because of the ambient air conditions. The company was spending close to $4,000 every six months on replacement dust collector filters, and Waldner needed to sweep the utility room daily.

The leaking collector forced Waldner to abandon the return-air duct design and exhaust the dust collector outlet air to the outside of the building, which wasted warm air used to heat the facility during cold Minnesota winters. The continual pulsing of the collector and its top-mounted fan right next to the return-air duct were generating excessive noise that made work unpleasant, even outside the utility room.

Resolution

The company installed a Donaldson Torit Power-Core TG dust collector and a Delta P Plus control with a variable-frequency drive.

The collector is designed for thermally generated dust and features a footprint up to 65 percent smaller than most cartridge collectors. It is available up to 10,000 CFM. The solid-state control provides pressure-drop measurement, digital display, and pressure-drop control with an alarm function. It controls the pulse-cleaning timer, starting and stopping the cleaning process at user-chosen limits.

Since installation of the collector, the work environment needs cleaning every couple of weeks instead of daily. The stable pressure drop and the variable-frequency drive have contributed to considerable energy savings. Waldner has confirmed a 10-amp power draw from the new collector versus an 18-amp draw from the previous unit. A year-to-year comparison of monthly electric bills showed a decrease of more than $800.

The collector’s filter packs work in conjunction with the drive so that the fan consumes less energy while maintaining dust control at the lasers. Additionally, the company now uses the collector’s return-air duct design to allow the filtered, warm air to return to the building during the winter months.

Automation upgrade saves manufacturer programming, nesting time

Situation

Interroll Automation LLC, Jeffersonville, Ind., manufactures engineered-to-order material handling systems. The company outsourced its sheet metal component laser cutting until purchasing a CNC laser machine in 2007 to bring the operations inhouse.

“The increasing volume of work, our ability to improve lead-times, and desire to improve the quality of the finished part to our customers were all driving factors to bringing the manufacturing inhouse,” said Stephen Book, production controller.

The company purchased a Hankwang 3015 CNC laser, which was supplied with Jetcam Expert 1 as standard. This allowed automatic import and application of tooling to CAD files using Jetcam’s Single Component Automation Processing facility.

Resolution

As Interroll developed its in-house manufacturing, it decided to automate its nesting processes and purchased Jetcam’s Free-Form High Performance Nesting (FFHPN) module to produce optimized, dynamically generated nests. Interroll also upgraded to the latest version of Expert.

The time taken to apply profiling information and create nests was reduced by two labor-hours each per day. FFHPN delivered a 10 percent savings over previously created nests.

The menu system is organized and laid out by functionality, which helps reduce the time to perform functions such as setting up machine tech tables and to tool and nest parts. Surges in orders can be quickly tooled and nested, according to the company. The custom report writer produces reports that allow the operator to identify and tag parts as they come off the machine.

“We can now remodel nests to select the best sheet size for the parts,” Book explained. “For example, we nest on a 4 by 8 and 5 by 10 at the same time and then pick the best material utilization. High-performance nesting works really well for us, as we have a wide variety of small-run parts.”

Remnants are managed using auto-nesting. The operator selects the remnant size before pushing the nest button, reducing the time needed to manage material remnants while also reducing inventory and handling costs.

Interroll saw a return on investment on its upgrade in four months through material and time savings. “One intangible cost was a reduction in lead-time in the order preparation,” said Book. “Our customers are date-driven because they are project-driven, meaning our due dates have to be achieved.”
