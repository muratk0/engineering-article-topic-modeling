# DOWNSTREAM PROCESSING

[TARİH: 01.11.2011 ISMR]

ISMR compares and contrasts laser and waterjet cutting processes.

Waterjet cutting is the fastest growing profiling method world-wide. It is a process used to cut materials using a jet of pressurised water as high 60,000 pounds per square inch (psi) forced through an orifice ranging from 0.1 to 0.35mm.

This creates a fine bead of water which is strong enough to cut a wide range of soft metals such as foam, rubber, plastic, glass fibre etc. Heat and gaseous materials-free, the process brings accurate and cost effective cutting options to customers over a wide range of materials, thicknesses and applications.

An abrasive waterjet system can be created by the introduction of an abrasive, usually garnet, into the water stream as it leaves the nozzle. Combining the high pressure water with the abrasive produces a stream that is capable of profiling nearly any material such as titanium, stainless steel ceramics, laminates and Kevlar. This enables more materials to be cut cleanly to close tolerances, squarely and with a good edge finish.

Other benefits include virtually burr-free cut edges (reducing finishing operations), no material discolouration and high yields of material (for cost savings). Multi-head operation enables the competitive cutting of flat materials whilst abrasive water jet cutting can cut virtually any material, in any shape, up to 200mm thick. Heat input, warping and distortion are eliminated.
