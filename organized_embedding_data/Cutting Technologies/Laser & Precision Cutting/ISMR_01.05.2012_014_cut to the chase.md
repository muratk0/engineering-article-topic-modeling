The Eckert Company has installed almost 950 systems for oxy-fuel and plasma cutting in Poland and throughout Europe over the last 18 years. Its clients include Daimler Chrysler Rail Systems, Rolls-Royce, Becker Warkop, Manesmann-Siegen, H. Cegielski, Siemens-Chemnitz, Huta Stalowa Wola, Tagor Tarnowskie Góry and GEA.

Employing 100 specialists, such as 20 construction engineers, electronic engineers and IT specialists, it has launched a range of innovative cutting systems including the Diamond Fiber, its latest new launch for fiber laser cutting. An increase in production and six awards (for product innovation and its contribution to regional development in Poland) are just some of its achievements last year.

Its head office is in Legnica, Poland, and a further branch is located in Zwickau (Germany). Across its 25,000 m2 site in Poland, there are four production halls, a warehouse and office buildings. It currently produces 25 machines per month.

## An eye on the future

“Despite the fact that we heard a lot about the financial crisis from the media in 2011, according to industry experts and from our experience, as a manufacturer of machines used in the cutting industry, 2011 was a good year,” Rafael Eckert, Eckert’s sales manager for Germany, told ISMR.

“The CNC machinery market is directly dependent on the state of the steel market and the market for processing other materials. The World Steel Association forecasts indicated that the overt world steel consumption was expected to grow in 2011 by 6.5% to about 1.4 billion tons. Experts estimate that, in 2012, demand will increase by another 5.4% reaching 1.47 billion tons. The consumption of steel products this year may be a key factor in determining the situation in the industry,” he continued.

“According to the Polish Union of Steel Distributors, the number of machines manufactured by Polish producers of machine tools for metal increased in 2011 by over 145%. Preliminary data for the nine months of last year show that the production of this type of equipment may be exceeded this year (10,000 devices). From their perspective, we can safely say that 2011 may be regarded as one of the most successful in the entire 22-year history.”

Rafael Eckert has high expectations for the future, having just launched a number of new product innovations into the market including a fiber laser cutter and combination waterjet/plasma cutting machine.

“Ever-increasing customer expectations and the rapid development of new technologies mean that the cutting market has rapidly evolved,” he told ISMR. “Although plasma cutting machines are still very popular, we have observed the growing demand for combined technologies. Our WaterJet Combo machine, combining plasma and water technology, takes advantage of both. This machine is very popular with clients.

“However, the future will probably belong to fiber laser cutting technology, which will become a kind of combination solution for the comprehensive treatment of a variety of materials. In other words, buying one source enables you to vary your cutting (including cutting in 3D technology) and welding requirements. This investment is an extraordinary saving. According to our development strategy for the next few years, 80% of expenditure will be allocated to our B+R department to improve laser functionality.”

This year, he intends to focus on export development. New technological solutions, he says, will be explored by Eckert’s research and development department which is working on future fiber laser developments, an area where he sees great potential for the company.

“Our clients are giants such as power plants, steel mills and coal mines. Our machine tools are involved in creating stadiums, ships, bridges, machine builders and flyovers. However, in terms of promising market potential, we are looking outside the EU to countries such as India and Brazil. These are in the throes of developing and restructuring their industries,” Rafael Eckert, sales manager (Germany), told ISMR.

“It is estimated, for example, that India is the seventh highest country for machine tool consumption in the world. According to The Economics Times, the economic slowdown or weak economic situation has not affected steadily growing demand in the manufacturing sector for metalworking machinery. Despite growing demand, the Indian manufacturing sector is still dependent on imports of machinery as it is currently able to meet only about one-third of its requirements. These optimistic forecasts bode well for the success of any outreach activities and we currently have a firm foothold in the Indian market.”

## Product innovations

Treatment with water under high pressure has been used in the industry for at least 40 years and is now one of the fastest growing methods for blast-cleaning. Improvements of this versatile method of separation process consist primarily of increasing power and cutting speed by using higher pump power.

Several years ago, Eckert introduced an innovative product to market – a cutting machine using a combination of plasma and water dubbed the WaterJet Combo. By combining these two technologies, it has managed to exploit the advantages of each. Currently, it is also developing a 3D waterjet cutting machine. This year, it created a threedimensional waterjet cutting head. The head allows for phasing of the surface at angles that were not possible in traditional 2D cutting machines. The process is extremely clean and does not generate dust or fumes that are harmful to health, says Eckert.

“Our waterjet 3D cutting head allows you to rapidly cut parts with high spatial accuracy and virtually zero diagonal cutting edge. It is designed with small-size drives, high gear ratios and is capable of high-precision angular positions (positioning accuracy of <1 arc/min). The head design allows for a tilt in the axis of the nozzle A + / -90 and C-axis by + / -400 degrees. The head body is made of aluminium and its small size and compact design make it an extremely accurate and versatile device,” Rafael Eckert explained.

Fiber laser is the latest innovation from the Eckert Company. The Diamond Fiber laser cutting machine, based on fiber optic technology, is designed to be highly efficient and process a wide range of cut materials.

“By using optical fibers, it was possible to eliminate an expensive system of mirrors,” Rafael Eckert explained. “This resulted in a significant reduction of maintenance and servicing costs. Additionally, a new source of highly efficient laser brings significant energy cost savings.

“The optical fiber bundle is used to direct the beam from the resonator to the cutting head. The optical fiber enables uninterrupted and energy-loss-free transfer of the bundle. The fiber laser source uses up to 70% less energy than the CO2 one and is built for 100,000 hours of operation. The fiber laser is able to cut materials at higher speeds than a CO2 laser with the same power input.”

Eckert also produces a wide range of 3D plasma cutting systems for high quality welding preparation for sheet and tube. The system can process pipes from 40 to 900mm in diameter and wall thicknesses of 2 to 100mm. Eckert engineers have also, by integrating a Fanuc robot, developed an innovative robotic system which enables the cutting of curves on pipes with direct preparation of these pipes in suitable welding chamfers, ultimately creating complicated tubular joints.

VERSATILITY IN VIEW ISMR analyses the benefits of and chronicles waterjet cutting the latest cutting developments.

ISMR SAYS:

“Waterjets can successfully complement other cutting operations”

Many sheet metal subcontractors and job shops are finding waterjets to be a great asset to their operations – enhancing their productivity, versatility and profitability. They require few secondary operations, produce netshaped parts with no heat-affected zone, heat distortion or mechanical stresses caused by other cutting methods, can cut with a narrow kerf and make better usage of raw material since parts can be tightly nested.

The flexible waterjet process either replaces or complements other technologies such as milling, laser, EDM, plasma and routers. No noxious gases or liquids are used in waterjet cutting and waterjets do not create hazardous materials or vapours. No heat-affected zones or mechanical stresses are left on a waterjet cut surface – it is a truly versatile, productive, cold cutting process.

In today's environmentally-conscious environments, waterjets can also lay claim to being eco-friendly in terms of cleanliness, material diversity and noise abatement. "Waterjets can profile a wide range of materials, as diverse as 100mm thickness armour plate, titanium and stainless steel, through aluminium and down to lightweight foam, with everything in between ......," as one subcontractor told ISMR.

## Processes and products

Overall, the actual capital expenditure of a waterjet system is lower than a laser cutter or a punch press. Waterjets complement these technologies, rather than compete with them. However, from an hourly running cost perspective, waterjets are more expensive than lasers or punch presses and the cutting speeds are slower. What you need to look for, however, is its fit into a business.

Bystronic's ByJet series waterjet cutting machines are, amongst other features, equipped with up to four high-precision cutting heads, automatic cutting distance adjustment and fully automatic height sensing. From the high-pressure pump, the precision cutting head and right through to the cutting basin, the components that influence process security are produced in-house. Models range from the Byjet Smart, Pro and Classic.

Flow International recently introduced its new Paser 4 abrasive cutting head system which is now available for Flow abrasive waterjet shapecutting systems operating at either 60ksi or 87ksi pressures. The Paser system consists of the Paser 4 cutting head, the Paser 4 abrasive metering valve, and the new Low Profile on/off valve. These new products can easily be retrofitted to existing installed Flow waterjet machines so operators can immediately begin to enjoy the benefits of this new technology.

Flow continues to raise the bar in performance by incorporating new patent pending technology to significantly extend component life. This means lower cost of operation since fewer parts and abrasive are consumed and time between maintenance intervals is greatly reduced. Orifice component life is increased 3-6 times over earlier generations and competing cutting heads, it told ISMR.
