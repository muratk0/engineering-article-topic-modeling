# A SHARP FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2014 ISMR]

Amada previewed a range of innovative laser, bending, punching and software advances at EuroBLECH, but the undoubted highlight was its new Direct Diode laser machine

On a 2000-square metre booth, Amada’s main focus at EuroBLECH was on highlighting innovation, excellence and productivity. From fiber laser cutting to folding, punching, software and robotics, its expertise crosses all aspects of sheet metal working as well as the complete production process.

Its undoubted surprise of the EuroBLECH week was the unveiling of its new laser technology for sheet metal cutting – a direct diode laser cutting system. It is the first time it had ever been shown to the market and a great draw for visitors to Amada’s booth.

## The future is fiber

“The real emphasis for us here at EuroBLECH is our laser technology. We have had so much interest in our ENSIS laser cutting machine. Operators can cut 20/25mm mild steel on the fiber laser – people just don’t believe it or believe that the machine is only 2kW. Traditionally, 20mm mild steel is cut on a 4kW CO2 machine. The ENSIS just uses 25% of the power of a 4kW CO2 machine. That has really opened a lot of customers’ eyes this week,” Matt Wood, Senior Product Manager, Marketing, Amada Europe told ISMR.

The other great surprise for the sheet metal industry was the unveiling of Amada’s new laser technology for sheet metal cutting – its EXC direct diode laser system. The machine’s efficiency is upwards of 40%, compared to 32/33% for a fiber laser and 10% for a CO2 laser cutting system. The Japanese machine tool specialist is eventually aiming to produce 4kW and 6kW models.

“EXC gives a cut surface finish, particularly on mild steel, equal to or better than the best CO2 machines but with fiber laser running costs. The beam quality is not so different from a CO2 machine but, because of the stability of the oscillator, you can get a very high quality finish, faster than CO2 but not as fast as fiber. So there will be a very clear distinction between fiber and DDL technology. For thinner material ranges, fiber is the clear choice because it will always be faster. However, if you need to cover the whole material range from 1-20mm mild steel or 1-12/15mm aluminium or stainless steel, then the DDL technology will cover that range faster than a CO2 laser with fiber laser running costs,” explained Matt Wood.

“We are showing this machine as a prototype here and are hoping it should be production-ready for the market in the near future. We are the first company in the world to exhibit a direct diode sheet metal cutting laser. Traditionally, it has always been used for welding and hardening but the problem has always been harnessing the power of the high quality beam to cut sheet metal. We have found a way to harness the diode light directly, increase the brightness and feed it straight to a fiber cable to the cutting head. Other companies make diode lasers for welding applications but no other company is making this for sheet metal cutting yet.”

Amada was also promoting its fiber laser punch combination machine, LC2515 C1-AJ. Because it is servo-electrical punch-driven, with a 2kW fiber laser, it uses less power than Amada’s hydraulic punching and combination machines, so it is highly efficient. It also boasts a very compact footprint for a 3m sheet, even with a double tower automation system.

These laser technologies provoked an interesting debate on the future of laser cutting technologies for sheet metal, which ISMR posed to Matt Wood.

“I believe that fiber laser will always cut faster in thin material because its beam quality is better than DDL (Direct Diode Laser) or CO2 so, if a customer processes material of 3mm or 4mm and below across the board, fiber laser will be suitable. However, the advantage with DDL technology is that, with 2kW of power here, operators can already cut up to 16mm mild steel already and DDL is in the fairly early stages of development… With a 4kW DDL model, you could maybe process 20mm mild steel cut finish will be like a CO2 laser – so the machine cuts like a CO2 machine but with fiber laser running costs. The DDL technology is 40% efficient and CO2 is 10%. In the future, CO2 will enjoy a niche market of thicker aluminium and stainless steel materials to cut. If the customer’s day-to-day business demands good quality, I still feel that, for the next few years at least, CO2 technology will still be in demand,” he told ISMR.

## Packing a punch

Amada’s final exhibit on the blanking side was its EM-ZRB punching machine, with brush-covered turret so that operators can process punched components scratch-free.

“You can program the EM-ZRB so that the die disappears below the level of the material every time it moves or you can just leave the die in position if you don’t need high speed punching. The only die that is used is the one you are currently processing – all the others sit below the brush bed. It is linked to our ID TOGU grinding machine and the ID tooling system. The tools have a QR system on the side so that the customer can scan them, put the tools into the turret and, when the machine picks up the tool from the tool changer and drops it into the processing turret, it will check which tool it is and ensure it is at the right angle, checking how many hits the tool has performed in the past,” explained Matt Wood.

“If the tool has reached a limit that you have set and needs maintenance, it will warn the operator and tell him how much needs to be ground off the tool using the system. Once the tool is loaded back into the main turret, it is scanned and measured and the machine adjusts the die automatically for the newly ground tool. It avoids incorrect tool settings and problems with badly maintained tools. It has a 33-station buffer turret and a 32-station main turret (65 tools) and can auto-index any B- and C-station tool.”

## An eye on innovation

“Amada UK has increased its turnover year on year and has done so since 2008. This year will be another record year. New markets for Amada UK in Scandinavia are increasing. The Spanish division will increase its turnover by 50% this year, which is against the trend we see in the Spanish economy. We have been overseeing the Scandinavian market since the start of the year, and the Spanish market since 2010, and our sales have increased by 30/40% this year,” commented Gary Belfort, Engineering Manager, Amada UK.

“The UK market is very good for us right now. In the first half of this year, we increased our sales by 14-18% in the UK with a mix of new and existing customer business. The biggest jump for us has been in the laser market (a mix of CO2 and fiber laser cutting sales).”

Amada also showcased several bending innovations on its booth at EuroBLECH. The first was a new range of press brakes, based on its traditional downstroking HFE range, but in a 3i version (its latest 3D graphic control interface).

We are the first company in the world to exhibit a direct diode sheet metal cutting laser

“This means that we can get all the bending information that the operator needs on one screen – the 3D modeller can plan, zoom and rotate and check the bending process before the products are placed on the machine. We have also introduced mechanical and laser bend angle measurement systems so that we can check correct spring back bend results and the operator does not need to pick the parts up twice to get the correct bend results – it is done automatically,” explained Belfort.

Another bending feature was its energy-saving systems (inverter-controlled stop/start technology) which reduces energy consumption when bending by 60% and, consequently, overall running costs.

On the automation side, Amada has launched two new robotic bending models – the HG-ARs (Automated Robot – small version) and the HG-RM (robotised medium version). In the very near future, the company expects to launch an H ‘heavy’ version.

“We have basically taken our HG range of high accuracy, high speed, servo-driven, hybriddrive hydraulic system press brakes and used stop/start technology to control the servos. So, you get the 60-70% energy savings with these models as well as very small reservoir tanks for the machine (as we have reduced the amount of oil needed). As we can quickly adjust the flow rate, we can increase bending and approach speeds (ie 200mm/sec. approach speed and 20mm/sec. bending speed). It is a very efficient machine. Because there is only small volume of oil, there is no effect on temperature change so we can position the beam very accurately (at 0.001mm) so repeatability is excellent,” added Belfort.

Different automation and robotic options can be attached to these press brakes (robot sizes range from 20kg to 165kg) and Amada also offers robotised automatic tool-changing systems using tool carousels. Amada also previewed its new VPSS3i software at EuroBLECH – a new integrated software package that provides one interface for customers, whether they are welding, bending or using a punch or laser.

“It is about keeping ahead of the game for our customers,” concluded Matt Wood. “If they are not competitive, we cannot prosper. It’s an evolutionary partnership and our motto is ‘For Your Success’.”
