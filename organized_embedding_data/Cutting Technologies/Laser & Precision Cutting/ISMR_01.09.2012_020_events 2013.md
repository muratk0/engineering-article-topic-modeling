The majority of Amada’s machines are sold to second and third tier suppliers and subcontractors – they cover a vast array of businesses from aerospace and cabinetry to heavy machinery, for example. 60% of the machines that it sells come with Amada’s own finance package for customers, which can be re-structured if needed.

The pre-owned machine segment has also been successful for Amada, particularly during the economic downturn in 2008/2009.

Pre-owned re-conditioned machines come with Amada’s warranty and service back-up.

“Many of the customers who took preowned machines from us are now coming back to us with interest in a new machine purchase – they have got used to our machines and our technology. We are certainly seeing more and more work that had been outsourced coming back to UK shores,” both men told ISMR.

## Process and technology trends

“There is still quite a lot of panel work around for entry-level press brakes,” added Wood, “but we also now see an increase in customers wanting to reduce set-up times on the machines, adding drawings so that you can create 3D images, more advanced back gauges to increase flexibility for more complex components etc. We have also started adding power-saving features to our press brakes i.e. stop-start technology, so that they are only using power when the operators are using the machine. Most of our new press brakes now are around 50% more efficient than our older style machines, which is appealing for customers.

“When it comes to more complex work, tool change and set-up times are significant for customers. We have just brought onto the market a new automatic tool changer (the hybrid drive or HD ATC) which will load all the tools onto a machine for an operator whilst he is, for example, collecting new blanks. That can dramatically reduce set-up times and allows massive changes to be made during the whole process, especially when multiple tool changes are being made per shift over the course of a day.”

“These machines also have the ability to convert a drawing to a 3D model so that the operator can rotate the drawing on the screen to familiarise himself with the component and bending process to eliminate any possible mistakes,” Williams added.

A lot of advances in press brake developments have been driven by the fact that press brake operator skill levels have been generally dropping over the last few years.

“To find a skilled press brake operator/setter now is quite tricky,” Wood outlined. “A manufacturer may now just have one skilled press brake operator/setter overseeing four machines and a lower-skilled employee to run the machines once it is set up. All these advanced features on press brakes have been conceived because operator skill levels are dropping so machines are so much easier to run than they were ten years ago, especially for inexperienced operators.”

Advances in laser cutting developments, however, have been more affected by the move towards automation and lights-out operations, Wood told ISMR.

“Laser technology now has reached a point where the machine will be able to check if the cut quality is dropping, can change the parameters, clean the nozzle, adjust the head etc. However, more of that is driven towards full 24-hour operation,” he added.
