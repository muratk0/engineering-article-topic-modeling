# Gouging shield for air plasma systems

[TARİH: 01.04.2016 ISMR]

HYPERTHERM, a manufacturer of plasma, laser and waterjet cutting systems, is now offering a new gouging shield for greater control when gouging with Powermax® air plasma systems.

“The new Max Control Gouging shield is designed for jobs in which the operator needs to remove a very precise amount of metal. The tip of the shield is engineered and machined in a way that allows the operator to create a very shallow gouging profile. The introduction of the Max Control gouging shield expands the offerings of Powermax gouging consumables which includes the existing Max Removal Gouging shield and HyAccess™ extended gouging consumables. Max Removal shields are designed to remove a large amount of metal and leave a deeper gouging profile while HyAccess provides the extra reach needed when gouging in hard to reach or confined spaces,” it told ISMR.

Max Control, Max Removal and HyAccess Gouging consumables are available for Powermax systems using a Duramax™ or Duramax Hyamp™ torch. This includes the Powermax65® through to the Powermax125® and older systems with Duramax retrofit torches such as the Powermax600®, Powermax900® and Powermax1650®.

“We are continuously investing in market research and engineering development to ensure we meet the needs of our customers,” added Brenda Mahoney, a torch and consumable product manager. “The result is eleven different patented torch styles for our Powermax systems alone plus an array of unique consumable offerings that make it easy for people to choose the right tool for each job.”

KEMPPI new A3 and A5 MIG systems offer welding mechanisation equipment for straight line or orbital MIG/MAG welding applications.

“The A3 MIG System is a durable and simple straight rail solution. Powered from a reachable battery, the A3 torch carriage is a low cost solution to mechanise longitudinal welding and cutting process. A5 MIG Systems offer integrated welding carriage solutions for either straight rail or orbital MIG welding, incorporating a weaving unit, remote control, plus either straight rail or guide ring sets for orbital MIG welding,” said the company.

Upgrading from manual to mechanised welding, said Kemppi, means a substantial improvement in welding quality and productivity through faster welding speed, greater duty cycles and increased deposition rates.

## Collaborative automation

FANUC EXHIBITED its wide range of robotics and automation solutions at the MACH 2016 exhibition recently (Birmingham, UK). For the first time in the UK, visitors to the event saw the FANUC CR-35iA, a heavylifting collaborative robot which enables humans and machines to work together without the need for safety fences. The robot, which can lift up to 35kg, is the first of many collaborative robots to be rolled out by FANUC across the globe.

In addition, FANUC’s smaller collaborative robot, the CR-7iA, made an appearance. The robot is capable of lifting payloads of up to 7kg, giving manufacturers access to collaborative automation while working with lighter materials. FANUC’s ROBODRILL was demonstrated in different scenarios including DDR-T, ROBODRILL with robot automation, ROBODRILL 4-axes impeller machining with servo door, the ROBODRILL D21MiA5 and the ROBODRILL Robot Cell. MACH visitors also saw FANUC's ROBOCUT solutions in different settings, including its C600iA with an EDM (Electro Discharge Machining) hole drill attachment for one set-up of hole start and cutting, and C400i electric discharge offering plus a PCD tool cutting cell with robot loading of tools.
