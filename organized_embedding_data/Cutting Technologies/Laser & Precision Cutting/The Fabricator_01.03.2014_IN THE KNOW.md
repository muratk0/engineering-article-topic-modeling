21st Annual ALAW: Advanced Laser Applications Workshop

June 2-4, 2014 | Plymouth (Detroit), Michigan.

The theme for this year’s ALAW is Innovation + Affordability = Profiting With Lasers. As the use of lasers becomes more widespread because of their affordability and efficiency, product designers and engineers need to gain familiarity with the applicability of the laser welding process. They also must learn how to design components, assemblies, and systems for successful implementation. This workshop is designed for job shops, manufacturers, suppliers, and anyone interested in learning more about laser material processing systems to reduce costs, improve quality, and provide flexible laser manufacturing.

2014 Financial Ratios & Operational Benchmarking Survey Now Open

The Fabricators & Manufacturers Association Intl. (FMA) encourages industry participation in the 2014 Financial Ratios & Operational Benchmarking Survey. The results will provide comprehensive data for company owners and managers in the metal forming and fabricating industry.

What Pushes the Canadian Manufacturing Community?

Dr. Chris Kuehl FMA Economic Analyst

Summer Mfg. Camps

FABTECH trade show

Continuing Education

Networking Events

The issue with Canadian manufacturing is that it tends to get lost in the shuffle. The bedrock of the national economy has long been commodities — everything from oil and gas to food, minerals, and lumber. The second-largest contributor has been the service economy in general and then comes the manufacturing sector.

To complicate matters further, there is Canada’s connection to the U.S. Much of the national economy is dependent on what happens south of the border, and many of the country’s manufacturers are directly under the sway of their American owners or they depend on sales to the U.S. for their survival. As goes the U.S., so goes Canada — at least to some extent.

The country’s leadership is not all that happy with this state of affairs and would like to reduce this dependency on the U.S., which has prompted yet another major effort to bring the national infrastructure into the discussion. One provision in Canada’s federal budget pushes more money into infrastructure build, and that does appear to be beneficial to those manufacturers that feed into the system, but there are loads of caveats when it comes to these big programs. Among the ideas is another bridge connecting Windsor to Detroit, as well as upgrades to the nation’s seaports and airports. Some roads have been on the drawing board for years, and then there are all the other infrastructure-related projects that involve telecommunication and energy distribution. The list is far longer than the budget will support, which ultimately means competition among projects to get approved.

The fear in some circles is that this competition will invite the kind of scandals that affected Quebec as it tried to allocate resources for construction projects. The intent is to change the process to avoid that taint, but this is always easier said than done. Manufacturers are not directly connected to these projects, but they will build the machines and make the products that go into these efforts. The stakes are high as this is the kind of work that can sustain growth for a long while, but it is also the kind of business that has as much to do with political clout as anything else.
