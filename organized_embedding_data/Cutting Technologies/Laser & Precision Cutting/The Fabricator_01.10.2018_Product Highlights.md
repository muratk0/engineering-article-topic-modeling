Laser fume extraction system includes five filtration stages

ULT LLC offers the LAS 1200 laser fume extraction system. It uses five different filtration stages, which, along with the use of efficient filter media, helps ensure a separation rate higher 99.9 percent, says the company.

In the prefilter and main filter stages, solid particles down to the nano-range are separated; filtration or adsorption of gases and vapors is executed in an activated carbon filter using a fan with a large pressure reserve.

The unit is suitable for the capture and filtration of dry, noncombustible dusts occurring during laser processing applications including cutting, welding, marking, and ablation. The fume extraction system can be used as a stationary or mobile unit.

Parts cleaning system designed to give media full access to parts

The Mefo-Box parts cleaning system from Metallform offers an open structure that gives cleaning media, ultrasonic waves, and spray jets unobstructed access to parts. It provides good draining to help reduce drying time and ensure uniformly dry parts, even in bulk material processes, the company states. The design also minimizes the carryover of cleaning media.

The unit is manufactured from stainless steels rounds with electrolytic polished surfaces. The absence of closed edges and corners prevents the formation of dirt traps and residues in the container, preventing recontamination of parts.

8-kW laser heads added to coil blanking system

LaserCoil Technologies LLC has introduced 8-kW fiber laser head featuring a proprietary Z-axis design integrated directly into the laser blanking system. This feature allows the laser head to adjust rapidly to height variations in the material, maintaining consistent stand-off for higher speed when cutting materials that are less than perfectly flat.

The laser blanking systems can be sold with a single 8-kW laser head or with multiple heads that share the cutting workload. Additionally, the systems offer multimode capability, allowing users to choose either feed index mode (stop/start) or continuous mode.

With 8-kW systems, simple shapes in material thicknesses from 0.02 to 0.03 in. can be cut at speeds approaching 7 ft./sec., making laser blanking a suitable choice for volumes of less than 100,000 per year.

Torch booster delivers 150-PSI natural gas for HVOF thermal spray

G-Tec offers its fourth-generation TB-HVOF torch booster, which raises utility natural gas service to 150 PSI for high-velocity oxy-fuel (HVOF) thermal spray. New features include a variable 1- to 150-PSI pressure controller and, according to the company, an 80-percent reduction in annual maintenance expenses.

The unit connects to a building’s natural gas supply. Coating quality is equal to propane/propylene, according to the manufacturer. The torch booster is compact, quiet, and fits near the spray booth without dedicated ventilation or site preparation.

Machine automates cutting in small footprint

HGG’S new UPC 450 Ship Profiler helps increase cutting and prefabrication productivity by eliminating the need to cut ship stiffeners and spools by hand. Production automation combines ship stiffener cutting and 3-D profile cutting with optional tube cutting. Its small footprint helps save floor space.

The machine offers full process cutting integration through the ProCAM soft-ware suite, integrating thermal cutting ProCAD design, ProGRAM work preparation coordination, and 3-D plasma cutting intelligence. It delivers precision stiffener and tubular spool cutting that reduces fitting and welding time, the company states.

The machine cuts ship stiffener profiles from flat bars, bulbs, and angles, as well as cutting spools from tubular pipes. The cutting cell includes a cutting trolley that controls all movement and a patented cutting head and biaxial cutting torch. Two fume-extraction modules capture and dispose of plasma dust, smoke, and fumes.

Brazing system removes flame variability

The Harris Products Group has introduced the Perfect Flame

®

system for repetitive, high-volume, hand-held brazing applications. It includes torches, tips, flow control devices, and software.

With the built-in software, companies can duplicate flame settings across an organization. The software measures flame chemistry and flow rates and calculates temperatures and BTU values to determine and control the best flame in production environments. These flame attributes then are saved into the system as presets and assigned to a project or part type. The brazing flame can be replicated repeatedly.

By producing an engineered flame with the correct BTU setting, the system ensures that the proper amount of heat is being transferred, which prevents heat input problems and decreases the amount of time spent on each brazed joint.

The unit also features software that collects data on each operator, such as flame setup, gas usage, and torch-on time. Data is presented on a dashboard so that management can monitor brazing operations. This allows companies to apply Six Sigma principles–define, measure, analyze, improve, and control–to the brazing process.

Two models are available. The ST (single torch) allows the braze operator to toggle among five different settings. The MT (multiple torch) is suitable for high-production operations in which individual operators are brazing the same or different joints.
