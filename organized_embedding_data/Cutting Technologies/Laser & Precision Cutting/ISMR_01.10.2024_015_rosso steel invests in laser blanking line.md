# Rosso Steel invests in laser blanking line

[TARİH: 01.10.2024 ISMR]

Czech steel service centre, Rosso Steel, has ordered an LBL 2.21 laser blanking line (for body panels) from Schuler.

The LBL 2.21 (for body panels) will be installed at Rosso Steel's Zaječí site and is the first Schuler laser blanking line in the Czech Republic.

The new line can handle coil weights of up to 30 tons, strip widths from 400mm to 2,150mm and strip thicknesses from 0.6mm to 3.0mm at a maximum strip speed of 60m/minute. The laser blanking line also has two laser cutting heads, each with a rated output of 4kW, and operates at a maximum cutting speed of 100m/minute.

"Blanking lines with lasers are particularly suitable for production processes with frequent product changes. The system can be used to produce both outer skin blanks and structural parts in high product quality. It is precisely these flexible production conditions that are in demand at Rosso Steel," explained Schuler.

"The laser blanking line also offers optimum material utilisation, which ensures costeffectiveness and efficiency. This is achieved by, among other things, optimised nesting on the material web, the elimination of separating strips between the blanks, the combination of different parts on the material web and the positioning of the blanks at different angles," it continued.

Rosso Steel is one of the largest family-owned companies in the Czech Republic. Its two service centres in Zaječí and Mirošov are equipped with modern, partially automated technology. Ultra-high-strength materials of up to 1,200MPa are processed.

The company has been operating for over 33 years, purchasing, processing and selling an extensive portfolio of different flat steel products. Every year, up to 180,000 tons of steel coil, strip and sheet are supplied to over 80 regular customers in Central and Eastern Europe. Its focus is on the automotive sector, but the products are also used in the building industry, metal construction and electrical engineering.

“As the laser blanking line eliminates die-related downtimes or increased changeover times, and the system usually only stops briefly when a coil is changed, this technology usually has a higher OEE (Overall Equipment Effectiveness) than a conventional cutting system. Production of the new system is scheduled to start at the beginning of November 2025," Schuler confirmed.
