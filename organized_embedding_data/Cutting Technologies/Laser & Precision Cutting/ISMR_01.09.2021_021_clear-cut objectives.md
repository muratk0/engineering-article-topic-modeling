This includes the purchase of new fibre laser cutting and press brake bending systems from Swiss manufacturer, Bystronic.

## New equipment purchases

Global Steel's business model is based upon product quality, JIT delivery and a repeatable service experience. A Bystronic BySmart 3kW fibre laser cutting machine was purchased in 2020 by sales manager, Sean Larkin, to satisfy the needs of its customers. It was the first of its type in Ireland, according to Bystronic. To match the new increased capacity, another press brake bending system was ordered from Bystronic, with a 4-metre fold capacity.

In June 2021, Global Steel placed an order for a second Bystronic fibre laser machine which is due for delivery in September. This 4kW machine will significantly increase the company's ability to penetrate the contract cutting and folding market, it told ISMR.

The BySmart Fiber laser cutting system is designed for high part output and can process thin to medium sheet metal thicknesses. Installation of the laser took place in March 2020 and, in August that year, Kevin Power was appointed as Global Steel's business development manager. He has particular expertise in the development of laser cutting, fabrication and assembly of high-precision steel components for engineering; civil engineering; construction; pharmaceuticals and chemical industries.

The introduction of the fibre laser machine presented a technical solution for the expansion of the business, particularly against a background shortage of qualified welders. The company is currently re-organising to expand laser run-time by introducing shift patterns in its laser department. These plans for additional shifts will significantly increase capacity to service the downstream capabilities of the business.

Led by Global Steel's production manager, Paul Cullen, training was provided to a group of highly motivated individuals who were cross-trained on the key requirements of this department, namely drawing, laser machine operation and folding.

## Preparing for growth

A significant new factory extension has been completed to provide the additional floorspace necessary to prepare for growth at Global Steel. This space allows a re-organised layout to cater for the laser machine, relocation of the fabrication shops and the creation of a material storage area.

"This layout achieves a seamless flow of material and product consistent with the Lean fundamental of minimising transport time within the process," the company explained.

In addition, Global Steel is currently pursuing a site which is ear-marked for the future expansion necessary to fulfil its plan in two to three years' time.

Global Steel's enhanced capabilities are targeted to better meet the needs of its existing customers as well as increase its customer base. Hugh O'Byrne, Global Steel's general manager, emphasized that expansion with new customers will focus on laser cutting and folding, whilst providing complementary services for fabrication and powder coating/painting where required.

In the waterjet arena, the company can cut heavier/specialised materials of up to 150mm and will target customers with these specific needs. In fabrication, Global Steel plans to primarily target customers requiring fabrication who have repeat products which the company believes will lend itself to automation, where possible.

Specific skills and expertise in high-technology manufacturing are well-established in Ireland. Global Steel management believes that its "track record in providing products and services, ability to meet deadlines and policy of customer care are its greatest strengths."

Confident of future growth, it looks forward to presenting new capabilities to existing and new customers in the years ahead.
