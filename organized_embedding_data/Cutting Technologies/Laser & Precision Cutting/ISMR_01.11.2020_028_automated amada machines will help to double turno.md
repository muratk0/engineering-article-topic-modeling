# Automated AMADA machines will help to double turnover at Wrekin Sheet Metal.

[TARİH: 01.11.2020 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS: “This investment in manufacturing technology has led to a 150% increase in punching and laser cutting capacity, largely thanks to automation.”

Telford-based Wrekin Sheet Metal, in the UK, has invested £1.1 million in AMADA automated punch press and laser cutting technology during the UK’s pandemic lockdown.

An AMADA EMZ-3612MII electric punch press with AS-III MP300/40 tower and PR-III UL part remover, alongside an AMADA VENTIS-3015AJ fibre laser cutter with AS LUL3015 tower, were installed in August-September 2020. By leveraging the speed and capacity of these new machines, the company is aiming to double its turnover to £5.5 million in the coming 12 months.

Specialising in subcontract sheetmetal fabrication, Wrekin can design and manufacture a range of parts from simple mounting brackets through to complex multi-part products for prototyping or batch production. Founded in 2002, the company was subject to a management buyout in 2009, with Claire Robinson and Simon Orpe taking the reins as managing director and sales director, respectively.

## An eye on growth

In 2013, Wrekin Sheet Metal relocated to a 40,000 square foot factory in Telford, providing the foundation for growth under its new leadership. One of the first machines to arrive at the facility was a new AMADA laser cutting machine, and Wrekin’s philosophy of investing in the latest AMADA technology has continued ever since.

“We see ourselves as an extension of our customers’ manufacturing facilities, with AMADA machines central to this offer,” stated Wrekin’s sales director, Simon Orpe. “At present, we are extremely busy serving the refrigeration sector, predominantly with parts for chillers used by bars, restaurants and hotels. We also provide a range of components to the retail sector, including major supermarkets, while the supply of gym equipment solutions and investment casting dipping tanks are further examples of our current workload. Providing steel doors and screens for the vacant property security industry also keeps us busy.”

With an expanding list of customers across the UK and mainland Europe, Wrekin Sheet Metal could see the need for investment, particularly with the recent arrival of a major new account.

“We see Britain as a manufacturing powerhouse and we want to help drive it - there is plenty of work out there,” added Mr Orpe. “With this thought in mind – along with our new contract wins – we revisited our entire manufacturing capacity, both machines and people. Throughout the lockdown period, we were hiring new staff, from qualified setters to front-office management.”

## Industry 4.0 in focus

With a rapidly expanding order book, Wrekin Sheet Metal decided the time was right to invest in new automated machinery.

“We’ve been using AMADA machines since day one,” explained Mr Orpe. “Although we assessed a range of machines from different suppliers, we favoured returning to AMADA as it offered solutions that were compatible with our shift to Industry 4.0. The new machines ticked a lot of boxes regarding data communication using the AMADA V-factory software.”

Wrekin Sheet Metal first decided it would move towards Industry 4.0 as part of its strategy to help combat the effects of Brexit.

“One of our major selling points is customer communication, and Industry 4.0 is very much that,” confirmed Mr Orpe. “We recently set about investing in the necessary network and IT infrastructure that would be able to gather information for our customers. We typically have up to 400 different live parts in progress on our shop floor at any one time, and we want to reach a position where our customers can log in and see the status of their components.

“This capability, which is currently being developed in partnership with our MRP software supplier, will really set us apart from our competitors. Industry 4.0 compatibility was therefore very important when it came to investing in the new machines,” he concluded.

## Efficiency and turnover

With the arrival of the automated AMADA EMZ-3612MII electric punch press and AMADA VENTIS-3015AJ fibre laser cutting machine – as well as the company’s investment in a powder-coating plant and new personnel – Wrekin Sheet Metal has spent circa £1.5 million since the COVID-19 lockdown was imposed in March 2020.

“When the pandemic first arrived, we very quickly got involved with the UK’s Ventilator Challenge, which recently drew to a close with nearly 5000 ventilators being produced in total,” said Simon Orpe. “This not only kept the cogs turning, so that we could remain open during lockdown, but was one of our proudest moments.”

Proposed investment in the new automated AMADA machines also prompted Wrekin Sheet Metal to redesign its shop floor to further improve production flow. Such has been the benefits of both the new technology and layout that the company is expecting to double its turnover, in the coming 12 months, to £5.5 million. Wrekin confirmed that its investment in AMADA manufacturing technology has led to a 150% increase in punching and laser cutting capacity, largely thanks to the automation.

“For instance, with the EMZ-3612MII electric punch press being fully automated from loading to unloading, we could get 72 hours of consecutive manufacturing if we had the right product to go on it, which is incredible,” explained Simon Orpe.

The AMADA VENTIS-3015AJ fibre laser cutting machine is also providing Wrekin Sheet Metal with 40% more efficiency, according to AMADA, thanks to its singlediode module 4kW oscillator and proprietary LBC (Locus Beam Control) technology. Simon Orpe confirmed that the fibre technology not only provides a speed increase over the company’s former CO2 laser, but reduces power consumption by 50%.

Wrekin processes material, on a daily basis, ranging from 0.5 to 20mm in thickness (mainly mild steel, but also stainless steel and aluminium). Because of the company’s investment in its first fibre laser cutting system, reflective materials such as brass and copper can now also be processed.

Wrekin Sheet Metal also offers a bespoke design service supported by an ISO9001- accredited engineering department which provides complete quality-assured solutions. In addition, the company holds approved supplier status in the aerospace, construction, security, retail and heating markets.

“We’re staying focused and positive about the situation out there,” concluded Mr Orpe. “You might think that sectors such as refrigeration for refreshments are quite vulnerable, but lately we have seen a sudden spike in orders from this industry. The confidence levels of these customers and many others has completely transformed from where it was back in March and April, and with our new AMADA machines we’re ready to capitalise on this.”

## Transfluid outlines a novel and customised method of producing the tubes required for exhaust manifolds.

Transfluid Maschinenbau GmbH produces tube bending and tube processing machines. It has been developing customer-focused technologies for tube processing since 1988 and offers tailor-made solutions incorporating equipment and machines for the automotive, energy and shipbuilding sectors and the manufacturing of medical equipment. Its bending systems are designed to offer tailored and high-performance production.

“Customers often request tube processing machines with specifications that are particularly challenging. And each time, our engineers will work hard to find the best possible solutions,” it told ISMR.

An example of this is a real project on which transfluid worked, requiring production of a stainless steel exhaust manifold with a very complex shape, which needed complex bending, forming and calibration of the ends.

“The specific challenge was to just manufacture the tubes needed for one manifold, so that the client could take four or six pieces to always obtain one manifold in the welding process, even for large-scale production. The result: when one single tube fails in the automated welding unit, the cell receives a signal and produces one extra single tube. That´s one-piece flow….,” explained the German tube specialist.

## Clean cut solutions

The individual requirements of the customer also included the production of a sequence of at least four tubes that were not the same, without pre-cutting the tubes. The automated cell included a loading magazine for long tubes, a device for the orientation of the weld seam and a bending system with five bending levels capable of cutting the finished component, when the bending process is complete.

In addition to this, components were calibrated internally and externally using finger expansion. The final complex bending shape was put in an axial combination machine at the end to finalise end-forming of the opposite end.

“This machine can perform the final exact cut in an orbital and chip-less way. This makes it possible to re-shape the tube axially with one clamping process,” added the company.

The loading magazine can take an entire bundle of tube ‘in one go’. The tubes are then separated by using eddy current seam detection to recognize the welding seam. The welding seam is positioned and the tube is fed into the bending machine by a linear handling system.

“As these components are very compact, it is possible to produce up to 20 units from one tube. There are up to four bending levels, plus one cutting level, available to bend the individual components. After the bending part of the process, the tube is cut in a controlled manner by long-lasting carbide blades. The result is a clean cut and a component with a perfectly round tube end,” outlined the manufacturer.

A robot takes the tube and uses an expander to calibrate the tube. This is necessary because the cut on the tube is very close to the bend. With this last step, processing of the component side is completed and the component is placed in the deburring device for a smooth cut. After the deburring process, a robot conveys the component to a combination forming machine for further processing on the opposite tube end. A robot moves the tube in compound clamps to the combination machine for forming, calibration, trimming with a chip-less orbital method and axial forming asymmetric geometry in three steps, with very tight tolerances and complex results.

“The asymmetrical and very challenging forming operation is not a problem for our engineers. Because the forming of the tube ends depends upon the tube processing, the forming is done using an additional clamping technique to reduce the amount of tools needed,” added transfluid.

## An established rolling technique

At this point, a robot will place the four different components in separate containers. A separate unit will manufacture an EGR tube in parallel with complex 28 x 1 bend geometries. A flange with a 45mm diameter has to be formed on this tube.

During this process, the longitudinal seam should not be damaged and the flare should not split. To complete this delicate step, Transfluid starts its established rolling technique. Using this technique, maintains Transfluid, it is possible to form tubes that have already been bent and have a very short clamping section.
