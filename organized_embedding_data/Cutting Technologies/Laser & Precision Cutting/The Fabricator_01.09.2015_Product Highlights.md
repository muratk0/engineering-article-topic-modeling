PAPR system added to ADF welding helmet

The e3000 PAPR system from Optrel Inc. now is available with its e684 welding helmet with autodarkening filter (ADF). The helmet features Adaptive Shade Autopilot technology, which automatically adjusts the ADF to the appropriate shade level between 5 and 13 during welding. As the intensity of the arc changes, so does the shade level—hands-free and in high-definition viewing. The PAPR offers two-channel air distribution inside the helmet for a high level of comfort, as well as a HEPA filter design.

Fiber laser offers consumable-free metal part marking

REA Elektronik has developed the REA JET FL fiber laser, which permanently marks metal and plastics. The system uses no consumables and offers long operational life, the company states. The high-resolution print provides machine-readable codes for product traceability. Typical applications include embossing and color change marking (annealing) of metals, layer removal in day-to-night design, and marking by using laser transfer foils.

The laser can be used for direct part marking (DPM) of metal components. The high beam quality and energy density allow short marking times and small-character readability. Texts and graphics may be freely designed, and the use of TrueType fonts and UTF-8 coding allows the display of international characters.

New line of surface conditioning belts introduced

CGW-Camel Grinding Wheels has added 12 new belts to its surface conditioning belt line. They are available in brown (coarse) 120 grits, maroon (medium) 180 grits, and blue (very fine) 320 grits. Dimensions are 3 by 21 in., 1 by 72 in., 4 by 54 in., and 4 by 72 in.

X-flex and low-stretch backings are available. The X-flex-backed belts, for hand-held tools, are made with more flexibility to reduce the probability of cracking. The low-stretch-backed belts, intended for stationary machines, are made to hold their dimensions and do not stretch.
