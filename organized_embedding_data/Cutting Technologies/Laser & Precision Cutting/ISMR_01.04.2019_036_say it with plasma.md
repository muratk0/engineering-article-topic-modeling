# Say it with plasma

[TARİH: 01.04.2019 ISMR]

TODAY, PLASMA CUTTING is one of the most important thermal cutting processes in the metalworking industry. It has also improved its cost-efficiency in recent years. However, the sound level emitted during the cutting process has presented a particular challenge, until now. Kjellberg Finsterwalde’s team of developers has succeeded in developing Silent Cut technology, significantly reducing sound levels.

Kjellberg Finsterwalde's development activities for plasma cutting focused mainly on achievable cut quality and costs per cutting metre. Using these improvements, the quality of cut edges and structural elements (particularly holes and inner contours) as well as productivity could be increased by up to 45%, the cutting specialist told ISMR. Today, developments such as Contour Cut and Contour Cut Speed technologies for Kjellberg plasma cutting machines in the HiFocus and Smart Focus series determine the performance of the technology as far as mild steel sheet with a thickness of between three and 50mm are concerned.

## Keeping it down

During plasma cutting, noise emissions can amount to significantly more than 100dB(A) resulting in a high noise exposure in the direct as well as the extended working area of the plasma cutting machine. Noise emissions at this level clearly exceed the admissible trigger threshold of 85dB(A), requiring ear protection measures pursuant to occupational health and safety regulations. One premise of the development work at Kjellberg Finsterwalde was to reduce noise whilst preserving high cut quality and cutting speed, if possible.

"To reduce the sound level, different primary and secondary measures can be taken. Secondary measures are, among others, to use an enclosure for the plasma machine or the plasma torch or to cut under a water cover. In contrast, primary measures are focused on reducing the direct sound emission from the plasma torch. From the user's perspective this requires minimum effort, or no effort at all. Therefore, our development activities focused on the consumables of the plasma torch and the process parameters. In particular, the nozzle was improved in terms of fluid dynamics. As a result, we were able to reduce the sound level during the cutting of 12mm mild steel at 130A by 10dB(A)," commented the manufacturer.

"Due to the logarithmic relationship between the sound pressure and the sound pressure level, the reduction of the sound pressure level by 10dB(A) is perceived as a halving of the loudness. Because we have additionally succeeded in reducing the whistling sound typical for the highly-constricted plasma cutting process, the perceived sound reduction seems, in part, even higher." [dB(A)]

Kjellberg Finsterwalde now offers its new plasma cutting technology, Silent Cut, as a solution for reducing the sound exposure during plasma cutting. When cutting mild steel with a material thickness of between four and 30mm and currents ranging between 60 and 160 A, the sound pressure level can be reduced by up to 10dB(A)*. Silent Cut includes modifications not only in the nozzle design but also in the cutting parameters.

"The reduction amounts to max. 10dB(A) depending upon the amperage and the material thickness. The reduction of the sound pressure in Pascal by more than 60% is even more impressive. Depending upon the frequency, this reduction is perceived as a significantly higher sound reduction. The cut quality and cutting speed of the new soundreduced Silent Cut technology are comparable to current technologies with highly constricted plasma arcs. Therefore, Silent Cut makes an important contribution to safety at work – with consistently high cut quality. The technology can also be used for existing plasma systems."

"We achieved high quality i.e. a low rectangularity and inclination tolerance, low dross formation, smooth surfaces and a small heat-affected zone as well as a reduction of the sound pressure level (illustrated below). Small differences can occur as far as contour elements are concerned. The cutting speeds and therefore the costs per cutting metre are comparable," said the company.

*Sound pressure level measurement at a distance of 1m from the plasma machine; single, short-time noise peaks are not taken into account.
