Fiber laser system offers 20 kW

Eagle Fiber Laser has released what it says is the world’s first 20-kW fiber laser with reliability for 24/7 operation. It has the capability to cut ¾-in. steel at 120 to 160 IPM, faster than most plasma cutters.

The key to mastering ultrahigh laser power is the patented eVa cutting head. The most common cause of optical failure is contamination from external sources or motorized motion of lenses. According to the company, the cutting head eliminates these contaminants to extend head life significantly, even at 20 kW.

The machine offers a top speed of 13,780 and acceleration of 6G to cut thin sheet at unmatched speeds, reports the manufacturer. It is available in North America from Fairmont Machinery.

Software helps move 3D designs to production faster

IronCAD has released IRONCAD 2021, designed to help create designs and production drawings quickly. New capabilities include quick symmetrical sizing of shapes with a right-click drag, quick access to handle values attached to the cursor, and more powerful snap options.

EtherCAT terminals offer compact drive technology in metal housings

The new ELM72xx EtherCAT terminals from Beckhoff are fully functional servo drives in robust metal housings that deliver an output current of up to 16 amps at 48 VDC for the power supply.

The metal housings provide optimum heat dissipation even at high output power, as well as shielding against electrical interference. The new servo terminals can connect directly to other EtherCAT terminals as an integrated component of the Beckhoff I/O system. The comprehensive functionality includes the direct connection of the motor, feedback, and brake via the connector front end; an integrated absolute value interface; and One Cable Technology.

Cladding cell helps simplify overlay welding

Fronius has developed the Compact Cladding Cell for overlay welding on components with a diameter of up to 1 m, maximum weight of 2,500 kg, and maximum height of 1.9 m. In addition, the system enables the surfacing of a variety of bore geometries (cylindrical, conical, multidiameter) from a bore diameter of 25 mm.

The cell platform measures less than 6 sq. m. The entire system uses a single power supply. It’s available with the GTAW hot wire process or the SpeedClad Twin process (two tungsten electrodes and two wires).

The horizontal rotary table helps ensure precise part positioning. The stand arm boom allows horizontal pendulum movements and enables the welding torches to be fitted.

Software helps maximize weld analysis capabilities

Olympus has released WeldSight companion PC software for the OmniScan X3 phased array (PA) flaw detector. Compatible with conventional UT, PA, and time-of-flight diffraction acquired data, the software enables inspectors to perform thorough postinspection analyses that comply with validation requirements of international standards.

Experienced PAUT inspectors can perform advanced validation of indications using the software’s weld analysis tools. Its screening capabilities reduce the need for cross-validation. Inspectors can characterize and orient flaws faster, even in complex geometries, by merging distinct scan files and using multiple view options to examine the weld in detail from all sides, the company reports.

Welding inverter handles GTAW, SMAW

Weldcote, a division of Zika Group, has introduced the Striker 160, a compact, dual-voltage, single-phase welding inverter for GTAW and SMAW. Weighing 21.4 lbs., the machine automatically identifies the power grid voltage and operates accordingly.

The unit features power factor corrector to help increase power efficiency, reduce input power requirements, and stabilize the welding current. As a safety precaution, it has a voltage reduction device that reduces the voltage across the outputs when idling.

Designed to work with input voltages from 98 to 265 V, the unit is suitable for use with generators.

Automated system offers swarf handling, oil separation, filtration

Accustrip offers a compact and complete swarf handling, oil separation, highpressure cooling management system designed to help minimize downtime by automatically removing chips, recycling, and providing coolant free of impurities.

The company supplies installations of all sizes, from small machine shops with a single CNC machine to large plants. The system can transport tons of chips and coolant from far-flung CNC machines to a central plant, then separate chips and particles from the coolant and transport the chips back to the foundry to be cast and machined again.

The system handles chips efficiently, making it possible to switch between different materials and sort them out to the right container. The system also removes lubricant from the coolant without the need for slow processes in enormous tanks. This is done in the same flow, taking advantage of the oil’s natural propensity to aggregate, so after it is collected at a high speed in a patented capillary system, it can be routed out of the flow.

Impurities down to 15 microns are filtered off with no outside intervention. Filters are flushed clean with coolant and compressed air, and impurities are collected and discharged.
