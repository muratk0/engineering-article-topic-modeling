TRUMPF unveils latest in automated parts sorting technology

TRUMPF’s SortMaster Station removes parts from the skeleton and delivers them to the SortMaster Vision for bin and pallet placement.

Images: Dan Davis

As lasers grow more powerful and faster than their predecessors just a few years ago, many metal fabricating companies are stuck with the same issue: removing laser-cut parts from the skeleton in a timely manner.

Machine tool manufacturers have done an excellent job over the years figuring out ways to deliver sheet metal to the laser cutting chamber, removing the laser-cut parts and skeleton from the bed without losing any parts along the way, and delivering a new sheet for the next job—all without human involvement. But those parts still have to come out to feed downstream processes or the shipping department, and for many shops, that means people need to manually shake, break out, and stack those parts and then dispose of the skeleton. Most people don’t like that job, but it’s necessary.

TRUMPF has unveiled parts sorting technology for shops looking to make life easier for the people in charge of separating laser-cut parts from a skeleton—or to remove humans from the process entirely. On April 28, it ran its new SortMaster Station and SortMaster Vision, both of which were connected to a TruLaser 5030 12-kW laser cutting machine, for a select group of metal fabricators and the trade press at its Smart Factory facility in Hoffman Estates, Ill.

In addressing the attendees, Kartik Iyer, the Smart Factory director, said that even with all of the advanced fabricating technology available to metal fabricators, parts spend more time as work-in-process than they do having value added to them. Their time in a laser cutting machine, on a press brake, or in a welding fixture is a lot shorter than most metal fabricating management teams realize.

"We found that parts spend about 80% of the time involved with indirect processes, which our customers don’t get paid for," Iyer said. Those laser-cut parts waiting to be removed from a skeleton are an obstacle to getting them to the customer in the most efficient manner possible.

The SortMaster Station is designed to remove parts from the metal, discard the skeleton, and then present the parts for sorting and stacking. TRUMPF’s LiftMaster Compact loading/unloading automation system transfers the laser-cut sheet to the sorting machine. Once inside, clamps grab the short end of the sheet and shake it to break any nano joints holding the parts in the skeleton. The chamber’s ceiling is lowered close to the sheet at this time to prevent the parts from being tossed around indiscriminately. At the end of the process, the system can detect if any further shaking is needed to break parts from the skeleton, said Claudio Santopietro, TRUMPF’s Smart Factory consulting manager.

Separate parts are then sent to the sorting area. The skeleton does not make the trip outside of the chamber; it’s deposited underneath the SortMaster Station for later removal.

For companies interested in automated sorting, TRUMPF offers the SortMaster Vision, developed in cooperation with Intrinsic, a software and AI robotics company affiliated with Google.

Upon parts delivery, a robot uses cameras to scan their placement and assess where bins and pallets are inside the cell. At this point, the system can determine what parts need addressing first, such as those that might be slightly on top of another coming out of the Sort-Master Station.

The system’s software autonomously calculates the robot’s motion plan, including the gripping points. Using AI and information from the CAD files, the SortMaster Vision runs simulations to figure out the best way to grasp, pick up, move, and deposit the parts in the bins and pallets. A machine programmer no longer has to figure out the best way to handle automated sorting.

Chris Golombek, an Intrinsic account executive, called this the emergence of "physical AI," where AI-assisted robots can perceive an environment and plan appropriate movements to accomplish a task. He called it the missing piece for unlocking automation’s potential in high-mix manufacturing.

Santopietro said the automated sorting system can handle parts up to 25 mm (0.98 in.) thick. During the demonstration for the metal fabricators, the machine processed parts 16 mm (0.63 in.) and 3 mm (0.12 in.) thick. Nests are required to have at least 14 mm (0.55) along the short ends of the sheet to accommodate the clamps inside the SortMaster Station.

He added that the smallest part that can be processed is 50 mm (1.97 in.) by 50 mm and the largest is 1.2 m (42.24 in.) by 1 m (39.37 in.). The heaviest part it can process is 120 kg (264.56 lbs.).

Iyer said that this automated parts sorting system does not require jobs to be sent through in a particular order to accommodate a stringent parts sorting process. Sorting can be organized according to various parameters, including kitting requirements.

The SortMaster Station and Sort-Master Vision will be available to metal fabricators in North America later in 2026.

Fabritex receives Georgia Small Business Rock Star Award

Fabritex, Hartwell, Ga., has been named one of five 2026 Small Business Rock Stars by the Georgia Department of Economic Development and the Georgia Economic Developers Association. The second-generation, family-owned company specializes in custom industrial metal fabrication, wire carriers, and stem packs.

"Small businesses power the Georgia economy. From rural towns to growing metro areas, these small business Rock Stars demonstrate the remarkable strength and diversity of Georgia’s economy, while highlighting the critical value of local ties," said GDEcD Commissioner Pat Wilson.

The awards recognize the innovations, community engagement, and positive impact of the state’s small businesses. Recipients have fewer than 300 full-time employees, are involved in charitable organizations in their communities, and were founded in the state of Georgia.

Winnebago Manufacturing Co. announces facility expansion

Winnebago Manufacturing Co., a provider of custom fabricated steel products, has announced plans for a major expansion of its production facility in Blue Earth, Minn. The company will add 37,000 sq. ft. to its existing 60,000-sq.-ft. building.

Once complete, the facility will total 97,000 sq. ft. The additional space will introduce new fabricating processes and support expanded welding capacity, with the addition of at least 10 new welding cells.

The family-owned and -operated company offers custom steel fabrication, welding, and metal rolling.

Dakota Bodies plans $21.9 million investment in Missouri

Dakota Bodies, Watertown, S.D., has announced it will invest more than $21.9 million to expand its operations in Liberty, Mo. The project, which will double the size of the company’s manufacturing footprint, is expected to create 172 jobs.

Upgrades to laser cutting technology, metal forming capabilities, and powder coating systems will help speed production and shorten lead times, the company states.

Part of the ATW Truck Equipment family of brands, Dakota Bodies is a custom manufacturer of utility and service truck bodies serving industries including telecommunications, utilities, and rail.

Basic Metals expands operations with 42,000-sq.-ft. addition

Basic Metals Inc., a privately held metals service center based in Germantown, Wis., has announced plans to expand and consolidate its operations.

The company acquired adjacent property in August 2025. As part of the project, it will relocate its corporate headquarters into a 12,000-sq.-ft. office building on that property, which will undergo a full renovation to support administrative, sales, and support functions.

The project also includes construction of a new 42,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility, bringing total operational space to more than 120,000 sq. ft. Construction is scheduled to begin in summer 2026, with completion targeted for early 2027. Basic Metals supplies aluminum, stainless steel, and carbon steel prod-ucts, including cut-to-length sheet, slit coil, and laser-cut parts, to customers in the power generation, appliance manufacturing, HVAC, truck and trailer, marine, defense, and automotive markets.

Marion Manufacturing named 2026 Connecticut Manufacturer of the Year

The Marion Manufacturing Co., Cheshire, Conn., has been named the 2026 Connecticut Manufacturer of the Year by the U.S. Small Business Administration as part of National Small Business Week.

Presented in partnership with the Connecticut Business and Industry Association, this award recognizes outstanding small businesses that demonstrate excellence, innovation, and a commitment to strengthening their communities and the broader economy. The precision metal stamping company is actively engaged in workforce development, industry associations, and community initiatives throughout Connecticut. Through partnerships, internships, and training programs, Marion is committed to building the next generation of manufacturing talent.

Davi strengthens North American operations with new headquarters

Davi Inc. has announced that it will move its North American operations into a new, larger facility located in Carrollton, Texas, near downtown Dallas.

The 18,500-sq.-ft. facility will feature an area with eight machines for live plate and angle rolling machine demonstrations and customer runoffs. An expanded call center will provide customers with support 24 hours a day, five days a week. The new location also will house a warehouse of spare and replacement parts and a machine refurbishment and rebuild department.

Additionally, the facility will include the growing Davi Academy, a customer and distributor training program focused not only on the company’s products but also on the plate and angle rolling processes.

FTI to create 200 jobs in Indiana

Faith Technologies Incorporated is expanding to Pittsboro, Ind., and has begun construction on a 500,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility. The location will support the company’s Excellerate brand of modular electrical assemblies and energy systems.

The new facility will create more than 200 skilled manufacturing and electrical jobs, beginning with openings for foremen, superintendents, and manufacturing leads. Additional positions, including entry-level roles, paid apprenticeships, and support functions, will be added over time.

Virginia Transformer breaks ground on Georgia Transformer expansion

Virginia Transformer has broken ground on a major expansion at its Georgia Transformer manufacturing facility in Rincon, Ga.

The expansion will create about 400 new jobs and increase the facility’s production capacity for large power transformers by 50%. The project includes the installation of advanced manufacturing equipment and upgrades, as well as expanded on-site material handling systems.

Saginaw Control & Engineering to expand in Michigan

Saginaw Control & Engineering Inc., a manufacturer of electrical enclosures and precision sheet metal fabrications, has announced plans to expand its operations in Saginaw, Mich., where it has two manufacturing facilities.

The expansion includes a 230,000-sq.-ft. facility and substantial new machinery and equipment purchases. The project is expected to generate at least $50 million in capital investment and create at least 95 new jobs across the two locations.

The company’s products are used in industries including robotics, agriculture, food and beverage, and data centers.
