# LASER IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2010 ISMR]

Salvagnini attests that it is the manufacturer with the highest number of fibre laser cutting machines sold and installed all over the world. The experience and expertise acquired during these years of continuous investment in fibre laser technology has enabled the Salvagnini Group to design a new machine, the L3, dedicated exclusively to fibre and providing high levels of speed, precision, reliability and performance.

“Thanks to the efficiency of the fibre source, very low power consumption ensures that the machine is also environmentally friendly,” Salvagnini told ISMR.

At the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover recently, Salvagnini exhibited the new L3.hd version, featuring a patented mechanical system consisting of a compass structure. The compass is driven by a pair of conventional motors which make it possible to move the cutting head on the XY plane over a distance of 170mm with dynamics of up to 5g with extremely low consumption.

THE SALVAGNINI ‘AIRPLANE’ From a structural point of view, the L3 maintains the patented airplane architecture of Salvagnini laser systems: the bearing beam principle designed by Salvagnini guarantees the extreme rigidity of the structure, but also rapid accurate positioning and easy access to the workpiece.

The absence of an optical path, a feature of machines designed exclusively for cutting with a fibre source, eliminates design constraints and makes it possible to make a more compact streamlined manipulator, with a lighter Y axis carriage. This reduction of inertia results in improved dynamics (up to 2g acceleration) in the basic configuration, with lower power consumption.

The cutting head has been created to optimise the characteristics and dynamics offered by optical fibre transmission. The object of the redesign was to reduce the mass and inertia as well as guarantee high dynamics by using linear motion in the Z axis. The head has a single focus plug-in lens housing cassette, which makes it possible to cut the entire range of permitted thicknesses and materials without changing the lens, thereby guaranteeing rapid production changes and eliminating the need for adjustments.

The SiX numerical control unit is complemented by the Ethercat high-speed communications system, necessary for guaranteeing the performance required by the compass dynamics and the high cutting speeds achievable from fibre sources. The numerical control unit manages the movement of the axes, the focal position of the beam, the control parameters of the source and the assist gases.

To guarantee maximum performance, the L3 is equipped with Laser TRADJUST, the series of Salvagnini functions incorporated in the control unit which combine the trajectories defined by CAMLaser with the requirements of the cutting process.

INTEGRATED OPERATIONS The SL4 punch-laser is the new Salvagnini solution that combines four different functions in a single compact structure: the S4Xe punching system, laser cutting with an optical fibre power source, complete automation and the proprietary programming software. Simplicity of use is helped by the CAMSL4 and NESTSL4 programming software, which make it possible to develop the drawings and automatically assign punch or laser machining and subsequent automatic nesting.

“Within the patented multi-press head, every tool is controlled by an individual press to enable independent and multiple operations without any need to stop for tool changes: this principle means that the S4Xe is unrivalled in terms of cycle times and tool wear,” Salvagnini told ISMR.

The SL4 was conceived to meet the increasing market demand for small batch production runs of parts with a high aesthetic value, whilst maintaining low production times and costs. The combination of the punching machine with the laser makes it possible to make folds with heights of up to 16mm, thereby maintaining all the advantages of the multi-press head. At the same time, the laser allows the user to create extremely articulated profiles. The shear cutting thickness limits are also overcome, and the range of machinable sheets is therefore extended in terms of dimensions and materials.

The patented manipulator moves on guides constrained to the lower part of the “C” structure, providing extremely high positioning accuracy; it consists of a light mobile element, seven pincers and seven mechanical locators to provide an efficient centring cycle and high process reliability. The long stroke also makes it possible to machine sheets measuring 3000mm x 1500mm without repositioning, for higher productivity.

In the absence of a shear, it is the fibre optic laser that carries out all the sheet cutting operations. The flexibility and speed of this technology eliminates all constraints associated with geometry, scrap and machinability, and also reduces operating and maintenance costs. This is because the SL4 uses a fibre laser with a 2000 W source which makes it possible to achieve high linear cutting speeds as well as absolute precision when making irregular contours.

The running costs of the SL4 have been considerably reduced. Machine maintenance has been eliminated on the laser, as the fibre source does not require periodical maintenance. It incorporates advanced beam reflection control systems which guarantee integrity and safe operation.

“The power consumption of the entire SL4 system is extremely low - the average power consumption is 35 kW during the cycle. Production costs benefit from the optimisation management of the material: no scrap ‘dead zone’ and the virtual absence of sheet trimming so that waste is practically eliminated,” Salvagnini concluded.
