# BLM Tube Laser investment doubles productivity at Gratnells

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 ISMR]

With a history that can be traced back over 125 years, through four generations of the Hudson family, Gratnells has consolidated its position as a leading supplier of storage, furniture and staging to schools, industry and the medical sector. The Harlow (UK)-based company is now embarking on its next technological leap, using technology from BLM that would, said the company, have ‘even been beyond the imagination of H G Wells, who published his classic work of science fiction, The Time Machine, in 1895, the year that Gratnells was founded as W. A. Hudson’.

The decision to install a BLM LT5 Tube Laser machine, the first in the UK, is further revolutionising Gratnell’s business to meet increased demand for its tray storage products from schools, industry and the medical sector. These were previously produced using manual machine technology.

## New revenue opportunities

“Within a very short time of installing the BLM Tube Laser, productivity was doubling. Batches of racking that would previously have taken two people around two days to complete are now being produced, mainly unmanned, in less than a day,” explained Murray Hudson, Gratnells’ managing director.

“Not only that, but we are now able to process shapes on tubes that would have been impossible to achieve previously and this has led to innovative new product designs that will have significant impact on the market. The speed at which the BLM LT5 operates has also created extra capacity, which we are able to offer to those in need of quick responses for low volume laser cutting.”

The arrival of a BLM Laser Tube machine has not only improved productivity, but also taken the business in directions it hadn’t originally envisaged.

One area of particular interest is motorsport. Murray Hudson’s son, Rory, is among the top 10 kart racers in the UK and has been selected to be part of the McLaren Performance Academy. He is, in fact, the only UK driver on the programme at the moment.

“It was the connection between BLM and Tony Kart in Italy (whose karts Rory drives) that sealed our interest in getting the BLM LT5 machine. The Tony Kart chassis is cut and bent on BLM machines and having this information has enabled us to work with two universities, manufacturing the chassis for their Formula Student cars. The LT5 is allowing us to achieve multiple angle cuts that, in turn, create complex joints on these chassis that we couldn’t achieve without the laser. Working in a racing environment breeds quality and, while motorsport is just one area where we can see potential for our laser cutting service, the potential is huge thanks to the capability of the BLM LT5,” added Murray Hudson.

## Maximising efficiency

The move from manual slotting and cutting of tubes to the use of the BLM LT5 Tube Laser has been a massive leap forward in technology for Gratnells, but one that is paying dividends. It now has the capability to quickly and efficiently process batches of any size tube within the machine’s range of round tubes of up to 120mm diameter, square of up to 100mm and rectangular, oval and elliptical semi-flat stock of up to 120 x 70mm. It also has the capability to cut wall thicknesses of up to 6mm (mild steel), as well as process stainless steel, aluminium alloys, copper and brass.

For the larger batches of Gratnells’ own products, the BLM LT5 Tube Laser maximises efficiency with its automatic tube manipulation capability. From loading the raw material to measuring and unloading the finished part, the unloader can also separate, collect and palletise the finished parts, with every operation synchronised by the Siemens 840D control.

The laser enables Gratnells to brand all of its products.

“This means that the machine is capable of running unmanned for a significant length of time, thanks to the reliability of the fibre laser, combined with the large-capacity tube loader. The control is also capable of using the measurements of the tube, and location of the weld seam, to optimise part nesting and reduce waste material. During the entire process from loading to cutting, the tube is fully supported and guided, ensuring maximum accuracy of cut parts and protection of the tube surface,” BLM, the machine manufacturer, told ISMR.

“Further efficiency of the BLM LT5 Tube Laser comes in part from its fibre laser that allows a wide variety of materials to be cut along with the agility of the six digitally controlled axes and the speed at which it can be changed from one job to the next. This efficiency and versatility has enhanced Gratnells’ production and created other business opportunities, such as a new subcontract laser cutting service.”

## New subcontract laser service

As a result, Gratnells has created a ‘business within a business’, namely Gratnells’ Laser Cutting, to provide laser cutting for small-to-medium batch quantities with rapid response. Typically, this is the type of work that wouldn’t be commercially viable for traditional sub-contractors to accept.

“We justified the purchase of the BLM LT5 on the work that it would be undertaking on our products, so any spare capacity that we have available after that is already costed into the equation. This allows us to be very competitive in the sub-contract market, particularly for short batch runs where the customer needs the work with short lead times. We are not looking for large batch work as we do not want to commit the BLM LT5 to extended periods on sub-contract work as the main focus remains our own products, but this spare capacity does allow us to provide a niche, bespoke service for customers,” said Richard Picking, Gratnells’ international marketing director.

The installation of the BLM LT5 Tube Laser machine is just another step in the 125-year development of Gratnells. The company has constantly evolved, moving from manufacturing metal cornice poles and curtain fittings to cornering the market for television stands in the post-war boom years and being one of the first businesses to develop flat pack wardrobes. It was the latter that paved the way for its current business success, as each wardrobe included slotted frames to allow shelving to be positioned.

When schools changed their curriculum to include more science experiments by pupils, Gratnells (as it was now known) saw the opportunity and grabbed it. It took the design of the wardrobe shelving system, added standardised plastic trays and the company became one of the largest suppliers of storage systems to schools. Current production stands at 10,000 plastic trays per day that are shipped worldwide along with their associated shelving. ■
