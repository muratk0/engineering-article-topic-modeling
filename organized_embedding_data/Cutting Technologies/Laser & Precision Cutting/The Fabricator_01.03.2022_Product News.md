Fiber laser equipped with locus beam control

Amada America offers the Ventis 3015 AJ, a 4-kW fiber laser cutting machine equipped with locus beam control (LBC) technology. This beam-forming technology controls kerf width for optimal efficiency based on the material type and thickness being processed, making it possible to cut at higher speeds and raise quality to improve productivity, the company states. It can freely manipulate the laser beam to create an infinite number of locus patterns.

The machine features three functions: Productivity Mode, for improved productivity when cutting stainless steel and aluminum; Quality Mode, for high-quality processing that helps improve surface roughness and eliminate dross; and Kerf Mode, which enables long-term, stable processing during automated system operation.

Software monitors order from raw material to finished part

LVD has released CADMAN v8.7, the latest update of its sofiware suite, which introduces several key features, including the ability to integrate foreign operations such as tapping, chamfering, deburring, grinding, and painting. Stepper motor I/O terminal provides dual-channel EtherCAT motion interface

With the new EL7062 dual-channel EtherCAT terminal, Beckhoff enables the direct connection of two stepper motors in the medium power range of up to 3 amps and for a voltage range of 8 to 48. With flexible parameterization and minimized channel costs, the flexible motion interface is suitable as a drive for a variety of stepper motor applications.

The compact, 24-mm-wide terminal contains two stepper motor output stages, two digital inputs for limit switches, and one encoder interface per channel for a variety of 5-V encoders. Via parameterization, it adapts to the connected stepper motor and its corresponding application requirements. The module supports the entire spectrum of TTL encoders (5 V differential, single-ended/open collector).

Industrial controller processes machine sensor signals

Banner Engineering has released the DXMR90 industrial controller, a compact device that serves as a central hub for processing incoming signals from equipment, including sensors attached to machinery. It then outputs a combined data stream that provides direct visibility of active processes, detects opportunities for specific productivity improvements, and enables advanced predictive maintenance capabilities.

The controller contains individual Modbus clients for concurrent communication with up to five independent serial networks. It converts Modbus RTU signals to Modbus TCP/IP, Ethernet I/P, or Profinet, and it communicates with devices that use internet protocols such as RESTful, MQTT, and APIs. Data output can be sent over Ethernet (or a connected cellular-enabled DXM controller) to cloud platforms.

System monitors weld pin position in projection welding

CenterLine (Windsor) Ltd. has launched the VeriFast IA (integrated amplifier) nut detection system for monitoring weld pin position in projection welding. It can sense fastener presence and orientation at the point of weld, piloted and nonpiloted nuts, welding stud lengths, and infinite weld pin positions, as well as measure projection collapse.

Monitored by the PLC/HMI or MicroView, the system’s signal indicates the position of the fastener weld pin in various stages of travel.

The values of this signal are then compared to programmed setpoint values (with tolerances) to indicate the presence and orientation of fasteners and parts. Results that do not match the setpoint values can be used to trigger either an interruption in the cycle or a warning message.
