# FOCUS ON LASER CUTTING

[TARİH: 01.02.2023 ISMR]

## Breaking new ground

After extensive market analysis, in 2021 the company opted for the ELEMENT 400 L laser cutting machine from Messer Cutting Systems with a new 'Bevel-U' bevel head and 8kW IPG laser source as well as the automatic Laser Nozzle Control (LNC) and Global Connect machine control system. Further digitisation of production is possible with the latest version of the CAD/CAM nesting software, OmniWin, and the digitisation suite, OmniFab, with ERP-Connect, Job Management, PDC and Machine Insight modules.

"When we decided on this solution, we were very aware that not only were we breaking new ground with the ELEMENT L, the LNC and the Global Connect, but so was Messer Cutting Systems," explained Mechthild Beuting.

Ingo Staudinger, Product Manager, Material Handling and Lasers, Messer Cutting Systems, added: "It was also clear to us that the introduction of three new products at a customer's site would mean a maximum challenge for both sides. But the partnership in place since 2006 was so good that we were able to take this step together."

First, the old plasma cutting machine had to be dismantled and the foundation for the new machine created. This was followed by assembly of the special track by Messer Cutting Systems and construction of the shuttle table system by Beuting. Regular productive operation of the ELEMENT 400 L with one shift has been running since December 2021.

## Partners in progress

"The ELEMENT L for Beuting was our prototype for new laser cutting machines that went beyond the 6kW limit that had existed for years. The highest priority was to secure the combination of new machine, new Global Connect control and LNC as a prerequisite for automation. The alliance with our partner Beuting helped us to bring the project to market maturity together under production conditions," outlined Ingo Staudinger.

Alfred Beuting, managing director of the company, summed it up: "Everyone involved, including the management of Messer Cutting Systems, has repeatedly contributed to solving pending, sometimes very difficult, tasks. Open communication and the fact that we have known and valued each other for many years helped us a lot. The project was a stress test for our long-standing business relationship. While we're still not quite there, both sides are optimistic that we will be shortly."

The business relationship between the two companies began in 2006. At that time, Messer Cutting Systems qualified Beuting as a supplier for environmental technology. The cooperation resulted in an extensive range of products, some of which were jointly developed, such as vibratory conveyor tables; belt conveyor tables; filter systems; water conveyor tables; shuttle table systems etc. Over the years, however, the business relationship developed into a partnership on an equal footing.

The integration of the ELEMENT 400 L with LNC and Global Connect into Beuting's production became a joint development project. Beuting only acquired the prototype because there was a close, long-term relationship. It was able to contribute a great deal of its own expertise into the cutting process. Introduction of the new products also brought the cooperation to a new level as a development partnership in which both were willing to take risks to open up new opportunities.

"Our partnership has emerged stronger from the project. It has brought both sides much further and improved our market positions," summarised Alfred Beuting.

The new, highly dynamic laser bevel unit, Bevel-U, is designed for the production of precise and repeatable bevel parts (a major benefit when preparing edges for automated robotic welding).

Ingo Staudinger agreed: "By combining the Messer machines with the environmental technology from Beuting, we have developed a competitive advantage together. Both sides benefit from the close cooperation. We pay attention to the interests of each other in dialogue with our customers. We were always able to find a solution, even for difficult tasks."

Open communication, mutual trust, a common focus on the benefit to the end customer and the will to innovate are, both parties agreed, guarantors of success.

## From plasma to laser

Even if not all the issues have been resolved, the experience to date is clear.

"The precision with which the machine cuts the components has clearly exceeded our expectations. One of our customers can now weld all the parts automatically and only wants components from this machine. We now automatically cut a wide variety of structural steels and fine-grain structural steels, even with different surface qualities, without manual intervention. The cutting times, compared to the plasma cutting process, have been significantly reduced with the same assemblies. Our operators are happy with better working conditions (in terms of dirt and noise) and have a lot of fun working with the new machine and its new control system," highlighted Stefan Tenhumberg.

With laser cutting, the kerf is very small compared to other thermal cutting processes. This is due to the small focus of the laser beam. Heat input into the material is very low, so that even small geometries can be cut.

"We use the laser-cutting process for smaller sheet metal thicknesses of up to 30mm, especially where the parts are further processed automatically. Even tighter tolerances are required than can be achieved with plasma cutting. Bevel laser cutting is our specialty. In material thicknesses of up to 30mm, we can cut maximum cutting angles of up to 50°," said the company.

The new MESSER ELEMENT 400 L 8kW fibre laser bevel unit has a maximum working area of 3,000 × 8,000mm. It now joins the 3.2kW TRUMPF TLF 3200 CO₂ laser at the company's premises.

The Beuting family is already planning next steps. Automation and digitisation are central themes. The ELEMENT L is to be further optimised and the material logistics automated. The plan is to connect the machines to the ERP system so that manual recording of times and quantities becomes superfluous. Production planning and order processing is further digitised with OmniFab. The evaluation of machine data should bring more transparency and further improve cutting processes.

## Next steps

Mechthild Beuting sees the whole thing very positively from a management point of view. The competitive edge that the new machine has brought is clear. Product quality has been greatly improved and working conditions for operators have improved. "We are already using the system very successfully, even in semiunmanned shifts," she said.

Ulrike Beuting echoes her mother on the advantages of digitisation.

“Several work steps in order processing, manual material bookings as well as rework costs have been eliminated, which reduce time and costs. The higher cost transparency means that we can further optimise processes,"she explained.

"Through this joint development work, we were able to significantly reduce nonproductive times and increase the productivity of the machine. We were also able to influence many details. Another advantage is the intuitive Global Connect control, which does not take long to learn," added Lisa-Marie Beuting, the second daughter of the family and successor to the management team.

Laser power is to be increased. The management wishes to counteract the growing shortage of skilled workers with attractive jobs.

Rising technical requirements from customers, who are also automating and digitising their processes, will be met with the help of innovative technologies.

“Despite the stress test, we would choose this solution again any time," concluded Mechthild Beuting. “We have just ordered the second ELEMENT L, this time with 15kW laser power."
