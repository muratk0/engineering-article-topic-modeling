Laser machine offers versatility for cutting, welding

TRUMPF’s TruLaser Cell 3000 is a 5-axis laser machine for fine cutting and precision welding of 2-D and 3-D components. It handles small to mediumsized parts from one-off prototypes to high-volume production. With solid-state laser options, available with up to 8 kW of laser power, the machine also is suitable for processing nonferrous metals.

The focal position is adjusted automatically to the thickness of the sheet metal. The diameter of the focus can be expanded. The user can switch between welding and cutting applications without changing the focusing optics.

The electrical control, cooling units, and laser optics are integrated into the machine, minimizing floor space requirements. The modular design allows the laser machine to adapt to different requirements in the production environment.

GTAW process integrated with robotic system

The TIP-TIG process from ABB offers improved coping with joint fit-up gaps, higher travel speeds, and lower heat input requirements when compared to traditional GTAW, according to the manufacturer.

Integrated with a robotic system for the first time, the process is installed at the ABB lab in a cell combining the slimdesign IRB 2600ID robot and the IRBP250A workpiece positioner. The robot is designed for welding, with the process equipment fully integrating in the upper arm, providing 15 percent shorter cycle times, the company reports.

2-kW fiber laser cutting system provides 4-kW cutting capabilities

Amada America Inc. offers the new ENSIS 3015 AJ 2-kW fiber laser cutting system that provides 4-kW cutting capabilities.

Amada reports that it is the first manufacturer of advanced metalworking machinery to develop its own fiber laser oscillator. Joint development between Japan and America has resulted in this high-powered fiber laser source designed specifically for cutting.

In addition, the resonator automatically and precisely adjusts the diameter of the laser beam. This feature enables continuous processing of thin to thick materials by a single machine, and no lens change or additional setup is required. As a result, the system provides fabricators with full cutting capabilities in materials up to 1 in. thick.

Solid-state technology does not require laser gas to generate the laser beam, thereby reducing environmentally harmful emissions. In addition, the power requirements for the system are substantially less than with CO2 systems, the company states.

Fiber laser available in capacities up to 5 kW

Prima Power North America Inc. has developed a new version of its Platino laser system focused on fiber cutting applications.

The Platino Fiber offers increased productivity through new piercing devices, particularly for mid to high mild steel thickness. The system also can cut highly reflective materials and thickgauge mild steel up to 0.98 in.

All materials are cut with a single, universal lens. The optical chain is totally sealed and protected from contamination. The Safe Impact Protection System (SIPS) protects the machine head in case of collisions with workpieces or fixtures. The focusing head also features the OPC quick alignment system, a high-dynamics focal axis with 1.38-in. stroke, and a variety of nozzles.

No manual intervention is required during machine operation. Once the production schedule is programmed, the system takes care of the necessary settings, tip replacement, and sheet change and storage.

6-kW fiber laser source added to cutting machines

Bystronic has extended the BySprint Fiber portfolio with the addition of a 6-kW fiber laser source for its 3015 and 4020 cutting machine formats.

The higher power increases the machine’s cutting speed in thin to medium sheet metal thicknesses. For example, a 6-kW fiber laser cuts 0.125-in. stainless steel up to 70 percent faster than a 4-kW fiber laser and three times faster than a 6-kW CO2 laser, the company reports. Depending on the type of material and the sheet thickness, parts output can be increased by up to 400 percent.

The 6-kW version is equipped with Cut Control to monitor the cutting process. When a cutting tear occurs, Cut Control automatically stops the laser and the cut is repeated, reducing the risk of miscuts.
