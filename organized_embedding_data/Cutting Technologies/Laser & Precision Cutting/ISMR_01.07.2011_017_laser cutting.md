From the broad range of high-performance $\mathsf{CO}_2$ lasers for cutting applications, Rofin launched the DC 025. Rofin has more than 3,000 lasers of this power class in use at manufacturing sites all over the world. ## AN EYE ON FIBRE

The BySprint Fiber 3015, from Bystronic, with a two kilowatt fibre laser source can currently handle thin sheets of up to four millimetres for strong parts output with high accuracy levels. In addition to steel, stainless steel and aluminium, it can process nonferrous metals such as copper and brass reliably and accurately. The user also benefits from very high energy efficiency.

A further Bystronic focus in the field of laser cutting is on highly productive laser cutting with automated processes, both on and beside the machine. Its fully automated laser cell is the heart of a new laser cutting system BySpeed Pro 3015 – either the ByLaser 4400 (4.4 kilowatts) or the ByLaser 6000 (6 kilowatts) can be fitted. The automation side of the machine was expanded to include the newly developed BySort.

Amada has announced plans to jointly develop a second generation suite of kilowatt class fibre lasers with up to 4 kilowatt output power. The lasers will be used in the Amada high-powered laser material processing system.

JDSU’s collaboration with Amada builds on the companies’ partnership for the first generation laser, which is currently being launched. Focused on improved cost, reduced size and enhanced beam quality, this next generation kilowatt fibre laser will enable Amada to further proliferate the technology into the materials processing market.

Amada integrated JDSU’s fibre optic technology into its sheet metal cutting system, which provides one of the fastest linear cutting speeds available worldwide. The laser has the power to efficiently cut through highly reflective metals such as titanium, brass and copper, and offers the flexibility required to cut different and very precise shapes by simply changing the system’s settings.

Metal processing is one of the largest markets for lasers, with fibre lasers gaining traction in recent years. Industry analyst firm Strategies Unlimited estimates that the laser market for kilowatt materials processing will grow to more than US$1 billion by 2013.

Jenoptik has focused on the development and improvement of the pumping characteristics of diode laser bars and, in particular, for pumping fibre lasers and has launched a more powerful, highly brilliant diode laser module.

For laser processing, Prima’s new Evoluzione2 combines high performance with high energy efficiency, thanks to the use of a fibre laser, a new generation of laser sources allowing important energy savings.

Rofin-Sinar Technologies Inc., a developer of laser beam sources and laser-based solutions, has acquired a block of buildings in Tampere (Finland) for the increased manufacturing capacity of its fibre laser series.

At the BlechExpo exhibition, Rofin presented the FL 030 C, the new compact version of its fibre laser series, which has been specially developed for direct integration into existing machine concepts. With an output power of 3,000 W, the FL 030 C is available as a multi-mode laser with a 50 μm or 100 μm fibre.

Years of continuous investment in fibre laser technology have allowed the Salvagnini Group to design a new line of lasers, called L3 and L5, dedicated exclusively to fibre providing high levels of speed, precision, reliability and performance. The L5 features a patented mechanical system consisting of a compass structure.

Salvagnini has designed the L1Xe with a view to creating a laser cutting system that is not only flexible, reliable and safe, but is also easy to run and to program. Optical fibre is, in fact, used both to

generate the beam inside the electronic source and to transport the beam from the source to the machine. This offers a variety of advantages, including the elimination of both optical path and laser gas and the creation of a more simple and compact layout. The electronic source requires virtually no maintenance, as its life expectancy is practically the same as that of the machine.

Volkswagen AG has ordered a significant quantity of TruLaser Cell 8030 series laser cutting machines from TRUMPF Laser und Systemtechnik GmbH. The first batch of machines will be divided between Volkswagen's headquarters in Wolfsburg and its sites in Kassel and Spain. This batch forms just part of a bulk order that will see TRUMPF deliver more than 50 machines to Volkswagen during the next two years. Volkswagen will be using the laser cutting machines in high-volume production of hot-formed parts for car bodies. The carmaker chose the TruLaser Cell 8030 based on its very high productivity, and combined it with the energy-saving, fibre-guided TruDisk laser, creating an overall package designed to drive down operating costs and minimise the cost per part.

The new 2in1 fiber, developed by TRUMPF specifically for TruDisk disk lasers, allows users to rapidly switch from welding to cutting without changing the laser light cable. Switch applications in the TruLaser Cell 3010, for example, is done simply by changing the machining optics. This greatly reduces re-tooling times while protecting the lenses from dirt and scratches.

Just over a year ago, TRUMPF launched the TruLaser 5030 fiber solid-state laser machine and has since sold more than 50 units. ■
