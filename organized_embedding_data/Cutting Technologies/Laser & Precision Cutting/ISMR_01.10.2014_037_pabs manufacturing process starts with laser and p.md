# PAB’s manufacturing process starts with laser and punching technology to produce the blank, which is then passed on for bending and trimming to the finished shape

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

By working in partnership with Prima Power since 2006, PAB in Coventry has been able to maintain and expand its leadership in the manufacture of prototype and production runs of formed pressings and panels for the prestige automotive market. The UK company specialises in niche products and supplies parts for defence, aerospace and HVAC applications, and has a string of supporting quality and performance accreditations.

Currently the company has two Prima Power eP-0520 servo-electric press brakes, an E5x servo-electric punch press, a Platino CO2 laser with a fully palletised automated material handling and stacking system, and a Rapido 5-axis laser.

Rob Lewis, sales manager for PAB, explained, “We have invested over £1 million with Prima Power. This is a strategic commitment that will continue into the foreseeable future.”

## Cut, punch and bend

The Platino CO laser was purchased around eight years ago to cut aluminium sheet of up to 5mm thick while achieving high edge quality. Its integrated pallet system delivers material to the machine from the automated stack, enabling it to run ‘lights-out’ 24 hours per day. The company produces parts to its customers’ schedules, manufacturing thousands of components each week.

Rob Lewis said, “For our investment in laser equipment, response and back-up was very important to us, as was the automation of the machine which was essential to meet production requirements. The technology and service offered by Prima Power ensures that we can provide the best possible continuity of delivery to our customers at the right quality. We have had no serious breakdown problems with the machines, and the service technicians have been extremely efficient.”

2012 saw a major investment programme for PAB as it replaced its old press brakes with the two new Prima Power eP-0520 servo-electric press brakes. The 55-ton eP-0520 press brakes incorporate a pulley belt system actuated by servo-electric drives. This spreads the bending force evenly along the whole bending length and offers high repeatability and accuracy. PAB was the first company to install these in the UK.

Just a few months later, PAB added an E5x servo electric punch press. Rob Lewis explained: “We added this machine as some parts, such as automotive grills, have lots of holes and manufacturing is far quicker using punching technology rather than laser cutting, where hole-piercing times are too long for this type of work.”

The E5x uses less power than a hydraulic punch press and the shearing action is kinder on tools. This means savings on energy as well as on replacement punches.

“The punching and laser processes save us significant cost in hard tooling, which we would otherwise need for small prototype runs,” added Lewis.

## From blank to part

PAB’s manufacturing process starts with laser and punching technology to produce the blank, which is then passed on for forming on the company’s SMG 500-ton and Muller 250-ton hydraulic presses, or bending on the eP-0520 press brakes.

Once panels have been formed, trimming to the finished shape is completed on the Prima Power Rapido 5-axis laser. The machine has a teach mechanism for easy programming and a double safety nozzle. This enables the nozzle or the whole laser head to safely move out of the way in the event of a collision. Should this happen, it can be quickly and easily repositioned by the operator so that cutting and programming can continue without damage to the machine itself.

“We can trim low volume, high value parts in batches from 2 to 300 very quickly and efficiently. Typical parts include bracketry and welded fabrications in all shapes and sizes,” added Rob Lewis.

Close collaboration has been a major driver for PAB when it chose to work with Prima Power. Rob Lewis concluded, “The cost of capital equipment is a major factor, but the quality of service and support is of equal importance. It is a false economy to ignore the benefits it can bring to production efficiency, customer satisfaction and the lifetime costs of the machine. We rely entirely on Prima Power for maintenance, training and advice on the use of the machines, making us a very satisfied customer.” ■
