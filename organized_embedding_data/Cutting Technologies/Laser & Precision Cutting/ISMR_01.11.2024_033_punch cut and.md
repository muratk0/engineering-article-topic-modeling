Boschert celebrated its 75th anniversary in 2021. Founded in 1946 as a small workshop for the textile industry, today it builds a wide range of sheet-metalworking equipment. This includes notching; punching; small and mid-sized press-brake bending systems; plasma; laser; combined punching/plasma/fibre laser and copper processing machines. It also builds special machines to meet specific customer requirements.

Based in Lörrach (near Freiburg, Germany), Boschert employs 120 members of staff. It recently made significant investments to expand its production line and has put in place key partnerships with European machine-tool manufacturers. This includes a joint venture company in a new factory with Greek manufacturer, Gizelis SA, particularly on press brakes and a new partnership with Greek producer, Steelburner, on fibre laser cutting systems.

At the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition in Hanover, Germany, from 22-25 October 2024, it showcased some of its latest new machines and systems. ISMR met Philipp Schöchlin, Boschert's export manager, at EuroBLECH to discuss the company's new partnerships, latest technologies and future plans.

ISMR: What are you showcasing on your booth here at EuroBLECH?

PS: For the first time at EuroBLECH in Hanover, we are showcasing the new control for our combined fibre laser and punching machine (Combi-Laser). We will deliver the first machines featuring this new control to our customers by the end of this year.

I am also delighted to confirm here, for the first time in Hanover, that (for markets in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and France), we will now offer the fibre laser from Steelburner as an addition to the press brakes and shears from Boschert and Gizelis. As well as sales, we will also provide after-sales service, warranty, training etc. We started selling these last year and are showing the system here in Hanover for the first time.

ISMR: How do you view the market, in general, for laser cutting systems?

PS: Our CombiLaser is still a strong-selling machine. Users can add forming/punching to their fibre laser cutting operations. It combines the benefits of both punching and laser cutting. Although punching machines are becoming less popular, there are still applications for punch presses that are not possible for laser cutting systems. Combined machine operations offer great advantages for manufacturers.

The market for stand-alone flatbed lasercutting systems is a bit like a 'shark tank'. There are too many competitors and very close price points, so we saw an opportunity to step in with our combined punch-laser machine as well as our stand-alone laser (FiberLaser). Our FiberLaser is slightly different from other stand-alone laser cutters because it moves the sheet using clamps, and features a y-axis cutting head, which allows the customer to work differently than on a flat bed laser.

ISMR: How are you approaching automation around sheet metal systems?

PS: Automation is so important now in sheetmetal markets so Boschert and Gizelis have been working for a while on the RoboBend cell, which is being requested more and more by customers. Users can operate the machine with or without the robot arm, so the customer can also operate the machine manually if he wishes.

For loading and unloading punch presses and laser machines, we will deliver our first unloading/loading machine for metal sheet (featuring a sorting unit) this year. This is a big step for us as we are still a small machine-builder from the south of Germany.

ISMR:What are your views on the global business climate for sheet metal professionals in 2025?

PS: If you look solely at the sheetmetal machine market in Europe, I think 2025 will be a very difficult year. The signs are not good. Large companies are reacting to slower market conditions with short working and pay cuts.

## New member of the Boschert family

Steelburner from Greece, a producer of fibre laser cutting systems, is the latest new member of the Boschert family. The family-owned, third-generation company has been producing machines in Thessaloniki since 1970, starting with plasma cutting systems and now its FiberLaser fibre laser cutting systems.

Pagnaotis Apostolellis and his sons, Leandros and Vassilis, run the company and look after R&D and machine production. Boschert GmbH will be the sales partner for the D-A-CH region as was well as France (with P&G Outillage). It will handle machine set-up, training, service and spare part delivery, as well as being the primary customer contact.

This strategic alliance allows Boschert to close a gap in its portfolio and offer a complete range of sheet metal machines. The flatbed FiberLasers from Steelburner, featuring 6-20kW of power, are complementary to Boschertproduced machines as well as pressbrakes and shears from Gizelis S.A.

This international business currently appears to be developing steadily. Both markets together will lead Boschert stably through the next challenging year.

We will shortly finish building our new production and assembly hall (600 square metres). It will be ready for production by the end of this year.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes or achievements for Boschert?

PS: This year has been a very exciting year for Boschert. We bought a new machining centre for our production line (replacing a very old one), which we installed at the beginning of this year. We still perform most mechanical operations in-house (e.g. machining, welding and painting machine frames, assembly etc.).

We will shortly finish building our new production and assembly hall (600 square metres). It will be ready for production by the end of this year. Machines are now getting larger (with automation and loading/unloading systems bolted on) and we need more space to produce them. This represents a significant expansion of our production facilities and an important investment for us.

ISMR: Please outline your strategic and technical focus over the next few years?

PS: Next year, we aim to implement and deepen connections between Boschert, Gizelis and Steelburner (and our allied companies) to become better known in the marketplace and capture greater market share. We will then need to further increase our production capacity for certain machinery.

We are building greater capacity now and will need the skilled manpower to fill our new production hall. Increasing our production capacity is vital. We want to satisfy our existing customers and not lose new customers because of longer delivery times.

ISMR: Are you looking at moving into any new market areas or regions next year?

PS: No, I think that we have to focus on the markets and regions that we already serve. It makes more sense to develop existing regions with existing dealers, than to find ten new dealers every year in new regions that we need to train properly to use the machines.

Our main target next year will be to invest in the training of our existing dealers to increase their knowledge of our machines. Once that is done to our satisfaction, then we could possibly look at adding a new market area. South America is an interesting region where we could potentially grow our market. However, next year our main focus will be on our core markets (in Europe and North-America). New contacts and dealers are always welcome in the Boschert family.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal markets?

PS: There is an increasing trend towards automation. This will be the greatest trend over the next few years. Skilled labour shortages mean that most manufacturers are trying to automate their production. It is not just for large companies; this also applies to smaller companies dealing with smaller batches/quantities who wish to automate their production. It is a tough challenge, and I believe that some jobs may still have to be done by hand.

The trend in manufacturing is to make components that are lighter in weight, use less material, conserve resources and integrate functionality (i.e. thinner sheet structures).

The biggest change I see, in the copper bar arena, is that materials are becoming thicker. We are being asked to punch 20mm copper now. Luckily, we have great tools from our partner, Pass Stanztechnik in Germany, that enable our machines to do this.

ISMR: How are you handling sustainability in your products and organisation?

PS: For our press brakes, we are moving more towards hybrid machines (that save more electricity), rather than hydraulic systems. Customers can also buy completely electric shears from Boschert Gizelis to process up to 6mm steel.

In our production area, we produce machines almost completely in-house which saves our overall CO₂ emissions. We are also incorporating energy-saving measures into our new production hall.

However, I must stress that customers need to invest additional money into eco-friendly machines. They need to be ready for this change and these solutions must be accepted by the market to become more widespread.

ISMR: Is there anything else that you wish to add?

PS: Despite the slower situation in European sheet metal markets, EuroBLECH in Hanover has been a great show for us. We received several new orders and took part in some great meetings, that we are now following up.
