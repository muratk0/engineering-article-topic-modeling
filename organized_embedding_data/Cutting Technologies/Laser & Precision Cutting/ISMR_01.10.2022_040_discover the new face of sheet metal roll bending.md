ISMR: Which trends and growth areas do you see developing in global laser-cutting markets?

CB: Currently, the two biggest trends are towards higher laser powers and the implementation of automation systems.

Not long ago, few fibre laser manufacturers were betting on high powers. Back then, high-wattage laser systems were introduced by pioneers like Eagle because it was clear to us from the start that this technology would be a game-changer. Our focus from the beginning was to build the ideal fibre laser machine; powerful, fast and reliable, able to produce more in less time, with less maintenance, less energy consumption and more flexibility regarding materials and thicknesses.

However, it's impossible to speak about high powers without mentioning motion dynamics, and we have raised the bar on both flanks. The Eagle iNspire system delivers 6G acceleration, a cutting speed of up to 150m/min. and a 350m/min. positioning speed, maintaining accuracy and reliability even at 30kW.

Understanding that power and motion dynamics work hand-in-hand is crucial. If we're talking about high wattages, we need to consider dynamics. Without acceleration that rises to the challenge, it's not possible to exploit the full capabilities that high powers have to offer. A simple way to understand the benefits of dynamics is if you double the acceleration, you are technically halving the time that it takes to reach a programmed speed.

Another aspect where acceleration is essential is when cutting complex shapes or dealing with arcs and corners. The rate at which the machine can decelerate into and out of an angle has a higher impact on reducing the cycle time than power or cutting speed. Improved cycle time means more parts per hour, so acceleration is a crucial parameter to consider when choosing a machine.

To add to the benefits, shorter work cycles also mean decreased energy use and fewer consumables resulting in reduced operational expenses and a lower cost per part. This translates into higher profits, which we are all pursuing.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

CB: The biggest challenge now is production. Manufacturers worldwide are affected by the current supply chain crisis and looking for the best ways to overcome it. But, as with every crisis, it's also an opportunity to improve and to develop new concepts and ideas.

It's a difficult time for many markets but also a chance to move towards new technologies and more environmentally friendly processes and materials. So, we need to approach the future positively and stay flexible.
