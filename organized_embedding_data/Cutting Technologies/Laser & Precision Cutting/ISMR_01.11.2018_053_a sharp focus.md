# A SHARP FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Below: Mazak's 6kW DDL laser

Gaetano Lo Guzzo, Director, Lasen Business Europe at Mazak Europe, explains why Direct Diode Laser is the future for laser cutting in sheet metal.

Yamazaki Mazak took five laser cutting machines and three automation solutions to the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover this October. The highlight on its booth was the new 6kW version of its OPTIPLEX 3015 DDL (Direct Diode Laser) machine equipped with a multi-control torch, new MAZATROL PreviewG and a variety of intelligent functions that, Mazak told ISMR, deliver a 15 per cent increase in productivity, due to the machine's high-speed cutting capability. Mazak has invested substantially in DDL technology and has been a frontrunner in developing its potential for the sheet metal market.

ISMR also saw the new 3D FG DDL, a new Direct Diode Laser version of the company's tube and pipe cutting machines on its booth at EuroBLECH, along with 10kW and 8kW versions of its OPTIPLEX FIBER III lasers. The 8kW machine was being loaded by a Mazak Smart Manufacturing Cel automation system. The final machine on its stand was a 6kW OPTIPLEX NEXUS 3015 FIBER, equipped with CST automation.

Gaetano Lo Guzzo, Director, Laser Business Europe at Mazak Europe, took ISMR through Mazak's latest technology innovations and cutting trends in sheet metal markets, on Mazak's booth at the exhibition.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers and how are you addressing these issues for them?

GLG: Customers today are looking for the best quality - they want to finish the part in one set-up and keep fixed costs as low as possible. That is why automation is important. DDL (Direct Diode Laser) is our first priority, here at EuroBLECH, and automation is our second priority. We want to review and expand our automation products and here, at EuroBLECH, is the first time that we have exhibited three new automation products with different applications. We believe that the demand for automation will keep increasing.

This also ties into the pace of new technology development. With high-power fibre laser and DDL, cycle times are short and machine speeds are rapid. A human operator simply cannot keep up with the machine's high speed – you need automation for this. Automation levels are consequently growing.

ISMR: Please tell us about your new products here at EuroBLECH in Hanover? Which technologies are you showcasing?

GLG: Here on our booth, you can see five machines and three automation systems. Out of the five machines, four of them are new. The three new automation systems include a loading and unloading sheetmetal tower system, a multi-axis robot for sorting parts from the sheet metal skeleton and a new automatic loading system for the tube machine.

The new version of our OPTIPLEX 3015 DDL (Direct Diode Laser) machine is the next step beyond the fibre laser. We have expanded the range with this 6kW version. It is ideal for cutting medium and thick sheetmetal. The resonator, the most advanced of its kind, enables fast cutting of stainless steel and aluminium and is up to 15 per cent faster than its Fibre equivalent power. Most importantly, the new DDL machine also delivers stable cutting for both thin and thick material, thanks to Beam Diameter Control, along with Intelligent Monitoring Functions (IMF) and Intelligent Set-up Functions (ISF). This highly efficient machine is also available as part of the OPTIPLEX 4020 series.

Two years ago, at EuroBLECH, we exhibited a 4kW DDL laser. We received a lot of orders for this – customers are very happy with DDL cutting quality as well as how easy it is to use the machine. They can also make larger margins when cutting, as they can cut with less set-up needed. We therefore strongly believe in the growth potential of DDL, even though the largest segment of our sales today is for fibre laser.

We are also extending the DDL product range to our tube cutting systems with the new FG-220 DDL 4kW, a new Direct-Diode Laser version of our tube- and pipecutting machine, that you see on the booth, and will also be extending this to other models. DDL is the future and we are first to market with this technology. The FG-220 DDL 4kW is designed for maximum productivity utilising high-speed approach piercing to improve cutting speeds on both thin and medium-thick material by up to 20 per cent compared to CO₂ lasers. It can cut highly reflective material, such as copper and brass, and is suitable for cutting long structural material, including round, square and rectangular pipe along with H, I and L beams.

DDL is the future and we are one of the first to market with this technology. We have increased the power in our fibrelaser range to increase the speed and feed rates, as well as to reduce cycle times. Higher productivity levels give customers a lower cost per part. We are presenting here our new 10kW OPTIPLEX FIBER III fibre laser – we can now offer 2-, 4-, 6-, 8- and 10kW in this range. The 8kW version on our stand is equipped with a Mazak Smart Manufacturing Cell automation system which offers detailed reporting of production status and operation, automatic parts separation detection and dynamic kinetics to calculate weight and speed. It is an Industry 4.0-ready solution for laser users and has an open interface to aid further expansion, along with an option for a robot solution. The final machine on our stand is a 6kW OPTIPLEX NEXUS 3015 FIBER, equipped with a CST automation system.

EuroBLECH is the pre-eminent laser trade show in Europe and we are proud to be bringing an outstanding range of new machines and equipment to the Hannover Messe. Our theme this year is speed, specifically new technology, such as the OPTIPLEX DDL with its powerful 6kW resonator, and the ability to combine laser cutting technology with automation solutions, to provide an integrated cutting and machine handling solution that can take productivity to the next level.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal laser cutting markets?

GLG: I am noticing that customers who have never bought a fibre laser before are finding it more convenient to buy a machine, so that they can own it rather than outsource the cutting operation.

There are still very large margins, in terms of market size, to grow fibre laser technology. The latest forecasts indicate that it is still a young technology, with lots of room to grow. The automotive market is moving more to hybrid and electric cars and there are even more applications in these sectors for fibre laser cutting. I can see even more applications for laser in the future.

ISMR: What is your approach to Industry 4.0, as a company?

GLG: We have been working on Industry 4.0 for a couple of years. One of our refurbished factories in Japan is dedicated to Industry-4.0 development (we call it the high smart factory). We are also working towards equipping all our systems with Industry 4.0 devices for other European factories. We produce our own software (using a Cisco platform).

GLG: This year has been very good for us. Last year, in Europe, we had our best results ever. In 2017, (our financial year ends in March), we were 10% up on turnover in Europe compared to last year (for laser). We have seen even higher growth in machine tools in general The European economy has been strong this year.

For next year, I think it will continue to grow. We forecast some reductions in growth but, overall, I believe that the next two years will be busy for us.
