Laser cutting productivity improved with ERP software upgrade

Situation

Australian laser subcontractor Online Laser Pty specializes in laser cutting for the agricultural, manufacturing, and engineering industries, as well as for companies in the aviation, boat/marine, and transportation industries.

The two Bystronic Bystar precision fiber laser cutters at its facility in Bendigo, Victoria, Australia, cut materials including carbon steel, stainless steel, aluminum, titanium, brass, and copper.

Online Laser realized that its ERP system was limiting its productivity, even with these strong laser machines. With that ERP, the company had difficulty managing 100 live orders.

Resolution

Online Laser now can manufacture twice as much as before with the help of Lantek Systems’ sheet metal CAD/CAM and ERP software.

The company has doubled its output by using Lantek Expert CAD/CAM, MES, Integra, and WOS software to power its two fiber laser cutters. It’s also added the Lantek Opentalk connection directly to the laser machines for automatic validation of machine status and parts manufactured.

According to Ben Harris, programming and production manager, CAD drawings arrive in several different formats, all of which can be downloaded into Expert, where they are analyzed to determine cutting times. That data can be found in the Integra software, which combines the times with subcontract costs such as folding, powder coating, or machining to produce an accurate price for the whole job. What previously took several days now takes one to two hours, or sometimes just minutes.

When the material type and thickness are the same, the company nests parts from different customers in the same sheet. "The inventory system is worth the investment on its own. We can manage remnants of material to use them up and keep track of stocks so that we don’t suddenly run out of material," said Harris. "Now, we only need to do a physical stock check every few months."

Once the job is complete, the software creates the invoice, delivery note, and pallet label. It also knows when a customer has an account so that parts can be shipped immediately or, if it is a new customer, what the payment terms are before shipping. The interface to Online Laser’s Xero accounting software automatically keeps this up to date with shipped and invoiced goods.

"The MES system and the real-time reporting from the machines gives us an accurate picture of delivery times. Everyone has visibility of the information, so we always give the customer consistent and accurate information on the status of their job. We can quote at 8:30 a.m. and have the parts cut by 11:00 a.m. in urgent situations," said Harris. "We just installed V42 of the software, and we can now do layouts at the quote stage, so prices are now even more accurate."

With the company now able to manage more than 200 live orders, operations have become much easier.

"The software has definitely made the workplace a lot more efficient," Harris explained. "We are a lot more transparent on cost, and we can see where the profit is, which was almost impossible before. It is a vital part of our operation, making us much more competitive, faster, and efficient."

Robotic welding cell delivers high-quality welds for fabricator’s series production job

Situation

Viñolas Metall is a family business in Cornellà del Terri, Spain, that specializes in the construction of metal components, sheet metal processing, and boiler making. The company serves such industries as winemaking, water treatment, swimming pool accessories, mechanical engineering, plant construction, toolmaking, power plant construction, and railway construction.

A 43,000-sq.-ft. production site in Banyoles, Spain, was recently opened to complement the existing 8,600-sq.-ft. assembly workshop in Cornellà del Terri.

Welding currently accounts for 70% to 80% of production times. Carbon steel, stainless steel (304, 316, duplex, and super duplex), aluminum, and COR-TEN are the most common materials processed.

Working with sheet from 2 to 40 mm thick, the company mainly joins smaller components, but also large and heavy components. It typically uses GTAW and GMAW for manual tasks and GTAW keyhole welding for automated applications, along with orbital welding.

Welding is carried out in the flat, horizontal vertical, and horizontal positions, while out-of-position welding on vertical beams and pipes is performed in the vertical-up and vertical-down positions.

Specializing in technically demanding, complex metal structures, Viñolas Metall needed to find a way to create high-quality, reproducible welds for series production that meets industry specifications and welding standards for components exposed to high loads and used in highly corrosive environments.

Resolution

As a result of good experiences with Fronius welding machines and processes, Viñolas Metall added a cold metal transfer robotic welding cell in combination with a TPS 400i power source for the series production of identical parts, specifically welding beams for swimming pool roofs from austenitic chrome-nickel steels 304 and 316.

The robotic welding cell includes a hollow-arm welding robot and an H reverser, which has two vertical turntables on a horizontal axis on both sides. Custom-made clamping devices are mounted between the turntables, each of which can accommodate four of the stainless steel beams.

Once one side of the H reverser is loaded with four steel beams, they are turned into the welding cell and joined in a single cycle. Meanwhile, on the other side, the finished welded components can be removed and four new ones positioned, saving cycle time. As soon as the teaching of the system is complete, the sequence program together with axial movements is specified and the weld quality is ensured. The component handling can be carried out by trained operators who do not have to be experienced welding specialists.

"We want our best welders to work on large projects with high added value, while smaller series parts can be welded with the robotic welding cell," explained Mechatronics Engineer Matheus Borborema.

The cold metal transfer process used in the welding cell is characterized by its minimal heat input, which reduces the heat-affected zone and thus the deformation of the base material while offering high arc stability. The process enables higher welding speeds with low spattering, minimizing uneconomical rework and costs for consumables.
