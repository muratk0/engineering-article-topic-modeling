# RAISING THE BAR

[TARİH: 01.05.2011 ISMR]

ISMR examines the features, benefits and efficiencies offered by the latest new punching innovations.

Water jet cutting technology is one of the fastest growing major machine tool processes in the world due to its versatility and ease of operation.

Water jet cutting is often chosen as a preferred method of profile cutting in many industries because of the positive characteristics of the process. Unlike laser, plasma and oxyfuel cutting, there are no complications due to heat – meaning materials including rubber, plastics, stone and metals can be worked. Using a jet of water to perform the cold cutting operation ensures that the material structure remains unchanged. The water jet cutting process has always been a green method, as there are no gases or smoke produced from the materials being cut.

Other benefits include virtually burr-free cut edges (reducing finishing operations), no material discolouration and high yields of material (for cost savings). Multi-head operation enables the competitive cutting of flat materials whilst abrasive waterjet cutting can cut virtually any material, in any shape, up to 200mm thick. Heat input, warping and distortion are eliminated. Material hardening is a thing of the past - you can tap holes straight away and machine your edges without damaging your tools. Tight tolerances of less than +/-0.1mm can be achieved and there are virtually no limits on intricacy or hole size restrictions. Water jet can also be faster than machining, CNC routing and ERW.

## AN EYE ON ACCURACY

For Advanced Profiling, the cutting head is the essential element of a water jet process. It is the point from which the company derives its cutting accuracy and therefore the better the quality of the cutting head, the better the waterjet stream that is created.

This in turn increases nozzle life – one of the most expensive consumables for any waterjet company.

It therefore chose new replacement AccuStream Dialine heads with replaceable rather than fixed diamonds. These could be renewed in the future in the event of any damage to the head, giving a major cost saving over cutting heads with permanently embedded diamonds. Another main attraction was that the heads could be simply fitted into the existing set-up without any need for expensive adapters.

The company cuts a variety of materials from 0.1mm to 200mm. “The Dialine head really showed the difference when we had to cut some 200mm brass. The edge quality was not important but speed was and we managed to get through it at 6mm per minute, whereas our previous speed was only around 4mm per minute,” it told ISMR.

A key factor for any waterjet cutting company is downtime and Advanced Profiling found that the Dialine heads never have to come off the machine. This in itself probably saves a couple of hours per week. There are two Dialine cutting heads on the Flow machine and, on average, around 65 hours of actual cutting are undertaken per week. It is some 4000 hours since any head parts have been replaced.
