# What to consider when purchasing a laser cutting system

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

By Bradley McBain, managing director, MBA Engineering

In the manufacturing industry, time is money i.e. selection of the right laser cutting system is imperative for a manufacturer. With renowned reputations, flashy machines and brands promising what seems impossible, it can be difficult for companies to separate the wheat from the chaff when it comes to identifying an efficient, precise and cost-effective solution to its laser cutting needs.

Investing in machinery is a big decision for a manufacturer, with lots to take into consideration before purchasing a new piece of equipment. The laser cutting market is set to reach US$ 6.72 billion by 2024, meaning more and more companies are delving into the world of metal cutting.

But the market can be overwhelming. With different cutting bed sizes, cutting speeds and energy usage, how can the machine options available be whittled down to find the perfect machine for a business?

Functionality and benefits
Firstly, the exact functionality of the machine should be identified. If a manufacturer requires equipment to perform small cutting jobs, a simpler model may better suit the business' needs, whereas a more sophisticated machine would be ideal if multiple materials require cutting for more complicated projects.

Identifying all the advantages, and disadvantages, of having a laser system in-house should be taken into account. If a company is making the leap from outsourcing these services to taking them in-house, how will this benefit the business? It could be down to lead times and faster turnarounds for clients, or lower costs as there will be less need to transport materials back and forth to a subcontractor. It's important to identify these to help narrow down the search.

If a manufacturer is looking for a machine to fit within a specific space in a workshop, a bespoke laser cutting system may be required. Don't rule this out as some suppliers will provide this service – putting their customers' needs first. For example, Polish fibre laser systems manufacturer, Kimla, recently created a bespoke machine for a client which required equipment with a unique cutting area.

Another key consideration is technical service and support. With large machines working long hours, regular maintenance checks are essential. Making sure a supplier can be relied upon to get a machine up and running again as quickly as possible should anything go wrong is a major consideration – any downtime where equipment is out of action can cost manufacturers money, as well as putting a business's reputation at risk if clients are kept waiting.

Some machinery suppliers provide additional services to become a one-stop-shop for businesses looking to replace or add machinery within its workshop. At MBA Engineering, we offer a machine moves service to assist our customers with installations and factory moves. Taking the time to find out which companies offer additional services like these could ultimately save precious time and money before a machine is even in place.

Performance and automation
When it comes to a laser cutting machine's performance, cutting speeds and accuracy are two key factors, as they both massively impact on a company's work flow. There are accuracies of 0.03mm available on the market, and unrivalled cutting speeds to match, meaning more output in a smaller amount of time, while still delivering precision parts.

Automation is becoming much more prevalent too and should be taken into consideration. With the ability to produce precise and consistent products at high speeds, it dramatically reduces the rates of human error. An automated machine will reliably cut products at a set rate – meaning manufacturers can easily determine output using its statistics, which is ideal for accurately quoting clients and managing expectations.

Making the right choice
The laser cutting market has been dominated by key players with big budgets for years – each of which invests in clever, targeted marketing. Smaller equipment manufacturers that may offer more cost-effective, efficient machinery or better service are often overlooked because attention is drawn directly to the larger brands creating a buzz.

Putting this aside will open manufacturers up to more options. A machine from one of these brands may suit a company's needs perfectly, but it's important to weigh up all the choices on the market. Other companies may have superior accuracy and shorter cutting times but don't have the budget to effectively communicate these messages to potential customers.

Ultimately, the most important thing to bear in mind when purchasing a laser cutting system is knowing the market. Compare prices to ensure that money is being invested in a machine supplier which offers the best option – in some circumstances, this may be a bespoke piece of equipment specialised to suit specific needs. It's essential to look at how a machine is built and engineered, along with the level of customer support offered for the price being paid. There are manufacturers offering 24-hour support and quick turnaround on repairs, should anything go wrong, which is invaluable in an industry where clients are relying on you to deliver on time.■
