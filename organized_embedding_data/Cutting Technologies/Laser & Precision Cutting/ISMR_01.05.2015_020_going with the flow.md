# GOING WITH THE FLOW

[TARİH: 01.05.2015 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Waterjet cutting is a natural erosion process involving no chemicals or heat. Because of this, the warping and distortion typically associated with laser, plasma and oxy-fuel cutting is eliminated, minimising the need for secondary processing. Unlike laser, plasma and oxy-fuel cutting, waterjet lends itself to common line cutting.

## An eye on innovation

As one of the fastest growing machine tool industries, waterjet cutting can save time and money on countless applications. Tolerances tighter than +/- 0.005" are achievable, especially in thinner materials such as 1" stainless steel. Waterjet cutting can also vary tolerances in different locations on a part, helping to ensure quality.

Swiss manufacturer, Bystronic, recently introduced a twin-head ByJet Flex for waterjet cutting, which has the ability to accept up to two newly developed, 3D cutting heads for producing bevelled edges on components. Upgrading from 2D to 3D technology takes less than an hour, said the manufacturer.

“Many job shops add waterjets to their operations alongside other cutting technologies such as EDM, laser, milling and plasma”

Materials ranging from 10" stainless steel to 0.010" acrylics can be cut by waterjet, making it a very versatile tool. Many job shops add waterjets to their operations alongside other cutting technologies such as EDM, laser, milling and plasma.

“ByMotion enables safe integration of 3D technology. The control software supports all cutting functions on a ByJet Flex, regardless of whether it is using 2D or 3D technology. It coordinates simultaneous 5-axis cutting with two heads and employs continuous height sensing on both,” Bystronic told ISMR.

“The ByJet Flex platform can be integrated into a wide variety of production environments and used for both 2D and 3D applications, operating with two CNC heads for straight or bevelled cutting. The basic version is equipped with all prerequisites that allow it to be upgraded with functions such as 3D cutting heads, drill spindles, automatic parts holding during cutting (FixMaster), precise positioning of the cutting head (PositionPointer) and intelligent fine adjustment for rapid, multi-head cutting (PrecisionAligner).”

“Waterjet cutting is still the first choice for producing high quality edges, especially in thicker materials and for components where a heat-affected zone has to be avoided,” added UK subcontractor, and Bystronic customer, Water Jet Cutting.

UK subcontractor Charles Day (Steels) Ltd is now offering around the clock waterjet cutting in response to increasing customer demand. To coincide with the additional operating hours, Charles Day has also added a new 100hp pump to power its two existing waterjet machines.

“The installation of the new pump has increased our capacity by 50 per cent, meaning we’re able to have more heads cutting at full power at the same time, ultimately enabling us to increase our cutting speeds on a whole range of materials and thicknesses, especially thinner materials,” explained Alan Tranter, waterjet sales manager.

Charles Day waterjet cutting operations

“The general belief in the manufacturing industry is that waterjet cutting is expensive on thicknesses below 20mm compared to other methods, namely flame and laser. The new power provided by the pump means we’re able to increase speeds on cutting 10mm thicknesses and above, making waterjet profiling more competitive,” he concluded.

Waterjet cutting provides a heat-free alternative to profiling materials and, as such, has helped Charles Day to expand into markets previously inaccessible with its laser, flame and plasma cutting. In addition to the new pump and increase in operational hours, further investment is planned for 2015 for Charles Day’s waterjet cutting division. With six laser machines and one of the UK’s largest waterjet cutting beds, Charles Day remains one of the UK’s largest family-owned profiling firms in the UK.

Eckert’s COMBO waterjet cutting machine uses two modern technologies of cut; plasma and water. The system allows up to ten times higher cutting speed and 70 per cent reduction in costs, according to the manufacturer. ECKCAM Professional Plus is modern software for design offices which allows users to choose the preferred cutting technology and decide which elements will be cut by water technology and which by plasma.

Eckert’s OPAL waterjet is available in working widths from 1000 to 4500mm and working lengths from 1000 to 10000mm (pressure of 4130 bar).

Since Flow first invented abrasive waterjet technology in the early 1980s, the technology has evolved rapidly. The abrasive waterjet is much more powerful than a pure waterjet and is capable of cutting hard materials such as metals, glass, stone, and composites - none of which can be cut with a pure waterjet.
