FOCUS ON LASER CUTTING

TITANIUM ISMR SAY "Helix has worked with Prima Power to ensure rapid and precise laser bevel cutting of its titanium tubular components"

Above: The Helix bike side-by-side folding design puts the wheels beside the frame and between the cranks. The bike's 'go-anywhere compactness' with greater performance and mobility does not, said the manufacturer, sacrifice durability.

Helix uses laser bevel cutting of titanium tubular components in its drive towards a 'smaller, lighter, safer and easier-to-use' foldable bike.

Helix is a bicycle start-up located in Toronto, Canada. Its stated goal is to 'make the best folding bikes on the planet'. Helix told ISMR that its patent-pending, side-by-side folding design puts the wheels beside the frame and between the cranks – 'the most efficient use of space possible', according to the company. Helix folds down to the size of its wheels.

Beyond creating a novel bicycle, Helix also made the bold move to automate its manufacturing facilities to meet requirements for cost, quality and consistency. A LASERDYNE 430 BeamDirector fibre laser machining system was a key part of the new process to precisely bevel the cut titanium tubing that is used throughout the bicycle.

Above: Titanium tubular components are positioned on the Laserdyne 430BD's horizontally-mounted rotary table. The table features a through-bore chuck to locate and hold the titanium tubes for cutting to the correct angled shape.

Above: Helix bike components are bevel-cut at many different steep angles on the Laserdyne 430 Beam Director fibre laser system. Top right: All tubular components are made of high-strength, lightweight titanium.

With its claim of creating 'the most compact, lightest and safest folding bike yet', Helix Bikes utilises innovative manufacturing processes that make its design possible.

Working closely with Prima Power Laserdyne applications engineers, Helix created a robust, high-quality bevel cutting capability based on the 430BDY system. For Helix, the steeply bevelled edges of the titanium tubing required precise and clean cuts to correctly fit-up the tubing for accurate robotic welding. Additionally, the 430BD fibre cutting system produces holes and slots in the tubular parts.

## Flexibility and strength

"The key is the steep angle bevel cutting of the titanium tubes. This 430BDY capability allowed Helix engineers the flexibility to design the bike assembly for maximum rigidity and strength with the least weight, where other tube cutting systems had limited or no titanium angle cutting capabilities," commented Prima Power.

"To meet these challenging and different bevelled angles, there are various capabilities within the LASERDYNE 430BDY system," reported Corey Hansen, regional sales manager for Prima Power Laserdyne who helped to develop the process.

"A key system feature utilises a horizontally-mounted rotary table with a through-bore chuck to locate and hold the titanium tubes for cutting to shape. Titanium is the ideal material for the Helix Bike design because of its high strength, lightweight, remarkable corrosion resistance and unparalleled durability," added Mr. Hansen. "But it can be challenging to process. The 430BD produces the bevelled cuts up to 45° from the surface without dross or spatter residue so tubes are mated perfectly, ready for the automated welding operations. No secondary deburring or tube clean-up was needed before welding."

## Helix explains why users can benefit

- Safer: Pass-through locking design with secondary safety for a worry-free ride.
- More rigid: Spring-loaded locking device compensates for wear and never needs adjustment.
- One-piece frame: Side-by-side folding allows for a one-piece hingefree frame that won't flex or creak while pedalling.
- Folds in any order: The rear wheel, front wheel and handlebar can be folded in any order.
- Pedals won't get in the way: No need to rotate pedals – Helix's wheels fit between them.
- Rolls when folded: Helix is easy to carry and easier to roll.

## An accent on control

Another helpful 430BDY feature is the system's integrated control of 6-axis laser motion allowing LASERDYNE application engineers the capability to develop the robust and repeatable process for angle-cutting the titanium tubular components. The 430 BeamDirector® system incorporates LASERDYNE'S third generation BeamDirector®. This gives users, such as Helix, the capability for not only bevel cutting but also drilling cylindrical and shaped holes and welding a wide range of materials in addition to titanium, all with the same system. With up to six axes of motion, the system provides strong flexibility.

Helix will be available in three specs: 10-speed derailleur, 11-speed internal gear hub and a single speed. Titanium, said Helix, is 'the only material for a folding bike that is lightweight, durable and can last forever'.

"Titanium is lightweight, durable, remarkably corrosion-resistant and has excellent ride characteristics. It is an exceptional material for bicycle manufacturing. A titanium frame can rival the weight of a high-end carbon frame but with a level of durability that is unparalleled. Folding bikes are constantly being handled; they are folded, lifted, put in trunks and bags – durability is a necessity," it told ISMR.

"Helix was designed to go everywhere, whether it's in the subway, the trunk of your car or in a suitcase. World class engineering and analysis have gone into these bikes, making Helix as compact and light as possible without compromising ride quality and durability. These bikes are proudly made in Canada using the latest manufacturing processes. We are the first company in the world to robotically weld bicycles in an inert chamber. This process creates the best possible welds. Our titanium tubes are laser cut in-house with a proprietary process for perfect weld fit-up and mechanical properties," explained the company.

Helix CEO, Peter Boutakis, also underlined: "The 6-axis LASERDYNE 430 BeamDirector is fast, flexible and provides the high-quality processing that we needed to produce an industry-leading bicycle for our customers. The addition of the 430BD was a critical aspect of our manufacturing and the LASERDYNE team showed a strong willingness to partner towards the goal of producing a high-quality laser cut. This helped make possible our drive towards a smaller, lighter, safer and easier-to-use foldable bike.

"There is a lot of excitement in the bicycle market about the Helix design," added Mr. Boutakis. "We have a backlog of over 1700 Helix bike orders at present. Full production has begun and we anticipate shipping our first bikes in September this year."
