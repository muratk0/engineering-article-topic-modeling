# A FIBRE FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

ISMR caught up with Gaetano Lo Guzzo, Director, Laser Business Europe, Mazak Europe to discuss Mazak’s latest technology launches and new initiatives as its workforce returned from the COVID-19 lockdown.

YAMAZAKI MAZAK, which celebrated its 100th anniversary last year, was established in 1919 in Japan as a manufacturer of straw mat weaving machinery. Today, it is a leading machine tool manufacturer with a product portfolio which includes multi-tasking centres, CNC turning centres, machining centres and laser processing systems as well as automation.

The company has a strong global footprint with 38 technology centres, 10 factories and 47 technical centres worldwide. It specialises in laser cutting technology, particular fibre laser cutting systems, CO lasers and, more recently, direct diode laser (DDL) technology. Europe is a key market, taking more than 30% of its global sales.

## Investing for success

“We celebrated our 100-year anniversary last year – a major milestone for us. We ran a lot of events worldwide to celebrate this.

We also established a new factory in Japan (bringing our total to five factories in Japan now) at the beginning of 2019. Over the last year, our other four factories in Japan have been revamped – a massive investment for us. We also opened an underground machine tool museum (10,000 square metres) last November in Japan which showcases Mazak and other machine tools,” Lo Guzzo told ISMR.

Mazak completed the massive iSMART Factory™ upgrade of its Minokamo 1 and 2 factories in Japan last year and has now upgraded its UK factory to iSMART status.

iSMART Factory™ status is Mazak’s highest level of Industry 4.0 connectivity. These facilities join Inabe and Kentucky (in the United States) at the cutting edge of its connected factory capabilities. Mazak now has five factories in Japan, one in Singapore, two in China, one in the USA and one in the UK.

“We had planned to launch new products at the EuroBLECH exhibition this year but it has now been postponed to March 2021, because of the COVID-19 outbreak. We will, however, be at the virtual EuroBLECH Digital Innovation Summit (27-30 October 2020) and will be highlighting our newly launched FT-150 FIBER laser cutting system for tubes – a complementary activity to sheet metal. We started moving into tube processing in 2000, manufacturing machines for processing larger tubes. However, this new machine is designed for the high-speed, large batch cutting of small- and mediumdiameter tube,” added Lo Guzzo.

The new FT-150 FIBER is a compact laser processing machine with the operator area, loading station and unloading station all located on the same side. Equipped with a 3.0kW fiber resonator, the FT-150 FIBER can process single workpieces up to 6,500mm in length and 150kg in weight, with a maximum tube diameter of 152.4mm, 8000mm and 180kg options are also available.

It can perform multiple processes, including cutting, tapping, drilling and thermal drilling. In-process time is reduced as it negates the need for processes such as punch press, deburring and hole tapping. Productivity is further improved due to its large capacity bundle loader, which can automatically load raw material weighing up to four tonnes to enable continuous operation over extended time periods.

The FT-150 FIBER also has several features designed to deliver accurate and high-quality cutting. These include an additional U-axis in the cutting head, which facilitates ultra-high-speed cutting, as well as a tilting laser head for bevel cutting.

THE FT-150 FIBER tube laser cutting system can unload workpieces in a variety of ways, from standard solutions to automatic sorting systems for the efficient production of large-batch and multiple part models. The machine can unload finished parts up to 3000mm in length (a 4500mm option is also available) into parts buckets, or via an optional conveyor belt. The sorting system can sort and unload small, medium and large finished parts in four different areas.

The machine’s ‘Visual Seam Locator’ uses a high-performance camera and image analysis software to detect welding seams on tubes to determine the required radial position for cutting; while the ‘Work Centre Location’ detects any bends or twists in the workpiece, and automatically makes any adjustments before cutting. ## COVID-19 response

The COVID-19 outbreak this year, and subsequent lockdown, led to a slowdown in Mazak’s general laser business. The company has been following safety protocols in each of its factories, including measures such as social distancing, hand washing, temperature checking etc.

“From March until June/July this year, many of our staff were working from home but almost everyone is now back working in our offices and factories. However, we have drastically reduced travel – we will not attend any exhibitions this year – and a lot of our meetings are being held online. We have also held demonstrations of our machinery and equipment to customers online. We only meet them face to face when it is strictly necessary,” explained Gaetano Lo Guzzo.

“We need to stay as closely attuned to our customers as possible, which includes after-sales, service etc. During the COVID-19 outbreak, this has become even more important. To avoid unnecessary machine downtime, we offer remote servicing capabilities to customers – our engineers can remotely diagnose and fix systems. This also includes virtual demonstrations for customers,” he added.

Lo Guzzo hopes that, by Spring next year, the level of COVID infections will drop and travel will resume. In such unprecedented and challenging times, it is difficult to make predictions but he emphasized that Mazak intends to stay agile and ready for any market upturn.

An example of this flexibility, during the COVID crisis, is the development of a ‘non-contact COVID-key’ by one of Mazak’s application engineers. To help avoid contact with possible contaminated surfaces, Tyler Van Wyhe, Applications Engineer, Mazak Optonics Corp, designed and developed the non-contact “COVID-Key”. This laser-cut brass key is suitable for avoiding contact with surfaces when in public places. From opening doors, to pushing buttons on a gas pump or elevator, this key is designed to keep hands less vulnerable to being exposed to germs.

While this key can be cut from a range of metals, brass provides strong protection against germs due to its antimicrobial properties. Antimicrobial properties have an inherent ability to kill harmful microbes quickly on the exposed surface. This makes brass the right metal to use for these non-contact keys.

Mazak customers can cut their own noncontact COVID-Key using their Mazak laser cutting systems – Mazak has made the DXF files publicly available to enable them to do this (email applications@mazaklaser.com).

## Laser in focus

One of Lo Guzzo’s technical focuses is to expand the market for laser applications.

“Laser technology is developing rapidly and power is increasing exponentially. We want to expand laser performance and applications, and this new laser tube processing machine is an example. We also aim to launch a range of new laser cutting systems in 2021, in conjunction with selected partners,” he told ISMR.

“The laser market has been growing continuously over the last ten years. New technologies, such as fibre laser cutting and direct diode technology, have really added value. These have also opened new applications for laser cutting technologies. Fibre laser sales have massively increased and they make up the bulk of our laser sales (78%). We aim to continually increase the power of our laser cutting machines to the market. We currently offer a 10kW fibre laser cutting model and aim to offer higher power fibre lasers to the market next year,” he added.

The company has continuously increased the power in its fibre laser range to increase speed and feed rates, as well as to reduce cycle times. Higher productivity levels give customers a lower cost per part.

“There are still large margins, in terms of market size, to grow fibre laser technology. The latest forecasts indicate that it is still a young technology, with lots of room to grow. The automotive market is moving more to hybrid and electric cars and there are even more applications in these sectors for fibre laser cutting. I can see even more applications for laser in the future,” continued Lo Guzzo.

Mazak is the only player in the sheet metal market to offer DDL (direct diode laser) technology commercially to users – it currently has three DDL models in its portfolio. In 2018, it launched the 3D direct diode laser processing machine, FG-220 DDL, to the market.

The 6kW version of its OPTIPLEX 3015 DDL (Direct Diode Laser) machine is the next step beyond the fibre laser. The resonator enables rapid cutting of stainless steel and aluminium and is up to 15 per cent faster than its fibre equivalent power. The DDL machine also delivers stable cutting for both thin and thick material, thanks to Beam Diameter Control, along with Intelligent Monitoring Functions (IMF) and Intelligent Set-up functions (ISF).

Mazak also extended the DDL product range to its tube cutting systems with the FG-220 DDL 4kW, a Direct Diode Laser version of tube and pipe cutting machines.

## An eye on automation

“Customers today are looking for the best quality – they want to finish the part in one set-up and keep fixed costs as low as possible. That is why automation is important. We want to review and expand our automation product portfolio and believe that the demand for automation will keep increasing,” said Lo Guzzo.

“This also ties into the pace of new technology development. With high-power fibre laser and DDL, cycle times are short and machine speeds are rapid. A human operator simply cannot keep up with the machine’s high speed – you need automation for this. Automation levels have consequently been growing over the last five years,” he added.

He aims to increase cooperation and partnerships with manufacturers on automation (such as load/unloading), hinting that Mazak may differentiate the product range and find partners in different technologies for next year.

Mazak has its eyes firmly set on the future and is determined to stay agile and flexible in the face of any further COVID-19 disruption.

CONSUMER ELECTRONICS, or home electronics, is electronic (analogue or digital) equipment intended for everyday use, typically in private homes. Consumer electronics include devices used for entertainment, communication and recreation.

A wide-ranging field of electronics for the general user includes devices such as TVs; DVD players; radios; cameras; walkietalkies; hi-fi stereo; home theatre; handheld and software-based games; smartphones; tablets; laptops; smartwatches and Internetconnected appliances.

“The consumer electronics’ market size was valued at US$ one trillion in 2019 and is estimated to grow at a CAGR of over 7% between 2020 and 2026. Continuous investments by industry players in R&D for the development of new consumer electronic products (including smartphones, smart wearables and home appliances) will drive market growth,” commented analyst, Global Market Insights.

## Market outlook

Globally, the Consumer Electronics (CE) market increased in value during 2019, adding just over US$ 3 billion to present a worldwide total of US$ 683 billion in trade value. “Connected CE products are no longer ‘sell and forget’,” added Forrest. “There are challenges in maintaining software upgrades and ensuring lifetime value for products already in the market. Finding the optimum moment to discontinue support without alienating customers or weakening brand value is a delicate proposition.”

Across the product categories, headphones, wearables and smart home posted the strongest growth in value throughout 2019, said the analyst. ## Impact of COVID-19

While 2019 was a strong year for the Consumer Electronics industry, the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 has altered the outlook significantly.

“Some categories such as TVs and laptops performed well in H1 2020 as a result of consumers’ response to lockdown,” explained Forrest. “However, in many cases, this is expected to have drawn sales forward from H2, which is likely to be impacted by the economic repercussions.”

COVID-19 has contributed to US$ 88 billion being wiped from the overall forecast CE trade value in 2020, in comparison to the analyst’s pre-COVID-19 forecasts issued last December. The pandemic has dented consumer confidence and disrupted global supply chains. Turbulence across the CE market will persist for several months, said Futuresource, with analysis showing a further US$ 66 billion deficit globally in 2021 trade value compared to previous forecasts.

“Nevertheless, the market shows resilience and we expect it to rebound reasonably quickly, returning to growth next year and with value largely restored to pre-COVID levels by Q4 2022,” explained Forrest.

Headphones, wearables and smart home posted the strongest growth in value throughout 2019.

Despite the uncertainty and imminent recession across regional economies, the long-term outlook for the Consumer Electronics market remains positive, according to Futuresource. The market is expected to increase 20% in volume to total 5.2 billion-unit shipments annually worldwide in 2024, with these products contributing an additional US$ 121 billion of trade value globally.

## Trends and directions

Many consumer electronic devices are built in China, due to maintenance cost, availability of materials, quality and speed. Cities, such as Shenzhen, have become important production centres for the industry, attracting many consumer electronics companies such as Apple Inc.

“The global electronics devices market (including consumer electronics and industrial electronic devices) is expected to be impacted significantly by COVID-19 as China is one of the major suppliers for the raw materials (used to manufacture devices) as well as the finished products. The industry is on the brink of facing a reduction in production, disruption in supply and price fluctuations,” commented analyst, Grand View Research.

“While this can vastly encourage local manufacturers to step up and address the growing demand, the scarcity of raw material can still pose a challenge to this industry. The sales of prominent electronic companies is expected to be affected in the near future. COVID-19 is a key market contributor,” it added.

The industry trends of incorporating microLED technology in televisions and large displays are expected to augment the market size of audio and video consumer electronics over the coming years.

“Leading market players are investing heavily in the development of this technology to cater to high consumer demand. For instance, in January 2020, LG and Samsung, leading consumer electronics manufacturers, announced their plans to launch microLED TVs for residential and commercial applications. The ability of this technology to provide high contrast and brightness compared to conventional LEDs is expected to enhance its market representation,” explained analyst, Global Market Insights.

However, short product lifecycles and increasing product variants are posing significant challenges to the growth of the consumer electronics industry. The dynamically changing technology landscape is encouraging consumers to shift to electronics integrated with the latest technologies. This has led to rapid reduction in the product lifecycle of consumer electronics (such as smartphones, televisions and cameras) hampering market demand. Manufacturers are facing challenges, such as reducing their product development time and time-to-market, in this highly competitive market.

“The rapid spread of the COVID-19 pandemic, since the beginning of January 2020, has adversely impacted the consumer electronics sector. Several factors (including disruptions in supply chain & logistics; restrictions on trade and the acute shortage of raw materials) have hampered the production capacity of consumer electronics manufacturers. Companies including Samsung, Apple and LG suspended their production in several countries (including India, Malaysia and China) to comply with government regulations and contain virus spread. According to the industry forecast, the market is expected to regain momentum in Q1 of 2021,” added Global Market Insights.

According to the analyst, the audio & video consumer electronics market held over 70% share in 2019 owing to the integration of advanced features, such as voice assistant-

enabled control and enhanced connectivity. The proliferation of voice assistants in residential and commercial sectors is disrupting the market. Voice assistants (such as Alexa, Siri and Google Home) enable consumers to monitor and control their devices with a simple voice command. Integration of voice assistant with personal consumer electronics, such as speakers and earphones, will provide a positive outlook. New product launches by consumer electronics manufacturers are also influencing market statistics.

## A global view

“The Asia Pacific consumer electronics market revenue is poised to reach US$ 650 billion by 2026, growing at 8% CAGR during the forecast period. Rapidly growing urbanisation and population growth in the region are driving the adoption of consumer electronics in middle and high-income households. The availability of low-cost consumer electronic products, such as smartphones and TVs, from several local brands is supporting their adoption in low income groups. Increasing awareness related to upcoming technologies amongst younger people is also augmenting the demand in these markets,” added Global Market Insights.

The growing demand is encouraging market leaders to launch new products in the household consumer electronics segment. For instance, in February 2020, Panasonic announced its plans to launch several new connected home appliances for Japanese and Indian markets. The new consumer electronic products in the enhanced product portfolio include smart LED and OLED TVs, kitchen appliances and personal care consumer electronics.

Companies operating in the consumer electronics industry are also laying emphasis on acquiring other firms operating in the ecosystem. Acquisitions aid market players to enlarge their customer bases and significantly increase their market shares. Other significant strategies adopted by market participants include product innovations, new product launches and business expansions in new geographic areas.

5G is anticipated to support wide connectivity among devices and strengthen security, along with other advantages. The rapidly rising construction of smart homes with Wi-Fi enabled households and remote connectivity among smartphones, coupled with enhanced voice recognition technology, will prove to be beneficial for the consumer electronics industry, according to analyst Market Study Report, LLC

“From a regional standpoint, the Middle East & Africa will witness tremendous growth owing to the rapidly rising construction of smart homes equipped with smart household appliances, thereby contributing to the growing market share. The rapid urbanisation and development of smart cities across Saudi Arabia will drive regional demand for consumer electronics over the coming years,” added the analyst.
