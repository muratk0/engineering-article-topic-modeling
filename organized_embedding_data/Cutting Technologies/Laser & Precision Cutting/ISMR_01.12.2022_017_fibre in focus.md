## We highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the global market.

ISMR SAYS:

"Clean-cut edges, rapid cutting speeds and efficient operation characterise fibre laser cutting systems.

In fibre lasers, the laser light is generated and delivered by an inherently flexible medium, which allows easier delivery to the focusing location and target. This is important for laser cutting and welding sheet metal. Other benefits include their high output power and very high optical gain, compared to other types of laser. They can support kilowatt levels of continuous output power thanks to the fibre's high surface-area-to-volume ratio, which allows efficient cooling.

The fibre's waveguide properties reduce or eliminate thermal distortion of the optical path, typically producing a diffraction-limited, high-quality optical beam. Fibre lasers are compact compared to solid-state or gas lasers of comparable power, because the fibre can be bent and coiled (except for thicker rodtype designs) to save space. They exhibit high temperature and vibrational stability, as well as extended lifetime. High peak power and nanosecond pulses improve marking and engraving. The additional power and better beam quality provide clean cut edges and rapid cutting speeds.

## Fibre laser innovations

As a relatively new innovation in laser cutting systems for machining sheet metal (including non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium), the fibre laser offers accuracy, low maintenance costs and efficient operation. It is easy to cool, converting around 70-80% of its power from the pump source. This is better for power consumption, but also means that less power is being converted to heat. It can also be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials, as well as fine feature cutting.

In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

AMADA's new three-axis REGIUS-AJ fibre laser with linear drive, variable beam adjustment and autonomous laser functions is its fastest fibre laser system to date. Promoted at Blechexpo in October 2021, all system and process parameters are automatically monitored and adjusted by its laser integration system (LIS). LIS functions include the Nozzle Checker, the i-Process Monitor and the i-Optics Sensor. Readiness for cutting is monitored during operation and automatically restored after collisions. "Variable beam adjustment automatically and continuously matches energy distribution in the laser beam (not just focus diameter and focus position) to the material to be processed and its thickness. The mode can be adjusted as required during piercing and cutting, making it possible to run the piercing process at high speeds and with increased productivity," AMADA explained.

With its VENTIS-3015AJ 4 kW, AMADA recently introduced a laser cutting system with flexible beam guidance.

"With a 4kW fibre laser resonator, a single laser module and no power combiner performance is achieved that's usually only possible with 6- or 8kW systems. Using innovative LBC technology, the machine can oscillate the laser beam in the kerf in accordance with predefined patterns. All this results in significantly increased cutting speeds for thin, as well as thick, sheet metal. Achieved surface roughness values are unparalleled in the fibre laser sector and are comparable in quality to those of a CO2 laser. At the same time, the otherwise usual fibre laser burr can be almost entirely eliminated," AMADA told ISMR.

The new ASF II-3015 storage system, which is laid out for a sheet format of 3 × 1.5m and a cutting pallet load of 920kg, is operated electrically without any hydraulics, and equipped with a new pallet changer. It works with a new rolling system so that the raw material is always on the upper pallet and the cut material is always discharged from the laser machine at a lower level. This prevents remnants from falling from the upper to the lower table and causing collisions. The storage system can be operated in automatic or semiautomatic mode, as well as in manual mode using its manually loadable, single-sheet pallet.
