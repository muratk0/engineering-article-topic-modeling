# DRIVING SUCCESS AT R&S

[TARİH: 01.09.2013 ISMR]

Continued investment in new equipment and automation drives success at R&S

Not all subcontract profiling and fabrication shops are the same. Some display such tenacity for investment in the latest manufacturing technologies that others are frequently left playing catch-up.

UK-based R&S Laser Cutting & Fabrications Ltd is a case in point. Here, recent investment in a new Amada LC 4020 F1 4kW laser with ASLUL automation tower has added to an already impressive machine capacity list, making R&S one of the best equipped mid-sized shops in the Midlands.

## The path to automation

Disillusioned with working in the employment of others, Rob Frain and his business partner, Stuart Hutchins, set out alone in 1996 armed with little more than a welding set and a drilling machine. However, by January 1997, the company had already invested in a pre-owned Amada laser cutter and new Amada press brake – the latter is still in daily operation as one of seven Amada press brakes on-site today.

“We take good care of our machines – they all have services by Amada personnel twice a year which extends their useful life considerably and helps avoid painful downtime,” explained Rob Frain. “Amada’s back-up and service is second-to-none - it’s the reason why they have retained my business.”

R&S has enjoyed considerable growth in the past five years. In 2007, the company re-located to its present site in the Hockley area of Birmingham, doubling its floor space to 30,500 sq. ft. The move also paved the way for the acquisition of an Amada EML 3610 punch/laser combination machine with MP300 automation.

In a similar vein, the company has invested in an Amada F0 3015 4kW laser and, most recently, an Amada LC 4020 F1 4kW linear drive laser. Both machines feature an Amada ASLUL automation tower, allowing unmanned running for extended periods thanks to their 21-tonne handling and storage capacity (R&S Laser is a single-shift operation).

## Culture of continuous investment

We have always believed in continuous investment to improve our processes,” said Rob Frain. “Today, we can offer our customers a full portfolio of services, from concept and design, through to laser profiling, punching, forming, welding, machining, degreasing, painting, assembly and delivery.”

Developing so much in-house capability demonstrates that this is a business with a management team determined to make headway in a fiercely competitive marketplace. For instance, R&S recently installed its own nitrogen-generating plant for the laser machines and is now looking at other energy-saving measures such as ultra-efficient factory lighting and alternatives to conventional chiller plant.

R&S Laser, which is accredited to ISO9001:2008, says that it excels at projectbased work. Receiving a CAD file, developing it out for cutting and bending, and making design suggestions to save on manufacturing costs for the customer is a distinguishing factor in the success of this 34-employee business. It has enjoyed considerable success helping automotive companies who want to see how a new part functions before committing to a press tool which could cost in the region of £250,000.

Ultimately, this business is based on quality, flexibility and service

Ensuring that projects are completed in the required timescale whilst maintaining total quality is a core focus at R&S. KPIs are monitored regularly throughout the business, and the company operates its own fleet of dedicated delivery vehicles.

R&S processes a range of materials from 0.5mm thick, including mild steel of up to 12mm, stainless steel up to 10mm and aluminium up to 6mm. In terms of batch sizes, anything from 1- off upwards can be accommodated

## An agile response

“Ultimately this business is based on quality, flexibility and service,” explained Rob Frain. “Yes, we can offer an ultra-cost-competitive price based on a budget service, but we also always present the alternative option of quality which we believe is of far greater benefit to the customer. The parts we supply are typically passed on further up the supply chain. If the quality isn’t there then the job is probably lost. In this industry, everyone is only as good as their last job. That’s the way it works.”

The next exciting phase in the evolution of this progressive manufacturing business is the development of its own product line. The company is already in the final stages of developing its own portfolio of radiant ceiling panels commonly used in offices, schools and hospitals, as well as a range of trench floor heating units. R&S has the manufacturing capacity in place and will sell the products via satellite agents located strategically around the UK, and through an online ‘boutique’.

“Some private businesses see their resources drained over the year but, at R&S, we prefer to re-invest in the company rather than pull money out, even when times are tough. This continuous investment has kept us at the forefront of our industry,” concluded Frain.
