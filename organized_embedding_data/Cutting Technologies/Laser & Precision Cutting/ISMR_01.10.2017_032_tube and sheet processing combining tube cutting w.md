# TUBE AND SHEET PROCESSING Combining tube cutting with flat sheet processing on a new BLM machine fits ML Fabcuts’ philosophy to be a one-stop shop for its customers.

[TARİH: 01.10.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“ML Fabcuts is diversifying with integrated tube and sheet processing”

Seeing an opportunity 14 years ago, Matthew Levett and his wife Maria founded ML Fabcuts to provide a subcontract service for waterjet and flat sheet laser profiling. When the need to relocate to its current premises in Crayford, UK, arose in 2015, Matt saw this as an opportunity to review ML Fabcuts' output with greater emphasis placed on laser profiling, while retaining some waterjet cutting capability.

Now, two years on from that move, the company is tapping into new potential business with the addition of laser tube processing following a major investment in a BLM LC5 flat-bed laser with integrated tube cutting module.

## A rapid response

ML Fabcuts built its reputation on its rapid response to customers' needs, which it achieved through its willingness to stock sheet in various materials from 0.7mm thick up to 50mm thick, alongside a strong team ethic at the family-owned business that makes sure the job gets completed on time. The result is an extremely diverse customer base ranging from motorsport through to the construction industry and signmaking sector to processing one-off commissions for artists. The arrival of the BLM LC5 will further enhance its level of customer service, it believes.

"We are all motivated to deliver on our promises, and our list of long-standing customers is a testament to the fact that we are achieving that," said ML Fabcuts' production manager, James Waldron-Stevens.

While Matt Levett identified the potential opportunities presented by tube laser cutting, he remained cautious and did not want to commit to a dedicated tube laser machine. However, during a visit to the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, both Matt and James had what they describe as a 'eureka moment' after seeing the 5kW fiber laser BLM LC5 with integrated tube cutting module.

"We had known about BLM and its reputation for tube processing, but when we saw that it was possible to combine tube cutting with flat sheet processing in a seamless package it was almost an instant decision to place an order. The combination of flat bed and tube gave us peace of mind as the machine could still be used as a flat-bed machine if the tube business didn't develop," said James. As it is, with the machine only being installed in May 2017, it is already running at 50/50 tube/sheet capacity with the aim of increasing its tube processing activities to 75 per cent.

## Making the switch

The efficiency of the BLM LC5 is enhanced by its use of a single laser source and cutting head for both sheet and tube processing which makes switching from one process to another extremely quick. ML Fabcuts already makes use of this feature to meet customer demands.

"We simply push a couple of buttons and we can stop processing sheet, cut some tube, then return to where we left off with the sheet once we have finished the tube work. It is a feature that I’m sure we will make very good use of to meet urgent deliveries," added James.

Automation is central to the design of the LC5 as it comes with automated tube bundle loading, capable of handling tube up to 6.5-metres in length and up to 120mm in diameter. Sheet loading is handled by the BLM Electric sheet pallet changer, which can be specified as 3000 x 1500, 4000 x 2000mm or 6000 x 2000mm in size and can be transverse or in-line configuration. ML Fabcuts also took the option of automatic nozzle change which, as well as changing the nozzle with up to 18 available nozzle positions, automatically adjusts for material and thickness changes, with a vision system monitoring nozzle wear and providing verification that the change has been completed.

Given the size and configuration of the LC5, it comes with two operator panels, one next to the tube processing area, with the second in front of the sheet processing area.

"We look at the LC5 now as our flagship machine. While we bought it for its tube processing capacity, the option of being able to instantly switch to flat sheet means that we have also increased our sheet processing capacity and it fits our philosophy perfectly, adding to our commitment to be a one-stop-shop for customers," explained James.

ML Fabcuts has also witnessed an improvement in cut quality from the BLM Fiber laser, due to the additional power that it offers over its existing lasers. This has improved cut capability and virtually eliminated any deburring requirement, particularly when cutting aluminium.

"The entire process from examining the machine at EuroBLECH to installing it at our Crayford factory was straightforward. During the initial discussions, Jon Curtis at BLM answered all our questions and gave us the confidence that we were ordering exactly what we needed, with no pressure to sign on the dotted line. Once the machine was installed, Matt and experienced estimator Connor Dulson spent a week at BLM in Italy to familiarise themselves with the Artube offline programming software. This initial training was followed up with on-site training from BLM at our factory in Crayford for Simon Coe and two other operators as back-up," concluded James Waldron-Stevens.

"From there, it has all been plain sailing. Our challenge now is to keep it fed with work as its efficiency means that it is a hungry beast, but the reaction we are getting from customers now that we have this combination of tube and flat sheet is very positive," he added.

A SHARP FOCUS

Kjellberg Finsterwalde presented a number of new plasma cutting and welding innovations at the Schweissen and Schneiden exhibition in Düsseldorf, centred around Industry 4.0.

Kjellberg Finsterwalde presented a number of new and exciting innovations at the Schweissen and Schneiden exhibition in Düsseldorf recently. Showing ISMR around its booth at the show, the German manufacturer explained how many of its new innovations are centred around the central pillar of Industry 4.0.

"Industry 4.0 is a major focus for our industry right now," Carsten Trentau, the company's managing director, told ISMR. "It is the future and I believe that the digitisation of production is really important. We have initiated some major projects in the company around Industry 4.0 and here, on our booth, you will see various new innovations designed with Industry 4.0 in mind."

For Kjellberg Finsterwalde, Industry 4.0 still puts the user at the heart of the issue because, as it told ISMR, the user's productivity should be increased and work processes should be made easier. As a well-known manufacturer of plasma cutting systems, the company offers plasma and laser cutting equipment for a wide range of tasks in automated, mechanised or manual cutting. As a manufacturer of welding systems and welding electrodes, it also offers its customers equipment and filler materials for various welding processes i.e. submerged arc welding, InFocus high-performance TIG welding, PTA welding and manual welding.

## Plasma bevel cutting

For over 20 years, Kjellberg Finsterwalde has developed and manufactured special plasma torches for bevel cutting. A new addition to its family of plasma bevel torches, the PB-S75 WF, was showcased on the company's stand. It can be used for multiple bevel cuts up to 60°.

"Using a particularly flat design, the torch is suitable for straight and bevel cuts and also in cases of limited accessibility (i.e. profiles). If it is used in multiple-torch bevel fixtures, work steps are reduced and productivity can be increased," the manufacturer told ISMR.

"We will, in the future, provide bevel cutting data under the name 'PerfectBevel' for preparing sheet with thicknesses of between 5 and 30mm. Using the reference values in the database, users can make precise bevel cuts quickly and easily in Contour Cut quality for mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium. Taking optimum weld preparation into consideration, and depending upon the material and material thickness used, compensation values are specified for V, Y, X and K welds. The high precision and reproducibility reduces material usage for welding and increases productivity because waste, and therefore also rework, can be minimised. The PerfectBevel cutting database will be available for all machines in the HiFocus neo series."

## Plasma-transferred arc welding

At Schweissen & Schneiden, the company also presented a new torch for Plasma-Transferred Arc (PTA) welding. The PlasmaT 410M Fidur torch can process two types of welding powder – either pre-mixed or separately in a defined sequence. With the same torch, it is also possible to bring a single welding powder before or behind the arc. Depending upon the wear type and the corresponding powder mixtures, this is used to set the weld penetration and dilution as well as the optimum structure of the hard coating.

"Reliable and constant supply of the welding powder guarantees an even application of the coating. Efficient cooling with two cooling circuits allows a current load of up to 400 Amperes at a duty cycle of 100%. This way, up to 9kg welding flux can be processed per hour and, if required, the coating speed can also be increased," explained Jana Müller, Marketing Welding Technique.

"The technical requirement for using this torch is the PSI-400, a powerful inverter power source for plasma-transferred arc welding. In addition to a maximum welding current of 400 Amperes at a duty cycle of 100%, this machine is very easy to handle; operation is via a touch display."

Different user levels open several options of how deeply to influence the welding parameters and data management. In the simplest case, the pilot current, main current, gas and powder quantities can be adjusted via a menu. At the next level up, the welding regime, pulse parameters, gas and powder quantities are entered via a graphical user interface. The highest level offers free parameter configuration and extensive process control. At this level, the two powder supply units are controlled separately. The powder can be calibrated directly at the machine. This data is also stored in the database.

At Schweissen & Schneiden, the company also showed ISMR welding examples produced with the PlasmaT 410M Fidur torch.

## Cutting mild steel with reduced sound

Due to the high energy density and flow velocity during plasma cutting, sound levels can be more than 100 dB(A), depending upon the cutting task. With its new Silent Cut technology, introduced at the show, Kjellberg Finsterwalde now offers a solution to reduce the noise exposure, which is perceived as disturbing for human hearing (particularly in the upper frequency range).

"As a further development of our patented Contour Cut technology, Silent Cut reduces sound pressure levels by up to 15 dB(A) during plasma cutting of mild steels with a material thickness of between four and 30mm in the current range between 60 and 160 A. High frequencies perceived as particularly disturbing can be considerably reduced. A reduction of the sound level by ten decibels is already perceived in human hearing as a halving of the loudness. So, Silent Cut makes an important contribution to health and safety at work – with high cut quality," Carsten Trentau told ISMR.

"Users can cut holes, bars and contours with the same high quality as our Contour Cut as well as benefit from considerably reduced sound pressure levels. The technology can also be used for existing systems so Silent Cut will be available for our Smart Focus plasma series from December 2017, and for HiFocus neo from January 2018."

ISMR saw a live demonstration of mild steel plasma cutting on two guiding systems. During the direct comparison of Contour Cut technology, with Silent Cut, the high cut quality of both technologies was clearly visible, as was the reduced sound pressure levels with Silent Cut.

## InFocus – a special TIG process

"InFocus welding is an established, highperformance alternative to the conventional TIG welding process. Offering a wide range of applications, with InFocus you can master diverse welding tasks more efficiently than with conventional processes. Each user has new or different demands for the use in fully and partly automated systems. Ranging from standard aluminium and special aluminium alloys to mild steel and stainless steel i.e. 1.4301 or 1.4471, but also copper, titanium zinc and galvanized sheets or profiles can be welded with InFocus," Jana Müller told ISMR.

"Our application centre for welding techniques in Finsterwalde is equipped to carry out feasibility studies. There are robots, a longitudinal welding machine and a linear axle available for determining the optimum welding parameters. With this equipment, we can identify the welding parameters for the most diverse component materials and geometries. Even weld seams in constrained positions on sheets with a thickness of between 1-6mm can be done with InFocus."

A highly focused arc and cooling system are features of the InFocus system. Torches with small geometric interference and small design can be used on components which are difficult to access. ISMR watched the application of InFocus technology on a longitudinal welding machine on Kjellberg Finsterwalde's stand at Schweissen & Schneiden.
