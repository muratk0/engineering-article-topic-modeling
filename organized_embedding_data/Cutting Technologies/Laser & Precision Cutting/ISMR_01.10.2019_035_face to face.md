## Laser cutting in focus

Vega, the process developed by CYLASER in 2018 that exploits laser beam diameter and a Programmable Shape laser beam, enables users to automatically select the laser spot dimension with the same optics set-up. It is designed to increase the cutting quality for any metal, substantially reduce the cutting roughness on stainless steel and increase the quality of the cut on thicker materials. This includes enhanced cut quality on stainless steel and aluminium.

Vortex is a process that is designed to enable users to obtain higher cutting quality on >8mm material thicknesses. With the Vortex feature, the user can take full advantage of fibre laser’s potential through beam emission frequency modulation and in-kerf gas flow management. Benefits, Vanin told ISMR, include lower roughness on stainless steel cutting edge; reduced gas consumption in comparison to standard parameters; lower material heating so that most complicated patterns can be cut and easier part extraction from scrap.

CYLASER's Vega machine uses a new type of generator plus its proprietary cutting head to fill the quality gap with CO2 laser cutting systems on stainless steel. Vega fills this gap for stainless steel cutting of stainless steel of between 1-6mm thickness. Vortex is CYLASER's fibre laser cutting system for medium to high material thicknesses (8-20mm). Vega offers up to 4kW of power, whilst Vortex starts from 4kW and above.

"Nowadays, many manufacturers are offering increasingly higher power laser cutting systems to cut thicker material but we disagree with this policy. Users have to look at operating costs. It does not make sense to invest in a 12/15kW machine if 90% of your production is processing 1mm material, as the ROI would not be justified. What type of precision cut do you need on 14/15mm plates? You can do that on a plasma machine or with oxyflame cutting, which is far less expensive. 10kW power cuts basically the same as 6kW, at a higher speed, but the investment is significantly higher. It’s all about smart cutting and we always advise our customers to bear this in mind when purchasing a fibre laser cutting system. We care about a customer’s investment. Our philosophy is to advise him on the best technology or the most suitable power needed, with the aim of reaching ROI in the shortest time possible," Federico Campana, President, CYLASER told ISMR.

As the industry gears up for Industry 4.0, CYLASER systems can be integrated seamlessly with other machinery in the production cycle using CYLASER Open Source software (which can manage most automation systems). Remote and selfdiagnosis software applications enable remote control and assistance.

## Looking to the future

With a steady plan for growth and the continuous development of new and more innovative fibre laser models, the future looks promising for this fibre laser specialist.

Federico Campana outlined his vision for the development of various business units within CYLASER.

"We want to build up our 3D cutting and tube business units for the future and I have also been researching the suitability of additive manufacturing for future CYLASER operations," he told ISMR,. "We still believe that fibre laser technology has a strong future, although we have looked at other technologies such as Direct Diode Laser (DDL) cutting. Our company philosophy is to build solid and reliable machines, offering flexibility and partnership to our customers. The key is to balance a machine’s operational costs with the power, precision and speed required by a customer, and build from there…" he concluded.
