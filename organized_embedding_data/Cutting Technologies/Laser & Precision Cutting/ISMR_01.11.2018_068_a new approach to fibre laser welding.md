# A new approach to fibre laser welding

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

"OUR NEW CLEANWELD technology represents an integrated approach to fibre laser welding that delivers up to 80% spatter reduction, as well as minimal cracking and porosity. In addition to improved process consistency, it allows certain welding processes to be performed with up to 40% less laser power," Coherent/ROFIN told ISMR.

Its experience in delivering beam intensity profiles, including the new "ARM" fibre laser technology, is just one aspect of the CleanWeld approach. The company's processoptimised delivery optics and focusing heads, welding knowledge and in-house applications development capabilities provide customers with laser welding solutions tailored to their specific requirements.

"While fibre lasers have been used in welding for over a decade, many end-users are still seeking to improve part quality, production throughput and process costs," reported Frank Gäbler, Coherent's director of product marketing. "We provide an integrated solution that is much more than just the laser itself. This is necessary because there are numerous factors besides the laser that affect the welding process. The real goal is to control and maximise the stability of the keyhole and melt pool during the welding process – that's what produces superior results.

"Accomplishing this can require a variety of techniques that range from changing the intensity distribution of the focused laser spot and introducing beam motion to other factors, such as controlling vapour evacuation. CleanWeld addresses all of these areas, and the benefits of this approach are already being adopted in diverse applications, including zero-gap welding of galvanized steel, automobile powertrain component welding and aluminium and copper welding for electric car batteries."

Another important product debut was the new HighLight SQD high-power fibre lasers which incorporate "smart" QD fibre connectors. These lasers include lightweight, compact process sensors which are integrated directly in the SQD fibre connector. These sensors deliver information to signal important process events, such as piercing end, loss of cut and plasma formation. Welding processes can be monitored to detect defects like blow holes, missing connections and insufficient penetration. This data can then be utilised by quality management systems to increase yields, reduce scrap and rework, and lower production costs. See www.coherent.com
