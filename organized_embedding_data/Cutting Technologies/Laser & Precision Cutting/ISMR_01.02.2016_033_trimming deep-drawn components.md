# Trimming deep-drawn components

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 ISMR]

AT THE PLATE component manufacturer, Solvaro, a combination of robots and lasers results in speed and process reliability in the manufacture of deep-drawn components such as air grilles for agricultural and construction machinery. The company’s 100 m2 3D laser machine features no less than three automatic co-workers that see to the uninterrupted processing of the parts which are cut into shape by the laser.

Components that have been deep-drawn can then be trimmed in a 3D laser machine. This offers a wide array of advantages. No expensive cutting tools are needed, and this makes the processing of small batches economical. The set-up times are also short when switching between the component types being processed. But the most important factor, said the company, is accurate cutting. With the manufacturer often producing assembly parts, this is very important since the individual parts must fit precisely when put together.

“The time-consuming and costly corrective work often required when the components are deep-drawn after being cut is completely eliminated by our 3D machine,” confirmed managing director, Gerhard Bullinger. “The machine is primarily used to manufacture air grilles for agricultural and construction machinery such as the Kubota M7-171 tractor.”

No less than three Kawasaki robots working alternately ensure that the machine is continuously fed. Once a component has been picked up by a robot, it can be tackled from any angle. Flexibility is increased further by a movable cutting head. Up to 300 components are cut into shape during each shift. The 100m2 machine is operated by an employee behind a protective wall. ■
