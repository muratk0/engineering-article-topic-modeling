An eye on savings

Looking more closely at how the new Kerf plasma will create significant savings, Chris Reynolds continued: “As a business, we typically have six staff manufacturing parts to complete an average throughput of two trailers each day. Most of the parts require guillotining; notching; drilling; grinding; corner rounding; bending and welding before galvanising. Each of these processes can be slow and labour-intensive and, with each subsequent operation, there is increased opportunity for error and potential scrappage.

“With the Kerf machine, we can do many of these operations in a single setup. Going forward, this will free at least two employees for other tasks. A labour saving of at least £40k is great, but equally important for a company in a rural area is that the plasma cutter allows us to grow the business whilst re-distributing the highly skilled staff that we have,” he added.

Providing a practical example of savings, the mudguards on each trailer are processed in quantities of six to eight from an 8 x 4ft sheet of 1.5mm thick black steel.

“The batch of mudguards would take ten minutes to cut on a guillotine, then another ten minutes on our variable angle notching machine. This is followed by another ten minutes for marking and subsequent drilling with the final process before bending as the grinding and rounding of the corners, which takes another five minutes. If you add a further 35 minutes of additional time to move the mudguards from machine to machine and position the parts in jigs, you are looking at over 40 minutes,” explained Reynolds.

“The Kerf plasma will profile and cut the holes in less than ten minutes with far superior precision. Not only does the Kerf machine reduce our cycle times by more than 75%, but it also removes the opportunity for operator error, improves quality and consistency, eliminates hand finishing and frees capacity from existing machines. We’ve only had the Kerf machine for a matter of weeks and it has already lightened the workload of our staff and our machines, streamlining our production. Our guillotine was working for over four hours each day; now it’s only used for one hour each day.”

## Design for life

Manufacturing ATV; livestock; flatbed; beavertail; container; motorbike; boat; tipper; tilt-bed; signage; car transporter and camping trailers to name a few, each design can contain over 200 components. At present the Kerf high-definition plasma is only cutting 15- 20 different components but this will rapidly change, according to Mark Reynolds.

“Our designs and aesthetics were limited by our machine capabilities as well as a limited appetite for innovative designs from the ‘function-first’ approach of the agricultural sector. The Kerf machine unlocks a world of opportunity to re-design trailers for improved aesthetics, functionality, light-weighting and even the potential for reduced components and shorter assembly times. The mudguards are one component that we have already redesigned to improve the aesthetics, reduce material usage and weight, and also offer a wider range of shapes and sizes. This is the first demonstration of how we can move our designs forward to create a greater appeal among the consumer market,” he told ISMR.

Another example of design for manufacture and aesthetics is the winch post for boat trailers. Previously manufactured from three pieces of 5mm thick steel that are each cut to size, punched, notched and then jigged for welding, each winch post takes thirty minutes to produce – a time only achieved when efficiently processing in batches of over twenty.

“The capabilities of the Kerf machine have allowed us to redesign the winch posts and these are now profiled with holes in less than two minutes, with an additional minute for second-op bending,” continued Mark Reynolds.

“We have also re-designed the hinge assemblies for loading gates on agricultural trailers. We produce over forty of these thin long parts every month and they were cut and punched before welding two gudgeon pins to each hinge. The gudgeon pins were made externally so, by re-designing the hinge assemblies, we have eliminated the requirement, cost and lead time of the gudgeon pins. Most importantly, the cycle time has been cut from six minutes per part to 30 seconds,” he added.

The hinges are an example of how the capabilities and precision of the Kerf machine have enabled CLH Trailers to redesign an existing part to eliminate subcontract costs and reduce cycle times, material requirements and the weight of the part. Additionally, it can set the RUR2500p to cut forty hinges and leave the machine running to do other tasks.

“Essentially, the Kerf machine is giving me more time to be productive elsewhere and even look at other opportunities for the business,” explained Mark Reynolds.

## The future in focus

Looking to the future, CLH Trailers’ managing director, Chris Hussell, had a few observations:

“We have always undertaken an element of subcontract metalworking and fabrication, but now we can extend this significantly. The Kerf machine has only been here a short while and we are already doing decorative wrought iron fencing, cutting letters, signage and much more. The potential is huge.

Equally significant are the savings. On top of the labour and cycle time savings, the reduced waste and added capacity throughout our facility has been hugely noticeable in a short period,” he commented.

However, one of the greatest savings for CLH Trailers is in material. It orders six tonnes of sheet steel every month and an additional four-five tonnes of box section. Up to 30% of this is wasted in off-cuts that are sent back for recycling.

“With the Kerf plasma cutter, we have already increased our material utilisation from 70% to 85% and the more familiar we become with the machine, the more material and costs we will save. This is because of the Lantek software and Ultrasharp cutting technology that nests the parts very close together to minimise waste. Furthermore, we can use off-cuts from larger parts to nest and produce small batches of smaller parts, such as brackets, so that we waste minimal amounts of material. We are very excited about the future of our business,” concluded Chris Hussell. n

THE SMART ISMR SAYS: “The UK subcontractor is pushing towards automation, high efficiency and lean manufacturing in the drive to achieve its smart factory vision.” FACTORY VISION

Alpha Manufacturing has improved its profitability with new nesting software for sheet metal parts.

Powerful automatic nesting in new CAD/CAM software has helped a sheet metal subcontractor to plan its raw material utilisation as part of its Smart Factory vision.

Alpha Manufacturing, in the UK, provides a full range of sheet metal services across several industry sectors, including automotive, agricultural, healthcare, retail and electronics. The company was established in 1989 specifically to manufacture products for its sister company, Bri-Stor Systems, which had been set up six years previously. Bri-Stor specialises in light commercial vehicle conversions and supplies internal racking, roof equipment, on-board power and accessories.

Bri-Stor and Alpha Manufacturing share a 35-acre site at Hixon, in Staffordshire. Bri-Stor’s solutions comprise consultancy and design through to in-house manufacture, installation and livery application. Customers range from those with just a few vehicles to hundreds, including many well-known UK fleets.

## Sheet metal production

Modular products for standard conversions include options designed for particular sectors while bespoke solutions create kits which are fully customised around the specific needs of a customer’s fleet. For example, Alpha Manufacturing’s Vantage range is an innovative storage system designed to maximise usable space within the van. Unlike most internal racking systems, this features an asymmetric design which allows up to three times more tools or materials to be stored.

Initially, Bri-Stor outsourced its sheet metal fabrication requirements before the decision was taken to build up its own manufacturing arm with Alpha Manufacturing. Alpha’s marketing manager, Chris Kidney, said: “Originally, we were dedicated to producing the van kit-out components for Bri-Stor but we now have a large portfolio of subcontract work, which actually outweighs our Bri-stor production.”

Alpha Manufacturing’s Vantage range is an innovative storage system designed to maximise usable space within the van

All components for both sides of Alpha’s business go through dedicated sheet metal CAD/CAM software, RADAN, from Hexagon Manufacturing Intelligence. Chris Kidney highlighted how important it is that parts are high-precision, often needing to be within +/- 0.2.

The process begins with laser cutting and punching parts on TRUMPF, Bystronic and Pullmax CNC machines programmed with RADAN toolpaths and nests, before moving through the factory for folding, fabrication, welding, powder coating and assembly.

## Improving efficiency

“Our greatest challenge was meeting customer deliveries on time, which RADAN CAD/CAM software overcomes with speedy programming, and by nesting components for individual van conversion kits on the same nest,” explained Chris Kidney.

Alpha Manufacturing’s team of three full-time programmers makes full use of the software’s powerful nesting functionality, analysing the true shape, material and thicknesses of all components in a batch, separating and sorting automatically, to produce manufacturable, high-utilisation nests both from full sheets and offcuts.

“As there is no limit to nesting components of different sizes and shapes onto different materials and thicknesses, this delivers substantial material savings and improves machine efficiency,” added Chris Kidney.

When the nests have been finalised and are ready for cutting, two of Alpha’s machines – a TRUMPF TruPunch 6000 laser/ punch combination machine and a TRUMPF S12 punch press – can both feed from a STOPA automated storage system for greater efficiency and time savings. The entire STOPA system now runs 62 metres down the centre of the factory and contains 230 pallets capable of storing around 600 tonnes of sheet metal. It manages the process of automating stock storage, and movement between machines, with all raw sheet metal stored in the towers for call-off at the touch of a button.

## Profitability in focus

Alpha Manufacturing’s programming manager, Mark Clews, explained: “At the very start of the process, our design team is given a SolidWorks drawing which it breaks down into a DXF file. This is then imported into RADAN. The programmers open the file, along with the nesting works order which tells them exactly what needs to be programmed. They put that into their nesting schedule, sorting it into parts of the same thickness and those of different thicknesses. The tool paths are applied at the nesting stage to maintain the quality and integrity of the parts, while optimising the cutting sequence, enabling the machine tool to perform to its full potential.”

Chris Kidney added: “We can process the parts extremely quickly from initial design to getting them on the shop floor ready for machining. This has a significant effect on delivery times to Bri-Stor and subcontract customers which, in turn, improves our profitability.

“We’re currently pushing towards automation, high efficiency and lean manufacturing. The focus of this plan is on utilising high-tech machine automation to realise a ‘smart factory’ vision. Now, close to £4 million has been invested to secure our position as one of the most technically advanced factories in the industry. The Stopa storage system is a major part of that, and RADAN software plays a vital role with quick, powerful automatic nesting and good sheet utilisation. We have a standard procedure that every sheet nested must be utilised to a minimum percentage. And we achieve that, using RADAN software.”

## Business development strategy

Back in 2015, Alpha Manufacturing outlined a robust business development strategy to elevate its overall service and grow the business. This strategy included an investment plan designed to bring Alpha’s manufacturing facility to the ‘cutting edge’ of sheet metal fabrication.

Over the past five years, over £4 million has been invested in new machinery, including the TRUMPF TruLaser 3030 fibre laser cutting system; TRUMPF TruPunch 5000; TRUMPF TruBend Cell 7000; Salvagnini P4 Lean Panel Bender and the STOPA Automated Storage System.

“The first installation of the STOPA was completed in 2017, allowing for early steps to be taken towards a vision of factory automation. The first few processing machines were integrated with the system, allowing raw material and part processed items to be loaded/ unloaded and stored automatically. It also dramatically reduced the amount of WIP (work in progress) stored around the shop floor on pallets, as well minimising manual movement of this WIP,” commented Alpha Manufacturing.

In 2020, a second installation marked the final stage of the 2015 investment plan and extended the STOPA system, further integrating it with the processing machines. The STOPA system now runs 62 metres down the centre of the Alpha factory and can store over 600 tonnes of material and parts in 207 pallet spaces.

“It has removed all WIP from the shop floor completely and now allows operators to access any part, or raw material, in storage with delivery to them at the touch of a button. Its integration into our ERP system means it is also capable of monitoring and managing stock levels automatically,” concluded Alpha Manufacturing.

A new five-year plan is currently being developed to grow the business still further.
