# FOCUSED DRIVE

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Innovation is at the heart of its company, demonstrated in both its control systems and machine tool production. More recently, this was highlighted in its laser cutting machines with linear drive technology, which were showcased at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover. With more than 3,000 machines installed globally and employing over 300 people, Kimla is committed to constantly developing new solutions to help increase productivity for customers worldwide.

ISMR caught up with its owner and chief engineer, Przemyslaw Kimla, to discover how the company is positioning for growth with the adoption of new technologies in sheet metal markets.

ISMR: You launched a new linear drive fibre laser cutting system recently – please tell us about it.

PK: Linear drives are a key focus for us as we believe they are the future for every CNC machine.

In an industry where accuracy is of the upmost importance, it surprises me to still see new machinery without linear drive technology.

It is no longer necessary to use servomotors to drive the machine axis. This generally needs gears, pinion racks or ball screws to change rotation from the servo motor to the linear axis in Cartesian coordinates CNC machines (XYZ movement). This is mechanically complicated and is a wearable process – new machines will start to lose their accuracy after two or three years and need extensive parts replacement.

Our company uses linear drive technology, where the force to drive the machine is directly generated by a magnetic field. This does not require rotary servo drives, ball screws, drives or pinion racks. Force is transferred without touching any mechanical parts so no surface is worn, ensuring a new machine retains its accuracy, even after 10 years – saving manufacturers valuable time and money.

ISMR SAYS:

“Linear drives can double the speed of laser cutting machines”

Speed is another important factor for manufacturers. Linear drives can double the speed of laser cutting machines; this is because mechanical drives have limitations due to the heat generated via friction and part wear. Linear drives, however, exhibit no friction and magnetic fields do not wear, resulting in a machine that can be two or even three times more efficient than standard drive systems.

We have placed linear drives in our large CNC machines for cutting aluminium sheets or other light alloys, to help increase efficiency, which have been particularly popular in the aerospace industry.

Linear drive technology has historically been limited by price, with only around one per cent of CNC machines in the world taking advantage of its benefits. We are one of the first in the world to develop linear drive production in our machines to optimise costs and make the machines more affordable for manufacturers of all sizes. We are working towards putting linear drives in all our machines and waterjet cutting systems.

To demonstrate the advantages of linear drive technology, we recently showcased two lasers at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover. We showcased our new Extremecut linear flatbed laser, enabling quick and efficient cutting of any shape out of sheet metal. In addition, we displayed our hybrid fibre laser system, which can cut sheet and tube (6m or 12m) and automatically unloads the tubes. We also presented a large area milling machine, based on linear drive technology, as well as a milling machine for small parts.

ISMR: Please give us a brief overview of Kimla and the control systems that it offers?

PK: Kimla is a private company, established more than 20 years ago. We started out by manufacturing electronic software and control systems for CNC machines. This control system is focused on accuracy and speed - perfect for machinery.

Our error-free control system separated the accuracy of the cutting edge from the speed of the machine. We quickly found that speed, position, acceleration and jerk limitation were the movement parameters for a winning formula.

Most laser manufacturers use control systems from companies that develop software designed for milling or drilling machines (slow movement and no feed, which is limiting). These are not really suitable for laser cutting systems. Our error-free control system can be used with all types of fibre laser cutting systems. We showed the laser for the first time at EuroBLECH four years ago.

I believe that we are the first company to design the control system around the machine. We put the CAD/CAM and nesting function into the CNC control of the machine. Therefore, no technician is required for the CAD/CAM software. If a correction is needed on the program, the operator can make the change on the machine directly, making it much faster and easier.

Our software system links directly to the CAM module, calculating the exact machining time before it starts. This means that we can automatically estimate/calculate the cost of cutting/machining, using the machine's capability.

This is not possible with other control systems that use external CAM software and time calculations can sometimes be incorrect. Thin sheet can sometimes be cut two or three times longer than the CAM software has calculated. Our control system can automatically load the DXF files, nest the parts on a sheet, generate the tool part and estimate the time of cutting. It then generates the report, including costs and price and, in minutes, we can make the offer to the customer, saving a great deal of time.

As well as our factory and R&D facility in Czestochowa, we have a programming facility in Wroslay (Poland). We are involved in different areas such as electronic design, software, manufacturing and mechanical manufacturing and mechatronics. We not only assemble machines, but also design control systems around fibre laser cutting and offer our own software for automation.

ISMR: How do you explain the increasing popularity of fibre lasers?

PK: The demand for fibre lasers has certainly driven the price down as more machines enter the market. In some cases, CO₂ laser owners are being forced to exchange their CO₂ laser cutting systems for fibre lasers to stay competitive. Fibre lasers are three to four times cheaper and three times more efficient (in terms of cutting cost) than CO₂ lasers – this is especially true for thin sheet. This means that the cutting cost per part can drop by an order of ten – sometimes, just changing the technology can lead to a massive cost saving.

On a fibre laser we can concentrate the beam onto a smaller spot, which means higher energy concentration that can be cut with a narrower kerf and faster cutting, using the same power. With one micrometre wavelength in a fibre laser, instead of 10 micrometres in CO₂ lasers, material absorbs the laser beam much quicker, thereby increasing cutting speed and enabling faster formation of complicated shapes and corners (ovals etc.), depending upon material type.

ISMR: Which success and achievements can you highlight this year?

PK: We have continued to grow at a rate of 25-30% year-on-year since we started more than 20 years ago, with no external financing. Now employing around 300 people, with our own R&D plant and factory in Czestochowa, we are currently constructing a new factory to keep up with demand and our sustained growth.

This year we have increased production to two lasers per week but are hoping to increase this further in our new 25,000 square metres facility to one laser per day.

ISMR: Where will you be directing your strategic efforts going forward?

PK: Our strategy has always been to sell affordable machines, which are highly efficient and very cost-effective - the three most important factors for customers. Our efforts going forward will be to increase our market share globally to ensure that more manufacturers can benefit from our technology.

Around 70% of our production is sold in Poland and we have established a strong reputation within this market. We have been increasing our distribution worldwide, including in the UK through our exclusive distributor, MBA Engineering.

We are now looking to extend our distribution channels and have already appointed new distributors around the world. We are very protective over what we have built so we only work with distributors that understand the market and can provide customers excellent technical service and support.
