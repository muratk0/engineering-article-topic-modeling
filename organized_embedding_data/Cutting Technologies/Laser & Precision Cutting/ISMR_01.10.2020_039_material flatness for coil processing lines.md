# Material flatness for coil processing lines

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

LASER CUTTING MACHINES are efficient and profitable but require extremely flat sheets that will remain flat during the whole cutting process, without residual internal stresses causing distortion in the cut parts.

Incorrectly levelled material affects the quality of the products processed in laser cutting machines, presses, robotic welders etc.

“Most visible flatness or shape defects occur because of length variations which develop during the rolling process. Edge waves occur when the strip is rolled with edges longer than the centre, for example, and centre buckle happens when a strip has a longer centre than the edges,” explained coil processing specialist, Athader.

Stretch levellers are designed to eliminate visible shape defects (such as edge waves, centre buckles, quarter buckles, camber and mandrel breaks). Athader’s Leveltek process is also designed to eliminate ‘memory’, the distortion that can occur in cut parts after laser or plasma cutting of visibly flat sheets by plastically elongating the strip past its yield point. The only measurable effect on the coil is a slight reduction in material width, and a slight increase in length.

The stretch levelling process is simple. The coil is decoiled, stretch-levelled and either recoiled or cut-to-length.

If significant width reduction has not occurred, as in the roller leveller example, permanent correction of length disparities will not take place.

Stretch levellers can stretch strip with the required force to exceed the yield strength and permanently elongate it. Even with materials that can be flattened using other methods, the process provides a higher standard of flatness for critical or special surfaces such as bright annealed, polished, painted and plated/coated.

Coils of these materials, as well as hotrolled carbon steel, can be processed in a wide range of thicknesses and weights.

## Stretch levelling in focus

Stretch levellers process many materials including: carbon steel; stainless steel; titanium; aluminium; brass; copper; hightemperature alloys; galvanized steel and other ferrous and non-ferrous materials, to make them commercially acceptable.

To reduce variability test results, coils were processed into 60” x 60” sheets, the same A36 ¼” x 60” Grade 50 black carbon Steel coil using the three technologies.

“The stretch levelling process far exceeds roller levelling. The results are very comparable to temper mill CTLs, which are far more expensive and difficult to operate. They also require a substantial roller leveller after the temper mill to achieve laser-quality flatness,” commented Athader.

Athader has supplied coil processing lines for over 28 years. It cooperates with partner, Leveltek International, on the stretch levelling process and has designed, manufactured and installed stretch levelling systems for retrofit and new light to heavy gauge cut-to-length and coil-to-coil lines for 27 years.

Athader – Leveltek supply cut-to-length (CTL) lines with stretch levelling and reduced installation costs. Civil works are kept to a minimum since the stretch leveller is built on a flat concrete floor (no foundation digging is needed). Furthermore, the use of a flying shear (instead of a start-stop shear) eliminates the need for a pit. The average speed of the line increases by nearly 50%, compared to a start-stop shear,” Athader told ISMR.

“Through a Leveltek proprietary nonmarking process that grips and stretches the uncoiled strip, our equipment eliminates shape defects, such as edge waves, centre buckle, quarter buckle, camber and mandrel breaks, restoring to perfection coils that would otherwise end up as scrap metal, and is guaranteed not to mark or scratch the surface being stretched,” it added.

Leveltek’s proprietary non-marking grippers conform to the sheet metal/plate profile to achieve 100% total contact, equally distributed across the entire width of the strip contact area. The entire cross section is uniformly stretched, it explained.

“Leveltek-processed metal achieves a ‘first place’ LAE score due to the uniformlyaligned grain structures (affecting state, heat conductivity and temperature) and the release/discarding of surface debris/ scale from the uniform stretching process. The Leveltek stretch levelling technique allows users to sell high-quality material that would otherwise end up as scrap and can accommodate a wide range of coil widths, gauges and weights. It produces no scrap material at all, due to threading, and can process the entire coil. It qualifies normal products for the most difficult applications,” concluded the manufacturer.

The Leveltek process is suitable for coils that require exceptional flatness in laser cutting, stamping, slit mults, cut-to-length, perforating and burning, for the manufacture of a wide range of products. These include cathode plates; pipes and tubes; architectural panels; coiled plates; tread plates; refrigeration panels; lithograph plates and food processing equipment.

Athader - Leveltek International’s stretch levelling equipment is routinely used by manufacturers utilising processes such as laser cutting; roll forming; drawing (single or multi-stage); cladding; polishing/buffing; waterjet cutting; plasma cutting; slitting; stamping; painting and re-rolling.

They can provide both new cut-to-length and coil-to-coil lines and retrofits of cut-tolength lines. The Leveltek stretch leveller also fits easily into new or existing cut-tolength lines.
