1. The laser has completed the cut. Why is that moment becoming so important for fabricators?

Because that is often where the real productivity test begins. A modern laser cutting machine can process material very quickly, but the plant only benefits fully from that speed if the parts move efficiently into the next stage of production.

After cutting, parts have to be removed from the table, identified, separated from the skeleton, sorted, stacked and directed to the correct downstream operation. In many factories, that work is still manual or semi-manual. It can be physically demanding, inconsistent and difficult to synchronize with bending, welding, assembly or shipping.

For fabricators, the objective is no longer simply to cut faster. The objective is to maintain flow. If the cut parts remain on the table waiting for operators, if they are mixed incorrectly or if the next department does not receive them in a controlled way, the overall productivity of the factory suffers. AI Smart Sorting

®

was developed to bring automation and intelligence to exactly that transition point.

2. What is AI Smart Sorting

®

designed to change in day-to-day production?

AI Smart Sorting

®

automates the unloading, identification, sorting and palletizing of cut parts. Instead of treating the end of the cutting cycle as a manual operation, the system turns it into a controlled, data-supported process.

The system uses production and nesting information to know which parts have been cut, where they are located and how they should be handled. From there, the robot can extract parts from the cutting table and direct them to the correct pallet, container or production route.

The practical result is that parts do not simply leave the laser cut. They leave the cell already organized and prepared for the next operation. That reduces friction between processes and helps manufacturers convert cutting performance into real factory throughput.

3. Why has sorting remained such a difficult area to automate?

Sheet metal sorting is complex because production is rarely static. A factory may process many different part geometries, materials, thicknesses, batch sizes and nesting layouts in the same shift. The sorting logic also depends on what happens next: bending, welding, assembly, painting, packing or delivery.

This means the challenge is not only to move a part from one place to another. The challenge is to understand the production context and organize the flow correctly. A wrong pallet, an unidentified part or an inefficient extraction sequence can create problems later in the process.

That is why post-cutting automation needs more than mechanical movement. It needs data, software and decision logic connected to the cutting process. AI Smart Sorting

®

was created to make that stage more predictable, repeatable and aligned with the rest of the factory.

4. Where does the AI element create value compared with conventional automation?

The value is in the decision-making layer. Traditional automation is often based on fixed movements or predefined rules. That can work well for highly repetitive operations, but sheet metal fabrication requires flexibility because every nest can be different.

AI Smart Sorting

®

works with production data and nesting information to support the most efficient extraction and sorting strategy. The system can calculate how parts should be removed from the cutting table and how they should be organized according to the production flow.

For TCI Cutting, AI is not an abstract concept or a marketing label. It is a way to solve a real industrial problem: how to reduce manual intervention, shorten downtime after cutting and ensure that parts move through the factory with greater consistency and traceability.

5. What information does the system use to know what to pick and where to place it?

The system works from the production data associated with the job and the nesting information generated before cutting. That gives it a clear view of the part geometry, position, order and destination within the production plan.

Based on that information, AI Smart Sorting

®

can define the extraction sequence and palletizing logic. The robot then executes the process so that each part is removed and directed to the correct location.

This is important because sorting is not only about physical handling. It is also about maintaining the relationship between the part, the order and the next production step. When that information remains connected, manufacturers gain better traceability and a more reliable flow between departments.

6. What measurable improvements can manufacturers expect from this type of solution?

The improvements usually come from a combination of factors. Manufacturers can reduce manual handling, improve operator safety, minimize sorting errors, shorten the time between cutting and downstream operations, and create a more stable production rhythm.

Traceability is also a major benefit. When parts are identified and organized correctly from the start, the next operations can work with more confidence. This reduces confusion, searching time and rework caused by parts being mixed or misplaced.

The biggest gain is often overall flow. A faster laser cutting machine is valuable, but the full benefit is only achieved when the rest of the production cell can keep pace. AI Smart Sorting

®

helps connect cutting performance with the next stages of manufacturing.

The starting point is to focus on a real manufacturing problem. In this case, the problem is clear: post-cutting work can be slow, labor-intensive and difficult to control. AI is valuable only if it helps solve that problem in a measurable way.

11. What does this say about the future of AI in sheet metal fabrication?

AI will become increasingly relevant when it is applied to specific production challenges. The next step is not simply more automation, but more adaptive automation: systems that understand context, use data and support better decisions throughout the factory.

In sheet metal fabrication, that can include sorting, planning, order prioritization, machine coordination, material movement, traceability and real-time production management. The factory of the future will not be defined only by individual machines, but by how well those machines communicate and work together.

For TCI Cutting, this is the direction of the industry. AI Smart Sorting
