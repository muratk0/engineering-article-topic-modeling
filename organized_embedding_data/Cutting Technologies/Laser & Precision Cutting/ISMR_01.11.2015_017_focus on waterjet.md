As a manufacturer of plasma and oxy-fuel cutting solutions, Kerf Developments has now extended its portfolio with the introduction of a new range of waterjet cutting machines. The Optima waterjet series now enables its customers to process hard materials such as mild, stainless and tool steel, aluminium, titanium, brass and copper whilst brittle materials such as carbon fibre, ceramic, granite, Kevlar and marble are also easy to process. This is enhanced by the option of a 3-, 4- or 5-axis cutting head. The Optima is offered with an X-axis of 2, 3 or 4m and a Y-axis span of 1, 2, 4 or 6m with bespoke dimensions available upon request. The Z-axis stroke enables cutting of material up to 180mm thick with precision levels of 0.1mm.

At EMO MILANO 2015 this October, OMAX® Corporation and distributor partner, ITEK, jointly showcased the OMAX 60120 and MAXIEM® 1515 JetMachining® Centers loaded with efficient direct drive pumps, advanced cutting heads and the latest Intelli-MAX® Software Suite. The bridge-style OMAX 60120 JetMachining Center has a high accuracy of motion and can cut complex part sizes of up to 1.52m by 3.05m from a variety of materials and thicknesses. The abrasive waterjet system’s performance is driven by its Intelli-MAX Software Suite and EnduroMAX® Pump technology.

“The user-friendly software automatically optimises the machine’s tool path for consistent quality and repeatable results, while the direct drive pump provides faster cutting speeds per unit of input than intensifier designs. Available in 22-, 30- and 37kW options, the EnduroMAX Pump runs continuously at 4,100 bar. At EMO 2015, a 37kW pump powered the 60120 to demonstrate exceptional part-processing speeds and low operating costs,” the company explained.

The MAXIEM 1515 JetMachining Center is a versatile, small-format machine that provides high-performance cutting for a wide range of material stock. It employs a 3,450 bar direct-drive pump that regulates the electricity input to the motor to reduce overall energy costs. MAXIEM direct drive pumps are available in 15, 22 and 30-kW options, with the 30kW version on display at EMO 2015.

The optional A-Jet® multi-axis cutting head adds two motion axes to the 1515 for complete 5-axis machining, which makes it easy to cut bevelled edges, angled sides, 3D shapes and countersinks while compensating for taper.

Bystronic is a Swiss supplier of solutions for the economical processing of sheet metal, other sheet materials and tubes.

Application-oriented systems and services for laser and waterjet cutting processes, as well as bending, were highlighted on its booth at this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart, Germany.

The company’s headquarters are in Niederönz, Switzerland. Two additional development and manufacturing locations are in Gotha (Germany) and Tianjin (China). Bystronic has its own sales and service companies in approximately 30 countries across three continents, as well as being represented by agents in other countries. Since 1994, Bystronic has been part of Conzzeta, a Swiss industrial holding company primarily active in machine construction.

Its focus, it told ISMR, is on the automation of the complete material and data flow in the cutting and bending process chain. We met Johan Elster, Bystronic’s head of markets, at this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

## New and upgraded technologies

“You can see our Xpert 40 press brake here on our booth at Blechexpo – it was launched at EuroBLECH last year. This small press brake has already won a German design award and aims for rapid, efficient parts production. When we developed Xpert 40, our customers took an active part in the development process. This was very helpful and useful – we will, no doubt, employ this principle again for future product development,” Johan Elster, Head of Markets, Bystronic told ISMR.

“We are also showing our fundamentally redesigned ByStar Fiber laser, the future platform for fast fibre laser cutting, here at Blechexpo. The old Bystar laser was the ‘pick of the bunch’ for many years. The new ByStar Fiber laser is a very flexible, dynamic machine, offering users easy access to the cutting area. It is also very intuitive and features a new ByMotion control system and new ByVision cutting interface. We will be launching new variants of this machine over the next few years.”

With a completely new machine concept, the ByStar Fiber laser cuts thin sheet metal at high speeds. However, Elster told ISMR, it also achieves outstanding cutting performance in the thick sheet metal range with low operating costs and low maintenance requirements. The ByStar Fiber is aimed at users who want to rely fully on fibre laser technology.

The new machine design, with a short cutting bridge, enables strong dynamics even when cutting complex contours. The ByStar Fiber’s newly developed control regulates laser power, speed and focus. Bystronic has further increased the fibre laser’s versatility by providing access to the cutting area over the long side of the machine. The large sliding door allows users to insert small express jobs and residual sheets at any time, even when largescale jobs are running.

“We have also equipped the ByStar Fiber with a newly developed user interface. ByVision Cutting unites all the important functions associated with laser cutting on a touch-screen. This allows the user to carry out all the cutting preparations with a few swipes of the finger i.e. sorting the job list, assigning cutting parameters and defining the automation mode,” added Johan Elster.

With Bystronic’s BySmart Fiber laser, users can launch directly into fibre laser technology without detours or lengthy preparations. In the thin range of sheet thicknesses, the BySmart Fiber significantly speeds up the cutting process compared to a CO laser.

“However, for higher sheet thicknesses,” Elster told ISMR, “Users can benefit from comparably low operating and maintenance costs. Hence, for users whose range of applications lies in the thin and medium material range, this means faster cutting processes, lower costs and higher profits with every cut part.”

At Blechexpo, Bystronic also showcased its new automatic tool changer for a standard Xpert press brake (18m of bottom tooling and 9m of upper tooling stored at the back of the press brake) to free press brake operators for other tasks and eliminate errors.

“We intend to launch a global performance line, which will be on display at FABTECH next week,” added Elster. “It features a mid-range fibre laser and press brake for mid-range performance and will be launched in North America and Asia. We intend to add more models to the Asian line. In general, we focus more on high-end technology for Europe.”

Bystronic also profiled its Xact Smart press brake at Blechexpo – it is available in several versions with press capacities of between 100 and 225 tons and with up to four axes on the back gauge. Depending upon the press capacity and the back gauge system, the Xact Smart enables a broad spectrum of simple angles through to complex bent parts to be processed. This creates a high level of flexibility for rapid job changes and use as a fixed bending station.

A wide selection of Bystronic’s own bending tools, which can be set up in a few steps using the optional RF-A Pro fast clamping system, offers more freedom for users. With the XPT Pro clamping system (also optional), tools from third-party manufacturers can also be used with the Xact Smart.

“The simple handling of bending technology is a key issue for many users. Load the bending plan, set up the machine and start bending. This is precisely what the Xact Smart with the ByVision Bending user interface offers. Operators control the entire bending process using a 22-inch touch screen with a few swipes of the finger,” explained Elster.

## Markets and trends

Elster outlined his views on the current business climate for sheet metal users to ISMR.

“Globally, there is slow growth. In some areas, there is also uncertainty e.g. in Russia, the Ukraine and Syria. Some customers are postponing projects. In Southern Europe, growth is much better. Northern Europe is still very strong i.e. UK and Scandinavia. China is not growing as rapidly and this has had an influence on other countries.

“The Brazilian, Russian and Ukrainian markets are not doing well. Otherwise, the outlook is fairly positive. The US market is still growing although it has slowed down a little. The majority of our customers are in Europe. The two growing regions right now are North America and Asia – they are both growing faster than Europe.”

He sees production shifting slowly towards the east which means faster growth prospects for the Asian region. Customers in ‘old’ Europe and the US will increasingly see smaller series production than before and so will need more flexible, automated machines for smaller series production, he told ISMR. This is one of the reasons for the development of Bystronic’s new tool changers – designed for rapid, efficient productivity.

“Industry 4.0 connectivity is another trend that we see,” added Elster. “Software is a key part of this. Our ByOptimizer service takes Industry 4.0 elements into account. The business model behind it is new for the industry – the client uploads files and gets back an optimised cutting plan.”

Compared to conventional cutting plans, ByOptimizer achieves material savings of, on average, up to 10 per cent on a standard-sized metal sheet of 3 x 1.5m, Elster explained. For example, for a cutting job with approximately 60 parts, for which the conventional programming method required two metal sheets, ByOptimizer manages to group them so that all the parts fit on one sheet. This not only saves material but also set-up time because, with this cutting job, there is no need to load a second sheet.

“We strive for a close and uncomplicated relationship with our customers and partners. Openness is one of our three company values, together with commitment and innovation. These three values apply not only internally, but also in relation to our partners,” the company concluded. ■
