# ASIA IN VIEW

[TARİH: 01.04.2013 ISMR]

Prima Power is focusing its attention on BRIC markets such as India

Prima Power, the Machinery Division of Prima Industrie Group, is a leading specialist in machines and systems for sheet metal working with a wide range of technologies for laser processing, punching, shearing, bending and automation. With manufacturing facilities in Italy, Finland, USA and China, it delivers machines and systems all over the world. Its sales and service network is active in over 70 countries and its installed base now numbers over 10,000 systems.

A well-known manufacturer of 3D laser machines, the company is also a key player in the 2D laser segment with a wide range of machines used around the world. It develops, manufactures and markets laser machines for cutting, welding and drilling of 2D and 3D components, as well as machines for sheet metal fabrication and forming that were formerly marketed under the brands Prima Industrie and Finn-Power. All its products are developed under its “Green Means” concept, combining sustainability and productivity.

The Group’s consolidated revenues at 31/12/2012 equated to an increase of 13 per cent compared with 2011. For the first time in the Group’s history, sales outside Europe (51 per cent) exceeded those in Europe (Italy included), confirming the shift in the centre of gravity of the global economy. Sales by geographic area showed an increasing turnover to the markets of Asia and the Rest of the World (in particular Russia, China, Korea, Brazil and Turkey), where turnover reached 28 per cent of consolidated revenues, and to North America (an increase from 20.4 per cent to 22.8 per cent of total sales).

## An eye on India

“Prima Power has enjoyed a long term business relationship with India dating back to the 1980s, when Prima Industrie SpA sold large 3D laser systems directly to well-known, high-end companies such as TATA Motors, ITL, JBM etc.,” commented Roberto Costantino, newly appointed general manager of Prima Machine Services India.

“Later in the 1990s, Finn-Power (part of the Prima Power Group) took the unconventional step of penetrating a developing market with high-end technology when many others were selling cheap, affordable technology. We believe that Prima Power made a winning choice, as today more than 100 Prima machines have been installed in India and Indian job shops, large and mid-sized manufacturers have used this technology for years.”

After years of brand building in India and growing machine tool sales in the country, Prima Power has now established a direct presence in the market with a subsidiary based in Pune Maharastra, a technology hub and home of the automotive industry in India.

“Prima Power India has a motivated team of 10 local Indian experts on lasers and sheet metal equipment, concentrating on sales and after-sales activities, who are always available to answer and solve problems of a different nature,” added Costantino. “Considering the size of the Indian sub-continent and the number of machines in the country that need support, we also have a number of local senior engineers in main cities like Delhi, Mumbai, Pune, Chennai etc. As long as the Indian market is galloping at a growth rate of two digits per year, the need for technology from the majority of Indian customers will also grow.”

Powerica, a well-known Indian manufacturer, has ordered a fully automated PSBB line (punching, shearing, buffering and bending system), which is a long system of multiple machines linked together which, completely unmanned, takes raw material at one end and delivers finished components at the other end. Powerica is increasing its market share by constantly investing in design and concentrating on the quality of products.

“The decision to have a direct presence in India responds to a clear trend we have witnessed in our industry. Our share of revenues in the BRIC countries outside Europe and North America has grown from 19 per cent to 26 per cent in the last four years. This shows a strong shift of machine tool consumption to these countries,” explained Jan Tapanainan, Prima Power Vice President, Sales Asia, Pacific, RoW & Systems.

"India is not as dependent upon the globalised manufacturing process as some other areas in Asia," added Costantino. "2013 is predicted to be a year of moderate growth for India, in spite of the stagnant political situation and the global downturn. The situation can only improve..."

The sectors served by Prima Power in India are varied. 3D laser cutting, welding and drilling machines are mainly used in the automotive and aerospace industry, whilst sheet metal processing machines are used by manufacturers of control cabinets, switch gear, generators, elevators, subcontracting and agricultural equipment industries. There is also a clear trend towards higher levels of automation, which require specially trained engineers.

## A new service concept

"Aesthetic design trends of recent years are understandably being reflected in component design and thus fabrication requirements. More curved and roundish shapes are in fashion, in contrast to the situation a few years ago. The need for roundish shapes calls for the use of lasers or punching/integrated lasers instead of technologies more suited to fabricating square and rectangular parts," explained Roberto Costantino.

The Operator is Prima Power’s new concept, launched at the EuroBLECH exhibition a few months ago, to offer rapid and analytical Just In Time Service for its customers around the world.
