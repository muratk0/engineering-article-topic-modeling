Punch/laser machine grows with user’s business

TRUMPF Inc. has introduced a compact and automation-friendly punch/laser machine that is designed to grow with a customer’s business. The TruMatic 1000 fiber is a combination punch/laser machine developed by adding a 3-kW TruDisk solid-state laser and retrofitting the TruPunch 1000 with a laser cutting system, a laser evacuation unit, and a beam guard system.

Customers with an existing TruDisk solid-state laser have the added flexibility of connecting to the fiber via the TRUMPF laser network. These fabricators can switch from strictly 2-D laser cutting to punch/laser processing. The machine is designed to sort finished parts up to 7 by 7 in. automatically and can be configured with the manufacturer’s SheetMaster Compact, an automation system that loads small to medium-sized sheets and blanks and unloads microjoint sheets and scrap skeletons.

Gripper designed for small components

Schunk has expanded its range of grippers for small components with a basic gripper for simple applications. The pneumatically controlled MPC is suitable for a variety of applications in assembly automation. It is available in six sizes from 16 to 270 N with maximum jaw strokes from 2.5 to 15 mm (0.09 to 0.59 in.). The smallest size, with a weight of 50 g (1.76 oz.), allows handling of components weighing up to 80 g (2.82 oz.). At the top end of the size scale, part weights of up to 1.3 kg (2.86 lbs.) are possible.

Aluminum electroplating process is phosphate-free, primer-free

LumiShield Technologies has introduced a new aluminum electroplating process called Lumidize™ as an alternative to common surface preparation technologies based on heavy metals and phosphates.

This proprietary aqueous process plates aluminum oxide onto any conductive substrate. The resultant electroplate (0.1 mil or less) forms a hard barrier layer that helps maximize the adhesion of organic materials and improve corrosion resistance.

The process binds to any conductive material, particularly carbon steels. Those surfaces then chemically bind to epoxy and urethane topcoats without the need for phosphate or primer. The process is compatible with pulse and DC systems and can be optimized with temperature, pH, current density, and additive packages for fine control of coating properties.

Digital force gauges can be hand-held, mounted

The L.S. Starrett Co. has introduced two digital hand-held force gauges. Depending on the series, advanced automatic testing can be attained with the DFC force gauge controlling a Starrett FMM digital force tester, or basic testing can be performed using the DFG gauge.

The gauges are part of the company’s new L1 line of entry-level digital force measurement systems. They feature a high-resolution OLED color backlit display and auto-off function. A primary and secondary window shows test results, and out-of-tolerance results display in red. A multifunction keypad has soft keys that are programmable to the user’s most repetitive functions.

Adjustable sampling rates help capture peak loads, and filters can be applied to peak and display values. The battery provides more than 30 hours of continuous operation, and the USB port allows data transmission to a computer.

App conveys welding information through play

Fronius has launched the new Welducation Basic App to help welders build up their knowledge while having fun. It has a game function that enables users to experience welding interactively and features a quiz that conveys useful information on the joining process.

The app conveys information on welding through play. In the quiz, users choose their answer from four options and are told immediately whether they were correct. To complete a round of the game, users must answer 10 questions. The scores then are shown in order of ranking. An international high score ranking allows users to compare themselves with all app users worldwide.

Those who prefer to learn through practice rather than theory can gain experience in the game app with virtual welding. The touchscreen becomes a workpiece and the user’s finger becomes a welding torch. The virtual trainer supports the player by showing the ideal welding speed and position using color signals. The aim is to score as many points as possible through consistent and accurately welded seams. Users can also save their points from the game in the online rankings.

Large grinders feature safety brakes

Metabo Corp. has introduced large angle grinders with safety brakes, available in 7-in. (WEPB 24-180 MVT) and 9-in. (WEPB 24-230 MVT) models.

The wheel stops within 2.5 seconds of releasing the paddle switch. The nonlocking paddle switch and an electronic clutch help protect the user against kickback if the wheel binds. Both grinders also include electronic overload protection to keep the motor from overheating, as well as restart protection, which prevents the tool from restarting on its own in the event of a power interruption.

The 7-in. model has a no-load speed of 8,500, suitable for weld seam prep and beveling work. The 9-in. model has a no-load speed of 6,600 to cover a larger surface area when grinding and add extra depth capacity for cutting. Additional features of the tools include a three-position antivibration side handle and a three-position vibration-dampened back handle.
