## Scope of Laser Safeguarding

A tool-mounted laser safety device can monitor a defined area near the punch and die during selected portions of a press-brake closing stroke. When an obstruction enters the active field, the device changes its safety outputs. However, detection is only the first part of the safety function. Protection depends on the complete chain from detection through safety logic and final control elements to the actual stopping of hazardous ram motion.

A device certificate does not by itself demonstrate that an installed machine is safe for a particular task. The installed arrangement must account for field position, tooling, operating mode, stopping performance, reset behavior, muting or blanking functions, and all foreseeable routes by which a person may reach the hazard.

## Complete Stopping Performance

Total stopping time begins when the protective field is interrupted and ends when hazardous motion has stopped. It includes:

- response of the sensing device;
- processing by the safety-related control system and transmission of the stop command; and
- response of valves, drives, braking elements, ram, and tooling.

Component response data may be useful for diagnosis, but it does not establish the performance of the complete installed machine. Machine-level validation requires measurement of total stopping performance and ram overrun under relevant conditions.

Protective distance is conceptually based on approach speed, total stopping time, and applicable allowances for factors such as detection-field geometry and intrusion before detection. The applicable calculation method, machine instructions, and relevant requirements must be used. Nominal ram speed multiplied by a stated response time is not a substitute for direct measurement of actual overrun, because deceleration may not be constant.

Stopping performance can change with wear, hydraulic behavior, drive condition, load, temperature, tooling, speed, stroke, and operating mode. A baseline record and later comparison of worst-case results can help identify deterioration. Following repairs, retrofits, control changes, or changes affecting speed or deceleration, qualified personnel should determine whether revalidation is required under the machine manual and applicable requirements.

## Coverage Beyond the Front Detection Field

Laser sensing covers only the area within its configured field. It may not protect side, rear, below-support, frame-opening, or other access routes. Large workpieces may also create trapping points with supports, fixed structures, or nearby equipment. Backgauges and rear mechanisms can present separate crushing hazards.

The risk assessment should identify access during loading, alignment, bending, support of flanges, part rotation, unloading, clearing, setup, fault recovery, and maintenance. It should include helpers, setters, and other persons who may approach the machine.

Depending on the identified risks, complementary measures may be needed, such as fixed guards, fencing, interlocked access, safe work methods, controlled setup procedures, or energy isolation. Production safeguarding is not a substitute for safe maintenance, fault-finding, tool changes, or other work within a danger area.

## Muting, Blanking, and Operating Modes

Muting temporarily suspends detection, blanking ignores part of a detection field, and mode changes can select different safety logic. These functions may be needed where a workpiece, flange, or tool enters the sensing area, but they can also create a path for hands to enter the hazard.

Each such exception should be treated as a distinct safety configuration. The risk assessment and validation should address what enters the field, when protection changes, how long the changed condition remains active, which motion is available, and whether a foreseeable hand path can be distinguished from the material path. If that separation cannot be reliably achieved, another handling method, fixture, tooling arrangement, physical guard, or process may be necessary.

Changes to tooling, adapters, workpieces, programs, speed, stroke, software, blanked areas, or muting conditions can invalidate prior evidence. These changes should be reviewed by qualified personnel using the machine-specific manual, risk assessment, and applicable requirements.

## Functional Checks and Validation Records

Routine functional checks confirm that the machine remains in a previously validated condition; they do not prove that stopping performance remains within an approved limit. Use only the test object and method specified for the installed device and validated machine configuration. Never use a hand or an improvised object to challenge a protective field.

A prescribed check may include confirmation of detection, alignment, expected stop response, interlocks, indicators, fault status, muting or blanking status, reset behavior, and prevention of unexpected restart. Clearing an interrupted field should not itself initiate hazardous motion.

Instrumented testing of stopping performance should be scheduled according to the risk assessment, machine instructions, usage, operating conditions, maintenance history, and applicable requirements. If a functional or stopping-performance test fails, the affected machine or configuration should be removed from use or otherwise secured against unintended operation until qualified personnel restore the safety function, complete required verification, and document release.

Useful validation records identify the machine and relevant software version, approved tooling and workpiece envelope, operating modes, field geometry, stopping and overrun results, calculation method, test equipment, raw results, fault-response checks, and responsible approval personnel.

## Acceptance Principle

Laser safeguarding is appropriate only for defined configurations where measured stopping performance supports the required protective distance, all foreseeable access routes are controlled, and operating exceptions have been assessed. A passing sensor test or component certificate cannot compensate for an unguarded approach route, unsuitable machine stopping performance, or an operating mode that permits hand access during hazardous motion. Machine-specific validation and ongoing control of changes are essential.
