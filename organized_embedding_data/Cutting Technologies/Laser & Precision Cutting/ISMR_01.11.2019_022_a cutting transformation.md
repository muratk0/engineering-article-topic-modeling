# A cutting transformation

[TARİH: 01.11.2019 ISMR]

Watson Gym Equipment managing director, Simon Watson, confirms that a partnership between the company's CNC machine tool supplier Mazak, and CAD/CAM specialist RADAN, has completely transformed the way it now cuts box section steel.

It is all carried out on a Mazak Fabri Gear 220 II laser tube cutter, preinstalled with Mazak's FG-CAD/CAM software powered by RADAN's laser tube system, Radtube. The Radtube software processes complex pipe and box section shapes in 'a fraction of the time taken by traditional methods', according to RADAN.

"It replaces what we used to do manually, which was to take an eight-metre length of steel, cut it to the length we needed for the component on our piece of apparatus, mitre it and put the holes in," confirmed Simon Watson.

It also enables tabs to be created in a style that the apparatus can only be put together one way.

"We use different sized tabs and make them non-symmetrical, so that when the welder comes to fit the pieces together, it's like a jigsaw puzzle, completely eliminating human error at the assembly stage," explained Simon Watson.

The software can also readily program a scalloping-out procedure, giving added strength to the joints.

"It does everything we were doing by hand and a lot more besides, such as the tabbing. Previously, we had to draw both the tabs and slots. But now I just click where I want the tab, input the gap around it and it automatically inserts the slot immediately. The software really is phenomenal. It's definitely transformed the way we manufacture, by automating around 80 per cent of the manual work that the welders were doing on cutting and preparation," added Watson.

### The key to productivity

Another part of the process that the Radtube-powered FG-CAD/CAM quickens is when Simon Watson is designing box section components which are identical, except for the addition of holes...mainly for the frames of the company's weight machines.

"One side may have numerous holes in it, but none in the other. Instead of having to draw it twice, I draw it once with the holes, create a mirror image, put everything into FG-CAD/CAM and simply take out the holes," Watson told ISMR.

The company has used a Mazak mill, flatbed laser and two lathes to cut other components for several years. But it was seeing how much time the welders spent manually cutting and processing the box sections that prompted Watson to take the next step and invest in a laser tube cutting system.

"As well as the actual cutting being so time-consuming, there was a lot of downtime for changing blades and maintaining saws. Also, when someone is cutting lengths of steel twenty times, they're not going to get all twenty to the exact same length. But, with the laser tube cutter CNC machine and specialist software, we noticed massive improvements in time and accuracy right from the start. And it's so easy to use. I felt quite comfortable doing basic tasks after just one day's training. Within a couple of weeks, I was completely comfortable doing anything," explained Watson.

Concluding, he added: "The Fabri Gear and FG-CAD/CAM make everything consistent and uniform, and also means that if we need to replace any parts on an existing piece of apparatus, we know that the new part will be accurate."

ISMR caught up with Thorsten Frauenpreiss, MD, XLase GmbH, at Blechexpo in Stuttgart to discuss his view of the markets and plans for the company.

XLase GmbH is a manufacturer of medium- and high-power fibre laser cutting systems and has more than ten years of experience in the development and manufacture of intelligent laser equipment. The product spectrum ranges from flat sheet cutting machines to highly automated solutions, such as sheet/tube hybrid machines and pipe cutting systems, as well as robotic and portal welding concepts.

XLase GmbH is a wholly owned subsidiary of Foshan Beyond Laser Technology Co. Ltd., known as HSG Laser. The company was founded in 2018 in Hamburg, Germany, and acts as the headquarters for all HSG activities in Europe. It aims to 'further improve existing sales and after-sales infrastructure and to accelerate the interaction between customers, partners and manufacturing companies'. It is also looking for distributors in different regions in Europe.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements?

TF: HSG Group was founded thirteen years ago in Foshan, China. The founder is the CEO and major shareholder and he is young and dynamic, like the company, with plenty of energy. The company has three factories in Foshan and a new factory in Suzhou. We just conducted a ground-breaking ceremony in Foshan for our new 40,000 square metre factory which will open in Autumn next year. We will keep one of the three factories in Foshan but the other two will move into the new factory which aims to produce around 3000 fibre laser cutting sets per year.

For the HSG Group, the greatest achievement over the last 12 months is that the group is still growing, in numbers and volume, despite weak economies in certain markets. By introducing a lot of different new products, by quickly adapting to market requirements and special customer needs as well as really listening to the requests/ wishes of customers, it has outperformed most of its competitors quite significantly.

The company has been looking, for some time now, at the 'internationalisation' of the business. You can see this reflected in the formation of the new company, XLase GmbH, which was founded last year in Europe – this is a major milestone for us. The company is a 100% subsidiary of HSG Laser. We are also working on forming subsidiaries in Japan and in Vietnam. HSG had, up to then, mostly focused on the Chinese market and these new subsidiaries reflect its growing international focus.

Another milestone is the variety of new products that we introduced across all areas e.g. our flat sheeting cutting laser, the GX, is specifically designed for the European market as a cost-effective, entry-level machine. In the very high-power range, we also produce the GV series for powers exceeding 10kW - it is designed to cut thick plate material, with higher acceleration rates and speed. Our new G3015X 4kW laser cutting machine model boasts acceleration rates of 1.4 and combined axis speed of 140. So, we basically added greater capability to both ends of the sheet laser cutting spectrum.

We followed the same pattern with the combined GE series machines. These are flat sheet cutting machines with extra capability to process tube. It is a machine that has been designed for special markets in Europe. The tubes are manually loaded and unloaded. Tube is an interesting and complementary activity for sheet metal cutting. The machines are bestsellers in China and in Europe. We produce entry-level machines (GA, GX) and higher power, higher acceleration mid-level machines (GH). We also manufacture tube processing systems, such as the TM, for small diameter tube. We recently introduced, in China, a new 5X 3D tube processing machine with a fully automated loading and unloading and storage facility. This will be introduced in Europe in 2020.

We also recently introduced a new press brake offering to China in September and we intend to bring this to Europe in the future.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals?

TF: We consider the global business climate to be weak, at the moment. In most of the

markets, some of the major players expect a decrease of 10-15% and we agree with this assessment. Fortunately, we have not yet seen this weakness reflected in our numbers yet. One of HSG's strengths is not only its range of products but also its versatility and readiness to adapt to changing market conditions and needs. So far, the HSG Group has been quite successful at that and we believe that our turnover will be higher this year than last year.

We are pleased with the progress of our new start-up in Europe this year. However, it is tough to get into the European market because a lot of Chinese companies are pushing into this space and competition is strong. The German market is currently suffering as investment in the automotive sector has decreased. GDP has been negative for the last two quarters in Germany, so the market has slowed down. However, the European market still looks strong, despite the risk factors, and 2008 set valuable lessons for companies to learn how to deal with and prepare for market tightening.

Of course, we also see geopolitical tensions in markets. The tariff situation between the U.S. and China is influencing markets in the US and China. However, this does not appear to be affecting our markets in Europe at the moment.

ISMR: Please explain your strategic and technical vision/objectives?

TF: Our technical strategy is to complement laser cutting with steps before and after i.e. press brakes etc. We are not currently looking at highly sophisticated, AI, Industry 4.0 systems as these are not really our market. We really focus on cost-effective systems and will customise our machines to customer requirements and, if the requests are there, we will expand these. However, we consider our speciality, and the majority of our business, as being around fibre laser cutting and the steps that precede and follow this.

We are complementing our product offering with higher powers, although smart cutting techniques do not always call for higher power. Customers focus on what they actually need and what this means for their margins. We do not want to produce over-engineered machines but offer cost-effective solutions for customers so that they can save money – this is the driving force behind our R&D efforts. These machines must make money, and add value, for customers.

Our strategy for the new start-up company in Europe is to initially use distributors in European markets, in the medium term. We have appointed a couple of distributors to handle sales and service of our systems in Europe. If there is a region where we get a lead, and we have no distributor, the customer can decide whether he buys his machine directly from China and gets sales/ support/training from there or whether he uses one of the distributors we have. Our main target for Europe is to find more partners to cover the region. Our second target, for the sales strategy, is to use an partner who sells our systems in a country without HSG branding but as a tailored solution for specific customers.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

TF: Customers always ask for services/spares and machine reliability. We generally invite them to our factory in China, which is very high-tech, so that they can see the standard of our manufacturing processes and product testing etc.

Of course, they also ask about price. Customers look carefully at investment to ensure that they are making the investment at the right time. Our machines are very robust – many of them run 'full pelt' in factories all over China, in unheated conditions. This makes them eminently suitable for markets in Europe.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal markets?

TF: I see definite trends towards higher automation, smarter solutions and smart factory solutions. We concluded an agreement with Lantek, who has a strong Industry 4.0 focus, to handle the adaptions and interfaces for our machines so that they can be more easily integrated into European ERP systems. It is important to offer this connectivity to our customers as control systems are different. Lantek nesting software will be used on our sheet cutting systems. We prefer to work with software experts on this issue.

I also see a trend towards the replacement of traditional methods and a move towards higher power in laser cutting systems. Although laser does offer the opportunity to open new markets for cutting thicker materials, for example, I am not personally convinced that the cost-benefit analysis is always strong enough when comparing, for example, the cost of doing this on a 20kW laser to the cost of doing this on a plasma cutter. If at all, these markets will be smaller for laser cutting systems. The technology must always suit the application.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

TF: Current market conditions are definitely a challenge. From a Chinese perspective, there are simply too many Chinese laser cutting manufacturer players/competitors in the market. There will be a consolidation in the market, at some point in the future, in China and worldwide. Survivors of this consolidation will not be the smaller players.... The large players will get even larger and may be willing to sacrifice margin to ensure that they remain in the market. So, this is a major challenge.

I changed from a German/American to a Chinese company because I firmly believe that machine quality, cost-effectiveness and the Chinese ability to adapt and move quickly injects real strength into the entry-level, medium machine segment.

ISMR: Which exhibitions will you be attending over the next few months?

TF: We will be at FABTECH in the U.S. with our distributor. We are currently looking for a distributor in Germany. We will also be at exhibitions in France, Holland, Poland, Spain and Germany next year.
