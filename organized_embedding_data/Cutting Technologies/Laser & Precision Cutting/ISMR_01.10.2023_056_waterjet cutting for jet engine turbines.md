# Waterjet cutting for jet engine turbines

[TARİH: 01.10.2023 ISMR]

When fabricating jet engine turbines, wire EDM is often used since the technology provides narrow cuts for precise and complex shapes. However, wire EDM is also a timeconsuming manufacturing process (set-up time and processing). It is also a thermal manufacturing process which influences the properties of the material. However, new and innovative waterjet cutting technology is now being proposed for this aerospace application.

“Fabricating turbines for jet engines is a time-consuming process that requires precision. Since wire EDM is recognized as a quite slow machining technology, an international jet-engine supplier explored the possibility of reducing the manufacturing time of fixed turbine parts by replacing wire EDM with other processing technologies. The challenge was to find a technology that could meet the tough requirements," explained Water Jet Sweden (WJS).

"Thermal cutting technologes, such as laser and fibre laser, were tested but there was too much heat impact on the material, so laser cutting had to be rejected. The next step was to investigate waterjet technology as an option. The drop-shaped contours of the jet turbine required five-axis machining and, at the same time, a very narrow cut to handle small contours, sharp corners and small tolerances, with a fine cutting surface," it added.

Traditional waterjet cutting was difficult to apply, but the challenge was met by the new FiveX Ultra waterjet cutting system from Water Jet Sweden. FiveX Ultra is a full 3D cutting machine (0-120° cutting) designed to manage precision cutting assignments. As the FiveX Ultra can be equipped with a FAWJ Micro Cutting Tool, it also provides extremely fine cuts for precise and complex shapes.

Following a customer visit to the Water Jet Sweden Demo Centre in Ronneby (Sweden), the development of a new manufacturing routine for jet engine turbines started.

Waterjet cutting is a cold cutting process, with no heat-affected zones, and the cutting surface is "satin smooth" according to WJS. With the narrow cutting beam of the micro cutting tool, it cuts sufficiently sharp corners but adjustments needed to be made to the slightly cone-shaped cutting beam (a characteristic of waterjet cutting). The TVL (tapered angle control, variable speed and delay compensation) function was fine-tuned to eventually provide an approved cutting profile.

"The fact that it was not possible to make direct measurements of the parts was a challenge in the work process. Instead, interpreters are used to check the contour accuracy. In small steps, the process was gradually optimised to finally obtain the correct and precise profile. We have now developed a process to optimise cutting parameters for turbine production, using a unique combination of our new 3D waterjet cutting machine and our FAWJ micro cutting tool," explained Tony Ryd, Co-Founder and CTO, Water Jet Sweden.

Time savings were made over several stages of the manufacturing process. Cutting times were improved, set-up time could be reduced to a minimum (with fixtures instead of time-consuming wiring) and a new auto calibration tool significantly reduced the time from loading to production.
