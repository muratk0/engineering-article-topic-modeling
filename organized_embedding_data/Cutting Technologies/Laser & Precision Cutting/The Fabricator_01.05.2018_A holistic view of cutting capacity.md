# A holistic view of cutting capacity

[TARİH: 01.05.2018 The Fabricator]

Andy Tucker President and CEO Metalcrafters Inc.

Methuen, Mass.

A

ndy Tucker remembers when he saw his first tower and automatic load and unload system for a turret punch press. It was the early 1980s.

"I was in high school at the time. My father was one of the first [custom fabricators] on the East Coast to embrace automatic loading and unloading." He added that his father had invested in automation so that the punches could run overnight unattended.

When Metalcrafters brought in its first CO

2

laser in 2001, the company used it for prototyping. As volumes grew for a job, it would be transferred to the automated turret punch presses, making the punch tooling investment if necessary.

Today, of course, the shop uses all machines for all levels of production. This includes the company’s two fiber lasers, which it purchased within the past three years.

What drew Tucker and his team to fiber lasers was, of course, their speed. Still, they looked not just at the cutting inches per minute but also the total cycle time, from sheet loading until a cut blank arrives in the press brake department. This included scrutinizing secondary operations like deburring.

The lasers produce a clean, burr-free edge. Programmers place microjoints only where needed, to keep small or oddly shaped parts from tipping or falling through the slats. If possible, they place those needed mircotabs where they’ll be hidden by a weld bead. But for the most part, laser nests have no microtabs. No microtabs means that operators need not shake parts out of nests. They also don’t need to send those cut blanks through a deburring machine.

The company has two CO

2

lasers, two fiber lasers, and three turret punch presses. They’re feeding 16 press brakes, including a new automatic tool change press brake that, according to Tucker, has the capability of more than tripling the throughput of conventional brakes. The fabricator also has significant welding capacity, including three robotic welding cells plus more than a dozen manual welding stations.

Here’s the kicker: The company has one fewer cutting machine than it did two years ago, and yet it has more blanking capacity than ever. "Our second fiber laser actually replaced two other cutting machines, a CO

2

laser and a turret punch press," Tucker said. "Still, even after getting rid of two machines, our cutting department’s output increased by about 35 percent."

You could look at Metalcrafters’ blanking machines as the engines that feed the rest of the plant. Over the years the fabricator has fewer engines (blanking machines), but each is more powerful, productive, and reliable, able to feed more downstream stations (bending machine, welding cell, etc.) than ever.

Lights-out production, unattended operation, and significant cross training are key elements to Metalcrafters’ blanking success. "We have two operators running four lasers and one operator running three turrets," Tucker said, adding that most of them can run press brakes and other machines as well. This allows them to move to the bottleneck.

Say the blanking systems cut all night unattended. Operators arrive in the morning, download the cut lists, and set up the machines to run. After this, some may help move parts to the bending area and, while there, set up a press brake to boost forming capacity and avoid a bottleneck. The result: Machines keep cutting and bending, and jobs keep flowing. And flow, of course, is what it’s all about.

The company has one fewer cutting machine than it did two years ago, and yet it has more blanking capacity than ever.
