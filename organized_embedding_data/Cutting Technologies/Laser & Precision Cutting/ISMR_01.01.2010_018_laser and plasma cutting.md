## RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

## Separable Thickness

What is readily obvious from Figure 2 is that, with increasing laser power, separable material thickness increases too. Increasing the laser power from 1000 W to 3000 W enlarges the separable thickness of stainless steel from 0.3mm up to 0.8mm. A power enhancement to 5000 W delivers a separable thickness of approximately 1.3mm.

Please note that these thicknesses are definitely separable. Cutting thicker materials at the same power level results in incomplete cuts. The remaining molten material prevents self-separation of the cut parts. Furthermore, vef diminishes rapidly at increased sheet thicknesses. For example, only 15 m/min. were achieved for the 2mm thick material at a laser power of 5000 W.

However, cutting speeds for lower material thicknesses are attainable. They cover 44 m/min. for 1mm thick material as well as 270 m/min. for 0.2mm at a laser power level of 5000 W. On closer inspection of the graph in Figure 2, some values of vef seem to be irregular. The speeds for 0.1mm as well as for 0.05mm look as if they are independent of the applied laser power. The values of vef are equal for 3000 W and 5000 W respectively. The cause of the equal values at 0.05mm is the limit given by the scanning system used. Due to the inertia of the system processing, speeds above 800 m/min. are not feasible. Hence, the limit is determined by the scanning system and not by the process itself.

Cutting the whole thickness by two instead of one scan repetition reduces vef from 800 m/min. to 400 m/min. Cutting the metal sheet by n = 1.5 is not possible. It is obvious that the increment is very rough at low numbers of scan repetition. For higher numbers of n, the increment is fine. Cutting a sheet with n = 20 or n = 21 delivers “only” a difference in vef of 2 m/min.

Thus, especially for very thin sheets, vp should be adapted. Significantly higher vef can be achieved. What is readily obvious is a significant gain in vef at 0.1mm. Because of this modification, cutting speeds of up to 700 m/min. are feasible. The cut can be done with one scan repetition. A further increase at 0.05mm is not possible because of the afore-mentioned system characteristics.

## Cutting Rectangular Corners

Whether working with processing speeds of 700 m/min. or 800 m/min., cutting rectangular corners requires extremely high dynamic behaviour of the scanning system. The processing speed should be higher than the critical one at any time. That means, at a corner, that the processing speed generally has to be, for example, 800 m/min. These dynamic requirements lead to insufficient cut results. Rectangular corners will be rounded off, see Figure 3.

To overcome this limitation, one solution is to add running-in and running-out phases at the corners. These phases give the scanning system added flexibility. During the running-in and the running-out, the laser can either be kept switched on or off. Based on the geometry, inner and outer corners must be defined. The laser must be switched off during the running-in and running-out at inner corners and can be activated at outer corners. Two cut examples for this method are given in Figure 4.

## CONCLUSION

Investigations by Fraunhofer IWS Dresden in Germany have shown further application areas for remote cutting. The fundamental mode fiber laser used with an output power of up to 5000 W demonstrates the possibilities generated by enhanced laser power. Thicknesses of stainless steel are now certainly separable up to 1.3mm. Nevertheless, outstanding performance can be seen at sheet thicknesses of up to 0.5mm. Cutting speeds of up to 700m/min. for 0.1mm thick sheets are feasible. The achieved cutting speeds already hit the limits of the scanning systems used.
