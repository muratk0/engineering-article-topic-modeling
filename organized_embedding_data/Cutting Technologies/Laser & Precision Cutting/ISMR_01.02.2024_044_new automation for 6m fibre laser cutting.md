# New automation for 6m fibre laser cutting

[TARİH: 01.02.2024 ISMR]

“In the world of laser systems, automation plays an increasingly important role in improving the effectiveness of loading and unloading operations. Larger lasers face even greater challenges than three- and fourmetre lasers when it comes to these tasks. Whether it's because of the delicacy and size of the sheet or the weight of the material to be handled, it's important to note that as the size of the sheet metal increases, so does the time required for handling (before and after the cutting process)," Pierandrea Bello, Laser Technology Product Manager, Salvagnini, told ISMR.

The long handling times convinced Salvagnini to develop its ADLU loading/ unloading device and MCU automatic sorting device (in 6020 size) for processing sheet metal formats of up to six metres in length for its L3 fibre laser cutter.

"In just 60 seconds, the ADLU device automates the process of loading sheetmetal from packs and unloading the cut sheets, eliminating all the intermediate handling steps usually done by the operator. The ADLU is also a flexible solution in terms of layout as its modular, floor-based structure can be adapted to available spaces in the workshop," explained Bello.

The L3-6020 model also features an 8kW high-power density source (50μm fibre). It is available in two different versions with the same cutting speed performance but differing energy consumption levels (the standard U version and a high-efficiency E version).

"The 8kW high-power density source enables a cutting speed that is higher than sources with the same or higher power. It also delivers affordable energy and gas consumption," Pierandrea Bello outlined in detail to ISMR.

## Automatic sorting

Investing in an automatic sorting device, added Salvagnini, offers several benefits such as the elimination of manual operations leading to reduced operating costs; decreased reliance on labour; lower risk of accidents and reduced material damage during handling.

"In addition, sorting times can be precisely planned to ensure consistency and repeatability. Automatic sorting systems can also separate the cut material in any unmanned shifts. They improve production flow by swiftly providing material to downstream workstations and simplifying part tracking by grouping them by job, kit or the next workstation. This benefit is particularly attractive for six-metre configurations where more parts can be mixed, increasing tracking complexity in proportion to the sheet metal size," continued Bello.

The MCU automatic sorting device handles parts of various shapes, sizes and weights. It can work in full multi-gripping mode, picking up several parts in sequence with the same gripping device. It activates the necessary suction cups or magnets individually. It can handle thicknesses ranging from 0.5 to 12mm. Each manipulator can handle weights of up to 65kg, or 130kg when both gripping devices work together. The manipulators are flexible and mobile, with a full 360° rotation capability, allowing them to pick up parts of any size or shape.

Cutting samples: 3-, 4-, 6-, 8- and 10mm.

## Sheet Vision System (SVC)

The L3-6020 also incorporates a new Sheet Vision System (SVS) which aligns the sheet (as an alternative to AVS or a capacitive sensor). It can recover any portion of leftover sheets to quickly and automatically nest new parts. Its 'Multisheet' function is designed to simplify multi-sheet management.

## A sustainable focus

Both ADLU and MCU prioritise sustainability by using AVC (adaptive vacuum control) technology. This technology monitors the vacuum level in the suction cups during gripping and activates vacuum generation only when necessary.

"This results in a remarkable reduction in compressed air consumption, of over 90%," confirmed Bello.

While the minimum sizes are 100 x 200mm, Salvagnini now offers the smart cluster function to overcome the dimensional limitation when unloading parts below the official threshold, and to prevent automatic sorting from reducing the sheet metal yield. This feature quickly joins small parts together without the need for a cutting frame or complicating office programming, effectively reducing overall unloading times.

MCU uses NEXUS software to automatically handle sorting programs. If needed, operators can edit these programs interactively. NEXUS provides full control over the entire production process, including individual sheets at precise points in the cycle. It also offers checking through an integrated 3D simulator.

"NEXUS eliminates the need to select a specific production strategy during the design phase. It adapts to various requirements whether it's for large batches, small batches, kits or one-off batches," outlined Pierandrea Bello.
