# Strong momentum for German laser industry

[TARİH: 01.05.2015 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO DATA compiled by the German Engineering Federation VDMA, Laser and Laser Systems division, companies represented at German locations crossed a magic boundary for the first time by producing laser-based manufacturing systems which exceed the billion-euro mark. Development of sales in high quality laser beam sources also proved to be very strong.

In 2014, association members produced about EUR 852 million of laser-based machines, corresponding to an 11 per cent growth compared with 2013 (then +8 per cent). Global consolidation i. e. the output following allowances for the added value created by internationally acting groups, records EUR 1.01 billion, a 10 per cent growth over the previous year’s figures (+9 per cent in 2013). This underscores a dynamic trend that is considerably greater for systems equipped with solid-state lasers.

“The global market in 2014 for laser material processing systems, according to research by Optech Consulting in Switzerland, has grown by 8 per cent, achieving EUR 8.7 billion. The 2013 increase of 4 per cent in incoming order volume, decidedly more moderate than in production, soared in 2014 by 20 per cent to EUR 1.04 billion, mainly driven by foreign demand,” said the VDMA.

Laser system exports also proved strong in 2014, growing by a healthy 16 per cent to a value of EUR 664 million. The share, compared to production value, rose to a present 78 per cent. Renewed growth was recorded by Western European destinations, with particularly powerful contributions from the USA, China and Japan, plus a slight growth for “Other Asian countries” like India, Korea, Taiwan or Southeast Asia.

EUR 502 million of production volume generated in Germany (+9 per cent) compare well with orders amounting to EUR 519 million (+17 per cent). The global consolidation of production volume yields EUR 668 million (about a third greater than the result in Germany itself), as well as a 5 per cent rate of increase. These records include CO lasers, solid-state lasers and complete diode lasers for direct applications.

2014 exports of laser beam sources recorded a value of EUR 302 million, or a 15 per cent growth. The share compared to production value rose to its present 61 per cent.

THE TUBE BENDING MACHINE tool manufacturer, Schwarze-Robitec, has opened its own subsidiary in North America. Since 1 April 2015, Schwarze-Robitec America Inc. has been implementing sales and service for its tube cold bending machines which are made in Germany.

“Regular customers and new customers from the USA, Canada and Mexico will benefit from customer-specific on-site advice and shorter delivery times for spare parts,” the manufacturer explained.

## Additive manufacturing European conference

CECIMO, the European Association of the Machine Tool industries, is organising the first “Additive Manufacturing European Conference” at the European Parliament in Brussels (Belgium) on 23 June 2015. Representatives from additive manufacturing (AM) companies, EU institutions, think tanks and the machine tools sector will discuss how the full-scale industrialisation of AM could be achieved in a rapid and cost-effective manner, and how this disruptive technology could help to create high value for the economy and society. The event will be hosted by Reinhard Bütikofer, Member of the European Parliament (Group of the Greens/European Free Alliance).

The speakers’ session will tackle AM from the perspective of technology, markets, economic development and sustainability. During the session that follows, panellists will look into business and regulatory framework conditions in Europe and their impact on the development of AM. The discussion will focus on the role government, industry, academia and the research community in AM development and opportunities provided by pan-European initiatives. Participation is free but places are limited. Confirmed sponsorships include Prima Power as Premium Sponsor of the conference and ASTM International as Gold Sponsor.

“Additive manufacturing, or 3D printing, is identified as one of the major disrupting technologies of the 21st century. It offers great potential to enable design freedom and the development of complex free-form structures, the efficient and accelerated production of customized products and the minimisation of material waste. As application fields rapidly expand from prototyping towards direct parts production, its transformative impact is being revealed in all areas of manufacturing – product design, production, supply chain organization, services and skills,” said CECIMO.
