# SAY IT WITH FIBRE

[TARİH: 01.01.2019 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

A fibre laser features the active medium of an optical fibre that has been doped in rare elements; typically, erbium, ytterbium, neodymium, thulium, praseodymium, holmium or dysprosium. The fibre laser is a relatively new innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin, and now thicker, sheet metal (including non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium). However, the technology has quickly been embraced by subcontractors and job shops as its extended cutting capabilities have increased their reach into new markets and new customers. A range of manufacturers have therefore invested heavily in fibre laser offerings to the global market.

The use of fibres eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized. This also makes fibre lasers very stable and easy to use. The beam quality is high and continuously becoming more powerful. Fibre lasers are also very efficient and easy to cool, converting around 70-80% of the power from the pump source. This is better for power consumption, but also means that less power is being converted to heat. Fibre lasers can be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials and fine feature cutting.

Fibre lasers work with low amplitude noise, are extremely resistant to heavy environments and offer low maintenance costs. We therefore highlight here a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

## Fibre in focus

AMADA presented its latest fibre laser highlights from the world of sheet metal processing at EuroBLECH (October 2018), with a special focus on laser technologies and production benefits delivered by IoT. Recent developments in AMADA's ENSIS laser technology, powered by in-house developed fibre oscillators, were showcased. ENSIS technology uses variable beam control to provide high speed and high quality for the processing of flat sheet and tube and even for welding.

With 12 kilowatts, the high-end ByStar Fiber laser cutting system from Bystronic is designed to boost the profitability and speed of the cutting process for sheet thicknesses as high as 30mm. To integrate the higher laser power into the cutting process, Bystronic has enhanced the design of the ByStar Fiber's cutting head to process a wide range of materials.

The Swiss manufacturer launched its first-ever high-power 10kW fibre laser machine at the end of 2016, capable of cutting up to 25mm mild steel. It has also expanded the ByStar Fiber laser cutting system with the rotary axis for tube processing. The new function enables users to switch between flatbed laser cutting and tube processing in just a few simple steps.

At the "China International Machine Tool & Tools Exhibition" (CIMES) in July 2018, Bystronic presented the new generation of BySmart Fiber. The new laser cutting system unlocks the full potential of fibre laser technology to customers in the entry-level segment. It is a versatile cutting system with up to 6 kilowatts of laser power and matching automation solutions.

Bystronic's new generation of cutting nozzles boast a longer service life, which Bystronic achieves by using a special alloy for the nozzles. Cutting tests carried out by Bystronic's development engineers have shown that this alloy reduces the wear on the nozzle, even when cutting with the highest laser output power. The new long-life nozzles are more resistant to the adhesion of cutting particles that can have a negative impact on the cutting characteristics of the nozzle.

CYLASER has been pioneering the application of solid-state fibre optic laser technology for industrial cutting applications since 2004. Today, and hundreds of installations later, it has refined and improved its original vision of a robust, precise, efficient and productive fibre laser cutting system with two new laser cutting systems: Vortex and Vega (designed to offer excellent cut quality), which were on display at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover (October 2018).

"Vega is our latest innovation and uses a new type of generator plus our proprietary cutting head to fill the quality gap with CO2 laser cutting systems on stainless steel. Vega fills this gap for stainless steel cutting of stainless steel of between 1-6mm thickness. Vortex is our fibre laser cutting process for medium to high material thicknesses (8-20mm). Vega offers up to 4kW of power, whilst Vortex starts from 4kW and above," Frank Vanin, its general manager, told ISMR.

Innovative features on CYLASER machines include their unusual 'aeroplane' structure. The cutting head runs on a beam that is mounted to the main structure, which is independent from the table. It is not a gantry system. This means that the system is not subject to vibrations, helping to control cutting quality stability in the long term. The cutting head is also at a higher level than a gantry, so it is not subject to thermal drift.
