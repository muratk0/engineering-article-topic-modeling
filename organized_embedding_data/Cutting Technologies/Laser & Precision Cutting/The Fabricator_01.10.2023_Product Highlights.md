5-axis laser cutting system delivers fast processing

MC Machinery has introduced the Mitsubishi FV 5-axis laser cutting system. Along with a fiber-optic laser, the machine uses a double-rail gantry motion system for high-speed, accurate cutting and 4.0G H-axis acceleration for high-speed height sensing—a combination that delivers the fastest processing in Mitsubishi Electric’s history, according to the company.

An open-architecture fixture table allows for simple access to cut parts and loading/unloading of material. Magnetic crash protection allows for faster recovery time, eliminating the need for mechanical or optical alignment and reducing the risk of drive system damage.

Press brake bends oversized parts quickly

TRUMPF is bringing the latest generation of its TruBend 8000 series to the North American market. With a 34-in. open height and a stroke of 27 in., the press brake allows workers to easily bend and remove workpieces that require a large box height, the company states. Press forces are 440 to 1,100 tons for processing long, thick pieces of sheet metal and high-tensile materials.

Available options include a bending aid that automatically positions the workpiece for bending and a tool clamp for high-tonnage applications. In addition, with the station bending option, workers can move from one station to the next, each equipped with different upper and lower tools, to perform a variety of bending operations without changing the machine setup for each operation.

Cermet blade cuts metals quickly, coolly

Diablo Tools has introduced a line of cermet (ceramic and metallic blend) saw blades for cutting metals and stainless steel with cordless and corded saws.

The Steel Demon blade features a proprietary design that delivers up to 25 times longer life, 50 times cooler cuts, and 10 times faster cuts compared to standard abrasive cutoff discs, according to the company.

The formulation of the cermet-tipped teeth allows users to make burrfree cuts with minimum sparks in metals and stainless. The Triple Chip Grind tooth geometry produces large chips, reducing airborne contaminants. When combined with the blade’s stabilizer vents, this tooth geometry helps create a controlled cutting action. The teeth produce clean, cool-to-the-touch cuts.

The blades are available for thin, medium, and thick metals and stainless steels, offered in diameters from 5⅜ to 14 in.

Weld fume extractor delivers power, maneuverability

RoboVent has introduced the portable ProCube II weld fume extractor, featuring a 4-HP motor and 153 CFM, for small fabrication shops and manufacturers requiring flexibility in weld fume extraction. It pairs with one or two fume extraction guns for manual welding. These systems integrate a weld torch with built-in fume extraction to capture most fumes as they are created. The extractor also can be attached to a small, movable backdraft plenum for manual welding or used with the RoboVent Flex-Trac tip extraction system for light-production robotic welding.

With four heavy-duty casters, the compact unit can be moved by one person, so welders can stay mobile while working on large weldments. It also is suitable for welders working in tight spaces or remote areas where traditional source capture is not possible or practical.

CNC designed for automated plasma, oxyfuel cutting

ESAB has unveiled its Vision T6 CNC for automated plasma and oxyfuel cutting machines. It features a multitouch, high-definition panel with a Windows operating system and includes functions such as swipe-to-zoom and dragand-drop. The home button gives quick and easy access to a variety of features and capabilities, letting operators select and edit programs, create nests, locate parts on a plate, and cut. Biodegradable lubricants save cleanup costs

Renewable Lubricants offers patented, ultimately biodegradable Bio-Air Tool lubricants. According to the company, they have better natural lubricity than petroleum-based oils but are safer for employees and the environment.

Because they are bio-based, there is no danger of polluting the air, water, soil, or work environment with spilled lubricant. Should a leak occur, operators can simply wipe it up and throw away the towels or rags, with no reporting or special disposal required.

The lubricants emulsify water for improved lubricity and corrosion protection in moist conditions. They can be fed through an airline lubricator or with a squirt directly into the tool through the quick coupling air connection.
