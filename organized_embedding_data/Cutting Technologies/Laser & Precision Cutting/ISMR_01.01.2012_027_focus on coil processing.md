## Laser cutting from coil

IRON has been building upgraded demonstration lines for punching, bending and coil feeding/laser cutting at its facility and now has a demonstration coil-fed laser cutting line ready at its Italian facility to show customers. The coil-fed laser cutting line, dubbed TEMNOS, has also been shipped to the US for customer demonstrations. Designed in cooperation with Chicago Slitter under a new agreement, the 2m-wide line (handling up to 3.2mm thick material), mainly for automotive applications, will be built and installed in the US.

“The entry feed line (6/10-tonne decoiler/levelling) is done by IRON and shipped to the US. The levelling operation uses 19 rolls with 6-high backup rolls, then loop compensation,” Massimo Dalla Pace told ISMR. TEMNOS is a laser cutting centre, available in 500mm, 1000mm and 1500mm versions, and can be integrated in a programmable unloading system. It is equipped with Siemens hardware solutions, including NC and digital drivers, and various CAD/CAM solutions as well as an IPG laser generator.

“The process of laser cutting from coil is a good example of our flexibility and ability to face new challenges and provide answers to current market needs, requiring more competitive solutions to speed up production steps and lower costs,” added Dalla Pace.

“The main advantages of using coil over sheet is that you don’t need to cut to length, there is no need to nest (hence less scrap) and no downtime as you do not have to wait for blanks to be loaded onto the machine. There are substantial time and cost advantages to using coil over blanks with less material used overall. It is a more efficient process and certainly less costly.

“The customer can manufacture, for example, metal furniture parts (a cupboard’s side panels, door, back panel etc.) using just one coil width,” he added. “The line can punch and cut to length as required. A full range of parts can therefore be produced from just one coil – a very flexible process. This reduces scrap levels and increases productivity and efficiency on the line.”

## Punching and bending

“Our new punching and bending system, with full servo bending, is now faster, more reliable and more flexible. The line offers full panel bending – mostly for metal furniture, refrigerator panel doors etc. It offers huge flexibility in sectors such as home appliance, air conditioning, lifting etc.” he added.

IRON retrofitting activities include electronic and mechanical improvements to the line as well as faster speeds, better controls and higher quality tolerances. IRON has also equipped its machines to process higher strength materials and new generation materials. The punching and bending line is a flexible modular line that incorporates a punch press (with thick turret tooling), a part-turning device (after punching), positive and negative bends on the punched parts and can handle part dimensions ranging from 650mm width x 1500mm length to 1500mm width x 3600mm in length.

“The strip holder and bending process is completely automated – using servo drives. We have developed a CAD system that works directly from a customer’s drawing. The line itself is very complex, with different stations – coil handling; a punching unit; a shear; a buffer; a tilting device and four bending stations – and is designed for high production volumes. Each station has four servo drive motors (16 in all), which guarantees faster operation and better, cleaner process control,” Dalla Pace told ISMR.

Other design elements promote high yield output such as tolerance quality and accuracy in both operations – punching and bending – as well as the ability to hold tight strip tolerances, which can potentially increase production by 100 per cent, says IRON.

The bending functionality features four electronic servo axes. The sheet remains fixed and is passed through four synchronised bending stations, so there is no downtime. IRON counts three seconds for transport, three seconds for bending and three seconds for unloading – a total of nine seconds for the finished part. This is extremely rapid bending in very short cycle times. There is no downtime or part turning time – it makes the punch-bending process much more flexible with no bottlenecks, IRON told ISMR.

## An eye on the future

This year, IRON has sold various cut-to-length lines to customers in Germany as well as laser coil feeding lines to clients in Italy, Germany and Finland. A major focus for the company in recent years has been markets in Russia, India and China and it has now set its sights on the US market. Multiple operations on specialised lines, including punching, are IRON’s speciality.

Under a recently signed agreement, IRON’s four main products – its cut-to-length lines, coil feeding lines for mechanical presses, punching and bending line from coil and laser cutting from coil line (dubbed TEMNOS) - will be sold through the Chicago Slitter portfolio in the US. ■
