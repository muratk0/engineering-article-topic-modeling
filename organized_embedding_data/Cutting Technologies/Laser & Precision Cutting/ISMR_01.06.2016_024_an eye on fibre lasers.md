# An eye on fibre lasers

[TARİH: 01.06.2016 ISMR]

WITH MANY profiling and fabrication shops instinctively drawn to the increasingly higher output capabilities of the latest fibre laser cutters, Amada argues that careful consideration should be given to correctly balancing the investment before any decisions are taken.

Amada, which offers 2kW, 4kW and a soon-to-be-launched in Europe, 6kW version of its LCG-AJ fibre laser cutting system, advises that users need to consider their overall productivity targets to ensure that they invest in the correct power level and balance it with suitable forming capacity. In short, it wants its customers to be able to justify the cost of a laser cutter while still giving downstream operations the attention they may need.

"Goals such as increasing productivity by 50% or maintaining current productivity but reducing total costs have a major effect on choosing the right fibre laser power level. In many instances, a lower power fibre laser makes a more sensible and cost-effective choice," added the Japanese manufacturer.

Fibre laser systems have witnessed a meteoric rise in popularity, thanks largely to their ability to broach the limits of conventional CO2 lasers, and these attributes are showcased in Amada's latest LCG-AJ laser technology. Instead of the racks of laser diodes found in most fibre laser resonators, Amada's technology utilises 2kW diode modules, which negate the need for many complicated splicing points. As a result of the fewer components and connectors, beam quality is enhanced and resonator life extended.

"The Amada LCG-AJ can process a wide range of materials, including brass, copper and titanium, making it a pragmatic choice for fabricators that work with a varied material mix. It is particularly adept at reducing overheads, from energy consumption to labour costs. In fact, with the LCG-AJ, business owners can cut their electricity bills by more than 60% as it uses a third of the electricity required by CO2 systems of the same power," the company told ISMR.

## Multi-slide motion mechanical press

INGYU is a precision stamping press manufacturer, in operation for 45 years in Taiwan. It aims to provide high-precision, high-speed presses for automotive, can-making, motor lamination and complex forming applications.

"Our new Saga multi-slide motion mechanical presses enable users to produce highaccuracy sheet metal parts, increase the number of products per minute and reduce their tooling cost. With the one-piece frame and rugged plunger and post guiding system, the press's vertical accuracy is within 0.01mm for extended die life and improved part quality. Slide velocity decreases towards Bottom Dead Centre (BDC) to reduce the impact of stamping, noise and vibration," explained the manufacturer.

There are three different slide motion modes available for various product applications. In forming mode, pressing time is longer for optimum material flattening results. In bending mode, slide velocity decreases while bending the material and speeds up again for quick cycles. In drawing mode, stable pressing avoids uneven part surface. Users stamping a variety of parts can choose three slide motion modes in one press to maximise their productivity.
