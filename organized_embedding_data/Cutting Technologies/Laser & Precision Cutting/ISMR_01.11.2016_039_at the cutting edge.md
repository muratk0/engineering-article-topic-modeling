## A flexible focus for cutting and welding

"Here at EuroBLECH, we are showcasing our welding and cutting products. Cutting is a very important business for us. Job shops are a major part of our end customer base. They need the flexibility to process a range of materials with varying thicknesses (from thin to very thick). Job shops appreciate one machine that can process different types, strengths and ranges of materials - flexibility is key. We bring this flexibility with our BIMO-FSC laser cutting head. It handles a wide range and thickness of materials that include aluminium, carbon steels and more. The key feature is the fully automated, machine-controlled adjustment of focus diameter and focus position. It guarantees efficient flat sheet cutting and high productivity in manufacturing," Dr. Kuba told ISMR.

Dr. Kuba outlined several key trends in today's laser cutting markets, including moves towards higher laser power and the advent of the Direct Diode Laser (DDL).

"Diode lasers could be the 'next big thing' in laser cutting markets – we have already designed our products to handle diode laser wavelengths. We observed, in tests with our BIMO-FSC, good results in speed and in cutting quality for this laser, although DDL is not yet widely adopted as a laser cutting technology," he told ISMR.

He also sees significantly increasing numbers of fibre laser installations in today's job shops.

"There is a very large installed base of CO2 laser cutting systems in the field today. However, the vast majority of new laser cutting installations employ fibre lasers because of their higher efficiency, lower cost and smaller footprint. Users who already have CO2 lasers will continue to work with them, but new installations today tend largely to be fibre lasers," Kuba explained.

Laser welding heads, laser light cables, laser modules and laser optics, including fused silica optics, were also displayed on the II-VI booth at EuroBLECH. These optics can be supplied in different configurations, including aspherical lenses, spherical lenses and debris shields.

"There are definite synergies between the welding and cutting businesses. We are also showcasing a head with a flexible beam manipulation feature that is used for welding technology. One of our core competencies is our knowledge and expertise of optics design," added Dr. Kuba.

## Customised consultancy

"I am proud to say that we have invested in and completed full-blown application laboratories with laser sources, tooling, a 3-axis gantry system, process monitoring systems and robots etc. for cutting and welding. The purpose is to test features for customer projects, R&D projects or feasibility studies. This is a great achievement for us," explained Dr. Kuba.

Validation of a technology for real-time monitoring of the welding process with the "Weldeye" system from Lessmüller Lasertechnik with the II-VI HIGHYAG laser processing head, PDT-B.

Customers can review first-hand the feasibility of applications, fabricate samples and evaluate process windows, allowing processes to be optimised before they are transferred to serial production. The application laboratories are also used for validating process optimisation technologies developed by industry partners. Recent examples are the validation of laser welding process monitoring systems and the Inline Coherent Imaging (ICI) technology, a method to measure the depth of the weld.

As of July this year, II-VI HIGHYAG now provides customised, application-specific consulting services in its own application laboratories. Collaboratively, with customers, II-VI HIGHYAG's application engineers develop system and process set-ups for mission-critical laser applications that require a high degree of customisation to meet performance and reliability requirements.

Areas of expertise include laser welding, laser soldering, laser brazing and laser cutting. The application laboratories are equipped with II-VI HIGHYAG laser processing heads, direct diode and fibre lasers (up to 8kW) and various drive systems in the form of industrial robots and gantry systems.

"We invested significantly in our tailor-made facility near Berlin with the new application laboratories. We have also employed more people this year to help us meet our targets - all in all, I am very optimistic for the future."

## LineAr technology in focus

"At EuroBLECH, we are presenting our new LineAr Plus press brake technology and offline software which will soon be integrated into our CNC controller," Zinetti told ISMR.

LineAr technology starts from the linear concept of three beams at the top and three beams for the bottom tables. The main advantages of the application are that the tool-holder beams (upper and lower), under a uniform load, remain straight. No crowning, or any other systems to compensate for the deformations of the tool holder beams, are needed. The effects on the quality of the bending work are immediate - the bending line is straight. There is no need to correct the theoretical crowning compensation for each different material reaction. There is no need to calculate the required crowning compensation before each different bend. There is also no curvature in the line of bend, which is a typical effect of traditional crowning systems.

Schiavi has further improved the LineAr concept, ensuring even greater accuracy for the bend.

With the LineAr Plus, Zinetti told ISMR, Schiavi has improved the LineAr concept, ensuring even greater accuracy for the bend. The "Plus" package is an optional extra that is available on all LineAr machines. Schiavi's LineAr Plus 220-40 (220 tonnes x 4 metres) large press brake features this capability.

"LineAr technology is the only technology that can solve the 'canoe effect', when you bend long parts, for 'canoe-free' bending," explained Nicolò Zinetti. "Constancy of the angle along the length of the components is guaranteed on LineAr press brakes. Customers who buy LineAr machines want great jobs with a great finish. They do not want press brakes that adjust the bend every time, they want instant accuracy and the ability to differentiate their product lines with this type of high-tech machinery. The press brake is bolted, not welded, for greater accuracy."

With LineAr technology, Schiavi manufactures press brakes of up to 400 tons and up to 6 metres in length with no pit.

"We already have customers for the LineAr Plus – last year, the largest machine we sold was in the Czech Republic (630 tonnes and 7 metres) and, this year, we sold several large machines in Italy (up to 800 tonnes and 6 metres). Customers need certain degrees of accuracy that regular crowning devices on press brakes cannot meet. The German and Japanese markets particularly prize accuracy and I believe these are good markets for Linear Plus technology," continued Zinetti.

## Accent on software

The focus for Schiavi is also firmly on the software driving its press brakes. Last year, it showed the Moses projector on the pressbrake. The projection of graphical information onto the frame of the press brake (rather than to the side of the machine) is both user-friendly and time-saving. Operators do not have to walk between the control and the work-piece to check processes, just simply glance upwards to check the finer details of a job.

"We have now gone ahead with integration between the control and the projector so that the operator can interact directly from the upper beam, sending parameters like angle direction or flange length. The programme updates this simultaneously so there is bidirectional communication between Moses and our ATLAS control. The next step is to integrate our new offline software into the Atlas control and we aim to have that ready very soon - that is our main software objective," explained Zinetti.
