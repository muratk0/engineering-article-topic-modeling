Laser metal fusion system for 3-D printing introduced

TRUMPF Inc. has introduced the Tru-Print 1000 laser metal fusion (LMF) system for 3-D printing. The company offers the laser beam source, machine, powder, services, and application consulting from a single source.

The system uses a laser and metallic powder to build any desired component, based on data supplied directly by a CAD program. LMF systems create the component, layer by layer, from metal powders with grain sizes as small as 20 microns. This technology is suitable for parts that are complex in their geometry, such as those with internal channels and hollow spaces, and for manufacturing individual parts or short runs. The user interface with touchscreen control steps the operator intuitively through the individual phases of the process. All the components, including the laser, optics, process enclosure, filter unit, and control cabinet, are integrated into the system’s compact housing.

Drum blast machine handles small, delicate components

Agtos offers a flexible drum blast machine for blasting small, delicate components. According to the company, parts are processed with optimum circulation, without jamming, and no workpieces are displaced or damaged. The machine also provides defined blasting results and prevents interruptions in the production process, the manufacturer says.

The drum begins in the loading position. Before the blasting process starts, the machine closes and the drum swivels into a suitable blasting position in front of the turbine. At the same time, it rotates around its own axis. The blasting process starts and continues until all workpieces are well mixed and completely blasted.
