# THE FUTURE FOR FIBRE

[TARİH: 01.03.2016 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today

According to Optech Consulting, more than half of the market for laser material processing systems is accounted for by micro and fine processing. The fibre laser is a relatively new innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin, and now thicker, sheet metal (including non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium). Linear drives enable rapid positioning and cutting speeds to be achieved.

A range of manufacturers have invested heavily in fibre laser offerings to the global market while subcontractors and job shops have quickly found that its extended cutting capabilities have extended their reach into new markets and new customers. Fibre lasers can be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials and fine feature cutting. Cut capability on steel is from thin gauge to approximately 20mm.

The use of fibres eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized. This also makes fibre lasers very stable and easy to use. The beam quality is high and continuously becoming more powerful. Fibre lasers are also very efficient and easy to cool, converting around 70-80% of the power from the pump source. This is better for power consumption, but also means that less power is being converted to heat. We highlight here a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

## Fibre in focus

In 2015, UK-based Generic Punching Systems Ltd became Amada UK’s launch customers for its ENSIS 3015 AJ new fibre laser technology. The ENSIS features an Amada-designed oscillator. With just 2kW of laser output from the integrated oscillator, the ENSIS 3015 AJ can manufacture mild steel products that previously required 4kW of laser power.

“The secret behind the new innovation lies in the ability to modulate the laser beam (flexibly) as a function of sheet thickness, delivering efficient profiling operations with reduced power requirements. This is in marked contrast to conventional fibre laser cutting systems that require changing the focus lens in order to cut a range of material thicknesses,” Amada told ISMR.

Processing times have also been significantly reduced. Designed specifically to deliver high processing of both thick and thin materials, the ENSIS 3015 AJ comes with a high torque motor and helical rack drive in the X/Y axes, and a carriage with a low centre of gravity. Its cutting range reaches 3000 x 1500 x 100mm in the X, Y and Z axes, resulting in feed rates of 100m/min. being achievable.

Amada’s LC-2515 C1 AJ 2kW punch-laser combination machine is equipped with a fibre laser and multi-purpose tool turret for faster processing speeds and fewer secondary operations. On the company’s FOL3015 AJ 4kW fibre laser, material processing is rapid thanks to 340mm/min. linear drives and 5G acceleration.

At Blechexpo 2015 in Stuttgart, Swiss manufacturer Bystronic showcased its new re-designed ByStar Fiber laser, its future platform for fast fibre laser cutting.

“The old Bystar laser was the ‘pick of the bunch’ for many years. The new ByStar Fiber laser is a very flexible, dynamic machine, offering users easy access to the cutting area. It is also very intuitive and features a new ByMotion control system and new ByVision cutting interface. We will be launching new variants of this machine over the next few years,” Johan Elser, head of markets at Bystronic, told ISMR.

With a new machine concept, the ByStar Fiber laser cuts thin sheet metal at high speeds. However, Elster told ISMR, it also achieves outstanding cutting performance in the thick sheet metal range with low operating costs and low maintenance requirements. The ByStar Fiber is aimed at users who want to rely fully on fibre laser technology. The new machine design, with a short cutting bridge, enables strong dynamics even when cutting complex contours. The ByStar Fiber’s newly developed control regulates laser power, speed and focus. Bystronic has further increased the fibre laser’s versatility by providing access to the cutting area over the long side of the machine. The large sliding door allows users to insert small express jobs and residual sheets at any time, even when largescale jobs are running.

At the Birmingham factory of UK subcontractor, Wheeler Fabrications, a 3kW BySprint Fiber 3015 fibre laser cutting machine from Bystronic UK has taken over from an older, second-hand CO2 laser, dramatically raising productivity and product consistency as well as allowing the subcontractor to reduce both lead-times and costs to customers.

Production manager, Mark Ashford, said, “Eighty per cent of our work here involves sheet of between 1 and 3mm thick, which is ideal for extracting maximum benefit from fibre laser cutting. The BySprint Fiber machine, due to a combination of its technology and the high power of the laser, is five times faster at profiling components than the machine that it replaced. It is also significantly less expensive to run, as it pulls less power and does not require costly servicing of optics. Neither do we have to buy bottles of helium or CO2 resonator gas any more. It means that we can manufacture more economically, allowing us to pass on cost reductions to our customers and at the same time maintain profitability.”

Both flat sheet and tube section can be processed on BLM’s Adige LC5 laser cutting system. Capacity of the LC5 includes the ability to process flat sheet measuring up to 1500mm x 3000mm and round, square, rectangular and oval section tube up to a maximum diameter of 120mm. The user has the option of choosing either a 4.5kW CO2 laser source or, a 3kW fibre laser which opens up the potential to process reflective metals such as copper, brass and aluminium.
