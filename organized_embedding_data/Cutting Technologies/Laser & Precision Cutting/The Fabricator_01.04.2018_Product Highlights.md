Robotic abrasive waterjet cutting system cuts 3-D metal parts

Alliance Automation LLC has introduced a fully enclosed robotic abrasive waterjet cutting cell for cutting small 3-D metal parts.

The enclosure comprises sounddampening panels that lower sound levels to 82 dB at the operator station. A turntable wall indexes and seals shut for operator load and unload.

Two 6-axis Yaskawa Motoman MH24 XP robots are needed for the abrasive environment. The XP version is IP67-rated through the entire arm. A custom jacket also is added so the robots can be washed down with a hose for scheduled maintenance.

The system lowers operating costs by eliminating the need for long setup times of different parts. All jobs can be preprogrammed and selected through the touchscreen HMI or scanned from RFID tags on the tool.

Pipe cutting/3-D profiling machine offers automated pipe handling

HGG’S new SPC 1200 RB pipe cutting and 3-D profiling machine combines the logistics of an automated roller bed for moving pipes in and out, while incorporating a floating chuck for pipe cutting accuracy, precision, and repeatability, the company reports.

The machine accommodates pipe from 3 to 48 in. dia., 20 to 40 ft. long, and with wall-cutting accuracy up to 6 in. thick. It combines two types of pipe cutting with infeed and outfeed conveyors, eliminating the need for cranes to move individual pipes in and out. The integrated chuck helps provide users with accurate, on-the-fly measurement and compensation of longitudinal pipe movement (creep) during rotation of the pipe, along with repeatability.

The machine is equipped with patented biaxial cutting heads that can be used to cut with oxyfuel and plasma. It can be configured to multiple layout combinations, allowing users to fit the machine to a variety of floor space requirements and logistical flow. Fume extraction is built-in; when connected to a proper dust collector, the machine draws most fumes out and keeps the shop nearly smoke-free.

CAD software add-on streamlines design processes

IronCAD LLC has announced the availability of the latest version of its Mechanical addon for the IronCAD Design Collaboration Suite 2018.

In this release, the Steels tool has been enhanced to support tolerance setback values to account for welding. In addition, users now can extend multiple steel segments at once to streamline the process of trimming steel structures. New capabilities for pairing gears to the correct fitting locations have been added. The user selects two gears, right-clicks and drags the gear command into the scene, and the gears become paired. In addition, new capabilities to export all the unfolded flat patterns for sheet metal to individual drawing files for manufacturing have been added.

Sheet metal design has been further enhanced to support the creation of tabs and slots that are commonly found in fabrication design.

CAD/CAM software updated with CNC punch programming automation advancements

Striker Systems has announced the availability of Striker 2018 CAD/CAM and nesting software, with significant advancements in CNC punch programming automation. This release addresses the unique requirements of CNC punching while advancing the technology with the objective of complete punch programming automation.

In CNC punching, with a multitude of tooling, tool placement, and machine options, optimum tool selection and fit can be a challenge for fabricators. Special tools, tool sequencing, microjoint placement, drop door support, and sheet repositioning also must be taken into consideration. As with previous versions, this software includes advanced automatic punching algorithms and flexible tool substitution capability, and the new release refines this technology to minimize or eliminate user intervention in the tooling process.

Fiber laser requires minimal floor space

The new TruLaser 2030 fiber from TRUMPF Inc. offers up to 4 kW of laser power and optional automation. It requires only 25 by 20 ft. of floor space, including pallet changer.

Components such as the switch cabinet, suction system, and laser are built into the machine frame. The 5-by 10-ft. working area is accessible along its whole length. Large windows and LED illumination optimize viewing inside the machine, and the design ensures safety from the laser.

The machine’s control guides the operator through setting up and programming the machine right on the control user interface until the program is ready to start.
