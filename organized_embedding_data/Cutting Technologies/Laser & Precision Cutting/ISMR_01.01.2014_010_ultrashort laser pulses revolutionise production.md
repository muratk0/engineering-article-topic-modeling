# Ultrashort laser pulses revolutionise production

[TARİH: 01.01.2014 ISMR]

RESEARCHERS FROM TRUMPF, Bosch and the University of Jena were awarded the German Future Prize 2013 on 4 December 2013. Dr. Jens König from Bosch, Dr. Dirk Sutter from TRUMPF, Professor Dr. Stefan Nolte from the University of Jena and Fraunhofer IOF were awarded the prize by German President, Joachim Gauck.

“Being nominated for the German Future Prize is a great honour,” said Dr.-Ing. E.h. Peter Leibinger, head of the TRUMPF Laser Technology/ Electronics Division. “We’re very happy that the German President is honouring the entrepreneurial daring we showed over many years in driving this highly complex research topic of the ultrashort pulse laser to industrial maturity.”

TRUMPF is the world’s largest manufacturer of lasers for industrial production. Ultrashort pulse lasers used for the micro-processing of all kinds of different materials – from glass smartphone covers to medical implants and injection valves – are an important and fast-growing market. The fact that the ultrashort pulse laser has such a broad application here – while still being only on the threshold of its full potential – is primarily the achievement of joint research and development work by TRUMPF, Bosch and the University of Jena.

Today, Bosch is mass-producing structural components in their millions with the ultrashort pulse laser for sectors such as the automotive industry. One example is a direct injection valve for gasoline where the ultrashort pulse laser drills geometrically complex injection holes with smooth walls. Ultrashort pulse lasers are also suitable for the high-precision machining of almost all materials. There are also the minimal invasive processes used in medical technology – in the production of stents, for example, which are used to keep blood vessels open.

Sectors such as the automotive industry, semiconductor production, industrial technology, medical technology, the photovoltaic sector and the electronics industry are all developing applications for ultrashort pulse lasers. By 2013, some 30 million parts manufactured with the help of USP lasers were delivered to Bosch customers.

“The route taken by the ultrashort pulse laser is a perfect example of the German research model,” explained Dr. Peter Leibinger. “Universities do the basic research, companies develop the results in accordance with market requirements and the value-add remains in Germany.” ■

MATERIAL ISSUES ISMR SAYS: “With a stronger focus on end-client markets, Tata Steel aims to enhance its market position and increase sales”

As Tata Steel realigns its distribution and service sales activities in Europe, ISMR speaks to Dr Jens Lauber, Managing Director, Distribution Mainland Europe, Tata Steel

The European operations of Tata Steel, whose main steelmaking sites are in the UK and the Netherlands, comprise Europe’s second largest steel producer. It supplies steel and related services to construction, automotive, packaging, rail, lifting and excavating, energy and power, aerospace and other demanding markets worldwide. The combined Tata Steel group is one of the world’s largest steel producers, with an aggregate crude steel capacity of more than 29 million tonnes and approximately 80,000 employees across four continents.

Tata Steel, the second largest steel producer in Europe, recently realigned the sales activities of its distribution and service centres on the European continent to better serve the needs of its customers. This completion of a company-wide shift in sales structure has led to a clearer focus on customer end-market sectors and product groups, and a closer relationship between Tata Steel’s sites. The aims of the realignment are:

• A clear focus on customer end-markets and product groups.
• Gives customers access to Tata Steel’s wide range of differentiated and standard steel products and related services.
• Ambitious growth plans for a range of differentiated products tailored to deliver value for customers.

“Tata Steel is now able to offer a more targeted response to end-customer markets, and tailor its products and supporting services to better meet the specific needs of its customers. The realignment reflects a closer working relationship between Tata Steel’s distribution, sales and service teams, its steel production plants in the Netherlands and the UK, and the company’s research and development organisation,” Dr Jens Lauber, Managing Director, Distribution Mainland Europe, Tata Steel told ISMR at the recent Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart.

“Following the realignment, each distribution and service centre will have sector experts dedicated to the individual needs of particular industries, such as automotive, construction and lifting and excavating.”

## Invest for success

In addition, sales from all of Tata Steel’s locations in Europe have been arranged by the light or heavy gauge product group and processing services that they offer. They have been clustered so that customers can access the group’s entire product and service portfolio from one point of contact.

## TATA STEEL Q2 EUROPEAN RESULTS

Production in Q2 FY’14 for Tata Steel in Europe increased to 3.86 million tonnes from 3.74 million tonnes in the previous quarter. Q2 FY’14 production was 16% higher than the 3.34 million tonnes produced in Q2 FY’13. Deliveries totalled 6.6 million tonnes in H1 FY’14, compared to 6.63 million tonnes in H1 FY’13. Q2 FY’14 deliveries increased by 10% to 3.46 million tonnes from 3.14 million tonnes in Q1 FY’14 and by 1% from 3.42 million tonnes in Q2 FY’13. Sales of differentiated products rose by 13% in H1 FY’14 compared to the previous year.

At the same time, Tata Steel continues to invest in improving its production and processing equipment. The latest example is a new slitting line for Service Centre Gelsenkirchen, Germany, which is scheduled for completion in early 2014. The line enables the company to process advanced and ultra-high strength steel which is used to manufacture the latest automotive components. The total investment in the expansion of the production facility as well as in the new slitting line is worth around €9.5 million.

“The purpose of the realignment and these investments is to make a lasting improvement to our market position, both quantitatively and qualitatively. We aim to increase our sales through our distribution network in continental Europe by 30 per cent in the next three years – to 1.8 million tonnes of steel per year – while significantly increasing the proportion that differentiated products make up of that amount,” explained Dr Lauber.

“Overall, Tata Steel aims to have a sales volume of 40 per cent in customer-specific differentiated products by 2016. These are products offering distinctive capabilities for specific applications. It is important to understand that this represents a new mindset for Tata Steel and a new way of doing business – we are offering our customers in the 150-million tonne market in Europe a local service with centralised contacts, if they wish, whilst connecting all those involved in the supply chain. We do not want to extend the delivery chain beyond 250km to the customer.”

By supplying its customers from the bestqualified and closest location for the specific application, Tata Steel is also improving its service quality. The company’s distribution network offers hot rolled, direct rolled, cold rolled and metallic coated strip products, pre-finished steels and plates. Its facilities enable the supply of material that is cut and finished to the exact size and specification from the customer.

“We need to help our customers to identify the value of materials in their processes and we do this in our product applications centre where all product development takes place. The centre also develops understanding of how our products perform in customer processes such as bending, welding, forming etc. We can troubleshoot for customers and help them improve their own processes so we take a long term view on this,” explained Dr Lauber.

## An eye on cost

“Another highlight is our newly launched engineering service, Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Scan. This service identifies and quantifies how Tata Steel’s advanced products support customers in the automotive industry by reducing the total cost of ownership in comparison to existing materials available on the market,” Basjan Berkhout, Product Marketing Manager Body-in-White for the Automotive Sector, Tata Steel, told ISMR at Blechexpo.

“Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) Scans can lead to dramatic cost reductions at all stages of the manufacturing and end-user process. The services are not only offered to the automotive industry but also to customers in other sectors, such as the lifting and excavating market or the building industry.”

Previously, Tata Steel has supported vehicle manufacturers and automotive suppliers with TCO Concept Studies – virtual studies which evaluate how using certain product solutions can offer an optimum balance between performance, light weighting and cost. The new TCO Scans take this process a step further by assessing how a particular Tata Steel advanced steel product will function in the customer’s own production process by using study methods tailored to the specific steel characteristics.

TCO Scan MagiZinc® is the first new service to be launched and targets press shop efficiency by carrying out live trials on the customer’s production lines to calculate the processing benefits of MagiZinc coating compared to a conventional zinc (GI) coating. MagiZinc is Tata Steel’s innovative alloyed-zinc coating which can be used in thinner layers due to its improved corrosion-protection properties, offering light weighting benefits. It also causes less zinc abrasion, reducing tool pollution and maintenance downtime. The TCO Scan MagiZinc calculates how much more efficient the customer’s pressing process could be by using the product.

For a complex component like a tailgate, the TCO Scan has identified a 25 per cent reduction in tool pollution when MagiZinc is used, leading to a significant increase in up-time and therefore reduced total costs of ownership.

Dr. Dominik Schwarz, Director Sales Automotive, Tata Steel, added: “With our new TCO Scans we offer our customers the opportunity to really measure the individual benefits of our advanced products to balance costs, performance and light weighting for their specific application and processes. In future, we will introduce more TCO Scans for our advanced products, including our DP800 HyperForm® with improved formability characteristics.”

From its production facilities in the UK, Netherlands and India, Tata Steel can ship to key locations not just in Europe and India but also to South Africa, Brazil, Mexico, the US and Turkey. The company is also looking at setting up a supply chain to Africa and China.

## Dent-resistant steel

Tata Steel’s product portfolio is continuously being extended, including additions to its Ympress range of high-strength, low-alloy steels. The company is also focusing on optimising its supply chains for its customers by making existing steel grades available locally. One example is dent-resistant steel, BH260, which it supplied to a UK-based car manufacturer from its Zodiac line in South Wales, with additional contracts expected soon. This material allows manufacturers to produce car doors, panels and bonnets which are up to seven per cent thinner and lighter whilst retaining the strength of heavier-weight materials.

Following a year-long development programme, BH260 is now being supplied to the market from Tata Steel’s integrated steelworks at Port Talbot, before being finished on the newly-upgraded Zodiac galvanising line at Llanwern, South Wales.

The product’s high-strength properties are due to the chemistry of the steel which hardens during the vehicle’s paint-bake cycle. The vehicle components are painted and baked at around 170 degrees centigrade, which results in an increased yield strength of the outer panel and therefore an improved dent resistance. Due to its low alloy content, BH260 can be readily welded and also maintains good formability.

Tata Steel recently announced that it would build a Vacuum Induction Melting (VIM) furnace at its Stocksbridge site in South Yorkshire, UK, to enable it to tap into new market opportunities and develop innovative new products for the aerospace and oil and gas industries. The £15 million furnace is expected to be commissioned early in 2015.
