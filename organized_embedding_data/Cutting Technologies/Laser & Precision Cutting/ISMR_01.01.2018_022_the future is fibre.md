# THE FUTURE IS FIBRE

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new fibre laser cutting innovations in the market today.

The fibre laser is a relatively new innovation in laser cutting systems for machining thin, and now thicker, sheet metal (including non-ferrous materials such as copper, brass and titanium). However, the technology has quickly been embraced by subcontractors and job shops as its extended cutting capabilities have extended their reach into new markets and new customers. A range of manufacturers have therefore invested heavily in fibre laser offerings to the global market.

The use of fibres eliminates the need for elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance and the space requirement can be downsized. This also makes fibre lasers very stable and easy to use. The beam quality is high and continuously becoming more powerful. Fibre lasers are also very efficient and easy to cool, converting around 70-80% of the power from the pump source. This is better for power consumption, but also means that less power is being converted to heat. Fibre lasers can be comfortably used for the efficient processing of gauge and fractional materials as well as fine feature cutting.

## Fibre in focus

AMADA launched its ENSIS-3015 AJ fibre laser CNC cutting machine, featuring a 3kW source, in August 2017.

"At 3kW, the machine offers the industry's largest single-diode module size. Unlike other machines, there is no diode module beam combiner, improving reliability and increasing machine uptime. Two machines have already been sold into the UK market," the Japanese manufacturer told ISMR.

AMADA's patented ENSIS technology can change the laser beam mode, not just the focal spot size and focus position, for better beam control in thin and thick sheet processing. The machine also offers single-lens processing for all materials and thicknesses, and features a large-capacity nozzle changer to cover the full range automatically.

"The machine is capable of cutting up to 25mm thick mild steel, which is equivalent to the performance levels of a standard 6kW fibre laser, but uses just half the power to do the same job. With energy prices proving a major overhead for profiling and fabrication shops, this presents the opportunity to make significant savings," explained AMADA.

"ENSIS technology provides a high-speed pierce in 20mm mild steel, and faster cutting than a 4kW fibre laser. This performance is achieved by instantaneously changing the beam mode between pierce and cut. For piercing materials at the thicker end of the material spectrum, oil-shot functionality is available."

The 3kW ENSIS also offers WACS II, the latest version of AMADA's proprietary Water-Assisted Cutting System.

Turkish manufacturer, Baykal, introduced its new fibre laser cutting system, BLS-1530, at the Blechexpo and FABTECH 2017 exhibitions. Its BLS Series fibre laser cutting systems cover a wide range of industrial applications. Manufactured with an "All-in-One Box" design structure, the BLS has a built-in resonator and a chiller with dual motorised tables. A range of fibre power sources, from 500-2500W, are available. The BLS uses a new generation, high-performance linear axis system with rack and pinion.

"With fibre laser technology, there is no need to move the laser source or sheet, enabling high-speed, high-accuracy cutting and dual full-size pallet maximum production throughput. Machines have a simple and user-friendly operator interface, as well as an extensive cutting database," the company told ISMR. See further details on page 50.

Swiss manufacturer Bystronic launched its first-ever high-power 10kW fibre laser machine at the end of 2016, capable of cutting up to 25mm mild steel. It is now expanding its ByStar Fiber laser cutting system with the rotary axis for tube processing. The new function enables users to switch between flatbed laser cutting and tube processing in just a few simple steps.

"When purchasing a new ByStar Fiber, it can now be equipped with an integrated rotary axis. The operator simply inserts the tubes that are to be processed through a hatch on the side of the machine housing or directly through the sliding door on the long side of the machine. Thanks to the sliding door on the long side of the cutting area, ByStar Fiber users always have unlimited access to the parts that are to be processed. This facilitates both the set-up for tube processing jobs and the fast unloading of the completed cut parts," said Bystronic.

Bystronic is also presenting a new generation of cutting nozzles. The newly developed long-life nozzles distinguish themselves through a longer service life, which Bystronic achieves by using a special alloy for the nozzles. Cutting tests carried out by Bystronic’s development engineers have shown that this alloy reduces the wear on the nozzle, even when cutting with the highest laser output power. The new long-life nozzles are more resistant against the adhesion of cutting particles that can have a negative impact on the cutting characteristics of the nozzle.

Ermaksan Makine, one of the leading manufacturers in the sheet metal processing sector, has set up ‘Turkey’s first private sector semi-conductor plant’ and started exporting the machines it produces using a domestically developed national fibre laser beam source, national laser chip and controller systems.

“The domestically-produced laser beam sources have been put into operation as has the first national laser chip, developed by Optoelectronic AR-GE Centre, Ermaksan’s R&D Centre established with its own engineering experience. This YGL-3000 fibre laser beam source is the first to be 100% produced in Turkey, by Turkish engineers,” the manufacturer told ISMR.

HAWK is the new fibre laser cutting machine that it has brought to the market. It can be equipped with 0.5kW or 1kW Ermaksan laser sources. It has no covers but holds the CE mark. It is Ermaksan’s budget fibre laser cutting machine solution with a 3 x 1.5m bed size (without shuttle table). Its small footprint, rapid installation and easy-to-use interface make it suitable for job shops who cut thin material.

At Blechexpo and FABTECH 2017, its flagship laser cutting system, Fibermak, was also showcased with a new 4G optional upgrade. This offers 4G acceleration to users and is now available for the Fibermak Momentum series. See further details on page 32.

Polish innovator Eckert launched an award-winning innovative Piercing Control System (PCS) for its Diamond Fiber Laser 2 cutting machine, which substantially reduces piercing time from 10 seconds to two seconds, as well as cutting gas/energy costs.

Highyag/II-VI's new fibre laser combiners enable fibre laser systems to scale to 10kW by combining the output of multiple kilowatt-class fibre lasers to achieve faster cutting or welding speeds, or to process thicker sheets of metal.

Its new high brightness laser diodes feature a 190μm wide output facet designed to achieve coupling efficiency into widely used 200μm core fibres. The increased output power per emitter enables fibre laser manufacturers to generate higher power levels with a reduced number of emitters. The company's BIMO-FSC laser cutting head is available in different versions for focus control.

"Fibre laser cutting systems have several advantages over CO2 laser systems as they require virtually no maintenance, are more energy-efficient and take up less space. The power supply is much smaller than that of a CO2 laser, yet fibre delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables," explained fibre laser machine manufacturer, Hypertherm, which produces the HyIntensity™ Fiber Laser.

Kjellberg's XFocus 4000 is a compact system with a 4,000 Watt fibre laser source for cutting and marking thin sheets of mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium with all common guiding systems that are suitable for plasma cutting. The laser system can be operated on the same guiding system with a plasma cutting unit, either in change mode or separately.

Belgian sheet metal machinery specialist, LVD Company NV, recently announced the launch of its Electra FL 3015 8kW fibre lasercutting machine.

"The ultra-high-speed Electra cuts a wide range of ferrous and non-ferrous materials as fast as the thermal process allows, without dynamic compromise. The Electra 8kW can maintain 2G acceleration speed while cutting, producing high-quality, high-accuracy cuts in simple to complex configurations. The Electra-FL also features a new cutting head, a new 'smooth lead-in' feature, an advanced drive system and the latest generation of our intuitive Touch-L control," said LVD.

Electra is equipped with an advanced cutting head, featuring automated adjustment of focus position and focus diameter (zoom focus). The ability to change the focal point allows Electra to pierce 20mm material in two seconds for significant productivity gain on fully nested sheets. This also means that less heat is generated in the material, making it more efficient to cut small holes.

LVD also produces the Phoenix and Lynx fibre laser cutting systems. The Phoenix FL-4020 and Phoenix FL 6020 laser cutting machines are designed to handle sheet dimensions of 4000 x 2000mm and 6000 x 2000 mm respectively. They are available in 3-, 4- and 6kW versions.

Mazak's OPTIPLEX 3015 Fiber offers stable cut performance and greater throughput for applications using stainless steel; copper; brass; bronze; aluminium; hastelloy; Inconel; titanium; thin gauge mild steel and other exotic metals. There is no need for additional surface cleaning when painting mild steel cut with nitrogen.

OPTIPLEX 3015 Fiber incorporates Mazak’s Intelligent Technology to directly increase the productivity of the end-user by allowing the machine to optimise the torch set-up automatically per program. This can dramatically improve cut speeds, increase throughput and require less operator intervention, delivering more predictable processing. The combination of fibre beam diameter and focus control provides a wide range of improved cut performance in all material types and thicknesses. The eight-position nozzle changer facilitates unattended operation.

The OPTIPLEX 3015 Fiber also features the new Preview 3 control with a 15” touch screen and straightforward user interface to simplify machine operation. The graphical interface visualises the cutting path and automatically determines process conditions including the required lens, nozzle, feed rate and laser output for different materials and thicknesses. The OPTIPLEX Fiber series machines are available in 2kW, 4kW and 6kW configurations.

Its 2D laser cutting machine, Laser Genius 1530 with the Combo Tower Laser automation system, was showcased for the first time with a 10kW fibre laser source by IPG Photonics, which further boosts its performance especially for high thicknesses. Laser Genius is managed by the Prima Power Open CNC and the HMI framework is the new Prima Power Tulus Laser 2D.

Other fibre laser cutting systems from Prima-Power include the Platino® Fiber laser platform, with Laser Piercing Monitor to give real-time feedback and check when piercing is completed, and Prima Power Fiber Plasma Monitoring (detecting problems with the cutting and formation of plasma when cutting is lost).

Salvagnini's L5 high-dynamic fibre laser system cuts across a large range of thicknesses using its compass, a mechanical drive system and the single-optic cutting head with dry cooling feature.

In 2017, Finnish manufacturer, Casemet Oy, introduced a fully automated Salvagnini L3 fibre laser cell, while the P4Lean bending machine used by Mikemet was integrated into the fibre laser solution, forming the first FlexCell production cell in Finland and Scandinavia.

Salvagnini’s automatic FlexCell ZERO cell is a smart and flexible FMC (Flexible Manufacturing Centre) which integrates three smart machines with OPS-FlexCell integration software. The Flexcell on display at EuroBLECH 2016 featured an integrated L5 laser cutting system, a P2lean panel bender and a B3 ATA press brake.

TRUMPF has launched a new fibre lasercutting machine, the TruLaser 2030 fiber. Equipped with up to four kilowatts of laser power, the TruLaser 2030 fiber is also capable of fully automatic manufacturing.

"Just 7.8 x 5.9m of installation space is required for the TruLaser 2030 fiber, including pallet changer. Important components such as the switch cabinet, suction system and laser are built into the machine frame. The 3m working area is easily accessible along its whole length and is designed for large formats. Large windows and LED illumination provide optimum views inside the machine," the German manufacturer told ISMR.

"TruLaser 2030 fiber is available with two-, three- or four kilowatts of laser power. With its TruDisk solid-state laser (see page 58), the TruLaser 2030 fiber can cut highly reflective materials such as copper, even using nitrogen. All materials and sheet thicknesses can be processed with the same cutting unit. This saves set-up time, as there is no need to change the lens and cutting head. Along with other parameters, the focal position of the laser also adjusts automatically to the respective material. The cutting head’s collision protection and an automatic nozzle changer with space for eight nozzles reduce set-up times even further."
