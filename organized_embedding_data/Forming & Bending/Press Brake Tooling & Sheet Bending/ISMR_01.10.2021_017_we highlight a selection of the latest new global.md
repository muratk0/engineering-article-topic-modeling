# We highlight a selection of the latest new global folding technologies and innovations.

[TARİH: 01.10.2021 ISMR]

For sheet metal folding operations, and particularly for bending items using thin materials that are long, wide and with short flanges, folding machines (folders) are a good fit. This is because they allow the material to rest on the folder bed for easier handling. They are also inherently accurate as they 'fold' any sheet dimensional errors into the first bend, providing more accuracy in overall part sizes.

Rapid, efficient and precise, they service a range of industry sectors for many different products and project types. Folders are easily identified by their large support tables and swinging 'folding beam', which bends the flange while the workpiece remains flat on the support table. These machines are suitable for many scenarios, especially for large blanks that would otherwise be difficult to handle.

"During the folding sequence, the blank rests on the sheet support table. A gauging system positions the part to the bend line. The upper and lower beam clamp the blank. During the bending cycle, the folding beam moves up around a pivot point. On up-down acting machines, the folding beam moves upwards or downwards, depending upon the bending direction," explained folding specialist, RAS.

## Process benefits

There are many levels of folding machines, but all of them follow the same sequence for air-bending a desired angle: position -> clamp -> bend. The process is suitable for flexible applications because the tooling is not specific to the material thickness or angle required. "Universal" tool geometries allow for unlimited angle possibilities and are rated up to the maximum machine capacity and height. These tools should last for many years because of the swing-beam folding process. Flanges are bent to the correct angle with flat tooling that eliminates scratching or sliding on the workpiece. Not only does this equate to better quality parts, but it also means almost zero tool wear.

On systems equipped with segmented clamping tools, a new part can be set up by rearranging the tool segments on the tool rail. Machines without automatic tool changers still simplify the set-up process by advising the operator where to put tool stations. This common tooling allows for rapid changeover between parts and makes it easy for a single employee to program, operate and set up the machine for any compatible part.

The clamping tool of a folder enables the folding beam to form an acute angle bend for hemming applications. That acute angle is then positioned under the clamping tool foot to close the hem to a specific height. This height can be selected for parallel hems, teardrop hems or even open hems used in assembly.

On up-down acting machines, the folding beam moves upwards or downwards, depending upon the bending direction.

When the part needs a large radius to be formed, the same tooling can be used on a folding beam to smoothly make incremental bends until the necessary radius is achieved. While not a one-size-fits-all solution, folders offer strong flexibility for a wide variety of needs.

"CNC folding machines have pre-aligned upper beam and lower beam tools, minimising tool set-up time and allowing the fabricator to change from part to part very quickly. In today's pressures on small batch sizes and large volume, this is one of the main advantages of folding." RAS told ISMR.

Another advantage is part handling. Larger parts can be manipulated on the gauging, eliminating the need for multiple operators and increasing part accuracies. Folding machines clamp and bend the part when it is laying flat on the gauging table and the flange is being bent, which helps accuracy, so that the operator does not need to 'manage' the part during the forming process.

Larger parts that require two operators typically can be manipulated by one operator on a folder because the bulk of the part is resting on the table and the operator can rotate it by himself. The CNC control will prompt the operator on how to position the part. Precision tooling and CNC control technology will check for bending collisions and select bend sequences, shrinking the actual set-up time and providing more run time.

Folders and press brakes complement each other in sheet metal bending operations - they each have individual advantages and benefits in the press shop.

Established in 1948, is a Swiss equipment manufacturer that has become a market specialist in metal folding technology. The company has been putting significant resources behind two new developments in long folding that aim to change the way that sheet metal processors operate their double folders. The prime benefit for sheet metal processors, it told /SMR, will be a significant reduction in manual labour and part manipulation using Thalmann TD and TC double folders.

The first development is a sheet metal loading unit which feeds sheets into the double folder. With the integrated sheet metal feed-in device, sheet metal is fed automatically, rapidly and flexibly into the machine. This can be done as a single or multiple sheet feed, from a stack of sheets up to 100mm high, or with material from a slitting and cut-to-length line.

Depending upon the length of the machine, the loading device can load several sheets onto the machine e.g. 4 x 2m sheets on an 8m machine, and these can subsequently be folded simultaneously. This unit is an option that can be added onto Thalmann TD and TC machines.

The second development is a fully automated flipping device which turns the already folded part without any manual interference. The flipping operation is done by suction cups mounted on the upper folding beam in combination with the extendable folding table. Both developments aim to reduce the manual operation on a Thalmann TD and TC long folder, reducing cost and improving safety.

Its latest innovation is an automatic part unloading unit for the folder, where finished parts are removed from the machine and conveyed to the folded parts buffer unit. They are then ready for easy removal. A major advantage of this innovative automation solution is the option to have the machine producing not just in full automation but also in semi-automation mode.

"Within a few seconds, the entire unloading unit can be automatically folded away and 'parked' underneath the part buffer framework. The space between the double folder and the buffer unit is therefore freed again for the operator and orders can be processed without the use of full automation," the Swiss folding specialist explained.

The TD double folder range can be custom designed to optimise throughput, automate sheet loading and increase operational efficiencies. The maximum bending capacity is up to 2.5mm at 400N/mm². The standard throat depth is 1250mm and the standard length of the machines can vary from 3.2 metres to 12 metres. Different machine lengths can be custom produced upon request.
