# Diagnosing and Managing Press Brake Deflection with Shims

## Purpose and limits of shimming

Long bends can show a repeatable angle difference along their length, with a more open angle near the middle than near the ends. The article attributes this pattern to elastic deflection of the press brake structure under load: side frames are relatively rigid while the ram and bed may deflect across the working length. Material thickness variation, tooling condition, and springback can intensify or imitate the same pattern.

Shimming is a static compensation method. Thin, stable material placed beneath portions of the lower die can create a raised center profile intended to counter a repeatable center-to-end angle difference. Because the compensation is static, it may no longer be appropriate when material, thickness, bend length, tooling, load, or process conditions change.

Shimming should not be used to conceal damaged tooling, permanent machine deformation, unstable pressure, or an unexplained left-to-right imbalance. Follow the machine and tooling manuals, site lockout/tagout requirements, and approved procedures. Die-area access, tooling removal, and any machine adjustment must be performed only by trained, authorized personnel using required safety measures.

## Confirm the cause before compensating

Before deciding that deflection is the cause, inspect the process systematically:

- Compare bend angles at multiple locations along the part, including both ends and the center. A consistent center-to-end pattern is more relevant than a single reading.
- Check whether the pattern repeats across consecutive parts produced with the same material, tooling, bend length, and operating conditions.
- Inspect die contact surfaces and tooling for contamination, burrs, uneven wear, or damage. Debris or hardened residue beneath a die can introduce local height errors.
- Distinguish center-to-end deviation from left-to-right imbalance. A left-to-right angle difference may indicate ram level, alignment, or another machine condition rather than a deflection curve.
- Consider material variability. Differences in thickness, hardness, or grain direction can change springback and cannot be corrected reliably with a fixed shim arrangement.
- Investigate suspected permanent deformation or hydraulic instability through the machine manufacturer’s approved inspection and service process rather than attempting to compensate for it with shims.

## General principles for a shim arrangement

The article describes the objective as a gradual counter-deflection profile rather than an abrupt local lift. A broad-to-narrow layered arrangement centered on the location of greatest repeatable deviation can produce a smoother transition than placing one short, thick shim at the center. The exact placement, length, thickness, and number of layers must be determined by the approved tooling setup and measured trial results.

Stable metal shim stock is generally more suitable for repeated production than paper or other compressible materials. Paper may compress or absorb fluids and therefore can change thickness during use. Any shim material used must be clean, undamaged, compatible with the tooling arrangement, and approved for the application.

Avoid unsupported or excessive shim stacks. If a large correction appears necessary, stop and investigate the underlying condition. A substantial stack can concentrate loading, prevent the die from seating properly, and increase the risk of tooling or machine damage.

## Verification under controlled conditions

Evaluate a compensation setup using production-representative conditions. Test material should match the intended material type, thickness, grain orientation, bend length, tooling, and programmed cycle as closely as possible. Measurements should be taken at multiple positions along the bend, not only at the center.

If the center becomes tighter than the ends after compensation, the adjustment may be excessive. If angle variation changes from part to part, first examine changing material or process conditions rather than repeatedly altering a static shim setup.

Process conditions can affect repeatability. The article notes that machine warm-up state, hydraulic behavior, material consistency, and dwell settings may influence bend results. For meaningful comparison, keep relevant operating conditions consistent and use the established production cycle for verification.

## Documentation and maintenance

For recurring jobs, record the conditions under which a verified setup was obtained. Useful records include material and thickness, bend length, tooling identification, observed angle readings across the part, and the approved shim configuration. These records can support repeatable setup while making clear that the arrangement is conditional on similar process inputs.

Maintain clean die seats and tooling contact surfaces, and inspect tooling for wear. Hydraulic-system maintenance, machine alignment checks, and correction of structural or control issues should be carried out according to the machine manual and by qualified service personnel. Shimming is a limited process-compensation method, not a replacement for maintenance or repair.
