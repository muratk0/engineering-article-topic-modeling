## Scope and safety

Press brake tooling selection depends on the combined geometry and load capacity of the workpiece, punch, die, holders, machine, and forming method. A punch and die that appear compatible may still create interference, inadequate flange support, excessive local loading, or collisions during forming or part removal.

Before any cycle, confirm clearance, tool condition, seating, machine capacity, and applicable load ratings. Tool installation, alignment, machine setup, and physical clearance checks must follow the machine manual, approved site procedures, and qualified personnel requirements. Do not use forming pressure as a way to detect collisions.

## Define the part and material

Start from the drawing rather than from available tooling. Identify the finished angle, specified inside radius, shortest flange, bend length, tolerances, surface requirements, and nearby features such as return flanges, hems, offsets, hardware, or formed walls.

Verify the actual production material. Nominal gauge alone may not represent measured thickness, and material variation can affect penetration, radius, springback, and required force. Record measured thickness, material grade, condition, grain direction, coating, and protective-film condition. Test material should represent production conditions.

Higher-strength material may require more force and may exhibit greater springback than mild steel of the same thickness. Grain direction can also affect crack sensitivity, particularly where a tight radius is required. If material properties or conditions are uncertain, treat calculations as estimates and use a controlled test bend rather than applying unverified force.

## Select the forming method and die opening

Choose the bend method before approving tooling because contact conditions, springback, radius development, and force requirements differ.

- **Air bending** uses die shoulders as the primary support points, with ram depth controlling the angle. The V opening and material response strongly influence the resulting inside radius and springback.
- **Bottoming** creates fuller contact between the workpiece and tooling. It may reduce springback but does not eliminate variation caused by material, tool condition, fit, or machine deflection.
- **Coining** uses high pressure to plastically compress the bend zone. It can increase load, wear, marking, and local thinning, so it requires verified process justification and capacity.

Evaluate V-die openings against the required radius, shortest supported flange, surface condition, and calculated forming force. A wider opening generally lowers force but can produce a larger air-formed radius and require a longer supported flange. A narrower opening can improve short-flange support and reduce radius, but may increase force and local loading.

Common sizing rules may be useful as initial screening tools only. They do not approve a setup or replace calculations and test bends based on actual geometry and material.

## Select punch geometry and verify clearance

For air bending, the punch angle must provide enough clearance for penetration and springback compensation. The punch angle is not necessarily the finished bend angle. If the workpiece contacts punch flanks prematurely, the intended bending condition changes and increasing force can be unsafe.

Select a punch nose radius appropriate to the material and drawing requirements. A very sharp nose can concentrate strain and contribute to marking, creasing, or cracking, while an oversized nose can obstruct penetration or affect the formed radius.

Check the full forming and removal path. Consider the part during loading, initial contact, maximum penetration, springback, and removal. Verify clearance for previous bends, return flanges, walls, and machine components. A tooling profile that permits forming but prevents part removal is not a viable sequence.

Inspect physical tooling rather than relying only on markings. Wear, damaged noses, mixed-height segments, embedded scale, and mismatched sections can affect angle consistency and surface quality.

## Confirm compatibility and load limits

Verify that punches, dies, holders, clamps, and any approved interfaces are compatible and correctly seated. Do not use improvised shims or unapproved adapters. Confirm stack height against available daylight and usable stroke, including the space needed for installation, forming, opening, and safe part removal.

Calculate force using the actual material, measured thickness, bend length, V opening, and bend method. Compare the calculated requirement with the approved ratings of every item in the load path, including the machine, clamps, holders, punch, die, bed, ram, and frame. The lowest applicable rating governs the setup.

Concentrated loading is important for short bends and small die openings. A setup can be below an overall machine rating while exceeding a local rating for a tool segment or machine area. Off-center bending may also create unacceptable eccentric loading. Follow machine documentation for permitted bend positions, loaded lengths, and concentrated-force limits.

When ratings are missing, calculations disagree, the load approaches a limit, or tooling is damaged, modified, or unusually shaped, stop and obtain qualified technical review.

## Inspect, test, and monitor

Inspect tooling, holders, clamps, and seating surfaces for damage, contamination, uneven wear, and poor segment joints. Ensure tools are properly secured using approved procedures. Confirm the physical setup matches the active program, including tool selection, die opening, bend sequence, backgauge condition, and relevant machine settings.

Make a controlled test bend using representative material. After springback, measure angle, flange dimensions from specified datums, inside radius, straightness, twist, cracking, galling, dents, and shoulder marks. Check multiple locations along the bend when lengthwise consistency matters.

Use measured error patterns to guide diagnosis. Uniform angle variation may indicate springback or depth-related causes; consistent flange variation may point to gauging or datum issues; variation across the bend may indicate seating, deflection, alignment, clamping, or mismatched tooling. Change one variable at a time and document the result.

Release production only after the test part meets drawing requirements without exceeding any applicable limit. Record the proven material, tooling, machine, program, bend method, measurements, and inspection requirements. Recheck the process after material changes, adjustments, interruptions, unusual noise, tool movement, or changing force behavior. If inspection fails, stop the run, identify affected parts, and diagnose the cause before resuming.
