# GOING WITH THE GRAIN

[TARİH: 01.08.2014 ISMR]

## Crop processing machine production relies heavily on CAD/CAM software

A company manufacturing crop processing machines eradicated a production bottleneck by driving four press brakes with Radbend software.

When CAD/CAM engineer Graham Robbins joined Alvan Blanch two years ago, the press brakes were programmed manually. “Although the operators are highly skilled they still needed time when working on new components to look at engineering drawings, decide on the tooling and calculate the bend sequences. It was slow.”

One of his first tasks was to address that, and the Radbend module from Radan now controls four Amada press brakes – an HFE 8025, HFE 220-4, HFE M2 170-4 and HFE M2 100-3.

“We’ve put mini PCs on the press brake controllers and, with Radbend Viewer linked to the server, the operators can interrogate the 3D model, rotate the part and query anything at the machine. They also see a full animation of how the part should be fed in, along with its orientation and step-by-step of each fold,” he added.

With Radbend automatically calculating everything the operator needs to know, engineering drawings are only used as backup now, rather than being a necessity. All the information is readily available in 3D by pulling up the programme on the mini PCs.

## Flexibility is key

Alvan Blanch is a British manufacturing and project engineering company with a global outlook, specialising in the design, production and supply of quality machines and integrated systems for the primary and secondary processing of agricultural produce and waste materials. Typical products provide processes for grain drying; grain cleaning; fruit processing; vegetable oil processing; feed milling; rice processing; harvesting equipment; environmental and organic waste processes. Since introducing project nesting recently, it has reduced the scrap of its mild, galvanised and stainless steel by around 20 per cent.

Design Manager, Tim Baker, confirmed that its larger machines comprise around 300 components, with approximately 100 parts in each of the smaller threshers and roller mills. As well as Radbend, Alvan Blanch has a number of other Radan modules, including Radraft, Radprofile, Radpunch, Radnest, Radan 3D and e2i.

“Every sheet metal component goes through Radan and the accuracy of the files is extremely important. As customers are demanding ever-faster turnaround, we don’t have time to deal with parts that are wrong,” it told ISMR.

As Radpunch drives the company’s Amada EM 2510 NT punch press, and Radprofile programmes an Amada LC 4020 F1 NT 4m x 2m-bed 4kW laser cutter, Graham Robbins confirmed that it is extremely easy to switch a part from one machine to the other. “For instance, if other parts take priority on the laser, it’s simple to re-tool a component and produce a punch programme.”

After components are designed in Autodesk Inventor, Alvan Blanch uses DTM software from Radan to take the 3D model and package it into one file containing both the 3D and 2D information.
