# Selecting V-Die Openings for Air Bending

## V-die selection is a conditional process

A thickness-based V-die rule of thumb can provide an initial estimate, but it is not a universal setup instruction. Air-bending results depend on material grade and condition, actual thickness, tensile and elongation behavior, grain direction, required inside radius, bend angle, flange geometry, tooling geometry, and the press brake’s allowable load distribution.

A setup based only on nominal sheet thickness can produce an unintended radius, excessive springback, surface damage, cracking, unstable flange support, or loads beyond tooling or machine limits. Material documentation, the part drawing, tooling data, and the press brake manual should be reviewed before bending. Final setup and operation should be performed by qualified personnel using approved procedures.

## Air bending and formed inside radius

In air bending, the sheet is supported on the shoulders of the V-die while the punch drives it into the opening. The resulting inside radius is influenced strongly by the V-die opening and material behavior; it is not necessarily equal to the punch-tip radius.

The article presents approximate relationships between inside radius and V-opening for particular materials, such as a smaller fraction of the opening for mild steel and a larger fraction for 304 stainless steel. These relationships should be treated only as article-specific starting estimates, not as fixed design values. Actual formed radius can vary with alloy, temper, thickness variation, tooling condition, bend orientation, and the bend method.

For a specified inside radius, a useful planning approach is to work backward from the required part geometry: estimate a candidate V-opening using validated material and tooling data, then confirm the expected formed radius by trial parts or an approved process-validation method. Flat-pattern allowances and bend deductions should be based on verified production results rather than on an assumed universal radius multiplier.

## Material strength, ductility, and cracking risk

Materials of equal thickness can require different bending conditions. A material with greater resistance to deformation or reduced elongation may require a larger formed radius or different tooling than a more ductile material. Tight bends in resistant materials can increase the risk of outside-surface cracking and can increase springback.

The article describes wider V-openings as one way to reduce required bending load and spread deformation over a broader radius. This trade-off can make a bend more feasible for some materials, but it also changes the formed radius and may conflict with drawing requirements. A die opening selected to reduce load must therefore be checked against the required radius, flange geometry, tooling capability, and machine limits.

Do not assume that a general thickness multiplier applies to stainless steels, high-strength steels, heat-treated aluminum, wear-resistant plate, or other materials with different bending behavior. Confirm bendability and allowable radius using the material specification, approved shop data, tooling documentation, and the machine manufacturer’s load information.

## Flange support and die-opening limits

Short flanges may not remain adequately supported on the shoulders of a wide V-die. If the workpiece cannot bridge the die opening reliably, it can slip, deform unevenly, or become trapped in the die. Acute bends may require different support conditions because the punch travels deeper into the die opening.

The article gives percentage-based minimum-flange guidelines, but these values are presented without sufficient tooling, angle, and material qualifications to serve as universal limits. Instead, flange feasibility should be evaluated for the actual bend angle, die geometry, material, part width, and intended bending method. When a required radius calls for a wide opening but the flange is too short for stable support, the setup should be reviewed rather than forcing the bend through a narrower die.

## Load distribution and machine protection

Bending load is not governed only by the press brake’s nominal overall tonnage. A short bend can impose a high localized load per unit length even when the total calculated load appears low. Die opening, material strength, thickness, bend length, and bending method all affect required load.

Narrowing a V-die can increase bending load substantially. If that is done solely to accommodate a short flange, it may exceed punch, die, ram, bed, or concentrated-load limits. Exceeding localized limits can damage tooling or machine structure and can create an unsafe condition.

Before operating, qualified personnel should verify the proposed setup against:

- The approved material specification and actual material condition
- Required inside radius, bend angle, and flat-pattern requirements
- Tooling ratings and compatible punch/die geometry
- Minimum flange support for the specific bend
- Machine tonnage and concentrated-load limits from the machine manual
- Applicable workplace safety procedures

If radius, flange length, material bendability, and load capacity cannot all be satisfied within documented limits, the bend should be referred for engineering or process review rather than attempted by changing settings or forcing the material into the tooling.
