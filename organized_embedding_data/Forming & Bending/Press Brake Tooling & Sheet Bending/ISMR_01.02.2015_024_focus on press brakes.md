# FOCUS ON PRESS BRAKES

[TARİH: 01.02.2015 ISMR]

Its turnover of £10 million in 2013 was 25 per cent up on the previous year and over double the 2009 figure

A large part of AET’s output goes into the transport sector, including road, rail, coach and bus, as well as into yellow goods for construction, access platforms and security towers, conventional and renewable energy, agriculture, aviation and defence. Customers are to be found both domestically and in other countries, notably the USA and Canada.

## Investing in bending

It was in 1995 that David Larcombe, then applications engineer at Edwards Pearson UK, first met AET press brake team leader, Andrew Richards, and helped him to better understand, and maximise production output from, the new press brake that the company had purchased. Messrs Larcombe and Richards are now managing director and operations director of their respective companies.

AET now has four CO laser cutting machines from Bystronic, ranging in power from 4.4 to 6kW, capable of cutting up to 25mm thick mild steel, and with bed sizes of up to 4 metres x 2.5 metres. It also has four press brakes, with a fifth planned for later this year.

A high rate of investment was maintained at the end of the last decade with the help of regional growth funding, which was one of the reasons that AET was able to pull out of the recession in the UK so strongly. Over £1.5 million was spent between 2009 and 2013 acquiring a Bystar 4025 CO laser profiler, an Xpert 400 tonne/5-metre press brake and a ByAutonom 3015 CO machine, all from Bystronic. The same period saw an additional 17,500 sq. ft. of factory space added, bringing the total to 60,000 sq. ft. on the 2.5-acre site.

Further investment in 2013, including a new 4,000 sq. ft. factory to house the new shot-blast and wet paint facility and another Bystronic laser machine, added a further £1 million to the tally. This year has already seen the purchase of a Faro arm laser tracking inspection system. A new Bystronic 200-tonne/4-metre press brake will follow later in the year, along with the first of two Panasonic robotic welding cells. The second is due to arrive in March 2015.

Stephen Woollen, AET’s sales director, commented, “Investment in modern plant and equipment is key to maintaining profitability in most businesses, and particularly so in ours. We will continue to buy new machinery and already have plans for two more Bystronic laser profilers in 2015 and 2016, one of which will be a fibre laser machine for high productivity when processing thinner materials. Our green credentials will be further boosted by the use of solar panels next year to add to the LED lighting and a new heating system powered by recycled waste and energy from our machinery.”

Managing director, David Birch added, “At this level of growth there is a strong possibility that we will need to re-locate to a bigger site in the Sheffield area within the next three years to cope with continuing strong demand for our products and services. We are also looking at various options that may mean we have a presence in the Far East to service the coach and bus industry in that region, where we currently export a lot of fabrications.”
