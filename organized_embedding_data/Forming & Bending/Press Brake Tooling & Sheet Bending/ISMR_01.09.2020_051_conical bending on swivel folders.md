# Conical bending on swivel folders

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

THE MAKU, from Hans Schröder Maschinenbau, is (the company told ISMR), ‘the first swivel folding machine worldwide with a two-axis back gauge for automatic conical bending’. Job and repair shops, it added, can now produce sheet metal profiles which can be ‘expertly joined’ to each other.

Hans Schröder Maschinenbau presented this technical breakthrough, conical bending on a swivel folding machine, for the first time at the Dach+Holz exhibition this year in Stuttgart, Germany.

“Attica- or wall coverages, roof edge profiles and panels with a slope – are just a few examples of bending sheet metal where edges are not parallel. So far, this has only been possible using manual intervention or by processing the material on long folding machines with the right equipment. The production of connectable profiles, with just a slightly conical edge, requires a degree of tact and skill. Manual reworking was often needed when two profiles could not be precisely joined to each other,” explained the folding specialist.

“We are the first manufacturer to offer conical bending on a swivel folding machine,” added Bernhard Feierabend at Hans Schröder Maschinenbau. “The MAKU offers a working length of 2,020mm x 2.5mm, 2,520mm x 2.0mm and 3,220mm x 1.5mm and is a great introduction to automated thin sheet metal processing. The ‘U’ in its name stands for ‘universal’. In this application range, it replaces the MAKV with precision drives and an easy-to-operate nanoTouch software control integrated into the robust machine body.”

Options include a two-axis back gauge, equipped with the more powerful graphic control ‘POS 2000 Professional’, and a range of tools. Another option is a folding beam moved to the back (including cranked folding blades), previously only available for long folding machines.

“With the MAKU’s two-axis back gauge, job shops can now accept larger orders for long attica coverings and other sheet metal jobs. Where they earlier had difficulties with tapers of about 2mm, they are now able to achieve high precision levels (tenths of a millimetre) with the MAKU,” concluded the company.
