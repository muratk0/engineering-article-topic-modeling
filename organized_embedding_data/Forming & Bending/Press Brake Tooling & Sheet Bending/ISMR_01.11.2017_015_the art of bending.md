# THE ART OF BENDING

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“SafanDarley is considering using Augmented Reality (AR) for remote service applications”

SafanDarley presented a range of product innovations for sheet metal forming at this November's Blechexpo in Stuttgart, Germany. Its high-end generation press brake, the E-Brake Ultra series, boasted a new design and was equipped with CNC crowning. SafanDarley also showcased its H-Brake Hybrid series of press brakes with an innovative hybrid drive, a combination of hydraulics and electronics. Machines from its standard range, such as the M-Shear 310-6 with Return-to-Sender function, were also on display.

SafanDarley's new CEO, Maarten Coerman, and its area export manager, Tom Borgers, took ISMR through its latest bending innovations, at the exhibition, and highlighted some of the company's new developments.

##

"It has been a very good year for us this year. We have seen double-digit growth this year. We are currently working on some important developments for next year and hope to increase our business again in 2018. We are building up capacity at our facilities in Lochem (the E-Brake competence technology centre) and at Eijsden (the H-Brake/hybrid technology competence centre). We are investing in both facilities and see much more potential for the future," Maarten Coerman told ISMR.

SafanDarley's new CEO, Maarten Coerman, highlighted its latest bending innovations at this November's Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

"We see a very good market in central Europe and a rapidly growing market in North America (Canada and the USA). We expect this to continue next year, with more demand for high-end and automated equipment. Our focus has really been on Europe and the U.S. and I expect that to continue in the short- and mid-term."

Coerman remarked on the current positive state of the industry and the willingness of customers to invest in new technologies.

"The industry is changing its focus from mechanical to electronics and process. From our point of view, the logistical process around the machines, and the customer's own internal processes, are becoming more and more important. It is crucial to have accurate and fast equipment, but the processes around it are also important. Customers want to make the best use of their equipment, with no machine downtime, and all the set-up and preparation done during production for the next job. A radical idea that seems to be taking route is using one operator to work on two press brakes i.e. he works on one press brake while the other machine is re-tooling for the next job, he then works on the second machine while the first machine re-tools etc.," he told ISMR.

Left to right: Tom Borgers, SafanDarley's area export manager, and Maarten Coerman, CEO, SafanDarley

Tom Borgers pointed out the continuing need for rapid set-up times (i.e. using hydraulic clamping systems and programming for more efficient use of the machines). He projects good growth levels in the U.S. market as demand rises for these types of higher-end machines.

"We see that products, drawings and parts are becoming more and more complex. That correlates to a higher demand for higher tonnage press brakes to make more complex parts (where welding is not needed and the part can be made from one piece). We also see more tonnage on smaller machine lengths (for example, 100 tons on 2m is now possible on the hybrid press brake and we are selling more of these to customers now)," Borgers explained.

SafanDarley recently sold a 500-ton H-Brake to a customer in the U.S. and has just received an order for an 800-ton H-Brake for the North American market.

The H-Brake Hybrid can be equipped with the E-Bend L Blue angle measurement system. The E-Bend L Blue uses two blue laser sensors, one on each side of the table. The new blue laser sensors offer an huge energy savings with very low noise levels and uses only a fraction of the usual amount of oil. The hybrid drive is only operational when actively bending (the pump stops in the intervening periods).

Above: The E-Brake Ultra press brake

## An eye on bending

SafanDarley also showcased its H-Brake Hybrid series of press brakes, introduced in 2016. These H-Brakes are equipped with an innovative hybrid drive; a combination of hydraulics and electronics. The system delivers important benefit compared to the usual red. Red laser light has an almost identical wavelength to natural light, which means that the measurement is often affected by local conditions. The blue light spectrum of the E-Bend L Blue is much easier to distinguish from "natural" light, so reflections and sunlight do not interfere with the measurement.

At Blechexpo, SafanDarley presented the high-end generation press brake, the E-Brake Ultra series. The Ultra series boasted a new design and was equipped with CNC crowning. It has, as standard, a dual-screen monitor to help create a paperless environment. Options for the E-Brake Ultra include a 3D back gauge and the E-Bend L Blue laser angle measurement system.

"Another option for the E-Brake Ultra series is EC40 Smart software. In addition to EC20 functionality, the EC40 Smart has the ability to read-in 3D drawings on a mobile device, to unfold and to carry out a bending simulation. Through 'dock', the system can be synchronised with the machine control allowing data and the programme to be accessed in the EC20 and produced on the machine. This enables programming in 3D at the machine or at any other location," Tom Borgers, SafanDarley's export area manager, told ISMR.

"At Blechexpo, we are also presenting an E-Brake 35T with an extremely large Q-dimension. As standard, the E-Brake 35T is equipped with a Q-size of 590mm; the machine we are showing at Blechexpo has Q-size of 1090 mm. The enlarged Q size gives the ability to bend products with high upright sides," added Maarten Coerman.

The company's AutoPOL simulation software, for offline programming of press brakes, was also on display. A new generation, called AutoPOL GT, has just been released. The software has been developed for use with modern touch-screen equipment, which makes AutoPOL easy to program on a tablet. This makes the offline simulation software suitable for the latest SafanDarley EC40 Smart control. In addition to multiple user interface improvements, there are also many enhancements to its technical functionality.

"Our new generation of offline software is one of the highlights on our booth. Offline support is becoming more and more important with connected Industry 4.0," commented Tom Borgers, Safan Darley's area export manager.

Machines from SafanDarley's standard range, such as the M-Shear 310-6 with return to sender functionality, were also on display in Stuttgart.

## Industry 4.0 and automation

"Industry 4.0 is a key trend that is becoming more important," added Borgers. "Our customers are working with smaller production batch sizes now, so it is even more important to have smaller batches with quicker set-up times on the press brakes.

"One of our big USPs is having a Windowsbased control that can be plugged into any network belonging to our customers. Because we develop our own software, we can connect to other software programs. An important element of Industry 4.0 is developing your own hardware/software to connect to the client's ecosystem. That is a great benefit and makes us really strong in this area for press brakes."

SafanDarley's own software development with FCC, the AutoPol project, can now connect to other systems. Software is an important step in automation and standardisation for SafanDarley's customers and an important link in the overall eco-system. Coerman points out however that, although demands for automation are rising, different levels of automation are often required.

"We work with robotic manufacturers to provide automation on our press brakes and we are seeing more demands for automation. However, some of our customers might not need the full automation package (i.e. the tooling and product). We have, for example, just installed a product in the Netherlands where the E-Brake automatically changes just the tooling – it is not handling the product itself (the operator does that) – so the system is a cross between a fully manual and completely automated cell. We see different levels of demand for different levels of automation in the market," he explained.

## Augmented Reality – an eye on the future

At EuroBLECH 2016, SafanDarley demonstrated its SafanDarley Eye system in combination with the Microsoft Hololens. By using Augmented Reality (AR), productivity on the press brake can be increased. The SafanDarley Eye shows additional information from the pressbrake to the operator, via the Hololens, and fits within its Industry 4.0 digital remit.

"We are also considering using the Augmented Reality (AR) concept for service. For remote service, AR functionality can be very useful as we do not need to send out technicians to different countries, which can be logistically difficult. AR also has applications in daily production," explained Maarten Coerman.

"We are currently participating in a SmartHub academy and have just signed an agreement with a local university (UT Twente) so that students can bring new and innovative ideas to the table. We have defined several concrete projects that they will be working on…"

SafanDarley is currently working on new concepts to be presented at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hannover in 2018. The Group has undergone a recent management change, with its new CEO, new R&D manager and a new service manager. Coerman is currently putting together the new management team as he tries to stay at the forefront of new technology developments.

"We are really focused on press brake and shear technologies and we specialise in these areas. This will continue to be our strategic focus for the mid-term future," he told ISMR.
