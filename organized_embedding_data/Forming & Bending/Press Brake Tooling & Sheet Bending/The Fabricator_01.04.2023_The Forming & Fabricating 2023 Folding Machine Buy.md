Long embraced by the architectural fabrication companies, folding technology has made inroads into the precision sheet metal market over the last decade. The attraction was the folding machine’s ability to process large, unwieldy parts, the kind that would require two or three people to process on a press brake.

How can this happen? On a folding machine, the blank rests on the sheet support table. The upper and lower beam clamp the blank. During the bending cycle, the folding beam moves up around a pivot point to create the bend. (Some folding machines are up-downacting machines. On these, the folding beam can move upward or downward.)

These awkward-to-handle parts are still a good reason to purchase a folding machine, but the motivations are now much more than that. Metal fabricating companies are finding that folding machines are proving to be the right fit for today’s manufacturing realities.
