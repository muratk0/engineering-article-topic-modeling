# TUBE IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.03.2012 ISMR]

ISMR chronicles the latest tube processing developments

ISMR SAYS: “We highlight new tube forming and bending technologies”

A tube can be bent in multiple directions and angles. Common simple bends consist of forming elbows and U-bends. More complex geometries include multiple two-dimensional (2D) bends and threedimensional (3D) bends.

ISMR profiles some of the latest tube bending technology developments here....

## An eye on precision

Bending pre-finished tubes accurately (i.e. tubes with slots, holes etc.) is a growing requirement. BLM has released a system to identify any machining on the tube by detecting any holes, slots or seams (or any other type of machining) so that the pre-finished pieces are directed correctly. It has also added another machine to its range of ELECT series tube benders. The new ELECT 52 is an all-electric multi-radius tube bending machine (9 axes) for tubes of up to 50mm diameter.

At TUBE 2012 in Düsseldorf, TRACTO-TECHNIK will present machinery, measuring technology and software solutions around tubes and pipes.

Rooted in the Swedish industrial tradition, Herber Engineering has been a manufacturer and supplier of machines used for the cold-forming of tubes since 1945. Its machines handle tubes ranging in size from 4mm to 160mm in diameter. Herber will exhibit its latest products at Tube Düsseldorf from 26-30 March 2012, with Zopf GmbH.

German mechanical engineering company TRACTO-TECHNIK (TT) develops and manufactures machines for trench-less pipeline installation and renewal as well as machines, measuring techniques and software solutions for tube fabrication. Established in 1962, the company presently has about 500 employees.

At TUBE 2012 in Düsseldorf, TT will not only present innovative technology for bending and processing tubes but also high-precision measuring systems for quality control and reverse engineering as well as intelligent software solutions for tube shop automation, workflow optimisation and lifecycle management for piping (Hall 5, Booth E 21).

As a highlight, it will show a complete tube manufacturing cell with separation from the tube magazine, robot in-feed and integrated bending piece measurement. The centrepiece of the system is the servo-electrically driven, multi-radii CNC bending machine TUBOTRON 25 MR for tubes from OD 4 to 25mm (1’’) with no hydraulics. The bending machine’s exposed compact bending head enables the production of complex tube shapes at bending speeds of up to 170°/sec.

TT will also present software innovations at TUBE 2012. Its modular workflow management software, PIPEFAB, has completely been re-engineered and is now available with various advanced functions and new features. By automating the workflow, the software package significantly improves the manufacturing process in the pipe shop and supports the complete lifecycle of pipes.

The TT product range comprises CNC and semi-automatic tube bending machines, assembly machines for hydraulic tubing, tube end forming machines, tube measuring systems as well as system and software solutions for tube and pipe processing.

The Laser Cutting Company of Sheffield, a subcontractor specialising largely in tubular fabrication, has taken delivery of the UK’s first TruLaser Tube 7000 tube laser cutting machine. Although this is its fourth tube laser on-site, it is the first from TRUMPF.

“The arrival of the TruLaser Tube 7000 not only brings us up to date, it nearly doubles capacity and fortifies our strategy of acquiring the most efficient machinery available,” says the company’s Chairman, Jon Day. “There are three factors that secure business in this market: a competitive price, the right quality and on-time delivery. The fact that the machine can also accommodate open and square section beams and channels also boosts our flexibility.”

The new TruLaser Tube 7000 brings several benefits to the rapidly growing, £3 million turnover business. For example, the machine cuts tubes and profiles with large diameters and wall thicknesses without sacrificing productivity. It can process tubes and profiles up to a diameter of 200mm (optionally 250mm), while wall thicknesses of up to 8mm in mild steel can also be accommodated.

All-electric tube bending machine specialist Unison will be demonstrating its lasercontrolled spring-back correction system at the MACH 2012 exhibition. Now available as a build-to-order option on any of the company’s bending machines for tubes from 50mm diameter upwards, the system will be shown on a 76mm model to highlight its benefits for manufacturers producing relatively large diameter tubular parts.

Unison will also be highlighting its new quick-release tooling and barcode identification system, which enables machine operators to handle complete tool change-overs within 2 to 5 minutes, with full verification of the process. This tooling system is designed for manufacturers seeking to accelerate small batch size production of tubular metal parts.

The company’s extensive Breeze range of tube bending machines accommodates all types of tubing materials, from mild steel to exotic alloys. Recent extensions to the range include a rotating head machine that can perform right- and left-hand bending in one continuous cycle, and a machine that features a fully integrated cutter to facilitate automated single-step manufacturing of volume parts. ■
