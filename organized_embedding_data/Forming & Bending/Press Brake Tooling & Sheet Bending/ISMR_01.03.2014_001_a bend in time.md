## GLENROTHES-BASED FIFE FABRICATIONS

FIFE FABRICATIONS, one of the UK’s largest and most advanced manufacturers of precision sheet metalwork, recently installed its first SafanDarley technology in the shape of four E-Brakes (electronic press brakes). The machines have replaced five conventional hydraulic models. Installed during the Christmas shutdown at the end of 2013, the E-Brakes are expected to generate a 30% increase in productivity over the previous machines, and a minimum 50% saving in energy consumption.

Fife Fabrications installed one 35-tonne (1250mm bed) E-Brake, two 50-tonne (2050mm bed models) and a single 100-tonne (3100mm bed) machine. The new partnership sees Fife Fabrications become the first company in Scotland to invest in this eco-friendly technology. The E-Brakes have replaced all five of the company’s existing hydraulic machines, and are now fully operational across a two-shift pattern.

The absence of hydraulics makes each SafanDarley E-Brake press brake as environmentally friendly as possible. The machine only consumes energy when the upper beam is in motion. The servo-electronic drive, based on the pulley principle, ensures a very even distribution of forces so that, in most cases, no crowning table is needed.

“It’s still early days but we are anticipating a 30% increase in throughput as a result of our investment, along with a minimum saving in energy consumption of 50%,” said Archie Smith, managing director of Fife Fabrications.

The E-Brakes are being employed to process work of between 0.8 and 6.0mm thick, although typical everyday work falls in the range of 1.2 to 2.0mm. Around 70% of jobs are steel, with the remainder comprising materials that include aluminium and zintec. All four E-Brakes are fully integrated with the company’s ERP system via a network card and second screen, a move that is becoming increasingly popular among manufacturers seeking a paper-less environment and rapid job processing. Press brake operators at Fife Fabrications book on and off jobs using barcode scanners at the machines.

Currently, 70% of the company’s sales are outside Scotland, serving markets that not only include the wider UK, but also Germany, Malaysia, Hungary and the ASEAN countries. While Mr Smith admits the company was once beholden to the telecommunications industry, Fife Fabrications has successfully spread its business risk in recent years by diversifying into a host of new sectors. These include medical, bio-science, military, audio-visual, security, renewables and oil and gas. ■

## PRIMA POWER OPENS DOORS

ON 2-3 APRIL 2014, Prima Power will open its doors in Kauhava, Finland for its forthcoming Technology Days. The company intends to showcase a wide range of fabrication technology in action. This event will also be the world premiere of a new integrated punching-shearing cell for sheet sizes of up to 2,500mm x 0.250mm. Most products on display include new improved features introduced for the first time. Prima Power manufactures a range of punch, laser, combination, shearing and bending solutions.

“During the Technology Days, the focus will be on demonstrations – no lectures or presentations are scheduled. However, product managers and other professionals will be glad to give more information on the machines, demonstrations, software and so on,” the company told ISMR. ■
