# FOCUS ON BENDING

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

The TruBend 3000 Series press brakes from TRUMPF enables, says the company, costeffective bending even with low throughput.

“High productivity through easy set-up, fast axis speeds and rapid tool change, with automatic crowning for consistent angles, help with this. High application flexibility and simple operation makes these press brakes suitable for those introducing automated bending into their processes for the first time or established users wishing to expand their capability,” added TRUMPF.

The latest addition to the range is the TruBend 3066 with a press force of 600kN and 2m bending length. It is supplied as standard with a two-axis back gauge but can also be specified with a four or five-axis back gauge. This makes it possible to position sheet metal securely, even where complex part geometries are involved.

The operating concept is simple and intuitive. The machine’s operator can sketch components and program them graphically at a large display with modern multi-touch control. The exact bending parameters are calculated by the control circuits, based on TRUMPF technology data. 3D visualisation further simplifies the work and examines the design for potential collisions. If suitable bending programs already exist, they can be imported either via a USB interface or a network connection.

The fastest option for set-up is automatic hydraulic clamping. In addition, all the clamping systems automatically centre the tools which can be inserted vertically. Both these features reduce clamping times.

A variety of features ensure the press brakes provide a safe and ergonomically correct workplace. Among them is the optoelectronic safety system, BendGuard. It uses a light field to monitor the area beneath the upper tool. If there is an interruption, a hand breaking the light field for example, the machine automatically stops. The operator can therefore make full use of the machine’s full speed, without risk.

“As well as being compact, the TruBend 3000 Series press brakes consume less energy thanks to their advanced drive technology. This is complemented by automatic shutdown of the hydraulics during extended downtimes. The machines are also quick to install as they are delivered completely assembled,” added TRUMPF.

TRUMPF UK recently received its biggest ever order for CNC press brakes. Nine TruBend Series 5000 press brakes were ordered by KMF, a leading UK sheet metal subcontractor based at Newcastle-under-Lyme in Staffordshire. KMF has ordered seven 2.5- metre bed TruBend models (85 tonnes’ capacity) and two 3-metre bed models (130 tonnes’ capacity). All nine machines feature an extended open height for bending components such as deep boxes, and all offer tooling compatibility. One machine comes with TRUMPF’s bending aid, which reduces a twoman operation to a one-man function when handling large panels.

Dutch tooling specialist, Wila B.V., has been specialising in tool holders (clamping and crowning), tooling and accessories for more than 80 years. With continuous innovations, it has been able to reduce the adjustment time of the press brake and to further increase accuracy. Three of WILA's latest innovations – E2M® (Easy to Move), Smart Tool Locator® and V-Lock – were showcased at its booth at EuroBLECH 2014 in Hanover.

Last year, Wilson Tool International, the worlds’ largest independent manufacturer of tooling systems for the metal fabrication industry, celebrated the 20th anniversary of its press brake tooling division and the Wilson Wheel® family of punch press tooling.

Today, the company offers a comprehensive selection of press brake tooling solutions. From standard tooling solutions for European style, American Precision®, WILA/TRUMPF Style Precision™ and Exacta conventional press brake tooling to special customised tooling for unique bending applications, and an array of clamping solutions designed to speed set-ups, Wilson Tool provides a broad selection of tooling options to press brake fabricators around the world.

“We would also like to work more on special tooling for press brakes with our customers,” it told ISMR. “There is now more automation around for press brakes and press brake software is becoming more sophisticated and simpler to use.” ■
