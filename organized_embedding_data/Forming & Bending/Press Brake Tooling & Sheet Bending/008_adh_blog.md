## Define the Required Geometry and Inspection Method

A specified radius must be identified before tooling is selected. Confirm whether the drawing refers to the finished inside radius, outside radius, centerline radius, or a reference/tooling dimension. These values are not interchangeable, and the relationship between inside and outside radius depends on material thickness and applicable process effects.

The acceptance requirement should also state how the part will be inspected. Define the permitted radius range, bend-angle tolerance, datums and tangent locations, measurement positions, and whether local flattening, tool marks, or profile variation are acceptable. A radius gauge may provide a quick comparison, but it may not evaluate the full contour. Depending on the tolerance, a contour template, optical measurement, or coordinate measurement system may be more appropriate.

Angle, radius, flange projection, and nearby feature location are related. A part can meet an angle requirement while placing tangent points or adjacent holes incorrectly. If the qualified formed radius differs from the radius assumed in the flat pattern, bend allowance or bend deduction may need review through the approved engineering process.

## Account for Material and Production Condition

Material variation affects springback, cracking risk, and the unloaded bend profile. The setup and qualification record should identify the material grade, condition, actual thickness range, grain direction, coating or film, surface condition, and relevant secondary operations.

The required radius should be evaluated in the production condition specified by the drawing. A bare part measured immediately after bending may not match the same part after welding, heat exposure, painting, plating, or subsequent forming.

## Select a Forming Method Based on the Requirement

In air bending, the workpiece contacts the punch and the two die shoulders rather than fully conforming to the die. The V-die opening and material behavior largely influence the natural inside radius, while ram penetration primarily changes bend angle. Springback affects both the unloaded angle and profile.

The article presents an approximate air-bending starting relationship for common mild steel: the natural inside radius may be in the range of roughly 16% to 20% of the V-die opening. This is an article-specific planning estimate, not a universal rule. Actual results depend on material strength, thickness, grain direction, punch contact, tool geometry, and springback. Tooling manufacturer data, qualified shop data, and controlled trials should govern the final selection.

A punch nose radius is not automatically the finished part radius in air bending. The nose should be compatible with the intended profile and should not create excessive localized strain, creasing, surface damage, or cracking. Tool ratings, included angles, clearance, and compatibility must be verified using the machine and tooling documentation.

Dedicated radius tooling, bottom bending, coining, or bump forming may be considered when air bending cannot meet the required profile, tolerance, surface condition, or part geometry. These methods have different contact conditions and loading requirements. Air-bending load assumptions must not be used for bottoming or coining.

## Verify Support, Clearance, and Load Before Forming

A large die opening may help produce a broader air-bent radius but can require greater flange support and may create clearance conflicts with short flanges, narrow webs, nearby features, holders, the frame, or the backgauge. Check that the part remains properly supported on the die shoulders and that all tool and machine clearances are adequate.

Forming load depends on material strength, thickness, bend length, die opening, and forming method. Use qualified machine and tooling documentation or an approved calculation method that matches the actual process. Compare the result with machine capacity at the working position, punch and die ratings, concentrated-load limits, clamping requirements, alignment, and deflection or crowning capability.

Do not attempt to increase penetration until an air-bent part seats in the die as an informal correction. This can change the forming method toward bottoming, substantially alter loading, and exceed verified limits. Setup, tool installation, machine programming, and any adjustments must follow the machine manual and be performed by qualified personnel.

## Qualify with Representative Test Pieces

Use test coupons from the production material lot where practical. They should represent the finished part's thickness, condition, grain direction, surface condition, coating or film, edge preparation, and bend orientation. Use fresh coupons for meaningful trials, since prior bending changes the material condition.

Measure the unloaded angle and inspect the complete radius profile separately. Change only one process variable between trials and document the result. A short coupon may establish local response but does not demonstrate full-length consistency. For long bends, qualify a representative production-length part and inspect multiple positions across the bend.

If the radius is incorrect while angle is acceptable, evaluate the radius-producing geometry rather than treating additional penetration as the primary correction. If variation occurs across the length, investigate material variation, tool seating, alignment, deflection, crowning, and measurement repeatability. Stop production if outside-surface cracking is observed and assess material condition, grain direction, edge quality, and the suitability of the selected radius.

## Document the Controlled Process

A passing sample demonstrates feasibility, not repeatability. Record the qualified material condition, tooling, forming method, machine, approved program revision, inspection method, and relevant process settings in accordance with site procedures. Define inspection frequency and reaction rules for radius, angle, flange dimensions, cracking, and surface condition.

Requalification is appropriate after changes to material lot, thickness, temper, grain orientation, tooling, machine, bend length, or program revision. If expected process variation consumes the full permitted radius tolerance, a different qualified forming process or tighter control of material and tooling may be necessary.
