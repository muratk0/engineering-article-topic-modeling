# SafanDarley EBend L2

[TARİH: 01.11.2014 ISMR]

SafanDarley recently acquired a CAM software company and introduced a new dual drive bending concept to EuroBLECH

One of the highlights of Dutch press brake specialist, SafanDarley, at its EuroBLECH booth was the presentation of its new generation of servo-electric press brakes. Their well-known drive-based pulley principle has evolved to a new machine concept offering further advantages than its E-brake concept. The new press brake boasts a C-frame and narrower press beam, with higher pressure load per metre. Other benefits of this new principle are rapid and direct positioning with higher acceleration and accuracy.

“Our latest technology is a new dual drive system for our E-brake bending concept. Normally, we use a single belt drive in our E-brakes. This new dual drive system uses more motors in the system which gives us four, rather than two motors, and the drive system is split into two parts. Two motors are dedicated to high speed operations while the other two motors are dedicated to low speed operation. This gives the machine very high dynamics and much greater speed, so energy is greatly reduced and it is a very quiet machine. This new patented technology is a big step forward for us,” commented Wim van Kranen of SafanDarley.

“The belt drive is in the upper beam, so you can’t see it, and the system has a lot of flexibility around the upper beam – lots of freedom and flexibility with bending parts. This is our concept machine – it is still under development and will be ready by the second half of next year. The feedback, however, from customers at this exhibition on the machine has been great.”

## Precision and speed

Another SafanDarley highlight at EuroBLECH was its new, E-Bend L laser angle measurement system to measure precise bends. It also showcased its augmented reality concept, the SafanDarley Eye. The software is now ready and can be used for tool change on the press brake, for example, to give additional machine tool information (such as location data for the positioning of the products) to the operator through the glasses that he/she wears when using the machine. It is a very innovative development.

“It is getting much harder to find skilled operators and also to attract younger generations to the industry. This type of technology appeals to younger machine tool operators and helps to demonstrate how high-tech the sheet metal industry really is now,” added van Kranen.

“We have had a great year with lots of orders and expanded our market share in western Europe and the US. We are optimistic for next year – there are possibilities for us to expand. We have decided to set up our North American headquarters next year. Certain areas in the world are still under investigation,” explained van Kranen.

“Offline software is becoming more important in the sheet metal industry – we acquired Autopol that unfolds 3D models and automatically generates the bending programme for the press brake. We have worked closely with them in the past and believe that this type of software is growing in importance and will be a good complement to our product portfolio. We already focus on our own HMI Windows-based software for press brake controls – it is a continuous innovation process. Industry 4.0 means some exciting challenges for us. We want to ensure that our systems are developing in line with these requirements.”

Around 70% of SafanDarley's turnover is in Europe although the US market is becoming more significant for the company. Wim van Kranen told ISMR that he sees a greater awareness of green technologies and machine safety in the US market.

“People often think that safety features on machine tools will slow down production. In fact, on our machines, safety features increase production by 30% and still make the machine safe. Combined with energy savings on the machines, this makes the technology attractive. For example, we use our lightguard system to signal to the machine that a process has been safely completed and the next process can start. The operator doesn’t need to cue the machine to start the next process step. While the operator is turning the sheet, the machine is already preparing itself for the next step. It is still safe because, if the operator makes a mistake, the machine will stop automatically,” he explained.

SafanDarley has made some recent significant investments in both its factories in the Netherlands. This year, it invested over 2 million euros into the SafanDarley factory in Eijsden to make it more efficient for larger equipment (it now manufactures press brakes of up to 1250 tonnes). This factory makes all its hydraulic press brakes, larger hydraulic shears and special equipment such as automated shears for cutting nickel or automated bending cells.

“We are growing our turnover again this year. We have offices in the UK, Germany and Taiwan and will open a new office in the USA. We also export to 50 countries working through distributors,” he concluded.
