# Press Brake Capability Verification Through Controlled Bending Tests

## Purpose of Verification

Rated tonnage, machine weight, drive type, and general appearance are useful screening factors, but they do not by themselves demonstrate finished-part consistency. Press-brake capability is better evaluated through controlled bending trials that represent the intended process.

A useful verification focuses on whether the machine can repeatedly position the ram and backgauge, maintain alignment across the working length, manage deflection compensation, and produce parts within defined geometry requirements. A single acceptable bend may demonstrate that a setup can work; repeated results at multiple bed locations and operating conditions provide stronger evidence of process stability.

## Define Requirements Before Testing

Establish the required part characteristics before beginning a test series. These may include:

- Bend angle
- Flange position or flange length
- Bend-line straightness
- Inside radius, where relevant
- Twist, bow, surface condition, and other finished-part requirements
- Part-to-part variation

Angle alone is not sufficient. A part can reach the intended angle while having incorrect flange geometry, a bowed bend line, or unstable results from cycle to cycle.

Separate absolute deviation from repeatability. A stable offset may be addressable through approved setup or calibration procedures, while unstable variation is more difficult to manage in production. Required limits should be derived from the part drawing, downstream assembly needs, and the applicable machine, tooling, and process requirements.

The test should use representative material grades, thicknesses, bend lengths, and bend locations. Long bends can reveal the effectiveness of deflection compensation. Short bends can reveal localized control behavior. Off-center bends can challenge synchronization and asymmetric-load handling, but must remain within the machine manufacturer's permitted loading limits.

## Control Test Variables

Tooling is part of the forming system. Record the punch profile, die opening, tool length, segment arrangement, clamping method, and tool condition. Clean and inspect tooling and mounting surfaces before testing, since burrs, contamination, damaged segments, or poor seating can create errors that resemble machine misalignment.

For each coupon, document material specification, measured thickness, blank dimensions, grain direction, and intended bend line. Use identified, consistent material rather than mixed scrap. During a controlled series, keep the program and setup variables fixed unless a change is specifically being investigated. Relevant variables include ram-depth command, backgauge target, speed, dwell, crowning setting, and angle correction.

Measurements should use a defined datum, a suitable instrument, known measurement positions, and a consistent recording method. Retain raw values rather than only pass/fail labels. Trends toward a tolerance limit can reveal drift that averages may conceal.

## Inspect Motion, Geometry, and Tooling

Before forming parts, inspect accessible contact surfaces, guides, clamping interfaces, backgauge components, hoses, fittings, and drive-related components for damage, looseness, leakage, abnormal wear, vibration, or unusual noise. Verify that planned gauge movements, tool changes, and ram motion have adequate clearance.

Measure ram-to-bed relationship at multiple locations using approved datums and calibrated equipment. The article cites approximately 0.02 mm per meter only as an initial reference; it is not a universal limit. Required values depend on machine design, bed length, temperature, measurement method, and finished-part tolerances.

For controlled ram axes and backgauge axes, compare commanded positions, displayed feedback, and independent physical measurements where this can be performed safely. Repeated approaches from different directions may expose backlash, compliance, or inconsistent return behavior. The article also mentions a provisional backgauge target near ±0.05 mm, but states that the actual requirement must be determined by the flange tolerance and applicable acceptance criteria.

Do not perform controller adjustments, calibration changes, disassembly, or hydraulic or electrical intervention as part of general testing. Follow the machine manual and use qualified personnel for diagnostic or corrective work.

## Test Under Representative Load

Begin with controlled, centered bends using documented tooling and material. After any agreed setup adjustment, maintain a fixed test configuration and inspect every part consistently. Record angle, flange dimensions, relevant radius, twist, bow, surface marking, and other required characteristics.

Continue the series through cold and warmed operating conditions where relevant. Record cycle sequence, alarms, commanded positions, correction values, and observed changes. If warm-up is required for normal operation, it should be included in the defined process conditions.

Test identical coupons at left, center, and right bed locations. If an error follows a moved tool segment, the tooling may be responsible. If it remains at the same bed location after cleaning and reseating, further investigation may be needed into bed or ram geometry, crowning response, leveling, or synchronization.

For long bends, measure both ends and the center. An acceptable average can conceal opposing angle errors or bend-line curvature. For backgauge evaluation, verify finished flange geometry as well as gauge movement, because angle and radius changes can affect apparent flange dimensions.

## Diagnose Variation Systematically

A failed bend is a symptom rather than a complete diagnosis. Preserve the part record, verify the measurement method, clean and reseat tooling, and change only one variable at a time.

Variation that follows individual coupons may indicate material or springback differences. Variation associated with cycle sequence or temperature may warrant investigation of positioning stability, feedback, lubrication, or hydraulic behavior by qualified personnel. Stable angle with changing flange dimensions directs attention toward backgauge behavior or measurement datums; changes in both angle and flange dimensions may also involve material seating, bend deduction, forming depth, or control response.

Foundation support, leveling, anchoring, ambient conditions, oil temperature, and electrical supply can affect machine behavior. When conditions are corrected, repeat sufficient portions of the original controlled test to determine whether the failure pattern has been removed.

## Maintain Traceable Records

Assign each coupon a unique identifier linked to material, tooling, bed location, program revision, crowning setting, relevant operating conditions, observations, and inspection results. Keep nonconforming results in the dataset. Evaluate individual results, spread, and trends rather than relying only on average values.

A documented protocol and traceable test record support consistent commissioning, troubleshooting, and ongoing process verification.
