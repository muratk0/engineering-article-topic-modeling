# AN EYE ON ACCURACY

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new bending innovations on the global market

B produces a V-shape, U-shape or channel shape along a straight axis in sheet metal. Commonly used equipment include press brakes and other specialised machine presses. Typical products that are made like this are boxes such as electrical enclosures and rectangular ductwork.

In folding, clamping beams hold the longer side of the sheet. The beam rises and folds the sheet around a bend profile. The bend beam can move the sheet up or down, enabling the fabrication of parts with positive and negative bend angles. The resulting bend angle is influenced by the folding angle of the beam, tool geometry and material properties. Large sheets can be handled in this process, making the operation easily automated. There is little risk of surface damage to the sheet.

Many of the latest bending innovations are centred upon more user-friendly and simpler processes, more sophisticated sensing and tool-changing technologies, lower power consumption and faster, accurate bending. In this article, we highlight a selection of some of the latest bending developments (panel benders, press brakes, folders and automated bending techniques) on the global market.

## The latest innovations

Spanish manufacturer Adira is one of the leading manufacturers and global suppliers of sheet metal working machinery, specialised in the production of laser cutting machines, hydraulic press brakes, shears, robotised bending cells and automatic sheet metal transforming systems. It has a large portfolio of press brakes, targeting all common bending needs as well as specific custom-tailored bending projects. It can also supply robotised bending cells and fully customised solutions to suit bending needs.

The integration of manufacturing technologies is an industry trend being driven by a market desire for greater efficiency. With this in mind, the ability to combine multiple processes with one single system solution is a concept that Japanese manufacturer Amada (www.amada.co.uk) is exploiting to the full with its latest automated bending systems.

At the last EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, Amada demonstrated three fully automated bending cells. The first of these, designed for small component bending, was the Amada EG6013AR, a system that features a 1300mm, 600kN servo-electric press brake mounted by a front-traversing six-axis robot. The compact cell layout is optimised for system simplification and space reduction, while the dedicated robot features a new gripper design for handling small and complex parts. Both work-piece manipulation and tool changing are facilitated by the same robot. Components measuring up to 300 x 300mm and weighing up to 1.6kg can be accommodated.

Amada's HFE 3i range of press brakes

Other benefits included a new servo drive system for higher performance and lower power consumption, and the use of Amada’s new AMNC3i control. Completed work-pieces are stacked by the robot or, if this is not possible, placed in boxes or on a synchronised conveyor belt.

Another automated bending cell offered by Amada, this time for larger parts, is the HG1003ARs. The cell features a HG1003ATC (auto tool change) servo-hydraulic press brake, which for short run, high mix production can be used by an operator in manual mode, or by an HG robot mounted on a separate track for higher volumes.

Able to handle sheets measuring up to 1000 x 800mm and up to 6mm thick, the HG1003ARs is all about automation. For instance, the robot itself features automatic gripper change functionality, while angle adjustment and high accuracy positioning are also fully automated.

“Using the HG1003ARs, parts can be loaded and unloaded at different positions as required – an integrated LD camera system is deployed to avoid positioning errors when picking up and loading the sheets. The camera system records the precise position of the work-pieces, which may have been pushed together on the stack, and passes this information to the robot. Even the tiniest discrepancies can be compensated,” Amada explained.

The third automated bending cell is Amada’s HG1303Rm. Based on an Amada HG1303 press brake, the cell features an innovative bending system (special gripper and motorised repositioning device) for long parts – sheets of up to 2500mm can be accommodated by the HG robot.

Amada’s HG press brake features a patented hybrid drive system and a new ‘Smartphone style' 3D control unit. Its new Product Designer software can unfold 3D sheet metal models and automatically apply unfold data and feature recognition ready for manufacture.

Amada also showcased several bending innovations on its booth at EuroBLECH in Hanover last October. One was a new range of press brakes, based on its traditional downstroking HFE range, but in a 3i version (its latest 3D graphic control interface).

“This means that we can get all the bending information that the operator needs on one screen – the 3D modeller can plan, zoom and rotate and check the bending process before the products are placed on the machine. We have also introduced mechanical and laser bend angle measurement systems so that we can check correct spring back bend results and the operator does not need to pick the parts up twice to get the correct bend results – it is done automatically,” Amada told ISMR.

Another bending feature was its energysaving systems (inverter-controlled stop/start technology) which reduces energy consumption when bending by 60 per cent and, consequently, overall running costs.

Turkish manufacturer Baykal is a leading manufacturer and global supplier of sheet metal working machinery specialising in the production of press brakes, shears, punching machines, laser cutting systems, plasma cutting machines and waterjet cutting machines. Its APHS range of press brakes is highly versatile. For its manufacturing operations, Baykal uses three factories which together combine a production area of 60,000 square metres, making it one of Europe’s largest facilities for sheet metal working and fabricating machinery.

Boschert Gizelis presented its new production range of CNC press brakes and CNC shears at EuroBLECH 2014, the ‘i-bend‘ and ‘i-cut‘ respectively. The machines have been designed using input from discussions with Gizelis customers on changes that would help them to improve quality, productivity and efficiency. Small details, says Gizelis, make the difference…

On show for the first time in the UK at the recent Bystronic Open House was its Xpert 40 press brake, the smallest press brake currently manufactured by Bystronic. It is designed to be moveable by fork lift truck to anywhere within a factory, so it can be relocated easily next to other machinery that it serves, maximising productivity.

Ermaksan’s EVO II hybrid press brake

“The innovative press brake design results in highly dynamic movements at bending speeds of up to 25mm per second, allowing small parts to be processed three times faster than on larger press brake models. On component lengths of up to one metre, Xpert 40 generates a bending capacity of 40 tonnes,” Bystronic told ISMR.

Bystronic’s latest ByMotion drive control system ensures finely tuned interplay between speed, precision and power. By coordinating the acceleration of the upper beam and the back gauges, it delivers bending results with a high degree of repeatability.

Ermaksan, a Turkish manufacturer specialising in the production of sheet metal working machineries, has won three awards in two different international design competitions for both its EVO II hybrid press brake and Fibermak laser cutting machine. Its EVO-II the hybrid press brake with its green energy, silence, low oil usage, high repeatability precision and maintenance-free features compared to ordinary hydraulic press brakes picked up a best design award from the GOOD DESIGN institution.

“In conventional press brakes, energy is consumed even in standby mode but the Evolution II press brake only uses energy whilst it is bending. It can bend at a rate of 20 mm/sec. using servo motors and a highly efficient pump. It is also quiet – at 63dB noiseless and has high bending accuracy of between +/-0.01mm. These new generation press brakes are developed to require fewer oil changes (every five years),” Ermaksan explained.

Other Ermaksan press brake models include the SPEED-BEND (synchronised hydraulic press brake), the POWER-BEND PRO 3-axis CNC press brake and the ECO-BEND CNC hydraulic press brake. A 4V bottom die on the ECO-BEND allows users to make variety of bends on different material thicknesses. A movable control unit also allows the operator to choose his working position.

The company’s MICRO-BEND CNC hydraulic press brake is a small, productive hydraulic press brake with the capacity to bend 5mm mild steel with 1m x 40 tonnes of press force and one-axis back gauge. Ermaksan’s HEAVY DUTY press brake features a dynamic crowning system and 6-axis servo motor back gauge system. It also boasts special top and bottom tools and a 610mm throat depth.

Italian manufacturer Gasparini offers a press brake range that includes the X-BEND (press force from 50 to 170 tons) and the X-PRESS ECO. The X-PRESS is available in a wide range of models and powers, with a tonnage that varies between 25 and 1500 tons and lengths of between 1250 and 16000mm. The company offers stand-alone, tandem and tridem configurations, with a wide range of accessories (back gauges, front supports, clamping systems, sheet followers, controls etc.) and a tailor-made approach.

Gasparini’s new Dener hydraulic press brakes were designed to meet a wide variety of needs, accommodating weights ranging from 40 to 2000 tons and offering from 3 to over 11 axes capabilities. Its new electric MC press brake is leaner, greener and more reliable delivering accuracy, quiet operation and faster cycle times.

The Diamond BB Series features a compact, ball screw design driven by an AC servo motor with 40 metric tons of force and one-micron repeatability. It utilises a dual-drive system with a high-speed back gauge, larger stroke and hydraulic power.

LVD Company nv announced the next generation of its Easy-Form® and PPEB Series of high performance press brakes last year featuring a new, modern design that is more energy-efficient, easier to use and incorporates added safety features. Aesthetic and ergonomic, the new generation press brakes also feature advanced control technology, servo drives and scanning sensors, ensuring more responsive operation and higher productivity.

LVD’s latest bending technology includes the Dyna-Press, its compact press brake for the bending of small and simple parts. The new generation Easy-Form with advanced inprocess adaptive bending technology and an advanced touch screen control ensures more responsive operation and higher productivity.

The ToolCell, LVD’s press brake with an integrated tool-changing system, significantly reduces press brake set-up time. LVD is now offering a 220-ton, 3-metre model of the ToolCell. A large selection of top and bottom tooling is stored in a tooling stadium/warehouse under the back gauge. An innovative gripper design built into the machine’s back gauge finger serves as the tool changer mechanism. When a job is complete, the press brake automatically changes the top and bottom tooling as required for the next job. All tool stations are precisely positioned, eliminating the need for manual adjustments.

PunchBend is Prima Power’s new, compact fabrication solution for cut-to-size materials, which processes pre-cut material automatically into ready-bent, high quality components. The essential characteristic of the concept is tight integration of all the elements needed: software, punching and bending technology as well as automation. Both punching and bending technology are servo-electric.

UpDownCenter – an RAS bending centre for one-click programming of up and down bends, including automatic tool change

At EuroBLECH 2014, Prima Power also displayed, inside its FMS line, an updated version of its EBe5 panel bender with 2650mm of bending length and new NC control. It is a fully electrical panel bender. Close to the panel bender was its 50-ton, 2m electrical press brake. All its machines are electrically-driven (no hydraulics) to reduce electricity consumption and maintenance as well as to be more environmentally sustainable.

Located in Sindelfingen, near Stuttgart, RAS Reinhardt Maschinenbau GmbH recently celebrated its 75th anniversary. The company offers a number of solutions for bending components, depending upon material type, thickness, length and part profile. The machine range is extensive in size and capability and includes automated models with automatic tool change, material loading and part manipulation.

The range of forming products manufactured by RAS includes swaging machines, forming and flanging machines, seamers and duct zippers. The firm also offers shearing machines for material up to 6.3mm thick which are supplied as standard with a powered back gauge and digital control. Options include return-to-operator, sheet supports, conveyors and a programmable control.

One of the highlights of Dutch press brake specialist, SafanDarley, at its EuroBLECH booth was the presentation of its new generation of servo-electric press brakes. Its well-known drive-based pulley principle has evolved to a new dual-drive machine concept offering more advantages than its E-Brake concept. The new press brake boasts a C-frame and narrower press beam, with higher pressure load per metre. Other benefits of this new principle are rapid and direct positioning with higher acceleration and accuracy.

Another SafanDarley highlight at EuroBLECH was its new, E-Bend L laser angle measurement system to measure precise bends. It also showcased its augmented reality concept, the SafanDarley Eye. The software is now ready and can be used for tool change on the press brake, for example, to give additional machine tool information (such as location data for the positioning of the products) to the operator through the glasses that he/she wears when using the machine. It is a very innovative development.

SafanDarley made some recent significant investments last year in both its factories in the Netherlands. Last year, it invested over two million euros into the SafanDarley factory in Eijsden to make it more efficient for larger equipment (it now manufactures press brakes of up to 1250 tonnes). This factory makes all its hydraulic press brakes, larger hydraulic shears and special equipment such as automated shears for cutting nickel or automated bending cells.

The issue of bending and improving efficiency downstream from the cutting process is high on Italian manufacturer Salvagnini’s agenda. Its B3 ATA press brake is its benchmark for efficient sheet metal bending.
