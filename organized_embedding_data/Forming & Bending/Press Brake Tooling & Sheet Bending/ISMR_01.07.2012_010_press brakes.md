TRUMPF unveiled the latest generation of its TruBend Cell 5000 all-purpose bending system in February this year. It features improved interplay between the brake press and the robot. The BendMaster’s travel is even more closely harmonized and the regulation of the travel path has been further refined. These and additional measures have resulted in productivity increases of as much as thirty per cent, depending on the specifics of the application, says TRUMPF.

“Shorter throughput times make the individual parts even more economical. The ‘productivity package’ for the TruBend Cell 5000 thus reduces the costs for processing parts by about one fifth. And it does so for a very broad spectrum of components – since this bending cell is designed for shops needing a flexible automation solution to machine sheet metal components of all sizes,” TRUMPF added.

A special sheet metal sensor is used to guarantee process reliability and parts quality during automated machining. Before each sheet is picked up, the sensor detects the exact orientation of the uppermost sheet so that the grab can be properly positioned for pick-up. To keep two parts from being picked up at once, the jaws are monitored with a camera and compressed air is used to separate sheets. ■

The stainless steel welded tube and pipe market is one of the most important segments of the whole stainless steel market. In 2010, it accounted for 13% of worldwide stainless steel flat products and 11% of the total stainless steel market. Product segments for steel pipes and tubes include: electric resistance welded pipes (mechanical steel tubing, structural tubing, structural steel pipes, pressure tubing, standard pipes, oil country tubular goods and line pipes), seamless pipes and tubes, continuous welded pipes and tubes, and gas spiral welded pipes and tubes.

“Tubes and profiles are employed in a wide spectrum of applications. Typical end products include furniture, but also agricultural machinery, construction and utility supply systems. The existing production capacity for tubes and profiles is barely sufficient to cope with the steadily increasing demand. So there are plenty of opportunities for all players in the tube-processing industry,” systems manufacturer, TRUMPF, told ISMR.

Tube processing operations demand a high standard of workmanship from machines capable of delivering consistent results at high output rates. Low maintenance and a full range of automation options are also requirements. Quality is a major prerequisite when manufacturing products that call for a maximum of precision in a process comprising the minimum of work steps. Flexibility is another important aspect i.e. the ability to machine different profile geometries and contours in a wide range of materials and batch sizes.

The existing production capacity for tubes and profiles is barely sufficient to cope with steadily increasing demand so there are plenty of opportunities for players in the tube processing industry.

As a highlight, TT displayed a complete tube manufacturing cell with separation from the tube magazine, robot in-feed and integrated bending piece measurement. The centrepiece of the system was the servo-electrically driven, multi-radii CNC bending machine TUBOTRON 25 MR for tubes from OD 4 to 25mm (1’’) with no hydraulics. The bending machine’s exposed and compact bending head enables the production of complex tube shapes at bending speeds of up to 170°/sec. The main applications are in aerospace technology, automotive or furniture industries.

After being bent, the tube figure is taken out by a robot and transported to the measuring room of the innovative optical measuring cell, TUBOSCAN S. Within a few seconds, the tube figure’s geometry is recorded and documented using the measuring and evaluation software TeZetCAD. Finally, the tube figure is deposited while the next tube is already being formed by the bending machine.

In addition to machinery and measuring technology, TT also presented software innovations at TUBE 2012. Its modular workflow management software PIPEFAB has been re-engineered and is now available with various advanced functions and new features.

Radan’s new Radtube incorporates a library of parametric tube shapes simplifies the creation of the tube material to be cut. A library of parametric hole shapes means that specialist joint features such as duck tails, clips and key holes can easily be added. Radan’s nesting capabilities help users to nest multiple parts on a single pipe or section by creating individual programs for each part, then simulating and checking the cutting path. Previously machined parts can be nested into a multiple number of tubes where each nested tube can be post-processed to form a complete NC program - giving maximum yield and minimum waste from expensive materials.

With Radan’s Radm-ax, cutting operations are generally divided into outer trims - the external forms of the part - and inner trims, which represent the internal cut-outs and other features to be machined.

“Toolpath verification and simulation in both Radtube and Radm-ax ensures there are no nasty surprises when cutting starts,” said Radan. “A full solid toolpath simulation shows the angle of the nozzle as it moves around the part, providing instant feedback on whether machine head movement or accessibility means a specific move is possible. Any collisions which are detected are highlighted both on the model and via on-screen messages and can be manually edited or corrected automatically. Once the toolpath has been completely verified and optimised Radtube and Radm-ax quickly generate reliable NC code. A wealth of features help reduce machine tool wear while ensuring feeds and speeds are maintained across the job.”

German company Transfluid has developed new chip-less tube cutting technology, an economical process to use for coil work when chips are to be avoided in the production process or when large amounts of tubes need to be separated. The Transfluid systems provide a cutting output of max Ø 30 x 3 for stainless tubes and offer a production speed of up to 1,600 pieces per hour. The shortest cutting length is 25mm (without pulling apart) and 55mm (with pulling apart), all with a residual length of 110mm. It handles tubes with diameters of up to 76mm regardless of wall thickness. The software is specially programmed for improved cutting so as to ensure that almost no waste originates from the process.

AutoForm Engineering GmbH, a supplier of software solutions for the sheet metal forming industry, has enhanced its hydroforming products. AutoForm Hydroforming contains innovative functionalities for the rapid analysis and simulation of the entire hydroforming process. One important new feature is the support of cutting process steps. As a result, tube length can be optimised and the forming of tube ends as well as the complete forming process can be simulated. Two cutting types are available: plane cut and laser cut for tube ends trimming and holes piercing.

“The AutoForm hydro solver now supports parallel computing achieving significant speedup levels (comparable to AutoForm’s incremental solver). CECIMO takes the lead in promoting sustainable manufacturing in Europe

CECIMO has launched a European initiative which aims to raise the sustainability bar in the machine tool industry: ‘Blue Competence Machine Tools.’ With this initiative, CECIMO aims to increase awareness and knowledge in the machine tool industry about sustainability.

CECIMO is moving ahead to guide the sector in setting standards in the area of sustainable production and products. The machine tool industry is playing a key enabling role during the shift towards a resource-efficient low carbon economy.

CECIMO is the European association of machine tool manufacturers. It represents the common interests of the European machine tool industries. Members of CECIMO are the fifteen national associations of machine tool builders that represent over 1600 industrial enterprises in Europe, many of which are SMEs.

## European alliance for a sustainable future

In the face of climate change and depleting natural resources, CECIMO sends a strong message to companies to pool their expertise, knowledge and resources to generate solutions which will help tackle these challenges. It offers European machine tool builders a common platform under the Blue Competence Machine Tools initiative, which will enable them to join forces and coordinate efforts for the development of energy- and resource-efficient production solutions and products.

The machine tool industry is already a key provider of production solutions which help reduce energy and material consumption in the production process in a broad spectrum of user industries, including automotive, aerospace, railway vehicles, energy generation equipment and more. Thanks to innovative solutions offered by machine tools, these sectors can drastically improve product features. Advances in materials science go hand in hand with advances in production technologies and, thanks to this, user industries can produce, for example, more fuel-efficient aero engines or lighter cars and ships which have an important impact on the environment.

“However, a comprehensive framework within which manufacturers could cooperate to this end has been missing so far. Blue Competence Machine Tools is the first common European initiative which defines objectives and transparent criteria and standards for sustainability in the machine tool industry covering production, products and business practices. We consider the initiative to be a long-term commitment which will embed the principles of sustainability into the sector,” CECIMO explained.

CECIMO introduced Blue Competence Machine Tools for the first time with a press conference in Brussels, followed by a second press conference at the METAV Exhibition in Düsseldorf, Germany.

## ’Manufacturing’ solutions for Europe 2020 targets

“The initiative is in coherence with the Europe 2020 strategy objectives with regard to climate change and resource-efficiency. Blue Competence Machine Tools will help promote sustainable manufacturing as a means for achieving more responsible use of natural resources and energy, thus contributing to the protection of the environment and to sustainable development. The wide adoption of sustainable solutions in manufacturing industries will make an important contribution to the conservation of natural resources, the protection of the environment and the fight against climate change,” CECIMO told ISMR.

Sustainable manufacturing solutions enable user industries to ensure that they can continue to grow and create value for the society without compromising the needs of future generations for natural resources. A world-competitive manufacturing base will be the main driver to improve the quality of life of the European society and in the rest of the world. If it develops in the right direction, manufacturing will give Europe full control over its future economic development and provide solutions to grand societal challenges. Blue Competence Machine Tools, believes CECIMO, has the ambition to become a landmark initiative which will help open up a sustainable development path for European manufacturing.

“Sustainability is increasingly becoming a factor of differentiation for machine tool companies, leading to a higher level of competitiveness in the global marketplace. Besides high precision, accuracy, speed and performance, customers increasingly demand solutions which reduce the total cost of ownership. Blue Competence Machine Tools will reaffirm the position of Europe as the global leader in sustainable manufacturing solutions and it will help the sector make a leap forward to an even more successful future. Growth in manufacturing output very often translates into growth in jobs creation throughout the manufacturing value chain. Manufacturing which generates high added value through the provision of solutions for future challenges will be a major source of job creation in Europe,” added CECIMO.

## The environmental challenge

The global economy and the demand for energy are constantly growing. According to World Bank Data, global energy consumption increased from 5,508,537 to 11,787,115 kt (of oil equivalent) between 1971 and 2008. CO2 emissions at global level increased from 9,420,523 to 32,082,583 kt between 1960 and 2008 according to the World Bank, as a result of mostly economic activities. According to CECIMO, this constantly growing energy consumption, associated with more CO2 emissions and over-use of natural resources and raw materials, endangers society. To connect the dots between economy, ecology and society, energy- and resource-efficient solutions are needed.

## How does it work in practice?

CECIMO started operating the Blue Competence initiative for the machine tools sector in February this year. The Blue Competence initiative was launched in Germany by the German Engineering Federation in 2009 for the entire mechanical engineering industry. CECIMO, with the support of some national member associations, has brought the initiative to a European level for the machine tool sector. Operated by CECIMO, Blue Competence Machine Tools is open to participation for all machine tool companies across Europe as well as to national associations.

A company which wants to participate in the initiative has to meet the pre-defined sustainability criteria in its production and business practices. Thus, companies make a public commitment to add value to the economy, environment and society. Companies which have met the preconditions can thereafter confirm to their customers and to the public that they are a member of Blue Competence Machine Tools.

Participating national associations play an important role in the implementation of the Blue Competence Machine Tools initiative. They are the first contact point for companies in their respective countries and they assume the main responsibility for the promotion of the initiative within their national borders. They also conclude individual agreements with companies and institutes operating in the field of machine tools.

The CECIMO Steering Committee decides on the general policy for the Blue Competence Machine Tools initiative. Applications from companies are evaluated by CECIMO. Overall, the initiative mobilises a broad network of industry associations, companies and research institutes across Europe to pool resources, expertise and ideas to build-up a vision for a sustainable future for the European machine tool industry and for the entire European industrial base.

“The Blue Competence Machine Tools initiative merges economic, ecological and social goals in one, and it demonstrates the contribution the machine tool industry can make to achieve smart, sustainable and inclusive growth in Europe,” CECIMO explained.

“Blue Competence Machine Tools is a voluntary initiative and participating in the initiative does not imply compliance with any national or European regulation. Nevertheless it is in line with European policy objectives and it highlights the commitment of the industry to contribute to EU’s climate and energy goals.”

Laser cutting systems for machining sheet metal are now available with fiber lasers: systems are on the market that are able to machine not only steel materials but also non-ferrous materials like copper, brass and titanium. Fiber lasers are suitable for different applications such as welding, hybrid welding, remote welding, 2D and 3D remote cutting, heat treatment and cladding.

“Linear drives enable positioning speeds of up to 240 m/min. to be achieved. For laser machining of stainless steel sheets to a thickness of 1mm, cutting speeds of up to 60 m/min. are possible. Using fibers means that elaborate and costly optics for beam guidance can be dispensed with, and the space requirement downsized,” Amada Machine Tools Co. Ltd. explained.

The systems are smaller than CO2, yet fiber delivery enables the beam to travel greater distances allowing for installation on larger tables.

## Flexible support

Higher laser power and automatic adjustment of the focus position make Bystronics’ BySprintFiber more flexible. Since the turn of the year 2011/2012, Bystronic has been offering its BySprintFiber with two-kilowatt laser power and now with a three-kilowatt laser source. The new Fiber 3000 laser source increases the maximum thickness of sheet to be processed from 12 to 20mm with steel; from 6 to 8mm for stainless steel, from 6 to 12mm for aluminium; and for copper and brass to 6mm. As a result of its higher power, the BySprintFiber with the Fiber 3000 laser source is considerably more flexible in its application.

A further innovation with fiber laser cutting is the ByPosFiber, on which the focus position can be automatically adjusted. This is particularly necessary when the type of material is changed between two orders. ByPosFiber will be fitted as standard to all BySprintFiber systems that are ordered after the New Year, independent of the selected laser power. For systems that were delivered without the ByPosFiber, a retrofit kit will be available from the third quarter of 2012.
