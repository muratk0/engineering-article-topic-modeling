# Selecting Multi-V Dies for Press Brake Bending

## Purpose of Multi-V Tooling

A multi-V die provides several selectable V-openings in one die body. It can reduce tooling changes where multiple openings are needed, but each opening must be evaluated as a separate tooling option. A multi-V die is not automatically suitable merely because it contains an opening near a calculated target.

Compared with a single V-die, a multi-V block may introduce clearance concerns from unused grooves, adjacent faces, shoulders, and sidewalls. Suitability depends on the complete part geometry and on clearance during loading, bending, and removal.

## Select an Opening from Material and Bend Requirements

For air bending, opening-to-thickness ratios such as 6T, 8T, 10T, and 12T can be used only as an initial shortlist, where T is the measured material thickness. The article presents 8T as a common starting point for some sheet applications, but material properties, bend geometry, and tooling limitations determine final selection.

A narrower opening generally:

- Produces a tighter air-bent inside radius.
- Provides support for shorter flanges.
- Requires greater bending force.
- May increase the risk of marking, cracking, or excessive tooling stress.

A wider opening generally:

- Requires less force in air bending.
- Produces a larger inside radius.
- May provide inadequate support for short flanges.
- Can create clearance or dimensional problems for the part.

The article uses approximate air-bending estimates for mild steel: an inside radius near 16% of the V-opening and a minimum flange near 77% of the V-opening. These are screening estimates, not universal rules. They depend on material behavior, die profile, punch geometry, bend method, and the actual support condition. They should not be applied without verification to bottoming or coining.

## Consider Bend Method and Material

Air bending, bottoming, and coining have different contact conditions and loading requirements. In air bending, the sheet contacts the punch tip and die shoulders, and ram position affects the formed angle. Bottoming and coining depend more strongly on punch radius, die angle, penetration, compression, and springback. A die opening acceptable for air bending may not be suitable for the other methods.

Material type also affects force, radius, springback, and cracking risk. The article notes that equal-thickness materials can behave differently because of differences in strength, ductility, springback, alloy or temper, and grain direction. Material thickness alone is therefore insufficient for final die selection.

## Verify Geometry and Clearance

Evaluate candidate openings against the drawing requirements for inside radius, angle, flange length, dimensional tolerances, and surface condition. A short flange must remain securely supported across the die shoulders. A minimum-flange estimate does not prove that the part will remain stable during the bend.

Check the complete part path through the operation. Include return flanges, hems, previously formed features, holders, backgauge components, the die body, adjacent faces of the multi-V block, and the removal path. A part that clears at initial contact can still collide with another face as it rotates. A setup is unsuitable if the part becomes trapped, requires prying for removal, or has unstable support.

Equal V-openings from different dies do not necessarily produce equivalent results. Shoulder radius, die angle, groove depth, body geometry, capacity, and surface condition can affect support, marking, clearance, radius, and angle behavior.

## Confirm Load Capacity and Setup Integrity

Calculate bending force using the actual material, measured thickness, bend length, selected opening, and bend method. Use the applicable machine and tooling documentation or approved workplace calculation method. Do not use air-bending force data to approve bottoming or coining.

The lowest documented rating in the load path controls the permitted setup. Review the ratings of the press brake, punch, clamps, selected groove, die body, holders, adapters, table, and any segmented tooling. Consider both total load and applicable force-per-length or concentrated-load limits. Off-center bending requires particular review because it may impose uneven loading.

Tooling must be clean, seated correctly, secured, aligned, and checked for correct centerline and working height. Rotation of a multi-V block can change the active groove position and elevation. Setup, lifting, rotation, clamping, machine adjustments, and any diagnostic work must follow the machine manual, tooling documentation, and approved site procedures, and be performed by qualified personnel.

## Validate with Controlled Test Bends

Calculations identify candidate grooves; test bends validate the setup. Use representative material and confirm the specified material condition, thickness, grain orientation, surface condition, and flange geometry. Conduct the test under approved operating procedures.

Inspect unloaded test pieces for:

- Bend angle and variation along the bend.
- Inside radius.
- Flange length and bend-line location.
- Straightness, bow, twist, and localized distortion.
- Surface marking, scoring, galling, indentation, or coating damage.
- Stable support, clearance, and safe removal.

Repeat accepted bends without unapproved setup changes to assess repeatability. If cracking, unstable angles, excessive marking, inadequate support, collision, or removal problems occur, stop and reassess the tooling selection. Changing the V-opening changes force, radius, support, contact conditions, and clearance, so all relevant checks must be repeated.

## Tooling Choice

A multi-V die is appropriate only when a selected groove meets geometry, capacity, clearance, finish, and repeatability requirements. A single V-die may be preferable where unused multi-V faces interfere with formed features or where a standardized setup is needed. Other tooling profiles may be required when fixed V-openings cannot provide the specified radius, access, clearance, or surface condition.

Document the qualified material, bend method, tooling identity, active opening, physical orientation, inspection results, and approved machine settings. Reassess the setup after relevant changes, tooling damage, collisions, alarms, or unexplained quality trends.
