“For very narrow and thin strips, we produce slitting systems without loops before or after the shears. This means that the machines work with direct tension, so that brakes can be done away with altogether. The only remaining critical factor is the uniformity of the strip tension on the unwinder and the winder side,” it explained.

“To ensure consistent values for this, an electronic dancer control before the slitting shears is combined with direct tension measurement after it. The measurement is made by strain gauges in load cells integrated into the bearing of the deflection rollers before the winders, which can be used to determine the actual tension value. This is reconciled by the software with the pre-set values and, if necessary, automatic fine adjustment is performed. The permanent tension adjustment also has a positive effect on the quality of the cut. The cut strips can then be wound onto the coilers, either individually or several at a time depending upon their level of delicacy.”

For wide input materials, large coil diameters and high throughput speeds of up to 400m/min. a looping pit is needed after the shears, otherwise it is not possible to wind up the different lengths of strip with the same tightness. Such differences in length can be compensated for by the strips hanging down by different depths into the pit, although this will also require a brake before the coilers.

For this reason, in addition to the dancer control, B+S supplies special vacuum brake rollers which do not come into contact with the top of the strips. Instead of clamping the material, as on tension pads (which could damage the surface and will certainly rub off coatings) the bottom of the strip is pulled onto the roller by negative pressure. This in turn uses regenerative braking which results in hardly any relative movement between the strip and the roller. “This is the only technology that can guarantee that the surface will remain free of damage,” said Baral.

## Precision cutting and measuring systems

As another measure to protect the delicate product, all rollers which come into contact with the product on both system variants are coated with polyurethane to prevent metal-on-metal contact. Precision shears are not only adapted to the material in advance, they are also equipped with precise roller bearings and a play-free overcut setting, which ensures precise positioning of the blade at all times.

“This design for the shears, in conjunction with careful guidance of the material and the controlled strip tensions, ensures that the quality of the outgoing strips is always equal to that of the incoming plates – regardless of how high the surface quality of the input material,” concluded Baral. To check the quality of the cut – including burr, edge indentation and deformation – measurements can be taken with the newly developed EP 300 measuring instrument.

Modern measurement technology now makes it possible to test samples of different lengths and thicknesses on-site without extended preparation and without contact with the product. The systems are also under continuous development, so that a broader spectrum of samples can be tested with maximum precision. Today, it is possible to achieve resolutions of 2.2 x 10μm to an accuracy of 1μm using the Lateral Chromatic Imaging (LCI) process. Strip will therefore satisfy the customer’s expectations despite delicate surfaces and low tolerances. ■
