# Compensating for springback

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

ITALIAN BENDING PIONEER, Gasparini, has developed an innovative device, GPS4, for measuring springback whilst bending. A sensor embedded into the die mechanically measures the profile angle within the die cavity, so as to always reach the exact value.

“Springback is a natural phenomenon derived from residual elastic recovery of the sheet. After the bending force has been removed, the profile will tend to open and the resulting angle will be 2.5°-3° more than the expected result. When bending High Strength Steels (HSS), elastic recovery often ranges from 10° to 25°,” said Gasparini.

“This effect can be compensated for by overbending the profile at a sharper angle, so that springback will make it reach the desired angle afterwards. It can be a trial-and-error process - you bend a sample, measure it, correct the press brake set-up and then proceed to a few more workpieces. After a while, you realize that the sheet metal you are now working comes from a different batch and has different properties. You have to start the process all over again from the beginning…”

GPS4 enables users to bend complex shapes, with Z-bends or with narrow flanges. In these cases, optical sensing can be ineffective or lead to problems due to error accumulation. Material surface finish also has no effect, said the manufacturer, and set-up times are reduced as the operator does not need to know thickness, length, material or bending force.
