Wilson Tool sees strong growth in press brake tooling this year

This year, Wilson Tool International, the worlds’ largest independent manufacturer of tooling systems for the metal fabrication industry, is celebrating the 20th anniversary of its press brake tooling division and the Wilson Wheel® family of punch press tooling.

Launched in 1994, Wilson Tool’s press brake division began by manufacturing a single line of tooling for the press brake. Today, the company offers a comprehensive selection of press brake tooling solutions. From standard tooling solutions for European style, American Precision®, WILA/TRUMPF Style Precision™ and Exacta conventional press brake tooling to special customised tooling for unique bending applications, and an array of clamping solutions designed to speed setups, Wilson Tool provides a broad selection of tooling options to press brake fabricators around the world.

“Certainly press brake tooling is seen as a big part of our future,” Ian McCartney, Wilson’s business manager, told ISMR at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover. “A large part of our booth here at the exhibition is dedicated to press brake tooling and solutions (clamping, accessories etc.). We have a complete tooling range for all major press brake manufacturers.

“We are also showcasing some concept tools on the stand for customer feedback – our Xpress air clamps, a pneumatic clamping system for European tooling, are on the Safan press brake here on our booth. On the punching side, we also have the SmartMark tool – both these products will be launched in 2015. We use this show to gather feedback on them both, and fine tune them as they come out of R&D.

“We are getting some great engagement on the press brake side in Europe this year – it has been a real growth area for us. We are looking at 30% growth in press brake tooling this year. We are starting to be seen more as a press brake tooling company – we had previously been so engaged on the punching side of the business, it takes a while for customers to see the other parts. But the last twelve months have really shown a difference. We now have a much larger range to offer our customers. We can provide full tooling solutions for most manufacturer press brakes now.”

Jeff Paulson, Worldwide Marketing Manager, Wilson Tool International, agreed: “It’s the same situation in the US – our press brake products' division has grown enormously. We are growing really well in developing markets such as Asia and South America. We have a very comprehensive range of press brake tooling and are not tied to any particular type of machine.”

SmartMark® is an ink jet sheet-marking solution that fits into a turret punch press. It can be loaded directly into the C-station of most thick turret machines. Fabricators can program the printer to mark parts with alphanumeric text, lines, timestamps or even barcodes. SmartMark gives fabricators the ability to mark everything from part numbers and descriptions to assembly and part handling instructions – helping to errorproof production. It can also be used to improve the speed and accuracy of the fabrication process, saving time and money.

To complement its Express clamping system for Euro style press brake punches, Wilson Tool is developing an ExpressAir pneumatic clamp. Unlike other similar systems, which sometimes require specially adapted punches, the ExpressAir system clamps and seats standard punches. Other features of this new system are its extra slim profile, giving increased bend clearance, and clamp plates that allow full length and sectionalised tooling to be clamped and seated securely. The ExpressAir clamps are interlocking, allowing them to be placed end-to-end.

## Sheet metal trends

“In terms of trends, the punching side is fairly static. We don’t see huge growth there – however, it is not declining,” Ian McCartney explained. “A lot of it is moving from regions like western Europe to the Middle East, South America and parts of Asia, where there are good levels of growth. Lasers also have an effect on punching sales. But punching will continue long into the future – there is a lot of interesting new developments in punching and definite cost advantages compared to laser cutting. You can’t form on a laser… Special tooling is key, and we have always been able to provide special tooling (a growing business) to our customers.”

It has been a good year of modest growth in Europe for Wilson Tool, Jeff Paulson added, confirming that EuroBLECH was a busy show for Wilson Tool with lots of visitors prepared to buy.

“The press brake side of our business will see strong growth next year. We will continue to increase our stock levels across all our worldwide facilities,” added McCartney. “Wilson Tool has always been known for rapid deliveries in punch press tooling. We plan to follow that strategy for press brake tooling as well. We want to ensure that we can meet customer demands for fast deliveries of press brake tooling in all worldwide markets.”

Jeff Paulson also confirmed that Wilson Tool is looking more closely at its product development strategy, with a strong focus on customer collaboration.

“Wilson Tool has been successful in the US over the past 50 years but we need to ensure that, when we are developing products, it is what the customer wants and not just what we want to make. We are therefore taking more steps to involve our customers more in the front-end of product development. We already work well with customers this way on special tooling,” he told ISMR.

“We would also like to work more on special tooling for press brakes with our customers,” added Ian McCartney. “There is now more automation around for press brakes and press brake software is becoming more sophisticated and simpler to use. It’s been great to see some of the press brake machine developments at this show. OEMs are doing a lot to make machines easier to use and more productive. I have also seen some good punch press developments at the show. Punch presses are now able to do high forming and other operations that were previously done on a press brake.”

Bystronic made a raft of new technology announcements in laser, waterjet, press brakes, software and services at EuroBLECH 2014

“Our core message for EuroBLECH 2014 is world class manufacturing," Johan Elster, Bystronic’s head of markets, told ISMR at its booth at EuroBLECH. “This slogan is based on a comprehensive programme of products and services, with which we optimise the manufacturing processes of our customers. So profoundly that we are able to say that, with us as a partner, users in the sheet metal processing sector become world class manufacturers. It is our partnership philosophy to grow with our customers – if they are successful, we are successful. And being successful means to get to the top. We symbolise this with the Matterhorn.”

The Swiss manufacturer’s booth at this year’s EuroBLECH highlighted its competence in laser, waterjet, press brakes, software and services. It had a raft of new technology launches and announcements at the show in each of these fields. One highlight, new for the sheet metal industry, was its ByOptimizer. The online service creates optimised laser cutting plans so quickly and easily that conventional cutting plan creation becomes unnecessary. ByOptimizer is based on new Cluster technology by Swedish company, Tomologic. This technology automatically groups parts to be cut on a sheet. So tightly, in fact, that no more raw material is wasted on gaps between parts.

“The ByOptimizer concept is integrated in our BySoft 7 software. If customers upload their part drawings, they will, within minutes, receive an optimised cutting plan back for the parts. We are delighted to be able to launch the product here. With this new service, our customers can save more material and time, make more money and be more successful. EuroBLECH is an excellent platform for new product launches – the entire industry is here and customers visit from all over the world,” Johan Elster told ISMR.

“Compared to conventional cutting plans, ByOptimizer achieves material savings of more than 10 per cent on average on a 3m x 1.5m, standard-sized metal sheet. For example, for a job comprising approximately 60 parts (for which the conventional programming method required two metal sheets), ByOptimizer groups so efficiently that the parts will all fit on a single sheet. This not only reduces material consumption, but also set-up time because there is no need to load a second sheet.”

## Fiber laser advances

Bystronic is now offering its BySprint Fiber laser cutting system with a 6kW laser source – increasing the cutting speed even more. It cuts 3mm stainless steel three times faster than a 6kW CO2 laser, the Swiss manufacturer told ISMR. Depending upon the material and sheet thickness, this equates to a parts output increase of 300 per cent for the user.

“The 6kW BySprint Fibre laser cutting machine was launched in May this year and has been a huge success for us. It can rapidly cut mid-range material of up to 25mm (where a lot of customers have large volume). The big advantage, when you cut with nitrogen (from 2-8mm material), is that it is incredibly fast. With our new function PowerCut on the fiber laser, we extend the application range. We achieve terrific cutting quality on up to 30mm thick stainless steel and aluminium,” added Johan Elster.

“We recognized the trend towards the fiber laser at an early stage and reallocated our development resources accordingly. Now we can draw on sufficient development potential to set new trends in fiber laser technology as well.”

Detection Eye is another new Bystronic fiber laser function upgrade. This tool takes seconds to automatically measure the loaded metal sheet before the cutting process begins, allowing users to reduce set-up times and adapt cutting plans according to the material. This leads to precise cutting results that extend to the outermost edges of the metal sheet.

Bystronic is also expanding its existing ByTrans Extended automation module to the 4020 format. This automation unit handles the entire shuttle table loading and unloading process on 4020-format laser cutting systems, helping users to maximise material flow when laser cutting. The ByTrans Extended 4020 requires 75 seconds to load a metal sheet and subsequently unload the cutting table.

## The bending cycle

The Xpert 40 provides a first glimpse of Bystronic's vision for the small format bending experience of the future. The Xpert 40 will be launched on the market in Spring 2015 and will round-off Bystronic’s range of press brakes for the small machine segment. In line with the “plug and bend” principle, the machine is set up and ready to use in less than five minutes. The Xpert 40’s innovative, lightweight design delivers high levels of dynamics at bending speeds of up to 25mm per second. This means that it can bend small parts three times faster than larger press brake models. On bending lengths exceeding one metre, the Xpert 40 generates a press capacity of 40 tons.

Bystronic’s newly developed ByMotion drive control system ensures a finely tuned interplay between speed, precision and power. By coordinating the precise acceleration of the upper beam and the back gauges, it delivers bending results with high degrees of repetition accuracy.

During the development of the Xpert 40, Bystronic broke new ground. For the first time, customers were involved directly in the development process of a new machine. The result is an innovative press brake that will allow users to process small bent parts quickly and cost-effectively.

## The final cut

In waterjet cutting, Bystronic is continuing the trend towards flexible machine systems. The latest machine in its portfolio is the ByJet Flex. Bystronic won an exhibition award at EuroBLECH 2014 in Hanover in the cutting category for its ByJet Flex waterjet cutting system.

At EuroBLECH, Bystronic presented a newly developed 3D cutting head – an additional feature that will extend the machine’s broad spectrum of applications. In future, it will be possible to equip the ByJet Flex with up to two 3D cutting heads. This will allow users to cut beveled cutting edges on the ByJet Flex with twice the productivity. Thanks to the ByJet Flex's modular machine concept, upgrading from 2D to 3D technology requires less than an hour.

ByMotion enables the safe integration of 3D technology. This control system supports every ByJet Flex cutting function, regardless of whether it uses 2D or 3D technology. For 3D applications, ByMotion allows simultaneous 5-axis cutting with two cutting heads, employing continuous height sensing on both cutting heads. In future, users can program the cutting plans for 3D applications using Bevel Manager – an optional module that can be easily added to the BySoft software package.

## 2014 in focus

“2014 will be a very successful year for Bystronic,” explained Johan Elster. “Europe is still one of our largest markets, so we focus hugely on this market. It is not just Central and Southern Europe that are growing, but also some other countries like the UK.

Growth in China has been slower than before, in general. But we have positive expectations for the future. In China, we see continued growth. Southeast Asia is also an important market for Bystronic. Here, we have strong regional representation with our sales and service offerings.

“The US market has been a great success for us over the past few years. Our 6kW fiber laser machine has enjoyed good growth in that market.”

Yamazaki Mazak exhibited five laser cutting machines at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition, the largest number of machines ever exhibited at the show by the company. ISMR also viewed a new machining centre for friction stir welding on its booth – a special application developed in concert with Airbus. The VERTICAL CENTRE SMART 430A, complete with friction stir welding capability, can mill, drill, weld and tap flat components.

Overall, the company’s 1000 sq. m. booth demonstrated a ‘total solution’ for sheet metal cutting, combining laser processing and machine tools.

## Tube cutting markets

“We are showcasing a new laser tube cutting machine, the Tube Gear 150 – a high speed CO2 laser cutter for round and square tubes designed for efficient, high speed tube and pipe cutting operations which require high levels of precision,” confirmed Gaetano Lo Guzzo, Director, Laser Business Europe, Yamazaki Mazak. “We started in the laser tube cutting business fifteen years ago with larger machines (cutting 400mm max. diameter), a major product range for us with 20% of market share for tube applications. However, the new Tube Gear 150 can process 12mm to 152mm tube diameters and cover 80% of the required market so, with this model, we want to drastically increase our tube cutting market share. This is the first time that this machine has been exhibited in Europe.

Yamazaki Mazak exhibited five laser cutting machines and a friction stir welding machining project on its booth at EuroBLECH

“We have also launched a new 2.5kW CO2 laser machine for 2D sheet metal cutting, the Optiplex Nexus 3015, to help our customers to gain market share. It is an entry-level machine for first time users and features a small footprint, easy programming, shorter installation times and an ergonomic design. It can process small to medium-sized tubes.”

Yamazaki Mazak has been producing fiber laser cutting machines for three years. At EuroBLECH, it exhibited the Optiplex 3015 Fiber II and the Optiplex 4020 Fiber II, designed for the ultra-fast cutting of different materials. This is a larger machine with a 4kW fiber resonator (4m x 2m) featuring automatic nozzle change and automatic set-up for greater capability.

“The fiber laser market is drastically increasing,” explained Lo Guzzo. “Last year, the global fiber laser cutting market was around 35% (compared to CO2 cutting). This year, we expect that fiber laser operations will overtake CO2 laser cutting operations for the first time. Fiber technology is now suitable for thicker material cutting (up to 20mm) – something that was not possible a year ago. We believe that there is a strong future for fiber laser cutting – it offers higher efficiencies, lower running costs and higher speeds than CO2 applications. I counted 43 fiber laser machines at this show alone!”

The two Optiplex 4020 laser processing machines at EuroBLECH were equipped with an expanded table and available with both fiber and CO2 laser sources. The company also displayed a Super Turbo X 3015, a high precision 4kW CO2 laser cutting system. It was the first time it has exhibited this model with the new CNC and higher dynamics. The machine features a faster rapid traverse rate (50m/min X and Y axis) and is suitable for thick plate cutting applications.

“The friction stir welding machine project on display at our booth is fitted with DeltaN FS technology developed by Airbus. It combines machining with friction-stir welding on one machine. With friction stir welding, you can weld and combine different types of materials (ie plastics to metals, composites etc.) as it happens at the granular level – no need for grinding etc.,” added Lo Guzzo.

## On track for growth

“This year, we opened a new second facility in China (Dailing) – we already have a factory in Beijing – and a new office in Brazil. We also have a production facility in Europe (UK). Only our factory in Japan (Nagoya) produces laser cutting machines,” explained Lo Guzzo.

“Our Nagoya factory is a very impressive facility – it is all underground to ensure that our laser products are manufactured in the cleanest possible environment, which is important for the level of quality required. It is also a very sustainable environment because it is temperature-controlled by geothermal functions – giving it strong green credentials,” commented Maja Foster, Yamazaki Mazak’s marketing manager. “We have also expanded our European Parts Centre in Belgium, to offer an even greater level of customer support across the continent, which will open early next year. Yamazaki Mazak is a family-owned business and the expansion of our European Parts Centre emphasises how we continuously re-invest every year to give our customers the highest level of support throughout the life of their machine tools.”

Lo Guzzo confirmed that this year has been a better year for Yamazaki Mazak, despite the fact that the European economy has still not fully recovered. He told ISMR that sales had improved this year by about 20% to date.
