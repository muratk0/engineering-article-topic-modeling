# We highlight a selection of the latest new press brake bending technologies in the global market.

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“Automation, safety, environmental factors, increased accuracy and ease of operation requirements, as well as the move towards Industry 4.0, are affecting press brake design.”

SHEET METAL CAN be bent by a range of systems, including panel benders, folders and press brakes. The press brake controls the movement of either the punch (upper tool) or the die (bottom tool) and provides the press force using hydraulic rams or electrical servo motors. The press brake’s bending force, known as tonnage, determines how much pressure can be applied by the punch during a bend. Higher tonnage allows for bending thicker and harder metals, and lower tonnage is most appropriate for thinner metals.

The global automatic bending machine market is projected to grow from US$ 1.8 billion in 2018 to US$ 2.4 billion by 2024; it is expected to grow at a CAGR of 4.5% from 2019 to 2024, according to analyst, Research and Markets. Electric bending machines are easy to use and consume lower electricity than other types of bending systems (such as pneumatic and hydraulic). Press brake technology ranges from hydraulic and mechanical to pneumatic and servo-electric, with robotised bending increasingly being used.

Industry 4.0 is one of the major trends being witnessed in the global press brake market, with digital management of product assembly, quality check, inventory management, data and resources management and integrated communications with suppliers and vendors.

## Latest bending innovations

Safety, environmental factors (including energy efficiency and noise reduction), increased accuracy and ease of operation requirements, as well as the move towards Industry 4.0, have affected machine tool design. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new press brake technology developments in the global market.

ADIRA Metal Forming Solutions SA is headquartered in Portugal and operates the business under various segments such as press brakes, shears, software and AM. The company offers products such as PF Fast, PH Heavy Duty, GreenBender, Bluebender, and PA Hexa C. At EuroBLECH 2018 in Hanover, ADIRA presented a hybrid press brake (PF016040) with a new generation of Green dynamics technology (based on the Hoerbiger hybrid ePrAX®control), a fully electric Bluebender press brake (BB003515) with a new generation of Bluedrive systems and, for the first time, an electric press brake (BB005020) with automatic tool changer (ATC) to reduce set-up time and maximise machine productivity.

Japanese manufacturer AMADA offers products such as HG Series, HG-1003ATC, HD-NT Series and EG Series. Its HG1003ATC bending system, for its V-factory digital sheet metal processing solution, is a servo/ hydraulic hybrid drive system which features an Automatic Tool Changer (ATC) and an automatic bend indicator sensor plus an AMNC 3i control and ID scanner.

Its range of press brakes is designed for flexibility, productivity and energy-saving (inverter-controlled stop/start technology). Its HG press brake features a patented hybrid drive system and a new ‘Smartphone style’ 3D control unit. New Product Designer software can unfold 3D sheet metal models and automatically apply unfolding data and feature recognition ready for manufacture.

Its EG series electric press brake incorporates AMADA’s patented dual servo technology which enables high speed precision part manufacture with additional operator-focused benefits. Its HFE 3i, HG ATC and HG ARs press brake series encompass most bending scenarios from a manually-based scenario to a fully automated, robot-supported production cell. With the HG ATC series with automatic tool changer, complex parts in batch sizes starting at quantities of one can be produced whereas with the HG ARs even large series of highly complex parts (in unmanned operation) can be produced.

Its compact HFE-5012M2 press brake has been developed to offer fast and comfortable bending of small, medium-complexity parts. The machine provides 50 tonnes of bending force and a working width of 1270mm. The open height of the frame has been increased, enabling deeper box bending.

AMADA’s FAST (Finger with Active Security Technology) stop fingers can be fitted onto most new AMADA bending machines and feature an active safety device that limits risk to operators if they come into contact with moving parts, even at full speed.

The manufacturer also offers fully automated bending solutions and VPSS 3i software, which can simulate all processing operations at once.

Turkish manufacturer Baykal’s APHS range of press brakes is highly versatile. It has pioneered development of a fully automatic pole cutting and bending machine and boasts one of Europe’s largest facilities for sheet metal working and fabricating machinery.

Boschert GmbH has produced a dynamic hybrid-drive press brake, the Quick-Bend, with purported energy savings of up to 80%. Quick and nearly silent, the Quick-Bend only uses electrical energy during the bending process.

Based on the PROFI 28 CNC and PROFI 56 CNC press brake series, with bending lengths ranging from 1000 - 2200mm, the designers developed a press brake with an innovative electric-hydraulic hybrid drive, characterised by a combined E-motor and pump unit. The bending speed range is 8 - 10mm/s and repeatability is +/- 0.02mm.

Swiss manufacturer, Bystronic, has enjoyed success with its Xpert 40-tonne, one-metre ‘plug and play’ press brake with rapid bending speeds. This small press brake has already won a German design award and is designed for rapid, efficient parts production. It has also introduced the Xpert-80. A new Mobile Bending Robot is available for feeding the Xpert 40 and Xpert 80, turning it into a compact, automated bending cell for producing 3D sheet metal components. The automation module is wheeled in front of the press brake and simply interfaced to it in a plug-and-play arrangement. In minutes, users can set up the robotics and load sheet metal blanks for bending. Programming is carried out offline and ByVision Bending software allows data to be imported without interrupting the job currently in progress.

Bystronic has introduced a new app that monitors, in real-time, all processes taking place within its laser cutting machines, press brakes and water jet cutting machines. Called ByCockpit, the software fits neatly with the digital networking requirements of Industry 4.0. It evaluates the collected data and generates status reports, allowing factory output to be optimised. Bystronic and Lantek are also developing an MES software solution for sheet metal part production.

Other press brake models include the Xpert Pro, Xact Smart and Xpress.

CoastOne from Finland manufactures servo-electric press brakes with spindle drive, including controlled crowning, robot solutions for electric press brakes, electric threading machines and electric inserter machines with spindle drive. Its electric press brakes include the C, G and BULL series.
