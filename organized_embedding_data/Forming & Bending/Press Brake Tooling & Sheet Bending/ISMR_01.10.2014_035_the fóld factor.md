Marco Cappello, managing director of bending specialist Thalmann Maschinenbau AG, outlines the company’s strategy and philosophy to ISMR

Thalmann Maschinenbau AG is a pioneer in the manufacture of bending/folding technology, with a reputation for quality and excellence. The Swiss company offers a complete series of bending machines/folders as well as a range of equipment to support the lifecycles of the machines, such as original spare parts and technical support.

The one-man locksmith and metalworking business, founded by Otto Thalmann in 1948, has expanded to become a major mechanical engineering company with trend-setting ideas in the sheet metalworking sector; particularly so since the development and production of Thalmann bending machines in 1960. Since then, the company’s head office and production facility have been based in Frauenfeld, Switzerland.

Production and storage space has been increased to an overall area of 5500m2. The company pays particular attention to training young people by regularly offering a variety of plant, machinery and mechanical engineering apprenticeships. Marco Cappello, managing director of Thalmann Maschinenbau AG, outlined the company’s strategy and philosophy to ISMR.

## Swing folding technology

“The company was founded by Otto Thalmann in 1948 – he was a real inventor at heart who was not afraid of a challenge. He started designing and developing long folders at a time when folders were just 2m long for a sheet metal customer. In 1993, his three sons took over the company,” explained Marco Cappello, Thalmann’s managing director.

“Even today, with our drive shaft/torsion shaft technology (which is comparable to the 4-wheel drive of a car), we are still the only manufacturer worldwide to use this in our products. This technology ensures better accuracy, productivity and product stability. We have always focused our efforts on bending and folding technology and designed products according to customer needs – we have in-house engineering teams and can create specialised and custom solutions with our customers. Our aim is to be the technology leader in long folding technology with global distribution. We are on track to achieve this with our current expansion policy.”

Marco Cappello, managing director of Thalmann Maschinenbau AG

With the development of the first manually-operated, long-length bending machine in 1960, swing-beam bending technology became Thalmann Maschinenbau AG’s area of expertise. Simple, manually-operated machines were later followed by hydraulically-driven and combined machines for cutting and bending sheet metal. In addition to a wide range of standard types of bending machines, up to 18.2m in length and with a bending capacity for 3mm thick steel (400N/mm2), its experience also enabled it to develop and manufacture special machines, tailor-made for customer requirements, for sheet metalworking.

The machines are operated by modern CNC control panels which are continuously adapted to the latest technology. Many years of experience and collaboration with the same Swiss electronic company, REGATRON, has resulted in user-friendly, software-tested control panels.

## Customers and markets

Thalmann has closed some large deals recently, including one with a global organisation with facilities in the UK, Middle East and Asia. It supplied two large folder units to the company which is looking to expand its production base using Thalmann machinery.

“We also recently closed the largest deal in Thalmann’s history with a very large company in Australia, which already has 22 of our folding units. We sold over eight folders to be delivered over the next eight months. These folders will be used in the construction industry in Australia – mainly for roofing, wall cladding, flashing etc. This market is looking for quite sophisticated and customised façade designs, which is ideal for our double folder. Renovation, modernisation and project-based design is a key driver in this market and our products are suitable for this,” explained Cappello.

“We started supplying the Australian market about 40 years ago. Swiss companies moving to Australia, starting their own sheet metal business, asked us to supply Swiss machinery to them. Reliability for them was a priority. Over the years, we have installed over 250 units in Australia. There is also strong market demand in New Zealand, particularly in the Auckland area, for our products. There are a lot of housing and construction projects there right now and relatively little bureaucracy…”

Cappello believes that the basis of Thalmann’s success is the sophisticated level of technology that it offers. As he says, ‘it’s not always about price, it's about return of investment’.

“We are seeing very good market development and our order books are full right now. Orders are mainly coming from the US where there are good levels of construction. The double folder is the most requested system for that market, as it increases productivity. We have also had some good feedback from Germany, Austria, France and the UK and are in contact with several UK companies that need new equipment,” he explained.

“In general, we have to be positive about markets – they go up and down. As a manufacturer, we are present in the market and ready to supply when markets are ready to move. I am always positive – why invest energy in negative thoughts? Markets are driven by sentiment. The trick is to identify the market regions that do need equipment and not lose time trying to develop markets that don’t have those requirements at the moment…”

Our customers’ main concern is to have equipment that raises productivity whilst reducing labour costs

Approximately 80 per cent of Thalmann’s business is in the construction sector and 20 per cent is in industrial applications. One third of its business is in the US, one third is in Europe, 20 per cent in Australia/New Zealand and the remaining 20 per cent is spread across the world (projects in Africa, Asia etc.). It mostly works through global distributors, such as CIDAN Machinery in the US.

“Our customers’ main concern is to have equipment that raises productivity whilst reducing labour costs,” said Cappello. “That, again, comes back to our double folder. For example, in New Zealand, we contacted a company with eight shops across New Zealand. They sent us a video of two different products that they are manufacturing on a single folder with two people. They asked us to produce the same product on the double folder, on video, which we did with one person and in half the time. They worked out that they would achieve payback for the machine in 2.5 years.

“Another key aspect is health and safety – a fully automatic double folder does not really involve the operator in the production process. In New Zealand, there is a very strong accent on safety. The cost of industrial industries is staggering – the average cost of an injury is over £100,000…”

Strategically, Cappello will keep pushing Thalmann’s worldwide business to enter even more markets. He has seen single sales in Russia and India and is now pursuing a new cooperative agreement with CIDAN Machinery in China. His main focus over the next few years will be to penetrate these markets and gain customer recognition as a key supplier of equipment that drives their sheet metal business.

“We are not a mass manufacturer of sheet metal equipment – we are a supplier of high level customised sheet metal machinery. We will never be competitive on price but we do offer highly sophisticated machine tool technology that increases productivity and efficiency and saves overall time and cost,” he concluded.

## Trends and challenges

Process automation to reduce labour costs is a key trend that Cappello has identified. He also sees more demand for equipment that does not need a highly skilled labour force to operate. There is a shortage of skilled operators globally in the industry which calls for machine tools that are easy to operate and user-friendly. He points out that, after a few hours of instruction/training, Thalmann’s double folder could be operated.

“Our technology also allows customers to create new products and new geometric shapes so that they can pursue new business – customers ask for this as they want to create tailor-made products for their customers,” he told ISMR.

“Markets like China and India have huge potential for development. The challenge will be to introduce our new double folder technology into these markets and to gain market and customer trust for the technology. On 1 July 2014, Switzerland signed a free trade agreement with China which will support future business with China and mean reduced import duties for Swiss products being sold into the country. That is good news for us...”

Thalmann’s folding technology is suitable for industrial and construction applications as well as automotive and solar industries. A new and growing industry sector for Thalmann is sound protection against the noise from infrastructure developments e.g. sound protection walls along high speed rail tracks or motorways. Its double folder technology provides flexibility in the design of these products.

“We just started a cooperative agreement with a global partner, CIDAN Machinery in Sweden, that manufactures complementary products to us. We already have an agreement with CIDAN in the US. In Europe, we sell its portfolio in the Swiss market and it sells our products in its portfolio in Scandinavian markets. CIDAN set up a sales organisation in China about two years ago and we recently agreed to pursue the same strategy with it in China so we will be at the forthcoming Sheet Metal exhibition in China with CIDAN as sheet metal supplier partners. We also just started with a new dealer in the UK, near Manchester, called Metal Solutions,” explained Cappello.

## Products and technologies

Thalmann’s range of bending products include the THAKO folder, the ZR panel-beating machine, the TC and TD bending machines with two bending beams and the MAGNUM (cutting and bending up to 3mm sheet thickness).

The THAKO model, its flagship long-length folder, was primarily designed for ambitious façade and roof constructors as well for the industry sector. Individual selection of beam geometry, gripper systems for material handling, roll-former units as well as an insertion depth of up to 2000mm and machine lengths of up to 18.2 metres are just a few of the features offered by this model range.

## Above: THAKO 20 – 6.2m CNC single folder with slitter

The ZR model is a newly constructed, sturdy and fully hydraulic swing-folding machine (panel beater) with standard lengths of 3.2m (10.5 ft) up to 12.0m (39.37 ft); it was developed especially for sheet metal workshops. This model can be adapted to requirements with a variety of accessories.

The TD double folders also harness the advantages of double bending for large-scale operations of sheet metal, façade construction and general bending with an economical quality system – one bending beam above and one below enable bending from top-down as well as from bottom-up without having to rotate or turn the machined sheets. This facilitates the bending process and increases output. A safety light barrier and a laser barrier for the clamping beam and the slitter, or rollforming unit, are part of the safety concept for the TD double folder.

Thalmann’s TC double folder has two bending beams. Sophisticated profiles in façade and roof construction often contain z-bends. On conventional bending machines, these profiles must be repeatedly rotated and turned during the bending operation. With the double folder, the sheet metal is held by grippers which move the profile to its next position. Depending upon requirements, the sheet metal is bent upwards or downwards according to the pre-set angle.

Production and storage space has been increased to an overall area of 5500m2.

Thalmann drive shaft technology is a mechanical control system synchronising all mechanical axes using a massive steel shaft. It maintains constant operating pressure over the entire bearing length. This results in extremely precise bending. The double folder, said the company, achieves an accuracy of ±0.5° for a max. bending angle of up to 143° and requires just three seconds back and forth, thanks to the heavy duty hydraulics.

In addition to swing-folding technology, Thalmann AG is also introducing wedge cutting technology. This patented slitting system leaves neither scratches nor deformations.

At EuroBLECH, Thalmann will be launching various new developments. Dynamic forming technology (DFT) that it introduced this year offers new standards in performance. It also recently developed an interchangeable folding beam tool that offers more flexibility for modern sheet metal manufacturing. It is also about to launch a new control software generation.

## An eye on the future

Cappello also outlines Thalmann’s response to new control solutions using, for example, smartphone or tablet apps to control machine tools. “One of our New Zealand customers would like to have cloud-based capability on their machines – with products and profiles stored in the cloud and all the machines linked to this and able to exchange profiles with each other. They also want control of their machine tools through apps on iPads and smartphones.

“The technology nowadays has to be mobile and flexible – the time where you had just the machine and controller, as isolated units, is quickly disappearing. Major companies are driving for company-wide, interlinked solutions now…” ■
