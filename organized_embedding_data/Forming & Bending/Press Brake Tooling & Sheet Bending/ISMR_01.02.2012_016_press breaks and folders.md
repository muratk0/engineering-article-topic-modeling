# PRESS BREAKS AND FOLDERS

[TARİH: 01.02.2012 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS: “We spotlight some of the trends in today's bending markets”

Today’s sheet metal shops carry out bending operations using skilled workers, sophisticated machinery and, in some cases, robots attached to press brakes. Huge tandem press brakes can roll and bend long lengths of material to produce items such as lamp-posts, for example.

A lack of skilled human operators in the labour market and a constant focus on overall cost reductions to boost competitiveness and free up skilled resources for other work on the shop floor has led to more robotised press brake operations. The move to automation has enabled firms to start competing on price with overseas manufacturers with lower labour costs.

## The trend to servo

The new eP-1030 press brake by Prima Power is based on extensive experience in press brakes and servo electric machine tools for sheet metal working. The inherent benefits of servo electric technology include very low energy and maintenance costs, and a reduced impact on the manufacturing environment (minimum noise and vibrations). Servo drives help to shorten cycle times and the machine is productive and energy-efficient – the maximum power requirement of a 105 ton press brake is 6 kW. Energy is used solely during the actual bending process and only as much as required.

More and more fabricators are facing situations where large volumes are replaced by the need to produce small batches on a just-in time basis. Prima Power’s FastBend replaces the press brake with the automatic bending technology which allows more bends for each side in an automatic sequence without any manual intervention, using servo technology.
