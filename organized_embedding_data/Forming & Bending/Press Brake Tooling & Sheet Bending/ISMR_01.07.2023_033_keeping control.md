# KEEPING CONTROL

[TARİH: 01.08.2023 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest global press-brake bending systems; tooling and technology innovations; and outline new requirements.

Press brakes are used for the precision bending of sheet metals and form pre-programmed bends between the die and the punch. The process makes a variety of shapes (such as a 90° rib form; V bottom; channel; closing; double form; hat channel; offset etc.). A press brake bends metal by lowering a punch onto sheet metal that has been positioned on top of a die. The workpiece is clamped between matching punch and die to form a pre-set bend.

There are several types of press brakes (such as pneumatic, servo electric, hydraulic and mechanical). The bending length of a press brake is the maximum possible length of a metal sheet which can be bent. The press brake's bending force, known as tonnage, determines how much pressure can be applied by the punch during a bend. Higher tonnage allows for bending thicker and harder metals, and lower tonnage is most appropriate for thinner metals.

"Customisation is a key requirement for press-brake customers to produce components with different shapes, sizes and features with no need for redesign and set-up time. Smaller batches are becoming more prevalent, and manufacturers need to be able to switch from big to small runs with maximum flexibility without sacrificing productivity," commented Italian manufacturer, Prima Power.

Quality; speed; precision; flexibility; energy efficiency; automation; safety; sustainability; Industry 4.0 networking and ease of operation are key elements in today's press-brake designs. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new press-brake systems, tooling and technology developments in the global market.

AFH is the company's line of fixed height tooling designed to be compatible with AMADA fixed height tooling and machine control. Wilson Tools' Express CROWNING deflection control system helps to control deflection in press-brake applications of over four feet long.

The company's Brake Partner™ product allows users to form (punch) in the press brake. Wilson's ExpressRail™ m2000 hydraulic clamping system reduces set-up time with a wireless pendant control that clamps and seats press brake tooling in seconds, giving fabricators more open height and extended clamp life. Special or customised press-brake tooling is also available.
