# A STEP CHANGE

[TARİH: 01.06.2011 ISMR]

## Productivity gains meant an additional requirement for deburring at EBC.

Manufacturing over 1500 motorcycle disc brake rotors per week, EBC Brakes is constantly looking for productivity gains. However, one such gain brought with it an additional requirement for deburring.

The switch from machining the disc rotors to using laser to create the complex shapes and patterns that are practical and functional on modern motorcycle brakes created a product that had burrs and sharp edges. Whilst having no effect on the performance of the brakes, these burrs had to be removed for the health and safety of customers who would be handling the product and for aesthetic reasons. Initially, this deburring was done manually in between other operations, which posed a risk of injury in the handling of the discs and was also fatiguing and time consuming.

“It did not take us long to realise that we needed to find an alternative to hand deburring that would provide the quality of finish and an automated solution to drastically reduce the time being taken to deburr these rotors,” said Paul Britchford, EBC’s Production Manager. “We had seen the Timesavers machine on a visit to the MACH exhibition, so we invited Ellesco to put forward a proposal. It was confident that the Timesavers 42 series would handle the volumes and the application.”

The rotary disc pad ensures that every edge of a component is deburred in a single pass

## A SMOOTH FINISH

The Timesavers 42 series machines have a combination of abrasive belt deburring and rotary disc pad; linishing and deburring built into the same machine. This provides the ability to remove heavy burrs and/or linish the component using the belt, then breaking the edges, smoothing the surface and deburring using the rotary pad, or combining both in the same operation. With table widths of up to 1350mm wide, large volumes of parts can be fed through the machine at a time.

For further reassurance that the machine was capable of meeting EBC’s requirements, a consignment of brake discs was shipped to Timesavers in The Netherlands. These trials proved to be a success, with Paul Britchford remarking of the Timesavers machine: “You can put a part through the Timesavers machine and it will deburr and apply a small radius to every edge of the component in a single pass. At the same time, it leaves the flat surfaces of the discs with a lightly brushed finish, which makes them look much better than what we were achieving before.”

## The main reasons for the purchase were the elimination of sharp edges for safety and aesthetic reasons

ISMR SAYS

"EBC needed to find an automated alternative to hand deburring that provided the right quality of finish"

In what is a refreshing approach to investment in capital equipment, Mr Britchford states that EBC has not spent time poring over spreadsheets to analyse productivity gains following the installation of the Timesavers machine. The main reasons for the purchase were the elimination of sharp edges for safety and aesthetic reasons. Both of these aims were achieved virtually overnight.

The machine was delivered in November 2010. The investment was made ahead of a planned relocation of EBC’s entire manufacturing and distribution business in Northampton in the UK to a purpose-built 86,441ft2 factory, with an additional 21,380 ft2 set aside for further expansion.

“The new factory will create four times the existing manufacturing floor space,” said Paul Britchford. “However, this will not be ready to move into until August this year and we could not put off investing in new equipment until then. We are able to do this as we are still owned by Andy Freeman, our founder and managing director.

“EBC is very proud of the fact that its manufacturing is maintained in the UK and investment in equipment such as the Timesavers deburring system enables it to maintain the highest levels of quality, while remaining price competitive against overseas manufacturers.”
