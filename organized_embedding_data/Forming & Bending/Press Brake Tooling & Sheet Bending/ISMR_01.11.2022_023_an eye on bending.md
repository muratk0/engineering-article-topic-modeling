# An eye on bending

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

LVD's Ulti-Form robotic bending system handles small batch and long production runs, designed for high productivity and consistent bending accuracy. Ulti-Form offers a fast “art-to-part” process.

“CADMAN-B software automatically calculates the bend program and imports bending data to the robot software, CADMAN-SIM – no robot teaching is required. SIM automatically calculates gripper positions, generating a collision-free path for the robot, and relays the information back to B, enabling the operator to work with a single program,” said LVD.

“Our auto-adapting gripper accommodates various part geometries, automatically adjusting to the workpiece size. Its smart design saves investment in different grippers and gripper changeover time," it added.

The new Dyna-Press 60/20 electric drive press brake offers 60 tons of pressing force and two metres of bending length for the most capacity of any Dyna-Press machine. It bends small to medium-sized parts at high bending speeds of 25mm/s, producing more parts per hour at a lower cost per part. This model closes the gap between LVD's electric-drive and hydraulic press brakes, which start at 80 tons.

ISMR: There has been an increased move towards sustainability in manufacturing. How would you characterise your sustainability strategy?

MF: Companies must adapt and move with the times as things happen and respond to market forces. People are very focused on energy efficiency these days. If a user can see a higher machine throughput, with a wider mix of parts and lower power consumption, that means more sustainable solutions. It's not just about energy usage but using energy only when it is needed.

Throughput efficiency is also key in this equation. Over the last few years, the actual efficiency gain that you can make with power consumption on a fibre laser is better than previously thought. It brings us back to environmental awareness. There are many different elements to the sustainability equation.

Sustainability is our priority. Through advanced, digitally connected machinery, we help support economic sustainability for our customers. Programmes like our NexGen upgrade extend the useful life of our LVD press brakes, delivering as much as a 50 percent increase in machine productivity. In our production facilities, we promote the reduction of waste and encourage environmentally-sound practices which include the use of solar panels; the application of water-based paint and the reduction of electricity, water and heating oil by 30-50 per cent.

ISMR: What are your views on the business climate for sheetmetal professionals today? What about next year?

MF: Business has been very good for us this year. We have seen excellent growth from western European and American markets; not so much from

Asian markets. Australia, New Zealand and India have also been strong markets for us. Business has been very good in general. Everyone has been selling a lot of machines. However, the availability of parts and supply chain issues have also been a challenge for everyone. Our purchasing and engineering teams have done a great job in finding alternatives and staying dynamic, so that we can still deliver products.

In terms of the future outlook, we need to assess overall response at exhibitions like EuroBLECH. The footfall and enthusiasm levels have been very good for the first half-day of this show. Looking forward, we can see a positive outlook for the New Year. However, what will happen on energy costs, the Ukraine crisis and inflation is more difficult to gauge. All the national banks are trying to apply brakes to keep inflation under control. Despite these headwinds, however, the manufacturing industry in general has remained quite levelheaded.

ISMR: Can you identify any specific trends or opportunities in markets for you or your customers?

MF: The shortage of labour is a major issue, but also an opportunity, for everyone. Strategically, we have made some smart investments (e.g. in LRS and COMPAC) to combat this so we are well placed to respond.

The other challenge/opportunity is the ability to gain higher and more efficient throughput (hence shaping flows). How can we help our customers make the journey to the Smart Factory? A lot of small to medium-sized businesses now realise that Industry 4.0, automation and Smart Factory technologies add value to their businesses. That is a huge opportunity. We saw this trend at the beginning of the COVID pandemic, particularly in western European countries. That is why we are showcasing the cost-effective Smart Factory here. A Smart Factory does not need to mean 30,000 square metres and lights-out operation etc. You can start with a laser and a press brake to create your own Smart Factory......

However, margins are already tight and there is strong competition in the market so it is imperative for manufacturers to keep investing in technologies to keep their competitive edge. OEMs are currently reducing their stock inventories as they don't want to lock up too much capital. In fact, they just want their subcontractors to make the product for them so that they can ship it and get paid. Batch quantities are still small so there is more pressure on supply chains and, particularly, throughput supply chains. This is all moving manufacturers towards the need to ensure strong throughput and tight control of the software/process (using Big Data, for example).

Other challenges include shorter lead times and increased part complexity. Adapting processes, automation and the Smart Factory can meet some of these challenges.

We can see what is happening in markets.

We also experience the same challenges as our customers, as we are a manufacturer ourselves. We are trying, for example, to find and recruit people too so this gives us more of an insight.

ISMR: You have been taking your technology directly to customers with a mobile showroom. Tell us more about your Innovation Tour and any other new initiatives.

MF: Our Innovation Tour in North America has been a great success. Our customers love us bringing the technology to them. We have been using social media marketing to increase brand awareness of these tours via our mobile showroom on LinkedIn and Facebook.

The Innovation Tour worked particularly well during the COVID-19 pandemic. The manufacturing community was still very active, despite lockdown restrictions, and we had to adapt to the situation. We set up our mobile showroom at the customer's site and everyone comes out to look at the technologies. We can demonstrate systems to a customer's workforce and see all the operators this way, not just the ones that come to trade shows.

We are also doing more online training for our staff. We set up a knowledge hub during the pandemic, which we use for our sales team. They can now go to a central location where they can learn online and on-demand. It's all about keeping the knowledge in-house and disseminating it where it is needed.

ISMR: What can we expect from you next year?

MF: We are still enlightening customers on, for example, the benefits of robotised bending (which does not have to be for large-volume, specialist, high-cost parts). That is why you will see us launch new automated systems next year.

We are also planning more of a rejuvenation in the way that our Experience (XP) Centres are used. During the pandemic, we ran a lot of webinars on different topics and challenges. Different geographical markets exhibit different trends, so we are investing in bringing more automation systems into XP Centres in different regions. We set up a new subsidiary in Vietnam just before the pandemic hit. We can now put more time and resource into supporting the growth of that subsidiary. Vietnam is a very interesting marketplace for sheet metal.

We always assess new and emerging markets and are currently in the re-evaluation stage of assessing which geographical markets to focus on next year. For example, some of the Asian markets are taking a little longer to bounce back from COVID-19.

You will see us at the SteelFab exhibition in Sharjah, UAE, next January and at IMTEX in India. We will also be exhibiting at the CIMT show in Beijing next April, at Metaltech in Malaysia and at Intermach in Thailand. We will also participate in the MTA show in Vietnam and FABTECH in Chicago next year.
