## Scope

Metal bending results depend on the interaction of material properties, workpiece geometry, tooling, machine capacity, and the planned path of the part during forming. A machine should not be treated as a source of force alone: excessive force cannot correct unsuitable tooling, an impractical bend radius, inadequate support of hollow sections, or interference between the workpiece and the machine.

All setup, adjustment, capacity verification, and operation must follow the applicable machine manual, tooling documentation, workplace risk assessment, and qualified-person procedures.

## Material and Tooling Compatibility

Before bending, confirm the actual material thickness and identify the material condition and strength information available for the job. Compare the planned operation with the machine and tooling capacity information. Die opening, punch radius, material thickness, and material behavior affect the required force, bend radius, surface condition, and likelihood of cracking.

A die opening that is too narrow for the workpiece can increase forming load and force the material into an excessively small radius. This may contribute to thinning, surface damage, or cracking on the outer bend radius. Likewise, a punch radius that is too small for the material and intended bend can damage the workpiece. Use the machine and tooling supplier documentation rather than relying on a universal rule for die selection.

Material grain direction, actual thickness variation, and batch-to-batch behavior can affect bending results. A test bend made from representative material can reveal springback, cracking, surface marking, and angle variation before production parts are processed.

## Press-Brake Forming Principles

In air bending, the workpiece is supported at the two shoulders of a V-die while the punch applies force between them. The final angle depends on punch travel, tool geometry, and the material response. After unloading, material may elastically recover toward its original shape; this is springback. The amount of springback is not constant across all materials or setups, so it should be evaluated through controlled trials and documented process settings.

Bottoming and other higher-contact forming methods can require substantially different loads and tooling conditions than air bending. The selected method must match the rated machine capacity and the intended tooling application. Do not assume that a setup suitable for one bending method is suitable for another.

Long parts require additional planning because machine and tooling deflection can cause bend-angle variation along the length. A bend that is tighter near the ends and shallower near the middle may indicate deflection or an issue with tool seating and alignment. Inspection and correction of machine compensation systems, tooling support, or machine alignment must be performed only in accordance with the machine manual and by qualified personnel.

## Workpiece Clearance and Sweep Path

The full movement of the workpiece must be considered, not only the bend line. During a press-brake operation, a flange or free end can move through a large arc and may contact the machine frame, tooling, nearby equipment, or personnel. Check throat depth, backgauge position, tooling height, part orientation, and the complete travel envelope before starting the bend.

Keep people and obstructions outside the workpiece sweep area. Safeguarding around the tooling does not eliminate hazards created by a moving workpiece. If the planned part path creates interference, stop and revise the process plan using approved methods.

## Tube and Pipe Bending

Hollow sections behave differently from flat stock because the inside of the bend is subject to compression while the outside is stretched. Tube bends may show flattening, wrinkling, buckling, or cracking when the selected bend radius, tube dimensions, material condition, or tooling do not provide adequate support.

For rotary-draw tube bending, internal support tooling may be needed for some applications to help maintain the tube cross-section. The need for such tooling and its configuration depend on tube diameter, wall thickness, material, bend radius, and the required quality level. Mandrel selection and positioning are machine- and tool-specific tasks and should be set only by trained personnel following the relevant documentation.

Inspect forming surfaces for contamination, debris, galling, or damage that could mark the workpiece or affect material movement.

## Roll Bending

Roll bending forms a radius progressively through repeated contact with the rolls. Applying too much deformation in a single pass can cause localized kinks, slipping, polygonal curvature, overload, or surface damage. A controlled sequence of passes allows the operator to observe the material response and approach the required radius gradually.

The ends of a roll-bent workpiece may retain flatter regions because they are not fully supported by all forming rolls during entry and exit. Where end curvature is important, the process plan may require an approved pre-bending method. Springback should be checked during the operation using suitable inspection methods, since the released part may open beyond its in-machine shape.

## Defect Review and Process Control

Typical defects provide useful diagnostic evidence:

- **Cracks or tears:** may indicate excessive strain, unsuitable bend radius, unsuitable tooling geometry, or material limitations.
- **Wrinkling or tube collapse:** may indicate inadequate support, an unsuitable bend radius, or incompatible tube tooling.
- **Surface scoring or galling:** may indicate contaminated, worn, or damaged tool contact surfaces.
- **Angle variation along a long bend:** may indicate deflection, inconsistent tool seating, alignment issues, or material variation.
- **Unexpected springback:** may indicate that the material response differs from the assumed process condition.

When defects occur, do not compensate by simply increasing force. Stop, inspect the workpiece and tooling condition, verify the material and planned process, and use qualified personnel to determine whether a tooling, setup, capacity, or process change is required.

## Safety Essentials

Verify that the planned operation is within the rated capacity of the machine and tooling. Inspect the work area, tooling condition, and workpiece travel path before each run. Use representative test pieces where appropriate, maintain exclusion zones around moving material, and follow approved operating and emergency procedures. Never bypass safeguards or attempt unauthorized machine, hydraulic, electrical, or tooling adjustments.
