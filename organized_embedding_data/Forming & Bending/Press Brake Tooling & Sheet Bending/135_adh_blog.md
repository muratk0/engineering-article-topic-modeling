# Selecting Punch and Die Tooling for Press Brake Bending

Press brake tooling should be evaluated as a complete bending system rather than as isolated punch and die profiles. The punch, die, holders, clamps, adapters, machine geometry, workpiece material, bend sequence, and setup condition all affect whether a bend can be produced safely and repeatably.

## Tooling Functions and System Compatibility

In V-bending, the punch applies force to the workpiece from above while the V-die supports the sheet below. Punch tip radius, included angle, body shape, height, and rated load influence bend formation, clearance around previously formed features, and allowable loading. A punch selected only by nominal angle may not provide adequate clearance or load capacity.

The die opening influences the developed inside radius, required force, minimum supported flange, and potential for surface marking. In general, a wider V-opening tends to require less force and produce a larger natural radius, while a narrower opening may support shorter flanges but can increase loading, local pressure, marking, and cracking risk.

All tooling interfaces must be compatible with the machine's clamping arrangement, available daylight, stroke, working range, shut-height limits, and documented load ratings. Holders, clamps, and adapters are load-bearing parts of the system. The lowest rated loaded component governs the allowable setup.

For long bends, machine deflection can cause the center of a part to bend differently from the ends. Where applicable, crowning must be considered as part of the overall process validation.

## Bend Method Before Tool Selection

Air bending, bottoming, and coining have different contact conditions and force requirements.

- **Air bending:** The sheet contacts the punch tip and die shoulders without fully seating in the die. Bend angle is controlled mainly by punch penetration and material behavior. The marked angle of the tool does not alone define the finished angle.
- **Bottoming:** The workpiece conforms more closely to the tooling near the end of the stroke. Tool geometry, material thickness, and expected springback require closer agreement than in air bending.
- **Coining:** High localized pressure compresses the bend zone and reduces springback, but increases stress on the machine, tooling, and material surface.

Springback depends on material strength, thickness, grain direction, and normal material variation. Compensation should be verified with a controlled trial bend using the actual production material. Increasing force is not a general correction for an open air-bent angle; if the sheet begins to seat against the die faces, loading may rise sharply and the process can approach bottoming.

## Evaluating Die Openings and Part Geometry

For mild-steel air bending, the article identifies a V-opening of approximately six to eight times material thickness as an initial screening range. This is not a universal selection rule. The opening still must be checked against the required inside radius, flange support, nearby holes or webs, surface requirements, and documented load limits.

The article also presents screening estimates for air bending: an inside radius near 15% to 17% of the V-opening, and a minimum flange near six times material thickness. These estimates are material- and application-dependent and should be confirmed using approved tooling data and trial bends. Features near a bend, including holes, slots, and narrow webs, can stretch or distort; their suitability must be assessed for the actual part geometry and material.

High-strength materials may require greater force and may exhibit greater springback. Grain direction can affect bend ductility and cracking risk. Thickness variation changes the relationship between material thickness, V-opening, radius, penetration, and required force. Cosmetic surfaces may require additional measures to control contact marking.

## Selecting a Punch Profile

A candidate punch must satisfy three checks:

1. Its tip geometry can form the required bend.
2. Its body provides clearance throughout loading, bending, springback, and part removal.
3. The complete tool and mounting stack can carry the required load.

Straight punches are suited to open parts with adequate clearance. Acute punches may provide clearance for acute bends or overbending, but do not ensure that a material can tolerate a tight bend radius. Gooseneck profiles can provide relief for return flanges or box walls, although their geometry may reduce load capacity relative to a straight tool.

The smallest punch radius is not automatically preferable. A small nose can concentrate pressure and increase wear, indentation, creasing, or cracking risk. Collision review must include the complete motion and the removal path; a part that cannot be extracted is not an acceptable tooling result.

## Approval and Validation

Before production, verify four areas:

- **Geometry:** Required angle, radius, flange dimensions, and nearby features can be achieved through the full bending and removal path.
- **Force:** Total force and force per unit length are below documented ratings for the machine and every loaded tooling component.
- **Compatibility:** The tooling stack clamps, seats, aligns, and fits within the machine envelope, with adequate loading and removal clearance.
- **Condition and quality:** Working and mounting surfaces are clean and undamaged, segments are compatible, alignment is correct, and surface quality meets the drawing.

Required force should be determined from approved machine or tooling documentation using the actual material, thickness, V-opening, bend method, and loaded bend length. Setup, inspection, trial bending, and any adjustment must be performed by qualified personnel in accordance with the machine manual, tooling documentation, and site safety procedures. A controlled test bend is appropriate when springback, marking, radius, or final dimensions cannot be confirmed from documented process data alone.
