# BEND AND SHAPE

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

Greater flexibility, accuracy, safety and a focus on energy savings characterise many of the press brake models available today for leaner, greener bending operations

Today’s press brakes feature a raft of innovations from eco hybrid drive systems to automatic angle measurement, quicker tool change/set-up and more sophisticated sensing technologies. Press brakes can also handle smaller batches and, by integrating robotic and automation technologies, can deliver huge gains in production efficiency for metal bending operations.

Flexibility, accuracy, faster cycle times, lower noise levels and a greater accent on energy saving and overall safety also characterise many of the press brake models available today for leaner and greener bending operations. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new press brake developments in the global market.

## A focus on bending Japanese manufacturer Amada’s

comprehensive range of press brakes is designed for flexibility and productivity. With an extensive choice of tonnages and beam lengths, there is a variant to suit most requirements. Its energy-saving systems (inverter-controlled stop/start technology) reduce energy consumption when bending by 60% and, consequently, overall running costs.

Amada’s HG press brake features a patented hybrid drive system and a new ‘Smartphone style' 3D control unit. Its new Product Designer software can unfold 3D sheet metal models and automatically apply unfolding data and feature recognition ready for manufacture. Following impressive growth in Spain, Amada recently announced the opening of a new Technical Centre in Barcelona.

The company demonstrated three fully automated bending cells at the EuroBLECH 2014 exhibition in Hanover. It also showcased several bending innovations, including a new range of press brakes based on its traditional downstroking HFE range but in a 3i version (its latest 3D graphic control interface).

Turkish manufacturer Baykal’s APHS range of press brakes is highly versatile. It has been working on the project line to be used for the construction of the seven sports stadiums for the 2022 Qatar Fifa World Cup and has pioneered development of a fully automatic pole cutting and bending machine. Baykal Machinery completed the transportation of the line to Qatar in December 2014.

For its manufacturing operations, Baykal uses three factories which together combine a production area of 60,000 square metres, making it one of Europe’s largest facilities for sheet metal working and fabricating machinery.

Boschert Gizelis presented its new production range of CNC press brakes and CNC shears at EuroBLECH 2014, the ‘i-bend and ‘i-cut‘ respectively.

In October 2014 at the EuroBlech Exhibition in Hannover, Swiss manufacturer Bystronic showed its pre-launch Xpert 40-tonne, onemetre press brake with rapid bending speeds.

Three images above: Bystronic's Xpert 4-tonne, one-metre press brake

The machine was launched in the UK in March 2015 and, Bystronic told ISMR, 100 units have been sold worldwide. It has been designed as a ‘plug and play’ system - the machine can be moved by a fork lift truck, partly for offloading and installation but, more importantly, it can be moved to suit changes in a factory layout.

On the left and right of the operator’s legs are drawers which slide out to reveal lightweight segments of upper and lower tools. The operator can remain seated while quickly loading them from the tool drawers into and out of the machine. An optional bar code reader allows the program to be called from a bar code on a work sheet or from a QR code etched on the blank, should the owner have a Bystronic laser cutting machine. Energy consumption is extremely low due to the machine’s revolutionary design and the startstop technology employed in the drive train.

“Many customers that have ordered the machine are subcontractors and job shops making small components. Additionally, OEMs have been keen to free up capacity on their larger press brakes by installing the compact Xpert 40 to make smaller parts,” explained the manufacturer.

Ermaksan, a Turkish manufacturer specialising in the production of sheet metal working machines, has won three awards in two different international design competitions for both its EVO II hybrid press brake and Fibermak laser cutting machine.

“In conventional press brakes, energy is consumed even in standby mode but the Evolution II press brake only uses energy whilst it is bending. It can bend at a rate of 20 mm/sec. using servo motors and a highly efficient pump. It is also quiet – at 63dB noiseless and has high bending accuracy of between +/-0.01mm. These new generation press brakes are developed to require fewer oil changes (every five years),” Ermaksan explained.

Other Ermaksan press brake models include the SPEED-BEND (synchronized hydraulic press brake), the POWER-BEND PRO 3-axis CNC press brake and the ECO-BEND CNC hydraulic press brake. The company’s MICRO-BEND CNC hydraulic press brake is a small, productive hydraulic press brake with the capacity to bend 5mm mild steel with 1m x 40 tonnes of press force and one-axis back gauge. Ermaksan’s HEAVY DUTY press brake features a dynamic crowning system and 6-axis servo motor back gauge system. It also boasts special top and bottom tools and a 610mm throat depth.

Italian specialist Gasparini offers a press brake range that includes the X-BEND (press force from 50 to 170 tons) and the X-PRESS ECO. Its new Dener hydraulic press brakes were designed to meet a wide variety of needs, accommodating weights ranging from 40 to 2000 tons and offering from 3 to more than 11-axis capabilities. Its new electric MC press brake is leaner, greener and more reliable delivering quiet operation and faster cycle times.

The Diamond BB Series features a compact, ball screw design driven by an AC servo motor with 40 metric tons of force and one-micron repeatability. It utilises a dual-drive system with a high-speed back gauge, larger stroke and hydraulic power.
