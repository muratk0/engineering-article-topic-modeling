# Wedge-lock nut

[TARİH: 01.05.2016 ISMR]

TR FASTENINGS has introduced a new wedge-lock nut to its range. Developed and manufactured in Europe, the wedge-lock nut combines the locking ability of a washer combined with the ease of use of a flanged nut.

The wedge-lock nut is supplied preassembled with the nut and washer combined in a captive and rotary way, and uses tension instead of friction to positively lock the bolt in place. The principle of wedge-shaped cams, on the inside of the washers, and radial teeth on the outside results in increased clamping force being applied during periods of vibration or dynamic load.

The external radial teeth embed into the mating surfaces when tightened while the internal cams react to any movement in the joint by moving across each other. As the height of these cams is greater than the pitch of the bolt thread, the increase in the height of these moving cams increases the clamping force on the joint, thus causing the bolt to self-lock.

“The one-piece design eliminates the risk of misuse as the two component parts are fixed permanently in the correct position. The design makes the wedge-lock nut perfect for use in hard-to-reach locations and also offers considerable reductions in assembly time and effort. As well as being easy, safe and fast to use (even for non-specialist users), the nut is totally re-usable without any loss of function,” said the manufacturer. See www.trfastenings.com ■

## Laser and resistance welding systems

AMADA MIYACHI EUROPE highlighted a range of laser and resistance welding solutions for battery welding at the 10th Battery Experts Forum from 5-7 April 2016 at Stadthalle in Germany. On display was the NOVA3 Resistance Welding Workstation and JUPITER Fibre Laser Welding System for battery pack welding as well as New Horizon resistance welding modules for the resistance welding of battery tabs to coin cells.

“The MIYACHI EAPRO NOVA3 Resistance Welding System is suitable for battery pack welding in automotive and aerospace applications. All MIYACHI, MIYACHI PECO and MIYACHI UNITEK power supplies and weld heads fit the NOVA3 configuration, making it an extremely flexible choice,” said the company.

“The new Jupiter Laser Welding System is compact, reliable and energy-efficient. With high laser power, it offers faster welding. The Jupiter can interconnect lithium-ion and other batteries with metal tabs, and is the system of choice for larger production volumes and for larger batteries with heavier and more conductive tabs. It is the only system on the market that offers an integrated automatic combustion suppression system for safety handling defective batteries.”

Also on display was a range of New Horizon resistance welding modules for the welding of battery tabs to coin cells, including weld heads, power supplies and motor controls. See www.amadamiyachieurope.com/applications/batteries ■

## Edge preparation

TRUMPF’S TRUTOOL TKF series of bevellers, including smaller models such as the TruTool TKF 700 weighing 5.4kg, are not exclusively for shop use but are portable and can be used away from the factory (at a customer’s site, for example). TruTool TKF series machines are used on flat sheet, pipe and tubing applications to produce clean, oxide-free, K, V, X and Y edges.

“The TruTool TKF 700 can be used to prepare edges on sheet metal of up to 20mm thick in the vertical or horizontal position, or even overhead. It can either be guided by hand or mounted on a workstation for static use. The unit requires no extra tools to adjust the bevel height and sheet thickness, and comes with fixed angle cutter mounts that help users to produce bevel angles of 30°, 37.5° or 45° to suit their specific welding requirements. A quick-release lock allows three defined directions for tool carrier setting,” TRUMPF explained.

“With the TruTool TKF 700, users can prepare pipe with a minimum inside diameter of 80mm, while bevels can be produced inside cut-outs, starting at 90mm diameter, and curved edges with a minimum radius of 40mm. Angle grinders do not represent a real alternative to bevellers since the quality of the edge is unpredictable and thermal effects preclude bright cutting edges. This makes retouching necessary along with all the associated issues of time and cost.”

The largest TruTool TKF 2000 is used primarily for stationary work. Bevels can be created in mild steel or aluminium sheet measuring between 10 and 50mm thick. See www.trumpf.com ■
