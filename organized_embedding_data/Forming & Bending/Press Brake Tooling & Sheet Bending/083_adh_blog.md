## Purpose and Scope

Press-brake installation affects machine geometry, operating consistency, and safety. A machine may be positioned and powered while still requiring verification of its support condition, mechanical alignment, electrical supply, hydraulic condition, and control references. Installation should follow the model-specific machine manual and be performed or supervised by qualified rigging, electrical, hydraulic, and service personnel.

This note summarizes transferable installation and commissioning principles. It is not a substitute for manufacturer procedures, structural engineering review, or authorized service work.

## Assess the Installation Site Before Delivery

The supporting floor is part of the machine system. Its suitability depends on the machine’s mass, support-foot arrangement, operating loads, slab construction, reinforcement, and the supporting ground conditions. A floor that appears intact may still deflect under machine loading.

Before installation, review available building and foundation information and obtain an appropriate engineering assessment when floor capacity or dynamic loading is uncertain. Rigging personnel can place equipment, but floor load verification is a separate structural question.

Plan the machine location, access route, rigging method, required clearances, and service access in advance. Follow the manual’s requirements for foundation preparation, support points, anchoring, and allowable site conditions.

## Prevent Unintended Frame Loading During Placement

A press brake frame can be affected by uneven support, unsuitable shimming, or anchoring that pulls the machine into a stressed condition. The goal is stable, even support at the designated machine contact points without creating localized binding or twisting the structure.

Use only the support, leveling, shim, and anchoring methods authorized for the machine. Do not improvise support arrangements or force a machine into position by tightening anchors or adjustment hardware. The relationship between leveling supports, shims, anchors, and the floor can be machine-specific.

After placement and after any authorized leveling or anchoring work, allow for stabilization as required by the machine documentation. Recheck the machine rather than assuming that a reading taken immediately after mechanical work will remain unchanged.

## Establish a Mechanical Baseline

Geometric checks should use the reference surfaces identified by the machine manufacturer. Do not assume that every visible bed or cast surface is an appropriate measurement datum. The article emphasizes that the tooling interface and designated machined pads may be more relevant to production geometry than general structural surfaces.

Leveling and alignment are interdependent. Adjustment at one support point can influence readings elsewhere, particularly on equipment supported at multiple points. Therefore, alignment should be approached as an iterative verification process rather than as a one-pass sequence.

Use measurement instruments suitable for the required accuracy and maintain them according to their instructions. Measurements should be repeated after changes to support, anchoring, operating temperature, or production loading. If readings indicate persistent twist, abnormal ram behavior, or geometry that cannot be corrected through approved installation procedures, stop work and consult authorized service personnel.

## Electrical and Hydraulic Commissioning

Electrical connection, phase verification, grounding, hydraulic priming, bleeding, and pressure-related work present serious hazards. These tasks must be completed only by qualified personnel using the model-specific documentation, applicable electrical rules, and the site’s lockout/tagout procedures.

Before energizing the machine, verify that the supply characteristics, protective devices, grounding arrangement, and connection method match the manufacturer’s requirements. Incorrect motor rotation, unsuitable electrical connections, or grounding problems can affect safe operation and machine behavior. Do not use improvised electrical tests or modify wiring without authorization.

Hydraulic systems should be kept clean and inspected for leaks or damaged fittings. Air, contamination, incorrect fluid condition, and assembly defects can contribute to irregular motion or inconsistent results. Follow the manufacturer’s approved startup, warm-up, inspection, and service procedures; do not open hydraulic circuits, bleed valves, or alter hydraulic settings unless qualified and specifically authorized.

## Control References, Tooling, and Functional Checks

Automatic homing or control initialization does not replace verification of mechanical installation. The machine’s control system relies on physical reference conditions, sensors, encoders, and machine geometry. If shipping damage, binding, misalignment, or unstable support is suspected, do not attempt controller calibration or parameter changes as a substitute for mechanical diagnosis.

Tool installation, clamping, tool seating, reference setup, and zero-point procedures are model- and tooling-specific. Perform them only according to the machine manual and with appropriate safeguards. Keep people clear of the working area during commissioning and use approved methods for test operation.

Initial functional tests should use controlled conditions specified by the manufacturer. Evaluate results across the working length and under conditions representative of intended production only when the machine is approved for operation. Differences between low-load and higher-load results may indicate a need for further assessment of support, machine condition, tooling, or compensation functions.

## Reinspection and Escalation

Document installation measurements, observations, test conditions, and any changes made under authorized procedures. Recheck machine condition after early production use and whenever the floor, anchoring, tooling, operating temperature, or machine behavior changes.

Stop operation and contact authorized service personnel if there is unexplained uneven bending, persistent alignment drift, abnormal motion, leakage, unusual noise, interference, damaged components, or suspected structural or shipping damage. Do not force correction through additional shimming, tightening, controller offsets, or hydraulic adjustments outside approved procedures.

A documented mechanical and operational baseline helps distinguish installation-related conditions from faults requiring professional service.
