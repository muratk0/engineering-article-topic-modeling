# Responding Safely to Press Brake Electrical Trips

## Purpose of Protective Trips

A press brake may experience an electrical protective trip during operation. A tripped main or branch protective device indicates that a circuit has been disconnected and should be treated as a fault condition rather than simply an interruption to production.

The article identifies several possible contributing conditions:

- An abnormal incoming power supply, including a missing phase on a three-phase supply.
- Machine-wide overcurrent or overload.
- Voltage disturbances, such as a sudden surge in areas with unstable supply voltage.
- A fault associated with a particular control-power branch circuit when a sub-breaker, rather than the main breaker, has tripped.

These are possible categories of causes, not a complete diagnosis. The applicable machine documentation and electrical schematic are needed to determine the affected circuit and equipment.

## Immediate Safety Principles

Do not immediately restore power after a trip. A trip can be associated with electrical leakage, damaged components, or another unresolved condition. Re-energizing equipment before the cause is understood can increase the risk of equipment damage and electrical hazards.

Access to an electrical cabinet, investigation of supply conditions, circuit tracing, and any breaker reset must be performed only by qualified personnel following the machine manufacturer's procedures, the applicable electrical drawings, and site energy-isolation and lockout/tagout requirements.

A repeated trip is an indication that the underlying cause has not been resolved. Repeatedly resetting a protective device without diagnosis is not an appropriate corrective action.

## Diagnostic Approach

Qualified personnel should first establish whether the trip affected the main supply protection or a particular branch circuit. This distinction helps define the scope of the investigation.

For an incoming-supply concern, the investigation should consider whether the machine is receiving the expected supply and whether all phases of a three-phase supply are present. For a branch-circuit trip, the relevant electrical schematic should be used to identify the circuit served by the tripped protective device and the control components associated with that circuit.

The article notes that a machine may contain multiple individual control-power circuits, each protected separately. Therefore, a trip in one circuit does not necessarily identify the failed component; it identifies the circuit area requiring further examination.

## Restoring Operation

Power should be restored only after qualified personnel have determined, under the applicable procedures, that the affected circuit is free of the condition that caused the trip. If supply instability, overload, or a component-related fault is suspected, the cause should be evaluated before returning the machine to service.

For large or multi-system equipment, circuit investigation may require additional care because more than one coordinated system can be involved. Use the machine manual, electrical schematic, and authorized service procedures to guide the work.

## Key Practice

Treat electrical trips as protective events requiring diagnosis. Isolate energy as required, identify whether the issue involves the incoming supply or a specific branch circuit, investigate the cause through approved documentation and qualified personnel, and avoid repeated resets when the trip recurs.
