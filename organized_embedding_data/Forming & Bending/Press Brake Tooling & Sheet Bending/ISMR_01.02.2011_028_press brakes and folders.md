Sheet metal producer Darley in Eijsden, The Netherlands (currently owned by the Liet family) was taken over on 1 January 2011 by Nivora Holding in Lochem, The Netherlands. Darley, founded in 1934, is a leading Dutch producer of sheet metal working machines such as guillotine shears, press brakes and automatic bending and punching / cutting systems. The Nivora group also owns Safan in Lochem, also a producer of innovative sheet metal working machines, and BEWO Cutting Systems in Tilburg.

The amalgamation of the companies will create a strong producer in the Dutch machine tool industry. With more than 180 employees in The Netherlands and an annual turnover of more than 45 million euros, the group expects to grow quickly, nationally and internationally, due to synergies in development, purchasing and service.

## Delem recently introduced a number of new control solutions for press brakes

Delem recently introduced a number of new control solutions for press brakes, including the DA-41 dual-axis control, the DA-52 for conventional and synchronised press brakes as well as the DA-66W/DA-69W press brake controllers (with automatic bend sequence calculation and collision detection). Its VBend software maximizes machine efficiency and production output of press brakes.

Turkish manufacturer Ermaksan has launched a series of hybrid press brakes, dubbed Evolution, for near-silent working (63dB) and minimized energy loss when bending small parts.

Safan’s products include the E-brake servo electronic press brake (with the new Dual Drive 300T), the H-brakes and automated product solutions (APS) for automating bending cells. Safan also offers the E-Mate bending aid, angle measuring systems (such as the E-Bend L) and a range of shears. The Safan E-Brake Dual drive only uses energy when the upper beam is moving, generating potential savings of up to 50 per cent in comparison with conventional hydraulic press brakes. The single pulley drive used on machines up to 200 tons is extended to a double pulley drive which creates a very innovative and compact drive system. This new system is patented by Safan.

Safan’s I-Brake is an intelligent press brake with two robotic arms. Because the robotic arms are mounted on the frame horizontally to the left and the right, the floor in front of the press brake is free for the pallets containing the sheets to be bent. The SAFAN R-brake concept has all the advantages of the servo electronic concept on the E-Brake series, such as less energy consumption, low noise, no oil, high accuracy, no crowning needed, fast cycle-times and low maintenance costs.

Alongside its B2 hydraulic press brake range and portfolio of panel benders, Salvagnini now offers its new E2 electric press brake range.

“By using electric servo-drives, the E2 can eliminate idle times so as to reduce the bending time by up to 25 per cent,” Salvagnini told ISMR. “It can make energy savings of between 70 - 90 per cent and position the Y axes within +/- 0.002mm accuracy.”

In September 2010, production of Salvagnini’s E2 and B2 press brakes moved from Piacenza to Sarego.

For Euroblech 2010, TRUMPF bundled its automated bending products into a family called TruBend Cell and introduced a new member: the TruBend Cell 7000 which it dubbed ‘the world’s fastest system for the automated bending of small parts’. With an average cycle time of only 4 seconds per bend, it can replace two to three manual bending machines.

The components that make up this bending cell explain its speed: a TruBend 7036 Cell Edition, which is optimised for automated use, the BendMaster (15) bending robot, the LoadMaster Bend loading unit, a pallet system and tracks – all placed inside an enclosure that requires 21 square metres of floor space.

The TruBend 7036 Cell Edition has a press force of 36 tons and uses an electric torque engine to accelerate the beam. This makes it particularly dynamic and produces a maximum working speed of 50 mm/s. On-the-fly backgauges in a lightweight design help to quickly position the parts using intelligent sensors. And a press beam that can be divided into two 510-millimeter sections creates open space for the gripper. This makes it possible for the gripper to move to the right and left of each clamping element, reach into the machine and also place parts from behind – it no longer has to reach around.

Standard on the TruBend Cell 7000 is a pallet system in which up to 24 different blanks or 4,800 parts can be supplied at a thickness of one millimetre. The compartments can easily be retrofitted, are 200 x 200 or 600 x 400 millimeters in size and can accommodate removal of sheets from a stack up to 200 millimetres high. This makes the machine highly flexible when it comes to the automated bending of the most varying materials and geometries. As an option, the process of changing bending tools can also be automated using the new tool changer, the ToolMaster Bend. ■
