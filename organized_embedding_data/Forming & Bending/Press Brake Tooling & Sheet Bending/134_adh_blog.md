# Selecting Press-Brake Tooling for Aluminum Bends

## Start with Material Condition, Not Thickness Alone

Die selection for aluminum should not rely only on sheet thickness or a general V-opening rule. In air bending, the V-opening influences the natural inside bend radius, but the resulting radius also depends on alloy, temper, tooling geometry, machine behavior, grain direction, edge condition, and process variation.

A V-opening selected from thickness may be a useful preliminary estimate, but it does not establish that a particular aluminum condition can tolerate the resulting strain. Harder tempers can crack at radii that may be acceptable for more ductile material conditions.

Before tooling is selected, verify the complete material designation and relevant production information, including:

- Alloy and temper
- Actual thickness and product form
- Material lot or heat traceability
- Required bend angle and radius tolerance
- Rolling direction relative to the bend line
- Blanking method, burr location, and edge condition
- Surface-finish requirements

Material appearance alone is not sufficient to identify temper. Material records and approved identification procedures should govern.

## Establish a Feasible Bend-Radius Window

A forming process is feasible only when the drawing requirement and the qualified material limit overlap. The lower boundary should be the greater of the drawing minimum radius and the qualified minimum radius for the actual material condition. The upper boundary is the largest radius allowed by the drawing.

If the drawing calls for a radius tighter than the material can safely form, changing tooling alone does not resolve the conflict. A wider die may reduce strain but can produce a radius outside drawing limits. Conversely, forcing the material into a tighter radius can increase cracking risk. Such conflicts require engineering review rather than unapproved changes in punch penetration, material substitution, or forming method.

Where an allowable process window exists, avoid operating directly at the minimum qualified radius when drawing tolerances permit. Normal variation in material, tools, and machine response can reduce the achieved radius below the intended value.

## Match Tooling to the Qualified Radius

Air bending generally allows the inside radius to be influenced by the die opening. The relationship between V-opening and natural inside radius should be based on qualified shop data, tooling documentation, or controlled trials using comparable material and conditions. Data from another alloy, temper, or material type should be treated only as an initial estimate.

The punch radius is also important. A punch that creates a sharp groove or concentrated impression can become a local stress raiser even when the V-opening is appropriate. Select punch geometry that supports the required part shape without imposing an unqualified tight radius. Punch angle, clearance, springback compensation, flange support, part interference, and forming load must also be evaluated.

A wider V-opening can affect flange support, minimum flange length, bend allowance, setback, angular consistency, and the ability to extract the part after springback. The complete setup must remain within the approved capacities and operating limits stated in the machine and tooling documentation.

## Control Grain Direction and Edge Quality

Grain direction and edge condition can strongly affect bend performance. Bends parallel to the rolling direction may be less forgiving than bends across it. When part design permits, orient critical bends favorably and preserve rolling-direction traceability during blank preparation.

Inspect tensile-side edges, corners, and blanked features before bending. Burrs, rough sheared edges, laser-affected edges, punched edges, and corner damage can initiate cracks. Deburring and edge preparation should follow approved production requirements.

## Diagnose Cracking and Surface Damage

The location and appearance of defects can help distinguish likely causes:

- Cracks associated with a sharp punch impression may indicate localized creasing.
- Splits beginning at a burr, rough edge, or corner may indicate an edge-condition problem.
- Cracking that changes with grain orientation may indicate orientation sensitivity.
- Repeated microcracking despite adequate edge preparation and tooling may indicate that the achieved radius is below the qualified material limit.

Galling is different from tensile cracking. It involves aluminum transfer to tooling surfaces, which can create deposits and score subsequent parts at the same contact location. Cleaning, surface condition, approved lubrication, and suitable protective contact materials may help control galling and cosmetic damage. However, these measures do not increase material ductility or make an undersized bend radius safe.

If protective films, inserts, or nonmetallic contact materials are used, the complete forming setup should be requalified because these materials can change friction, support, penetration, and angular repeatability.

## Validate With Production-Representative Coupons

Tooling calculations should be confirmed with controlled coupons made from the intended production lot. The trial should use the planned grain direction, blanking method, edge preparation, tooling, lubricant or barrier, and forming sequence.

Inspect coupons for cracking, whitening, grooves, edge damage, pickup, protective-film damage, and contact marks. Measure the unloaded angle, inside radius, and flange dimensions. Do not use increased penetration as an uncontrolled response to springback; it can change contact conditions, increase local strain, and effectively reduce the bend radius.

Maintain setup records and requalify when significant inputs change, such as material lot, tooling condition, surface protection, lubrication, machine condition, or the appearance of cracking or pickup.

## Escalate Incompatible Requirements

If controlled trials show that the required geometry is below the qualified material limit, stop attempting to force the bend tighter. Possible engineering-controlled responses include changing the design radius, selecting a more formable approved material condition, reorienting the blank, revising the forming sequence, or redesigning local features.

Heating, annealing, score-and-bend methods, and multi-step forming can affect material properties, dimensions, traceability, and process consistency. They should be used only under an engineered and qualified procedure. Follow the machine manual, approved process documentation, and qualified personnel requirements for all press-brake setup, operation, and safety decisions.
