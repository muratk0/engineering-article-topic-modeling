# Big bending on electric tandem press brakes

[TARİH: 01.12.2025 The Fabricator]

Technology Spotlight

By

Dan Davis

The lifts on these tandem brakes help the operator process awkward parts efficiently and safely.

BLM GROUP USA

A fab shop had a job that required bending very long pieces of 0.25-in. high-strength, wear-resistant plate. The shop was interested in servo-electric technology, having seen it work successfully in tube bending machines, but wasn’t really sure if it would make sense in a press brake, especially one large enough to tackle a challenging job.

The shop asked BLM GROUP USA what it thought. The machine tool vendor had supplied other equipment to the shop, and this familiarity led to an open conversation about what electric press brakes could do in an application like this.

The direct-drive technology used in BLM GROUP USA press brakes is designed to deliver quick bending cycles, precision bends, and repeatability. A servo-electric torque motor drives a ball screw, which controls the movement of the ram. There are no gearboxes, which eliminates the possibility of backlash, and the absence of hydraulics results in fewer maintenance concerns and fewer worries related to inaccuracy due to oil temperature and pressure fluctuations. The direct-drive technology also is more energy efficient as it’s only consuming energy when the ram is engaged; hydraulic systems draw electricity even when idle.

Could the servo-electric technology deliver results on a part that seemed perfect for a large-tonnage hydraulic brake? Yes, but maybe not in the way most people might think.

Two Better Than One

"It’s possible for us to build bigger electric machines than the ones that we offer now [up to 330 tons], but the energy consumption is probably going to be above what you would expect with an electric press brake," said Israel Arenas, BLM GROUP USA product specialist for press brakes and flat sheet lasers.

With the idea of having one press brake long enough to accommodate the part off the table, BLM bending experts asked the fab shop if two brakes in a tandem setup might make sense. That way, the fabricator would get the length needed to form the part but still have two stand-alone brakes for the majority of the other forming work that dominated the work week. Gunar Gossard, BLM GROUP USA vice president of sales, said that the metal fabricating customer liked the idea because it addressed capacity issues in the bending department while also preparing the company for future growth.

"It really came down to a conversation about solving the problem," Gossard said. "We needed the length of the tandem brakes to handle the part, the tonnage to form the part, and the additional capacity to meet their everyday needs."

The Test of the Electric Tandem

The fab shop ended up purchasing a tandem setup comprising two 330-ton brakes, each with more than 10 ft. of bending length. Total bending length when they’re used in tandem is just under 21 ft. When used in tandem mode, the brakes can deliver 660 tons of bending force, sufficient to handle thicknesses up to ½ in. regularly, according to Arenas.

Gossard said the tandem arrangement gave the fabricator flexibility because the brakes also can work independently when the long material is not being formed. That posed a bit of a challenge, as BLM GROUP USA wanted to ensure safety and connectivity whether the equipment was being used in single or tandem mode.

"If I’m standing in front of the press brake on the right, I need to have something on the left-hand of the machine that stops the ram from causing a safety issue," Gossard said.

"It really came down to a conversation about solving the problem. We needed the length of the tandem brakes to handle the part, the tonnage to form the part, and the additional capacity to meet their everyday needs."

—Gunar Gossard, BLM GROUP USA

BLM GROUP USA came up with a sliding mechanism that moves into place when the brakes are being used individually. It contains the other side of the laser-based safety system. When engaged, the safety system can sense any obstruction around the punch tip and stop the machine to avoid impact. This section slides back when the press brakes are operating in tandem.

The operator doesn’t have to make too many adjustments when navigating between single and tandem modes. Arenas said a push of a button starts the transition into tandem mode, putting the sliding section into place and having the control shift to one master module.

"From a safety perspective, you still need to use two foot pedals," Arenas said. "If one of the foot pedals is not pressed, the machine will not run."

Arenas added that both brakes come with their own automated crowning technology, which automatically supports dies across the length of the press brake during the bending action. As bending occurs, the control is taking into account the crowning action, so that next time the same job or a job with similar characteristics is done, the machine will adjust to more closely deliver the expected results on the first try.

Even with the advanced technology, Arenas said it helps to have experienced brake operators, especially when working with abrasive-resistant metal alloys that exhibit aggressive springback when formed.

"They know the different techniques, such as holding the material at the bottom of the bend for additional seconds," he said. "That way you can reduce the springback."

Gossard added that the use of electric press brake technology in tandem setups is going to introduce a new level of efficiency in forming long blanks.

"With lifting arms, the ram speed, and all of the backgauge axes working together, you are going to see productivity gains," he said.
