# Protective Urethane Film in Press Brake Bending: Process Considerations

## Purpose of a Protective Interlayer

A urethane film placed between sheet material and a V-die can be used as a sacrificial protective layer when bending appearance-sensitive material, such as pre-painted, coated, polished, or otherwise easily marked sheet. The article presents the film as more than a lubricant: because it compresses around die shoulders, it can change the contact conditions between the workpiece and tooling.

This may help reduce visible marking caused by highly localized contact pressure, embedded debris, shoulder drag, or surface imperfections. However, it does not eliminate the need for clean tooling, appropriate die geometry, correct machine alignment, and a validated bending process.

## Film Changes the Forming Setup

Protective film occupies space in the die opening and can therefore affect the effective bending geometry. The article notes that a film layer may effectively reduce the working V-opening because material is present at both die shoulders. This can influence required forming force, inside radius, bend allowance, bend deduction, flange dimensions, and springback.

For this reason, a protective film should not be treated as an interchangeable afterthought. Any change in film thickness, film stiffness, number of layers, or mounting method should be evaluated as a process change. Production settings and flat-pattern assumptions may need verification through controlled trial parts.

Do not make controller changes, tonnage adjustments, or tooling modifications solely from generalized guidance. Confirm the setup using the machine manual, approved tooling documentation, applicable load calculations, and qualified process personnel.

## Material and Film Compatibility

The article states that both film thickness and hardness affect how the film behaves under load. A film that is too soft for the forming conditions may compress excessively or allow contact with the underlying steel die. A film that is too stiff or thick may provide limited cushioning and can alter bend behavior.

Material strength, sheet thickness, coating sensitivity, bend radius, die opening, punch geometry, and forming method all affect suitability. The article especially cautions that thick or high-strength material formed to tight radii can cause film to drag, bunch, split, or behave unpredictably in the die cavity.

Where film bunching, tearing, unstable part movement, or inconsistent bend geometry occurs, stop the process and review the tooling and forming method. A compressible interlayer may not be appropriate for every bend. In some applications, suitable bare tooling with an appropriate shoulder condition and radius may be a safer process choice. Selection must be confirmed by the tooling and machine documentation and by qualified personnel.

## Secure, Stable Installation

The protective layer must remain stable throughout the bend. If it shifts, wrinkles, or creeps into the die opening, it can change the effective geometry during production and lead to inconsistent flange dimensions, radius variation, surface damage, or scrap.

The article emphasizes avoiding loose or uncontrolled film placement. In practice, any retention method must be compatible with the tooling, machine guards, clamping arrangement, and operating procedure. Do not improvise mounting, taping, clamping, or under-tool retention methods. Such work can create pinch, entanglement, ejection, tooling-damage, and machine-capacity hazards.

Use only installation practices authorized by the machine and tooling manufacturers, and have setup work performed or approved by qualified personnel.

## Inspection During Production

Protective film is consumable and should be inspected regularly rather than replaced only according to an arbitrary bend count. The article identifies visible changes near the die shoulders, including whitening, thinning, tearing, permanent deformation, or loss of elastic recovery, as signs that the film may no longer provide consistent protection.

Inspection should also include the workpiece surface and bend geometry. Watch for recurring drag marks, coating cracks, uneven radii, flange-length variation, film fragments, or changes in part movement. These conditions can indicate film wear, debris, unsuitable tooling geometry, excessive loading, misalignment, or an unstable setup.

If film damage or inconsistent parts appear, stop the run and investigate before continuing. Replace worn consumables only through a safe, approved setup procedure, with the machine isolated as required by the applicable safety instructions.

## Process Planning

For cosmetic bending, protective film can be considered one option among several for managing tool-to-part contact. The decision should account for the required surface finish, material properties, bend geometry, expected production volume, setup repeatability, inspection requirements, and availability of suitable tooling.

A documented setup sheet can identify the approved protective material, tooling configuration, inspection points, and criteria for stopping production. This supports repeatability while recognizing that protective layers can alter the mechanics of bending and must be validated for the specific job.
