# Safety Principles for Lifting Press Brakes and Handling Tooling

## Scope and Risk Awareness

Lifting a press brake or changing heavy tooling involves more than handling the stated weight of an item. Load balance, center of gravity, sling geometry, equipment condition, travel-path clearance, and communication can all affect the lift. Full-machine relocation and routine tooling changeovers should be treated as different tasks with different hazards, planning requirements, and authorized personnel.

A press brake may have an offset center of gravity because of installed components such as the ram drive, backgauge, or electrical equipment. Its geometric center should therefore not be assumed to be its lifting balance point. Sudden motion, load swing, or improper sling configuration can add dynamic forces and create instability.

## Pre-Lift Planning

Before any lift, use a documented lift plan appropriate to the load and workplace. The plan should confirm:

- The machine or tooling weight, center of gravity, and approved lifting arrangement.
- Manufacturer-designated lifting points and any restrictions on handling or transport.
- Rated capacity of the crane, forklift, slings, shackles, spreader devices, and other lifting accessories, using the applicable load charts and required safety factors.
- The effect of sling angle and load distribution. Sling legs must not be assumed to carry equal load without verification.
- The condition and identification markings of lifting equipment. Equipment with damage, corrosion, illegible identification, or other rejection conditions must be removed from service according to the applicable inspection requirements.
- Clearance above, beside, and below the load, including building services, doors, uneven surfaces, floor capacity, and underground features where relevant.
- A controlled exclusion zone and a communication method with one designated signal person.

Do not improvise lifting points by attaching slings to moving machine components, hydraulic cylinders, guards, or frame areas not approved for lifting. Use only lifting points and handling arrangements identified in the machine documentation.

## Machine Relocation and Rigging

Complete press-brake lifts require qualified rigging personnel and equipment operators. Where a spreader device is specified or needed to maintain suitable sling geometry and prevent side loading, it should be selected and used by competent personnel. The lifting device and all attachments must be compatible with the intended load and configuration.

A controlled test lift can be used to verify balance, clearance, and security before travel. Raise the load only enough to assess its behavior, then lower it if tilt, rotation, interference, or other unexpected movement is observed. Any correction to sling arrangement, balance, or rigging should be performed only after the load is safely supported and in accordance with the lift plan.

Forklift handling introduces additional stability concerns because a large machine may have a high or offset center of gravity and may obstruct the operator's view. Forklift suitability must be determined from the actual load center, attachment configuration, and capacity data rather than from the nominal machine weight alone. Follow the forklift and machine manufacturers' instructions for fork entry, securing, transport direction, travel speed, and load height. Use a spotter where visibility or travel conditions require one, and keep people clear of the travel path.

## Tooling Changeover Safety

Tooling changes should begin with machine isolation and securing of hazardous motion. A stop control alone may not provide protection against unintended ram movement or stored energy. Follow the machine manual and site lockout/tagout procedure. If work requires access beneath a raised ram or other suspended component, use the approved mechanical support or safety device specified by the manufacturer and workplace procedure. Do not rely solely on hydraulic pressure to support a suspended load.

Assess tooling size, weight, shape, and handling features before removal or installation. Use suitable lifting aids or purpose-designed tooling handling equipment when manual handling presents a dropping, pinch-point, or ergonomic risk. Do not use unapproved cables, improvised clamps, or homemade lifting devices. For long or segmented tooling, prevent uncontrolled movement and secure individual sections as they are installed or removed.

Keep hands out of pinch points between tooling, clamps, the ram, and the bed. Verify that tooling is properly seated and retained using the applicable machine and tooling instructions before operating the press brake. Machine setup, clamping, alignment, and operating parameters must be verified by trained personnel following the manufacturer manual; this note does not replace those procedures.

## Communication, Exclusion Zones, and Emergency Response

Only one designated signal person should direct a lift, and operators should act only on that person's signals except for an emergency stop. Establish a clear exclusion zone: nobody should stand beneath a suspended load, in its likely drop path, or in a position where they could be trapped by load movement.

If a load becomes unstable, tilts, or begins to tip, personnel should move clear rather than attempt to restrain it manually. The equipment operator should respond according to the approved lift plan and workplace emergency procedure.

For crush injuries, trapped limbs, damaged rigging, or stuck tooling, stop work, secure the area, and contact emergency services and site emergency responders. Do not attempt improvised release, repair, striking, disassembly, or machine intervention. Recovery and equipment inspection should be directed by qualified personnel under the applicable emergency and manufacturer procedures.

## Key Principle

Safe lifting depends on planning, approved equipment, inspection, controlled movement, communication, and competent personnel. When the load, rigging arrangement, machine condition, or work environment is uncertain, stop the task and obtain direction from qualified rigging and maintenance personnel.
