# Time to get Xcited

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

The Xcite was the next EuroBLECH highlight from Bystronic. The new electrically-driven press brake is ‘a fast and economical way to high-quality bent parts’, says Bystronic. In concrete terms, it told ISMR, it is some 2.5 times faster than a hydraulic press brake. Using the Fast Bend+ safety system, the tool can be positioned very close to the metal sheet without the operator being placed in any danger. Only then does the machine switch over to bending speed.

Bystronic’s proprietary Force Dynamic Drive (FDD) enables it to bend within a tolerance of plus/minus 25 arc minutes and with repeatability of plus/minus 0.0025mm. The use of the ByVision control package for laser cutting and water-jet cutting machines has been extended for use with press brakes.

The new ByVision graphical interface for press brakes was demonstrated at EuroBLECH and a new voice control for Bystronic’s Hammerle 3P press brakes was also announced. Using a headset and microphone, an operator can give up to 15 commands to the press brake.

Lightly manned future production means that machines must become more autonomous – power and performance will become even more important.

ISMR: How do you view the European market next year? What is your view of the current ‘euro crisis’?

FT: I find it fascinating and positive that customers and consumers do not appear too affected by this ‘euro crisis’. In general, when the media starts talking about crisis, people start saving money. I have not seen this happen in many Western European markets so I feel that a lot of this is media and politics. Consumers are still spending money in countries such as Germany, UK and Switzerland. I get no feeling that consumers are deeply affected by this in western Europe. Of course, countries in southern Europe like Spain, Italy and Greece have been affected but other Western European markets have not been greatly affected. China has certainly been affected because there are fewer orders from Europe to fulfil.

It is the first time we have experienced this situation. German consumers are still spending and the economy is good. It seems to be more of a political issue and is certainly set to become a deep social issue, with very high levels of unemployment in countries like Spain.
