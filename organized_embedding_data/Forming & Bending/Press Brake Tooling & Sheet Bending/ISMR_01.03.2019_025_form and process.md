# FORM AND PROCESS

[TARİH: 01.03.2019 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of some of the latest tube processing innovations from around the world.

Tube bending involves metal forming processes used to permanently form pipe or tubing. Bending processes include form-bound and freeform, as well as heat-supported and cold forming. Factors such as material wall thickness, tooling and lubricants used all affect the bending process. Generally, round stock is used in tube bending. However, square and rectangular tubes and pipes may also be bent to meet job specifications.

Form-bound bending, such as press bending or rotary draw bending, are used to form the work-piece into the shape of a die. Straight tube stock can be formed using a bending machine to create a variety of single or multiple bends and to shape the piece into the desired form. These processes can be used to form complex shapes out of different types of ductile metal tubing. Freeformbending processes, such as three-roll-push bending, shape the workpiece kinematically so that the bending contour is not dependent upon the tool geometry.

Growing requirements from industries such as oil & gas; pipelines; refineries; power & water; food manufacturing; electrical; marine & offshore; furniture; construction & infrastructure and heavy industries & manufacturing have increased the need for bent tube stock. In this article, we highlight a selection of some of the latest global tube processing innovations, including cutting and bending.

## The need for flexibility

Czech manufacturer, 4ISP s.r.o., presented a professional HSG fibre laser for cutting metal tubes, pipes and profiles up to 210mm in diameter and 6500mm in length. The machine features fully automatic feeding of the processed material, as well as pneumatic chuck, automatic zoom and electromagnetic clutch with impact protection.

The AICON BendingStudio software platform now allows for the integration of a ROMER Absolute Arm with Integrated Scanner to quickly determine complex tube and wire geometries using 3D laser scanning technology at almost any point in the production process. The latest BendingStudio 5.0 offers a new evaluation strategy for the determination of the bending model. It provides benefits particularly for the measurement of components with flat bending angle (< 3°), bend-in-bend-geometries and bends larger than 180°. The latest version is compatible with all TubeInspect models and the ROMER Absolute Arm.

In May 2018, BAE Systems – one of the largest defence contractors in the world – chose AMOB as the supplier of its new tube bending cell. The eMOB-100 fully-electric CNC pipe bending machine was installed at the company's pipe shop in Scotstoun, Glasgow, to bend all the piping on the Type 26 frigate – which is used to hunt submarines. As the system included 49 different tool sets, the installation and training team was with BAE for just under two weeks.

The eMOB-100 covers a range of sizes from 21.3mm x 1.6mm to 88.9mm x 7.1mm. For BAE, the material ranged from extra heavy-duty carbon steel to stainless steel through to 90/10 copper nickel. The multi-stack capability of the machine allowed BAE to produce 2D and 3D bends in a single length of tube - something it did not have the capacity to do before. The eMOB-100 also enabled a new processing capability in variable radius roll bending - something that BAE has already been utilising and incorporating into the design of the Type 26.

BLM Group, a specialist in the metal tubeprocessing sector, showcased its LS5 (sheetmetal cutting) and LC5 (tube cutting) systems at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover (October 2018). Both systems offer material shuttling modules for loading and unloading and automated vertical storage lift systems for flat sheet and tube.

"Modular configurations, high-range functionality and exceptional performance are key characteristics of the LS5. To maximise throughput and expand capability, an automatic tube processing line can be integrated into the LS5 system to create LC5, a powerful combined system for tube and sheet metal laser cutting. It is unlike any system on the market," BLM Group told ISMR.

The systems are compact to reduce floor space requirements and minimise installation times. They offer a wide-access work area (for ease of use and maintenance) and are designed to be modular and versatile, with adaptable configurations, interconnected, with the ability to connect to other systems and communicate to process management systems in an Industry 4.0 environment.

The LC5 tube processing system is an automatic system that is able to cut tubes in diameters of up to 120mm. Starting from a bundle, single tubes are automatically loaded processed and unloaded one after another as finished pieces. The tube processing module is autonomous from the sheet metal processing module, only sharing the laser cutting head. It uses an independent operational panel to control the entire facility during tubeprocessing. LS5 and LC5 are offered with a fibre laser source of up to 8kW.

At EuroBLECH 2018, BLM also showcased its LT-FREE, a 5-axis laser cutting system for the processing of curved tubes and 3-dimensional deep-drawn components as another way of portraying the conjunction between sheet and tube. Different automatic loading and unloading options, including complete bin picking detection, make this system particularly flexible and suitable for any type of manufacturing.

In July 2018, Marcegaglia Group completed the acquisition of 80% of the capital of Novero Spa, a Turin-based company specialising in the production of cold-drawn tubes. The takeover of Novero Spa, which has a turnover of about 20 million euros and 80 employees, is part of the plan for the core business of Marcegaglia group, a steel processing specialist.

The partnership between Marcegaglia and Novero, which will keep Massimo Novero as CEO of the company, will see the formation (in three years) of "Marcegaglia-Novero Spa" with production of more than 20 thousand tons of cold-drawn tubes per year, compared to the current 12.5 thousand, with a turnover of more than 40 million euros.
