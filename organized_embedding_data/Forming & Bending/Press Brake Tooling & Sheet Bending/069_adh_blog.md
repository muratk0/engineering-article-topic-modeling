# Press Brake Bending with Urethane and Steel V-Dies: Surface Protection, Load, and Process Control

## Surface marking in steel V-dies

During air bending over a steel V-die, sheet material moves over the die shoulders as it enters the V-opening. On polished, coated, painted, anodized, or otherwise cosmetic surfaces, contact at these shoulders can create visible witness marks, scratching, burnishing, or coating damage. The risk depends on the surface condition, die condition, contact geometry, material, forming load, and cleanliness of both the tooling and workpiece.

Steel tooling provides a fixed support geometry and is generally suited to processes where dimensional repeatability and a defined bend geometry are primary requirements. However, it may not be appropriate for every visible finish without additional surface-protection measures.

## How urethane changes the forming contact

Urethane pads or urethane-lined tooling can reduce localized contact with rigid die shoulders. Under load, the polymer deforms around the workpiece and punch, distributing contact over a broader area. This can reduce abrasive marking and imprinting on sensitive surfaces.

The same deformation that protects the surface also changes the forming system. Unlike an open steel V-die, a urethane setup has elastic resistance that depends on the pad and its installation. Pad hardness, thickness, degree of confinement, temperature, prior loading history, and penetration depth can all affect its response. Therefore, a urethane setup should not be treated as a direct, unchanged substitute for a steel V-die.

## Load and machine-capacity considerations

Press-brake tonnage charts for conventional air bending in steel dies may not represent the load required by a urethane arrangement. The machine must form the sheet while also deforming and displacing the elastomer. Resistance may rise as penetration increases, particularly when the pad is substantially constrained.

Required force must be verified for the specific material, thickness, bend length, punch geometry, pad specification, retainer arrangement, and intended bend method. Do not rely on generalized percentage increases in tonnage. Review the press brake's rated capacity, tooling limits, load distribution, and applicable operating instructions before production.

Avoid attempting to compensate for uncertain angles by progressively increasing force or penetration without an approved process. High loads and force spikes can create risks for tooling and machine components. Machine manuals and qualified personnel should govern tonnage verification, setup approval, and any evaluation of capacity margins.

## Accuracy and repeatability

A steel V-die has a fixed opening and shoulder geometry. In contrast, the effective forming geometry of a urethane pad can vary as the pad deflects. This may affect bend angle, inside radius, springback behavior, and response to ram-depth adjustments.

Pad response can also change during a run. Temperature, cycle rate, and accumulated deformation may alter elastic recovery. Repeated loading can create localized permanent set or wear, especially in frequently used bend zones. A pad may appear intact while its changed geometry contributes to angle drift or variation along the bend length.

For this reason, urethane forming generally requires deliberate process monitoring. Use approved test bends, inspect angles and critical dimensions at defined intervals, and record the tooling and pad condition used for the job. Setup records should identify the relevant pad, its location and condition, the material and finish, and the validated production settings. Any adjustments should follow the machine manual and established shop controls.

## Selecting the tooling approach

The choice between steel and urethane tooling should start with the part requirement rather than a general preference for either method.

Urethane may be considered when visible surface protection is essential and the part can tolerate the resulting variation in contact geometry or bend radius. It may also be relevant where a compliant contact surface is needed to reduce marking on delicate finishes.

Steel tooling may be preferable where a specified tight radius, stable geometry, high repeatability, or efficient production is more important than cosmetic surface protection. If only minor witness-mark protection is needed, a lower-impact protective approach may be evaluated through approved shop procedures rather than assuming that a full urethane pad is necessary.

## Practical controls

Before production, define the surface requirement, allowable bend-radius variation, dimensional tolerance, batch size, expected inspection frequency, and machine-capacity margin. Inspect pads for indentation, localized wear, cracking, or permanent set, and remove them from precision work when their condition no longer supports consistent results.

Urethane tooling is best managed as a controlled consumable whose condition influences process output. Surface protection can be valuable, but it should be balanced against verified forming load, dimensional requirements, and the ability to maintain a stable, documented process.
