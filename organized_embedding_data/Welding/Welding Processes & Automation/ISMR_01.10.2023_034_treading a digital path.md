# TREADING A DIGITAL PATH

[TARİH: 01.10.2023 ISMR]

Harald Scherleitner, Business Unit Head and Global Director Sales & Marketing, Fronius Perfect Welding, outlined the company's strategic and technical direction, as well as the latest global welding trends, to ISMR.

ISMR SAYS:

“With over seventy years of welding experience, Fronius placed its focus at this year's SCHWEISSEN & SCHNÉIDEN exhibition in Germany on new product demonstrations, automation, sustainability and digital technologies.'

At the SCHWEISSEN & SCHNEIDEN trade show in Essen (11-15 September 2023), Austrian welding specialist Fronius focused on the major driving forces in welding technology at its booth. Fronius Perfect Welding presented key technologies to meet the global challenges of welder shortages, resource efficiency and health protection at the international trade show for welding technology. It is extending its welding portfolio to include several solutions designed to counter skills shortages.

"Our approach is to use digital tools to help ensure welding quality, reduce material usage and ensure ease of use and technological assistance both for beginners and professionals," Harald Scherleitner, Business Unit Head and Global Director Sales & Marketing, Fronius Perfect Welding, told ISMR at SCHWEISSEN & SCHNEIDEN.

He sat down with ISMR at the trade show to outline the company's strategic and technical direction, as well as the latest global welding trends, and highlight its latest new technologies.

ISMR: Which successes, achievements and important goals have you been able to achieve in the last two years?

HS: Fortunately, we weathered the COVID-19 pandemic well and used this time to strengthen our customer and supplier relationships. As a result, we were able to deliver systems and prove ourselves to be a reliable partner for our customers. Since then, we have launched several successful new products. We are currently focusing on issues such as skills shortages, resource efficiency and health protection. We are also convinced that the digitalisation of welding still offers a lot of potential to develop even better, simpler, smarter solutions for our customers so that they can unleash their full welding potential.

All the products that we launched in the past two years have been very well received by our customers. These include the high-end welding solution, iWave Multiprocess PRO, and TIG DynamicWire, two Exento extraction systems and the new Exento extraction welding torch, as well as flexible cobot solutions.

Strong interest has already been shown in our latest solutions, WeldCube Navigator and Dual Wirefeeder, as well as virtual welding training with the Welducation Simulator which we presented to the public for the first time here at the SCHWEISSEN & SCHNEIDEN trade show in Essen, Germany. We are focusing on automation as a solution provider, as we offer fully automated complete solutions from a single source.

We also hosted the Joining Smart Technologies Automotive Conference on 10-11 May this year. Experts from science and research met with automotive manufacturers and suppliers to discuss issues such as sustainability and digitalisation.

ISMR: How do you assess the current global business climate/markets for welding professionals?

HS: The shortage of skilled workers is noticeable everywhere and poses challenges for our customers. We have therefore been working for some time on solutions that can help with this. This starts with in-depth welding training and includes digital support during welding e.g. through digital welding sequence plans, easy operability, optimised characteristics and welding packages for the most diverse requirements.

We are also convinced that automation will prevail wherever it is useful and necessary. It is a solution to the shortage of skilled workers and frees up welding experts from routine work so that they can focus on other tasks. For example, we have easy-to-program cobots that quickly pay off, even in terms of smaller quantities. This also makes them interesting for SMEs.

Consultancy and excellent service continue to play a key role. Through our global sales and service network, we see how important it is to be close to our customers and develop alongside each other. The exchange and pooling of welding knowledge is valuable for both sides. We can also do this through our 24 subsidiaries, 59 representatives and numerous partners worldwide. We are also happy to make our expert knowledge available, for example in our prototyping centre where we search for the right solution for our customers for every challenge, from feasibility studies to the optimum welding solutions.

Digitisation is helpful as a support in many respects. On the one hand, to counter the shortage of skilled workers when it ensures quality and supports welding (e.g. by making it much easier to operate). ISMR: Which research and development areas are your current areas of focus?

HS: Fronius is known for pioneering world-firsts with which we have influenced the welding industry. To remain an innovation leader, we currently employ 840 experts in R&D (of which around 400 colleagues work exclusively for Perfect Welding) and hold 1,446 patents (1,016 in welding technology).

Since quality requirements are increasing everywhere, but the required welding specialists are scarce, one key focus of our development work is to find solutions to the shortage of skilled workers. Resource efficiency is of particular importance from an economic and ecological point of view.

However, all our technical solutions also take into account the health and safety of all skilled workers. We therefore place a clear focus on sustainability, health & safety and the shortage of skilled workers.

ISMR: Which trends have you identified in global welding markets?

HS: Automation, wherever it makes sense and is necessary, can and will provide relief from the growing shortage of skilled workers. For example, cobots can relieve skilled workers of routine tasks and free them up for other tasks.

Digital support is also becoming increasingly important e.g. weld sequencing. A digital welding sequence plan ensures consistent quality, shortens the learning curve and helps companies to transfer knowledge.

Welding data management & analysis systems ensure quality assurance and improve resource efficiency i.e. they are easy on the wallet and the environment. Reducing material consumption and optimising processes remain high on our agenda.

Metal 3D printing can be used flexibly and is resource-saving, offering a wide range of possibilities. Repairs can be done on-site and long waiting times for moulds or their modifications, for example, can be eliminated. Metal printing also means enormous flexibility in design and enables new geometries, which makes it very interesting for prototyping. Our CMT (Cold Metal Transfer) process already offers a very stable, low-spatter process with reduced heat input and we are working on further improvements.

Consultancy and services have been our strengths for years and we feel that we can support customers very well with our offers e.g. when our experts advise on production optimisation or carry out feasibility analyses or prototype production for our customers.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

HS: We are devoting more attention to the shortage of skilled workers, sustainability and health & safety, as we see both challenges and opportunities in these areas.

Skilled worker shortage: It will be important to attract skilled young people and especially women to the welding profession. That's why we are already starting with training and presented the Welducation Simulator to the public for the first time at the SCHWEISSEN and SCHNEIDEN trade show in Essen, Germany. This offers realistic, resource-saving and high-quality training to welders.

Trainees can use the Welducation Simulator to practice all three process variants (manual arc welding, MIG/MAG and TIG). Extensive weld analysis options help training participants to improve their technique and identify mistakes by letting them continually track their progress. Virtual training offers a safe, affordable and resource-conserving way to realistically simulate complex welding processes.

Health & Safety: Welding is one of the oldest and most traditional joining processes known to mankind, and it is hard to imagine industry and the skilled trades without it. However, the sophisticated interplay of metallurgy, physics and electrical engineering is also associated with certain hazards, from which all welders must be sustainably protected.

These include welding fume protection, UV and glare protection, flame and heat protection, and ergonomics. The protective measures that need to be taken depend upon the welding process, the base material and the filler metal. Those who weld with optimum protection and a high level of comfort will also achieve consistently high quality. The health of all welding specialists is therefore also a top priority for us. We pay particular attention to protection against exposure to welding fumes. We offer especially user-friendly and powerful extraction systems and welding torches.

At the same time, we are focusing on a high level of safety as early as the training stage with the Welducation Simulator.

We also offer a wide range of high-tech protective products such as safety helmets, mobile welding fume extraction systems, extraction torches and protective clothing.

Economical and ecological: Up to now, resource efficiency was exclusively an issue of saving costs. Now, saving time, materials and energy is also becoming a competitive advantage when it comes to being considered as a supplier for other companies or standing out from the crowd of providers. Sustainability is also becoming more and more relevant for an employer in terms of attracting good employees.

With the new Fronius WeldCube Navigator software, there is now a digital welding sequence plan available for TPS/i.

HS: We have mentioned the new Welducation Simulator for welding without risk. With virtual training, complex welding processes can be simulated realistically as well as safely, cost-effectively and with minimal impact on resources.

Our WeldCube Navigator is a real-time feedback solution for welding professionals. Faulty welded joints in the production process can result in enormous costs and have a negative impact on overall productivity and quality. The WeldCube Navigator software solution supports welders, step-by-step, in production. With the help of WeldCube Navigator's guidance and job presetting function, the training and familiarisation time of new welding operators can be shortened, minimising the risk of errors.

I would also like to mention our Dual Wire Feeder (two torches and two wire spools but only one welding system). By using the WF 25i Dual, welders always have the right wire at hand, regardless of whether there are two different filler materials or wire diameters. This means that they don't have to change wire spools, wear parts or torches. The operating concept behind the WF 25i Dual enables welders to switch the process line, filler wire and associated parameters in various ways.

## Cold Metal Transfer (CMT) process

The Fronius CMT (Cold Metal Transfer) welding process is used when joining different materials, for example steel and aluminum. Extremely low heat input and an exceptionally stable arc set CMT apart from conventional MIG/ MAG welding.

The digital process control detects a short circuit and then helps to detach the droplet by retracting the wire: during welding, the wire moves forward and is pulled back again as soon as the short circuit occurs. As a result, the arc only introduces any heat for a very brief period during the arcburning phase.

The short circuit is controlled and the current is kept low, resulting in spatterfree material transfer. The arc length is detected and adjusted mechanically. The arc remains stable, no matter the surface of the workpiece or how fast the user welds. CMT, says Fronius, can be used everywhere and in every position.

CMT welding is suitable for joining steel and aluminium, as the galvanised steel plate is wetted by this brazewelded joint while the aluminium melts. The process is also suited to the virtually spatter-free brazing of hot-galvanised and electrolytically galvanised sheets with a welding wire made from a copper-silicon alloy. Minimal distortion of the galvanised sheet occurs and the light-gauge welding of aluminium sheets (from 0.3mm) is also possible, whereby the low heat input eliminates the need for pool support.
