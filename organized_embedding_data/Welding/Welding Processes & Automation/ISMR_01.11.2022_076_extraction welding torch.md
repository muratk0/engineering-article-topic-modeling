# Extraction welding torch

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

The Technical Regulations for Hazardous Substances (TRGS) require welding fumes and harmful substances to be extracted at the point of origin to comply with workplace limit values (AGW) for hazardous substances. Efficient solutions for welding fume extraction are therefore necessary to protect employees from harmful welding fumes.

"Our extraction welding torch system for automated welding offers safe and effective extraction of welding fumes directly at the source. Up to 95 per cent of the fume is directly eliminated during welding at the point of origin near the arc. Employees benefit from clean air and a better working environment," German welding specialist, CLOOS, told ISMR.

The measures required for collecting, extracting and filtering the flue gases in robotic systems often mean large collection hoods with curtains, complex pipe systems and a large filter unit to extract and clean the polluted air. When extracting directly at the welding torch, the volume of polluted air is much smaller.

"By using the extraction welding torch system, users invest significantly less in extraction technology, air ducting system and filter device—with the same effect. A flexible hose with a small diameter replaces the complex pipe system for discharging the contaminated air to the filter unit. Due to the smaller volume of contaminated air, a smaller filter unit is necessary. Because of optimised energy efficiency, as well as minimised effort for cleaning and replacement of the filter components, operating costs are considerably reduced," added CLOOS.

The available geometries of the extraction welding torches correspond to the geometries of the standard welding torches in use. Extensive correction of the robot welding programs is not required so that, according to CLOOS, "existing robot systems can be retrofitted with the extraction welding torch system with no great effort."■
