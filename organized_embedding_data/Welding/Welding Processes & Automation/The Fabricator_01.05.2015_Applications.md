Shipyard increases capacity with welding portal

SITUATION

Located in North Kingstown, R.I., Senesco Marine LLC, a Reinauer Transportation company, is a shipyard for new construction and vessel repair. Comprising 26 acres of Narragansett Bay waterfront property, the small but efficient shipyard can produce an array of vessels for its customers’ needs.

Since 1999 the shipyard has been building steel vessels to regulatory standards with strict compliance to contracted delivery time of vessels. The shipyard has built and delivered on-time nearly two dozen 60,000-, 80,000-, and 100,000-barrel double-hulled fuel and chemical barges with accompanying ATB tugs.

Welding of double-bottom structures takes plenty of hours; the work is dirty and very tough for welders. To make the job more productive and ergonomic, the shipyard decided it needed automated welding. Also, it required more capacity to build these vessels at an increased pace to meet customer requirements. In a continuing build program for current customers, the shipyard wanted to look at new work for potential new customers as well.

RESOLUTION

The company determined the only way to increase throughput was to get a flexible gantry that would improve productivity and reduce labor-hours. It purchased a PEMA

®

WeldControl™ 200 Vision in Welding portal from Pemamek Oy with a twin robot welding gantry for the welding of double bottoms and subassemblies.

"We are making the investment … to increase the throughput, thus reducing the time frame required to complete these vessels, which leaves room for even more opportunities," said Michael J. Foster, vice president and general manager of Senesco.

The system offers user-friendly programming for the robotized welding of subassemblies and open-block structures. With two Yaskawa robots and Lincoln Electric Power Wave

®

power sources, all welding systems are integrated in the welding portal.

Truck engine manufacturer achieves tooling cost reductions with 3-D printing

SITUATION

The Volvo Trucks engine plant in Lyon, France, produces various engine types and sizes for the Volvo Group, including Renault Trucks, which the group bought in 2001.

The company was producing metal tooling, including clamps, jigs, supports, and toolholders, to manufacture the engines. The design and manufacture of some of the metal tools took 36 days from start to finish. In an effort to increase flexibility and efficiency and uphold delivery times, the company began to look for a tool manufacturing method with a faster turnaround time.

RESOLUTION

Volvo Trucks decided to incorporate additive manufacturing technology at its Lyon facility. It purchased a Fortus 3-D production system from Stratasys reseller CADvision; within a threemonth period, Volvo Trucks had 3-D printed more than 30 different production tools from ABSplus thermoplasic to facilitate the way its production line operators work.

"We’re working in the heavy-industry sector, so reliability is naturally critical. So far every piece that we have 3-D printed has proved to be 100 percent fit-for-purpose," said Jean-Marc Robin, technical manager. "This is crucial from a practical aspect, but also instills trust among operators and quashes any traditional notion that everything has to be made from metal in order to function properly."

According to Pierre Jenny, manufacturing director at Volvo Trucks, the company has reduced the time required to design and manufacture Truck engine manufacturer achieves tooling cost reductions with 3-D printing certain tools traditionally produced from metal from 36 days to just two days using the 3-D printing system. Turnaround times for assembly line manufacturing tools have been reduced by more than 94 percent. These gains in time are improving the production plant’s overall efficiency and flexibility; delivery times are upheld, and the use of additive manufacturing has saved costs by reducing waste.

From a financial perspective, Jenny estimated that the total cost of 3-D printing ABS thermoplastic items is, in some cases involving the production of customized or small tool quantities, about $1.13 per 0.06 cu. in. as compared to up to $113 per 0.06 cu. in. for manufacturing the same items from metal.

According to Robin, developing production tools using additive manufacturing also enables the equipment design team to be more responsive while avoiding possible waste in the event of last-minute design changes before tools are made.
