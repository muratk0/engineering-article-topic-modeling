Xtreme Fabrication to expand in Kentucky

Xtreme Fabrication has announced plans to add 7,200 sq. ft. to its facility in Leitchfield, Ky. The project, which includes the purchase and installation of a CNC plasma cutting machine and traditional CNC machining equipment, is expected to create 25 jobs in the coming years.

Xtreme is a metal fabricator and machine shop producing specialty parts for manufacturers throughout the nation. Services include machining, welding, plasma and metal cutting, metal forming, and equipment repair. The company currently employs 30 people in Leitchfield with 3,500 sq. ft. of office space and about 20,000 sq. ft. of existing production space.

Lerman Enterprises purchases Welded Tubes Inc.

Lerman Enterprises, South Bend, Ind., has announced the purchase of Welded Tubes Inc. (WTI), Orwell, Ohio, a producer of high-volume, small-diameter, tight-tolerance tubing for the automotive industry.

WTI joins Lerman Enterprises’ Lock Joint Tube Co. (LJT), a manufacturer of mechanical and structural-grade steel tubing. Together, the two companies will expand their size range and customer base. Currently LJT produces AHSS and DP grades, but not to the magnitude of WTI. Lerman Enterprises’ Steel Warehouse will source and process the steel.

Coremark Metals increases processing capabilities with tube laser

Coremark Metals, a Minneapolis provider of metals, fabrication services, and hardware, has expanded its production capabilities with a Mazak Fabri Gear 150 tube laser. The machine is designed to cut, notch, etch, bevel, add holes, and complete intricate cutouts or designs in one continuous step.

"The tube laser has eliminated the need for secondary processing and replaces manual processes with automated ones," said Steven Lachermeier, chief production officer. "Jobs that are processed on the tube laser come out with a clean, flush finish, ready for assembly. A significant amount of time is saved when secondary finishing processes become obsolete."

The tube laser is equipped with 3D cutting heads; 6-axis programming; and automated load/unload, feed, measurement, and profile centering functions.

Calstrip Industries installs Braner/Loopco multiblanking line

Calstrip Industries has installed a Braner/Loopco CNC cassette leveler flying shear CTL/multiblanking system in its Blytheville, Ark., coil processing center. The system has capacity to convert 30-ton by 72-in.-wide bare and coated carbon steel, aluminum alloy, and stainless coil in gauges to 0.135 in. into sheets and precision blanks.

The multiblanker is equipped with a cassette-style CNC roller leveler, PVC and interleaved paper applicators, precision quickset multiblank slitter, flat passline servo-driven flying shear, on-the-fly product inspection station, hands-off reject system, and automatic skid injector.

Schoeller Werk adds tube sawing center

Schoeller Werk, a manufacturer of longitudinally welded stainless steel tubes, has added an automatic sawing center at its facility in Hellenthal, Germany. The system is rated to the requirements of the automotive industry and includes an integrated tube end machining system.

The system is used to process tubes from 8 to 10 mm dia. They are cut with a double cut and then brought to the tube end machining center, which contains a fully automatic deburring station with defined chamfering of the tube ends.

Eight high-resolution cameras perform automated optical inspection of the tube ends. The tubes are washed directly inline with a double washing cycle, dried, and automatically packed.

Survey shows aerospace manufacturing executives fast-tracking automation technologies

Grid Raster Inc., Mountain View, Calif., a provider of cloudbased XR platforms that power augmented reality and virtual reality experiences on mobile devices for automotive, aerospace, and defense companies, has announced the results of an aerospace industry survey that illustrates how manufacturers are using more automation technology to deal with COVID-19 social distancing, staffing shortfalls, and the need for overall efficiencies in production.

The online survey, commissioned in July by Grid Raster, shows that 42% of respondents are implementing AR/VR technologies over the next 12 months, and 51% are beginning to research the potential for AR/VR to make future plans and implementations.

Twenty-eight percent of those polled said COVID-19 weighs heavily on their decision to fast-track these plans, and another 39% have already begun the process as a result of the pandemic.

The majority of aerospace companies that have made implementations indicated they are achieving a 15% to 20% increase in efficiency and a 5% to 10% increase in overall cost savings.

Aerospace companies are using AR/VR technologies the most in ARassisted workforce on production lines (83%); AR-assisted maintenance and customer visits (75%); employee training programs (53%); and AR-assisted design processes for aerospace engineers (30%).
