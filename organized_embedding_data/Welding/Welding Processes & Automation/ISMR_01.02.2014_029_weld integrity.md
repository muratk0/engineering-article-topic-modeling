# WELD INTEGRITY

[TARİH: 01.02.2014 ISMR]

WELD CLEANING machines serve an important part of the market. The latest on the market operate using a carbon-fibre brush system, which involves dipping the brush into a cleaning solution. As with a welding set, the circuit between the brush and the object being treated needs to be closed by attaching a cable to the stainless steel item.

An alternative system from Anopol, employing the same electrochemical principles, is available and this operates using a torch with a tungsten tip. The tip is covered by an absorbent pad and the cleaning solution is fed to the pad by a low pressure pump, incorporated in the machine’s housing. A continuous flow of cleaning solution allows continuous cleaning of the weld area (Fig. 1). Some of these machines are also fitted with in-built fume extraction as an added safety precaution.

“Weld cleaning machines have a number of good attributes; fewer health, safety and environmental concerns due to the use of fairly innocuous cleaning chemicals. Unlike areas treated with pickling pastes, no chemical etching occurs, so that welds on polished items can be treated without greatly affecting the polished surface. The downside is the initial outlay of several hundred pounds,” commented John Swain of Anopol, who sells both weld cleaning machines and pickling pastes.

“However, there are certain applications where pickling pastes offer some advantages over weld cleaning machines. They are particularly effective for long weld runs on large fabrications, as the paste can be applied and left on overnight to remove the weld burn if required (Fig. 2). A spray-on version of pickling paste can be used on fabrications too large for immersion in a pickling tank (Fig. 3). This treatment can also be applied to on-site items, too large or too heavy to transport to a pickling facility. The final result is indistinguishable from a fabrication that has been immersion-pickled,” he added.

Both weld cleaning machines and pickling pastes can be used to remove weld oxidation from TIG welds. For MIG welds, some mechanical treatment may be necessary to remove the resulting tenacious oxide layer by grinding the weld area. A wise precaution, to ensure corrosion resistance, involves treating the welds with a pickling paste after grinding.

“Clean welds will self-passivate when coming into contact with water or oxygen in the air. The resulting passive layer is essential for the corrosion resistance of stainless steel. A weld that has not been cleaned does not offer the same protection. By employing the right treatment and carrying it out correctly, the fabricator can guarantee the integrity of the welds on the fabrication he has produced,” concluded Swain. ■

## PRODUCT REVIEWS

## MAGNETIC PULL

GOUDSMIT MAGNETIC Systems B.V. recently expanded its range of demagnetising systems by launching a series of large format demagnetising tunnels. The tunnels, with the corresponding demagnetising generator, have been developed and tested in-house and are intended for products with a diameter of 2000mm.

“Residual magnetism causes problems in welding with the result that the quality and quantity of work is greatly diminished. By making use of the recently developed demagnetising tunnel, the production process can proceed uninterrupted. The first series of these Giga demagnetising tunnels is intended for the demagnetisation of gas transport pipes in Russia,” the company explained.

The new demagnetising tunnels are also suitable for small connected, long or endless products such as steel wire. This is due, in part, to the 100% duty cycle combined with a demagnetising generator. For these continuous processes, the demagnetising tunnels can be supplied with a conveyor belt or a roller belt with an optional speed regulator. www.goudsmit-magnetics.nl ■
