Noncontact 3D sensor system tackles battery tray weld inspection

Situation

Based in Germany, Waldaschaff Automotive is a producer of high-voltage battery housings, also known as battery frames or battery trays. The manufacturing process is a challenge, given the size of the battery housing and the complex process steps it takes to combine the individual components into the finished product.

The battery housing is a complex structure with seams that are difficult to reach and inspect. The subassembly has three laser welds and 22 MIG welds, the ladder frame has 48 MIG welds, and the final assembly has 38 laser welds and 28 MIG welds—all of which need checking.

Resolution

To tackle this challenge, Waldaschaff chose the Josy system from SmartRay, a high-speed, allin-one unit for noncontact 3D visual inspection of joining technologies and weld inspection. It incorporates precalibrated 3D sensors with intelligent software tools and advanced algorithm processing to deliver high-accuracy weld inspection that meets international standards.

The company installed 16 of the systems across four production lines on different battery housings. In total, about 200 MIG and laser welds are checked, enabling the company to manage the welding process proactively and preventively, saving time and material through a more efficient rework process.

The inspection machine’s integrated, scalable operating system and intelligent, intuitive software tools have been specifically developed for the assessment of weld quality, capable of classifying typical welding defects. SmartRay not only delivers its own developed sensors for weld seam inspection but also provides a complete software package for evaluating the weld seams.

"The SmartRay system enables us to inspect 100% of the weld seams on each component objectively, optically, and in a norm-based way, and this is essential to secure our quality," said Klaus Steigerwald, head of customer unit at Waldaschaff Automotive.

In addition, rework is steered through the systems, making the rework process much more efficient and saving time and material.

Beyond this, the included management dashboards help to enable sustainable process optimization. The user can see an evaluation of all welds over time, including the most vulnerable welds as well as the most defects in the system. With this information, the welding process can be steered and countermeasures taken. And the system stores all the data about weld quality and rework to secure warranty and recall scenarios.

To overcome the challenges with weld seam accessibility, SmartRay used its complete sensor portfolio to ensure the reachability of all the seams, using sensors with working distances from 60 to 150 mm. The high reflectivity of the shiny aluminum can pose a challenge for optical inspection systems, but the system’s complex reflection filters eliminate almost all reflections.

All customer requirements have been transferred to the system. Operators no longer have to separate 100% of the components for rework from the line, as the system displays the relevant defects, significantly increasing efficiency. In addition, variations in the welding process are recognized early by the system, allowing appropriate countermeasures to be taken.

Aerospace supplier expands capacity with second deep draw hydroforming press

Situation

In 2011, aircraft interior systems manufacturer Aerosud Holdings Pty. had a bottleneck in the production of aluminum canisters for the Airbus A320. The canisters were drawn to 6.5 in. deep through a six-step annealing process, which often resulted in high scrap rates due to excessive thinning.

Beckwood worked with Aerosud to transfer the production of these aluminum canisters to a Triform 16-10-7DD press. Material thinning was reduced to 10%, production costs were lowered by 25%, and scrap rates reached less than 1%. In 2023, Aerosud was presented with an aerospace manufacturing contract that required redundancy in its manufacturing capabilities, and the company once again enlisted the help of Beckwood Press—this time to expand its forming capacity.

Resolution

To ensure that it would be able to fulfill the contract agreement, and given the success of the A320 canister project, Aerosud placed an order for a second identical press, along with two spare parts packages. The packages ensure parts are readily available on-site for both presses for redundancy to reduce any potential maintenance-related downtime.

Just like the press that Aerosud has used since 2011, the new machine has a maximum chamber pressure of 10,000 PSI, a 16-in. bladder size and forming diameter, a 7-in. depth of draw, and maximum punch tool diameter of 12 in. With this new press, Aerosud is able not only to fulfill its contract obligations but also increase part production overall.

Before obtaining its first Triform machine, Aerosud had no prior experience with sheet hydroforming. With Beckwood’s straightforward user interface, customizable recipe menu, and recipe storage, Aerosud was able to quickly pick up on the ins and outs of deep-draw hydroforming and incorporate the machine into its manufacturing process.
