# SEAMLESS OPERATION

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

With the production of supply pipes for umbilicals, the Vallourec Corporation has not only expanded its product line but developed a new and innovative manufacturing process for this special type of pipe

In its production facility at Venarey Les Laumes, in the heart of France, Vallourec Umbilicals demonstrates how to use laser welding to produce pipes with high mechanical characteristics to reduce the wall thickness. With an 8kW CO2 laser (ROFIN DC 080) and a Profile Welding System (PWS) by ROFIN, this factory produces approximately ten metres of laser welded pipes per minute.

## Underwater pipeline production

Vallourec is a specialist in the manufacture of high quality steel pipes that are used primarily in the oil and gas industry. In just a few years, it has developed a new branch in the oil industry. One specialised area is that of “subsea umbilicals” i.e. underwater pipelines that serve as conduits for various media such as supply lines, cables or fibre optics.

Umbilicals are an essential component of underwater installations and also serve to transport fluids, supply power or the transfer of information between offshore platforms and their control station. Deployed at depths of several thousand metres, it is obvious that these supply pipes have to be extremely robust but little is known about how they are actually made. In 2011, the group founded its subsidiary, Vallourec Umbilicals, dedicated exclusively to the production of special pipes that are conducted through the interior of the umbilicals.

As they have to meet extreme demands, pipes that run through umbilicals are usually produced seamlessly. Their manufacturing costs consist of 50 per cent material costs and 50 per cent production costs. Using the laser welding process, material as well as production costs can be reduced besides the improvement of technological properties.

## From the coil to the finished pipe

The first challenge was the selection of the right material. Pipes that are conducted through media such as sea water require materials with an extremely high corrosion resistance. Rustproof Duplex steels, i.e. stainless steels with equal parts of austenite and ferrite, offer high corrosion resistance combined with great strength characteristics. With its special alloy composition (25 per cent chromium, 7 per cent nickel, 4-5 per cent molybden and 0.3 per cent nitrogen), Super-Duplex steel has suitable characteristics to combat the corrosive media of the offshore environment. The welding process takes care of splatters and porousness to avoid affecting tube quality.

Extremely high demands on these special stainless steel pipes are also reflected in the complexity of the production line and, when Guy Durand (head of R&D at Vallourec Umbilicals) explained the individual production steps, there is a definite sense of the levels of commitment and energy which have been injected into this project.

During the first production step, the material for the continuous process comes directly from the coil – individual coils are joined together using WIG welding. After the edge preparation by a scraping process, the stainless steel band is run through the moulding of the roll former before reaching the laser welding area.

“To achieve the required weld quality, the subsequent welding process requires the positioning of the laser beam to be exactly in the middle of the welding seam. The ROFIN laser welding system, consisting of an 8kW CO2 laser and the Profile Welding System (PWS) is equipped with a sensor to detect the welding gap position, the Weld Sensor. Thanks to the Weld Sensor, even the tiniest deviations in the gap position and the welding position are detected and corrected, keeping the laser beam precisely positioned at all times and thereby achieving the highest level of process reliability,” explained the ROFIN Macro Group.

After welding, an in-line seam heat treatment ensures a uniform microstructure.

With completion of the final brushing process, the welding seam is no longer visible to the naked eye.

The entire manufacturing process – from the coil to the finished pipe – is controlled by Vallourec with the help of various non-destructive methods of measuring as well as an intelligent in-line quality process sensor system. In case of any quality impairment, the respective pipe sections are marked, cut out and re-joined using orbital welding before being rewound on a coil as continuous pipe. A pressure test of up to 2000 bar (2,000,000 hPa) concludes the quality control process.

The finished pipes have diameters in the range of 16-40mm and wall thicknesses ranging from 1-3mm.

With the production of supply pipes for umbilicals, the Vallourec Corporation has not only expanded its product line but has equally developed a new and innovative manufacturing process for this special type of pipe. Laser welding has proved to be the best method to provide continuously welded pipes of extremely high corrosion resistance and high mechanical strengths. Last but not least, the intense cooperation and collaboration between all project partners has meant further consideration of the integration of further laser processes into the production line. ■
