# Agricultural Equipment Manufacturer Decreases Training Time with Welding Performance Management System

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 The Fabricator]

Applications

SITUATION

Almaco, Nevada, Iowa, is a manufacturer of custombuilt, precision agricultural equipment such as combines, planters, and threshers for the seed research industry around the world.

Demands from the industry can result in varied and changing training requirements for the company’s welding operators. The high quality necessary in the company’s products also means rigorous training programs for newly hired welding operators to reduce the occurrence of weld defects and rework. Training is important because so much of what the company manufactures—often smaller fabrication jobs on thinner materials—differs from what welding operators may be used to doing in past jobs.

Training for the welding operators has involved eight hours a day of weld coupon work for several days, with a training technician coming around to check on the welding operators every 30 minutes to inspect their progress and give feedback. The company was looking for a training method that would be more efficient, involving less waiting and saving money in training time and coupons.

RESOLUTION

Almaco began using the LiveArc™ welding performance management system from Miller Electric Mfg. Co. The welding training system combines a simulator and live arc welding to deliver arc-on welding experience.

"Our interest in LiveArc was the ability to use it as a training tool to minimize weld defects—to make sure that our employees have the fundamental skills necessary to produce quality welds to build a quality product," said Chris Mann, senior manager at Almaco.

Welding operators now get immediate feedback on their performance, both in the simulation and live welding modes. The instant feedback allows them to correct issues sooner, improve key performance parameters, and improve their welding fundamentals compared to when they had to wait for one-on-one time with a training instructor.

"The welders … can understand from the visuals [on the computer screen] what they need to correct," Mann said. "The biggest attraction for us is the fact that this is live, and there is a simulation mode in which they can go through and run it without welding first."

That way, when they do the live weld, they have already corrected the issues they may have encountered, which saves time and welding coupons.

The company has seen a significant reduction in the amount of time it takes to transition a welding operator from training to production. In addition, this new training process reduces the amount of time the training technicians need to interact with newly hired employees, giving the trainers more time to address their other job responsibilities.

The cost savings have carried over into the production area as well. "We have seen a reduction in rework with weld defects, which has led us to our primary focus on the other skill sets that are required once they transition from training to production, such as their layout and fabrication," Mann said.

The company is able to format the data collected by the system to show how a welding operator’s performance improves throughout the training process.

"The great thing about LiveArc is we have the ability to customize our training to our needs," Mann said. "Regardless of job description, we can cover every aspect of what we need. Our line of work is custom—it’s not all fixtures; it’s not all benchtop work. We do a lot of out-of-position welding. Some of the most frequent fundamental errors that we have are in the out-of-position welding, so that was very important for us to be able to get accurate feedback to correct those."
