# ROBOTICS AND AUTOMATION

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

Neuweiler AG cut welding times by 40 per cent using the largest welding robot system in Switzerland.

It quickly proved its performance capability so a second IGM robotic system was put into operation on the factory floor, just three years later.

Neuweiler AG, near Lake Constance in Kreuzlingen (Switzerland), specialises in large, technically sophisticated custom products, often in small quantities, which are easily processed by the two welding robots.

"As the robots are so easy to program, they meet all our requirements for high costefficiency, even with a batch size of one," it said.

## Automated welding for greater efficiency

Seven years ago, when Neuweiler AG opted for a type RT 330 S welding robot from IGM, it was specifically planning to carry out automated welding on large-format workpieces. The robot works synchronously with an RWM 2 10,000 RCi L-shaped manipulator and an RFP 10,000A 1 counter bearing. Each has a load capacity of ten metric tons, so together they can manipulate heavy workpieces of up to 20 tons. The robot system is also equipped with a highspeed RWM 2 1,000 RCi L-shaped manipulator for small parts. A pit in the machine foundation makes it possible to manoeuvre even very awkwardly dimensioned workpieces without the need for a lift mechanism.

According to CEO Christian Neuweiler, this is the largest welding robot system in Switzerland. Based on current information, said IGM, it may be the only one in Europe so far to weld complete bogie frames for railway locomotives in a single clamping.

The bogie frames, measuring 3,382mm long and 2760mm wide and weighing more than a ton, are tack-welded and clamped into the L-shaped manipulator. With the manipulators and the robot moving simultaneously, nearly all of the seams can be welded in the ideal flat position. Many of the seam locations are very difficult to access. Nevertheless, the speciallyangled torch welds the circumferential seam on the connecting pipe of the longitudinal beam components in a single pass, despite having only 55 millimetres in which to work.

This can be done without the media supply lines on the robot arm becoming entangled because the front robot joint is designed as a hollow shaft. The entire media hose package, including control and sensor cables as well as cooling fluid, is fed through this hollow shaft. Running the media lines through the hollow shaft allows up to two complete revolutions of the torch on the robot hand joint.

Before the robot was introduced, the bogie frames were welded by hand, which required 20 hours per bogie for each welder. In contrast, the robot requires twelve hours. By reducing welding time by forty per cent, the robot significantly contributes to increasing productivity and efficiency. Its simple control system programming enables the system to be reconfigured for welding other workpieces within a short time. This is the deciding factor for working with small production batches or single pieces.

These were the benefits that led Neuweiler to install a second IGM welding robot system three years later. In this system, an RTi 496 S six-axis robot works with an L-shaped manipulator and a counter bearing that, with two tons of load capacity each, can manipulate workpieces weighing up to four tons. The longitudinal slide system, on which the robot moves, currently enables the welding of workpieces of up to nine metres long. It is also possible to attach an additional six-metre carriage module to handle correspondingly longer workpieces.

## Highly sophisticated workpieces

As with the bogie frames, all weld seams undergo a comprehensive visual inspection (VT or a stray magnetic flux test [MT or Magnet Testing]) on most workpieces.

Although the systems can also be used to weld aluminium, this opportunity has not yet been exploited. Until now, both systems have only been used to weld workpieces of mild steel and stainless steel alloys using the MIG/MAG process, almost exclusively with spray arc welding i.e. at the top of the performance range.

The welding designs have been consistently impressive, with respectable dimensions, substantial weights and high technical sophistication, according to Neuweiler AG. They have been proved in areas such as environmental technology, goods transport, water engineering, energy technology and, periodically, in other highly specialised application areas. Most of the workpieces are subject to mandatory inspections according to international standards such as ISO-9001, DIN/EN/ISO 3834-2, EN 15085-2 CL1, AD2000, TÜV/SVTI and DIN 18800. As with the bogie frames, all weld seams undergo a comprehensive visual inspection (VT or Visual Testing) as well as a stray magnetic flux test (MT or Magnet Testing) on most workpieces.

The robots also weld autoclaves and other components for thermal processes, such as for medical waste disinfection units.

The welding of pressure containers for gas turbine testing equipment made of Hastelloy, for example, meant very high requirements. This very expensive, but also highly corrosionresistant, nickel molybdenum stainless steel alloy is extremely resistant to crevice, pitting and stress corrosion, even at high temperatures.

A few months ago, the company received media attention for its fabrication of a sevenmetre-long 60 bar pressure container with 25mm thick walls and a 2.5-metre diameter for a wind tunnel unit. At full pressure, the container holds nearly one ton of air, which shoots through the wind tunnel at four times the speed of sound at the touch of a button. Another example for the larger workpieces is a 4.5-ton stator housing for electric generators with a diameter of 3.8 metres and a height of 1.6 metres. The robots also weld autoclaves and other components for thermal processes, such as for medical waste disinfection units. With containers of complex design, the robot's ability to weld inside seams, up to three metres deep within an 800mm diameter tube, has proved useful time and again.

## Common user interface

The two robot systems differ in several components, based upon the intended production spectrum. The axis-controlled longitudinal slide systems and the cross and vertical carriages have different dimensions, with the robots suspended on their bottom end. As previously mentioned, the RTi 330 S is designed with the L-shaped manipulator and counter bearing for especially heavy workpieces. On the RTi 496 S, the L-shaped manipulator works in conjunction with a headstock manipulator that can be positioned as required on a displacement track and can also be used to handle longer workpieces.

In both systems, the control systems can operate up to ten external NC axes in addition to their six robot axes. Due to their suspended position on the bottom end of the vertical carriage, the robots have extensive working ranges and can reach deep inside box-shaped workpieces. The robots themselves differ in the length of their arms and thus their effective working ranges, defined at 3,800mm for the RTi 496 S and at 3,000mm for the RTi 330 S.

"The operator can use the handheld K5 teach pendant with colour display, weighing 1.3kg, which allows access to all relevant functions. Programming with the handheld unit is comfortable and practically self-explanatory for welding professionals. In the final phase of programming or at the beginning of the actual welding process, the gas nozzle of the torch can be used as a tactile sensor. It makes search runs to determine the exact position of the welding seam joints and, if it finds deviations from the reference points, automatically adjusts the subsequent program parts by the measured amount. To be able to execute this sensor function, the gas nozzle is subjected to a mild control voltage, which discharges every time the gas nozzle comes into contact with the workpiece. This discharge triggers the required computational operations in the control system," IGM explained.

"Arc seam sensors also operate during welding. The control system monitors the welding current fluctuations during the oscillation of the welding torch and ensures the necessary track corrections in the weld joint position in case of deviations from tolerance. Just after the ignition of the arc, it detects the distinctive criteria of the seam joint within a few strokes and then guides the torch into the exact position. The control system then guides the torch along the actual weld path. If the seam is multi-layered, the control system takes the data obtained from the previously welded layers into account. Depending upon the seam geometry, the programmer determines the number of intermediate points (to store the values and use them during the welding of the next few layers). So, even with long seams and large deviations, the system operator can program the seam path in a single step. This shortens programming time considerably."

The pulse power sources of the two robots continuously regulate the welding power over the entire power range of the system using a single guide voltage. This way, said IGM, welding is always performed with optimised parameters. In the gooseneck welding torches, the forced contact ensures even and constant power transfer.

A pressure amplifier takes care of the pneumatic interior cleaning, blowing the contaminants out of the torch with 15 bar blasts of compressed air. Although inverter welding power sources greatly reduce spatter formation, spatter can still adhere to the torch, especially in out-of-position welds. This interferes with the protective gas flow and, with sufficient accumulation, the wire feed. Mechanical torch cleaning stations installed directly on the robot base provide an effective countermeasure. The robot goes to the torch cleaning station when the workpiece is being turned or the carriage system is moving the robot into a different position. This way, torch cleaning does not cause non-productive downtime. When the torch is in the cleaning station, it is first affixed centrally by specially shaped clamping jaws. Then two rotating blades remove the weld spatter from the inside of the gas nozzle, as well as the outside of the contact pipe and the nozzle stock. Intervals for the inside and outside cleaning of the torch are programmable.

## Fast, simple, flexible

Wilhelm Kowatsch, plant manager at Neuweiler, summarised the reasons why the company decided on IGM welding robots in four points: speed, reliability, ease of operation and rapid changeover between workpieces. He pointed out that the robot systems sometimes have to process complex workpieces, which demand very high quality and efficiency despite the severely restricted spatial conditions that apply from time to time. With low piece counts and single pieces, IGM systems, he said, score points for quick changeovers and simple reprogramming. He also added that, especially in the final phase of programming, the robots have a collision safety shut-off.

At peak times, the two welding robot systems are in use practically around the clock - they are even welding during “ghost shifts" when no operators are present. The two systems are only taken out of operation once a year for two to three days of maintenance.
