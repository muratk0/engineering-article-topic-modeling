# 5th RoboPlan Symposium at CLOOS

[TARİH: 01.01.2020 ISMR]

ON 5 DECEMBER 2019, Carl Cloos Schweisstechnik GmbH invited customers to its 5th RoboPlan symposium. Around 40 visitors exchanged news on CLOOS RoboPlan software in welding technology.

“A varied programme with lectures, live demonstrations and opportunities for individual questions and sharing of experience awaited the participants. A highlight of the symposium was the presentation of our new optional RoboPlan functions, which greatly simplify offline programming and increase the efficiency of welding production, commented the welding specialist.

After the official welcome by Christian Paul, Head of Application and Process Engineering Development, CLOOS, Sven Müller presented the new RoboPlan version V7.03. Over the course of the morning, further contributions on the new pick modes in RoboPlan, various service packages for maintenance contract customers and numerous practical examples followed. After lunch, participants received a comprehensive insight into the new optional functions of RoboPlan during live demonstrations and were able to extensively test them with practical examples.

CLOOS RoboPlan software enables offline programming of automated robot welding systems. RoboPlan allows the generation of welding and travel paths as well as sensor routines at 3D models and their direct transfer into the robot controller. This increases the system utilisation, optimises the production process and makes welding more flexible. The new optional functions are designed to simplify offline programming

CECIMO confirms that the European machine tool sector is coping well with current industrial and trade uncertainty.

ISMR SAYS:

In a time of industrial and trade uncertainty, the European machine tool sector continues to perform well, as production volume stands at more than 28 billion in 2018. CECIMO (the European association of machine tool industries and related manufacturing technologies) is closely following the review process of the Machinery Directive, an important piece of legislation for the machinery sector.

CECIMO brings together fifteen national associations of machine tool builders under one umbrella. These represent approximately 1300 industrial enterprises in Europe (EU + EFTA + Turkey), over 80% of which are SMEs. CECIMO covers 98% of total machine tool production in Europe and about 36% worldwide. It accounts for more than 150,000 employees and a turnover of nearly 28 billion euros in 2018.

"CECIMO and its national associations closed 2019 amidst industrial and trade uncertainties. Production and consumption seem stagnant while trade with foreign markets is threatened by political challenges, from Brexit to the U.S.-China trade war. Moreover, business sentiment seems to be at a low point for the first time in many years which is additionally affected by the strategic challenges and uncertainty within the automotive sector," CECIMO told ISMR.

"However, the European machine tool industry has strong foundations and the technological expertise to successfully move forward. The environmental and political factors affecting the automotive sector also create increased motivation for manufacturers to improve productivity and sustainability, which will offer significant opportunities for innovation in our sector."

## Machine tool production in Europe

CECIMO's 2019 estimates show that machine tool production in Europe stands at 27.1 billion euros, a -3% drop compared to 2018.

Although this decrease breaks the upward trend started in 2017, it is nonetheless an adjustment after a record-breaking year that saw a production volume of 28 billion euros. It is also 3% higher than CECIMO's global turnover share of 2017 (32%). Global machine tool output was forecast to decrease in 2019. CECIMO estimates that global production currently sits at 77.5 billion euros (-3.4% compared to data from 2018).

"In terms of trade, European machine tool manufacturers continue to perform well abroad. The registered export volume for 2018 was 21.6 billion euros, an 8.3% increase on 2017 levels. It would be the second year in a row with a growth rate greater than 8% (2017 saw an increase of 8.1% compared to 2016, with total exports reaching 20 billion euros). European machine tool builders remain very competitive abroad and that strong position will continue. CECIMO's top five export destinations are: China (26% of exports outside CECIMO countries); the United States (18.4%); Poland (8.2%); Russia (4.6%) and Mexico (3.9%)," the association told ISMR.

CECIMO internal figures suggest that machine tool consumption will decrease -2.9% in 2019 (from 16.5 billion euros in 2018 to 16 billion this year). It would be the first drop in consumption after six years of consistent growth. Oxford Economics forecasts show that machine tool consumption among European companies will further decrease in 2020, but should pick up pace soon afterwards, with relatively strong growth rates expected in 2021 (3.9%), 2022 (3.1%) and 2023 (2.6%).

Trends might be shifting in the world economy and the global machine tool industry, but CECIMO data shows that European machine tool builders are still strong in production, exports and consumption.

Marcus Burton, Chairman of CECIMO's Economic Committee, assessed the situation of European machine tool companies in 2019 and stated: "Our businesses face uncertainty about the economy in the short term, and this is understandable given the political and trade challenges around the world, as well as the uncertainty in the automotive sector.

Umati is also a priority, as it aims to create a worldwide standard for the connectivity of machine tools.

"However, they continue to perform well delivering innovative new products and solutions that were in abundance at EMO 2019. Machine tool customers should remain committed to a long-term strategy of investing in technologies that hold the key to improving their future competitiveness. Short-term uncertainty should not steer them away from the right path."

## The Machinery Directive

The Machinery Directive (2006/42/EC) is one of the main pieces of legislation affecting the machine tool sector. In 2016, the European Commission started a process to understand whether the Directive needed a revision.

One of the main issues being considered is the need to update the Directive to take into account emerging digital technologies. CECIMO questions, however, whether there is a need to introduce changes in the Directive.

“From our perspective, the requirements of the Directive already provide a robust framework to place safe products, including new technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI), on the market,” it told ISMR.

“Although AI and technologies alike have evolved considerably in the last decades, the development of applications in the machine tool sector is still at an early stage. Companies looking at the possibilities of these technologies are developing applications mainly for process optimisation, predictive maintenance and condition monitoring,” said Massimo Carboniero, Chairman of the CECIMO Technical Committee. “Industry believes that rather than changes in legislation, there is a need to revise standards in line with technological developments and the state-of-the-art.”

“The machine tool sector”, he said, “can contribute to the health of the European industry by shaping the next EU regulatory initiatives and policies”.

Given that the world is evolving around three dimensions of sustainability (sustainability of economy, environment and society), CECIMO aims to play a crucial role in today’s challenges such as the circular economy and digitalisation. It can help to define standards on new technologies, such as Artificial Intelligence.

“Europe must lead the race on global standards at the international level, so that our regulations set the example for others to come,” added Schneeberger.

The new President also made clear that European Institutions should value the role of industries when drafting pivotal dossiers. On Artificial Intelligence, the EU representatives should broaden their view and value different perspectives, not only the ones of big tech companies.

The CECIMO General Assembly elected Dr Hans-Martin Schneeberger as President of CECIMO for a period of two years.

Cyber security is also an issue at stake, which involves a wide range of actors including not only manufacturers but also suppliers, software providers and users.

“The inclusion of cyber security requirements in a revised Directive would not lead to an effective approach to cyber security and, instead, may open the way to a situation where different legislations will include requirements on cyber security, making it inconsistent,” commented Carboniero. CECIMO supports a horizontal approach to deal with cyber security aspects.

Another issue under discussion is the possibility to provide instructions on digital format. CECIMO points out that manufacturers should be free to choose the best means to provide the required documentation, as long as the requirements of the Directive are fulfilled and the required information is provided.

“There are many advantages to the use of digital manuals including the possibility of easier updates, reduction of printing costs and environmental impact. Since complex machinery is usually accompanied by complex manuals, digital solutions would allow users to find the right information quickly, especially if integrated with Augmented Reality solutions,” argued CECIMO.

All these issues were re-stated during a breakfast briefing organised by CECIMO on 5 December 2019. The event was hosted by Maria Grapini MEP and saw the participation of many MEPs, EU officials, representatives from Permanent representations of EU countries and industry stakeholders. The debate on these issues was intense and, said CECIMO, showed support for its position.

## Confirming new appointments

During its meeting on 5 December 2019 in Brussels, the CECIMO General Assembly elected Dr Hans-Martin Schneeberger as President of CECIMO for a period of two years. His term will focus on the setting of global standards and the uptake of Artificial Intelligence. Succeeding Dr. Roland Feichtl, he has been entrusted with the responsibility of leading the European association that represents more than a third of world machine tool production.

“In many cases, these companies are suppliers to our industry, while we are the customers and therefore users. So why should the European Commission discuss relevant issues exclusively with the suppliers if they can have a well-founded discussion with the users?” argued Schneeberger. Under his presidency, CECIMO will be committed to be the voice of manufacturing in the definition of the digital single market.

The priorities of Dr Schneeberger are in line with the project of the next European Commission. Amongst others, Europe wants to set the right environment for digital networks and services to thrive and strengthen itself as a responsible global leader.

“CECIMO will closely follow all the dossiers on the table, such as cyber security, trade agreements and Intellectual Property Rights, where we can give valuable input,” the association told ISMR.

CECIMO also recently elected new chairmen to its committees. Massimo Carboniero was elected as Chairman of the Technical Committee, and Bruno Cathomen as Chairman of the Communication and Advocacy Committee, for a period of two years. Delegates also confirmed Marcus Burton as Chairman of the Economic Committee for another mandate.

As the successor of Juha Makitalo, Dr. Massimo Carboniero will lead the CECIMO Technical Committee, which will focus much of its work in the next two years on the future review of the Machinery Directive. Standardisation is another topic high on the agenda of the Committee. According to him, “standards play a strategic role in the uptake of new technologies and the digitisation of industry".

CECIMO is involved in the work of different standardisation committees at European and international levels. Sustainability is also another strategic subject on the agenda of the Committee. The aim is to support the sector to become more sustainable by building on existing good practices.

Bruno Cathomen will succeed George Blaha as Chairman of the Communication and Advocacy Committee. In his two-year term, he promises to look at some issues at stake for machine tools and related manufacturing industries. According to him, cyber security and digitalisation of the industry go hand in hand.

Mr Cathomen reinforced CECIMO's commitment to support the European Commission and ENISA to build a real “single cyber security market". Nevertheless, he said, 'we need to ensure that, while doing so, we secure the competitiveness of our industry and that we follow a risk-based approach. It means that cyber security requirements are associated with the intended use of the product and level of risk. Data flows, better data quality and data management are vital for machine tool businesses. Therefore, CECIMO will closely follow the work of the European Commission with regard to handling data responsibly.

Umati is also a priority, as it aims to create a worldwide standard for the connectivity of machine tools.

“We need initiatives like umati," said Mr Cathomen. "The main benefit is making data processing easier and, if data is standardised on many machine tool interfaces, the monitoring of this data, for example, is simplified."

## Strengthening strategic value chains

In November 2019, the European Commission published the “Strengthening Strategic Value Chains for a Future-Ready EU Industry – Report of the Strategic Forum for Important Projects of Common European Interest".

The report delivers specific recommendations by a group of experts (the Strategic Forum on Important Projects of Common European Interest) for the Strategic Value Chains. It contributes to the Commission's ongoing work to develop a long-term EU industrial policy with concrete measures for its implementation. Six key strategic value chains were identified, in March 2018, for their potential impact on Europe's industrial competitiveness and the interest of the member states, to support and develop coordinated actions in these areas:

- Connected, clean and autonomous vehicles.
- Smart health.
- Low CO₂ emission industry.
- Hydrogen technologies and systems.
- Industrial Internet of things.
- Cyber security.

CECIMO was selected as part of the Strategic Forum, together with a few selected industry representatives, member states and European Commission representatives, and it has been rapporteur for the Industrial Internet of Things Value Chain. In its pivotal role, CECIMO had the opportunity to underline the importance of data exploitation and artificial intelligence, digital twins and metal additive manufacturing.

Now that the report has been published CECIMO is looking forward to the next steps and is committed to contributing to future actions in the context of the cooperation between Strategic Forum members, other stakeholders and the European Commission.

It aims to ensure, it told ISMR, the competitiveness and technological sovereignty of European industry and manufacturing.
