# THE WORLD OF WELDING

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

Finnish welding specialist, Kemppi, launched a range of automated welding solutions at Blechexpo in the march towards Industry 4.0

It has been a good year for Finnish welding specialist, Kemppi. Its Executive Chairman of the Board, Teresa Kemppi-Vasama, was awarded the Veuve Clicquot Business Woman of the Year Award. The company has also won numerous awards for industrial design over the last few years.

Kemppi Group’s subsidiary Kemppi Oy is a family business, founded in 1949, and one of the leading manufacturers of arc welding equipment and suppliers of software and service solutions for welding. In 2014, its net sales came to about 114 million euros, with international markets accounting for 90% of that sum. Kemppi has production units in Lahti and Asikkala, Finland, and in Chennai, India. It has operations in 16 countries and regularly exports to more than 70. The company employs over 600 workers.

At the Blechexpo and Schweisstec fairs in Stuttgart this November, Kemppi presented its forthcoming solutions for automated welding for industries such as automotive, shipbuilding, oil and gas, and tube and pipe manufacturing.

## Automated welding solutions

“We took a major step in terms of welding automation and showcased seven new systems for automated welding at Blechexpo (six mechanised systems and one for robotic welding),” Olli-Pekka Holamo, Product Manager, Automation for Kemppi told ISMR at Blechexpo. “They offer a full functional package for arc welding and feature a new interface (web browser) that allows the user to control the robotic welding process. We have also placed our focus squarely on useability. We have the process expertise and have developed strong welding products.”

Kemppi Oy, one of the leading manufacturers of arc welding equipment and pioneer of welding management software and service solutions, is moving firmly towards automated welding. Its new mechanized product range will consist of six new mechanization systems for MIG and TIG welding for straight and orbital welding. The range will include systems in different value levels from simple and compact batterypowered systems to advanced solutions for the multilayer welding of pipes and tubes. The smart design is demonstrated in cleverly integrated solutions and easy user interfaces which enable more cost-efficient welding through savings in time and rework.

The new system for robotic welding will include a complete process package with a new robotic MIG welder. The A7 MIG Welder 450 is suitable for any industry using robotic welding for thin and thick mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

“Our robotic welding device features WiseIgnition that reduces welding spatter levels (with a smooth start and reductions in after-work) which means significant cost savings for our customers,” explained Olli-Pekka Holamo. “The new automation solutions will be sold and serviced by our worldwide sales and support network. The new products will come to market during 2016.

“We are value-oriented. It’s not just about features or price, it’s about total cost of ownership and shorter payback times. When you talk about productivity, it is not just about modifying welding processes to increase productivity. Net arc time is roughly 10-20% of a welder’s working time. So why not manage welding production more effectively with a higher arc on time? So, we provide higher arc on time per day for the welding machine and Kemppi Wise systems offer from 50 - 300% increases in productivity. So, we can offer a twopronged approach to customers.”

Kemppi Wise application software significantly improves welding production efficiency of the robotic and mechanized MIG systems. For example, WiseFusion can increase welding speed by 20% and lower heat input with the same amount. All automation solutions include Kemppi power sources.

“We see ourselves as providing internet for welding systems and are very well positioned for Industry 4.0. The big picture is connecting all devices in a network – with new quality and safety norms, this has to happen. You must be able to show that the correct processes and safety norms have been followed and provide a competitive edge to our customers at the same time,” added Frederic Lanz, VP, Marketing and Sales.

## Markets and trends

Kemppi has also placed more focus on APAC and South American markets, as part of its sales strategy.

“We have moved forward with our APAC strategy,” Lanz explained. “Anssi Rantasalo, our MD, has been based at Kemppi’s office in Malaysia since April to support the business and our growth targets in the region. Malaysia is a good strategic location for Asian markets. At the same time, we have also been building up more competencies and resources in European markets.”

Kemppi is strong in the APAC region although Europe is its home market. Sales in the Chinese market have grown by 50% over the last four years for the company, Lanz told ISMR.

“You have to be a global player. In Eastern Europe, more manufacturing has moved over. Germany will play a stronger position in the future, as will Central Europe. Many of our systems are used in sectors such as shipbuilding, oil and gas, automotive and heavy manufacturing. We are also gaining market share in Russia every day, despite the current economic and political issues there,” continued Lanz.

Consolidation is also a trend in manufacturing markets and Kemppi will be an active player in this. In December 2013, Kemppi acquired a software company. The automation business, it told ISMR, is growing faster in welding and there is strong potential in high strength steels and special welding applications.

“Automation is still a big trend. Quality norms and standards are increasing day by day. There is also a lack of qualified welders. We need to provide the tools for our customers to succeed, provide more product value and maintain the system advantage,” explained Lanz. “Materials to be welded are also getting thinner and more specialised (e.g. titanium). Mobile welding systems are also becoming more popular. User interfaces have become simpler and must be easy to visualise. The IoT (Internet of Things) approach is affecting the market hugely.”
