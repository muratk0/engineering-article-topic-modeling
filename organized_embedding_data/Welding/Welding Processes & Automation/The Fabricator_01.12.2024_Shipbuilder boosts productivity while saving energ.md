# Shipbuilder boosts productivity while saving energy with dynamic beam laser welding

[TARİH: 01.12.2024 The Fabricator]

Applications

Dynamic beam laser technology can produce welds up to 70 mm thick.

Situation

Italy-based Fincantieri S.p.A. is one of the largest shipbuilding companies in the world. Specializing in high-value-added segments, including cruise ships, defense vessels, and offshore vessels, the company also provides ship repairs and conversions.

Operating 18 facilities on four continents, the company has produced more than 7,000 ships, each of which can require 190 to 370 miles of weld seams to ensure structural integrity. That equates to more than 2 million miles of weld seams in Fincantieri’s 230-year history.

With welding such an important driver of ship construction, innovations in welding technology hold the potential to dramatically increase efficiency. So Fincantieri went in search of a thick-section welding process that could increase its welding speed while reducing costs and distortion.

Resolution

In collaboration with Castellini Officine Meccaniche S.p.A., a welding system producer and provider of large-scale panel line technology used by Fincantieri, the shipbuilder conducted an extensive study of dynamic beam laser (DBL) technology from Civan Lasers, focusing on a 28-kW system.

Unlike traditional lasers, which produce a static beam, DBLs can dynamically adjust the beam’s shape, intensity, and size in real time. This flexibility makes them effective for welding thick sections of metal with precision, control, and efficiency.

Using DBL technology, Fincantieri experienced a 40% increase in welding speed, allowing the shipbuilder to complete projects more efficiently. The pure laser welding process transfers less heat into the workpiece, which helped minimize distortion while also mitigating the risk of alignment. In addition, the smaller HAZ contributed to better mechanical properties in welded material, enhancing overall weld quality.

DBL technology offers up to 300-mm penetration depth in a single pass and welds up to 70 mm thick, allowing it to weld steel sections more than twice as thick as those handled by Fincantieri’s current methods. As a result, the shipbuilder achieved a 100% increase in weldable thickness while also experiencing a 60% reduction in energy use, helping to support its sustainability objectives.

Use of consumables also decreased during the study. Filler material usage fell 90%, drastically reducing production costs, while the requirement for protective gases, a major cost factor in traditional welding, was reduced to a minimum.

This research showed how DBL technology could be integrated seamlessly into Fincantieri’s production processes to set a new standard for welding thick steel sections and make the company’s shipbuilding operations faster, more efficient, and environmentally sustainable.
