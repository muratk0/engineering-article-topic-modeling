# 50 YEARS ON

[TARİH: 01.01.2014 ISMR]

Steigerwald Strahltechnik GmbH of Maisach, near Munich, belongs to Global Beam Technologies AG international group of companies, together with PTR Präzisionstechnik GmbH of Maintal, near Frankfurt, and PTR Precision Technologies Inc. of Enfield, USA.

It is not by chance that the name Steigerwald has become firmly associated with electron beam welding technology around the world. A short journey through the 50-year history of the company demonstrates why this is the case.

Dr Karl-Heinz Steigerwald was a pioneer in the field of electron beam technology. He developed and constructed the world’s first electron beam welding machine in 1947 as part of a working group at AEG’s research institute. The first electron beam processing machine followed in 1952.

After making a career detour to Zeiss in Oberkochen (from 1953), Dr Steigerwald discovered the deep welding effect in 1958 and founded his own company five years later. He had long recognised the potential of the electron beam within welding technology and wanted to further advance the technology under his own supervision from then onwards.

What began in 1963 with the founding of Steigerwald Strahltechnik GmbH in a garage in Wasseralfingen, Baden-Württemberg, was to develop rapidly over the next few years. The garage soon became too small and the company relocated its headquarters to Munich.

## Milestones in focus

The year 1968 was one of the first major milestones in the company’s history: the first EB welding system was proudly delivered to prestigious British aircraft engine manufacturer, Rolls Royce – and the foundation stone was laid for Steigerwald Strahltechnik’s intensive activities in the aerospace industry, which continue to this day.

In the years that followed, many more EB machines were developed, constructed and supplied throughout the world. The company continued to grow. Steigerwald Strahltechnik has moved on from the invention of the electron beam welding machine to become one of the world’s leading electron beam technology companies.

In 1980, Steigerwald Strahltechnik, based in Puchheim, incorporated laser technology into the company’s activities after it was taken over by Messer Griesheim and, three years later, it supplied Volkswagen with the first 3D laser processing machine for prototype construction. This foray into laser technology lasted until 2000, whereupon the company began to concentrate intensively once again on its core competence of electron beam technology.

During this period, further advances were also made in the engineering and construction of EB machines for different areas of application, including energy technology, the automotive sector and research and development. The first EB machine for welding airbags was supplied in 1989. That was at a time when most cars in Germany were not yet equipped with this protection system.

Until 2000, welding tasks in the Steigerwald plants were exclusively performed in a vacuum. In that year, the engineers and technicians at Steigerwald developed the first SST EB machine for welding at atmospheric pressure. The high productivity of these machines means they are used predominantly in the automotive industry for welding aluminium instrument panels.

## On track for growth

In 2003, as part of the Global Welding Technologies AG group together with igm Robotersysteme AG, Polysoude and Oxitechnik, the company was even more strongly positioned in the market as a specialist in EB technology.

In the same year, Steigerwald moved into its new company premises in Maisach, where GBT is also based. Bigger and better development and production facilities meant the company could respond to increasing customer demand even more effectively with a view to the future.

## WELDING AND JOINING

## Development and expansion

The first decade of the 21st century for Steigerwald Strahltechnik has been characterised by the steadily increasing number of machines produced and sold all over the world.

Meanwhile, technological software and hardware developments were not left behind. From 2005, strategic development priorities were defined to enable the company’s own development team based in Maisach to continue to perfect the electron beam as a tool for the GBT Group. In the years that followed, Steigerwald engineers developed EBOJUMP fast beam deflection that – in addition to automatic beam adjustment, electron-optical view and automatic online seam tracking – also features multi-beam technology, enabling the welding of multiple joining positions or the simultaneous execution of several welding processes.

Today, the company’s product lines cover the process technologies of welding, drilling and hardening (surface finishing).

## Thinking big

The year 2013 was the year of superlatives for Steigerwald Strahltechnik GmbH. The EBODISC modular chamber system is evolving and revolutionising welding with electron beams.

One, two or three precision-mounted eccentric discs form a rotation system that, combined with moveable and pivotable generators, enables the three-dimensional spatial handling of a workpiece for the first time. As a result of this operation, the chamber volumes and the pump output are also reduced, further increasing efficiency.

Another milestone in the company’s history was achieved in 2013: Steigerwald built its third chamber facility of 55m³. The largest SST EB chamber machine it has built so far is also under construction, with an impressive volume of 63m³. ■
