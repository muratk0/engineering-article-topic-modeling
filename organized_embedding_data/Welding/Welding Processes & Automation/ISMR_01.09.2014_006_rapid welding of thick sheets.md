# Rapid welding of thick sheets

[TARİH: 01.09.2014 ISMR]

AS PART OF THE HYBRILAS joint project, scientists at the Laser Center in Hanover have developed a process that can be used to make single-sided, zero-defect welds for aluminium sheets up to a thickness of 12mm. To achieve this, they combined a laser beam with two gas metal arc welding (GMAW) torches.

The ScanTracker on Precitec’s YW52 welding head has an integrated scanner mirror, allowing the laser beam to make a pendulum movement across or along the feed direction. This enables the effective width of the laser beam to be flexibly set. The welding seam width is as wide as necessary and enables high welding speeds. The joining process achieves high feed speeds of 5-6m/min. and is able to bridge gap widths of up to 0.4mm and edge misalignment of up to 2mm. With steel materials, sheets of up to 23mm thick can be welded in a single layer at a speed of 1.5m/min.

“The innovative process is not only extremely fast but, compared to conventional methods with multiple layer gas metal arc welding processes, the seam geometry is very narrow and filler material consumption is considerably lower. This creates a very high quality weld seam. The lower heat input and hence less severe component distortion means that the combined process has other advantages over conventional methods. The hybrid welding process uses a solid-state disk laser with an output power of 16kW,” explained the Laser Centre in Hanover.

To join a steel sheet with a thickness of 30mm, conventional gas metal arc welding requires tack welds and approximately six filler layers. The hybrid process just needs two to three layers. The first layer is made using the laser-GMAW hybrid process. It replaces the tack welds and the first four layers of the conventional gas metal arc weld.

The sheets are then subject to one or two backing runs with the GMAW process. The hybrid welding process is therefore able to weld a 1.5m-long component within just one minute, whereas the conventional process requires twelve minutes.

The joining project, “HYBRILAS: welding thick sheets with brilliant laser beam sources”, was part of an initiative called “MABRILAS: Material processing with brilliant laser beam sources”, promoted by the German Federal Ministry for Training and Research and coordinated by the VDI Technology Centre. ■
