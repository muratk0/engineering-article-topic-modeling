# SINGLE-SOURCE “Swiss engineering subcontractor, Huter und Söhne, uses a broad range of Fronius technology for welding, joining and finishing jobs.” WELDING Huter und Söhne, based at Innsbruck in Austria, outlined a tour of its metalworking shop, highlighting the materials and processes used.

[TARİH: 01.11.2020 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Innsbruck construction company Huter und Söhne’s metalworking shop alone employs 60 people, who have ten permanent welding stations and additional mobile welding systems at their disposal. Across all processes, Huter relies exclusively on welding technology from Fronius.

Innsbruck is at the heart of the Tyrol region in Austria. Huter und Söhne has been active in the construction industry there for more than 150 years – the city centre boasts numerous buildings that owe their existence to Huter. To date, Huter und Söhne has gradually succeeded in establishing itself in a variety of different branches of the construction industry.

## Universal construction concepts

From conventional construction, carpentry and laminated timber construction to joinery services and metalworking, Huter und Söhne is a general contractor offering complete solutions. From foundations to roof trusses, and solid wood front doors to corrosionresistant railings, the company makes more than 70% of the construction components itself. This results in turnkey construction approaches.

“With around 250 employees, we can cover a variety of different architectural styles from housing estates, office buildings and business premises, to industrial building and hall extensions – and we also have the expertise to build a sturdy home,” explained Thomas Gattringer, master metalworker, welding practitioner and instructor for Huter und Söhne.

“It goes without saying that we take up all the challenges posed by innovations in structural engineering. Energy-saving construction is also part of our service portfolio, for example.”

Right: Thomas Gattringer, master metalworker next to the headquarters of Huter & Söhne, Innsbruck, Austria.

## Touring the metalworking shop

It becomes clear, during a subsequent tour of the metalworking division’s facilities, that the metalworking shop is basically separated into materials.

Right at the start is the steel workshop: railings, stairs, ramps, canopies, car ports etc. are made here from conventional construction steel. The employees handling the solid steel girders that bar the way as you enter do not exactly hold back: “Brute force is needed here,” said Gattringer. “Mounting brackets for balcony boxes, for example. The metal plates are 20-30mm thick. We use 1.2mm wire and then weld the whole thing in three layers. So, you need an enormous amount of power, which is why we mostly use the Fronius TransSteel 3500c welding system.”

Gattringer also explains that, due to regulation EN 1090, welding tasks like this at Huter must only be carried out by the highpower TransSteel 3500c which also offers a water-cooling feature. Fronius’s TransSteel 2200 also comes under the prescribed certificate of conformity package.

“If the tasks are minor, we also make use of the older VarioStar systems which are simply indestructible,” added Gattringer.

A subdivision of structural steelwork in the next workshop hall demonstrates how little Huter has to buy; the door frames leaning against the wall are made ‘from scratch’ in-house. From foundations to roof trusses, and solid wood front doors to corrosion-resistant railings, the company makes more than 70% of the construction components itself. First, the fitters cut suitable strips from the 1.5mm galvanized steel plate and trim them to make complex profiles. The individual components are then welded together to make finished door frames.

The aluminium workshop is next in line. This is where all aluminium system construction takes place, producing profiles for windows, doors and facades, and complete roof structures.

The stainless steel workshop is just a few metres away. The workers wear T-shirts and white gloves – it is all about fine dexterity. The employees in this department are real TIG specialists and you can see the almost artistic requirement of their work. At present, they are mainly working on handrails, stainless steel door frames and railings. The visible weld seams were all made by the Fronius MagicWave 230i or the TransTig 230i.

At the end of the workshop visit, Gattringer added: “We have high quality standards so, of course, we need the right tools. When it comes to thermal joining, we only work with welding technology from Fronius.”

## Finishing and joining ‘on the go’

“We also rely on Fronius systems for mobile finishing work,” continued the welding practitioner. Smaller tack welding jobs on stair railings are quickly resolved with TransPocket. But when it comes to joining prefabricated handrails with high-quality visible seams, the fitters use the TransSteel 2200 “because the seam appearance is important with this type of work, TIG welding is essential,” they told ISMR.

Fitters highlight the TransSteel 2200’s touchdown ignition in TIG mode: “System operation is so smooth that we find it easy to work and remain highly professional at the same time. It is true to say that you only need the minimum of practice and, after just a few attempts, welding tasks can be performed at a level we would otherwise only manage in the workshop with the TransTig and MagicWave high-frequency TIG systems,” they added.

“The TransSteel 2200’s multi-process capability is a real plus, of course – especially the quick switch between processes. It takes no time at all to convert the system for the different process variants, as adjustments can be made intuitively and immediately, without requiring much previous knowledge. So, we are ready to go extremely quickly,” explained Gattringer.

“The fact that we do not need highvoltage current is a great benefit. Since this is often unavailable on construction sites, we occasionally found ourselves having to organise generators in the past – a significant amount of extra work that is now no longer required.”

Construction sites, where sometimes there is no clear definition of the tasks to be undertaken, are also part of everyday life for fitters.

“In these situation, we pack the little TransSteel in the car just in case, because then we are ideally equipped for all tasks,” they added.

Huter fitters really do have to draw on all the process variants in mobile use: “We can apply all the processes professionally and weld at more or less the same high level,” they told ISMR.

Just recently, a typical assignment for TransSteel was requested by one of Innsbruck’s famous jewellers. Part of the renovation work required solid steel girders to be put in to support ceiling construction. But, with so many upright structures (temporary supporting elements to support the ceiling) taking up space, Huter was severely restricted.

“There was not enough room anywhere; we had to weld in all positions and it was always out-of-position welding. The lack of space meant we would have been at our wits’ end with the larger MAG systems and if we had been forced to use smaller electrode welding systems, we would probably still not be finished. TransSteel flexibility definitely came to the rescue here!” they explained.
