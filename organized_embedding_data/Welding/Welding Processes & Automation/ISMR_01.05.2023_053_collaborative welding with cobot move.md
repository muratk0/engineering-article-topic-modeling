# Collaborative welding with Cobot Move

[TARİH: 01.05.2023 ISMR]

The new Cobot Move linear axis by Lorch Schweisstechnik is designed to significantly extend the previous application options for Cobot welding.

"The Cobot's working radius has been increased to more than 4.5 metres thanks to a traversing axis, making it possible to set up much larger workpieces or use multi-station operation on a single worktable. One important special feature is the Cobot's ability to operate without any safety enclosure, enabling easy integration into existing working environments without any great effort," explained the robot specialist.

"Companies will now be able to weld larger workpieces, or several smaller ones clamped on a worktable, automatically even more easily. The core of this new solution is a linear axis with a traversing range of 2,000mm, operated with a high-precision repeatable ballscrew drive that achieves traversing speeds of 2-160mm per second. The linear axis can be mounted on any standard welding worktable with a length of at least 2.4 metres. Even already-installed Cobot Welding Solutions with a UR 10e Cobot can be easily retrofitted with the linear axis to extend the Cobot's working range to more than 4.5 metres," it added.

The Cobot, said the manufacturer, can still be used collaboratively and remain CE-compliant. It can also, it explained, be used without any additional safety technology such as laser scanners or protective enclosures, which "makes installation quick and easy and greatly simplifies integration into the existing production environment."

"Welding is also possible while the Cobot is traversing on the linear axis. The Cobot joint position will not change at any point, ensuring a uniformly high-quality result of the weld seam. Even very long parts that used to require time-consuming repositioning can now be welded efficiently in a single continuous step, thanks to the enlarged working area," commented Lorch Schweisstechnik.

The use of the linear axis also brings benefits with 'nesting operation', in which series components are welded one after the other on a worktable in multi-station operation. Once the first component has been programmed, all subsequent ones can be welded simply by moving the Cobot.

"The joint position of the Cobot remains the same for each component and guarantees an optimal weld seam. This increases the Cobot's working space at constant repeat quality. However, nesting operation not only reduces programming effort but also significantly increases productivity. While the Cobot is welding in one station, workpieces can be prepared for the next welding process in other ones at the same time," explained the manufacturer.

Users can use the URCap Lorch Motion with several control options for programming the Cobot Move.

"This software is just as user-friendly as the powerful Cobotronic for all Lorch Cobot solutions. The Cobotronic itself has many additional functions for the user, such as the QuickPoints function for easy programming of waypoints and welding commands or the SmartCopy that allows copying of welding sequences once programmed for a part to transfer them to identical parts in different positions in the Cobot's workspace," added the manufacturer.

The latest innovation in this area is the automatic SeamTracking weld correction, allowing the Cobot to follow a weld seam's course, even if the workpieces are slightly distorted or slightly outside the tolerances.

Dr. Ing. Caren Dripke, department head of robotics development at Lorch Schweißtechnik, concluded: "The Cobot Move is the perfect complement to our welding Cobot. The additional linear axis unlocks a significantly larger working area, permitting automated welding of longer and larger workpieces and significantly increasing output in the production of series parts. Its sophisticated axis design also enables unchanged collaborative operation of the system. This means that the system retains its typical flexibility and full accessibility without compromising on safety."
