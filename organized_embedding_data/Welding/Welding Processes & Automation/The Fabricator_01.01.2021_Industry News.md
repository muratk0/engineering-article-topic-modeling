Bystronic, Cloos Robotic Welding establish strategic partnership

Bystronic Inc., Hoffman Estates, Ill., has announced an agreement with Cloos Robotic Welding to offer Bystronic robotic welding cells powered by Cloos in the U.S. market. Cloos supplies the Bystronic robotic welding cells and provides installation, training, and service, while Bystronic is responsible for the sales and distribution of the machines.

Cloos manufactures the robot weld positioner, the robot controller, the welding power source, and the cell integration. It also maintains its own offline software, RoboPlan QRP Professional, for programming.

Bystronic offers three robotic welding cell models powered by Cloos, each having a specific scope of application based on the size and weight of the parts to be welded, and each with a two-station workpiece positioner. The Micro Cell features an overall clamping length of 55 in., a nominal load capacity of 280 lbs. per side for the manual rotating workpiece positioner, and a 400-amp welding power supply. The Compact Cell offers a 61- in. clamping length, 562-lb. nominal load capacity per side, and 450-amp power supply. The Compact System has a 138-in. clamping length, 2,248-lb. nominal load capacity per side, and 450-amp power supply.

BTD Mfg. adds tube laser to Minnesota facility

Metal fabricator BTD Mfg. has added a BLM 4K Fiber LT7 tube laser to its production facility in Lakeville, Minn. With the machine, the company can process a variety of materials, including aluminum, stainless steel, and steel.

The laser processes round, square, and rectangular profiles, as well as special-shaped tubes, angle iron, channel, and flat bar. According to the company, the machine offers optimal cutting performance even on thickwalled steel for weld preparations and interlocking joints with a ±45-degree tilt.

The system features an internal suction probe that can be used when processing tubes to catch debris and vacuum out particles automatically. Its Active Scan feature automatically calculates any compensations needed to ensure tolerances are achieved, regardless of misshapes or insufficient rigidity, the company states.

Mayville Engineering Co. named to Wisconsin 75

MEC has extensive manufacturing operations in Wisconsin, with several facilities located in Mayville, Beaver Dam, Neillsville, and Wautoma. Overall, the company operates 20 facilities in seven states with nearly 3 million sq. ft. of manufacturing space.

Camfil Group to build new manufacturing facility in Arkansas

Camfil Air Pollution Control, a manufacturer of industrial dust-, fume-, and mist-collection systems, has announced the construction of a new manufacturing facility in Jonesboro, Ark., to replace its previous building that was destroyed by a tornado in March. The new factory and office space are scheduled for completion in early 2022.

Since the tornado, the company has been conducting manufacturing operations out of a rented local facility. Sales offices, filter cartridge production, warehousing, and shipping were not damaged. All these functions will move to the new facility.

Sheet Metal Supply purchases new equipment to expand production

Sheet Metal Supply, a fabricator and supplier of custom-formed roofing perimeter edge metal products and processed coil and flat stock, has purchased two machines that will provide the capability to produce heavier, longer, and more precise product for the glazing industry, as well as more precise custom substructural components for the façade products it already manufactures.

The company has added a Safan Darley E-Brake 130T-4100 and an AccurShear 625020 to its Grayslake, Ill., facility. The press brake allows for servo-electronic bending with reduced energy consumption and CO2 emissions. The shear features a 20-ft. blade that cuts metal up to 0.25 in. thick.

Hexagon launches program to offer CMM equipment access as a service

A new metrology subscription program launched by Hexagon’s Manufacturing Intelligence division gives businesses in the U.S. and Canada access to metrology equipment as a service without having to invest in new equipment.

Called The Works, the program helps manufacturers of all sizes gain immediate access to CMM technologies. At the center of the program is MyCare, a maintenance subscription that includes annual certifications, preventive maintenance, software updates, and unlimited technical support. The program also includes discounts on calibrations, repairs, parts, and cloud-based asset management.

All CMMs in the program include the Pulse environmental monitoring system, which uses a network of sensors to record and indicate potential crashes and variations in temperature, vibration, and humidity in the vicinity of CMMs.

ESAB, Yaskawa sign collaborative agreement for pre-engineered robotic welding cells

The ESAB Group Inc., a manufacturer of welding and cutting equipment and products, and robotics system provider Yaskawa have signed a global cooperation agreement to jointly develop and market a line of preengineered robotic welding systems called XCellerator. Andes Coil Processors adds 52-in. embossing line

Steel toll processor Andes Coil Processors has added embossing services to its Gary, Ind., facility with the installation of a new, 52-in. embossing line. The line produces a nondirectional stucco pattern in metal at a depth of up to 0.013 in. This pattern is suitable for use on shelving products, industrial flooring, and building products. Additional output patterns will be added as customer requirements warrant.

The line runs coils from 5 to 52 in. wide at more than 250 FPM. It handles thicknesses from 0.010 to 0.045 in. in steel and 0.010 to 0.080 in. in aluminum. Maximum incoming and outgoing weight capacity is 25,000 lbs.

Coldwater Machine, a Lincoln Electric Company

in Coldwater, Ohio, has launched a "Will It Meld" calculator on its website to help companies determine if dissimilar materials can be joined by its SpinMeld rotary friction welding systems.

Visitors to

www.coldwatermachine.com/will-it-meld

can select the two materials they would like to weld from a drop-down list of over 35 substances. Depending on the selected materials, the result will either say the materials can be joined, the materials have not yet been proven to join, or to contact the company for more information on the application. The SpinMeld system can join steels, aluminum, cast or sintered metals, magnesium, brass, ceramics with mixed metal connections, and dissimilar combinations of these materials.
