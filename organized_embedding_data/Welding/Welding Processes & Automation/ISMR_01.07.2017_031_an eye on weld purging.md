# An eye on weld purging

[TARİH: 01.08.2017 ISMR]

TITANIUM, STAINLESS STEEL and other corrosion-resistant materials are used for tubing and pipework, especially where there are highly corrosive materials flowing through them. When this pipework is welded for joining or repairs, it is necessary to protect the material in and close to the weld joint, which is done with a technique called weld purging. Oxygen and nitrogen can be harmful to the weld joint, in particular, as they will combine very easily with the hot material, forming oxides and nitrides, until the temperature falls below its oxidation point.

One accessory, called a Weld Trailing Shield®, can be added to a GTAW/TIG welding torch before beginning a pipe weld. This lightweight tool, designed and manufactured from Huntingdon Fusion Techniques HFT, can be easily manoeuvred around the pipe joint as it is being welded. The Trailing Shield is connected to an argon gas source and, when the weld is started, extra argon floods to the sides of the weld and covers the heat-affected zone adjacent to the weld. As the welding torch is moved forward, because of the length of the Trailing Shield, the weld remains under an argon gas shield until the welded metal has cooled below its oxidation temperature.

"For manual welding, there is no difficulty in dragging it along the surface being welded. One further benefit is that it carries the welding torch at 90º to the weld. With automatic welding machines, it is possible to raise the welding speed dramatically and enjoy the major benefits of automatic welding. Each Trailing Shield is delivered with a variable diameter connector so that it can fit any size of TIG/GTAW/PAW welding torch for manual or mechanised welding," explained HFT.

"Savings on expensive cleaning and pickling costs to remove oxidation and discolouration on these corrosion-resistant materials pay for these shields in the first weld and the shields can be used for years without replacement. Trailing Shields are available for any diameter of tube and pipe from 17mm upwards. They are also manufactured in flat format for sheet metal welding."

Luke Keane, Distributor Support for HFT, added: "The Trailing Shield is connected to the argon gas supply and it diffuses gas out evenly without turbulence through the multi-layers of stainless steel mesh built into the body of the shield. At each side, removable silicone rubber side shields provide a barrier to prevent the argon escaping sideways and remaining over the weld metal. These silicon shields are a consumable and are replaceable as they become worn."

## The process chain

LEVELLING AND DEBURRING specialist, ARKU, held its InfoDays 2017 event from 15-19 May 2017 at its headquarters in Baden-Baden, Germany, showcasing a triple linked line for the first time. This covered all of the crucial processing steps, such as levelling and measuring actual flatness. In addition to live demonstrations, the InfoDays also included tours around the machine park. Visitors were able to run their own parts on a variety of machines. InfoDays 2017 also focused on 50 years of levelling technology from ARKU as the company celebrated its anniversary with a look back at its own history.

Networked production and Industry 4.0 were key topics at the five-day event in Baden-Baden. The central exhibit at this year's InfoDays was a complete line of three linked systems - its core components consisted of an EdgeBreaker® 2000 Plus deburring machine, a FlatMaster®55 80 precision leveller along with the FlatJack® automatic flatness control. With this linked system, ARKU covered all central material preparation processing steps in one single line. The EdgeBreaker® deburrs and rounds the panels on both sides. The FlatMaster® transforms these into flat panels almost entirely free of internal stresses. Finally, the FlatJack® control system checks the flatness of the pieces.

All of the machines operate fully automatically and in sync. with each other using an integrated control system. This central computer is a Variable Customer Interface (VCI). Linking the systems also results in less handling. A conveyor transports the cut parts directly from one machine to the next, eliminating manual intervention.

In addition to the complete line, ARKU also presented individual deburring machines such as the EdgeBreaker®, the EdgeGrinder® and the EdgeRacer®. See www.arku.de
