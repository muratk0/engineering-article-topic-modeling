# FIXING ON THE FUTURE

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 ISMR]

David Landon, the American Welding Society’s President, sets out his vision for the Society and views on global welding markets to ISMR

The American Welding Society (AWS) was founded in the US in 1919 as a multifaceted, non-profit organisation with a mission to advance the science, technology and application of welding and allied joining and cutting processes, including brazing, soldering and thermal spraying. It is the largest organisation in the world dedicated to the welding industry.

Supported by a staff of more than 100 professionals, AWS serves nearly 72,000 members worldwide, including more than 15,000 international members. It is led by a volunteer organisation of officers and directors and is composed of 22 Districts with more than 250 local Sections and 200 technical committees throughout the world. It is also a standards-developing organisation that produces AWS practices, standards, product specifications and guidelines.

AWS is an advocate for US methods and practices in the development of critical international standards through the International Standardisation Organisation (ISO). It publishes more than 230 technical documents for the welding industry, including one of the world’s most widely used standards, D1.1/D1.1M, Structural Welding Code—Steel, which is updated every five years. ISMR caught up with its President, David Landon, to uncover its latest initiatives.

## Strengthening relationships

“The welding industry is growing and strengthening and that is good news for sheet metal workers, welding professionals and the industry workforce as a whole because it ultimately benefits business and job seekers. Our latest statistics indicate that, by the year 2024, the U.S. will have a need for 374,000 welders. This number include those whose occupations include welder training including welders, boilermakers, sheet metal workers, structural metal fabricators and structural iron and steel workers,” David Landon, AWS President, told ISMR.

“AWS strives to move the industry forward in both thought and action, as well as inspire a new generation to see the exciting career opportunities in welding that are available today. Education and training are top concerns for our members right now. As the industry grows, our members are showing interest in continued learning, advanced training and obtaining certifications. We have been working hard to develop our AWS Learning online programmes which offer online courses, virtual conferences, podcasts and an online educational library.”

Manufacturing, construction and mining is a US$ 3 trillion industry. Welding is a US$ 34 billion industry. There are more than 500,000 welders working in the US. The average age of welders in today’s US workforce is 54. As the trained work force of welders retires at a rate twice as fast as new welders are coming into the industry, the supply of qualified personnel is becoming a widespread demographic issue. The American Welding Society therefore rapidly introduced online training programmes to help expedite the development of skilled welding professionals.

AWS is very focused on strengthening relationships in the welding community. It recently launched AWS WeldLink, a career management system that brings together individuals, businesses and schools in the welding industry. The system is architected to address the complete lifecycle of professional welder development. It enables individuals to create an online profile that will aid them in mapping their current skills and knowledge by tracking certifications, education and work history, then uses that information to match them with welding career paths, locate welding programmes and directly register training programmes through AWS Learning. Users can also view open positions in top companies with direct connections to apply.

“Businesses will benefit from creating their own WeldLink profiles by being able to identify and recruit individuals with the right skills, post job listings and stay on top of the latest advances in welding. Meanwhile, academic institutions will be able to create their own profiles to access welding education resources, recruit students and help those students to find employment. WeldLink is open to the entire welding community and you do not have to be an AWS member to create a profile. This can be done at www.awsweldlink.org,” explained David Landon.

## New initiatives and industry trends

In the second quarter of 2014, AWS introduced a new logo as part of a comprehensive re-branding effort to help it communicate about the future of welding as a high-tech, highly-skilled industry. The new logo reflects a contemporary interpretation of the established AWS diamond insignia with modernised font and bevelled metal elements.

“In March this year, we also unveiled a revamped AWS website to enrich visitor experience by creating customisation and delivering full content and functionality across all devices. The new site has been designed to give visitors the ability to customise the website to fit their individual needs,” added David Landon.

AWS currently has two sheet metal codes under revision. These are:

■ AWS D9.1M/D9.1:2012, Sheet Metal Welding Code – This code covers the arc and braze welding requirements for nonstructural sheet metal fabrications using the commonly welded metals available in sheet form; sheet metal thicknesses of up to and including 6.07mm [0.239 in]. Also covered are the attachment of accessories and components of the system, and joining or attachment of any member, regardless of thickness, whose sole purpose is stiffening, supporting or reinforcing the sheet metal.

■ AWS D1.3/D1.3M:2008, Structural Welding Code—Sheet Steel - This code covers the requirements associated with welding sheet steel with a minimum specified yield point no greater than 80ksi [550MPa]. The code requirements cover any welded joint made from the commonly used structural quality low-carbon hot rolled and cold rolled sheet and strip steel with or without zinc coating (galvanised).

A welder’s successful career is based upon professional credibility. It brings value, higher pay, job security and recognition as one of the industry’s elite. AWS conducts preparatory seminars throughout the US and around the world, and has certified more than 52,000 CWIs since the programme’s launch in 1976. It offers certification in the following categories:

■ Certified Welding Inspector (CWI).

■ Certified Welder.

■ Certified Radiographic Interpreter (RI).

■ Certified Welding Educator (CWE).

■ Certified Welding Engineer (CWEng).

■ Certified Welding Supervisor (CWS).

■ Certified Robotic Arc Welder.

“Certifications bring value and can mean better pay, job security and recognition as an industry elite professional,” explained David Landon.

AWS also offers international, national and local educational opportunities including seminars, conferences, individual corporate programmes and online courses. AWS educational initiatives range from welding basics to the latest technology updates. The association has an award-winning programme, SENSE (Schools Excelling through National Skills Education), which offers administrative standards and curriculum guidelines for the qualification of entry-level, advanced and expert welders in high schools and colleges.

David Landon was keen to point out new and emerging trends that are impacting the global welding industry to ISMR.

“Additive manufacturing is an emerging trend that will impact welding markets. AWS recently formed the D20 Committee on Additive Manufacturing. The committee will be responsible for the development of AWS standards for additive manufacturing. New documents undergoing drafting and approval include the D20.1, Standard for Fabrication of Metal Components using Additive Manufacturing. Other trends include robotics and automation, off-line programming, arc monitoring, virtual reality and motion tracking camera training systems.”

One significant market trend is that new techniques have enabled resistance welding and friction stir welding to replace mechanical joining of non-ferrous and dissimilar metals. As global trade increases, the uniform quality of welded products (regardless of where they are produced) depends upon adherence to internally accepted standards.

Internationally, AWS is also a member of the International Institute of Welding’s (IIW) American Council, which is recognised as the largest worldwide network for welding and allied joining technologies (current membership of 56 countries from five continents).

## The skills gap

The skills gap and the ongoing need for qualified, committed welders have spurred AWS to launch initiatives to attract new blood to the industry.

“Attracting a new generation to the welding industry has been a challenge for several years but we are now making great strides. We need to continue to introduce young people to welding and be mentors,” David Landon continued.

“Through our online learning programmes and our social media channels, we are able to reach young people and show them how rewarding a career in welding can be. Followers of our Facebook, Instagram and Twitter accounts are able to view videos and photos showcasing interesting jobs, welding projects that members are working on and learn about services offered by AWS that will help them through their welding career. We also have a blog called Welding Wonders which highlights some of the most famous welding wonders that the world has to offer to show why welding is a trade we can’t live without…”

An analysis of projected data that was gathered through the efforts of Weld-Ed, a US National Science Foundation funded centre, showed that by 2024 there will be a need for at least 544,000 welding professionals.

Welding technology has changed dramatically. Many younger, technology-geared students are drawn to welding worksites where teams frequently work with lasers, robotics, computer programming and other complex technologies. In addition to welders, many other professional-level experts are needed from certified inspectors and engineers to specialised sales teams.

“From manufacturing to construction, the demand for welders is strong. Salaries are also good, given that many jobs don’t require extensive education. Average wages are approximately US$17 – 25 an hour, with more complex roles such as underwater welding, specialised certifications or supervisory roles offering even higher earnings,” explained David Landon.

The AWS Foundation began in 1989 to promote welding education and promote the study of welding and joining with scholarships and fellowships. It grants approximately US$ 300K/year in scholarships (six fellowships/year for post graduate work at US$ 25,000 each). It also collaborates with local training facilities, junior colleges, colleges and universities to provide trained, certified workers to help satisfy local workforce needs. The Foundation connects companies with enduser training institutions, junior colleges and universities that can provide them with long-term quality employees.

The Foundation also supports programmes that ensure the growth and development of the welding industry through strengthening research and educational opportunities in welding and related industries. Led by a volunteer Board of Trustees, it is organised as a not-for-profit 501(c)(3) charitable organisation. Administration, including programme development, fundraising, public relations, marketing and financial management is provided by development staff at the American Welding Society. Its operations are conducted from the Society's offices in Miami, Florida.
