Self-programming robot sets its own weld path

TRUMPF has introduced new sensors that enable its arc welding robot to program its own weld path. The robot makes use of the manufacturer’s new smart seam-tracking technology, developed in collaboration with the Fraunhofer Institute for Manufacturing Engineering and Automation IPA. Housed in the head of the welding torch, the sensor automatically calculates the weld path for each part.

The user places the welding robot in the start position, and the robot uses the sensor to determine the weld path automatically. The system software calculates the weld spots and creates the welding program for the part. The robot is ready to go in a matter of seconds, even for complex parts such as spiral arcs, the company states.

The intuitive user interface can be used to carry out a variety of welding tasks, such as sealing runs and identical weld seams.

Heated wire automatically cuts end of stretch wrap

TAB Industries LLC has introduced a Hot Wire Cutting System for its TAB Wrapper Tornado line of orbital wrapping machines. The optional system automatically cuts the end of the stretch wrap after pallet wrapping by drawing a heated metal wire through the plastic film, then conceals the wire for quick cooling.

The new design, which replaces the standard design that uses a blade, is available on the company’s standard, semiautomated, and fully automated orbital wrappers and can be retrofitted on machines in the field.

Rust inhibitor adheres to metal surfaces

Fluoramics offers HinderRUST Metal Fabrication & Welding rust inhibitor. It is surface active through adsorption, which spreads the product across the surface of metal and forms a molecular adhesion to the material. This creates a durable, thin film on metal surfaces.

The product is available in quarts, gallons, and aerosol spray cans. It can be applied to wet or dry metals by airless sprayer, brush, or roller. For long-term storage, the product can be reapplied as needed.

Automation system can support multiple lasers

The AMS 3015 CL from AMADA AMERICA is a flexible automation system that performs material handling operations automatically, reducing machine downtime due to manual loading.

The modular system allows fabricators to expand their automation capabilities. With additional modules, the machine can transition from a single-cycle loader to a multishelf tower that can support multiple lasers. A symmetrical design allows modules to be placed on either the front or rear side of a laser’s shuttle table to accommodate material flow.

Portable plasma cutting package severs metal up to 1¼ in.

ESAB has unveiled its new Cutmaster 50+ portable plasma cutting package. It has a recommended pierce and cut capacity of 5/8 in. and severs metal up to 1¼ in. With a rated cutting output of 50 amps at 60% duty cycle, the 35.3-lb. unit is available in 110- to 240-V onephase and 480-V three-phase primary power versions.

The system is compatible with the company’s MechPak for mechanized straightline cutting and gouging applications controlled with a simple start-stop switch. It includes a 180-degree torch and all required hardware to allow precision setup and control for many semiautomated applications.

The package features normal and grate cutting mode, automatic inlet/outlet air pressure detection, automatic postcut air purge to extend consumables life, and an automatic voltage detection function that sets maximum output amperage based on primary power to help optimize cutting.

Roller table transfers loads up to 1,544 lbs.

Designed to handle loads between 1,103 and 1,544 lbs., Palmgren’s Roller Table Support Stands can transfer heavy workpieces from one location to another for operations such as assembly, fastening, and welding. The tables can be bolted together to meet varying length requirements, and the legs can be adjusted from 25 to 37 in. high. The precision-machined steel rollers have durable roller ball bearings to help make movement fast and easy, the company states.
