# Welding helmets find the spotlight

Playing With Fire

Baseball players, country musician honor those who build and maintain the world around us

Baseball players and at least one country musician are acknowledging the hard work that welders take on each day.

mediaphotos/iStock/Getty Images Plus

M

ost of the time, our work happens out of sight. The sparks fly, the deadlines hit, and the job gets done—no audience, no applause.

But now and then, something from our world shows up where everyone can see it. Lately, that’s been a welding hood.

PITTSBURGH GOT IT RIGHT

The Pittsburgh Pirates started using a welding hood as part of their home run celebrations this season—a real hood, from a real welder. According to a recent Major League Baseball article, the whole thing ties back to Pittsburgh’s roots—steel, fabrication, and the men and women who do the work.

After a surprising request from the Pirates, fourth-generation steelworker Bernie Hall collected well-worn welding equipment from fellow steelworker baseball fans to donate to the ball club. And on the first pitch of his first at-bat as a Pirate on March 26, Brandon Lowe smashed a homer to right field and became the first to don the celebration welding hood, originally worn by steelworker Dave Stephenson.

"It’s well-worn, which is great," Lowe said. "It’s been through some trials. It’s been through the test of it."

The Pirates have certainly faced some trials as a ball club, but when a player hits a home run, comes back to the dugout, and flips the welding hood down, the cynical but loyal crowd goes wild. Maybe the hood will prove a good luck charm for the team? We’ll see how the season plays out, but this home run celebration is a perfect tribute to the passionate fan base of badass steelworkers.

IT’S NOT JUST A SONG; IT’S A LIFESTYLE

Over on the music side, country artist Stephen Wilson Jr. is tearing it up on his sold-out Gary the Torch Tour. And guess what’s hanging out on stage with him? Another welding helmet.

His song "Gary" (with a killer music video featuring Gary Sinise) isn’t about one dude. It’s about that guy. You know the one. You’ve met that guy. You might be that guy. (Or gal! Wilson calls her "Debbie.")

He’s the one who can "fix about anything a hammer can’t handle." The guy who works a full day and then still shows up to your place at 9 p.m. because your water heater blew out. The one who welds, wires, patches, and builds— whatever it takes.

AI might be coming for a lot of jobs, but as Wilson Jr. said during his recent Detroit performance:

"AI ain’t gonna show up when your septic tank blows. But Gary will, and if his name is Gary, you’re probably in good shape. At least, that’s just what I’ve experienced. The Garys that I grew up with were guys who could just fix everything. They always had a cigarette kind of stuck to their lip while they were talking, and they’d always shoot you straight and always did you a solid."

So even as AI challenges identities, jobs, and sense of purpose in this ever-changing world, Gary keeps doing his thing.

The song itself is a raw, four-chord repetitive loop with a tempo that’s hustlin’, just like Gary.

So when Wilson Jr. throws a welding hood on stage next to that story? It just makes sense.

WHY IT MATTERS

Sure. It’s a dugout celebration. It’s a stage prop. But it works.

The welding hoods are a nod to the blue-collar spirit—and the people who build and maintain the world around us. And seeing them show up in these places? That’s just fun!
