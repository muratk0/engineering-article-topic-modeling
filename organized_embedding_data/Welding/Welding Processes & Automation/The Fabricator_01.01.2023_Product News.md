Phased-array ultrasonic system tests entire weld area

Magnetic Analysis Corp.’s Echomac robotic phased-array ultrasonic NDT system detects weld zone defects in ERW tube caused by scarfing or welding processes. The system scans and tests the entire weld area without the need to change transducer settings when the weld wanders.

Installed in the hot zone near the welder, the test gives immediate feedback on weld quality so corrections can be made to prevent flawed product and ensure tubes are free of weld flash. The system can monitor scarf, evaluate laminar defects, and detect longitudinal OD/ID weld defects, including lack of fusion, pin hole penetrators, hook cracks, and misalignment of the parent material.

If more accurate defect detection is needed, installation can be in the lower-temperature area after forming and shaping.

Press brake includes features for handling large, complex parts

The HRB series press brake from AMADA AMERICA offers several features designed to help resolve specific manufacturing challenges. The crowning system provides consistent angle accuracy across the entire bend length, and L-Shift allows gauge fingers to be set at different depths to process asymmetrical workpieces. Agrip-A punch holders lock tooling into the appropriate position quickly to help reduce setup time by up to 60%, the manufacturer reports.

The addition of a Sheet Follower system helps ensure efficient processing and handling of large, cumbersome parts. Inclusion of a double-clamp backgauge provides accurate processing of complex shapes.

Entry-level laser sheet cutting system offers high functionality

BLM GROUP USA has introduced the LS7 laser sheet cutting system, designed as an entry-level product with the features and functionality of a highend system. It has a conventional aluminum mobile gantry structure actuated by a rack and pinion system, which slides on a very strong metal structure filled with polymer concrete to ensure high rigidity even with high accelerations up to 2G.

The cutting area has been designed to protect the bellows from the glowing sparks thrown in all directions, especially during piercing. Access is only from the short side, and the entire machine remains completely enclosed. The cutting process can be viewed on the operator console, an external monitor, or remotely.

The pallet change takes place on the long side in 9 sec. Handling is fully electric, without pneumatic or hydraulic actuators.

Cobot welding cell helps workers with repetitive, challenging tasks

The GoFa collaborative robot welding cell from ABB is designed to work alongside humans to automate processes involving heavy loads and long reaches and help workers with repetitive, ergonomically challenging tasks.

Intelligent sensors in each joint bring the cobot to a stop in milliseconds if they sense any unexpected contact, allowing the cobot to work safely sideby-side with humans without the need for fences.

According to the manufacturer, users do not need to rely on robot programming specialists; they can operate their cobot within minutes of installation without special training.

Robotic arc welding workcell made for large-quantity runs

The ArcWorld 6000 family of pre-engineered robotic arc welding workcells from Yaskawa Motoman features a design and standard options for largequantity runs of medium to large parts.

Configurations with one, two, or three AR series arc welding robots are available. Streamlined arm designs allow simplified access to parts in tights spots, avoiding potential interference with fixtures.

The compact YRC1000 controller is integrated into the workcell design, which includes separate cabinets for high- and low-voltage components, as well as dedicated locations for power sources, water circulators, pneumatics, and sensors.

The controller features patented multiple-robot control with coordinated motion between devices.

3D software supports welded assembly operations

Virtek Vision has added several enhancements to its Iris 3D laser projection system, enabling job shops and OEMs to accelerate the manufacture of welded assemblies and components. It requires no prior training or experience to operate.

Using a standard CAD file, the system identifies the part geometry (mounting brackets, studs, bushings), determines each part’s exact position in 3D space, and projects high-visibility laser lines to direct workers in the placement of each part on the assembly, including drill locations, punch locations, fixture placement, and assembly positions.

The software achieves accuracy of ± 0.015 in. or ± 0.38 mm for most applications.
