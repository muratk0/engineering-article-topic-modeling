# Welding and 3D printing robots

[TARİH: 01.01.2024 ISMR]

Weld butt-joints in ultra-thin sheets (e.g. for coil-to-coil applications) pose a challenge for current welding technologies. Dreamweldtech's welding robot can process butt-joints in ultra-thin sheets, ranging from 0.03 to 2.0mm, contingent upon the materials. The company is developing two models (a semi-automatic and a fully automatic welding robot) both designed to substantially reduce lost time and lost raw materials.

"Our welding robots have been designed to perform butt-joint welding of 'coil-to-coil' in stamping/pressing lines, revolutionising the manufacturing process for a wide range of industries. By incorporating our welding robots into these processes, companies can experience a remarkable reduction of more than 90% in downtime and over 95% in raw material wastage. This not only vastly improves productivity but also has a positive environmental impact by significantly reducing solid waste generated from raw material losses," explained company cofounder, Dr. Nguyen.

Specialising in the manufacture of welding robots and mini/compact metal 3D printing (WAAM) robots, Dreamweldtech houses its Research and Development (R&D) Centre in Buckingham, UK, and its main factory in Hanoi, Vietnam. Plans are in progress to inaugurate a global business office in Texas, USA, by 2024.

Dr. Nguyen has also identified a gap in the current landscape of metal 3D printing technology and is working on a wire-arc additive manufacturing (WAAM) technology development.

"This initiative introduces modern printing heads, an innovative printing heat source and the latest wire feeding systems utilising tiny wires. This breakthrough enables the printing of ultrathin walls, potentially less than 1mm and even 0.5mm, with exceptional surface accuracy," he told ISMR.

He is developing a mini/compact WAAM system tailored for printing biomedical components (implants/bones) and electronic parts as an alternative to laser/electron beam, powderbed, additive manufacturing technologies. This also positions the WAAM robot as a solution for educational institutions.

“It serves as a valuable platform for students to gain hands-on experience and learn about metal 3D printing in a practical and accessible manner," added Dr. Nguyen.
