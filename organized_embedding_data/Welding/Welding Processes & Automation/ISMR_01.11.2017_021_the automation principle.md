# THE AUTOMATION PRINCIPLE

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

Yaskawa is investing in new product and software launches, new factory facilities, improved functionality and a new 'collaborative' robot initiative.

Yaskawa Europe GmbH was present at this year's Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart with the entire portfolio of its three divisions – Drives & Motion (drives and control technology), Robotics (industrial robots) and VIPA Controls (automation and control technology). Its broad product range enables end-to-end solutions to be realized from the drive level to control and visualisation. It demonstrated all-in-one robotics solutions for inert gas and spot welding, from a standard cell to customised and complex systems.

With the compact Motoman MS80W II spot welding robot as a stand model, Yaskawa responded to the global 'downsizing' of spot welding guns and expanded its portfolio to include an 80kg spot welding robot. Besides its Motoman robots for welding, cutting and handling (material feed), the company also offers extensive drive and control technology, specifically for metalworking machines, that is designed to be easily integrated into existing machine architectures.

ISMR visited the company's booth in Stuttgart and sat down with Dietmar W. Guttroff, Manager Sales System Business, Robotics for Yaskawa Europe GmbH to discover the company's latest innovations.

ISMR: What is the focus of your booth presence at Blechexpo?

DG: We are focusing on robotic arc welding systems here, our core competence. It is also responsible for our main market share in Europe, where we deliver complete systems (not just welding robots but complete systems, turnkey solutions, tooling jigs and fixtures). We have our own tooling centre in Slovenia, where we build the tools. We are mainly targeting smaller and midsized companies for automation potential.

Here on our booth, we are also highlighting new and different software functionality on our systems. This includes an error recovery function where, for example, if a robot faces an obstacle, the other robot will finish its job. An error recovery unit is a defined sequence for assuring component quality in the event of disruptions to the welding process.

Automated metalworking processes require high precision and throughput, but also stringent standards in terms of the durability, reliability and ease of maintenance of the technology used. We meet these demands with our wide range of products, for example our latest generation of Sigma-7 servo drives, the new GA700 inverter drives and machine controllers for a variety of applications, such as the MP3300iec and the VIPA Motion Controller. With our own servo drives and scalable controller models from mini-PLCs to the classic IEC controller, we offer a full range of solutions from a single source, from simple systems to complete handling robots for material feeding.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes or achievements?

DG: It has been a great year for us - our order books are full. We are working on so many projects right now. In Germany and Europe, the economic situation is fantastic. However, this does affect delivery times so we are working to improve these. We have grown turnover this year by over 10%, although our financial year ends at the end of February 2018 so it is too early to say right now.

There will be a grand opening of our new facility near Munich, in Germany, at our current headquarters on 15 January 2018. The new factory, with 2500 square metres, is a huge investment for us into our future. We are also building new manufacturing facilities in Slovenia, France and Sweden which will be finished in 2018. We have put a lot of investment into our European operations as we expand in this region.

The greatest success this year for us was a commitment from the Yaskawa Board in Japan to give us robot manufacturing facilities in Europe. We will start manufacturing our own robots in Slovenia, for the European market, at the end of 2018. This will be the biggest signal to the market, and to our competitors, that we will start manufacturing operations in Europe for the European, and especially the German, market. This will shorten delivery times and make us more flexible and versatile.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

DG: According to the IFR (International Federation of Robotics), the average market growth going forward will be 8-10% per year, so we expect this solid and growing scenario to continue into 2018.

ISMR: What makes Yaskawa different from other robot manufacturers?

DG: Our key selling points include our ability to offer complete solutions, turnkey solutions and manufacturing solutions for part quality, as well as the quality and reliability of our robots. We are a problem-solver for our customers, using robots.

ISMR: Which other new systems have you launched recently?

DG: We launched our new Motoman ArcWorld V2 turnkey, robot-based arc welding cell at Schweißen & Schneiden (25–29 September 2017) at Messe Düsseldorf. It features a small footprint, short delivery times and a high degree of flexibility: robot, positioner, controller and energy source are combined on one platform. If necessary, the robot cell can be relocated quickly and easily.

The new model has a light, ergonomic workstation for the operator, where the required materials are always close at hand. The new compact cell offers a straightforward introduction to robot-based welding – the second generation can be easily integrated into various production processes and is ideal for welding small and medium-sized components. Workpieces can also be loaded and unloaded with an indoor overhead crane.

We also plan to launch new robots in Spring 2018.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in robotic and automation markets?

DG: Everyone is talking about Industry 4.0 right now. To comply with this, we offer our customers smarter functions to connect different devices in the robot systems with other devices in their machine manufacturing production. We aim to deliver data analytics from our systems to them i.e. robot production hours, cycle times etc. So, we need to develop new systems in accordance with this. For example, a customer with different production plans around Europe may need to know capacities for robot systems in different countries to be able to plan his overall European production.

In line with this, we are also developing our own software which will be called Yaskawa Cockpit. The first version is ready and is in the testing phase. The second phase will be to test this software with customers, analyse their feedback and customise it to their needs.

Another trend we see in the automotive market is e-mobility and new production trends for this. We have heard that large automotive OEMs have committed to a policy whereby 25% of all produced cars should be electric or hybrid cars. This is a massive number considering that, in Germany, that there are more than 3.5 million new cars per year. The industry apparently intends to deliver this level of mass production by 2022.

ISMR: What about your stance on collaborative robots – humans and robots working together?

DG: We have just launched a collaborative robot, the Motoman HC 10. It is a very flexible collaborative robot with a payload of 10kg, that can be used either in collaborative mode or in full industrial mode and brings all the benefits of Yaskawa industry robots.

We have had a great deal of interest in collaborative robotics from automotive OEMs as well as Tier One companies. They see a future where humans and robots work together on the shop floor, in assembly areas, which is a key trend in the market.
