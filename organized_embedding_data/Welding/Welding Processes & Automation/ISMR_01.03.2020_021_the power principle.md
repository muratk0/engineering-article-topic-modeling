# THE POWER PRINCIPLE

[TARİH: 01.03.2020 ISMR]

Bilfinger Industrial Services chose a solution to help its welders work efficiently, ergonomically and safely on steel components for the Traunleiten hydroelectric power plant in Austria.

ISMR SAYS:

Fronius push-pull welding equipment, with a TPS/i power source for the weld seams, enabled welding inside steel components for the Traunleiten project.

PHOTOS: Fronius International GmbH

Limited space and high-quality requirements demand solutions which enable welders to work efficiently, ergonomically and safely. Fronius advised Bilfinger Industrial Services in Austria to invest in one of its push-pull welding torches with a long hosepack, with a TPS/i power source for the weld seams.

The purchase was prompted by an order for steel components for the nearby Traunleiten hydroelectric powerstation, which is undergoing significant reconstruction work. The contract covered the engineering, production, delivery and assembly of 23 different steel components, weighing around 350 tons in total. These components included intake gates, turbine intake bulkhead gates, coarse screens to protect the turbines and two weir gates (known as fish belly flap gates).

## Fish belly flap gates

In run-of-river plants, fish belly flap gates control the level of the water. They owe their name to their arched shape, which is reminiscent of a fish belly and makes them particularly torsion-resistant. For the Traunleiten project, two rotary gates (each measuring 44 metres in length) were ordered. They are connected to the side wall of the barrage at one end by a hydraulic positioning drive. The 2.5m-high gates are connected to the threshold of the barrage via a rotation axis. This allows the fish belly flap gate to be raised and lowered and the water level to be regulated quickly.

"Due to transport restrictions, the gate bodies were divided into three segments of up to 16m each," explained Bernhard Brindl, project manager in the Hydro division of Bilfinger in Wels. “The parts were joined on the construction site using flange plates with high-strength bolted connections, which requires regular checking of the tightening torque."

A resealable manhole, with a diameter of 650mm, had to be provided for each segment. For an access point to be sufficient for maintenance, Bilfinger needed at least two manholes to weld the 16m-long segments with the welding equipment previously available. Welders would have needed to take the power source, including the wirefeeder, into the inside of the gate segments to achieve all the necessary welding positions.

"We would have had to subsequently weld the additional access points shut again and check their tightness. Aside from the additional costs, each additional manhole represents a weak point where water could penetrate," added Brindl.

## 600 metres of weld seams

Fronius recommended that the Bilfinger experts use a long hosepack in conjunction with a push-pull welding torch and a power source from its latest generation of TPS/i devices. Bilfinger has long relied on Fronius.

"We have several hundred Fronius devices, used by more than 300 welders - both in workshops and on construction sites," explained Brindl. The industrial service provider therefore followed the recommendation and ordered an 8m-long PullMig welding torch as well as a TPS 500i for the torsion gates' construction.

The specialists mainly welded S355 steel plates with fillet welds and some V butt welds with a total length of roughly 600 metres. They started with the construction of a steel tray that forms one side of the gate, onto which struts and then the "lid" were welded.

The welders had to adhere to tight tolerances in the millimetre range and observe minimum welding distortion so that the parts could subsequently be bolted together precisely and mounted on the rotation axis. To largely avoid out-of-position welding, the welders repositioned the gate several times, using a roller crane.

Important welding parameters can be set via the JobMaster remote control, which can be called up directly on the torch body.

## More freedom of movement

"The result impressed us all," said Brindl. "The extended operating radius due to the long hosepack allowed us to dispense with the second manhole for each segment, saving us the additional work that this involved."

In addition, the workers were able to place the power source, together with the wirefeeder, outside the gate body and still reach all the required positions.

"This not only gives us better freedom of movement in the space with a cross-section of just 1,500 x 800mm but it also facilitates – and this is crucial for us – access and recovery for emergency rescue workers," added Brindl.

The equipment offers a number of other advantages, which also have a direct impact on efficiency, ergonomics and quality in pipeline, container and ship construction. Important welding parameters can be set via the JobMaster remote control, which can be called up directly on the torch body. This allows the welder to perform different welding tasks straight away without additional work and long interruptions.

"Our welders rated the steady wire speed particularly highly," reported Brindl.

"In addition, an LED light integrated in the welding torch makes it easier to work in poorly lit areas. The welding torch is extremely compact and weighs just 1.6kg, despite the integrated two-roller drive, making it one of the lightest in its class. Together with the rapid control of the arc and the stabilising PMC pulse process that are typical for the TPS/i devices, the highest quality requirements can be met efficiently, safely and conveniently," Fronius told ISMR.

"The welders would prefer to only work with this device," summarised the project manager. "We immediately reserved the TPS 500i with the long PullMig welding torch for our department. The container construction department followed suit and also ordered a long hosepack. This allows the welders to devote themselves entirely to the task at hand and benefit from the increased operating radius, even with larger containers."
