# THE 'INTERNET'OF WELDING

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

Kemppi launched a digital platform for industrial welding, a portable and cordless welding machine and new personal protective equipment at this year's EuroBLECH

Kemppi is a leader in the welding industry. Headquartered in Lahti, Finland, with production plants in Finland and India, the family-owned company employs over 600 welding experts in 17 countries and has registered revenues in 2015 of over 110m euros. Its portfolio includes welding solutions – intelligent equipment, welding management software and expert services – for demanding industrial applications and ready-to-weld needs. Its global partner network covers over 60 countries.

At EuroBLECH 2016, Kemppi launched three new welding innovations aimed at re-defining the performance and usability, health and safety of welders as well as welding management capabilities in extreme industrial welding production. These included a multifunctional welding system with a focused and efficient arc, upgradeable power source and practical wireless user interface with full integration with welding management tools, including digital Welding Procedure Specifications (dWPSs). Kemppi also introduced a new helmet with high TH3 level respiratory protection and a ready-to-weld MMA cell pack at the exhibition. ISMR met Frederic Lanz, Vice President, Marketing and Sales, on Kemppi's booth in Hanover to discover more...

## The 'Internet of Welding'

"The digitalisation of welding production is the main trend affecting competitiveness in all welding-related industries, when more and more devices connect via the Internet and multi-site production increases. We have been a leading innovator in welding digitalisation for years now, introducing the world's first digital power source in 1993, and the world's first universal welding management software in 2014. We are also an active member of international standardisation committees strongly affecting the development of the global welding industry," Frederic Lanz told ISMR.

"We offer advanced welding solutions for demanding industrial applications and ready-to-weld needs for all arc welding processes. However, nowadays, a significant competitive edge is mainly achieved through advanced welding management software, application software and welding programs," he added.

At EuroBLECH, Kemppi demonstrated the strength of its commitment to 'The Internet of Welding' and Industry 4.0 by launching the X8 MIG Welder, a multi-process welding solution for future-proof industrial welding. The solution is enabled by the latest IoT based technologies and connectivity. It is fully compatible with Kemppi's universal WeldEye welding management software and will be available for customers in 2017.

"With its focused and intense arc, groundbreaking user experience, Digital WPS feature and full integration with the WeldEye welding management software, the X8 MIG Welder lets you perform, control and manage welding production in a way that was not possible before," Lanz told ISMR.

The X8 MIG Welder is a multi-process system for MIG/MAG welding, stick (MMA) welding, MIG brazing, cladding and heavy-duty gouging. The solution consists of Kemppi's upgradeable power source with an all-in-one wire feeder, ergonomic welding guns, intelligent software and a wireless Control Pad for total welding control. In addition to high-speed welding (WiseFusion), the arc characteristics include, for example, narrow gap welding (RGT), pipe root welding (WiseRoot+) and low spatter welding in the globular transfer area (WiseSteel). It has power and accuracy up to 600 A, said Kemppi, using the most common electrical network voltages.

Traceability and flexibility is vital, Kemppi told ISMR. The issue is not the quantity of information, but the quality of information. Smart and relevant data is key.

"The X8 MIG Welder is the most capable and connected welding platform that you can imagine. Every machine is fully connected and has a website of its own. It is far more than a welding machine – it's a digital platform for welding," explained Lanz.

The user can view the content on the large 5.7-inch display and activate it to start welding. A user-friendly, all-in-one wire feeder and a serviceable power source increase usability even further.

"We believe that everything you use has to be intuitive - we have applied this philosophy to our Control Pad. It is very simple to use and designed for welders wearing heavy gloves. It has a barcode reader, NFC reader and a USB port. You can charge it separately and put it on your belt. It is, in essence, Welding 4.0," Lanz told ISMR.

"The X8 MIG Welder is designed to grow with the changing needs of manufacturers. It can be easily upgraded with software to work with new materials, applications and welding management demands. Even the welding power is upgradeable. It is the biggest project that we have ever undertaken and a truly innovative game-changer - the Internet of Welding (IoW).

"This year, we merged our Kemppi Arc System with WeldEye management software so, in effect, WeldEye is the internet and the X8 MIG Welder is the hardware platform. Equipment selection criteria today is about intuitive user interfaces, apps that can be used, interconnectivity etc. It's all about added value, applications etc. We are not promoting just machines with the X8 MIG Welder, but the 'big picture' here. It brings us back to the principles of Industry 4.0 – it is not simply hype or a buzzword, Industry 4.0 is here today and we are demonstrating this with our X8 MIG Welder platform."

## Automated welding

Kemppi Oy also complemented its new automated welding offering with an intelligent solution for robotised MIG welding. The A7 MIG Welder 450 is a comprehensive process package which can be integrated with any robot brand for robotic welding on thin and thick mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

Kemppi Wise software provides added value and adapts the A7 MIG Welder 450 for a variety of welding tasks.

"For example, WiseFusion's ability to optimise arc length speeds up welding and lowers the heat input by 20%. Its stable arc can support welding speeds of up to 2000mm/min," said Frederic Lanz. The system's enhanced arc ignition functionality, Touch Sense Ignition, stabilises the arc immediately after ignition to minimise the risk of spatter.

For improved usability, A7 MIG Welder 450 features a modern browser-based user interface that allows easy access to the power source from a standard computer via Ethernet. This means, Kemppi told ISMR, significant time savings in set-up time and provides quality control benefits.

The A7 MIG Welder 450 consists of a welding gun, wire feeder, power source (available also as 350 A model) and cooling unit. The system is connected to the robot controller via fieldbus hardware modules.

## New innovations

For the health and safety of welders, Kemppi has re-designed welders' personal protective equipment and optimised mobile solutions. The new GAMMA GTH3 welder respirator combines comfort with high TH3 levels of breathing protection.

Kemppi also launched its Minarc Cordless 150C portable and cordless welding machine at EuroBLECH 2016. It has been designed for demanding welding tasks.

"The Minarc Cordless is a contact-less charging battery welding system, which can be charged fully in half an hour. It is IP65 class, with no fan in the welding machine, and designed as a simple, usable welding system. It is another of our new innovations at EuroBLECH," explained Lanz.

Lanz expects single-digit growth in 2016 and, on the back of the new launches at EuroBLECH, higher growth next year. IoT and Industry 4.0 is the over-riding vision for this Finnish company, alongside safe welding.

## Laser in focus

"One of the highlights at EuroBLECH for us this year, on the laser side, is a 3kW version of the ENSIS fibre laser cutting machine. The 3kW version gives us extra speed for thin material processing and expands the machine's thicker range as well," Wood told ISMR.

"We are also displaying our LCG range of fibre laser cutting machines which we already offer in 2- and 4kW versions – we are now showing 6- and 9kW models here at EuroBLECH. The extra power offers extra speed for processing mid-range/thick material. 4kW power, for most people, is quick enough to process thick range material. The 6kW and 9kW version expands this for them so they can cut thicker materials more quickly. The higher laser powers they use, the thicker mild steel they can cut with nitrogen, which more and more people want to do now. If you cut it with oxygen, you have to treat it before you paint it. Now, users can nitrogen cut 12mm thick steel, paint it and move on."

The new ENSIS fibre laser on the booth was fitted with an 8-station nozzle changer and the new 'WACS II' Water Assisted Cutting System for thick mild steel processing. AMADA's patented beam control technology, which automatically adapts the laser to the material thickness, allows thicker material processing with much reduced power consumption and a positioning speed of 170 m/min.

AMADA also exhibited its new line-up of LCG-3015AJ flatbed laser cutting machines with AMADA-developed 6- and 9kW fibre laser oscillators. They are designed for high cutting speeds and low running costs with the ability to cut copper, brass and titanium. The LCG-AJ 6 and 9 kW have a lightweight Y-axis carriage with low centre of gravity, providing a 30% weight reduction.

"For the 6kW fibre laser, we are showing a fully automated tower system which has halved the sheet loading time to a minute and a half. This was designed primarily to keep up with the speed of the fibre lasers. The automation cell is completed with our new TK device, the part remover, on the machine. We are also showing a fibre laser version of our ACIES servo-electric combination punch/laser. This is available as a CO₂ laser cutting system, but we are showing the 4kW punch/fibre laser version with full automation available here for users who want to cut parts quickly with reduced power," explained Matt Wood.

The weakness of fibre lasers historically has been the quality of the aluminium and stainless steel cutting, although cutting speeds are high. AMADA has been focusing on ways to increase the quality of these materials with fibre lasers. At EuroBLECH, it presented samples of new fibre laser cutting technology, called Silky Cut Fibre. On the samples, the quality of the fibre laser cut is equal to that cut on a CO₂ laser cutting system.

"Customers are interested in how much a part costs to make on a machine. That is partly why we have expanded the range of machines because, if you can cut faster, the part cost can come down. However, we don't have a fixation on power. Our laser range now consists of 2-, 3-, 4-, 6- and 9kW versions but, in most cases, 2-, 3- and 4kW is more than enough for what most users want. With the productivity you get from a 6- or 9kW version, users have to consider whether their production can keep up with the high flow of parts from these laser cutting systems. Higher power does not necessarily mean that users will earn more money from their laser – a 4kW and a press brake may earn a user more money than a 9kW fibre laser. It is all about a balanced investment," he added.

## Expanded welding capabilities

AMADA's FLW-ENSIS, showcased on AMADA's booth at EuroBLECH, is designed to take laser welding to a higher level of quality and capability, as well as reducing overall lead times. Its new solution incorporates an AMADA-developed 3kW fibre laser oscillator, ENSIS technology to enable deeper penetration welds than an existing 4kW fibre laser oscillator and a new filler wire system.

AMADA's ENSIS technology enables deeper penetration of the welds. The new 'Push-Pull' filler wire feeding system ensures clean, continuous welds of large gaps, while the rotating fixture table has been designed to increase load capacity and range of movements. The robotic welding arm has a large working envelope.

AMADA Miyachi Europe also showcased fibre laser welding technology for high-speed welding applications at EuroBLECH 2016. The Miyachi EAPRO Jupiter Fibre Laser Welding System and the AWS3 Active Welding System 3, an integrated resistance welding solution, was also on display on the stand.

The fibre laser's power level and high beam quality allows power of up to 700W to be fired into very precise weld spots for the Jupiter system. The AWS3 Active Welding System 3 is an integrated resistance welding solution that provides process control, monitoring and quality analysis. Available in either servomotorised or pneumatic versions, the AWS3 can be used alone as a benchtop system or integrated into a production line.

## New bending solutions

"On the bending side, we are showing various solutions. One is a robotic solution, HG-1003ARs, aimed at automating the bending process. On the loading station, a camera can scan where the part is and ensure that it is the right part before the robot picks it up. We have also added an unloading conveyor system to increase the flexibility of the whole system. The press brake has our auto tool-changer connected to it so that, when it finishes one job, the robot can change the gripper, while the tools are changing in the background for the next job, then pick up the part and carry on," explained Matt Wood.

## Above: ACIES-AJ punching and fibre laser cutting combination machine

"We are also showing a 4m 220 ton standalone press brake with the auto tool-changer to fold various large parts. The average set-up time on a press brake is 30 minutes – the setup time with this system is two minutes."

European legislation imposes safety conditions on the operation of machines, which stipulates a reduction in the speed of moving parts that put the operator at risk. AMADA has tackled this point to develop a solution to manage the risk factors linked to these moving parts, while retaining maximum productivity rates that exploit top machine speeds.

The solution drastically cuts down the loss of time between bends, when the operator has to wait for the positioning stops to move into place. The "trick" that makes this happen has a name: FAST (Finger with Active Security Technology). These new stop fingers, that can be fitted onto most new AMADA bending machines, have an active safety device built in that limits risk to operators if they come into contact with moving parts, even at full speed. This safety feature and "productivity provider" was introduced at EuroBLECH 2016 on the HFE 3i 1003 machine equipped with the new generation of AFH tools and connected to the VPSS 3i BEND software.

## 3i - intelligence, interaction and integration

AMADA's solutions incorporate all the latest digital technology developed over a number of years, in line with industry innovations gearing towards the factory of the future, "Smart Factory" or "Industry 4.0". AMADA developed the VPSS 3i Software Suite to enable unified programming of all AMADA technologies (blank, bend and weld). It simulates and verifies each production step to prevent any possible error. The VPSS 3i suite and all associated software are part of AMADA's wider approach to Smart Manufacturing: V-factory.

"In line with Industry 4.0 and IoT, we are showing a prototype here of our V-Factory. It essentially takes the information from our machines, shows their productivity, running status, historical data etc. The next step is to use more cloud-based technologies to remotely configure the order, send it to the different software packages, produce the parts and then receive cloud-based reports. You could be anywhere in Europe, log in via the cloud and see all this data. The plans we have for the future of this are very interesting. We have a software booth here at EuroBLECH to demonstrate this, using smartphones, tablets etc. that display our machine data on the booth. The sheet metal industry, as a whole, is probably at the forefront of this Industry 4.0 revolution and I think other industries will follow. The younger generation expects information in this form - instant data for instant decision-making," explained Matt Wood.

"One thing I will also emphasise is the AMADA Digital Support System (ADSS). We can connect the machine to the internet, users can send reports of machine activity and we can compile a report on overall machine performance for a customer. It includes information like average production ratio, number of sheets processed etc. This is useful information that customers can compare month to month. We also use it as a proactive service tool for the machines. There are certain thresholds that are set on the machines and we can pick this up in the reports and arrange a technician visit to correct or verify any settings. Basically, we are booking a preventive breakdown visit before the machine breaks down. The whole point is to keep the machine running, and uptime high. Customers love this service."

AMADA's V-factory and 3i solutions, standing for interactivity, integration and intelligence, is a tailored approach to smart manufacturing which aims to fit sheet metal manufacturers needs, now and in the future.■
