Live welding guidance system helps welders establish proper technique

Realityworks

®

Inc. has introduced the guideWELD™ LIVE welding guidance system to its line of Real Career

®

welding products. The system takes the step from simulation to real life, providing live welding guidance to help users establish proper position and technique while welding.

The system provides immediate in-helmet feedback on three core aspects of welding technique: speed, work angle, and travel angle. This real-time feedback occurs inside the welding helmet in the user’s periphery vision, so both the feedback and the weld puddle can be monitored simultaneously. By monitoring that feedback, users can see—while welding—if they need to correct their technique.

Designed for education, the system includes comprehensive curricula that feature lessons on welding techniques, weld defect correction, and weld safety, as well as study tools for students and educational resources for teachers. It is preprogrammed with nine welding procedure specifications (WPSs), and customizable WPSs can be programmed. Features include an autodarkening welding helmet with guidance display, a speed sensor with base, and an angle sensor that works on any GMAW machine.

Roll grooving machine delivers accuracy for sprinkler pipes

Industrial Solutions & Innovation LLC offers the Copier GM2 Groove Master. The stationary machine presses a groove into metal pipe to allow the connection of a coupling for sprinkler pipe systems.

The pipe roll grooving machine grooves pipes up to 24 in. OD and 0.39 in. thick. Different roll sets are required for grooving different pipe diameters. Exchanging groove and drive rollers takes about 5 min., the company states. The depth of the groove is adjustable in the control panel of the machine. According to the manufacturer, the machine can groove one end of an 8-in. pipe with 0.31-in.-thick wall in less than 45 sec.

Required parameters are set up on the touchscreen. The unit can store IPC programs to be used again later. Program setup takes 2 min., says the company, and no mechanical adjustments for the grooving depth are required. High control of the grooving process helps reduce flaring. In addition, the system uses no hydraulics and is simple to install and move in the factory.

Once setup is complete, the operator puts the end of the pipe over the drive roll and pushes the start button. The groove roll comes down and stops when it touches the pipe, and the operator pushes the start button again to begin grooving. When the machine reaches the correct depth, it grooves a few rounds without feeding the grooving roll, then stops, and the grooving roll goes up, allowing the operator to check the dimensions of the groove.

Groove roll feeding speed and drive roll rotating speed are not influenced by grooving forces. If flaring occurs, the operator can adjust the speed to minimize it.

Small robots offer large work envelope

ABB Robotics has introduced the IRB 1200, a new family of small robots that feature a compact footprint and a large work envelope for material handling, machine tending, and small-parts assembly applications.

The robot is designed with no offset in axis 2, delivering a longer stroke that allows the robot to be placed very close to the workpiece and still be functional. Combined with other features, the longer stroke enables up to 15 percent smaller workcells and 10 percent shorter cycle times than comparable robots, the company reports.

Two models are available: a 27.5-in.-reach robot with a payload of up to 15.43 lbs., and a longer-reach 35.43-in. model with a payload up to 11.02 lbs. Both models can be mounted at any angle and come with IP40 protection as standard; IP67 protection is available as an option.
