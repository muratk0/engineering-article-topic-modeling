# Roofing systems specialist, Alwitra GmbH, is using a new type of automated welding process. This is designed to overcome the physical limits of the short arc and increase component quality using heat reduction.

[TARİH: 01.11.2024 ISMR]

The requirements for efficiency, quality and process optimisation are constantly increasing in the manufacturing industry. In light of these challenges, the use of innovative welding technologies is proving to be a decisive competitive advantage.

Alwitra, a leading German provider of system solutions for flat roofs, relies on the new EWM React process for automated welding for control of droplet transfer for weld seams. Benefits for the manufacturer include increased welding speed and reduced heat input.

This new type of welding process has been designed by arc welding specialist, EWM, to overcome the physical limits of the short arc and increase component quality using heat reduction.

## Roofing systems specialist

Providing over 175 million square metres of sealed roof surface, Alwitra GmbH is one of the leading providers of system solutions for flat roofs. It specialises in integrated flat roofing solutions including roofing membranes, aluminium profiles and system accessories.

Since setting up in Trier (Germany) in 1964, the company has launched numerous innovations. Its core business includes roof waterproofing membranes; roof edge profiles; roof edge covers; wall connecting profiles; fastening systems for secure installation of solar systems and installation of daylight systems; and flat roof drainage systems. Its products are developed and manufactured at sites in Trier and Hermeskeil, where around 300 people are employed.

Almost all its products need to be welded, which is why joining technology is so important for the company. The quality of the weld seam must impress across many designs, so that it is not only highly functional but also looks aesthetically pleasing.

"We are continuously adapting our product range to market requirements while taking innovative approaches," explained Florian Pfeifer, production manager in Alwitra's aluminium division. "To best meet the needs of our customers, we rely on the use of high-quality welding machines."

## Automation of standard products

Up to now, Alwitra has relied on a standard pulse MIG process to manufacture its components. Due to the large proportion of bespoke products that it manufactures, the level of automation in the company remains low. The proportion of automated welding at Alwitra is around 30 per cent, but this number is growing. This is because automation of welding processes is particularly well suited to the production of standard components.

"We see great potential here, as we can improve the quality of the results while reducing costs at the same time," emphasised Florian Pfeifer. "When using welding robots, process stability also plays a major factor for us."

“EWM React comes into play here. The combination of a short arc process and digital welding process control, supplemented by a dynamic torch drive, is designed to enable a precise automated welding process that delivers perfect weld seams," added welding specialist, EWM.

## Controlled droplet transfer

"React" stands for "reversing actively controlled transfer." In this new process from EWM, the welding droplet is transferred to the weld pool in a controlled manner. This is possible by regulating the welding voltage and welding current, as well as by moving the wire forwards and backwards.

The wire is actively moved towards the weld pool at high speed. The arc ignites on the molten metal and produces a molten droplet at the same time. At the point where the wire comes into contact with the molten metal, the molten droplet in the short circuit is transferred to the weld pool. The RCC module controls the short-circuit resolution. As a result of the active backwards movement, the wire is drawn from the weld pool and the arc reignites.

“The unique process is the result of combining our powerful Titan XQ R power source with integrated RCC (Rapid Current Control) module and React control. The active wire movement in the high-frequency range is generated using a special EWM robot torch with a highly dynamic motor. The EWM process variants (Speed, Positionweld and SuperPulse) allow the process to be tailored to the customer's individual requirements. In some cases, the demanding welding tasks require the conventional pulsed arc to be intelligently combined with the React process. This wide variety allows us to supply Alwitra with exactly the right setting for each individual seam," outlined EWM.

## Low spatter and minimal distortion

The high deposition rates of the spray arc range are combined with all the benefits of the short arc. The result is a low-spatter process similar to the pulsed arc, but cooler for all power ranges. According to EWM, because of the process stability, welding speeds twice as high as with a classic short arc can be achieved while heat input can be reduced by up to 35%.

“We were able to minimise the distortion of our materials," highlighted Florian Pfeifer. "EWM React also made it possible to improve weld seam quality by making welding spatter and burn-through a thing of the past."

“If the weld seam burns through, the weld metal on the underside must be ground away, an additional labour-intensive process. If aluminium is used, the entire component must be remanufactured if it burns through. EWM React now increases the quality of the welding results. Another benefit of the automated process is the increased welding speed. The high deposition rate allows components to be welded more quickly. At the same time, the amount of rework is significantly reduced because of the stable and spatter-free welding process. For Alwitra, this translates into time savings of more than one minute per workpiece, both during welding and for postweld work," added EWM.
