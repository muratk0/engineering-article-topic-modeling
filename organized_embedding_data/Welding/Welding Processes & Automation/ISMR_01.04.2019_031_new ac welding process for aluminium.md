# New AC welding process for aluminium

[TARİH: 01.04.2019 ISMR]

WITH THE TITAN XQ 400 AC puls, Germany's largest manufacturer of welding machines, EWM, aims to revolutionise the MIG welding of aluminium. Its new acArc puls XQ alternating current welding process for manual and automated welding can even be applied to extremely thin sheets. The weld seam is, said the company, 'as pure as a diamond'. Alongside the new MIG-AC welding process "acArc puls XQ", all the established and optimised EWM DC welding processes (such as the forceArc puls XQ, rootArc XQ or coldArc XQ) are included in the machine at no additional cost.

"The water-cooled Titan XQ 400 AC puls has been specially developed for aluminium welding. Even the thinnest sheets can be easily and safely joined together – without the molten metal falling through and with reduced distortion. In the new 'acArc puls XQ' welding process, the welding current alternates during the process sequence between positive and negative polarity. This makes the arc easy to control, even when it comes to AlMg alloys. It can bridge large air gaps well, even during automated application. The new process leaves behind barely any traces of smoke; the weld seam is clean and shiny thanks to the highly-reduced magnesium-oxide. In addition, welding fume emissions are reduced," EWM told ISMR.

"The Positionweld process guarantees optimum welding during positional welding. In combination with the acArc puls XQ welding process, MIG welding seams have a 'TIG look'. Even soft aluminium wire is accurately fed by the Drive XQ wire feeder. With many options available (such as the wire spool heater, the electronic gas flow control or the wire reserve sensor), users can configure a welding machine that matches their requirements and welding tasks."

The acArc puls XQ welding process is suitable for both manual and automated welding. It has also been designed for the additive manufacturing of aluminium parts (3D welding), due to its low heat input. See www.ewm-group.com/en/

## Laser marker

THE INNOVATION AND Research Centre (IFC) Tuttlingen of the Hochschule Furtwangen University of Applied Sciences (HFU) and FOBA Laser Marking + Engraving have officially launched their innovation cooperation for medical technology. At a ceremony on 20 March 2019 in the new IFC rooms on Katharinenstraße, FOBA presented its M2000-P fibre laser marking system to the university for its research, as well as to interested parties from the industry for demonstration purposes.

"Seventy-five per cent of our work is applied research," said innovation manager, Regina Storz-Irion who, together with Professor Griselda Guidoni, established contact with FOBA. "The goal of our cooperation with industrial companies is to create benefits for companies in the region. It also happens by putting science into practice, for example through students' theses."

In two lectures, it became clear (from a scientific and practical perspective) how the labelling of different surfaces (using laser technology) works, with technical challenges highlighted. Materials scientist, Professor Griselda Guidoni, researches which laser parameters are most suitable for which material. To achieve durable and high-contrast inscriptions, the laser beam must change the surface in such a way that, in addition to the desired marking effect, there is no damage to the material. Depending upon the metal or plastic properties, appropriate laser parameters (such as wavelength or pulse duration) are determined individually.

"Parameters for certain materials are already integrated into our system software," explained Christian Söhner from FOBA. "This way, the user can fall back on empirical values when setting up a marking job. The camera integrated in the marking system also contributes to safe and error-free marking."
