# Changing tube diameters without changing the setup

[TARİH: 01.10.2013 The Fabricator]

Technology Spotlight

Automated system eliminates tool changes, establishes flow

By Eric Lundin, Contributing Editor

The Flexistar comes in two models, handling wall thickness from 0.008 in. to 0.0039 in.

If you fabricate thin-walled tubular products, such as chimney flues, stovepipes, or catalytic converters, you probably use a conventional, labor-intensive process to form the tube and weld the seam. Many fabricators start with a stack of sheet metal blanks, form them on a manual rolling machine, then use a custom-made welding jig and a manual welder to make the seams. Others use an external seam welder, loading blanks one at a time, and relying on conventional manual setup and controls.

If you manufacture a more complex product, such as metal bellows, you probably use a purpose-built tube mill and a cold saw. Many of the mills in use today are decades old and don’t have modern controls or monitors, and they require manual setups and changeovers.

Flexistar, a forming and welding system from Weil Engineering, Novi, Mich., automates these processes. It handles the variety of diameters and shapes necessary for making many common thin-walled products.

“Traditional tube production lines use hard tooling in the forming section and at the cutoff,” said Dan Robinson, vice president of sales for Weil. “Changeover from one diameter to another is a time-consuming task, so fabricators change from one size to another as infrequently as they can, resulting in batch production.”

The operation starts with a suction cup blank destacker that separates up to four blanks from a stack and loads them onto an infeed table (a coil feeder is optional). The roll forming section uses three servo-driven CNC rolls to form the tube to a specified shape: round, oval, or rounded square. After the forming is done, the tube is secured in a variable-diameter support and conveyed to the welding area. The tube moves beneath the stationary welding head, which is powered by the user’s choice of CO

2

, fiber, disk, or diode laser.

Establishing Versatile Flow

“This unit does two things,” Robinson said. “First, it allows the user to make a variety of shapes and sizes without changeovers. The smaller model, the 150/2200, makes tubes in diameters from 2 3/8 to 5 7/8 in. in wall thicknesses from 0.008 to 0.032 in. The length range is from 11¾ to 86 5/8. The other model, the 400/1250, makes diameters from 3.15 to 15¾ in. in wall thicknesses from 0.016 to 0.039 in., and lengths from 9 3/4 to 49 1/8 in.

“It also is versatile enough to let the user establish a flow of parts,” he continued. “Let’s say you need a double-walled tube. You don’t have to make a batch of one diameter, then a batch of the other diameter. You can program the machine to make alternating diameters, providing a continuous supply of parts for the next step in the process.”

For increased automation, users that start with blanks can add a pallet changer. Downstream options include end-processing units to bead or expand the tube and a marking unit.

The machine works with carbon steels, corrosion-resistant steels, and coated materials, and the company can build units to work with other materials.

The Flexistar machines can be programmed for a series of unique parts, eliminating batch production.
