# THE WELDING FACTOR

[TARİH: 01.11.2019 ISMR]

Harald Scherleitner, global director of the Fronius Business Unit Perfect Welding, gives ISMR his views on trends and directions in welding markets as well as his strategic and technical development objectives.

Fronius is a specialist in arc and robot-assisted welding. As a systems provider, the Fronius Welding Automation division also offers customised automated complete welding solutions e.g. for the construction of containers or offshore cladding. The range is rounded off by power sources for manual applications, welding accessories and a broad spectrum of services. Fronius Perfect Welding has more than 1000 sales partners worldwide.

Fronius International GmbH is an Austrian company with headquarters in Pettenbach and other sites in Wels, Thalheim, Steinhaus and Sattledt. With 4,760 employees globally, the company is active in welding technology, photovoltaics and battery charging technology. 92 per cent of its products are exported through 30 international Fronius subsidiaries and sales partners/representatives in over 60 countries.

Harald Scherleitner, global director of the Fronius Business Unit Perfect Welding, gave ISMR his views on trends and directions in welding markets as well as his strategic and technical development objectives.

I believe that listening is our key success factor to fulfilling customers' welding requirements

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes/achievements and the reasons for this?

HS: Looking back to 2019, we are very satisfied with our development in terms of gaining market share and customers, in all industries, who trust us. Our defined "Customer Intimacy Strategy" focuses on our customers, their expectations and their requirements. I believe that listening is our key success factor to fulfilling customers' welding requirements, whether these involve manual, semi-automatic robot-assisted or fully automatic welding applications.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals?

HS: Global growth will be slower, but I don't see a massive recession coming in the next year. We do not know exactly what the situation in the automotive industry will be and this will greatly influence metal professionals. We are entering a new stage where, in effect, growth is slowing down – but only slightly. The global tensions caused by the trade war between the United States and China affect growth in both mature and emerging markets. This will lead to a certain amount of slowdown. Nevertheless, we are optimistic about the future.

ISMR: Please explain your strategic and technical objectives?

HS: Our strategy is closely linked to our three lines of business. For our Industrial Welding Systems business, we specialise in MIG/ MAG processes and distribute via direct sales. Manual welding systems, which we call Professional Welding Tools, require completely different sales channels, just like Welding Automation. Welding Automation, in particular, is a project-based business field in which we offer highly specific system solutions.

To pursue this strategy, Fronius is investing heavily in its sales organisation. More teams should ultimately lead to intensive collaboration with the customer, resulting in an in-depth understanding of the customer. We will ultimately become a solutions provider. We believe that this is the key to success.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

HS: In all industries, productivity and transparency are core issues that need to be addressed. In many sectors, material requirements are also becoming increasingly complex. We provide support with a wide range of welding equipment and characteristic curves.

High-performance devices, such as TPS/i TWIN Push, are particularly suitable for long seams and large-volume components. Their high deposition rate and welding speed increase productivity. In addition, a long duty cycle reduces the cycle time. In mechanised operation, the TPS 600i can weld continuously at 500 ampere. The TPS/i Steel Edition is an ideal machine for manual steel welding. With standard and pulse characteristics specifically for steel applications, it offers an attractive price-performance ratio.

Fronius welding systems can be networked with the WeldCube documentation and data analysis software. The software collects data such as current, voltage, wire feed and welding speed. With this information and various statistical and filter functions, welding processes can be optimised. The networking of power sources enables data to be documented not only per machine, but also at component level. This enhances transparency and traceability.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus/ vision over the next few years?

HS: Comprehensive market analyses have shown us where the highest interest in premium products can be found. At the same time, we asked ourselves which segments could be best served with our service portfolio. On this basis, we now focus on three essential customer segments.

In the Automotive sector, we are well established and we see e-mobility as a major opportunity over the next few years. To expand further, we are focusing on our newly defined Yellow Goods and Commercial Transport segments. Growing global population and continuous urbanisation are economic drivers for these customer segments.

An additional sector is important for market penetration. We believe this is the General Industry sector i.e. the metalworking industry. Fronius not only strives to be the technology leader but, with its excellent direct sales structure, is also focused on achieving the best possible customer relationship.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in welding markets? Which challenges and opportunities do they offer?

HS: Of course, digitisation is one of the main trends in all fields of life – including the welding industry. We see this as a huge opportunity. More autonomously working welding systems and production lines are an effective means to combat the shortage of skilled workers that many countries are facing nowadays. Moreover, digitisation in the industrial field offers opportunities for new business models.

Another important trend concerns mobility. Historically, the automotive industry is one of the most important customer segments for Fronius. This field is changing dramatically. Around 70 metres of weld seams used to be required for the body of a mid-range car; today, only 14 metres are required. However, e-mobility also offers new opportunities for the welding business: battery trays have long weld seams with very high requirements. We see this as a major opportunity.

ISMR: Are there any products/ technologies that you have released, or are releasing this year, that you would like to discuss?

HS: Fronius will be releasing a new generation of mobile TIG welding machines for manual welding in January 2020. The TransTig 210 and 170 are very compact welding machines (only 9.8kg) with many helpful functionalities for TIG and stick welding. One of the highlights is the long duty cycle. At a maximum current of 210 or 170 ampere respectively, non-stop welding for four minutes is possible – one minute longer than with comparable welding machines.

In January 2020, Fronius will also launch new Wire Sense technology – an assistance system for robot welding. This technology uses the wire electrode to scan the workpiece to detect workpiece edges, even on thin materials in robotic welding, without additional optical measurement systems. Compared to other sensor technologies such as optical measurement systems, Wire Sense is costefficient, rapid and robust. No adjustment between the torch (TCP) and sensor is necessary since the torch is the sensor. Detected component irregularities, such as gaps, are compensated for by adjusted welding parameters in the background – a big step towards adaptive robotic welding.
