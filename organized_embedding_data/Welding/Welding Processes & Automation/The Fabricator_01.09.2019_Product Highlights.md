Audio added to weld camera system

Xiris Automation Inc. has developed a weld microphone system that integrates into its weld camera system. This gives users the ability to use sight and sound while welding to fine-tune and monitor the welding process from a safe distance, such as in a control room.

The microphone system is designed for the welding environment and includes the microphone, cables, and a specialized signal processing card embedded in the HMI. Additional software features include the ability to record and play back audio with the video and to equalize the audio to enhance or minimize surrounding noise.

Electric-drive press brake can be combined with industrial robot

LVD Strippit has introduced the Dyna-Press 40/15 Pro high-speed, electric-drive press brake. The 40-ton press brake features a 59-in. working length and a 5-axis backgauge. Able to bend at speeds of up to 1 IPS, the machine can be combined with an industrial robot to create a compact, high-speed robotized bending cell (Dyna-Cell) for automated bending operations.

The electric ram offers a smooth transition from approach to working speed and helps minimize power consumption through the use of an optimal power-to-inertia-motor ratio. The coupling between the ram and the servomotors is made using two heavy-duty ball screws to distribute force and tonnage evenly across the working length.

Features include vertical removal tooling, seated or standing operation, an Iris Lazer Safe guarding system, and a 15-in. Touch-B touchscreen control.

LVD Strippit

www.lvdgroup.com

Flap disc designed for aluminum, nonferrous metals

Lawson Products Inc. has introduced the Blue-Kote™ soft material application flap disc. According to the company, it grinds aluminum and all nonferrous metals at high speed with minimal clogging or loading while keeping the work surface temperature low for quality finishing.

The 4.5-in.-dia., Type 27 abrasive disc is available in 40 and 60 grit. It simultaneously grinds and finishes, eliminating the need for product changeover. The disc cloth’s net structure resists clogging on soft materials, and its sanded surface stays cooler to prevent burning the work surface.

The disc is suitable for use in pre- and postweld operations in such applications as automotive, agriculture, aviation, construction, energy, food and beverage, metal fabrication, shipbuilding, technology, and mass transportation.

Lawson Products Inc.

www.lawsonproducts.com

Helmet offers clear view of weld

The new, automatic Force 900 welding helmet from Translas is suitable for welding, grinding, and cutting. Features include a ClearView cartridge, flip-up sensitivity lens, four sensitivity sensors, 107- by 75-mm viewing area, and a matte finish.

An extended hood made from fire-resistant cotton material and extended leather neck protection are available to help protect the helmet wearer from burns caused by welding spatters. The pieces can be used separately or as a set.

Translas

www.translas.com

Welding robot offers long reach

Offering an extra-long wrist range for flexibility, Yaskawa Motoman’s new 6-axis AR3120 robot features a 3,124-mm horizontal reach and 5,622-mm vertical reach.

According to the company, the robot has the highest payload, fastest axis speeds, and top wrist allowable moment in its class. It is suitable for use in high-density layouts with multiple robots working in close proximity. Its slim profile features a streamlined arm design for reduced interference, facilitating access to parts in tight spaces.

A 20-kg payload capacity supports a variety of motorized torches and sensors, and a symmetric wrist profile provides equal torch access to both sides of the part. An optimized feeder mounting area on the arm helps reduce equipment interference.
