Solid wire helps minimize spatter on galvanized steel

ESAB Welding & Cutting Products has launched OK AristoRod® 38 Zn solid wire. Through microalloying elements in its formulation, the wire allows zinc vapor to escape, which in turn minimizes porosity and spatter on galvanized steels.

It is available in diameters of 0.030, 0.040, and 0.045 in. in 33- and 40-lb. spools and 550-lb. Marathon Pacs. The wire is classified as an AWS A5.18 ER70S-G.

Press brake offers two angle measuring systems

TRUMPF offers the TruBend Series 5000, which comes equipped with an on-demand servo drive with fourcylinder drive technology. The servo drive operates quietly and consumes energy only during the bending process.

The machine features two angle measuring systems. Automatically controlled bending (ACB) takes measurements through sensors integrated into the upper tool. It is suitable for bending short sides and reflective surfaces and for multipoint measurement. The second system, the ACB Laser, measures the bending angle through a contactless optical process, making it compatible with any tooling. It is useful for processing acute and open angles, as well as thick sheet metal.

The bending process is facilitated by the Touchpoint TruBend control. This interface features 3D visualization and a multitouch screen that responds to the operator’s touch even when work gloves are worn.

Laser-based optical triangulation sensors designed for seam tracking

ABB Robotics offers optical seam tracking in two styles. The 2.5D optical sensors have one laser stripe to measure Y and Z and orientation around X. The 6D optical sensors use three laser stripes to measure both joint position (X, Y, Z) and orientation (three Euler angles).

Both styles of laser-based optical triangulation sensors perform a noncontact measurement of the joint profile, refine the garnered joint information, and then forward the information to the robot, allowing necessary weld path corrections to be made on-the-fly.

In addition to providing weld path corrections, optical tracking sensors also can send additional joint information to the welding robot, allowing on-the-fly adjustments to weld travel speed, wire feed speed, and weave shape and size.
