# Why automate the weld process? We highlight the rise of robotic welding and chronicle the capabilities of cobot welding systems for manufacturers.

[TARİH: 01.02.2023 ISMR]

To combat the chronic shortage of welders in the global sheet-metal industry, robotic welding offers an avenue that can speed up and automate the process for accuracy and less waste. Common welding processes that can incorporate robotics include arc welding; resistance welding; spot welding; TIG/MIG welding; laser welding and plasma welding.

“High quality requirements, sustainability aspects and safety standards are driving the demands placed on welding technology even higher. Nowadays, reproducible welding quality and complete welding data documentation are state of the art. But if welding takes place manually, these demands cannot be met. The solution is automation, even for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs)," stated welding specialist, Fronius.

Certified human operators still need to remain close to the robots. The operators program the controller using a teach pendant. These automated systems come with multiple safety features. Arc shields prevent the high-heat arc from mixing with oxygen. Enclosed areas protect operators from the temperatures and bright light.

## Cobot welding systems

Long dismissed as too heavy-duty a task for cobots, welding is now the fastest growing application segment for Universal Robots (UR).

"The channel grew more than 80% over the first nine months of 2022 compared to last year, with well over 1,200 cobots expected to ship in the welding vertical this year," said the robot manufacturer.

UR's partners are continuously developing new capabilities for the cobot welders. At the FABTECH exhibition at the end of 2022, it showcased new solutions from Vectis Automation and Hirebotics for welding larger and more complex parts. Laser welding applications with Cobot Systems were also highlighted at FABTECH 2022. At Universal Robots' FABTECH booth, Vectis Automation launched the new Park'N'Arc; a 'diving board' rotational range extender that allows for the base of the cobot to be manually moved to various locations.

"Compared to a short linear track, the Park'N'Arc is an improved design for increasing range as the cobot base can be translated nearly 8ft in a linear direction while maintaining simplicity, robust cable management and portability," said Josh Pawley, co-founder of Vectis Automation.

Another new take on welding larger parts, typically out of the cobot's reach, was shown by Hirebotics and partner, Kinetic Technologies LLC. They launched RT1, a new cobot-controlled rotary table designed to maximize welding capacity. Cobot Systems, a UR Certified Systems Integrator (CSI), also launched “the first commercially available IPG LightWELD laser welder to be guided and controlled by a cobot."

UR's new 20kg payload cobot, the UR20, greatly expands automation opportunities such as the ability to reach further into machines, tend several machines in the same cycle and handle 25% heavier parts than other UR cobot models.

Other manufacturers have also brought their own cobot welding systems to market. The CWC-S Cobot Welding Cell from Fronius, for example, includes a Fanuc Cobot CX10 iA with a reach of 1,249mm and a 10kg payload as well as a welding table with a working area of 1,500 x 1,000 x 700mm. A pneumatic glare shield, sliding side doors with viewing windows and an extraction hood protect operators.

“Programming a CWC-S is intuitive and can be quickly learned by any welding specialist; programming expertise is not required. Components can even be loaded and welding programs started by trainees," explained Christian Neuhofer, Product Manager, Fronius Welding Automation.“This saves on personnel costs and frees professional welders for more challenging work, making their job more attractive."

The welding torch is manually guided to the starting points, position points and end points on the component and these are confirmed by pressing a button. The software uses this information to calculate the welding contours and welding sequence, while the required welding parameters can also be selected using predefined characteristics. Motion commands such as "pendulum parameters" or “weld job" can be inserted by dragging and dropping on the touchscreen of the Cobot control.

Before starting the welding process, the operator can simulate the welding sequence once again to detect and correct possible interference contours. Once the CWC-S is fully programmed, it welds component after component with consistent quality and at a regular speed.

Robotic laser welding is inherently precise. Laser welding beams create deep and accurate welds without using nearly as much energy or resources as traditional welding processes. Since laser welds are so precise and focused, welds are cleaner and require far less touch-up or rework.

When combined with an advanced 3D vision system, welding accuracy increases exponentially, even for high-volume, multipass parts. Some of the most meaningful advances in laser welding technology have been from vision technology. Scanner optical configurations, scanner controls and real-time interfaces for optical sensors enable laser welding in far more complex applications.

## In conclusion

Robotic welding offers a variety of benefits over manual welding, particularly for larger jobs. These include speed; safety; consistent quality; efficiency; increased throughput and higher productivity (a robotic welder can operate continuously, maintaining the same speed, rate and weld paths over and over). Automating the welding process is an effective way of improving the accuracy and repeatability of the weld. Added value includes reduced waste/ rework; lower operating costs (although upfront costs can be high); job creation and better working environments for employees.

"In applications where customers expect uniform weld bead profile and penetration, mechanised, robotic and automated systems deliver consistency, reduce welding in uncomfortable positions and reduce operator exposure to fumes and UV radiation," concluded welding specialist, ESAB.
