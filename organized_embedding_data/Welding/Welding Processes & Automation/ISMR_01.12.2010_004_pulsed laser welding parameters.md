# PULSED LASER WELDING PARAMETERS

[TARİH: 01.12.2010 ISMR]

“For small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) which use lasers to weld thin metal sheets, lasers with continuous wave radiation are often too expensive. This is due to the high output powers necessary for the welding process. Pulsed Nd:YAG lasers are economically interesting, and offer an alternative to continuous wave lasers. Due to their high peak pulse output, welding with relatively low medium output power (100 W to 250 W) is possible, and investment costs can be reduced. However, the narrower welding seams from the pulsed lasers are not as strong as the seams from continuous lasers. Results can be improved if the parameter selection is optimised,” LZH told ISMR.

This was the goal of a recently completed project at the Laser Zentrum Hannover e.V. (LZH). The results of the project aim to simplify the parameter selection for pulsed laser welding, for example pulse duration, pulse form or pulse peak output. Reference parameters for welding with continuous wave radiation have also been recorded. For both types of laser welding, high seam qualities could be qualified and weld imperfections avoided, as shown in surface testing and seam cross-sections.

For the investigations, both ferritic and austenitic steels with sheets thicknesses up to 1.5mm were butt joint welded. Thin foils with a minimal thickness of 50 µm were also butt-joint welded. To accomplish this, LZH developed and constructed a high precision clamping unit, which not only ensures a zero gap, but also avoids a lateral edge mismatch.

SMEs can use the results of the welding tests, which are available in an electronic database on the LZH website (www.lzh.de). The database provides welding and laser parameters as well as pictures of the expected seams surfaces, cross-sections and micro-hardness tests. Firms can use this data to estimate welding results. Apart from simplifying the selection of appropriate parameters, users can easily expand the database themselves by adding parameters of their own welding tasks to the database and accessing them when needed.
