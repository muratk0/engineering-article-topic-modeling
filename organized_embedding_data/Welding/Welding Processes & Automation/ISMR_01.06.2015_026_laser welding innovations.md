# Laser welding innovations

[TARİH: 01.06.2015 ISMR]
A number of manufacturers have released new laser welding products into the global marketplace over the past six months. One of these, TRUMPF, has been working on accuracy improvements for the laser welding process.

“Our SeamLine Pro, a time-tested sensor system, helps the laser welding beam to meet the seam gap exactly. For remote applications using a scanner, this task is assumed by a newly developed sensor system. Here a high speed camera, located almost coaxial to the laser beam, senses the seam and the position of the laser focus point simultaneously and adjusts the focusing optics so that the laser beam actually hits the seam,” commented TRUMPF.

“This new system is especially interesting for the automotive industry, because increasing numbers of robots and programmable focusing optics are being used for fillet welds. The advantage of fillet welds is that they enable small flanges. This saves weight and material, increases energy efficiency and reduces costs. To weld a fillet cleanly, the laser beam has to strike the seam exactly, and this is exactly what the new sensors guarantee.”

Comparing laser welding with conventional procedures shows that the costs per item can be reduced by up to 80 per cent, TRUMPF told ISMR. On display at its EuroBLECH booth in Hannover in October last year was TRUMPF’s new diode direct laser, the TruDiode 3006, in combination with the welding cell TruLaser Robot 5020. The advantage of the diode direct laser is its relatively high efficiency level – as much as 40 per cent. This makes it thrifty in its use of energy. Additional cost savings are achieved by the laser due to its space-saving form and the resulting small footprint. At EuroBLECH, the robot cell was equipped with a TruDiode 3006 delivering three kilowatts of power.

With this new laser system, TRUMPF is appealing to the entire sheet metal processing industry. The laser is suitable for deep welding and heat conduction welding and thus substitutes for conventional welding techniques. In the deep welding process, the laser joins materials that are several millimetres thick, achieving very high strength at the joint.

“During heat conduction welding, the new diode lasers create an even cleaner and more attractive appearance of the welding seam than is already the case with the TruDisk lasers. It is worthy of note that passive cooling and the extended service lives of the diode laser modules promise great availability and long life cycles. The laser possesses emergency running characteristics so that laser output, even if one module should fail, is not reduced. In this way, continuation of the process is ensured; there are no power losses at the workpiece and production can progress unhindered.”

ROFIN recently launched its Performance Open, an open laser class IV system for fine welding on long and bulky work-pieces.
