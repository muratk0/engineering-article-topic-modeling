Metal Mania highlights Wisconsin sculptors’ quick skills

Rafael Guerrero

M

ost Midwesterners would rather spend a sunny, warm Saturday doing something outdoors that befits summer-like weather. But on one mid-May Saturday, several high school and college students as well as adults gathered at Madison Area Technical College’s Fort Atkinson, Wis., campus to feel the warmth of electric welding arcs at the second-ever Metal Mania, a metal sculpture competition that showcases the school’s welding program and facilities.

Many in attendance would agree that the welding event did succeed at displaying area welding, fabrication, and creative talent and how the college and community can foster and celebrate that talent.

"Events like this showcase what opportunities the students have here," said Roger Bratberg, a Madison College instructor.

On the Clock

Six teams of four spent nearly six hours crafting large, inventive metal sculptures according to Metal Mania’s theme: Wisconsin farms.

The finished projects included cows, cheeseheads, combines, and tractors—all common symbols of Wisconsin’s farm culture. The sculptures were so heavy that a forklift was required to move them from the shop to the campus parking lot, where other portions of Metal Mania, including the grand finale, were held.

Two teams from last year’s inaugural Metal Mania donated their sculptures to the college. Pictured is a scrap metal turkey.

Days before the competition, the teams visited Alter Metal Recycling (formerly Loeb-Lorman) to pick out the scrap metal they would use for their sculptures.

The six teams featured community members; students from the Fort Atkinson, Johnson Creek, and Marshall school districts; and students from Madison College.

Students from Johnson Creek fabricated a cow sculpture that featured a bell and a cheesehead. The teenagers said they spent hours cutting, welding, and grinding.

"It was really fun, we liked it," said Alyssa Hucke, one of the Johnson Creek students.

They also admired the sculptures by the other teams.

"It was cool seeing how different teams had different ideas when it came to Wisconsin [farming]," said Dameon Klingman, another Johnson Creek contestant.

Cristian Lindeman, along with his father, uncle, and his uncle’s friend, built a sculpture depicting a combine in a field. It also featured a metal robin "flying" overhead.

"I was welding, cutting, measuring, putting stuff together, getting things ready and prepared," Lindeman said.

This year’s winners, the Fort Atkinson Blackhawk team, fabricated a sculpture depicting rustic-looking farm equipment and metallic cornstalks.

Teams had six hours to complete their scrap metal sculptures. All work was done inside the technical college’s welding and metal fabrication space.

For their hard work, the hometown team won the $1,000 grand prize for placing first.

You Have To Be Impressed

While this was the college’s second time hosting Metal Mania, this type of competition and event format is nothing new in the Fort Atkinson area. A similar event was previously held at the local scrapyard before it disappeared for a few years, reemerging as Metal Mania.

"Last year, we decided to fire it up here," said Jimmy DeGidio, the program director (metal fabrication) at Madison College.

Reminders of the inaugural Metal Mania can be found at the Fort Atkinson campus. Two teams from last year donated their sculptures—a metal sandhill crane and a metal turkey—to the college. They can be found on the south side of campus building’s exterior.

"They took a chain and made the neck for that turkey," Bratberg said. "When you see stuff like that, you just have to be impressed."

He continued: "I’ve been an educator for 25 years. You give me something linear, and I can do it in my head well. But when I watch these people work out here and it’s all abstract—they’re all just taking and creating things from junk—I’m always impressed with that mindset."

Metal Mania 2024—which included FMA as an event sponsor—raised more than $1,000 for Madison College’s Metal Fabrication scholarship program. Besides the metal competition, attendees ate; participated in laser, welding, and blacksmith demos; and toured the college’s renovated welding and metal fabrication space.
