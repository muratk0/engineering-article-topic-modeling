# Rural manufacturer facing welder shortage turns to robotic arc welding

[TARİH: 01.08.2025 The Fabricator]

Applications

Situation

The Will-Burt Co. is an international OEM serving many different industries, including defense, telecommunications, and energy. The company’s campus in Orville, Ohio, has more than 250,000 sq. ft. of manufacturing and assembly space, some of which is used to fabricate sheet metal for heavy machinery companies such as Caterpillar and JLG.

Will-Burt’s manufacturing production is very high-mix, low-volume in both the machine shop and on the fabrication side. When performed manually, this kind of operation requires a lot of setup and product-specific knowledge. And while the company has produced more than $30 million in sales through this facility in the last eight years, its location in a rural area has hampered its ability to find skilled welders. That labor shortage began to affect the company’s ability to take on additional work.

"When we looked out over our five-year plan for growing fabrication, we knew that we needed to supplement with automation to grow those sales," said Mike Gibb, vice president of operations. "For every cell that we introduced, we estimated we could generate about $4 million more in fabrication sales."

Resolution

In 2020, the company started working with WeldBot, a FANUC Authorized System Integrator, to develop an automated robotic arc welding system using a FANUC M-710iC/12L arc welding robot for its large parts.

"It’s one of the largest-reach robots out there, especially in the arc welding world," said Levi Bowman, president of WeldBot. "So that allowed us to reach a very large part work envelope."

WeldBot designed the system to have a headstock/tailstock positioner on one side and a drop center 2-axis positioner on the other side. This allows flexibility from one side to the other, enabling Will-Burt to run different varieties of parts on each side. Also, operators can load, unload, and weld parts on one side while the robot performs an operation on the other side simultaneously.

The company added another automated system using a FANUC Arc Mate 100iD/8L robot. In 2024, WeldBot integrated the cell, putting the new, smaller robot on a FANUC servo-driven track. This automation has not only helped add production throughput but also increased the product quality and production consistency.

"There’s a tremendous amount of welds," said Gibb. "There’s over 120 to 150 different welds on these complex fabrication applications. The odds of missing one obviously doesn’t exist with automation, but that was a problem that we were having before."

The company uses FANUC WeldPro for offline programming and its Touch Sensing system for locating weld seams and teaching the robot where the part is located.

On average, the robots are three to four times more efficient than manual welding and yield better welding quality. For every robotic system added, Will-Burt has gained 240 to 320 additional hours of welding per week, or about $4 million of contract fabrication work. The extra capacity and capabilities allow the facility to control costs and remain competitive.
