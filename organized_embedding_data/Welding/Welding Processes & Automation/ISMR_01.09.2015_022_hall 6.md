## IGM Robotersysteme AG www.igm.at

IGM will be presenting its compact 10-axis welding robot system, Type RSX, at Blechexpo 2015. This configuration consists of a vertical pillar with a rotary cantilever arm, mounted on the common base of the 2-axis manipulator. The RSX further features a 7-axis robot model RTE497, mounted on the rotating arm for high manoeuvrability and access to welding joints, even for areas of a workpiece that are difficult to reach. The new RCE control system offers improved programming comfort using the K6 teaching pendant.

The robot is equipped with tandem welding equipment – the double wire drive is built into the robot’s main axis while all welding media is guided through the hollow shaft in the robot’s wrist joint. IGM will also be showcasing its iCAM, a selfdeveloped laser camera for online joint tracking and adaptive welding of variable groove geometries.

## We highlight a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing technologies and processes

ISMR SAYS:

“Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes”

Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes, such as punching, blanking, embossing, bending, flanging and coining. This could be a single-stage operation where every stroke of the press produces the desired form on the sheet metal part, or could occur through a series of stages.

Progressive die stamping is a metal forming process widely used to produce parts for various industries, such as automotive, electronics and appliances. Progressive die stamping consists of several individual work stations, each of which performs one or more different operations on the part. The part is carried from station to station by the stock strip and is cut out of the strip in the final operation.

The deep drawing process is a forming process which occurs under a combination of tensile and compressive conditions. A flat sheet metal blank is formed into a hollow body open on one side or a hollow body is formed into a hollow body with a smaller cross-section. [DIN 8584]. In the automotive industry, deep drawing is usually carried out using rigid tools.

Hot forming is a temperature- and time-dependent process. With this method, parts are formed in their soft state at elevated temperatures and then quenched in the tool. Parts produced by hot forming are characterised by high strength, complex shapes and reduced springback effects. Optimal material behaviour is achieved through the structural transformation of austenite into martensite. The most commonly used material in hot forming is Boron steel 22MnB5, which is available from many steel manufacturers. Hot forming or press hardening are processes of forming metal allowing making high strength parts through a quick quenching of boron steels heated above 900 degrees.

Hydroforming, or internal high pressure forming, is a forming process with an active fluid (often a water-oil emulsion). A hollow part is formed from a tube, any profile or two blanks, by applying internal pressure. The pressure required in this process is largely dependent upon the material that is used, the material’s thickness and the smallest of radii to be formed. The internal pressure required may equal up to several thousand bars.
