# WELD YOUR WAY

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

For more than 20 years now, RudolfHörmann GmbH & Co. KG, with its headoffice in Buchloe (Bavaria, Germany), has placed its trust in welding technology from CLOOS. Last year, the commercial and agricultural construction and power engineering specialist began operating two new CLOOS robot systems. This has enabled the company to considerably accelerate its production process for various components, whilst increasing quality and flexibility.

Welding robots have accelerated production, as well as increasing quality and flexibility, at Rudolf Hörmann GmbH & Co. KG.

Established by Rudolf Hörmann in 1960, Rudolf Hörmann GmbH & Co. KG has posted solid growth. In its early years, the company specialised in the steel construction sector. This was joined over the years by agricultural and commercial building construction and photovoltaics. About two years ago, Hörmann also included mobile lightweight construction and industrial buildings into its product portfolio.

"We support our customers from planning to the turnkey realisation of construction projects," explained Ewald Schunn, Production Manager for Metal Construction, Hörmann. The company now employs more than 600 people in Germany, Austria and Switzerland.

## The welding process

"Hörmann now has more than 30 CLOOS welding machines in use in different production areas"

Hörmann now has more than 30 CLOOS welding machines in use in different production areas. The first robot system from CLOOS for automated welding started operation at the company in 1989. It was joined by another robot system in 2007. Last year, the 1990 system was replaced by two new CLOOS systems. Now, a QIROX compact cell and a QIROX "All in one" compact system take on the welding of diverse components. Recently, Hörmann also invested in a large, used robot system for the automated welding of semicircular arches for mobile buildings.

ISMR SAYS:

"Hörmann is now welding mid-sized workpieces on the new QIROX 'All in One' system. All relevant components are positioned on a joint base frame. They are fixed on a base plate so that fine adjustment is not necessary. The heart of the system is the QIROX QRC-350-E welding robot. The large range of the seven-axis robot simplifies and speeds up welding of the partly complex workpieces," CLOOS told ISMR.

While the robot is welding the components on one side, loading or unloading is carried out simultaneously at the other station. The movable counter bearing can be used to adjust the clamping lengths within range (2.5 to 3 metres).

The QIROX QR-CC-40 compact cell for welding smaller components has a twostation workpiece positioner with vertical change and vertical rotation. Using the rotary axis, the station is turned from the loading area to the robot within three seconds. During this procedure, the loading area is protected by a light barrier and an additional lateral safety fence. There is a glare shield between the two stations. The system operator therefore has protection and can load a station whilst welding is done on the other station. All robot and positioner axes are fully synchronised.

"This provides excellent welding results, reduces secondary processing times and speeds the entire process run enormously. As 'ready-to-weld' systems, they are fully pre-installed electrically and mechanically. They could therefore be integrated into the production process quickly and easily. The flexible clamping system means that the systems can work on different components. Currently 50 to 60 part types, for example, are processed by the 'All in One' system. 100 to 120 different and constantly changing types are planned for the future," added CLOOS.

The fixtures can be used flexibly on both systems.

"The part change possible between the systems means that we can also guarantee our delivery times when one robot is already occupied," explained Schunn. "Before, we had to resort to manual welding in emergencies."

The robot system is programmed offline with CLOOS RoboPlan software.

Focus on automation Hörmann is certified to DIN EN 1090. With its new CLOOS systems, it has been able to further raise component quality with precisely reproducible welding results. An additional benefit is that the QINEO Data Manager (QDM) software from CLOOS enables continuous welding data monitoring and documentation of welding processes.

Hörmann has changed its overall building concept with the new robot systems. Material flow across its entire production has been optimised. Hörmann is looking to increase still further the level of automation across all of its production.

"Our technologies must be state-of-the-art because the requirements of our customers are becoming increasingly discerning," said Ewald Schunn. "Our customers expect process reliability first and foremost, something we are able to guarantee with automation."

Hörmann is also affected by the skills shortage and faces increasing difficulties in finding qualified personnel. Employees have attended internal and external seminars to familiarise themselves with the new systems. PG Schweisstechnik, the CLOOS representative, was at Hörmann for one week in a production support role.

"We greatly appreciate the personal contact to both PG and CLOOS in Haiger, and look forward to continued collaboration," concluded Schunn.
