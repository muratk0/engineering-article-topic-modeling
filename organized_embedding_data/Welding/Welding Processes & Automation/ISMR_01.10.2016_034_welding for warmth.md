# WELDING FOR WARMTH

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

Fibre lasers were used to produce an automated welding system for a Russian manufacturer's heated towel radiators

ISMR SAYS:

A complete range of towel radiator models was converted from TIG welding by hand to an automated laser welding process.

The fitness and wellness trend is popular worldwide. After a strenuous workout or relaxing spa day, a little pampering goes a long way. Heated towel radiators are popular luxuries found at gyms and spas worldwide, as well as in the home.

With this in mind, Dutch special purpose machine manufacturer, Rodomach, developed an automated welding system (using ROFIN fibre lasers) for a Russian radiator manufacturer.

"We try to gain as much information from the customer and his expectations ahead of the project, to analyse these in detail and then offer him what he really needs - and not what he thinks he must have. Added value is a major factor for us, which is also valued by our customers," commented Rodomach. Its special machines are used in the automotive and metal industries as well as in many other industrial sectors.

## A clear vision

The radiator manufacturer had a clear vision when it approached Rodomach to find a solution - the complete product range of towel radiator models was to be converted from the current practice of TIG welding by hand to an automated laser welding process.

The system had to be able to weld models with round pipes as well as models with angular pipes and special pipes. The welding depth was to be 100% and the 25-bar pressure test had to be passed. The last and most important requirement was 'a uniform, beautiful and flawless seam weld that required no post-processing' so the final step, electro-polishing, could shine the stainless steel radiators to a mirror finish.

Robin Le Roy, from the Dutch ROFIN subsidiary in Alblasserdam, was involved in the project at an early stage. "The end customer absolutely wanted laser welding and had enjoyed positive experiences with his two PWS tube welding systems from ROFIN," explained Le Roy. "It was clear that he also wished to use ROFIN lasers in the new system."

Initial trials in the Hamburg Application Laboratory showed that the austenitic Cr-Ni-Steel AISI 304 was easy to process, but the wide gap was too large and therefore a consistent, high-quality welding seam could not be guaranteed. The product requirements were then determined, together with Rodomach, and suggestions for improvement were recommended to the end customer to enable automation and weldability with consistent high quality.

"My experience with ROFIN's application staff was excellent. The people were extremely knowledgeable, provided solid recommendations for process improvement and we worked well with them," reported Roel Doornebosch, Rodomach manager.

## A simple solution

Clamping the part and preventing warping were the main challenges for this project as they are a key factor for successful welding results. With collaboration and "out-of-the-box" thinking, the team came up with a clever solution. Instead of using traditional clamp tooling, servo-controlled clamping with integrated cooling was employed. This tooling method evenly clamps the part at all the welding points and the cooling prevents the joints from warping.

After concluding the trials, the applicators from Hamburg recommended a 2kW fibre laser ROFIN FL 020 with a 300μm fibre and a focal length of 300mm.

"With the high depth of field, the customer has a greater process tolerance and can therefore reduce scrap parts and improve productivity," stated Peter Kallage, manager of the applications lab in Hamburg. Using a beam switch, a laser can operate two robot welding stations, which weld the two sides of the radiator, alternately.

"We have pooled control of the system, the two robots and the laser on one terminal, to enable simple operation of the system," reported Doornebosch. "The customer was aware of the complexity of the requirements right at the beginning of our collaboration and was eager to see what we could offer him. It seems that we have surprised him, because he had not expected such a simple solution in the end."

With a welding speed of 2m/min., successful pressure test results exceeded the 25 bar specification. The system, ROFIN explained, ensured constant high-weld quality which also surpassed the customer's expectations. The customer has now followed the initial order with an additional two systems.■

Editor's Note

Rodomach Speciaalmachines B.V. was established in 1997 by Roel Doornebosch and today employs approximately 25 staff at Deventer in The Netherlands. The medium-sized company specialises in special mechanical engineering, focusing on connection technology, and has been offering, in addition to conventional methods, systems with laser technology since 2010. These make up about half of all its sold systems.

The ROFIN Macro Group, in Hamburg, Germany, focuses on high-powered industrial material processing with lasers and is associated primarily with sheet metal processing in the machine tool or automotive industry. Fibre and CO2 lasers in the multi-kW range are used in production facilities all around the globe – mainly for cutting but also for welding applications. For surface treatment applications, the fibre-coupled diode lasers and the diode-pumped, Q-switched lasers add to the product range. Intelligent system solutions for scanner welding and welding of tubes and profiles are also offered.
