All-process, inverter-based welding system is first of its kind

ESAB Welding & Cutting Products has introduced the Rebel™ EMP 205 AC/DC, which the company says is the first true all-process, inverter-based welding system. Users can perform GTAW with AC and DC outputs, GMAW with solid wires (steel, stainless, or aluminum), FCAW with gas-shielded and self-shielded wires, and SMAW with a DC output. The rated duty cycle is 205 amps at 20 percent.

AC output frequency controls the shape of the arc cone and arc force, while wave balance controls the amount of cleaning action versus penetration. HF, or noncontact, arc starts are required for aluminum and are desirable (and sometimes required) in applications in which end users want to prevent accidental contamination from the tungsten electrode.

Commercial availability is expected in Q2 2018.

Fiber laser welding machine features rotating lens

Amada offers its Fiber Laser Welder (FLW) for high-speed welding. According to the company, the machine delivers precise, high-speed results even when processing reflective materials such as brass and copper.

It is equipped with a patented rotating lens; with this feature, the laser beam is no longer aligned with a specific point along the target path, but instead circles within a small area as it is welding. A rotating lens makes it possible to bridge larger and uneven gap sizes while producing a clean, even weld. The heat input into the material also is optimized and results in precisely defined weld edges with almost no unnecessary deposits of material, states the manufacturer.

Features include automatic beam focus, a Teaching Assist System (TAS), FLW-CAM software, and high heat exchange efficiency.

Grinding, milling, and polishing system introduced

Suhner offers the Sunmic 60 grinding, milling, and polishing system. The central element is an intelligent control unit with touch panel that can accept a variety of micromotors and tool hand pieces, depending on the application.

The control features touchscreen operation on a glass display, the option to save settings, and a rotation speed regulator with tangible resistance.

The tools are driven by 280-W micromotors. The compact, brushless BL/DC motors provide a speed range of 1,000 to 60,000 RPM and deliver a high torque.

FAWJ micro head package cuts fine parts

Water Jet Sweden has introduced the fine abrasive water jet (FAWJ) micro head package for use on its ordinary machine models. The micro head is designed for customers who want to cut fine parts with high precision and incisions as narrow as 0.008 in., without the need for the most extreme micro part tolerances.

The FAWJ process uses very fine abrasives to cut metals and other hard materials, leaving a smooth surface that requires little or no postprocessing. It can cut very thick materials as well, depending on the type.

The micro head upgrade can be used on the company’s regular machine models, X-series and Premium, as well as existing, similar-size machines.

Compact cutting system fits in shops with limited space

Koike Aronson has introduced the Shop-Pro compact cutting system, designed to fit in shops with limited space.

The CNC system features protected ways to keep out dirt and contaminants; a precision helical gear rack and AGMA 12 grade ground pinions; helical, ultralow-backlash planetary gearheads; bolt-on, high-precision linear rail axis guide assemblies; high-torque drives; 19-in. panel PC; and 2-in. maximum table capacity.

Other features include water or downdraft table, optional laser pointer, optional oxyfuel torch, straight line bevel in rail axis, and 1,000-IPM rapid traverse speed. The system offers ohmic plate contact initial height sensing, with additional secondary plate sensing backup for rusty or heavily scaled plate.
