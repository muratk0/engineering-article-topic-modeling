Robotic welding gun features centralized motor, magnetic drive

Nimak GmbH has developed a new concept for robot welding guns, designed for use in multimaterial mixes and in large-scale production using conventional steel.

The powerGUN robotic welding gun has a centralized motor and gear unit that enables a significant weight reduction and lower interference contours while providing superior joining results, says the company. It employs the magneticDRIVE drive type, which uses microprocessor-controlled electromagnets. This drive can be used in all resistance welding applications, from stationary spot and projection welding machines to robot welding guns.

With the ultrafast power-generation and slip system, the user can set an exact power profile that controls the rise and fall of the electrode force across a large range in milliseconds. This short, controlled welding process is suitable for spot and projection welding of aluminum with highly varied alloys that are used in auto and aerospace applications.

Fiber laser welding system joins dissimilar materials, thicknesses

Automation Alternatives has introduced the RubyLaser fiber laser welding system. With its spot size as small as 0.001 to 0.002 in. and fast weld speeds, the system delivers high power density while minimizing the HAZ, the company reports. The machine is suitable for welding dissimilar materials, welding very thin materials, and joining thin and thick parts. The laser welding system handles rapid prototyping from microwelding to high-strength welds. Typical materials include stainless steel, titanium, aluminum, INCONEL

Laser drilling system produces linear, cylindrical patterns in 2-D, 3-D parts

Prima Power Laserdyne has introduced Smart-Perf™ for programming and laser drilling of simple to complex patterns of holes in linear and cylindrical paths in 2-D and 3-D parts.

The system’s reduced cycle time allows for on-the-fly laser drilling. There is no need for acceleration and deceleration at each hole location. On-the-fly drilling is suitable for percussion drilling smalldiameter (0.008- to 0.02-in.) holes in turbine engine combustors. Thousands of holes can be produced quickly over a 3-D surface in a single setup, reports the manufacturer. The system also is used for producing patterns of holes involving both trepanning (cutting) and standard percussion drilling (move to a location and create the hole by delivering a sequence of pulses).

Complex patterns involving nonuniform hole spacing and varying hole size can be produced. Common applications involve drilling a pattern around a pre-existing machined feature and creating custom spray patterns based on an array of holes with nonuniform spacing, size, and shape.

Infeed station added to automated saw

TigerStop has added the AutoLoader automatic infeed station for integration with the TigerSaw 2000 saw system. With the station, an operator can load five pieces of material at a time, increasing operator capacity for performing value-added tasks, such as sorting finished parts.

The unit can accommodate material from 4 to 30 ft. long and can be configured with up to seven stations in either 3- or 6-ft. lengths.

Each station is designed to load material forward with a weight capacity of up to 100 lbs. per set of loading station dogs. Sensors allow the loading station to recognize when material is pressed against the back fence, signaling that the material is ready to be processed through the saw and to continue feeding material until more is needed or the cut list is complete.
