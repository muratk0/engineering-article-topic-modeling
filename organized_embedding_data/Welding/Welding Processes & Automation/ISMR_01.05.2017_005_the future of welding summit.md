# The Future of Welding Summit

[TARİH: 01.05.2017 ISMR]

LINCOLN ELECTRIC®, ALONG with the American Association of Community Colleges (AACC) and the National Coalition of Advanced Technology Centres (NCATC), hosted The Future of Welding Summit 2017 recently at the company's headquarters near Cleveland, USA. More than 55 representatives from 40 technical and community colleges representing 20 different states, as well as the American Welding Society (AWS), attended the two-day conference where delegates learned about the latest welding technologies and educational tools.

The summit started on 5 April 2017 with an evening reception and opening welcome remarks at the Wyndham Hotel in downtown Cleveland. The following day, participants travelled to Lincoln Electric's headquarters, where they attended sessions on mechanised systems for pipe-shop welding, robotic systems for controlled aluminium welding and compact multi-process advanced welding systems for schools or shops.

"They also learned how Lincoln Electric's Power Wave® welders equipped with web-based CheckPoint™ production monitoring software can assist manufacturing operations, or schools, with verifying arc time, wire usage and other factors from multiple weld stations," explained the welding systems manufacturer.

Participants heard and interacted with a panel of industry experts including Blake Craft, Director of Welding Application and Compliance, Trinity Industries, Inc.; Monica Pfarr, Corporate Director, Workforce Development, American Welding Society; Patrick Henry, Corporate Director, Education Services, American Welding Society and Steve Sumner, Vice President, Global Equipment, Lincoln Electric.

"This event gives Lincoln Electric the opportunity to build a relationship between AACC, its member schools and our company," said Jason Scales, Business Manager, educational products and services, Lincoln Electric. "We want to work together to create optimised talent pipelines for real jobs in every region and industry."

The Welding Summit programme was designed to help educators understand how manufacturing and contractor technology is changing and also familiarise them with the latest technologies used in the welding industry.

"Programmes like this are crucial because manufacturers are asking for skilled and knowledgeable prospective employees. This helps us educate the instructors on what is necessary in today's world," added Henry. "Creating viable programmes for economic development in regions across the country is a 'must' to build a knowledgeable, skilled workforce."
