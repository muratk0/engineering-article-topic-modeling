# EWM builds new centre in northeastern Germany

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

EWM AG IS expanding. This major German manufacturer of arc welding technology is building a new Sales and Technology Centre in north-eastern Germany. In addition to offering a complete range of welding systems, EWM in Wittstock (Brandenburg) will be giving practical all-round support along with comprehensive consulting on welding technology from September 2018. Ralf Reinhardt, District Administrator of the Ostprignitz-Ruppin region, and Jörg Gehrmann, Mayor of the city of Wittstock, were the first to offer their congratulations at the ground-breaking ceremony for the new building, which will cover an area of more than 2,000 square metres.

To continue to meet high demand for products and processes for welding production, EWM is currently building one of the country's largest Sales and Technology Centres in Wittstock.

"Our new location is to become the first port of call in north-eastern Germany for all issues relating to welding," explained Toralf Pekrul, EWM branch manager in Rathenow. "From September 2018 onwards, this technology centre will be offering our customers comprehensive and individual welding technology consultation for the production processes of the future.

1,150 square metres of storage area will hold the entire EWM system range, while 1,130 square metres will be available to the family-run company for offices and service facilities. This also includes a large division for application technology, in which EWM will act as a technology partner for its customers by developing welding processes and solutions and conducting trials using the latest welding and metallurgical tests.

Above: Reception at the ground-breaking ceremony for the new EWM site in Wittstock, Germany.

With the construction of the new Sales and Technology Centre, the family-run company is continuing its expansion strategy. EWM already opened branches in Bergkirchen (Bavaria) and Szczecin in Poland in 2017. The welding technology manufacturer plans to employ a total of around 40 people at the Wittstock site.
