# FLEXIBLE WELDING

[TARİH: 01.03.2013 ISMR]

Established in 1824, Cammell Laird Shiprepairers and Shipbuilders Ltd, Birkenhead, has a long and proud history on the banks of the River Mersey (UK) including the building of RMS Mauretania in 1938 and HMS Ark Royal in 1936, an aircraft carrier of World War Two fame. The yard specialises in commercial repair, conversion, heavy fabrication and military refit projects.

In 2010 shipbuilding returned to the yard when Cammell Laird received a £44m contract to build two 12m-high sections of the middle and aft flight deck for HMS Queen Elizabeth, the new 65,000-tonne aircraft carrier being built by the Aircraft Carrier Alliance for the Royal Navy.

Today, Cammell Laird has 50,000m2 of covered fabrication and construction facilities, the largest of which is a 15,000m2 Construction Hall that can support up to 300 coded welders per shift. Other facilities include five drydocks and an extensive wet basin as well as steel preparation, machining and pipework shops plus hardstanding areas.

## Versatile welding work

Jeff Jones, Structures Manager, Cammell Laird, whose father and grandfather worked in the yard before him, said: “We are just over 50 per cent of the way through the steel work on the carrier project. The fabrications are quite complex in the welding processes and different grades of materials we use. When completed, the two sections will be sent by barge to Rosyth where they will be welded into the hull to form the ship.

“When we were awarded the carrier contract, we ordered 30 x Kemppi FastMig™ Synergic 400A welding machines and they are used for MIG/MAG and MMA welding in the Construction Hall.”

It was for the carrier contract that Cammell Laird turned to its local Kemppi dealer, General Welding Supplies (NW) Ltd in Widnes. It purchased 12 Kemppi Supersnake™ GT02S air- cooled ultra-long 25m welding systems for distance wire feeding and restricted access MIG/MAG welding which are now playing an important role in the fabrication of the giant 800-tonne sections.

“It is a bit of a rabbit warren on board and the welders can be welding all over the place. We find the 30m reach of Supersnake™ used with the FastMIG™ Synergic 350A welding machines opens up all areas for access and, with its flexibility, can easily be threaded through hatchways, holes and gaps in the fabrication right to where the welders are working. At the end of each job, the welder can just move to the next taking his torch and hose with him. We can trail the hose vertically up through four decks to the top leaving the power source below, thereby eliminating the need to carry wire feed units to the point of welding. The welders do not have to arrange cranage and this saves a tremendous amount of man hours and effort,” the ship yard explained.

Supersnake™ was designed for complex fabrications with difficult access. Because a welder uses a standard welding gun on the end of Supersnake, the welder has no additional weight or strain in addition to the gun and he also has the freedom to weld over a large area. Kemppi’s GT WireDrive™ mechanism and patented low friction DL-teflon make it easy to push a filler wire through the feed length, whilst the bright orange composite material cable set is highly visible for workplace safety and durability. The welder can easily adjust the welding parameters at the point of welding using a single knob control and read his data on a clear display. These points all help to make this a useful tool for shipyards.

“We purchased these units over a year ago now and in that time they have been very reliable and are well used, so overall we are now doing more welding much faster and more economically than before. We are mainly using 1.2mm flux cored wire, sometimes 1.4mm, with Supersnake and both wires are very easy to feed through. This equipment has definitely helped reduce our production times and costs as well as helping us in the way we carry out welding on board,” concluded Jeff Jones.
