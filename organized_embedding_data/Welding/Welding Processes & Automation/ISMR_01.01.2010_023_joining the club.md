“Welding has been called ‘the best kept secret’ in career planning,” says the American Welding Society. “It is widely used in construction, manufacturing, and many other industries, making it a critical skill that will always be in demand. From the beginning of civilization, we have relied on the skills of welders to enrich our lives.”

However, it has predicted that there will be a projected labour shortage of 200,000 welding professionals in the US by 2010, as baby boomers retire, and dubs the shortage of qualified operators, technicians and engineers in the field of welding as a ‘potential threat’ to US industry.

Global welding manufacturers have, in the meantime, taken the opportunity of continually improving their product lines, with a particular eye on increasing the flexibility, speed and efficiency of welding operations. ISMR highlights a selection of these new welding developments here.

## NEW PRODUCT DEVELOPMENTS

These innovative welding processes allow all seam types to be realised from one power source.

Alpha Q allows the thinnest of panels to be joined at low heat, in addition to welding and brazing new materials and mixed connections. Root passes can be handled and deep fusion penetration, simple handling and a high welding speed mean that the high-pressure, forced welding arc can be used to weld thick panels while saving manufacturer-cited production costs of up to 50%.

EWM’s Alpha Q power source enables six different welding procedures to be used: coldArc and forceArc, MIG/MAG pulse and standard processes, TIG (liftArc) and MMA welding. It is possible to switch between applications at any time, as well as to combine different processes.

“We are the first manufacturer to launch a machine that handles all these processes,” commented Michael Szczesny, one of the directors of EWM.
