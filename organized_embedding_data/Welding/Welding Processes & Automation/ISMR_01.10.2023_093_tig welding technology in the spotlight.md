# TIG welding technology in the spotlight

[TARİH: 01.10.2023 ISMR]

The INVERTIG i on REHM's booth at Schweissen & Schneiden in Germany.

The new INVERTIG i 260-450 DC and AC/DC HIGH from REHM combines the company's fourth generation of inverter technology with its innovative SIRIUS digital operating system. This next generation of three-phase TIG devices has been designed for rapid and reliable welding, said the welding specialist.

"The dynamics of the power units set new standards. The inverter requires no output inductor; this allows the full potential of digital control engineering to be achieved. The result is highly efficient welding processes with a perfect arc," REHM told ISMR at the recent Schweissen & Schneiden exhibition at Essen, Germany.

SIRIUS is designed to simplify the operation of complex digitally controlled processes and offers graphically dynamic and transparent visualisation (including assist functions).

"For DC welding, the 140KHz arc has set the bar for all standard welding tasks. Directionally stable, starting at 5A, it is suitable for sheet thicknesses of 0.2mm or more," continued REHM. "The REHM HYPER.PULS constricts welding current to 17,500Hz, providing much higher energy density in the arc and on the workpiece. The DC balance can be used to set the time for high and low current. The REHM HYPER.PULS is almost silent," it added.

According to the manufacturer, the REHM HYPER.SPOT can do tack welds with four times the welding current used in the manual process. All tack welds, it told ISMR, are identical.

"REHM HYPER.SPOT uses the benefits of the HYPER.PULS: the quick weld pool generation. The INVERTIG i also controls the entire tack-welding process. Once set, the torch only needs to be positioned; the REHM HYPER.SPOT does the rest," commented the manufacturer.

For interval welding, the INVERTIG i can use all welding processes. The combination of interval, HYPER.PULS and HYPER.SPOT delivers clear benefits, especially for welding on tool edges and for repairs. The heat input is targeted to the point in question and can be set according to requirements. The results have so far been matched only with laser welding equipment," it continued.

The AC welding process generates a very stable arc that can be set according to user requirements for different tasks. The process removes the oxide layer to deliver a very stable arc. For the AC waveform, the INVERTIG i offers several basic forms including soft sine, hard sine, triangle and rectangle. Features include optimised frequency control and AC balance.

REHM's DualWave procedure combines AC arc welding and DC welding. During the AC phase, the aluminium's oxide layer is removed. In the DC phase, DualWave uses the benefits of the DC arc. More heat input in this phase means better weld pool out gassing, explained REHM.

"The INVERTIG i's high efficiency uses up to 30% less energy than conventional equipment," concluded the manufacturer.
