## Welding market trends

"The welding industry is developing well, and demand is strong. In fact, 2022 has been our best year ever for welding. We have full order books and see this positive trend in our Perfect Welding Business Unit's 25 subsidiaries and from our representatives worldwide. It seems the MMA welding market is slightly declining, but the GMAW market (MIG/MAG) is increasing. Welding professionals are in high demand," Harald Scherleitner told ISMR.

He pointed out ongoing industry trends such as lack of welders; wire arc additive manufacturing; growing applications for cobots/automation and digitisation.

## Digitalisation and automation

“Digitisation is an ongoing trend—going forward, we assume that everything that can be digitised will be digitised. This is also true for automation. However, creativity and innovative strength will always lie with people, and we want to unleash their full welding potential...," Harald Scherleitner told /SMR.

Digitalisation influences joining technology in many ways. Fronius has developed software solutions, such as WeldCube, to improve the performance of welding systems in terms of ease of operation, weld quality and speed. The documentation software logs all welding conditions. Software-based analyses are designed to enable economical use of materials – filler wire and inert gas, for example. Cloud applications help to evaluate data and identify and immediately resolve weak spots. Remote access enables condition analysis and service in real-time to provide a basis for smart maintenance planning.

As a solution to the worldwide shortage of skilled workers, it has also developed the CWC-S Cobot welding cell for small to large batchwork where welders need no programming knowledge. Welding specialists can be released from routine activities and are available for more complex tasks.

“We are digitising welding expertise so that customers are not dependent upon skilled welders. Digitising this expertise is a real challenge. The cobot cells are a great solution - they are simple to program and make jobs more attractive for workers. The cobot handles all the hard work and the worker's experience is poured into the application with the cobot. This process also unlocks new applications and opportunities," confirmed Scherleitner.

Precise torch movements, with a repeatability of +/- 0.04mm, are designed to guarantee weld reproducibility in the Cobot welding cell, even in batches if necessary. Intelligent Fronius welding technology controls the welding process and records all welding data as required. The WeldCube data management software, optionally available with the welding cell, stores and evaluates these data.

The welding cell's standard equipment includes a Fanuc Cobot CX10 iA with a reach of 1,249mm and a payload of 10kg, and a welding table with a working area of 1,500 x 1,000 x 700mm. Operator safety measures include a pneumatic glare shield, sliding side doors with viewing windows and an extraction hood.

The Handling-to-Welding robotic welding cell from Fronius Welding Automation, or HTW for short, joins parts of different geometries and materials. Two robots complete the job – the handling robot positions the workpieces, and the second robot performs the welding. Various configuration options and software solutions mean that the system can be seamlessly integrated into production processes.

## Processes and technologies

Fronius is also addressing industry requirements with a range of modular, flexible and user-friendly high-end devices e.g. the iWave Multiprocess PRO, which was launched in January 2022. It is a multiprocess machine for different welding processes. It can also be retrofitted (for example with welding packages) as required.

"We developed the iWave to enable the very best TIG weld quality and flawless results to be produced on different metals. The Option CycleTIG provides for maximum control over the arc and targeted heat input. Further highlights include improved ignition control and greater ease of use with intuitive operation. The series is available in power categories from 190A to 500A. Multi-process capability is important if you are switching between different welding tasks on a day-to-day basis. The Multiprocess PRO option gives the user unlimited access to all MIG/MAG processes in power categories from 300A. It is ideally suited for manual metal arc welding, even with cellulose electrodes," explained Harald Scherleitner.

"TIG cold wire components are now also available for the Fronius iWave. This new innovation lies in their intelligent control. With the patented new Welding Package, TIG DynamicWire, even amateurs can create perfect TIG weld seams. Because the dynamic wire control always selects the right feed speed, welders can concentrate fully on the arc and the welding system regulates the optimum wire feed speed at all times. The process adapts to the welder, not the other way around. This system will be available in October 2022," he added.

Fronius has also added to its range of smart, high-end TPS/i TWIN tandem welding systems.

Synchronised welding with two individually controllable arcs can now be done with new TPS/i TWIN systems and PMC TWIN/CMT TWIN welding processes. The TPS/i TWIN Push-Pull solution is designed for use in vehicle and railway vehicle construction and used to weld aluminium profiles or pressure containers using the PMC (Pulse Multi Control) process. The new TPS/i CMT TWIN system also uses the CMT (Cold Metal Transfer) process for welding aluminium, nickel-base alloys and high-strength steels. The controlled heat input provided by the Cold Metal Transfer process is particularly well suited for thin-sheet applications.

## Strategic focus and objectives

Scherleitner confirmed that emphasis has been placed very firmly on quality, sustainability and health/safety issues.

“These important issues drive our initiatives, research and our product development. The human very much comes into focus here. For us, quality awareness is not just an empty promise, but a corporate value that is lived and practised. We put our products through up to 13 different endurance tests during the development phase. This is the only way to guarantee a long working life. To ensure responsible use of resources, we emphasise durability, repairability and recycling right from the development stage. High quality always goes hand in hand with reliability, health protection and ease of use in all our products and services," he confirmed.

Fronius' customer-oriented strategy has also driven its focus on health and safety issues in welding. It has developed a range of equipment, such as helmets and clothing, that protect welders from hazardous fumes, spatter and flashes. It also intends to launch more health and safety equipment at the end of this year.

Scherleitner is keen, however, to point out that quality and accuracy are the cornerstones that underpin all Fronius products.

“Although we increasingly think about Industry 4.0 digitisation in this industry, you must still fulfil the basic requirements and that means, first and foremost, quality in welding. You still need to join two different materials accurately, before looking at further data analysis. Everything springs from this. Service then becomes key i.e. commissioning new systems, optimising product lines, supporting equipment life, training etc. It really is important to get the basics right," he confirmed.

The company has adopted the motto 'Let's Get Connected' to continue its ongoing discussions and feedback from customers and help it to develop further new technologies, software and services.

## New prototyping centre

In the Fronius 900 square-metre prototyping centre, at its facility in Wels (Austria), specialists develop customised solutions to tackle every welding challenge.

"We support our customers in the manufacturing of prototypes and feasibility studies because these are costly, labour-intensive and time-consuming. This is in response to the challenges we are seeing. We don't just offer technical support; we have expanded our range of services to include various high-tech analyses and simulations. We always try to find the perfect welding solution for the customer," added Scherleitner.

"At the moment, we are focusing on e-mobility issues as OEMs and car producers are facing new material challenges (for instance, how to weld battery cell trays). The centres recommend the optimum welding processes to meet these requirements. They also examine prototype wire arc additive manufacturing processes. This is a great technology for remote operations," he added.

Whether it is battery trays for e-mobility; axles; frame sections or various applications for other industrial sectors, parts of up to 1,500kg and 3 x 2 metres can all be joined in the new prototyping centre. It offers a range of services including 3D equipment fabrication, 3D measurement and an offline programming option complete with welding process simulation. Heat and distortion simulation; welding seam inspection; a laser seamtracking camera; hot active plasma technology for surface cleaning and a laboratory for metallographic examination all come as standard. WeldCube software, for documenting and analysing welding data, makes it possible to trace the entire component history in detail.
