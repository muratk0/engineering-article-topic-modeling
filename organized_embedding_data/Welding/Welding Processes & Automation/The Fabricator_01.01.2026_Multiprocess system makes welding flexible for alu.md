# Multiprocess system makes welding flexible for aluminum fabricator

[TARİH: 01.01.2026 The Fabricator]

Applications

SITUATION

Aluminum specialist Allucan AG, Bremgarten, Switzerland, produces prototypes and production components for industries such as railroad, power, shipbuilding, and aircraft. The company manufactures lightweight aluminum transformer enclosures and special housings used primarily in high-performance locomotives and high-speed trains. Strict safety requirements for these products demand precisely placed welds with consistent geometries and layer buildups. Welds can have 20 or more layers, depending on the material thickness.

The company’s welders usually use gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW, or TIG) to produce flat welds that support subsequent layers. The smooth beads help minimize notch effects and enhance structural integrity of the welded joints. To build up the layers, welders use gas metal arc welding (GMAW, or MIG), which is especially suitable on thicker materials. MIG offers high speeds and good control over the weld.

RESOLUTION

Company welders use the iWave AC/DC Pro 500i multiprocess system from Fronius. With the press of a button, they can change over rapidly from TIG to MIG. As CEO Markus Jäger explained, "This flexibility—in particular the savings in working time and space requirements—is greatly beneficial to us."

The CycleTIG feature controls the heat input and arc. The RPI (reverse polarity ignition) auto-intelligent ignition function helps ensure fast and reproducible ignitions regardless of material properties, without the need to adjust the ignition parameters manually.

An integrated authorization system allows Allucan managers to define individual access rights for each welder. When welders log in with a key card or key fob, the system immediately recognizes their corresponding authorizations using near-field communication.

When activated, automatic gas postflow protects the tungsten electrode during TIG welding. Software calculates the postflow time, depending on the set welding current and electrode diameter. The leaktight welding torch enables effective cooling, and its sidewall design helps keep the arc stable.

"A key criterion for us is component accessibility. Fronius offers us welding torches with variable contact tip lengths, which make access to the individual components much easier," explained Jäger. "This torch length flexibility is hugely important to us because it enables us to produce precise, high-quality welds even in hard-to-reach areas."
