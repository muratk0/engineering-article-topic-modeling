Robotic fiber laser welding system offers high speed, minimal weld zone heat

Amada’s Fiber Laser Welder (FLW) 3000 Ensis is a complete robotic welding system with automatic beam focus, a patented rotating lens, and a teaching assist feature that uses a camera to prevent deviation from the targeted welding path. The wire feed enables the system to switch from fusion welding to adding filler wire in the same program.

According to the manufacturer, the welding system’s greatest advantage over traditional welding is speed and minimal heat on the weld zone. Fiber laser welding is five times faster than GTAW and produces stronger welds with little to no distortion, the company states.

Software update accelerates robot programming

Hypertherm has announced an update to its Robotmaster offline robot programming software. Version 7.5 focuses on accelerating and simplifying the programming process and includes new features for a variety of robotic applications.

New material removal simulation capabilities help users see what the process will look like so they can forecast real-life collisions between the robotic arm and stock material. A cleaner user interface with improved tab system menu contributes to a 50% reduction in programming time.

Two new programming modes are offered. Operation-focused programming consolidates the tools and workflows needed for an error-free program onto one window, while Orientation helper, with its combination of axis configuration and optimized automatic path planning, provides live feedback on the feasibility of a planned operation. This makes it suitable for task experts with no robotics experience.

Strengthening glove helps prevent strain injuries

Rhino Tool House has introduced the Ironhand 2.0 strengthening glove from Bioservo. It strengthens the human grip, allowing operators to use less grip force when performing repetitive tasks to help reduce fatigue and prevent strain injuries.

This powered, soft exoskeletal muscle support for the hand is designed for applications in motor vehicle final assembly, heavy truck manufacturing, construction equipment, wind energy and power generation, and consumer products manufacturing.

Robotic welding cells offered

Bystronic Inc. has introduced three robotic welding cells powered by Cloos. Each has a specific scope of application based on the size and weight of the parts to be welded, and each comes with a two-station workpiece positioner.

The Micro Cell features an overall clamping length of 55 in., a nominal load capacity of 280 lbs./side for the manual rotating workpiece positioner, and a 400-amp welding power supply.

The Compact Cell features an overall clamping length of 61 in. and 562-lbs./side nominal load capacity for the automatic rotating workpiece positioner. The Compact System features a 138-in. overall clamping length and nominal load capacity of 2,248 lbs./side for the automatic rotating workpiece positioner. Both compact models feature a 450-amp welding power supply.

Push/pull GMAW torches feed wire at distances up to 33 ft.

Tweco, an ESAB brand, has launched the MXH 315PP and MXH 420W PP push/pull torches. They maintain constant wire tension between two sets of drive rolls: one set in the feeder and one set in the torch. This design helps solve common feeding problems such as wire slippage, erratic arc performance, wire burning back to the contact tip, cold lap, and birdnesting at the feeder rolls.

The torches come in two versions, both of which feed 0.035- to 1/16-in.-dia. wires at speeds of 96 to 709 IPM and come in cable length options of 20 or 33 ft. The gas-cooled MXH 315PP has a rated output of 270 amps at 60% duty cycle with mixed gas and 310 amps at 60% with CO2. The water-cooled MXH 420W PP has a rated output of 400 amps at 100% duty cycle with mixed gas and 430 amps at 100% with CO

Top module provides payload boost for autonomous mobile robots

ROEQ has introduced the TMS-C1500 top module, which boosts the payload capabilities of autonomous mobile robots (AMRs) from Mobile Industrial Robots. Using the top module, an MiR1000 or MiR1350 AMR can handle payloads up to 1,500 kg. It also allows an MiR500 or MiR600 AMR to be deployed in applications with payloads of up to 800 kg.

Eliminating the need for a docking station, the top module can pick up and drop off the shelf cart in free space and with precision. This helps ensure that the AMR is never left idling, as it can simply drop off the cart and immediately go and pick up a new one.
