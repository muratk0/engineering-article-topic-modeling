# These robot cells really are the backbone of our production

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

Above: High and constant seam quality reduce expensive re-working prior to the painting process.

Right: The long longitudinal seams on the vice are welded quickly using the MAG Tandem process so that both welding cells remain in the production cycle. A rotating positioner ensures the optimum position of the component to the torch.

Below: Turnkey, easily transportable robot compact cell Z3 with two rotating positioners. CLOOS supplies all the components required for the welding process.

In the Cloos tandem torch, two wires arranged closely, one behind the other, are fed simultaneously and melted in two separate arcs. For this to function well, two microprocessor-controlled pulsed arc power sources from the Quinto series are electronically synchronised; they use two electrically insulated welding current circuits which enable a separate adjustment of the welding parameters for each arc.

This guarantees precise and top quality seam results.

The weld seams for fixing the tube chucks are significantly shorter. That is why, in the second Cloos cell, a robot is used with single wire technology. The welding times are between 25 and 35 seconds long for both units. The cells have a 2-station operation for smooth production: whilst the employee loads raw parts into one station or removes the welded part, the robot is working in the other area.

## READY FOR OPERATION

The cells are equipped with a 6-axis robot, which is controlled by the Cloos ROTROL II robot control and operated via the PHG equipment with its large display and the clear touch-screen keyboard. With the tandem cell, the Cloos Duo-Drive system feeds the relevant quantity of wire from two drums to the torch.

The various fillet welds are welded using the MAG process: the computer-controlled Cloos Quinto pulsed arc welding machine produces a low splash arc and evenly high, reproducible seam quality.

“This minimises any re-working,” confirms Herbert Lorbach, “and complaints are less than one per thousand.”

The production cells each have one automatic welding torch cleaning station, which cleans the torches regularly. All components, including all cables and hoses, are fitted to a common basic frame and surrounded by the protective housing.

Each cell is around 3900mm long, 2200mm wide and 2000mm high and weighs around 2800kg. The customer can simply position the equipment tested in the factory in his production environment and start working it with little effort.

“For everything to function smoothly, our operators get to know the equipment during their training and the programming course at CLOOS,” explains Herbert Lorbach. “These robot cells really are the backbone of our production.”
