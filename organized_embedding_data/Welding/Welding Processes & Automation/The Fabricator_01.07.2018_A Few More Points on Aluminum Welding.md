Great article. I would like to add a few things about welding aluminum.

Don’t add filler until you have established a puddle. Why? The arc will take the path of least resistance, and the puddle is the path, even with the filler wire there. If you don’t have a puddle and put the filler wire next to the arc, it will melt back, ball up, and drop on the base metal because the filler wire becomes the path of least resistance. Try to remember: The arc melts the puddle, and the puddle melts the filler wire.

You have to hit aluminum hard at first and then back off because the high co-efficient of thermal conductivity causes the heat to run away and not puddle. So hit it hard, establish a puddle, dip, and then back off what is needed.

Try what I’m saying above. Take a piece of ⅛-in. scrap and try it.

Get a puddle. Do not chase the tungsten down with the filler. Once you have a puddle, dip at the front of the puddle. At the same time you dip, you may want to move the torch up because the puddle increases in depth and may go up and touch the tungsten. Some welders move back when they dip to prevent the puddle touching the tungsten.

Then pick a target like the front of the puddle and index forward. That will measure your dips evenly and give you that nice stack of dimes look. Try to develop a rhythm with the dip, moving up or back, and then moving forward. That is one way to do it.

By all means, if you see someone make a weld you like, ask if you can watch and see how he does it. Every job is different, and learning different ways will help you complete the different tasks.

—Jay Miller

I will add something about weaving, and it doesn’t apply just to aluminum. The wider you weave, the more you increase the possibility of a centerline crack. If you exceed the length-to-width ratio, the weld is prone to cracking during the cooling and contraction. Don’t forget, it also shrinks in the middle, and if the length-to-width ratio has been exceeded, it will crack.

What causes a crater crack? There isn’t enough metal in the crater, and when it contracts, it cracks. Or possibly pulling out too quickly may also add to the cracking. Fill the crater and cool down slower.
