Cobot takes care of simple welding tasks

Acieta offers the FastArc CW cobot, designed to perform simple welding tasks so that job shops and manufacturers can assign more challenging, complex jobs to skilled welders.

The system includes a FANUC CRX-10iA/L 6-axis robot mounted on a mobile platform to move quickly among production areas. It is compatible with Fronius, Lincoln Electric, and Miller welding units. According to the company, ROI with this standard, pre-engineered cobot is as little as six months, based on two shifts.

Intuitive software and a touchscreen interface help simplify setup and operation, and one person can operate several of the cobots simultaneously. People can work safely side-by-side loading and unloading parts while the cobot welds so the system runs continually. If the low-speed arm bumps into a person or object, the cobot stops immediately. No safety fencing is needed.

Fume-extraction GMAW gun designed for light weight

Abicor Binzel has introduced the xFUME Pro lightweight fume-extraction GMAW gun. Improvements made to the front-end design and internal components have decreased the weight by 20% and increase suction power and flow by 10% compared to previous models, according to the company.

The patented handle is designed to maximize airflow through the torch while maintaining comfort. The ball joint at the base of the handle allows the cable to move independent of the handle, so there is less weight for the welder to manipulate while articulating the gun.

Abicor Binzel

www.binzel-abicor.com

User interface with color LCD added to GTAW machine

Miller Electric Mfg. LLC has redesigned its Dynasty GTAW machine, now featuring an intuitive user interface with a color LCD. Dynamic on-screen images show how parameter adjustments will affect the weld so refinements can be made before striking an arc.

The Dynasty 210 and 300 series machines power AC/DC GTAW, pulsed GTAW, and SMAW. Input power on the 210 is 120 to 480 V and 1 to 210 amps (DC) and 2 to 210 amps (AC). The 300 features an input power range from 208 to 600 V and an amperage of 1 to 300 amps (DC) and 2 to 300 amps (AC).

Miller Electric Mfg. LLC

www.millerwelds.com

Multiaxis system supports rapid robot assembly, programming

Beckhoff has introduced Automation Technology for Robotics (ATRO), a modular system that enables flexibility in robot kinematics to fit applications with payloads up to 10 kg and reach of up to 1.3 m. Users can select the number of axes required and quickly assemble the robot or cobot needed for their application. Internal media feeds for data, power, and fluids are designed to support continuous rotation of all robot axes.

With a variety of modules, the system lets users create almost any custom robot design within the rated payloads for their application, from a single-axis rotary indexing table application and delta kinematics to multiaxis articulated robots. Expansive configurations with as many as 16 robotic arms are possible.

Robotic welding training system introduced

Comau and Seabery have joined forces to create an educational training program for students and workers that allows them to practice the skills needed to become proficient and certified robotic welders.

This system includes the Soldamatic welding simulator and the customizable, flexible, and scalable e.DO educational robot with programmable settings. The simulator features Sebery’s Hyperreal-SIM multisensory training interface that includes sight, sound, and touch.

Allowing for unlimited practice, the program uses augmented reality technology to reproduce a real-life robotic welding environment. It includes a programmable teach pendant and features hundreds of welding procedures and positions.

Comau

www.comau.com

Camera-based system compares parts to templates to find defects

Schuler has developed a camera-based system called Visual Quality Inspection that automatically detects bad parts so that errors in production can be corrected immediately.

The system can be configured with a few mouse clicks. The template is a flawless good part that the operator marks on the computer, a process that takes a few minutes and requires no special knowledge. The system compares all other components to this template and immediately identifies even the slightest deviations.
