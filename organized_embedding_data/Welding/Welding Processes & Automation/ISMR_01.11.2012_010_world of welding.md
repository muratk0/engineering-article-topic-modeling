# WORLD OF WELDING

[TARİH: 01.11.2012 ISMR]

Ray Shook, the American Welding Society’s Executive Director, sets out his vision for the Society to ISMR

ISMR SAYS:

“AWS has launched a new global initiative encompassing training, certification and skills development for welders to further its reach”

The American Welding Society currently has close to 70,000 members including over 15,000 international members. It was founded in 1919 as a multifaceted, non-profit organisation with a mission to advance the science, technology and application of welding and allied joining and cutting processes worldwide including brazing, soldering and thermal spraying. Headquartered in Miami, Florida, and led by a volunteer organisation of officers and directors, AWS is composed of 22 Districts with 250 Sections and student chapters.

AWS’ membership is made up of student members, individual members and corporate members. Individual membership consists of engineers; educators; researchers; welders; inspectors; welding foremen; company executives and business owners. Corporate members include welding manufacturers; independent welding shops; job shops; distributorships; end users; educational institutions and more. Member interests include automatic, semi-automatic and manual welding, as well as brazing, soldering, cutting, ceramics, lamination, robotics, lasers and safety and health.

The AWS is a co-sponsor of North America’s largest metal trade show, FABTECH, which is held annually for as many as 35,000 attendees. It also hosts trade shows in Mexico (AWS Weldmex) and FABTECH Canada. ISMR caught up with its executive director, Ray Shook, to find out its latest initiatives.

## Expanding its horizons

“After nearly 30 years in its previous location, we recently moved our headquarters to an 11,400m2 building in the Doral area of greater Miami, Fla. The purchase reflects expansion opportunities for the Society, which has a rapidly-rising membership,” Ray Shook, Executive Director of the AWS, told ISMR.

With international membership on the rise, the American Welding Society (AWS) has launched a series of country-specific websites, known as microsites, for members to access information in their native languages. Multilingual microsites are now live for Mexico, China and Canada, with AWS continuing to add other countries in the coming weeks. The microsites feature information on services offered by AWS in each specific country, membership benefits, exhibition information, online education and access to AWS publications and technical standards.

“Over the past few years AWS has seen a significantly increasing interest from across the globe in attaining AWS certifications, standards and membership,” Ray Shook continues. “We’ve launched a global initiative that will allow us to better serve the international welding community, and the country-specific websites are just one of the steps that we are taking to become more accessible to our members, wherever they are.”

## Plugging the gap

Manufacturing, construction and mining is a US$3 trillion industry (approximately one third of US GDP). Welding is a US$ 34 billion industry. According to the AWS, labour represented more than 70% of welding-related expenditures (US$ 22.4 billion) in 2000. There are more than 500,000 welders working in the US. The average age of welders in today’s US workforce is 54.

“The United States is currently enjoying a business expansion that is causing, in many locations, a shortage of skilled metal workers who are available to meet the need. Automation is being rapidly adopted to meet industry demand,” Shook told ISMR. “As the trained work force of welders retires at a rate twice as fast as new welders are coming into the industry, the supply of qualified personnel is becoming a widespread demographic issue.”

The American Welding Society is therefore rapidly introducing online training programmes to help expedite the development of skilled welding professionals. This is an effort that Shook believes will have worldwide success for AWS.

“In July 2012, the American Welding Society was a primary host of the International Institute of Welding Annual Assembly in Denver, Colorado, the first IIW assembly to be held in the US in decades. We have also begun a partnership with the Indonesian Welding Society to exchange experts and to establish a training centre in Indonesia based on AWS standards,“ Ray Shook told ISMR. He clearly sees growth opportunities for AWS training outside the US.

However, he is quick to point out other welding trends and challenges facing the industry.

“One trend is that new techniques have enabled resistance welding and friction stir welding to replace mechanical joining of nonferrous and dissimilar metals,“ he explained. “As global trade increases, the uniform quality of welded products (regardless of where they are produced) depends on adherence to internationally accepted standards for welder qualification, inspection and facilities certification.“

A revised edition of the American Welding Society’s ANSI Z49, Safety in Welding, Cutting and Allied Processes was published in April this year. The standard known as Z49.1:2012 supersedes the 2005 edition. The 68-page book is the standard for the protection of persons from injury and illness and the protection of property (including equipment) from damage by fire and explosions arising from welding, cutting and allied processes.

The standard is for the guidance of educators, operators, managers, and supervisors in the safe set-up and use of welding and cutting equipment, and the safe performance of welding and cutting operations. The publication is the joint effort of the ASC Committee on Safety. The committee is made up of volunteer industry suppliers, end-users and researchers.

## Attracting new blood

An analysis of projected data that was gathered through the efforts of Weld-Ed, a US National Science Foundation-funded centre, shows that by 2019 there will be a need for at least 238,692 new and replacement welding professionals.

AWS has been extremely focused on the future of the welding industry and launched the Careers in Welding trailer in late November 2011. The 16m, single expandable trailer features 60m2 of exhibit space including five welding simulator stations. The trailer is currently traveling to events across the United States to offer opportunities for youth and those searching for second careers to explore the welding industry through a hands-on approach. On board the trailer is also information about different welding career paths, training centres and scholarships available in the industry.

The 53-foot, expandable trailer is sponsored by the American Welding Society (AWS) and Lincoln Electric and features 650 square feet of exhibit space, including five Lincoln VRTEX 360 welding simulator stations. The VRTEX 360 feeds computer-generated data with a virtual welding gun and helmet equipped with internal monitors. The participant practices welding in virtual environments which include simulated welding-booth training environments or field-welding applications. The simulator also has a video gaming component that awards each “welder“ with a score for his or her weld.

In addition to the simulators, the Careers in Welding trailer provides other interactive educational exhibits including a display wall featuring 11 industry segments with trivia questions, fun facts and industry artifacts; a “Day in the Life of a Welder“ exhibit with videos depicting real-life environments in which welders work; a life-sized welder highlighting welding as a safe profession; a social media kiosk allowing visitors to take their photo and post to Facebook or email to a friend; welding scholarship information and more.

The AWS Foundation was established by the American Welding Society on 13 September 1989 to support programmes that ensure the growth and development of the welding industry through strengthening research and educational opportunities in welding and related industries. Led by a volunteer Board of Trustees, the Foundation is organised as a not-for-profit 501(c)(3) charitable organisation. Administration, including programme development, fundraising, public relations, marketing and financial management is provided by a small development staff from the American Welding Society. Its operations are conducted from the Society’s offices in Miami, Florida.

“As part of the focus to make welding a career of choice for the next generation, AWS worked with Boy Scouts of America to develop a Welding Merit Badge. The badge, launched in February 2012, enables Scouts to learn hands-on how to join metals using welding techniques. Scouts earning their Welding merit badges not only will explore the fundamentals of welding but also will learn about the different career paths within the industry.“ added Shook.

AWS education programmes include international, national and local conferences, seminars, individual corporate programmes and home study courses. Subjects range from the basics to the leading edge of technology. AWS offers to all qualified US welding training programmes the award-winning SENSE programme (Schools Excelling through National Skills Education) which implements national skill standards and associated curriculum guides to train and qualify welders. The programme advances students through three different levels, from entry to expert welder, to prepare students for today’s workforce and tomorrow’s challenges.
