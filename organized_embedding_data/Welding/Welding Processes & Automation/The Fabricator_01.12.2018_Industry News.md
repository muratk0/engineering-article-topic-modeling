American Institute of Nondestructive Testing to provide CWI exam prep course in Houston

Starting in 2019 the American Institute of Nondestructive Testing, Baxter, Minn., will provide certified welding inspector (CWI) exam prep courses in Houston.

The prep course begins with a 40-hour online course that students can move through at their own pace. The online portion covers the fundamentals of welding technology as established by AWS. This coincides with Part A of the CWI exam. In addition, the online course provides in-depth training to the welding code portion of the exam.

The remaining 40 hours of the course are held at the Houston Marriott North. This portion of the training comprises practical, hands-on, visual inspection techniques in accordance with the new Part B of the CWI exam, as well as additional lecture, study preparation, and practice exams for the welding fundamentals and code book portions of the exam.

Proos Mfg. under new ownership, adds manufacturing capacity

Nu-Way celebrates 50th anniversary by gifting community that supports it

Nu-Way is a vertical manufacturer, producing products in their entirety rather than components and assemblies, including a modern phone booth equipped with digital screens and connections; fast-food kiosks; digital cinema posters; onboard automotive equipment; and commercial ovens and other food service equipment.

In celebrating its 50th anniversary, metal fabricator and vertical manufacturer Nu-Way Industries, Des Plaines, Ill., displayed its products and thanked its customers and employees, as well as the state agencies and community that support it, while calling for more workforce and vocational education grants and programs.

"Our passion for this industry, our commitment to quality, and our endless desire to be better every day have remained unchanged in 50 years. But tangible improvements, like more accessible workforce development grants and more vocational education, are needed to ensure Illinois manufacturing stays strong as we move into the future," said Steve Southwell, Nu-Way president and CEO. "We’re grateful for the partnership and hard work of our leaders, but there’s more to be done."

The company, with both highly automated and manual operations, is one of the largest manufacturers in the region and employs 300 people. Customers include Siemens, Schneider Electric, Middleby, and Electrolux. That growth and success were not easily obtained. Nu-Way Executive Vice President and CFO Mary Howard, whose father and uncle founded the company, said that in the early days of the company, her dad would go out and sell and then come back at night to make the parts.

"My father and his brother started this company with a vision for doing things a better way—a ‘new way’—using cutting-edge technology to improve manufacturing processes."

In a new way, or departure from fabrication job shops, Nu-Way is a vertical manufacturer, working with its industry partners to supply entire products rather than components and assemblies. Starting as a small welding shop in 1968, the company now fabricates commercial-grade ovens and cooking equipment, electrical enclosures, bus stop enclosures, display screens for cinemas, fast-food kiosks, and onboard automotive equipment.

"We’re all about delivering the whole solution," Southwell said.

Regarding which types of skills the company needs and wishes to develop in its workforce, Howard said employees need STEM (science, technology, engineering, and math) skills and a curiosity about how things are made. "Our vision statement is inspiring manufacturing excellence, and that’s what we want to see—people who really value innovation and engineering, who go into a hardware store and ask, ‘How is that made? What could be done differently, better?’"

For young people wondering what skills they need to develop to become the next generation of manufacturers, Howard had this advice: "Get your face out of your smartphone and figure out how it works."

State, local, and industry leaders attended the event, including Illinois Sen. Laura Murphy, Illinois Deputy Gov. Leslie Munger, Des Plaines City Manager Michael Bartholomew, and Mark Denzle of the Illinois Manufacturers’ Association.

The company gifted the city of Des Plaines with one of the large interactive digital display boards it manufactures.

Solar Atmospheres of Western PA commissions large car bottom air furnace

Solar Atmospheres of Western PA has commissioned a 20-ft.-long car bottom furnace that has a total load capacity of up to 30,000 lbs. The Class 2 furnace, built by John Becker’s Heat Treat Equipment Inc. in Canton, Mich., has been installed and tested at Solar Atmospheres’ facility in Hermitage, Pa. The 60-in.-sq. by 240-in. hot zone envelope has passed the AMS 2750E temperature uniformity survey of ±10 degrees F from temperatures of 300 to 1,450 degrees F.

This new piece of equipment will complement the company’s lower-temperature work, especially where surface oxidation is of little concern. It also will be used to process many downstream processes, including multiple tempering operations, which typically follow vacuum austenitizing treatments.

Sun Glo Plating expands services to include passivation of stainless alloys

Clearwater, Fla.-based Sun Glo Plating Co. has announced that it now offers passivation to improve the corrosion resistance of stainless steel alloys.

"Passivation is accomplished by immersing stainless steel in a nitric or citric acid bath," said Dave Brackenhamer, director of business development. "The acid removes tramp iron and surface contaminants, which helps to clean and reoxidize the metal. This postfabrication process provides for a more passive and corrosion-resistant surface."

Passivation also can be used to restore damaged stainless surfaces by removing the surface iron that is exposed from mechanical use or chemical cleaners. After it’s complete, the surface retains its natural finish.
