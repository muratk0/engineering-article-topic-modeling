“The number of manufacturers and distributors globally has increased greatly over the past 10 years,” responded Steve Wallis, product sales development manager at TR Fastenings. “There was a time when there were less than half a dozen key manufacturers of, for example, the self-clinch product. However with the opening up of Far Eastern markets and manufacturing capabilities, there are numerous new companies dealing with this product range. This has resulted in the market price being driven down, as product has become available from numerous sources.”

China surpassed Japan to become the world's largest manufacturer of motor vehicles in 2009, providing industrial fastener producers with important new sales opportunities as output continued to climb. The country is now one of the largest producers and exporters of fasteners in the world. Producers in developed areas such as the US, Japan and Western Europe will continue to lead the world in production of more advanced, highly-engineered fasteners. However, Chinese firms are narrowing the technological gap as their access to capital grows and they acquire more manufacturing experience.

“Gains in industrial fastener demand in the world’s highly developed economies - namely, the US, Western Europe and Japan – were much slower than in industrialising countries between 2006 and 2011, and sales advances in most developed nations will continue to be subpar through 2016. Since the durable goods manufacturing sectors in these areas are mature, there will be fewer growth prospects for fastener suppliers.

“However, recoveries in motor vehicle production and construction expenditures following a period of decline will result in more rapid fastener sales gains going forward,” added Freedonia, pointing out that the US, Western Europe and Japan will continue to be major suppliers of aerospace-grade fasteners due to their long traditions of high-tech manufacturing.

## An eye on supply

2013 is a landmark year for TR Fastenings as it celebrates 40 years at the forefront of the fastenings distribution and manufacturing business. From small beginnings in the market town of Uckfield, East Sussex, it has now grown into a £100 million business, with global distribution, manufacturing and sales networks. Its core business is the supply of DIN standard (German Institute for Standardisation) branded and bespoke threaded parts but the company continues to introduce new innovations to the marketplace.

TR Fastenings has expanded its fastenings offer to end-users with the introduction of its Euro Rivet Bush range. This product, manufactured at the TR plant in East Sussex, is a variant on the company’s long-established Hank range but with subtle changes, providing an alternative in the mainland European market. The company is determined to win new business through product expansion as well as taking on the competition.

“The vast majority of fastenings originate from a male (screw) or female (nut) thread. The past decades have seen numerous shapes and sizes, new materials and better finishes. We have introduced a number of different products to our sheet metal fastenings range, particularly in the self-clinch range, but other products, such as the K-Nut and Euro Rivet Bush range, have also been introduced which are suitable for a number of applications. Moving forward, customers may want better corrosion-resistant products (A4 Stainless steel) or lighter fastenings (aluminium) but recent enquires have not indicated any major surge,” added Wallis.

The Binx Nut, originally designed in the 1960s, has been refined and developed by TR Fastenings to an all-metal, self-locking nut suitable for use in various industry sectors. Dimensionally no bigger than a standard full nut, the Binx Nut’s ingenious design incorporates two opposing cantilevers which lock into the mating part with inward and downward pressure minimising thread wear and enabling the nut to be removed and replaced repeatedly whilst retaining torque resistance. Binx Nuts are also unaffected by temperature fluctuations or contaminants.

TR Fastening’s Euro rivet bush range.

Another of TR Fastenings’ key developments is the Swage Nut, a sheet metal product developed in the late 1990s aimed at giving flexibility to those working within this industry. Dimensionally the same as a standard self-clinch nut, it is installed the same way as a Hank rivet bush. This combination allows manufacturers to use the Swage Nut in a number of different materials.

It introduced the new K-Series nut in 2012, a versatile product which can be used for sheet steel and some plastic applications too thin to be tapped by conventional methods. The K-Series nut has been specially designed for use in applications with a minimum sheet thickness of 1mm. The nut, which offers a permanent female thread, can be installed without the need for specialist tooling. Once fixed, the product offers a permanent thread with a high prevailing torque and pull-out resistance.

The global market for aerospace fasteners is projected to reach US$ 6.8 billion by the year 2018, encouraged by increased shipments of aerospace equipment and the growing trend towards large-sized aircraft, according to a new report by Global Industry Analysts. The resurgence of aircraft manufacturing and improved military expenditure is expected to boost market prospects. There is also a significant demand for high performance fasteners from the wind energy industry, where large diameter and high tensile fasteners are required for wind turbines.

New technologies in construction markets have heralded the advent of new fastener types for the construction market. New infrastructure facilities such as the 2018 World Cup will also boost fastener demand. To combat increasing imports, fastener manufacturers may increasingly shift toward precision-turned products, a specialised segment that some overseas operators have yet to enter.

## ASIA CALLING

Government investment, competitive labour and material costs plus beneficial local economic and regulatory systems are just a few of the key drivers promoting the Asian manufacturing market

ISMR SAYS:

“Asia is undoubtedly one of the most attractive locations for global manufacturing companies”

With consumer wealth growing faster than in the West and countries booming to meet this demand, Asia is undoubtedly one of the most attractive locations for global manufacturing companies. China and India alone have middle classes that are set to exceed 800 million people by 2020 and possess the spending capability of US$ 3 trillion per year, creating a necessity for multi-nationals to build a strong presence to meet the demand. Government investment, competitive labour and material costs plus beneficial local economic and regulatory systems are just a few examples of the key drivers promoting the Asian manufacturing market.

Key issues, according to the organisers of the Asia Manufacturing Strategies Summit being held this October in Singapore, include the sourcing of talent-driven innovation; government investment and incentives; rising labour and material costs; harmonising legal and regulatory systems; cost reductions; quality control and management plus lean deployment, maintenance and development.

China is set to become the largest economy in the world by 2025 with a nominal GDP value of US$ 38 trillion. Fuelled by a strong urbanisation rate, a favourable corporate environment, huge infrastructure investment and the largest working age population, the Chinese economy will finally transform itself from being the manufacturing hub of the globe to one of the largest consumer markets in the world.

The growing majority of urban and young consumers will shape the demands of the future influencing innovation and future products and solutions.

## Automotive sector sees growth

ASEAN is set to become the sixth largest automotive market globally by 2018 with vehicle sales almost doubling to nearly 4.7 million units as compared to 2.4 million in 2011, predicts Frost & Sullivan.

Vijayendra Rao, Research Manager, Asia Pacific Automotive Practice at Frost & Sullivan confirmed that individually, none of the ASEAN countries has featured in the top ten markets globally but, as a region, it has assumed greater importance in the last few years due to the implementation of ASEAN Free Trade Agreement in 2010 and healthy rivalry among ASEAN member countries to attract foreign investments.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan “CEO 360 Degree Perspective of the Automotive Industry in ASEAN” (covering four key automotive markets in ASEAN – Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and Vietnam) finds that the market is likely to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 10.1 per cent (2011- 2018), mainly driven by growth in Thailand and Indonesia.

Mr Rao said that Thai and Indonesian vehicle sales are likely to hit one million units by 2013 driven by local demand, increased buying power and significant investments from Japanese OEMs. He added that Indian and Chinese automotive companies are also looking at expanding to ASEAN. ASEAN is a competitive automotive production base and a net vehicle exporter with strong competency in certain product ranges.

## Sectors and markets

The commercial aerospace, defence and maritime industries are also set for high growth over the next few years in the ASEAN region. Increasing economic development and rising demand for travel and trade in the region have been driving the need for the region to strengthen its air and sea transport infrastructure to meet these demands.

On the commercial aviation front, the introduction of LCCs and airport developments in the region are expected to drive growth of this industry in the near future. According to Frost & Sullivan, passenger traffic across airports in the five major ASEAN countries of Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Philippines and Thailand is expected to reach close to 318 million passengers by 2017.

Growth of maritime is expected to be driven by increasing port development in countries such as Singapore and Malaysia in efforts to increase their traffic handling capacities. The total sea freight traffic handled in this region is expected to reach 2.52 billion tonnes by 2017.

As far as defence is concerned, the acquisition of UAVs (unmanned aerial vehicles), armoured vehicles, ships and aircraft will drive defence procurement spending in the near future. A lot of this increased spending is being driven by the rising geopolitical instability in the region and the need for replacement of obsolete military equipment. Defence spending in the ASEAN region is expected to grow at a CAGR of close to 10 per cent from 2012 to 2020, reaching more than US$ 61 billion by 2020.

"Overall, 2012 to 2020 is expected to open many growth opportunities for the aerospace, defence and maritime industries in the ASEAN region in the near future. The implementation of several governmental reforms, such as the ASEAN Economic Community (AEC) and open skies agreements, among ASEAN countries is expected to drive growth of these industries in the region," said Neil Dave, Consulting Analyst of Aerospace & Defence at Frost & Sullivan Asia Pacific.
