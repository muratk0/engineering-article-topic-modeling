## Canada, Europe and MEIA region

“In Canada, the residential market was badly affected by the relatively cool summer. However, commercial sales continued to benefit mildly from the good economics and from the continued, sustained growth in new construction. In Mexico, the drop was mainly in the commercial sector due to the low construction activity experienced along 2013 and early 2014. The residential sector failed to record a growth but the drop was marginal.

“The European region declined in value in 2014 to an estimated US$ 11.2 billion a drop by 5% over 2013. Improvements in the UK and Germany were offset by declines in Russia, Turkey, Italy and Spain. The biggest growth was seen in the UK market, reaching US$ 1.0 billion – a rise of 22% over 2013. The UK air conditioning market is strongly linked to commercial construction trends and, of late, much of the AC market has been driven by trends in the London area - especially for central plant products and the VRF market,” concluded BSRIA.

According to BSRIA’s figures, the overall German AC market has reached US$ 1.2 billion showing 2% growth. Europe’s biggest AC market, Italy, declined by 14% followed by the second biggest market, Russia, which declined by 22%. The absence of largescale projects in Italy, with the exception of the Expo in Milan and the mild temperatures in 2014, have affected the chiller and DX markets negatively. Economic sanctions, the weakening of the Russian rouble against the US dollar and the return of Crimea were some of the important factors that impacted the Russian AC market in 2014.

The MEIA region showed respectable growth in 2014, increasing from US$ 8.5 billion to US$ 9.2 billion. The biggest AC market in the region, India, was the major contributor to this growth in 2014. The market reached US$ 2.2 billion from US$ 1.8 billion. There were several contributing factors such as rising business and consumer confidence after the new government was formed in May 2014, rising income levels and an increasing real estate sector.

Amongst the other significant growing countries were Nigeria; Qatar; South Africa; UAE and Iran, in order of growth rate by value. Egypt and Saudi Arabia were the only two important countries where growth was not seen, said BSRIA.

## HVAC in focus

“On the HVAC market front, the stars are aligning for residential on employment, lower gas and higher consumer confidence, all on an easy weather comparison with better price/mix and lower raw materials costs, while commercial markets face a steady non-residential recovery,” commented ReportsnReports.com on the “Global & US HVAC (Heating Ventilation Air Conditioning) Industry, 2015” report.

The US commercial HVAC market, says the report, remains a more straightforward call with about 5% growth in 2015/16 based on an ongoing non-res. construction recovery (6-7% growth) and stable 4% replacement. Ductless continues to be the talk of the industry, a market that could come to represent 15% of the industry for both residential (~10%) and commercial (~5%).

“OEMs were able to realise solid pricing gains in 2014, with most companies announcing low- to mid-single-digit price increases for 2015,” said the report.

On similar lines, another US-focused research report, entitled, “HVAC Equipment to 2019”, confirms that the US demand for HVAC equipment is forecast to increase 6.8% annually through to 2019 to US$ 20.4 billion, recording gains at about twice the rate of the 2009-2014 period. In large part, advances will be the result of robust gains in building construction spending, especially growth in improvement and repair expenditures. Advances will also be propelled by rising demand for increasingly efficient HVAC systems and other types that have a higher degree of technological sophistication, as these are typically higher value systems.

The US HVAC equipment market to 2019 research report (www.reportsnreports.com/reports/354196-hvacequipment-to-2019.html) confirms that the non-residential market will continue to comprise the larger share of overall HVAC equipment demand in value terms, due to the larger size of HVAC systems used in these buildings and their subsequently higher cost. However, growth in the residential market will significantly outpace that in the non-residential market through to 2019. The number of new housing units will improve substantially through to 2019, providing ample opportunities for new residential HVAC equipment installations.

Analysts forecast that the HVAC equipment market in China will grow at a CAGR of 8.51% over the period 2014-2019. The market can be segmented into three based on product type - cooling equipment, heating equipment and ventilation equipment. ■

THALMANN has upgraded its double folders with new tooling geometry and speed gains. Its managing director, Marco Cappello, shares his view on innovation and technology development with ISMR.

HALMANN Maschinenbau AG can trace its history back to the one-man business founded by Otto Thalmann in 1948. The simple locksmith and metalworking business has expanded to become a major mechanical engineering company with innovative ideas in the sheet metal working sector, particularly since the development and production of its folding machines in 1960. Since then, the company’s head office and production plant have been based in Frauenfeld, Switzerland.

As a pioneer in long length bending machine construction, THALMANN has been awarded with the Bavarian State Gold Medal for excellent workmanship. It is constantly innovating with new products and technologies aimed at simplifying working practices in sheet metalworking plants and industries.

“Even as far back as 1987, our safety standards were accredited with the GS identification number. With the introduction of the European Safety Standards, all our machines are produced to conform to the latest standards and their safety has been attested with the CE safety mark,” its CEO, Marco Cappello, told ISMR.

## A focus on folding

THALMANN Maschinenbau AG presented a range of folding innovations at this November’s Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart, including the launch of its newly upgraded double folder TD Professional Plus series for roofing, facades and industrial applications.

“This upgraded model offers a 10% speed gain and new folding tool geometry which allows us to bend shapes or parts that I believe no-one else can do right now,” explained Marco Cappello. “The clamp beam tool has the flattest angle in market so it can form parts with proportion 3:4 (e.g. 5cm high x 7cm deep). We have also introduced a loading table for the machine that pops out automatically. This loading aid will simplify the material feed significantly. The device can handle parts of full machine length (3-12m) and thicknesses of up to 3mm mild steel. There is also new material handling functionality that can grab and position small parts down to widths of 14mm (5mm, with stop finger). This is the first time that this upgraded folder has been on display at a show.”

The machines are supplied with THALMANN’s own software – it offers upgraded versions of the software as free upgrades for customers as it does not believe in software licensing. Simple and intuitive operation were its primary goals in developing the latest machine control software. The controller is able to read BENDEX files and DXF files from AutoDesk Inventor. This is essential if it comes to machine integration into company ERP systems and processes.

“We have done well this year, considering the high Swiss currency rate levels. This tells us that customers want quality and high performance. Price becomes relative – RoI (Return on Investment) is far more important. We sell worldwide into sectors such as industrial, construction, facade/roofing, automotive and energy markets (noise-reducing panels) etc. There are still other folding applications out there which are currently being done on a press brake. Double folding is a much more economical and convenient way of bending – it is also safer as the operator is not exposed to moving parts,” explained Cappello.

The company’s innovations include ‘DFT System’ (Dynamic Folding Technology), introduced in 2014 into its bending models. It has developed an energy-efficient kinetic drive shaft for its folding models, with an eye on safety and continuous product development. The kinetic drive system distributes the power of all machines stands evenly on the clamping and bending beam. This is comparable to the mechanical flap control used in aircraft construction.

Marco Cappello with Peter Tafazoli (right), CEO of Cidan Machinery Sweden AB

“We try to push innovation,” continued Marco Cappello. “Productivity and process flexibility is our focus this year. You must keep innovating and investing. People are the lifeblood of THALMANN – their energy and expertise keeps us on a continuous innovation curve.”

Thalmann’s Professional Plus Series of up and down folding models offer great flexibility in the design of sophisticated sheet metal parts. Thanks to a new tool concept, equipped with interchangeable bending beam utensils, users can adapt the tool mounted onto the carrier bending beam, depending upon the situation and requirements. The working range of the machine therefore significantly expands.

There is also an option to retrofit standard tools to Hardox – this will increase the design scope of products. A further advantage over previous solutions includes service issues – the tool, designed as multiple parts, can be replaced singularly, quickly and without long interruptions in production. Downtime is therefore reduced to a minimum.

## An eye on the future

THALMANN delivers worldwide and is active on every continent. It has partnered with Cidan, a bending technology producer, to jointly market their technologies in Scandinavia, Sweden, Switzerland, Asia and the US. The two companies have a long history of cooperation as they offer complementary sheet metal processing technologies. Cidan launched its up/down folder at Blechexpo (on the same booth as THALMANN) and the two companies recently completed some joint installations in New Zealand.

Cappello also highlighted several trends in folding and bending markets to ISMR during the show.

“Increased levels of automation; safe process flexibility and higher output/productivity are constant market requirements,” he explained.

“There is also more of a need for green/energy-efficient working and we have developed several innovations in line with these requirements.”

He is also keen to keep expertise alive in the company by ensuring a constant flow of ‘young blood’ throughout the organisation.

“We pay particular attention to the training of the next generation since highly educated professionals are essential for the future of a successful company. We regularly train young people in plant and mechanical engineering (plant/machine builders) principles. Apprenticeships are key to our continued success,” he concluded. ■
