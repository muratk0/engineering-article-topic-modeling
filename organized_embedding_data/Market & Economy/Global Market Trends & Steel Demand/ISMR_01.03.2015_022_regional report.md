RUSSIA IN FOCUS

We analyse Russian market opportunities for sheet metal manufacturers

Russia covers one seventh of the world’s land mass. Its incredible size means that it spans nine time zones, has extremes of climate and weather ranging from sub-tropical in the south to Permafrost in the north as well as varying levels of development, plus differing social and economic factors.

Russian economic growth has slowed annually since 2010. The economy grew by 1.3 per cent in 2013. The economic slowdown is partly due to developments in the global economy but growth has also fallen further as a result of international reaction to Russia’s actions in the Ukraine. However, there are also structural constraints which restrain Russia’s growth potential including a large state sector, the inefficient use of labour and a challenging business environment.

The severe slowdown in Russia’s economy this year is largely due to weak oil prices, sanctions and the country’s dependence on energy and commodity exports. The weakened rouble has also dramatically impacted the rate of inflation in the country.

## Doing business in Russia

According to the UKTI, Russia has started major investment and modernisation programmes which will provide opportunities for overseas companies looking to do business in the region. It is looking for foreign investment, expertise, technology and resources to help. About 600 UK companies have a physical presence in Russia. Over 5,800 UK traders exported goods to Russia in 2013.

“Russia’s diverse regions also present opportunities for UK business. These regions are increasingly competing to attract international investment. Some regions have made significant improvements to make it easier to do business, such as Kaluga and Kazan,” it explained.

The benefits for businesses exporting to Russia include:

■ The desire of Russian companies to use international accounting and legal standards.

■ The anticipated increase in the number of Russian mid- and smallsized businesses.

Strengths of the Russian market include a young and well educated workforce, strong transport links and logistics as well as access to supply chains that could lead to exports to other markets in the Commonwealth of Independent States (CIS).

Routes to market in Russia include direct supply, Joint Ventures (JVs), taking part in the creation and running of engineering centres and joint Research and Development (R&D). The main forms of business activity used by overseas companies in Russia are direct sales, distributorship agreements, representative offices, branch of a foreign legal entity or subsidiaries.

There are also many Special Economic Zones (SEZ) in the country which grant certain tax, customs and other concessions to residents. The country is also a key automotive market in Europe with strong demand for luxury vehicles, although this has been badly impacted by the current economic slowdown in the country.

Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan have signed a treaty establishing the Eurasian Economic Union (EEU) which will form a Customs Union and a Single Economic Space The EEU aims to create conditions for the free movement of goods, services, labour and capital.

Russia became a full member of the WTO in 2012. It has committed to change customs duty rates to permit access to the Russian services market, apply recognised food safety, and animal and plant health measures, simplify import licensing procedures and make changes relating to the protection of intellectual property. Russia is also a signatory to major international treaties on intellectual property rights.

## Industrial requirements

Russian heavy industry, across all manufacturing sub-sectors, needs to update management practices, production processes and manufacturing equipment. Over 70 per cent of existing capital assets are estimated to be outdated, according to the UKTI, who estimates that Russian demand for new metal machining equipment alone is valued at US$ 1.5 billion annually. There are opportunities for companies to supply:

■ Energy-efficient manufacturing technologies and equipment, such as cleaning and recovery solutions and industrial automation solutions for resource management.

■ Machine tools and tooling.

■ Spare parts and components.

■ Advanced materials for manufacturing and assembly.

Russia’s oil and gas market is one of the largest markets in the world. It was valued at US$ 23 – 25 billion in 2012. The sector is transitioning from the production of hydrocarbons in traditional fields to development of hardto-recover and offshore reserves. Russia currently imports more than a third of its oil and gas equipment. There is still demand for expertise and technologies for extraction of hard-to-recover and offshore reserves.

The Russian nuclear industry is a world leader. The state atomic energy corporation, Rosatom, has orders for 80 units. This includes construction of nuclear plants in India, Bulgaria, Turkey, Armenia, Ukraine, Vietnam, China, Belarus, Czech Republic and Bangladesh.

Russia’s vast territory has massive potential for all kinds of renewables. However, Russian interest is growing from a very small base. Russian interest in partnering with overseas companies in the wind, hydro and solar energy sectors is increasing significantly. There are opportunities to supply equipment and expertise

Russia also ratified Renewable Energy Source Development Measures (RESDM). This will increase the volume of renewable energy projects in Russia and open up the market to providers of both goods and services.

There are also strong opportunities for improved energy efficiency across all sectors of the Russian economy and the entire chain of energy use. This includes energy generation, distribution and final consumption.

Russia will host the 21st FIFA World Cup in 2018, the first World Cup to be held in Russia. The government has developed an infrastructure programme to deliver the project and create a lasting legacy.

The designated budget of US$ 20 billion for 2013 to 2018 was to be spent on the design and construction of 12 stadiums, 48 training grounds, 64 team base camps, broadcasting and media centres and the modernisation of airports and transport infrastructure. Nearly 40 per cent of the budget will go into building, or renovating, multi-purpose football arenas.

Opportunities will also arise in a broad range of sectors including design; construction technologies; project management; transport planning; security and access control equipment; sport venue fit-out; venue commercialisation and hospitality.
