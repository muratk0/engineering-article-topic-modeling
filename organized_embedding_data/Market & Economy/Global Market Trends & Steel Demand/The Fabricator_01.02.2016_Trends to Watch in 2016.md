# Trends to Watch in 2016

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl, FMA Economic Analyst

Well, this is a nice way to start a new year. It seems that it doesn’t require massive snowstorms to make the first quarter miserable; this time it is enough to get worried about China and to watch the price of a barrel of oil plummet. The No. 1 question now is whether this is just a phase or a signal of what is to come in 2016. If last year is any indication, it is probably going to be hard to pin down just what growth will look like in 2016.

U.S.’s trading partners are struggling.

If you remember that roller coaster of a year in 2015, we had a miserable Q1 followed by a Q2 that was almost at 4 percent growth. The third quarter was anemic but not awful (2.0), and the estimate of Q4 is something under 1 percent. Should we look forward to another year of these ups and downs? Probably.

At the end of the day we are likely to see that 2015 finished with annual growth pretty near the norm of the last few decades — somewhere between 2.0 percent and 2.5 percent. This is not enough to cause either panic or dancing in the streets — it is just the kind of steady and somewhat disappointing activity we have come to expect of late.

For those who want to play economist at home, keep an eye on three trends — two that would make things worse and one that would herald an improvement. The first bad thing is a continued slide in global economies. The U.S.’s export levels have fallen, and that has been attributed to the strength of the dollar. This is a factor, but the real problem is that most of the U.S.’s trading partners are struggling. The second negative trend is the continuation of higher interest rates as set by Fed policy. If rates get high enough, they will cause the economy to stall.

The positive trend to watch is the consumer. They have the money to spend if they desire to do so. They have been willing to buy cars, but not much else. When they elect to open their wallets, the economy will start to hum.
