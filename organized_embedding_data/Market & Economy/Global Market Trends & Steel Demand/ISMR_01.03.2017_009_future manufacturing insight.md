# FUTURE MANUFACTURING INSIGHT

[TARİH: 01.03.2017 ISMR]

MORE THAN 200 advanced manufacturing leaders attended AMT's Global Forecasting & Marketing conference from 19-21 October 2016 in Miami, Florida. GFMC speakers identified emerging developments and business forecasts to help small- to mediumsized businesses in the U.S. to fine-tune their strategic plan for optimum performance in 2017.

The four economists who spoke at GFMC agreed that the market will remain challenging in 2016 and were split between positive and negative for 2017. President of ITR Economics, Alan Beaulieu, pointed out that manufacturing is the fourth largest employer in the United States, and that the U.S. continues to attract foreign manufacturing companies even though it has one of the highest corporate tax rates. He cautioned a 2019 recession, regardless of who won the White House race.

Gregory Daco, head of U.S. Macroeconomics at Oxford Economics, noted that the world economic outlook is growing at a very slow rate and that excess global savings is still a major constraint. Steve Kline, director of market intelligence at Gardner Business Media, expected a rise in consumption in 2017. Eli Lustgarten focused on agriculture, construction and mining, and anticipated slower demand for discretionary farm equipment. Likewise, demand is soft for mining equipment due to the slowing Chinese economy and an uncertain outlook for coal. Although the construction equipment sector is still impacted by lower energy costs, price increases for new equipment are being driven by regulatory cost increases.

Richard Aboulafia, vice president of analysis at the Teal Group, described the state of the aerospace industry as generally strong for the U.S. economy. With the aerospace industry hitting a monetary high for new aircraft deliveries at approximately US$ 181 billion, growth may be slowing, and he believed that a slowdown may follow a 12-year "super cycle" of high growth. Because of these issues and slowing demand in emerging markets, Aboulafia warned that growth will be uneven in the next few years.

For the automotive industry, IHS managing director Michael Robinet saw global growth in 2017 as markets strengthen in the United States, European Union, Brazil and Russia. The Detroit automakers probably reached peak production in 2016, according to Robinet, and the rise of the Asian carmakers will cause U.S. companies to lose market share. Structural changes such as electrification, automated driving and new metals and regulatory challenges will add complexity and costs to cars, signalling a looming cost cliff in the future.

GFMC 2017 will take place from 11-13 October 2017 in Atlanta, U.S. ■
