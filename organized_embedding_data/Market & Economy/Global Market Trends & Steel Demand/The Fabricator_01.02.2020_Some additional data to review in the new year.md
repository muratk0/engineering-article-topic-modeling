# Some additional data to review in the new year

[TARİH: 01.02.2020 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

The Chinese version of the Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) is expected to show a second straight month of improvement, and that would seem to signal that there is more stability in their manufacturing community despite the impact of the trade war and tariff s. The U.S. is expected to have yet another month in contraction territory (numbers under 50), and the decline in the Eurozone is expected to worsen as well.

The PMI is closely watched for a variety of reasons. In most cases, economic data is limited in one of two ways. It’s an old adage that one can have data that is either accurate or timely, but rarely both. It takes a while to collect data, and by the time the collection process and analysis are complete, the information can be stale. Surveys are used to get the most current information regarding the economy, but it is not uncommon for people to lie when responding to a survey or simply not know enough to answer a question accurately.

Purchasing managers have a specific job, and it’s one that puts them at the center of business activity. They are not the ones deciding what to buy or why. They are executing the directives of others in the organization and thus their information is not biased by their personal observations or opinions. It is what it is. Buying more will suggest expansion and growth, and buying less will signal decline.

It was only a year ago that PMI numbers for the U.S. were close to 60 and today these readings are in the high 40s. This is not yet catastrophic. If the decline were into the low 40s and upper 30s, the concern would be far more intense, and this is not the trend that anybody wants to see. It is especially worrisome that most of the rest of the world is looking at numbers that are even lower than those in the U.S. right now.
