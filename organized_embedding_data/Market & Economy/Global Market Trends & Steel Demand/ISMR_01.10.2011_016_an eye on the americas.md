# AN EYE ON THE AMERICAS

[TARİH: 01.10.2011 ISMR]

ISMR highlights trends and movements in selected Latin American economies.

Latin America was reasonably resilient to the global economic downturn, when the region recorded a positive performance thanks to high commodity prices and its increasing trade with China. Healthy economic growth across South America is delivering sharp increases in car sales and more money for governments to invest in infrastructure.

“It is widely recognised that Latin America and the Caribbean weathered the 2008-2009 recession much better than it had previous downturns. Less well-known is that the region also decisively outperformed many other regions during the same period with a decline in growth smaller than that of the middle-income country average and with a rebound that was swifter and stronger,” commented the World Bank.

The region’s growth of about 6 per cent last year exceeded the growth rate of Eastern Europe and Central Asia and the rate of high-income countries by more than three percentage points, according to a new report “Latin America and the Caribbean’s Success Put to the Test” prepared by the World Bank’s chief economist office for the region. Post-crisis growth performance has been uneven within the region. Several South American countries, most notably Argentina, Brazil, Peru, Paraguay and Uruguay, have registered remarkably vigorous recovery with growth rates that exceeded 7.5 per cent in 2010.

## ARGENTINA

The Argentinean base metals industry enjoyed total revenues of US$ 1.2 billion in 2010, representing a compound annual rate of change (CARC) of -2.6 per cent for the period spanning 2006 – 2010, according to Datamonitor.

“Industry production volumes decreased with a CARC of -3.8 per cent between 2006 and 2010, to reach a total of 189.8 thousand metric tons in 2010. The performance of the industry is forecast to accelerate, with an anticipated CAGR of 2.1 per cent for the five-year period 2010-2015, which is expected to drive the industry to a value of US$ 1.3 billion by the end of 2015,” Datamonitor told ISMR.

“Argentinean copper production saw total revenues of US$ 1,095.8 million in 2010. Market production volumes decreased with a CAGR of -5 per cent between 2006 and 2010, to reach a total of 146.6 thousand metric tonnes in 2010.”

“The performance of the industry is forecast to accelerate, with an anticipated CAGR of 0.3 per cent for the five-year period 2010 - 2015, which is expected to drive the industry to a value of US$ 1,111.7 million by the end of 2015.”

## Brazil is one of the fastest growing emerging economies in the world

## BRAZIL

Gross Domestic Product (GDP) in Brazil expanded 1.30 per cent in the first quarter of 2011 over the previous quarter. Brazil is one of the fastest growing emerging economies in the world. With large and growing agricultural, mining, manufacturing and service sectors, Brazil’s economy ranks highest among all the South American countries and it has acquired a strong position in the global economy.

Brazil will be staging the Football World Cup in 2014 and the Summer Olympic Games in 2016.

A large number of infrastructure measures are planned for these large-scale sporting events.

According to Business Monitor International (BMI), Brazil’s steel and aluminium industries are coming under increasing pressure from the influx of imports, which are taking an increasing share of the country’s burgeoning metals market. Margins are being squeezed by high costs, notably in raw materials and energy. Despite the challenges, Brazilian steelmakers continue to enjoy higher rates of output with a notable recovery in long steel output in Q1 2011, although crude output is still 5 per cent below full potential and producers were facing a squeeze on margins due to import prices.

For the first two months of 2011, Brazil’s crude steel output rose 8 per cent year-on-year (y-o-y) reaching 5.55mn tonnes, while its rolled steel output increased by 5 per cent to 4.05mn tonnes, including 2.44mn tonnes of flat products (up 5.6%) and 1.61mn tonnes of long products (up 4.1%).

Conditions will remain challenging for industry players throughout 2011 due to over-capacity, high costs of iron ore and scrap and limited pricing power in segments such as steel products used for the auto sector. The key drivers of growth in 2010, investment and private consumption, are likely to cool in 2011 as monetary and fiscal stimulus measures are withdrawn, with a knock-on effect on sales volumes. As such, BMI forecasts domestic finished steel consumption growing by a more modest 7 per cent to 25.65mn tonnes in 2011.

Production in the aluminium sector in Brazil remained flat in 2010 despite a 28.5 per cent y-o-y rise in consumption of aluminium fabricated products to 1.3mn tonnes, 100,000 tonnes above the forecast by industry body, Abal. Brazilian primary aluminium production reached 1.54mn tonnes, up just 0.1 per cent y-o-y and well down on the 1.63mn tonnes in 2008. Most smelters saw output stagnate or decline slightly.

BMI believes that Brazilian aluminium production will come under assault from a combination of cheap imports, exacerbated by the strength of the real, over the medium term. Cheaper imports are taking up more local demand growth, which could rise by a further 9 per cent in 2011. In order to halt the decline in domestic production, Abal has called for action against imports from China in the form of tariffs to counter the 12 -15 per cent export rebate which Chinese aluminium exporters enjoy.

“The Brazilian copper production industry had total revenues of US$ 1.7 billion in 2010, representing a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 14.8 per cent for the period spanning 2006-2010,” Datamonitor told ISMR.

“The performance of the market is forecast to accelerate, with an anticipated CAGR of 29.9 per cent for the five-year period 2010-2015, which is expected to drive the market to a value of US$ 6.4 billion by the end of 2015.”

## CHILE

According to the INE (Chilean Institute of National Statistics), economic indicators in December 2010 showed manufacturing reporting a twelve-month growth of 3.8 per cent, driven by increased domestic and external demand. Electric power consumption reported year-on-year growth of 3.1 per cent, mainly caused by increased demand in the mining, residential and commerce sectors. The annual trend showed a growth rate of 0.6 per cent compared to the same period in 2009. Increased demand led some industries to start investing in machinery and expand their production capacity. The Retail Sales Index (IVCM) recorded twelve-month real growth of 19.9 per cent and cumulative growth at the end of the year of 15.9 per cent over the same period in 2009. The expansion of this sector is due principally to increased sales of new and used cars, textile products and domestic appliances. Improved consumer expectations helped drive the sector in a context of falling prices of textile products and durable goods.

For manufacture of basic metals, there was an impact of 1.17 percentage points on the index caused by increased production of molybdenum, which is mainly used as a raw material in the manufacture of special steels and alloys.

The base metals market in Chile consists of lead, zinc, copper, nickel and tin.

## MEXICO

Mexico is the second largest economy in Latin America. The proximity of Mexico to the United States and its comparatively lower costs of infrastructure and labour to that of the latter are also strong drivers, especially for companies planning a further expansion into North America.

Today, Mexico is one of the world's leading manufacturing countries and a major consumer and exporter of manufactured goods. Manufacturing, construction and energy projects are growing in Mexico, demanding new equipment, services and technologically advanced products.

## PERU

The United Nations Industrial Development Organisation (UNIDO) and the Government of Peru recently strengthened their cooperation in trade capacity building, environmental protection and use of renewable energy.

UNIDO and the Peruvian Government have established an export consortia for small and medium enterprises. The project is financed by the Italian Development Cooperation. The 30 consortia cover a wide variety of sectors ranging from jewellery, to information technology and tourism.

The Government of Peru has also established a "National Rural Electrification Plan – Light for All" (2006 - 2011) to extend the national electricity coverage through the implementation of plans and electrification projects in marginal and rural areas.
