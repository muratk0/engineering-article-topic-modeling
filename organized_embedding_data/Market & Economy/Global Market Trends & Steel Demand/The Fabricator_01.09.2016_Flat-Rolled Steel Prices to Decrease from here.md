s this is written in mid-August 2016, the "Steel Market Update" (SMU) has just adjusted its Price Momentum Indicator from "neutral" to "lower." We believe flat-rolled steel prices will slide over the next 30 to 60 days.

Since June 14 the Price Momentum Indicator has stood at neutral, which means flat-rolled market pricing is either in transition or does not have enough momentum to push prices in one direction or another. On Aug. 16 SMU adjusted its indicator to lower. SMU made this adjustment because of circumstances that we believe will have an impact on the market in the coming weeks.

First, we cannot ignore that the domestic steel mills have begun to compete for orders by adjusting base prices, freight considerations, or other enticements (or a combination of the three) to fill their order books. As we look at benchmark hot-rolled coil (HRC), prices peaked in the North American market the week of June 6 when HRC average spot mill base prices hit $635/ton ($31.75/cwt average). Since then it has slowly eroded, and as of Aug. 16 HRC broke through the $600/ton level on the Steel Market Update index ($595/ton or $29.75/cwt average).

Lead times—the amount of time a domestic mill needs to produce a newly placed order—are becoming shorter. The shorter lead times typically suggest a weaker market. Steel buyers make fewer purchases because they can work from inventories that were built up when lead times were extended. These lack of orders puts pressure on the steel mills and the rest of the supply chain, with the normal reaction being to reduce pricing to get orders released and placed on the mill.

When reviewing NLMK USA lead times from July 11, we saw Farrell, Pa.-produced hot-rolled lead times were out to the week of Aug. 8, which was five weeks. Cold-rolled fully processed (annealed) was 10 weeks, and galvanized was 11 weeks.

Now with more than a month gone by, the NLMK USA lead times on hot-rolled out of Farrell are three weeks, fully processed cold-rolled eight weeks, and galvanized seven weeks.

We see similar results when checking the lead times for Nucor’s Berkeley, S.C., facility from July 12 against those dated Aug. 15. On July 12 hot-rolled was being quoted for the week ending Aug. 13 on product out to 54 inch wide, a five-week lead time, and on Aug. 15 the same product was being referenced as the week ending Aug. 27, which is a twoweek lead time. Cold-rolled lead time shortened from seven weeks to four weeks, while galvanized went from inquire only to five weeks.

SMU sources are advising us that the mini-mills, also known as electric arc furnace mills, are the most aggressive when it comes to hot-rolled pricing because they are able to produce rolled steel in less than two weeks, if needed. However, not all products are equal, and as of mid-August, we are seeing a price gap between hot-rolled steel and coldrolled/coated product of $200/ton or more. The normal spread between the base prices of HR to CR and coated steels is $100 to $140/ton.

The Metals Service Center Institute (MSCI) released inventory and shipment data on Aug. 16 that shows flat-rolled shipments in July were off 10.4 percent year-over-year, while inventories grew by 220,000 tons. The latter news came as a surprise, as industry expectations called for a reduction in inventories. The Steel Market Update Service Center Inventory Apparent Excess model has flatrolled inventories going from a 164,000-ton deficit to a 48,000-ton excess during July. The number of months of supply increased to 2.5 months according to the MSCI calculations.

August imports, based on license data through Aug. 16, are pointing toward a 2.8 million-ton month, with flat-rolled items trending toward no significant change from what we have seen over the last couple of months. Foreign steel imports at or above 2.8 million net tons is not positive news for the domestic steel mills.

It is becoming clearer that demand is not as good as was expected for the second half of 2016. The most recent proprietary Steel Market Update survey conducted the week of Aug. 15 provided an interesting snapshot of the changes in demand according to the approximately 150 manufacturing companies, service centers, toll processors, trading companies, and steel mills that participate in the bimonthly surveys.
