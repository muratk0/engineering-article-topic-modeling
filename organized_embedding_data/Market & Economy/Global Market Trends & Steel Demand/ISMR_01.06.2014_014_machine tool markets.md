# MACHINE TOOL MARKETS

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 ISMR]

Filip Geerts, Director General of CECIMO (European Association of Machine Tool Industries), discusses machine tool and sheet metal market trends and forecasts

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for manufacturers?

FG: Machine tool production trends depend upon business cycle. The present global economic environment is characterised by, on one hand, the improving business climate in developed countries and, on the other hand, slowing growth in emerging markets. We are especially happy to see increasing consumption in Europe. The European machine tool industry’s competitive advantage stems from the unique bond between machine tool builders and their clients which translates into a dynamic innovation system. We estimate European machine tool consumption to gain 2 per cent in 2014. Also, other developed markets, such as the United States and Japan, are performing relatively well. CECIMO’s exports to Asia declined 10 per cent in 2013 but the region still has potential.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to CECIMO. Please explain your mission and objectives?

FG: CECIMO was founded in 1950 with the purpose of organising a new type of machine tool exhibition, allowing buyers of machine tools, those working in the industry and those connected with it, a complete view of the market place. The first exhibition organised by CECIMO, E.E.M.O., was held in 1951 in Paris and then on every odd year in different European cities.

The exhibition in Paris in 1975 opened its door for the first time to non-European manufacturers, and was re-named EMO®. CECIMO then demonstrated its desire to provide machine tool users with an opportunity to compare products from all over the world at a single exhibition. A still greater concentration of production equipment for user equipment was included and assistance was provided to establish contacts between manufacturers and buyers.

So, CECIMO’s mission has widened over the years. Now, we work at facilitating the business environment, including legislation, innovation, environment, skilled workforce, financing, supply chain and infrastructure, to market the most competitive manufacturing solutions worldwide. We also promote a single European market that is open to global competition through harmonised worldwide standards and ensure a European level playing field in compliance with European standards.

Because the European machine tool industry’s competitive advantage lies in innovation, CECIMO also encourages a research and innovation strategy driven by the present and future needs of the market. It works to stimulate dialogue between science and industry and, to further foster innovation and integration of new technologies, it also facilitates the involvement of machine tool companies in national and European programmes.

ISMR: Who are your members (can you segment them)? Where are they based? Which issues are of prime importance for them at the moment and how are you addressing these issues?

FG: CECIMO represents the European machine tool industry with a focus on metalworking covering metal cutting, metal forming and electro discharge machining segments. CECIMO members are 15 national associations of machine tool builders in Europe (EU, EFTA and Turkey). Current issues of importance are the internationalisation of SMEs, the skills deficit, research & innovation and access to finance.

CECIMO provides European companies with market access support through the EMO and EMTE-EASTPO trade shows. Regarding skills, we recently issued a Skills Manifesto to attract the attention of policy-makers to the challenges we are facing. Moreover, we have introduced some projects on vocational education and training for EU funding.

Innovation is the only way to tackle fierce global competition; CECIMO provides its members with information and guidance about available EU funding in this area and helps them to find innovation partnerships.

There is still limited finance both for machine tool companies and their SME customers due to deleveraging in the banking sector. CECIMO publishes statistics and forecasts about the European machine sector to provide financial actors and investors with a clear picture of the industry, its performance and growth potential. We are currently in a close dialogue with EU policy-makers as regards possible measures, including financing schemes and tax incentives especially for customers, that could help revitalise productive investments in Europe in accordance with the reindustrialisation objective set out by the European Commission.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

FG: The low-growth, low-employment period we are going through has shown the importance of the manufacturing industry. Manufacturing is the major driver of private research & development investments and the creation of highly skilled, well-paid jobs. Also, manufacturing goods are tradable globally and are, as such, generators of wealth. Last but not least, manufacturing has the answers to most of the grand challenges facing our society including energy, climate change, sustainable mobility and health.

A major goal for CECIMO is to highlight the enabling role of the European machine tool industry for a competitive European manufacturing base, and to advocate for the favourable framework conditions for advancing it. Technological innovation will be critical to maintaining the leadership position of our sector. Issues on our radar screen include Industry 4.0, automation, energy and resource efficiency but also additive manufacturing.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in global sheet metal/manufacturing/machine tool markets?

FG: Global manufacturing is moving towards an increasing need for flexibility and so-called mass customisation. This environment is favourable for the European machine tool sector. Our CECIMO Business Climate Survey shows that 66 per cent of European machine tool builders estimate production to increase in 2014.

We expect a more balanced global economic environment in 2014 onwards. While China moves from an investment-based economy towards a more consumption-based economy, Europe is gradually exiting the worst economic crisis of the history. This year, we forecast the trend of investment in machine tool purchasing industries to turn upwards and result in 5 per cent machine tool consumption growth in the world.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

FG: The greatest opportunity is the increasing technical requirements of customers. User industries around the world are moving gradually towards better and higher quality production to respond to customer demands and to cope with fierce competition. Another factor that increases the stringency of technical requirements is environmental pressure which, for example, has a direct impact on car engines. Labour costs also show a growing increase in China, so end-users are compelled to automate their production lines.

The most important challenges are increasing international competition and the difficulties for European companies, especially for SMEs, to enter emerging markets where growth and the largest share of the cake are found.

ISMR: In which geographic markets do you see projected growth for your members’ products?

FG: Today, Asia consumes about 61 per cent of all machine tools in the world. Even if we do not foresee further substantial growth in that ratio, Asia remains an important market for European machine tool producers. Increasing labour costs and the need for high precision creates opportunities for European machine tools. If we add to that the growing middle class with its increasing demand that channels to several traditional machine tool consuming sectors, we certainly see a good export outlook for our industry.

However, as mentioned earlier, domestic markets are crucial for European machine tool builders. In Europe we see less fresh demand for products of the key machine tool buying sectors because of high penetration levels. However, the replacement demand creates continued investment spending that keeps the European machine tool sector innovating, looking for novel production solutions. We estimate European machine tool consumption to grow about 6 per cent annually during the next three years.

ISMR: Please outline details of the EMTE-EASTPO show this year and latest news on the event. Why should people attend?

FG: This year is the inaugural edition of EMTE-EASTPO, a strategic east-west partnership of two established machine tool show owners from Europe and China. It brings together advanced technology, cutting-edge innovations and high-performance machinery under one roof. It also serves as a one-stop business and networking platform for exhibitors to meet with their buyers in the region. Visitors and exhibitors alike will definitely be delighted not to have missed this first machine tool exhibition in Shanghai responding to European quality standards.

Several hundred companies have confirmed their participation at EMTE-EASTPO. They include Bystronic; Carl Zeiss; Comau; Doosan; Emuge-Franken Precision Tool; Fastems; Murata Machinery; Starrag and United Grinding (formerly known as Korber Schleifring) among others. Six national pavilions from Czech Republic, Germany, Italy, Spain, South Korea and Switzerland will also be represented at the exhibition.

In addition to the several knowledge-sharing, business matching and networking platforms, EMTE-EASTPO will feature a CEO Summit. Scheduled for 15 July 2014 at Kerry Hotel Pudong, Shanghai, the summit will host some of the region’s key industry leaders to share on their experiences on the theme Profitable Growth Through State-of-the-Art Manufacturing. The high-level panel of speakers include Prof. Hellmut Schutte, from the China Europe International Business School (CEIBS), Sancha Garcia, from the Volkswagen Group China and Dr Stefan Sack, COMAU China.

Visitors can pre-register at www.emteeastpo.cn/reg/Language/en/Register.aspx or see www.emte-eastpo.com/home.html
