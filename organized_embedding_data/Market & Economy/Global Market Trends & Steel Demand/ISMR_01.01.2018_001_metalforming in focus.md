# Metalforming in focus

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

METALFORMING MANUFACTURERS ANTICIPATE steady business conditions over the three months from November 2017 according to the November 2017 Precision Metalforming Association (PMA) Business Conditions Report. Prepared monthly, the report is an economic indicator for manufacturing, sampling 114 metalforming companies in the United States and Canada.

The November report shows that 30% of participants expect an improvement in economic activity for the three months from November 2017 (the same percentage reported in October). Another 54% forecast no change (compared to 53% the previous month) and 16% believe that economic activity will decline (down from 17% in October).

"PMA members continue to express caution regarding economic conditions for the manufacturing sector in the coming months," said PMA President, Roy Hardy. "Uncertainty over potential action in Washington that could profoundly impact the economic health of manufacturers, particularly with regard to the future of NAFTA and the potential imposition of steel tariffs, means that PMA members continue to have a guarded outlook.

"However, manufacturers also continue to show not only resilience, but also the innovation that sets U.S. manufacturers apart from their global competition. This was on display at the FABTECH tradeshow, the largest metalforming, fabricating, welding and finishing event in the United States, held in Chicago in November 2017. PMA is a show sponsor and sold a record amount of exhibit space to metalforming and stamping providers and suppliers, who displayed incredible new products and technologies."

Metalforming companies also anticipate little change in incoming orders during the next three months, with 49% predicting no change (compared to 47% in October), 32% forecasting an increase in orders (down from 35% in October) and 19% expecting a decrease in orders (up from 18% in October).

Current average daily shipping levels rose in November 2017. Thirty-nine per cent of participants report that shipping levels are above the levels of three months ago (compared to 34% in October), 46% report that levels are the same (up from 45% the previous month) and only 15% report a decrease in shipping levels (down from 21% in October).

The percentage of metalforming companies with a portion of their workforce on short-time or layoff dropped to 1% in November 2017, down from 3% in October. This figure is much better than during November 2016, when 14% of companies reported workers on short time or layoff.

Full report results are available at www.pma.org/public/business_reports/pdf/BCREP.pdf. PMA is the full-service trade association representing the US$ 137 billion metalforming industry of North America – the industry that creates precision metal products using stamping, fabricating, spinning, slide forming and roll forming technologies, and other value-added processes.■
