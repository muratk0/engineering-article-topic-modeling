# Italian machine tools record positive trends

[TARİH: 01.11.2014 ISMR]

FOR THE THIRD QUARTER of 2014, the Italian machine tool order index, published by Centro Studi & Cultura di Impresa (Centre for Enterprise Studies & Culture) of the Italian manufacturing association, UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, recorded a 7.8% increase compared to the same period of the previous year. The absolute index value reached 86.7, still significantly lower than the average set at 2010 = 100. The overall result was down to the positive trends recorded by manufacturers, both in Italy and further afield.

“In particular, the index for foreign orders increased by 5.3% when compared with the July-September period of 2013, for an absolute value equal to 102.8. The index for domestic orders also showed an increase of 19.1% compared to the same period in the previous year, clearly sustained by the implementation of the New Sabatini Law. The absolute index value is nevertheless still very low, only reaching 33.7,” clarified the association.

Luigi Galdabini, president of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, stated: “The increase recorded by this latest assessment is certainly a positive indicator for Italian machine tool manufacturers, also because it is the fourth consecutive month of growth as far as orders are concerned. If 2014 becomes the year of this trend inversion, one must still remember that the recovery remains weak and uncertain.

“Against a positive trend in activity abroad,” he continued, "activity in the Italian domestic market, although better than in the past, remains rather unexciting. On the other hand, the New Sabatini Law, which between April and September has financed purchases of capital goods of over 2 billion euros, is proving to be a useful tool, necessary for an increase in demand from Italian end-users.” ■

FIGURES RELEASED on 30 October 2014 reveal that more than £81 billion worth of renewable energy projects have been proposed in the UK by 2025, representing nearly half of all infrastructure spending in the UK.

Barbour ABI, a provider of construction data to the UK Office for National Statistics (ONS) and the UK government, has released new data revealing a total of 405 renewable energy projects in the pipeline. Worth a combined total of £81 billion, these projects now account for approximately 47% of proposed UK infrastructure projects.

All but 17 of these schemes are new-build, indicating the significance of the renewable energy sector within construction, with proposed projects including the £25 billion Severn Barrage, phases one to six of the East Anglia One offshore wind farm worth £10.8 billion collectively and the £3.75 billion Hornsea offshore wind farm.

The data also highlights the importance of wind power in the renewable energy mix, with wind farms accounting for nine of the top ten most valuable projects. It follows a report from the Adam Smith Institute and Scientific Alliance published on 27 October 2014 which argues that wind farms are “unreliable” and “intermittent.”

“The scale of renewable energy projects proposed in the UK is staggering and investment in this sector shows no signs of slowing. According to our latest Economic & Construction Market Review, utilities were the major infrastructure project type in September and this is no surprise given the number and value of contracts being awarded which is due, among other things, to the levels of subsidy currently provided by the UK government,” commented Michael Dall, lead economist at Barbour ABI.

“With a number of these projects already confirmed and many more at early stages of planning, the future of renewable energy construction in the UK looks bright.” ■
