# STRONGER, LIGHTER, GREENER

[TARİH: 01.12.2010 ISMR]

ISMR highlights new material advances and developments over the past year

It has not been easy for the materials producers over the last couple of years. The global economic downturn had a severe impact on many markets around the world, which affected material consumption and consequent production in various regions and market sectors.

“In contrast to the ongoing positive developments in the large economies in Asia, parts of Europe and Brazil, the situation in the USA remains critical. A similar ambivalence has to be stated regarding the various industry sectors: while areas such as automotive engineering, machine building, consumer goods, renewable energy and the exploration of oil and gas are enjoying a constant recovery, the outlook for the construction and building sector is less favourable due to the effects of cuts in public spending in many countries,” voestalpine commented.

## NEW MATERIAL INNOVATIONS

However, this year alone, there have been a host of new material developments and innovations. During the current financial year, for example, around 100 million euros were invested by voestalpine into R&D. An example of these efforts is its innovative steel aluminium blank, jointly developed with Fronius GmbH. The blank was awarded the Innovation Prize by specialist magazine Autorevue in October.

Although the welding of steel and aluminium has been considered virtually impossible for decades due to their different material properties, a research team at voestalpine’s Linz site achieved the breakthrough. The key to this combination, which is revolutionary for the automotive sector, is an innovative form of joint edge which enables the combination of excellent results in pull, pressure and deep drawing tests and provides excellent anti-corrosion properties.

From a technical point of view, the steel and aluminium combination is ready for series production. Actual implementation options are currently being discussed with automotive manufacturers. The advantage of the metallurgic combination using a CMT welding and soldering process is that the hybrid blanks can be reformed into construction parts after the soldering process – in the same way as with conventionally laser-welded steel blanks. The front carriage, cabin and roof are possible areas where the blanks could be used in vehicle production.

In October this year, Outokumpu launched a new duplex stainless steel grade featuring a higher mechanical strength than major duplex grades on the market. The new grade, Outokumpu LDX 2404, enables thinner gauges to be employed in a variety of applications, yielding considerable material savings. The excellent resistance to corrosion brings high durability and long service life.

Above: Press-hardening series production (voestalpine Polynorm Grau)

The new grade is suitable for all uses of stainless steel where its high mechanical properties and good corrosion resistance can be utilised - in storage tanks, road and rail tankers, building and construction as well as in several industrial processes. Weight reduction and lighter designs translate to cost-efficient projects with less material, and savings in transport and maintenance costs as well as lower energy consumption.

Increasing sales of special grades and products is in the core of Outokumpu’s strategy. Special grades offer high growth potential and provide significant value for customers. Outokumpu is the market leader in duplex grades with a global market share of some 50 per cent.

Outokumpu also supports higher education and research by donating one million euros to Aalto University. Outokumpu’s cooperation with higher education seeks to safeguard top-level research and education in areas that are important to the company, to create a sufficient competence base, and to recruit new experts. Traditionally, Outokumpu has engaged in cooperation in fields such as economic sciences, metallurgy, and material, automation and process technologies. Cooperation with Aalto University offers Outokumpu the chance to harness top-level expertise and a multidisciplinary approach. Aalto’s core research fields - materials research and design - will round out Outokumpu’s inhouse R&D, offering new opportunities for innovation and exchanging expertise.

## Industry and commerce need to engage in close cooperation with universities and invest in enhancing top-level expertise

“Industry and commerce need to engage in close cooperation with universities and invest in enhancing top-level expertise. In order to generate fresh business concepts, our globally networked corporate world requires new kinds of leadership models, communal thinking and knowledge sharing. Aalto University’s multidisciplinary approach is appealing when considering stainless steel’s potential as a building block of a sustainable future,” said Outokumpu CEO, Juha Rantanen.

Following the announcement in October by the EPSRC (Engineering and Physical Sciences Research Council) and the TSB (Technology Strategy Board) of a £9.5m award to an academic and industrial consortium led by Swansea University, Tata Steel has stressed the significance of the announcement for the development of hi-tech sustainable coated products.

The project, which with partner contributions has funding totalling £20m, is called SPECIFIC (the Sustainable Product Engineering Centre for Innovative Functional Industrial Coatings) and Tata Steel Europe is the lead industrial partner in this initiative. The consortium also includes a number of other internationally recognised academic and industrial partners. Located in South Wales, SPECIFIC aims to develop a portfolio of products that will turn buildings into power stations and generate over one third of the UK’s requirement for renewable energy by 2020.

SPECIFIC will be led by Kevin Bygate, who has for many years been at the leading edge of new technology development within the pre-coated steel and coatings areas. He comments:“I am convinced that we can deliver coated products that will revolutionise the way the roofs and walls of buildings are used. SPECIFIC represents a ground-breaking initiative for the industries involved and an opportunity to create significant economic value.”

## AN EYE ON AUTOMOTIVE MATERIAL

Earlier this year, Corus launched an innovative new grade of steel, Dual Phase (DP) 800HyPerform – an advanced highstrength steel designed specifically with the needs of the automotive industry in mind.

DP800HyPerform combines the strength levels of a DP800 grade with the formability properties of a DP600 grade, without compromising on weldability. This grade was developed in close cooperation with a number of automotive OEMs to meet their needs.

DP800HyPerform has been specifically developed for use in making lightweight automotive parts such as structural members and reinforcements that must be highly crash-resistant. The steel’s minimum ultimate tensile strength is 780 MPa, similar to that of Corus’s existing DP800 grade. The difference is that DP800HyPerform offers extra ductility, resulting in good formability in combination with good weldability. This makes it particularly well suited for complex shaped stamped parts. It is available as hot dipped galvanised material and fulfils all typical automotive processing requirements.

The improved formability of this highstrength steel results in higher processing yield and enables part consolidation, allowing for smarter and lighter design of crash and structural members.

In addition, DP800HyPerform has a hot dipped galvanised zinc coating, which incurs a lower cost than electrolytically galvanised zinc coating that is required for TRIP steels, for example.

Maurice van Giezen, Manager Corus Automotive Business Development, commented:“We are often faced with the apparent paradox of vehicle manufacturers wanting materials that have better crash resistance and offer improved press shop performance, but that at the same time can be supplied at lower cost. By combining the benefits of a number of different grades of steel into one product, DP800HyPerform achieves all three of these objectives.”

During the development of DP800HyPerform, Corus developed a new metallurgical process to enable the combination of favourable properties.

POSCO, together with SMEs, is leading and participating in the staterun project, ‘Development of 10 World Premium Materials’, a future industry fostering project.

On 2 August this year, the South Korean Ministry of Knowledge Economy announced the ten members of the consortium for the ‘World Premier Material (WPM)’ project, including POSCO which formed a consortium with SMEs.

The WPM project is a state-run project with funds totaling 1 trillion KRW, with 100 billion KRW for each task to foster the world’s leading materials industry with continuous market dominance and being the first to commercialise and create new materials.
