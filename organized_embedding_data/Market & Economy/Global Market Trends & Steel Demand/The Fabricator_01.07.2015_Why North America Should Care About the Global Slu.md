# Why North America Should Care About the Global Slump

[TARİH: 01.07.2015 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl FMA Economic Analyst

The Greeks are not bearing gifts, but one should beware anyway. The Chinese are in a slump, the likes of which they have not seen in over a decade. The Brazilians are facing a financial meltdown, and India is fighting to come out from under decades of oppressive bureaucracy. We should all care — right? It is a matter of life and death as far as the North American economy is concerned — right? The fact is that thousands of companies can ignore much of what happens in the world and suffer no ill effects. Goodness knows the consumer has little knowledge or interest. Why then should we care?

The first reason is pretty prosaic, but important nonetheless. The U.S. is a country that exports a great deal of what it produces. As good as we are at consumption, we can’t do it all by ourselves and we have to sell to other nations. That’s pretty simple, and we generally do a good job of it as long as the playing field is reasonably even. That is why there is always concern about currency values and the status of trade agreements. The connections, however, go beyond trade.

The second reason we should care is that the U.S. is an industrial and economic melting pot, which has allowed it to maintain a solid position when it comes to innovation, technology, and productivity. We attract the best and the brightest from all over the globe as their companies want to be engaged with the U.S. market. All companies of all sizes want this too. They invest and engage and we learn as much from them as they learn from us.

The third reason to care is that we need allies to make the world palatable for ourselves. We have long needed the oil-exporting nations, and even now that the U.S., Canada, and Mexico combine to supply the oil we need, there remains a connection that we need to nurture to some degree. We want countries that we can sell to that play fair, which generally means nations that share our basic outlook on democracy and capitalism; therefore, we want things to work out in Greece and elsewhere. The U.S. is often called upon to interact financially and diplomatically as well as militarily — sometimes more often than we would like. To have any control over these commitments, we have to be involved directly and consistently.
