# GERMAN MACHINE TOOL ORDERS

[TARİH: 01.09.2010 ISMR]

In the second quarter of 2010, order bookings in the German machine tool industry showed an unexpectedly steep rise of 71 per cent. Domestic demand soared by 81%; export orders were up by 66 per cent. During the first half of 2010, overall demand was 58 per cent up on the preceding year’s figure. Domestic customers ordered 51 per cent more than in 2009, while export orders rose by 61 per cent.

“The order engine is running at full throttle again”, commented Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Managing Director of VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association) in Frankfurt am Main. “The steepest slump in the history of the machine tool industry has now been followed by the highest growth rates we’ve seen since the early 1970s.” What he finds particularly gratifying is the high rate of growth in domestic demand during the year’s second quarter. This, he added, shows that production output has found its feet again in wide sections of German industry.

Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Managing Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders Association)

Experience has shown that, in the machine tool manufacturing sector, sales follow order bookings with a time delay of six to nine months, says VDW.

Capacity utilisation has climbed from its nadir in the middle of last year, at 65.9 per cent, to 76.3 per cent in June of this year, while the order backlog, following its low point of 5.6 months in October 2009, recovered to 6.9 months in June 2010. At the end of the year’s first half, the sector was employing approximately 63,800 people: this corresponds to a fall of almost 10 per cent compared to the preceding year. In the last few months, however, there has been discernible stabilisation.

Schäfer highlights factors such as setbacks in the US market, the anticipated slowdown in China’s growth and risks in the financial market. “The VDW’s forecast of a 12 per cent fall in sales remains in force”, he affirmed.
