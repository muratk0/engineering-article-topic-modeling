REGIONAL REPORT

GATEWAY TO THE EAST

For suppliers of sheet metal machines, tools and systems, the Polish growth market provides excellent sales opportunities.

ISMR SAYS: “Poland will enter the world's 20 largest economies by 2014, manufacturing output growth is forecast to rise to an annual average of 4.3 percent and foreign investment is expected to total 10,904 million euros”

For foreign investors, Poland is strategically important within Eastern Europe and is often seen as the gateway to markets in Russia and the Ukraine. As the largest of the Eastern European Union countries, Poland has become an attractive location for industries and supply chains in sectors such as automotive.

Poland has excellent links with its EU neighbours and the USA. As a result, English is the industry's most important foreign language, alongside German. Poland’s major trade partners are, among others, Germany; Italy; Russia; France; Hungary; Ukraine; Spain and China.

Polish intellectual capital is one of the key factors that make Poland an attractive place for FDI (foreign direct investment). Highly-qualified workers and well-educated specialists are easily available in Poland.

## Sheet metal in Poland

Numerous small and medium-sized component manufacturers, such as producers of pressed and punched parts, have started new businesses or re-located their production to Poland. Poland is now one of the largest European locations for the assembly of electrical and household appliances.

For suppliers of sheet metal machines, tools and systems, the Polish growth market provides excellent sales opportunities. The country has firmly established itself as a centre for the steel, car and electrical appliance industries and there is a readiness to invest in modern production facilities.

And whilst, in the past, sheet metal parts for the production of appliances were predominantly imported, they are now increasingly produced and finished in Poland. Achieving higher productivity has therefore become a major factor for competitive advantage in the country. This is noticeable in the growing proportion of new machinery used by Poland's manufacturing industries.

A healthy banking sector, low labour costs and low levels of debt are the main strengths of the Polish economy. Poland’s population also provides a large market for domestic goods that are now in demand, thanks to the success of comprehensive economic reforms in the 1990s that encouraged rapid expansion in the private sector. Multinational companies such as Fiat, General Motors, Whirlpool, Bosch and Electrolux have all located their factories in Poland.

Thanks to its strategic location next to Germany, in the centre of Europe, Poland is an attractive place for manufacturers to near-shore operations and to progressively compete for value-added manufacturing supported by a highly skilled workforce. Poland also attracts manufacturers from overseas markets (including BRICs) as it offers a regional platform for both the Central and Eastern European (CEE) region and the wider European market.

Due to its central location on the European continent, Poland has very favourable road, rail, port and aviation links with all the EU countries and is well placed to trade with the EU neighbours in the East. This makes Poland a strong location for firms that are active in Central and Eastern European markets.

Special economic zones were created in Poland to accelerate the economic development of regions, manage post-industrial property and infrastructure, create new jobs and attract foreign investors to Poland. These offer benefits for investors (exemptions from income tax, property tax, R&D and training grants).

Poland’s automotive sector is largely oriented towards exports, with key players selling into Western Europe (Fiat, Ford, VW and Opel), using Poland as a low-cost base for manufacturing. The presence of many major parts makers and suppliers (e.g. TRW, Faurecia, Delphi, Goodyear, Michelin and Valeo) has resulted in the formation of a highly integrated segment of suppliers. Poland is also an important bus producer and this sector is also driven mainly by export demand.

The aviation industry in Poland is highly concentrated: almost 90 per cent of the total production is concentrated in the south-eastern part of Poland, in the region of the Aviation Valley Cluster (Dolina Lotnicza). The cluster is not only a concentration of production plants but also consists of numerous scientific research centres, as well as educational and training facilities.

## Sheet metal showcase

The 7th sheet metal processing exhibition, STOM-BLECH, will take place in Targi Kielce, Poland, from 26-28 March 2014. The exhibition will be the showcase for a variety of sheet metal processing machines, machine tools and technological equipment, software and computer-aided processes in addition to occupational safety and hygiene systems.

A healthy banking sector, low labour costs and low levels of debt are the main strengths of the Polish economy.

“The exhibition halls will be the showcase for metal sheet processing appliances; including laser and water cutting systems, bending and forming, die shearing and painting,” the organiser, Kielce Trade Fairs, told ISMR.

“STOM-BLECH will be held simultaneously with three metal processing, welding and measurement technology trade fairs (SPAWALNICTWO, EXPO-SURFACE and CONTROL-STOM). The exhibitions will all, for the first time, be complemented by the Process Virtualisation Expo (WIRTOPROCESY), the presentation platform for technologies such as Rapid Prototyping, 3D Printing or the industrial utilisation of virtual reality.”

The ITM Poland (MACH Tool) exhibition, a well known and popular machine tool exhibition in Poland, will be held in Poznan from 3-6 June 2014. Last year, the event attracted nearly 1000 exhibitors from 29 countries across 8 exhibition halls. The four-day trade fair attracted 16,017 visitors from Poland and abroad as well as more than 200 journalists. It was also honoured by the presence of the President of the Republic of Poland, Bronisław Komorowski, who visited Pavilion 5.
