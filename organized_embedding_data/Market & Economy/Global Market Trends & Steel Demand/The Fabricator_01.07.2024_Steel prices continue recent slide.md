# Steel prices continue recent slide

[TARİH: 01.07.2024 The Fabricator]

Steel News

Survey responses suggest that the trend might be seasonal, instead of structural

Michael Cowden

here do sheet prices go from here? What is the state of steel demand? And is the drop in sheet prices we’ve seen just a case of the summer doldrums, or is it something more significant?

Rather than try to answer these questions ourselves, SMU polled its readers for a summer review of where things stand. Below is that collective wisdom, presented with only minor edits for grammar and syntax.

When Do Folks Think Hot-Rolled Coil (HRC) Prices Will Bottom?

A small number believe they already have bottomed (see

Figure 1

):

"Markets are trending upward. Demand increasing is driving pricing."

Some think it’ll be this month:

"Costs are bottoming out. Inventories are moderate, and lower imports expected to arrive."

"Best guess is based on futures. I assumed a mill increase would have been announced already."

"Based on the pace of the decline over the past month or two, we’ll reach my projected bottom price of $700/ton in about three to four weeks."

"I’m not expecting big price surges, but an uptick is in the cards. New orders for imports are dropping off."

"Construction and automotive demand are lower."

Some steel buyers are looking at July:

"We are anticipating a bit longer to drift downward. Not crazy numbers, but just slow and steady until mid-summer. Seven-hundred dollars per ton or slightly below seems realistic."

"I expect the mills to reduce capacity in some way to stop the [downward] trend."

"Mills will shore up the pricing, either with reduced capacity or with pricing increases."

Others are looking all the way to August:

"Summer slowdown has already hit. Automotive is very soft. Election looming. Could be September or later. But August is my guess."

"We will have hit cost support levels or forced mill shutdowns."

"Market uncertainty has customers transacting on a monthly basis now."

And a few suggest it might be September or beyond before HRC prices to bottom:

"The upcoming election is the trigger."

Given that most of our survey respondents think that HRC prices will bottom in June or July, where do they think they will be as lead time stretches into the typically busier fall months? In other words, where will prices be after that consensus mid-summer bottom is in the rearview mirror?

Where Will HRC Prices Be in Two Months?

We got a lot of comments in response to this question (see

Figure 2

). To keep things succinct, I’ve highlighted just one for each of the potential responses:

$900/ton or higher: "More of a hope than a thought."

$850 to $899/ton: "Heading into next contract season. I also expect the mills to reduce capacity in some way to stop the current downward trend."

$800 to $849/ton: "We will be past the summer slowdown by then, and demand will pick back up as imports and inventories fall."

$750 to $799/ton: "Decent demand will keep the market sideways for the next few months, with subtle moves up and down. No doubt there is more upside risk potential than downside risk potential. But without an extended outage from a mill or increased demand, hot roll will continue to struggle."

$700 to $749/ton: "Demand isn’t very strong. But utilization remains high and inventories low. Buyers will come back to the table, preventing a free fall."

$650 to $699/ton: "Economic uncertainty. Market still adjusting to Nucor and Cliffs indices."

$649/ton or lower: "Nucor’s CSP has been effective at keeping prices elevated. But eventually the fundamentals will take over, and they are not looking pretty."

The talk of lower imports and potential capacity idling above addresses the supply side of the equation. But what about demand?

How Is Demand for Your Products?

Most respondents continue to tell us that business is stable. But 30% said that demand is declining, up from 9% in late March. Similarly, only 10% say demand is improving, down from 22% in early/mid-April.

Those who see it improving report:

"Our short-term forecast is strong. Our best YTD."

"We are currently expanding our distribution area."

The Takeaways

The consensus: Prices will bottom this month or next, but a sizeable minority think that a bottom might not come until August or September. And many respondents think it will take an extended mill outage or an idling for prices to find a floor.

Another thing I find notable: 80% of respondents think that HRC prices will be roughly where they are now or higher come August. They don’t expect the market to come roaring back once lead times are into September. But they also don’t expect prices to continue to drop into the typically busier fall months either. To me, at least, that suggests most of them think the downtrend in prices is seasonal rather than structural.

Also, 60% tell us demand is stable, which supports the idea that the drop is temporary. Yet—and I’m not sure whether to read too much into this—the comments were more negative than usual, even among those who said demand was stable.

What’s up with that thinking? Maybe folks reporting declining demand were just more verbose than those reporting improving or stable demand. Perhaps it’s the negativity of election-year ads seeping into steel-market psychology.

More on Nucor

I want to revisit, briefly, a topic we’ve written a lot about. Namely, Nucor’s published weekly spot HRC price, which was introduced in April. For those who aren’t up-to-speed on this, Nucor announced a big price decrease on Monday, May 6 (down $65/ton), and the steelmaker made only minor adjustments for the balance of that month. It was a similar story in April, with the announcement lower than expected, but then only smaller changes until the end of the month.

That trend sparked speculation that Nucor’s price, while updated weekly, might, in practice, function more like a monthly one. Big adjustments in the first part of the month, and then small ones until the first Monday of the next month.

It was a nice theory. But a big decrease in June (down $60/ton) happened on the second Monday. Does that mean significant moves up or down can come on any given Monday? And let’s just say that people are still getting used to the idea that U.S. mills can announce both increases and decreases—after years of it (officially) being only up.
