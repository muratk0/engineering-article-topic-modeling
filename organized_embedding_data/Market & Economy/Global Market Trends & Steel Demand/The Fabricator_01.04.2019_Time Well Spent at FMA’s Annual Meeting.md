An economist can learn quite a bit from gazing at data — those charts and graphs and statistics will tell a pretty compelling story most of the time. But there are limitations to what can be gleaned from this information alone. On occasion, it is good to get away from the computer and actually talk to people about what is on their mind. This opportunity is presented to me each year at the FMA Annual Meeting, and I have just had that chance while visiting folks in Nashville.

Three conversations dominated overall. Two were fairly predictable and the third was a bit of a surprise. The top-of-mind issue was a familiar one — labor shortage. There is still a massive issue of finding the people needed, and despite lots of hand-wringing by various political leaders and educators, very little has been done to address the problem. Too few young people know enough about the industry, and too few educational leaders are focused on anything approaching a solution. The majority of people I talked with have been engaged in keeping people employed long past when they should have retired, and many are hiring people with very limited expertise in the hopes they can be trained relatively quickly.

The second conversation revolved around the trade wars and tariffs, and as one would expect, there were differences of opinion depending on whether one was a metal user or metal producer. The most common lament was that the effort to address unfair competition was missing the mark completely as nations blocked by tariffs and other tactics simply found ways around the barriers and made the competitive situation in the U.S. even worse.

The surprising conversation centered around technology. There is no doubt that robotics and technology have transformed the industry, but there is now some intense debate over how far it will go. The lack of skilled workers has been an incentive to invest in more machinery, but at some point this capital-intensive approach becomes challenging. In slow times a company can adjust the size of the workforce, but what are the options when these machines and robots have already been purchased and are being paid for?

THERE IS NO DOUBT THAT ROBOTICS AND TECHNOLOGY HAVE TRANSFORMED THE INDUSTRY.
