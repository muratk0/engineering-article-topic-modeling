# Grexit or no Grexit? will it Affect your Business?

[TARİH: 01.06.2015 The Fabricator]

For the majority of American business owners and managers, the events in Europe surrounding the potential "Grexit" (exit of Greece from the eurozone) is something distant and irrelevant — worthy of some little jokes about the strange doings in Europe — nothing that is going to matter much to them. Those days may be coming to an end as the Greeks and Europeans are heading for a train wreck that will likely alter the euro and the eurozone permanently. Suddenly it matters to U.S. businesses and, for that matter, businesses in the rest of the world as well.

The European negotiators tried everything to get the far left Greek government to compromise and make good on at least some of its obligations — all to no avail. The Greek attitude became so infuriating that talks broke off, and without Greek capitulation the government of Alexis Tsipras faced default and bankruptcy. The prospects for Greece are bleak, but there are major issues for Europe as well, and this is what concerns the U.S. business community. Two scenarios are likely for Europe, and neither are very good.

The first and most likely scenario is that Greece is booted out — the socalled "Grexit." The country returns to the drachma and instantly has the least valuable currency in Europe. That would be good for Greek exports if they had anything to export, but the reality is the country will be utterly broke, and the migration of Greeks into Europe seeking jobs will be massive — far more than can be handled. The social chaos in the Balkans will reignite and could even become violent.

The second scenario is that a Greek exit will stimulate other nations in trouble to follow the same path. This could mean Spain, Portugal, Italy, and even France will elect to dump the euro, which means the end of the common currency. This is still seen as unlikely, but some political parties in these nations are calling for exactly this step, and those parties are leading in many of the recent political polls.

U.S. companies face a Europe desperate to sell its products and a dollar that gains strength against the euro by the day. The U.S. essentially stands to lose the European market as well. This is a development that could take years to adjust to.
