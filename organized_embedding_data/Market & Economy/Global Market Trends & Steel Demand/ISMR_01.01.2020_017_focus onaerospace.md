# FOCUS ONAEROSPACE

[TARİH: 01.01.2020 ISMR]

As we approach a new decade, new trends, forecasts and drivers in aerospace markets are highlighted here for sheet metal manufacturers.

The aerospace and defence manufacturing market consists of sales of aircraft; aircraft components; weapons; radar and other military equipment by entities (organisations, sole traders or partnerships) that produce aircraft; aircraft components; weapons; artillery tanks; radar and other military equipment for aerospace and defence purposes.

According to a recent ‘Aerospace & Defence Market Global Market Forecast to 2022’ on ResearchandMarkets.com, North America was the largest region in the global aerospace & defence market, accounting for 36% of the market in 2018. Asia Pacific was the second largest region, accounting for 25% of the global aerospace & defence market. Africa was the smallest region, accounting for 4% of the market.

Additive manufacturing is continuously evolving and manufacturers are increasingly using 3D printing technology to produce parts for aircrafts and aircraft engines. The increased use of titanium, along with the growing usage of unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), are also noted in this report.

## Manufacturing aerospace parts

According to Grand View Research, the global aerospace parts manufacturing market size was estimated at US$ 877 billion in 2018. It expects it to expand at a CAGR of 3.9% from 2018 to 2025. Increasing demand for commercial aircraft, owing to the rise in fleet replacement to retire ageing aircraft with new-generation, lightweight and fuel-efficient aircraft, is anticipated to drive the growth.

“Energy cost continues to be the most significant expense in aircraft operations, driving the replacement of in-service aircraft with superior fuel-efficient ones. This is anticipated to create tremendous opportunities for market players. Technological advancements and the increased need for aircraft designed for specific missions are expected to further drive the market,” said the analyst.

“The U.S. emerged as the largest market for aerospace parts manufacturing in 2018. The trend is expected to continue over the forecast period owing to the presence of a large aircraft manufacturing base. The rise in demand for critical aircraft parts, such as wings and fuselage, are sourced from the U.S. on account of airworthiness and technological ability to produce high-quality products,” added the analyst.

Aircraft parts are supplied to multiple entities including aircraft manufacturers, airlines and Maintenance, Repair and Operations (MRO) service providers. The manufacturers are involved in the production processes and produce up to 35% of the components inhouse to retain competitive advantage. They source the remaining parts through thirdparty production.

“The market is moving towards the incorporation of lightweight materials in an attempt to improve the cost-efficiency and performance of aircraft. It is witnessing an increasing use of aluminium alloys, such as Titanium Aluminide (TiAl) and Aluminum Lithium (AlLi), for manufacturing aircraft engines, owing to the ability of these materials to retain strength and resist corrosion at high temperatures," explained Grand View Research.

The U.S. government imposed heavy tariffs of 25% on aircraft parts manufactured in China, which was expected to boost production in the U.S. In addition, the government also placed anti-dumping duties on steel and aluminium imported from China.

"Aircraft manufacturing is expected to dominate the market over the forecast period. However, the production of insulation components represents the strongest growth and this trend is expected to continue in the foreseeable future. Additive manufacturing is also becoming more attractive, owing to its benefits such as low cost and reduced errors," added the analyst.

The engine is one of the most significant parts of an aircraft. The use of innovative technologies, such as additive manufacturing, to produce aero engines is expected to minimise manufacturing errors and to simplify the assembly.

"Aircraft manufacturing leads the market and is estimated to reach US$ 579.04 billion by 2025. The segment comprises production and assembly of a complete aircraft, including aerostructure, development of aircraft prototypes; significant modifications and complete aircraft overhaul and rebuilding," said Grand View Research.

## Markets for aerospace part manufacturing

Commercial aircraft (passenger, freight and cargo) led the aerospace parts manufacturing market. The global demand for commercial passenger and cargo fleet is expected to grow in line with the annual number of passengers, which is expected to double over the next two decades.

"Commercial aircraft held the largest share of the aerospace parts manufacturing market and accounted for US$ 450.2 billion in 2018. Significant changes are being incorporated in the structure of commercial aircraft to reduce carbon emissions, which is expected to boost the manufacturing of lightweight parts over the forecast period," explained the analyst.

"Growing demand for business aircraft can be attributed to factors such as increased mobility and improved efficiency. Business aircraft boost productivity, as air travel requires less time compared to other modes of travel. This is expected to drive the segment over the forecast period."

Drones and other unmanned aerial vehicles are also expected to play an essential role in the aerospace industry, which is expected to drive the demand for aerospace parts. Initiatives for outer space exploration by various governments are also expected to further drive demand over the forecast period.

“The market exhibits increased competition, owing to the rising demand from the aircraft manufacturing industry. We have observed a trend of collaboration among major companies to establish joint ventures for product development, to dilute or eliminate the risks and increased costs associated with the development of new aerospace products," added the analyst.

"Because of significant product certification requirements to meet safety regulations, the market is also a highly competitive landscape that restricts the entry of new players. Even though North America and Europe have more established industry players, emerging players in Asia, offering low-cost innovative solutions to end-users, are expected to disrupt competition in the market."

According to the analyst, North America held the largest market share of more than 51.3% in 2018, owing to the presence of key players. Europe ranks second, followed by Asia Pacific (one of the fastest growing regions) due to the huge growth in passenger traffic.

North America is expected to account for over one-third of the global operational fleet, resulting in an increased demand for airframe and other aircraft parts over the forecast period. Ascending demand for new-generation aircraft is expected to boost growth of aircraft production industry in the region over the forecast period.

“Europe has experienced improved performance against the backdrop of economic slowdown and uncertainties in recent years, owing to an increase in investments in the sector. Europe is accustomed to using lighter and stronger aircraft, which is expected to drive demand for parts made up of lightweight, high-strength and cost-efficient materials,” added the analyst.

Asia Pacific is estimated to be one of the fastest growing markets, because of growing MRO activities in the region. China is estimated to commence operations of major production facilities on account of the easy availability of raw materials and labour in the country.

## Increased use of titanium

According to analyst Lucintel, the future of titanium in the global aerospace market looks promising with opportunities in the commercial aircraft, military aircraft, helicopter, general aviation and regional aircraft. It expects the use of titanium in the global aerospace industry to reach an estimated US$ 5.5 billion by 2031, with a CAGR of 3.5% from 2019 to 2031.

“The major growth drivers for this market are increasing deliveries of aircraft and the demand for lightweight titanium materials. Emerging trends, which have a direct impact on the dynamics for titanium in the global aerospace industry, include developing technology to reduce production cost and increasing applications for titanium in aircraft,” it told ISMR.

Lucintel added that the aircraft structure will remain the largest application over the forecast period. Major parts fabricated from titanium include seat tracks; fuselage chords; door frames; wing spars and crown bulkhead chords. The increased focus on weight reduction and fuel efficiency by aircraft manufacturers has augmented the use of titanium alloys in aircraft structure.

“For titanium use in the global aerospace industry, commercial aircraft is expected to remain the largest segment and witness the highest growth by aircraft type due to an increase in aircraft delivery and increasing penetration of titanium alloys in commercial aircraft. North America is expected to remain the largest region and witness the highest growth over the forecast period due to a significant increase in aircraft deliveries and increasing demand for lightweight materials,” it concluded.

It (Lucintel) expects the use of titanium in the global aerospace industry to reach an estimated US$ 5.5 billion by 2031, with a CAGR of 3.5% from 2019 to 2031.

Trends, which have a direct impact on the dynamics of the aerospace fastener industry, include the increase in penetration of titanium fasteners, due to their compatibility with composite parts, and the increased usage of titanium bolts in areas where high strength is required.

## Aerospace adhesives and coatings

Current and evolving growth prospects in the global aerospace adhesives and coatings market look promising over the next six years. Frost & Sullivan’s analysis forecasts the market to reach US$ 1.9 billion by the end of 2024, driven by an increasing need for lighter materials, investment in technically advanced products, evolving regulations and high-volume demand for new and refurbished aircraft.

“While manufacturers are increasingly investing in product development to enhance environmental sustainability and product efficiency, customers are seeking products that are easy to apply, highly durable, quick drying, lighter in weight, environmentally sustainable and regulatory compliant,” said Christeena Thomas, Senior Research Analyst, Chemicals and Materials, EIA at Frost & Sullivan.

Merger and acquisition activities are expected to continue, with global companies acquiring small manufacturers to access their products, technical competences, distribution channels and customer bases. To gain a competitive advantage, Thomas recommends that manufacturers invest in developing adhesive formulations that are superior in properties, such as durability and UV resistance, and compatible with multiple substrates while exhibiting reduced overall weight after application.

Key trends creating growth opportunities in the market include:

- A boost in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) demand in commercial, defence and general aviation segments, due to decreased technology costs.
- High demand for adhesives and coatings in the defence and aerospace segments in the Asia-Pacific and Rest of World countries, with increased investments in domestic manufacturing and operations.
- Customer demands for new, high performance adhesives and coatings.
- Growth in defence spending in the aerospace sector due to changing global economic conditions.
- Growing demand for adhesive and coatings formulations that are compatible with robotic applications.
- Replacement of traditionally used heavy fasteners with lighter-weight adhesives.

“Programmes such as Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals (REACH) in the European Union, U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), and Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) are expected to increase the research and development and transaction costs that businesses incur when developing and distributing new types of chemical compounds,” noted Thomas. “In addition, extensive testing, verification and approval cycles delay product launches, resulting in significant time and cost for material suppliers.”
