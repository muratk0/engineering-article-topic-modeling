# THE SMART FACTORY

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

TRUMPF announces its business results and unveils its AXOOM operating system – a key solution on the way to the Smart Factory

In the 2014/15 fiscal year, which ended on 30 June 2015, laser and machine tool manufacturer TRUMPF achieved a further sharp increase in profits. According to the company, income before taxes rose by 43.8 per cent to €357 million, while its net operating margin was 13.1 per cent. This growth is due in part to a one-off effect from the sale of the company's medical technology division, effective 1 August 2014, which positively impacted the result by an additional €72 million. Adjusted for this effect, the net operating margin improved from 9.6 to 10.5 per cent.

TRUMPF also boosted its sales significantly. Despite the discontinuation of the medical technology division, consolidated sales increased in relation to the previous year by 5 per cent to €2.72 billion – so TRUMPF managed to overcompensate for the €184 million contributed by the medical technology companies during the 2013/14 fiscal year. In an annual comparison adjusted for the medical technology factor, revenues increased by 12.6 per cent.

There was a further shift in regional sales distribution. Germany still remained its largest single market, but with a slightly declining trend. Behind it, the United States and China switched places and China became TRUMPF's largest overseas market for the first time. TRUMPF also registered good gains in Western Europe, especially in France and Italy.

A strategy for growth "In the final analysis, our strategy of growth through innovation, regional diversification and targeted acquisitions in our core business has been a successful one," said TRUMPF President, Dr. Nicola Leibinger-Kammüller. The overall number of employees – 10,873 as of 30 June 2015 – remained at the same level as the previous year. The research and development ratio, in relation to sales, stood at 9.8 per cent.

For the current fiscal year, Leibinger-Kammüller was cautiously optimistic, saying that various geopolitical uncertainties and also the slowdown of growth in China meant that forecasts could only be vague. Overall, she hoped that the level of the previous fiscal year could be maintained, adding that single-digit growth was possible group-wide.

For the first time, TRUMPF also unveiled its far-reaching plans for Industry 4.0. "For us, it is clear that we won't be leaving the Smart factory to third parties but will be taking production connectivity into our own hands," said Leibinger-Kammüller.

The focus here is on a new company, AXOOM GmbH, which was founded by TRUMPF in Karlsruhe and currently has 22 employees. It is developing an operating system with pre-installed apps for the world of production. The open, vendor-neutral platform enables data transportation as well as data storage and analysis. It also offers solution modules for seamless order processing within a production operation.

"With AXOOM we're establishing an entirely new business model. We're offering all customers with industrial production processes the chance to organize those processes into one single system all by themselves," said Leibinger-Kammüller.

So for the first time TRUMPF will be the provider of an operating system. "Our key advantage is that we have the machine base already in place. We're a software provider who really knows the market."
