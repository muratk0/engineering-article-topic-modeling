# Virus outbreaks and the global economy

[TARİH: 01.03.2020 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

China has had to come to grips with infections like coronavirus in the past — most notably the SARS epidemic a few years ago. It’s been a very bad couple of years for China where disease outbreaks are concerned. Last year the country was forced to destroy almost half of the hog population in order to halt the spread of swine flu, and there continues to be an issue of bird flu in the poultry population.

The plain fact is that China is too crowded, and now that travel is so much easier, overcrowding has become a nationwide threat. In years past these outbreaks were contained to some degree by the isolation of rural communities, but today there are more people in the air in China on a given day than in all of North America, and there are more people on trains than in the entire world.

The global economy has been affected already, and this is just the start. The initial response has been to radically curtail travel to China, and that will affect export and import activity right away. The level of investment in China had been dwindling in the last few years because of the trade war, and this just adds to the concerns. Beyond this, millions of dollars will be spent on dealing with the potential outbreak in other parts of the world. Travel, in general, will be affected as people will not want to risk exposure.

In the greater context, this is yet another reminder of how swiftly disease travels in the modern world. The outbreaks are common now, and there are still few mechanisms to handle them. Chinese officials have started to board planes as they land to assess the health of passengers, and those who are running any sort of fever or are judged to be ill are immediately quarantined. Nations, including the U.S., are looking at screening incoming passengers similarly, and suddenly flying becomes even more of an ordeal than it already was.

THE CORONAVIRUS OUTBREAK IS ANOTHER REMINDER OF HOW SWIFTLY DISEASE TRAVELS IN THE MODERN WORLD.

These are early days as far as this virus is concerned, but the impact is already far more serious than first assumed. It is spreading faster and killing more people. It is not at all clear that it has been contained, and given the length of time it takes for the disease to manifest, it may have already affected thousands of people. If this reaches the stage of a pandemic, there will be severe limitations as far as the global economy is concerned. The gloomiest of assessments have growth in China dipping by as much as half a percentage point due to the outbreak.
