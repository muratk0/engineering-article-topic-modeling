# EV-related investments and capacity challenges

[TARİH: 01.08.2022 ISMR]

Based on updated Electric Vehicle (EV) introduction plans announced recently by automakers, the complete transition to EV production for many OEMs could come as soon as 2030. According to global technology intelligence firm ABI Research, auto OEMs will invest an estimated US$ 515 billion in EVrelated technologies and manufacturing plant upgrades over the next five to ten years.

However, in its new white paper, "The Electrification Wave and its Impact on the Automotive Supply Chain", ABI Research argues that automakers have not adequately recognized other impacts that the EV transition will have on the supply chain and, consequently, the impact on the ability to achieve their new vehicular model rollout plans.

Specifically, automakers, from GM and Ford to VW and Mercedes, will need to maintain the simultaneous development of new/updated Internal Combustion Engine (ICE) models and the introduction of differentiated EV models (models that are not powertrain variants of existing ICE models). Incumbent automakers also need to contend with an influx of new OEM entrants beyond Tesla, including Lucid Motors, Rivian and Fisker. These market pressures will require an elevated level of new model "programmes" that will address operational capacity constraints, which have been largely overlooked," explained Ryan Martin, Industrial and Manufacturing Research Director, ABI Research.

A modern automobile is assembled from tens of thousands of parts, most of which are sourced from a global supplier network and many of which are custom components designed for a specific OEM vehicle model or platform programme.

"From our ongoing research, automotive programme management has not received the same managerial attention or level of investment as the supply chain that it supports," added Jake Saunders, Vice President, ABI Research.

Suppliers' resources to manage new model launches are finite and are already stretched thinly.

"Unless automakers and suppliers take the initiatives needed to expand overall programme launch capacity, their electrification goals are under threat," Martin concluded.

## CWIEME Berlin 2022

Hyve, organiser of the global CWIEME events (the world's largest event series dedicated to coil winding, transformers, electric motors, generators and e-mobility), successfully restarted CWIEME Berlin 2022. Held from 10-12 May 2022, it enabled technical engineers and procurement professionals to connect with manufacturers and shape the future of coil winding, electric motors and transformer manufacturing.

Thousands of international attendees enjoyed three days full of business talks, industry trends, presentations and networking opportunities. New trade show elements, such as the Meet the Engineer programme, were successfully launched. Highlights included a three-day conference and the presentation ceremony of the CWIEME Global Awards, which were given out in six categories. CWIEME Berlin also celebrated its 25th anniversary this year.

The trade show will take place next year from 23-25 May 2023.
