# Fourth quarter looking strong for steel

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 The Fabricator]

METALS NEWS

By

MetalPrices.com

staff

U.S. steelmakers expect the current solid demand for steel and firm prices to carry over into the next quarter. In mid-third quarter, the industry’s overall operating rate climbed to 80 percent, a level not reached in several years. This may be best reflected in the continued strength and consistency in sheet steel prices. Hot-rolled coil prices have averaged $675 per net ton free on board (FOB) the steel mill, and cold-rolled sheet was at $790 per ton for much of this year.

Cold-rolled coil prices had been as high as $800 per ton, but inched down $10 in mid-August. It is unclear whether this drop reflected weaker demand from some key users, such as automakers, or whether domestic steelmakers were cutting prices to meet competition from cheaper imported steel.

Steel imports have risen this year, but the threat of trade suits by domestic steelmakers could discourage steel users and importers from bringing in more. A ruling in favor of the U.S. mills could include retroactive duties, and that would erode what they gained from the lower-priced imports. U.S. mills won a battle earlier this year involving oil country tubular goods (OCTG). The modest softening in cold-rolled sheet prices was overshadowed by price increases posted for steel plate, reinforcing bar, and wire rod in August.

Another concern was the report that output of industrial steel scrap like No. 1 busheling had slowed. That could indicate a decline in production by auto- and appliance-makers, although there has been no alteration to their steel demand yet, several traders said. Some flat-rolled mills are booked out through October. Order lead-time has averaged four to six weeks for most sheet products.

The stability in finished steel prices has pervaded in the domestic ferrous scrap market. Most scrap prices were unchanged in both July and August. The last downward price push was in July when mills sought to lower prices by $20 per gross ton, but dealer resistance and delayed deliveries of scrap forced them to pay the same prices as in June. Going into September, some traders said the domestic mills were willing to accept modest increases of $5 to $10 per ton in scrap prices and use those as the basis for raising steel prices.

Many dealers were willing sellers in August, which was fortunate for steel-makers, because several mills were buying more scrap that month. In addition to stronger orders, some mills came off their midsummer maintenance shutdowns and increased steel production to catch up with unfilled orders. Some mill buyers believed it was useless to turn down dealers’ offers with the hope of cutting prices. If they hadn’t accepted current prices, dealers would have found a willing buyer at another mill.

Dealers and steel mills in the inland markets have benefited from stable prices, especially for key industrial steel scrap grades like No. 1 busheling and No. 1 bundles. Summer normally is the time of year when these prices soar because industrial scrap supply declines during vacation shutdowns. However, the strength of auto sales has kept the automakers and many of their suppliers busy. As a result, scrap flowed at a steady pace to its largest consumers: the mini-mill sheet steel producers.

That sideways pricing was reflected in

MetalPrices.

com’s Ferrous Scrap Price Index (Ferrous SPI) national averages during that period. No. 1 busheling has traded in a narrow $1-per-ton range since June, from $405.12 to $405.98 per ton on a delivered-to-the-mill basis. Shredded scrap displayed a wider price spread of $365.53 to $377.93 per ton in that period, and the index’s national averages showed No. 1 heavy melt trading between $352.59 and $355.67 per ton.

With the domestic ferrous market in a steady state, some traders and dealers were looking for a rebound in export sales this quarter to serve as a price stimulus. Prices for heavy melt and shredded scrap sold to Turkey, the bellwether market for U.S. ferrous scrap exports, haven’t risen much. At the end of August, the price for the benchmark 80/20 heavy melt was at $385 per tonne delivered to a Turkish port. At the same time, exporters are not selling obsolete scrap into the domestic market as they had done earlier in the year. Domestic prices are not high enough to persuade them to sell more at home.

Availability of freight transport remains a problem for many scrapyards and steel mills. Railcar availability has improved, but trucks are in short supply and are likely to remain so for a while. There are too few qualified drivers, and that challenge is not likely to be resolved soon.

Stainless Steel Scrap

Stainless steel scrap prices for both 18-8 and 316 fell in August to a three-month low, mainly because of nickel prices on the London Metal Exchange (LME), weaker mill demand, and a sufficient supply of scrap during the month.

Processors told

MetalPrices.com

that they are in no hurry to move material at current prices and are hoping for a fourth-quarter rebound. Another processor indicated that there’s a lot of material out there, and a bounce-back may not happen until first-quarter 2015.

Aluminum

As of the last week of August, aluminum rose to its highest level of 2014. Positive U.S. economic data and signs of strong demand have helped the metal to climb more than 18 percent since Jan. 1. Also contributing to this growth is news that some producers are stopping production at a few smelters because of the high costs of operations. This, in combination with dwindling LME aluminum stocks, has created positive sentiment at this time for aluminum. Reported LME warehouse stocks have dropped more than 11 percent since the beginning of 2014.

Domestic premiums for P1020 have continued to remain high and have surpassed the $20 level during the month of August. Traders feel that the high premium will persist as long as there are continued fears surrounding supply, and as long as freight rates remain high. The P1020 premium in combination with the LME cash contract reflects the cost to obtain primary material for delivery.

Prices for aluminum mill scrap have increased in response to the rise in the LME aluminum cash contract and P1020 premium. Since the beginning of 2014, prices for delivered material to the mills have risen more than 20 percent across most grades. Prices for secondary aluminum scrap grades also have increased this year, but on a much more modest scale at just more than 5 percent to 6 percent on average.

As of the last week of August, aluminum rose to its highest level of 2014.

Prices for obsolete material tend to follow the price for secondary ingot and do not always reflect the same increases as other markets. Recently there has been a slight decrease in secondary ingot prices, but the change has not moved scrap prices south.

Copper

In the first half of August, December COMEX copper prices dropped by a little more than $10/lb., but they have since reversed, now resting close to the monthly high. The recent increase has caused some copper consumers to widen spreads. This is also happening with consumers moving to the December futures contract, which has over a $.02 contango. The recent rise on the contract was attributed mostly to consumers’ confidence regarding progress in the global economy. As China slows down, the U.S. will have to pick up the demand to keep commodity prices at current levels. As of now it seems that copper is very reactive to any news regarding economic conditions.

Nickel

Indonesia plans to lengthen the ban on unprocessed nickel ore exports. Before the ban, Indonesia was the world’s top exporter of nickel ore. Indonesia’s Economic Minister Chairul Tanjung reported that billions of dollars are being spent on smelters and other related nickel projects, which will continue to extend the ban.

LME nickel warehouses continue to set new records, including another which was set in August for inventories reaching more than 325,000 tonnes. This has put to rest a rumor of a potential shortage of nickel in the foreseeable future.

Ferroalloy

Molybdenum demand improved this year, especially from China. Steel applications account for about 75 percent of molybdenum consumption, normally in the form of ferromolybdenum (FeMo).

Demand for FeMo and other ferroalloys, such as ferrovanadium (FeV) and ferrochrome (FeCr), is expected to strengthen for the remainder of the year.
