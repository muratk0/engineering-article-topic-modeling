# A BIRD'S EYE VIEW

[TARİH: 01.01.2022 ISMR]

We highlight trends, drivers and forecasts for global commercial aviation/aerospace markets.

ISMR SAYS:

"Worldwide markets for parts manufacturing, commercial aircraft and aerospace materials come under the spotlight."

The global commercial aviation/aerospace market has been greatly impacted by the COVID-19 pandemic as airports closed, production lines shut down and air passenger traffic suffered. However, as we move into 2022, analysts project signs of recovery and resilience, although much is dependent upon the virus response and the possible impact of any new variants.

Boeing's Commercial Market Outlook (CMO) projects ten-year global demand for 19,000 commercial aeroplanes valued at US$ 3.2 trillion. Boeing's 20-year commercial forecast through to 2040 projects demand for more than 43,500 new aeroplanes valued at US$ 7.2 trillion, an increase of about 500 aircraft over last year's forecast.

"The aerospace industry has made important progress in the recovery, and our 2021 forecast reflects our confidence in the resilience of the market," said Stan Deal, President and CEO, Boeing Commercial Airplanes in September this year. "While we remain realistic about ongoing challenges, the past year has shown that passenger traffic rebounds swiftly when the flying public and governments have confidence in health and safety during air travel. Our industry continues to serve an essential role of bringing people together and transporting critical supplies."

## Boeing CMO 2021-2040

Boeing's Commercial Market Outlook (CMO) 2021-2040 is its longterm forecast of commercial air traffic and aeroplane demand, including global and regional analysis. Annually, it provides valuable analysis to airlines, suppliers and the aviation community.

## Long-term market fundamentals and resilience will drive demand through to 2040 for more than 43,500 new aircraft valued at US$ 7.2 trillion

"The 2021 CMO reflects that the global market is recovering largely as we projected in 2020. Demand for domestic air travel is leading the recovery, with intra-regional markets expected to follow as health and travel restrictions ease, followed by long-haul travel's return to prepandemic levels by 2023 to 2024," explained the aircraft manufacturer. The 2021 CMO forecast highlights include:

While health and regulatory dynamics will continue to shape the near-term outlook, Boeing's analysis of market dynamics shows that commercial aircraft and services are exhibiting signs of recovery and resilience.

The availability and distribution of COVID-19 vaccines will continue to be critical factors in the recovery of passenger air travel. Countries with more widespread vaccination distribution have shown rapid air travel recovery, as governments ease domestic restrictions and open borders to international travel.

Long-term market fundamentals and resilience will drive demand through to 2040 for more than 43,500 new aircraft valued at US$ 7.2 trillion.

The global commercial fleet will surpass 49,000 aeroplanes by 2040. China, Europe, North America and other Asia-Pacific countries each account for about 20% of new aircraft deliveries, with the remaining 20% going to other emerging markets.

The global freighter fleet in 2040 will be 70% larger than the pre-pandemic fleet, due to sustained demand tied to expanding e-commerce and air freight's speed and reliability.

## Global market for parts

The market for aerospace parts manufacturing is primarily concentrated in North America and Europe, owing to the presence of major aircraft manufacturers. China and India are expected to emerge as leading markets in the forthcoming years, owing to the rapid growth of aircraft part manufacturing and export activities. The global trade in components and sub-assemblies has increased by approximately 25% over the past decade.

The sector remains consolidated with the top three countries (including U.S., Germany and France) accounting for over 60% of total aircraft manufacturing. The aerospace parts manufacturing market has stringent manufacturing norms, safety regulations and certifications which restricts the entry of new entrants. However, according to analyst Grand View Research, emerging players in Asia offering lowcost innovative solutions to end-users are expected to challenge the established players in North America and Europe, leading to competition disruption in the market.

There has been a growing trend of collaboration between major aerospace companies entering into joint ventures. This helps them to maintain their edge in a highly competitive market in terms of geographical expansion and technological advancements, as well as lowering the risk of failure. This also helps companies to access the market for long-term contracts.

The global aerospace parts' market is expected to be worth US$ 900.75 billion in 2021 and is expected to grow at a CAGR of 3.7% up to 2030, according to analyst Quince Market Insights. Aerospace parts are usually made from advanced materials such as nickel-based superalloys; titanium alloys; ultra hightemperature ceramics or special sheet.

Increasing demands for new-generation, lightweight and fuel-efficient aircraft across the world, with an emphasis on reducing greenhouse emissions, are driving the global aerospace parts' market. The increasing rates of merger and acquisitions in aerospace parts industries, with new technological advancements, are also creating various new opportunities for the market players.

Based on end use, the market is segmented into business aircraft, commercial aircraft, military aircraft and others. The commercial aircraft segment is dominating the market in 2021 and is expected to remain dominant during the forecast period.

According to the analyst, North America holds the largest market share and is expected to remain the largest during the forecast period. Additionally, increasing demands for new-generation aircraft and increasing demands for fixed-wing aircraft in the U.S. are expected to boost the regional aerospace parts market during the forecast period.

"As the COVID-19 pandemic continues across the world, many global aerospace parts manufacturing industries are suffering production disruption and slow demand due to restrictions on travelling and return of workers to their homes. Due to low production levels, overall revenue also reduced during the pandemic. However, with the ease in government restrictions across the world, business functions are returning to normal and the aerospace parts market is expected to gear up production capacity during the forecast period," added the analyst.

The global trade in components and sub-assemblies has increased by approximately 25% over the past decade.

## Aerospace materials markets

According to new analysis on ResearchAndMarkets.com, the global aerospace materials market is estimated to be US$ 379 billion in 2021 and is projected to reach US$ 579 billion by 2026, at a CAGR of 8.8% between 2021 and 2026.

The demand for next-generation lightweight and fuel-efficient aircraft is expected to be the major driver for the aerospace materials market. The aerospace industry is growing due to increasing air traffic in emerging economies, supported by the rising middle-class population and demand for new-generation fuel-efficient aircraft. This growth is expected to increase the demand for aerospace materials globally.

In terms of value, the composite materials-based aerospace materials segment accounted for the largest share of 72.3% of the overall aerospace materials market in 2020. The aerospace materials market in the commercial aircraft segment is expected to register the highest CAGR between 2021 and 2026.

The commercial aircraft segment is the largest end-user of aerospace materials. Commercial aviation involves operating aircraft to transport passengers or cargo. Strong economic growth in the emerging countries of APAC and South America and an increase in air traffic, as well as the number of low-cost operators in these regions, are also major growth factors for the commercial aircraft segment.

"Low-cost carriers (LCCs) have proved to be strong competitors in the market, particularly in developing economies, and have driven the commercial aircraft segment in the last decade. Many commercial aeroplane deliveries in the single-aisle, wide-body, twin-aisle and regional jet segments are also expected to increase the demand for aerospace materials during the forecast period and drive the commercial aircraft segment," said the report.

The aerospace materials market in the APAC region is projected to register the highest CAGR, in terms of value and volume, between 2021 and 2026.

The demand for next-generation lightweight and fuel-efficient aircraft is expected to be the major driver for the aerospace materials market.

"Demand for aerospace materials in the aerospace and defence industry in the APAC region is growing due to the increased production of indigenous aircraft. The increasing demand for aerospace materials in developing countries such as China, India, Singapore, Indonesia and Thailand in the APAC region will drive market growth," it added.

Increased fuel efficiency leads to a reduction in the operational cost of the aircraft. High economic growth in these countries, along with high urbanisation, industrialisation and improved standards of living, also plays a crucial role in the adoption of fuel-efficient, cost-cutting machinery.

Most of the aircraft manufacturing companies are integrated across the value chain and are highly active in the parts manufacturing process. These companies have in-house production facilities as well as supplier contracts for the procurement of these parts. The manufacturers are also involved in raw material procurement and design and quality control of the parts offered by third party suppliers.

The commercial aircraft segment is estimated to expand at the fastest CAGR of 4.6% over the forecast period, as significant changes are being incorporated in the aircraft structure to reduce carbon emissions by replacing existing parts with lightweight materials.

## Asia-Pacific market

Boeing estimates that air travel within Asia Pacific markets will account for nearly half of global air traffic by 2040, driving 20-year demand for 17,645 new aircraft valued at US$ 3.1 trillion.

"To support its commercial aviation industry, Asia-Pacific countries will also require aftermarket services valued at US$ 3.7 trillion," it added.

The Asia Pacific region has diverse air travel markets, including mature economies in Northeast Asia and Oceania as well as rapidly growing aviation markets in China, South Asia and Southeast Asia. With the travel recovery enabled by rising COVID-19 vaccination, Asia Pacific carriers are well-positioned to capitalise on recovering business and leisure travel as well as air cargo transportation, according to Boeing.

"We have seen strong resilience in Asia Pacific traffic when restrictions are lifted and passengers feel confident about travel," said Darren Hulst, Boeing vice president of Commercial Marketing. "Carriers with efficient and versatile fleets will be positioned to meet passenger needs and air freight demand with aeroplanes that reduce fuel use, emissions and operating costs."

Boeing's CMO analysis addresses 20-year demand for the five regions within Asia Pacific. Southeast Asia is forecast to need 4,465 new aircraft valued at US$ 765 billion and commercial aviation services valued at US$ 790 billion by 2040. Northeast Asia is expected to need 1,385 new aircraft valued at US$ 310 billion as well as services valued at US$ 555 billion in the next 20 years. In Oceania, the projected need is for 785 new jets valued at US$ 135 billion and services valued at US$ 165 billion by the end of the CMO forecast period.

Boeing estimates that air travel within Asia Pacific markets will account for nearly half of global air traffic by 2040.

Boeing's 2021 CMO also includes these projections for Asia Pacific through to 2040:

- Single-aisle jets will account for nearly 13,500 deliveries, about three-quarters of demand in terms of units. Widebody jets, including passenger and cargo models, will total nearly 3,800 aircraft.
- The cargo fleet will more than triple to 1,160 aircraft, including new and converted models, to support diversifying global supply chains and meet e-commerce demand. The Asia Pacific cargo fleet is expected to roughly equal North America's cargo fleet by 2040.
- Tied to economic and fleet growth, demand for maintenance, repair, overhaul and modifications account for a majority of projected commercial aviation services demand. Digital solutions, analytics and training services will also support the Asia Pacific fleet.

Boeing's 2021 Pilot and Technician Outlook (PTO) forecasts that the region will require nearly 820,000 new aviation personnel, including more than 230,000 pilots and nearly 250,000 technicians and 340,000 cabin crew members.
