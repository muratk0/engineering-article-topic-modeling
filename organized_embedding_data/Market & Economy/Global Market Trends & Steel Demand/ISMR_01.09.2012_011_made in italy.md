# MADE IN ITALY

[TARİH: 01.09.2012 ISMR]

We highlight new trends in Italy's machine tool, robotics and automation markets

ISMR SAYS:

“Italian machine tool, robot and automation production reached 4,762 million euros in 2011, a 13.5% increase over 2010. Consumption grew by 3.4% due to the increase in imports”

The structural characteristics of the Italian machine tool, robot and automation manufacturing industry are similar to those of the national manufacturing system: small-sized businesses, a strong predilection for exports and a high quality offering.

In 2011, world production of machine tools rose by 65,200 million euros (+27.7%). Consumption grew by 27.5%, reaching approximately 59,000 million. In 2011, Italy confirmed its position as the fourth world power for production (4,250 million) and third for exports (3,070 million). In terms of consumption, the Italian market – where demand fell under 2,000 million – took sixth place.

Italian machine tool, robot and automation production reached 4,762 million euros in 2011, a 13.5% increase over 2010. Consumption grew by 3.4% to 2,551 million due to the increase in imports (+19.9%, 1,049 million): foreign machines held a 41.1% market share. However, deliveries on the domestic market decreased by 5.7% to 1,502 million. Positive performance was registered in terms of exports, which grew by 25.2%, to 3,260 million: the export-production ratio increased from 62.1% in 2010 to 68.5%. The balance of trade grew by 27.9% to 2,211 million.

The sharp recovery of Italian industry in the sector was reflected in the level of production capacity usage, whose annual average grew from 68.7% in 2010 to 79.6%. The order book increased to 5.7 months of assured production, compared to 4.8 months in 2010. Machine prices rose by 0.6%.

## Machine tools and automation

A machine tool survey conducted by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE in 2010 (the most recent data available) indicated that 67.8% of Italian machine tool companies declared a turnover of less than 12.5 million euros, while 71.3% had less than 100 employees. However, it was the more structured companies that made a higher contribution to production and export: companies with more than 100 employees, representing just 28.7% of the units operating on the Italian territory, generated 74.9% of the production and exported 78.5% of the total.

Similarly, companies with turnovers of more than 25 million (14.8% of the overall number) were accountable for 59.2% of the total production and 63.9% of total Italian exports of machine tools. Turnover per employee ranged from 111,900 euros for small companies to 138,500 euros for larger companies, which clearly decided to outsource a higher number of processing stages in order to focus on more strategic activities (design, assembly, sales and after-sales services).

The geographical distribution of the sector is consistent with the overall Italian manufacturing system, confirming that companies building machine tools must be close to their clients and suppliers: most production units in this sector are located in Lombardy (40.9%), the Triveneto area (22.6%), Emilia-Romagna (15.7%) and Piedmont (15.7%).

Composition of the total turnover includes a 40.7% contribution from Lombardy, 26.7% from the Triveneto area and 18.7% from Piedmont. Emilia-Romagna companies are more driven towards export (69%, against the average of the sector of 65%).

The main user of machine tools (33.9% of the total) was the metal manufacturing industry, followed by the automotive industry (21.3%). In the robotics sector, the recovery in 2010 brought about an increase of large production units (turnover exceeding 5 million euros), representing 61% of the total, to the detriment of medium-sized companies (turnover between 2.5 and 5 million) and small businesses (turnover below 2.5 million). The average turnover per employee decreased from 231,500 to 221,800 euros.

The most industrialised regions boast the highest number of companies producing robots, due to the strong bond between manufacturers and users. The region with the highest density of robot manufacturers in Italy is Lombardy, with 50% of the total companies. However, Piedmont recorded the best percentage in terms of turnover (61.6%) and employees (49%).

## A bird’s eye view

The balance of Italian industry manufacture of capital goods is positive; in 2011, it consolidated the recovery started in 2010.

According to data from the FEDERMACCHINE Statistics Group, in 2011 production increased to 37.9 billion euros, 11.8% more than last year. Because of this increase, production value is back to 2006 levels, before the record years of 2007-2008. The main driver of growth was exports, which increased by 17.1% and reached 26.7 billion euros.

The main outlet markets for Italian industry were: Germany (2.4 billion euros +22%), France (2.3 billion +23%), China (2.2 billion euros +6%) and the United States (1.9 billion +42%). Deliveries to Italy’s domestic market increased by 1.1%, to 11.2 billion euros.

What penalised Italian manufacturers was the weakness of domestic demand. Domestic consumption increased by 3.1% and stopped at 17.4 billion euros. Aware of the downsizing of its domestic market, Italian manufacturers have oriented their activities abroad.

“The weakness of the domestic market in our opinion requires a structured programme of actions to re-start consumption,” underlined FEDERMACCHINE.

In 2011, the Italian machine tool, robot and automation industry reacted well to the difficulties arising from global economic downward trends, reaching the world’s top ranking where it occupies fourth position among producers and third position among exporters.

The sharp increase in production that grew by 13.5% up to 4,762 million euros was due to the strong performance of Italian exports that reached a value of 3,260 million euros, 25.2% more than the previous year. Nevertheless, the trend was different for manufacturers’ deliveries on the domestic market which decreased by 5.7%, not exceeding 1,502 million euros. The assessment of the domestic consumption trend, despite an increase of 3.4%, still remains quite weak at 2,551 million euros. This was the financial statement for 2011 presented recently by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian Machine Tools, Robots and Automation Manufacturers’ Association, at its annual meeting.
