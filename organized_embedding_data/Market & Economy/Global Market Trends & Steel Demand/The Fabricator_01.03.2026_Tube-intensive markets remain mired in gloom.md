# Tube-intensive markets remain mired in gloom

[TARİH: 01.03.2026 The Fabricator]

Tube & Pipe Market Update

Durable goods output up, but manufacturing continues downturn

By

Rick Preckel

MANUFACTURING PERFORMANCE

Data from the U.S. Federal Reserve indicates that the durable goods manufacturing output index improved in December by 0.1% after declining by 0.1% in November. Pipe- and tube-consuming categories were mixed, with improvements in machinery (+0.2%), electrical equipment and appliances (+1.7%), aerospace and miscellaneous transportation equipment (+1.5%), and furniture and related products (+0.8%). These gains were offset by negative month-to-month comparisons in fabricated metal products (-0.1%), motor vehicles and parts (-1.1%), and miscellaneous manufacturing (-0.1%).

Durable goods manufacturing in December was 3.1% above year-ago levels. Durable goods manufacturing capacity utilization held steady in December at 73.6%.

The December Manufacturing PMI Report, as published by the Institute for Supply Management (ISM), indicated that economic activity in the manufacturing sector, as measured by the Manufacturing PMI, remained in contraction territory at 47.9%, the lowest reading in 2025. (Growth in the sector is indicated by a reading above 50.)

December was the 10th consecutive month of contraction.

Looking at the index components, the New Orders Index contracted for the fourth straight month in December to 47.7%, a 0.3-percentage-point improvement over the November reading of 47.4%. The New Export Orders Index reading of 46.8% was 0.6 percentage point higher than the 46.2% registered in November. The Production Index for December remained in expansion territory at 51.0% but was 0.4% lower than the November reading.

The Pricing Index in December continued to indicate rising prices, holding steady in November at 58.5%. The Backlog of Orders Index registered 45.8%, up 1.8 percentage points from the November reading of 44.0%.

"Although the demand indicators are still in contraction, improvement in three indexes (New Orders, Backlog of Orders, and New Export Orders) and the Customers’ Inventories Index remaining in ‘too low’ territory (and at an accelerated rate) are positive signs for December," said Susan Spence, MBA, chairperson of the ISM Manufacturing Business Survey Committee. "But several consecutive months of gains in these indicators are necessary for a longer-term recovery. A ‘too low’ status for the Customers’ Inventories Index is usually considered positive for future production."

The only tubing-intensive manufacturing industry reporting growth in December, according to the survey, was electrical equipment—appliances and components. The tube-intensive industries reporting contraction in December were miscellaneous manufacturing, fabricated metal products, machinery, and transportation equipment. For more information, visit

www.ismrob.org

.

Please note that Federal Reserve data and ISM data are based on different metrics and may appear contradictory.

DOMESTIC PIPE, TUBE, AND STEEL MILLS

U. S. Steel

said it will restart one of the blast furnaces in the first half of 2026 at its Granite City works that was last run in 2023. The decision comes less than six months after

Nippon Steel

sealed a deal with the Trump administration to buy the American steelmaker for $14.9 billion. The deal included a provision that gives the federal government a say, for a period of time, in certain company decisions involving domestic steel production, including closing or idling U. S. Steel’s plants.

Nextpower

, formerly Nextracker, will double its steel solar tracker manufacturing capacity by adding a new torque tube fabrication line at its Memphis, Tenn., facility operated by

MSS Steel Tubes USA

.

Lock Joint Tube

, a manufacturer of mechanical and structural grade steel tubing in South Bend, Ind., has acquired the

Tectron

mechanical tube product line and manufacturing facility in De Pere, Wis., from

Atkore Inc.

With the acquisition, Lock Joint now operates six locations across Indiana, Ohio, Tennessee, Texas, and Wisconsin that serve fabricators, distributors, and OEMs nationwide.

SHIPPING AND IMPORTS

Preliminary pipe and tube shipments for December for the sectors of the market that we cover decreased from November by 0.7% and were 6.0% lower than a year ago. Because of a data lag remaining from the federal government shutdown, import volumes for November and December are based on licenses rather than normal Census Bureau figures. Manufacturing and construction product shipments in December increased from November by 5.5% but were 15.9% lower than a year ago.

December preliminary shipments of mechanical tubing products, those most closely associated with manufacturing activity, increased by 9.3% from November but were 22.0% lower than December of last year.

OUTLOOK

Mechanical and HSS pipe and tube product prices have been on the rise, with announcements from major producers coming in October, November, December, and January. These coincide with rising hot-rolled band prices. HRB price increases, according to analysts, are mostly due to a supply pinch as opposed to rising demand. Trade remains in flux as the latest geopolitical issue, Greenland, has resulted in both the imposition and reversal of additional tariffs.

The U.S. Federal Reserve Open Market Committee cut short-term borrowing rates by 25 basis points on Dec. 10 but signaled that only one rate cut was expected in 2026. Inflation remains in check but at a slightly elevated level, while U.S. job additions continue to soften.

FLAT-ROLLED STEEL

According to data from the SteelBenchmarker, the index for base domestic HRB prices moved to 1.18 in December from 1.14 in November. According to other sources, prices have continued to increase since the last reading. The index hit a low of 1.06 in September before starting to climb.

Please note, the Mechanical Tube price index is calculated using a blend of first point-of-sale import and domestic prices. October is the latest available because of a lag in government data.
