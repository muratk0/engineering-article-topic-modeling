An Eye On Russia ISMR analyses key sheet metal market opportunities in Russia

ISMR SAYS: “Defence, aerospace, automotive and shipbuilding are some of the key growth sectors for sheet metal in Russia”

Russia is the largest country in the world, spanning nine time zones. The landscape varies widely, from vast open tracts in the European heartlands and the taiga and tundra of Siberia, to mountainous terrain.

The main communications across the country are by air, and the Trans-Siberian railway. The road system is not well developed countrywide although, in August 2011, the Russian government confirmed that the length of Russia’s modern roads will nearly double in the next 10 years and started a road building support programme in certain regions. Russia's great rivers also play an important part in transportation as well as in hydroelectric power generation.

The country possesses a wide array of mineral and energy resources including major deposits of oil; coal; natural gas; iron ore; manganese; chromium; nickel; platinum; titanium; copper; tin; lead; tungsten; diamonds; phosphates and gold.

## Industry and growth

Moscow is Russia's capital and largest city. It is also increasingly important as an economic and business centre; it has become Russia's principal magnet for foreign investment and business presence. The second largest city in Russia is St. Petersburg and other key industrial centres in Russia include Yekaterinburg, Nizhny Novgorod, Novosibirsk, Rostov-on-Don and Vladivostok.

Besides its resource-based industries, it has developed large manufacturing capacities, notably in metals, food products and transport equipment. Russia inherited most of the defence industrial base of the Soviet Union, so armaments remain an important export category for Russia. Efforts have been made with varying success over the past few years to convert defence industries to civilian use, and the Russian Government is engaged in an ongoing process to privatise many of the state-owned enterprises. Special Economic Zones have been designated to help promote trade and industry in various regions and cities in Russia.

Russia’s leaders have put renewed emphasis on promoting innovation as key to economic modernisation as well as on the need to diversify the economy away from oil and gas.

According to Bombardier’s market forecast 2011-2030, the Russian economy remains overly dependent on energy export revenues to drive domestic growth. Energy prices have risen steadily since mid-2009. They are expected to remain elevated in the near-to-medium term. Exports have been driving the recovery, while domestic demand has remained slack. Consumer confidence has bounced back and household consumption has been adding some momentum to the economic recovery. Business confidence is improving as well, as shown by the strong growth in investment activity. Future growth will be influenced by WTO accession and the nation's newly cemented energy exporting ties to China. As part of the accession accord, Russia has agreed to undertake a series of important commitments to further open its trade regime and accelerate its integration in the world economy.

Key competitive advantages, according to The World Economic Forum’s ‘The Russia Competitiveness Report’, include an educated labour force, large market size and natural resources. Key competitiveness challenges include underdeveloped institutions, stifled competition, declining quality of education, underdeveloped financial markets and low business sophistication. Innovation could become a key driver of growth in the future should a comprehensive national framework be put in place.

## Automotive market

The Russian automotive market is back on the road to recovery and will become one of the key global growth engines of the industry in the coming decade, making it possibly one of the top six by 2020.

Although the Russian automotive market was severely affected in 2009, when annual sales fell by 50 per cent, the country continues to be an important growth market for foreign original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and original equipment suppliers (OES) businesses, according to a BSG study. In terms of structure, the Russian market is expected to continue to converge, forming European demand patterns with a focus on B- and C-segment cars and SUVs as the strongest growth segment.

The Russian Automotive Strategy 2020, approved by the Government in April 2011, has provided the framework for further development of the whole automotive industry value chain, thereby accelerating the localisation process and contributing to the improved overall quality and global competitiveness of Russianmade products.

“The most popular passenger car models in Russia up until June 2011 were the ones which are domestically produced by local players in Russia. The three leading domestic automobile players are AvtoVAZ, GAZ and KAMAZ. The first two produce lightweight automobiles while KAMAZ is a heavy vehicle producer,” said Research and Markets.

## Aerospace

Enormous investments are required in Russian infrastructure to modernise existing and create new assets. Transportation and, in particular, airports are key priorities in the Russian infrastructure sector. The strategic need to upgrade airports is emphasised by such large-scale events as the Football World Cup in 2018.

Passenger traffic increased by an average 10 per cent a year over 2000-2010 in Russia while freight traffic doubled over the same period. It is estimated that this trend will continue in coming years.

According to a UKTI report on opportunities within Russian airports, RUR 470 billion (£9.8 billion) of federal budget will be allocated for the modernisation of civil aviation airports and upgrading the unified air traffic management system, including facilities in Moscow, Sochi, Vladikavkaz, Kazan etc. Russia is and has been experiencing growth in air traffic numbers. Many airports need serious reconstruction to cope with demand. Russia’s aerospace industry enjoys strong governmental support and it is estimated that, in 2009-2011, the government allocated over 270 billion rubles (over 10 billion US dollars) for the industry's development.

The growing demand for aircraft is the consequence of the impressive increase of passenger traffic in Russia driven by consistent economic growth and ongoing airline consolidation. According to an Airbus forecast, passenger traffic in Russia is expected to increase at a rate of 5.6 per cent per year on average over the next 20 years, which is much faster than the world average of 4.8 per cent.

## Sheet metal opportunities

Apart from the aerospace and automotive industries, other key sheet metal markets in Russia include construction, shipbuilding, domestic/white goods, defence and energy generation and distribution.
