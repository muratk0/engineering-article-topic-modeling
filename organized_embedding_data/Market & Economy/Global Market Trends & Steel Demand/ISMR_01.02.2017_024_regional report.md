## Transformative trends

According to F&S, current technology trends and developments transforming the market include:

- Mobile broadband could be the main catalyst behind binding rural and urban India, which will widen the marketplace for businesses and help address bigger challenges for the government.

- Mobile app downloads in India will reach 10 billion by 2020. Key mobile-based industries, such as mobile banking and e-governance, would benefit as a result.

- Online retail will emerge as one of the fastest growing industries, with a market potential of US$ 35.08 billion by 2025, accounting for nearly 4% of total retail.

- Smartphone applications are encouraging the use of applications such as mobile payment, m-health, m-governance and m-commerce.

- Unstructured data will grow faster than structured data, by more than 30%, in the next three years. Enterprises must tap these important data sources to analyse customer behaviour for competitive advantages.

- Information Technology (IT), primarily driven by IT services, is becoming one of the major contributors of Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

- Due to strong adoption among small and medium organisations, cloud computing through Infrastructure as a Service (IaaS) will increase.

"Digitisation can only be achieved through proper implementation of strategies backed by the government and technology developers,” noted the analyst. “Revamping and restructuring policies as well as skill-set development are crucial to facilitate adoption of new services.”

## An eye on manufacturing

India is poised to become the third-largest economy in the world by 2030. Its manufacturing sector has evolved through several phases - from the initial industrialisation and license to liberalisation and its current phase of global competitiveness. Today, Indian manufacturing companies in several sectors are targeting global markets and are becoming formidable global competitors. Many are already amongst the most competitive in their sectors.

Strong consumerism in the domestic market is backed by key technical and engineering capabilities from scientific and technical institutes. The cost of manpower is relatively low compared to other countries. Every state in India has developed industrial parks to encourage industrial set-up.

National Investment and Manufacturing Zones (NIMZ) are a combination of production units; public utilities; logistics; residential areas and administrative services. India has also developed Special Economic Zones (SEZs) which are specifically delineated enclaves treated as foreign territory for the purpose of industrial, service and trade operations, with a relaxation in customs duties and a more liberal regime for other levies and foreign investment.

Sector-specific clusters, such as electronic manufacturing clusters and mega food parks etc., have also been encouraged, as have country-specific zones for industrial units e.g. the Neemrana Japanese Zone etc. Industrial corridors are also planned. The objective is to increase the share of manufacturing in the GDP of the country and to create smart sustainable cities where manufacturing will be the key economic driver.

The government of India provides sector-specific subsidies for promoting manufacturing which includes capital subsidies and area-based incentives. Under its Union Budget 2014-15, it has provided investment allowance at a rate of 15 per cent to a manufacturing company that invests more than US$ 4.17 million in any year in new plant and machinery. R&D, export and state incentives are also offered. The government has taken steps to improve the ease of doing business in India.

‘SKILL INDIA’, a multi-skill development programme, has been initiated with a mission for job creation and entrepreneurship for all socio-economic classes. India is also expected to spend US$ 40 billion on defence purchases over the next 4-5 years. The opening of the strategic defence sector for private sector participation will help overseas original equipment manufacturers to enter into strategic partnerships with Indian companies and leverage domestic markets.

## Market sectors

India is also expected to become a major automobile manufacturing hub and the third largest market for automobiles by 2020, according to a report published by Deloitte. Demographically and economically, India’s automotive industry is well positioned for growth, servicing both domestic demand and, increasingly, export opportunities.

A predicted increase in India’s working-age population is likely to help stimulate the burgeoning market for private vehicles. Rising prosperity, easier access to finance and increasing affordability is expected to see four-wheelers gaining volumes, although two wheelers will remain the primary choice for the majority of purchasers, buoyed by greater appetite from rural areas, the youth market and women. Global automotive OEMs have been ramping up investments in India to cater to growing domestic demand. Electric cars are likely to be a sizeable market segment in the coming decade.

The Indian automotive component industry grew by 8.8% in FY 15-16. Mr. Nirmal K Minda, Vice President, ACMA (Automotive Component Manufacturers Association of India) recently said: “The Indian auto component industry is at a point of inflection. Quality and technology will be the key differentiators for industry’s competitiveness. With the ‘ Make in India’ initiative and the thrust on increased localisation by OEMs, the component industry is actively focusing on delivering enhanced quality products with an accent on R&D and innovation.”

The Indian engineering sector has also witnessed a remarkable growth over the last few years driven by increased investments in infrastructure and industrial production. The electronics market is one of the largest in the world and is anticipated to reach US$ 400 billion in 2022 from US$ 69.6 billion in 2012.

The government is also expected to ‘take tangible steps’ to boost corporate investment in roads and shipping with business-friendly strategies that balance profitability with effective project execution. India’s labour force could expand by 160-170 million in 2020, depending upon various factors.
