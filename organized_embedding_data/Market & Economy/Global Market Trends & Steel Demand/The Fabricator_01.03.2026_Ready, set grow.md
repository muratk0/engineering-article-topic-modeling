# Ready, set grow

Executive Perspectives

Learn how to spend your time and energy more wisely in 2026

LISA WERTZBAUGHER

I

f you’re like most manufacturers, the past two years have been about maintaining revenue and limiting profit shrinkage.

At best, most manufacturers were flat (with the exception of those serving niche markets); at worst, some experienced reduced revenue with workforce reductions. Election years (2024) are consistently slow because of uncertainty. No matter who you root for, no one wants to make decisions until after the dust settles.

In 2025, there was a slight surge in Q1, but as the tariff policy rolled out without firm details and deadlines, checkbooks snapped shut again. The consensus was lots of quoting, no orders.

However, the start of 2026 has been better, with cautious optimism reported by most businesses. In the manufacturing company I own with my husband, our January quote log grew by 47% over Q4 2025. We still don’t have a ton of orders on the books, but the conversations are more serious, and the activity is picking up.

As a columnist, I’ve written about planning more than any other topic, and we complete rigorous planning exercises in our business each year. So, you will understand my surprise when my husband, Jason, called me this week for a quick decision on a $50,000 capital purchase. He’d been evaluating some options for about a month but wanted to run things by me for a second opinion.

I realized that we had been so focused on planning for profit preservation during the slow period that we hadn’t revisited our plans for growth. We immediately set a time to meet and revisit our routines, metrics, and capital plans. Here are some of the basics.

MEETING SCHEDULES

Most businesses have routine meetings: departmental, one on one, leadership. When business slows down, these often go by the wayside. There isn’t as much to discuss.

Jason and I reviewed our daily, weekly, monthly, and quarterly meeting cadence and refined the agenda to three to five bullet points that are relevant to our growth plans for 2026. We identified the key metrics we need to review at each meeting to scale as the market picks up. A set agenda helps us keep meetings short but gives us key data for decision-making and preventing problems.

CAPITAL PLANNING

We dusted off the list of wants and needs we’ve been compiling for the past 18 to 24 months. We prioritized purchases (which had changed since the last version) and set a budget to spend based on hitting certain revenue goals. If we meet the sales goal, we pull the trigger. If we don’t, we wait.

HEAD COUNT

Based on expected revenue sources and capital investments, what roles should we add? We discussed tasks we each complete that don’t play to the 20% of our skills that drive exponential business expansion.

We are reading "10x Is Easier Than 2x" by Benjamin Hardy and Dan Sullivan right now, and it has been eye-opening to realize how much energy people waste by not focusing most of their time on their unique skills and capabilities. So, we had to ask ourselves, "What are the next three to five hires that will free up time for Jason to focus on his engineering expertise and for me to focus on business development? At what revenue point does it make sense?"

BUSINESS DEVELOPMENT

The pipeline needs attention.

Focusing on cost control during slow periods takes effort away from business development activities. Some of our largest customers didn’t buy anything last year. Have we contacted everyone that has purchased from us in the past five years to check in?

Sales metrics should be set for phone work, existing customer meetings, prospect meetings, trade show attendance, digital marketing, and website development. Investment in sales and marketing should expand as revenue grows.

We are evaluating how the market has changed since 2023 and identifying key industries we want to serve. We are exiting some industries because of low margins or volatility. Again, "10x Is Easier Than 2x" means focusing on fewer high-potential opportunities and putting all your weight behind them.

NEW TECHNOLOGIES

I use AI every day, and I didn’t use it once before Q2 2025. I probably save five to 10 hours per week using this technology.

In 2025, I need to improve adoption and training for the rest of our workforce. I am working with a company that recently purchased 50 Microsoft Copilot licenses for its workforce. With training and adoption, it hopes to gain two hours of productivity per week per person, which is a low goal. If the company achieves that goal, it will gain 200 hours per week, the equivalent of two people.

IN SUMMARY

To simplify, I recommend checking these boxes in 2026:

Make sure the business development engine is running.

Identify the "who" behind everything that needs to get done this year.

Plan to free up high-value employees for what they do best.

Evaluate new technologies needed to scale, and create a capital budget and timeline for those investments.

Outline a meeting cadence with efficient agendas for keeping a finger on the pulse of your business.
