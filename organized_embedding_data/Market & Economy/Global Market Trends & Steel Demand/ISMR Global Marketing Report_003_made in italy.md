# MADE IN ITALY

[TARİH: 01.01.2015 ISMR]

ISMR analyses manufacturing and sector forecasts for Italy

2012 ended on a positive note for the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation system manufacturing industry, extending a positive trend recorded since the start of 2010. According to preliminary data from UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian association for machine tools, robotics and automation systems), in 2012 Italian production in this sector reached 4,930 million euros, a 3.5 per cent increase compared with the previous year. The result was enabled by a strong performance in exports which grew by 12 per cent, reaching 3,650 million euros. The main Italian machine tool export markets were: China, United States, Germany, Russia, France, Brazil, Turkey, India, Poland and Mexico.

According to the latest results, sales grew in China (+9.5 per cent) to 299 million; in the United States (+42.5 per cent) to 264 million; in Russia (+31.3 per cent) to 131 million; in France (+9.9 per cent) to 128.7 million; in Turkey (+52.2 per cent) to 107 million; in India (+1.2 per cent) to 99.6 million; in Poland (+36.2 per cent) to 94 million and in Mexico (+93.9 per cent) to 81.4 million. The opposite trend was recorded in Germany (-1.4 per cent), which still remains the third largest export market in this sector for Italy, with sales reaching 259 million euros.

A different trend was apparent for Italy’s domestic market. Consumption decreased by 13 per cent, reaching 2,220 million euros. This weakness of demand has had a strong impact on deliveries to manufacturers, which decreased by 14.8 per cent, down to 1,280 million euros, and on imports which decreased by 10.4 per cent, and only reached a total value of 940 million euros. UCIMU’s 2013 forecasts indicated a slow-down in the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation system manufacturing industry.

“Compared to the positive trends shown in overseas markets which, as shown by the export forecasts, will grow by 2.1 per cent, to 3,725 million euros, the domestic market will shrink significantly. Consumption will decrease by 1.6 per cent to 2,185 million euros. The structural reduction of the domestic market will have an impact on both the deliveries of manufacturers, which will decrease by 1.2 per cent to 1,265 million euros, and on imports which will decrease by 2.1 per cent to 920 million euros. In 2013, the import/consumption ratio will decrease to 42.1 per cent, while the export/production ratio will continue to increase, reaching 74.6 per cent,” said UCIMU.

## Machine tool orders

For the fourth quarter of 2012, the machine tool index of orders in Italy produced by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE showed a 1.6 per cent increase compared with the same period in the previous year, for an absolute value of 96.6.

“Specifically, the domestic index of orders showed a 38.8 per cent increase compared to the fourth quarter of 2011; the absolute value, equal to 85.9, is however still very low, confirming the weakness of the domestic market,” explained UCIMU. “As far as exports are concerned, the index of orders showed a 4.3 per cent decrease. The absolute index, equal to 102.5, is still above average although this is not enough to give confidence to Italian manufacturers who have been experiencing a gradual reduction in the number of orders for three quarters in a row.”

On an annual basis, the index shows a 4.8 per cent decrease compared with the 2011 average. This figure is due to a downturn in overseas demand (-5.3 per cent), and the decidedly low level of orders within the domestic market, +2 per cent, when compared with the previous year, added UCIMU.

“These last figures confirm the feeling that investments in the main outlet markets for Made in Italy products are slowing down. This is a very dangerous situation for Italian manufacturers who, in the last few years, have concentrated their activities over the border to counteract the stagnation of the domestic market. It is certainly impossible to grow indefinitely," said Luigi Galdabini, President, UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, “but the weakening of the main source of business for the companies in this sector has become a rather serious complication as, in Italy, investment in capital goods are still at a standstill.

“In response to the more difficult conditions in these sectors,” added Luigi Galdabini, “it is necessary that those who will run the country consider the central role of the Italian industrial system which, thanks to SMEs, provides employment to hundreds of thousands of families. For this purpose, UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in line with the requests made by Confindustria, asks that companies are supported in their growth and development.

“Aware of the need not to become an excessive burden on the State, we however do underline the necessity for a structured industrial policy capable of injecting new energy into the system. For this reason, we hope that at least two of the proposals made by the association will be taken into account and implemented, as they represent tangible tools for the achievement of this objective.

The main Italian machine tool export markets were: China, United States, Germany, Russia, France, Brazil, Turkey, India, Poland and Mexico.

"In particular, we ask for the introduction of a tax bonus for aggregations and mergers between companies with common objectives, together with a system that rewards companies working towards growth and consolidating their position for at least three financial years. The stimulus to increase the number of employees must then be supported with a reduction in employment taxes, with a fair reduction of the tax wedge.”

He also called for more government support for the promotion of Italian exports and better screening of imports as well a total de-taxation of all profits re-invested by Italian manufacturers in technologically advanced equipment in addition to tax bonuses for product and process innovation to re-energise the sector.

## Automotive in focus

“In 2012, Western Europe was a critical area of a global market that was expanding overall,” declared Roberto Vavassori, President, ANFIA (the Italian automotive industry association) earlier this year. “The major markets, except for the United Kingdom, closed the year with negative results and Italy (-19.9 per cent), France (-13.9 per cent) and Spain (-13.4 per cent) all reported heavy losses.

“Italy has to start work immediately to bring the market back up to levels similar with those in France and the UK i.e. around the two million mark. This goal is entirely achievable, provided representatives of the industry and the institutional organs can put in place a coordinated action plan to overcome the limitations on competitiveness and development posed by a number of damaging elements. These range from measures that are unfavourable to the sector itself, such as the reduction in the deductible costs of company cars, a tax on sports cars and IPT, to the disadvantageous conditions of the country’s system such as energy costs and conditions to access credit, to name just a few.”

Italy closed December 2012 with 86,735 registrations, representing a 22.5 per cent fall, back to the levels of December 1993. It was the thirteenth consecutive double-digit monthly contraction. In 2012 as a whole, 1,402,089 units were registered, 19.9 per cent fewer than in 2011. Italy has therefore lost 44 per cent of its market since the peak of 2007 (2,493,105 units). Italian brands recorded 50,653 registrations for the month in Europe (-17.9 per cent).

In his speech at the ANFIA General Assembly at the end of last year, the ANFIA President referred to the Association’s centenary which it celebrated in 2012, and highlighted the work done in the first eight months of his term as President before going on to examine what ANFIA can still do on international, European and global fronts.

“For Italy, the goals we have set concern the market and output,” explained Roberto Vavassori. “While we wait for a return to conditions conducive to consumer and corporate confidence – the first ingredient to boost sales – we must introduce tax measures that allow the Italian car market to return to a physiological level of about 2 million units per year. ANFIA is prepared to readjust the current tax burden with competent Ministries, introducing a lighter, simpler system that is still compatible with the needs of the tax authorities. Our most significant proposals include better coordination between industry representatives and the institutional organs to develop more competitive legislation.” ■
