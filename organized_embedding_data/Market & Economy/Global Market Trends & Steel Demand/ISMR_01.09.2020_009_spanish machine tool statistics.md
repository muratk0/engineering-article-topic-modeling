# Spanish machine tool statistics

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO FIGURES from the Spanish Association of Manufacturers of Machine Tools, Accessories, Parts and Tools (AFM), ‘after five years of continuous growth that exceeded 30%, the industry as a whole shrunk by 9.46% in 2019. Machinetool numbers fell by 11.95%, metal cutting by 8.77% and metal forming in 2019 fell by 17.17%. The rest of the sectors also underwent a drop in volume: parts -7.29%, tools -5.52% and accessories -7.37%'.

“Exports fell by 5.08% overall, with the following decreases per sub-sector: metalcutting -8.02%; metal forming -8.41%; tools -4.26% and accessories -1.99% (with the exception of parts, which enjoyed a slight growth of 2.9%),” said the association.

The list of Spain’s export destinations was headed by Germany, followed by the USA, Italy, France and Portugal. The sixth spot was taken by China, after which came Mexico, India, the United Kingdom and Canada. Compared to the previous year, Italy and France improved their positions, while Mexico and China fell behind.

The President of AFM Cluster, César Garbalena, analysed the current sector situation: “We have faced the worst stage of the pandemic during the last three months, a continuous struggle fought mainly on three fronts. First,

César Garbalena, President of AFM Cluster.

we had to guarantee a safe business scenario for the people involved, upon which to build the rest of our requirements. We acted quickly, designing and implementing protocols to protect people which have allowed the majority of our companies to continue operating during this precarious period.

“Secondly, we needed to finish building and shipping the equipment previously ordered by our customers. The equipment was assembled and fine-tuned while mobility was reduced to a minimum and aftersales services and machine maintenance gradually re-commenced. Thirdly, and now the most complicated and important

challenge of all, is our marketing and sales activity. We are implementing new remote contact strategies to maintain close relations with our customers, which is essential to ensure investment needs. However, our primary customers, such as the automotive, aeronautic, railway, consumer goods and energy industries etc., have been inactive and continue at very low levels of business activity, which severely compromises their investment needs.”

According to the association, orders over the first five months of the year fell by more than 35%, mostly concentrated in the last three months. This reduction was more acute in the metal forming sub-sector. This drop was more severe in overseas markets. Slowdowns are visible in major European (Germany, Spain, Italy, France) and American markets (USA, Mexico and Brazil), with the exception of Asia, where 'China has shown a degree of growth after April and started off the year with improvement over 2019'.

AFM expects orders to fall by approximately 40% in 2020, with a gradual recovery in the last quarter of the year, which ‘should gain momentum during the first six months of 2021’. For further details, access www.afm.es or www.afmcluster.com
