# Material handling market demand

[TARİH: 01.03.2015 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO A NEW REPORT, global demand for material handling products is projected to increase by 5% annually, through to 2018, to US $140 billion. Gains will be strongest for sales of advanced equipment such as automated conveyors and automated guided vehicles. Automated products reduce the required labour input in operating machinery, helping to curtail labour costs. Energy-efficient products, such as electric lift trucks, will also see solid gains as end users seek to reduce costs.

India and China will see the strongest gains, said the report, because of rising manufacturing output that will spur demand for equipment to facilitate distribution and production. Western European markets include many of the most intensive users of material handling equipment in the world. The US will continue to be the world's largest material handling product market, benefiting from an improved outlook for fixed investment spending. This will encourage US manufacturers to invest in new equipment, particularly labour-saving automated products.

“Automated storage and retrieval systems and automated guided vehicles will post the strongest growth of any product type through to 2018. This is due to efforts among end users to reduce labour costs, particularly in developed markets such as the US. Strong gains in automated equipment demand also reflect a shift in manufacturers' preference toward products amenable to integration into larger-scale factory automation and automated warehouse-type environments,” explained the report.

“Durable goods' manufacturers will account for nearly 50% of material handling demand through to 2018. Advances will result from increased production in durable goods' sectors such as machinery and fabricated metals. Sales of material handling products to non-durable goods manufacturers will also see gains, reflecting strong outlooks for the chemical and the food and beverage markets. Other markets, which include trade and distribution companies, will see growth above the global average, spurred by the rising prevalence of international trade, which requires goods to be handled at more points along the distribution chain than domestic commerce.”
