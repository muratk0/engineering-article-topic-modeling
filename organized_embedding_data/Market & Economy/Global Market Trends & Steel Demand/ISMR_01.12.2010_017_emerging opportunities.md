# EMERGING OPPORTUNITIES

[TARİH: 01.12.2010 ISMR]

The projects currently being undertaken in the Middle East are staggering, with government, government linked/owned companies and the private sector investing trillions into developing infrastructure of all types.

• Current and active civil engineering projects in GCC: US$ 1.3 trillion

• Value of current telecommunication projects: US$ 375 billion

• Value of current Power and Water utilities projects (usually joint IWPPs): US$ 217 billion

• Value of Energy projects required between now and 2030 - US$ 1.9 trillion investments

• Transportation projects estimated at around US$ 200 billion

Middle Eastern governments have committed over US$100bn on rail projects in the coming years. They are becoming increasingly aware of the importance of designing and building sustainable rail networks. The unprecedented surge of investment into the rail industry is due to both the surge in population and the need to stimulate the economy. Among the various envisaged rail projects is a planned 1940 km Gulf Railway project which will link all six GCC states.

“The potential growth of the rail industry in the Middle East is extraordinary. With many of these ambitious projects underway, the region will start to see a dramatic transformation in its economic performance and quality of life,” IQPC, which runs a Middle Eastern rail infrastructure event, explained.

More and more Public-Private Partnership (PPP) models, to build or operate infrastructure projects in the Middle East, are being set up. The OECD projects US$ 100 billion of PPP investments over the next five years.

## ON THE RIGHT TRACK GCC countries are set to invest massive amounts of cash to bring the region’s transport network up to speed over the next ten years, CityScape Intelligence told ISMR. Around 90% of the investment will be attributed to rail projects. This was revealed during a recent study by research company, Business Monitor International (BMI).

The UAE and Saudi Arabia will dominate spending on rail, road and public transportation projects – regional transportation networks to be in place by 2020.

Richard Pavitt, exhibition director of the Roadex-Railex exhibition, said: “Regional government policy and spending over the next ten years will define the region’s transport infrastructure. The amount allocated for investment in rail projects clearly demonstrates the region’s strategy for mass transit.”

Leading the Gulf in terms of investment, Saudi Arabia is spending US$ 25 billion on its rail network adding 3,900 kilometres of track through three major projects. The first of these is the Saudi Land Bridge project, a 950 kilometre railway which will connect Jeddah and Dammam. Secondly, the 450 km Haramain high-speed network will connect Mecca and Medina via Jeddah and finally the North South railway will join the northern mineral belt with Riyadh and the industrial city of Jubail.

The UAE has already committed US$ 7.6 billion in Dubai on the Metro (red and green lines) and another US$ 3.26 billion on several major road projects which is addition to a public-private partnership (PPP) with Italian construction and infrastructure company, Salini Costruttori, to overhaul sections of the UAE-Saudi Arabia Highway.

“In the UAE capital, there are plans for a US$ 7 billion 131 km monorail track, most of which will be underground. Further funds are budgeted for a high-speed rail link with Dubai, freight rail corridor and new roads all scheduled for completion within the next five years,” said Pavitt.

Elsewhere in the Gulf, significant amounts are being invested in infrastructure projects, in Kuwait, Bahrain and Qatar, notably the US$ 4 billion Qatar-Bahrain causeway whose plans, although currently delayed due to design, financial and technical issues, are at an advanced stage.

“In addition to all of this is the proposed GCC railway project. That alone, according to Mohammed Obaid Al-Mazrouie, the assistant secretary general for economic affairs in the GCC, could be worth between US$20 – US$ 25 billion, which may not account for the extension from Oman to Yemen announced last year. Although unlikely at this stage, the project is scheduled to start before the end of 2011 and would take six years to complete,” added Pavitt.

International companies have already equipped rail projects in the Middle East. Knorr-Bremse equipped the new metro in Mecca, Saudi Arabia – the largest order for platform screen doors in the company’s history.

In 2009, it was awarded the contract to supply braking systems for the trains and platform screen doors for the stations on the new metro in Mecca. Worth almost €40 million, the contract includes the biggest ever single order for platform screen doors in the history of Knorr-Bremse’s subsidiary, Westinghouse Platform Screen Doors. Knorr-Bremse was to supply complete braking systems for seventeen twelve-car trains and doors for nine stations.

“The new metro will link the cities of Mecca, Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah and should help them to cope with the traffic during the festival of Hajj – the pilgrimage that brings three million Moslems to Mecca every year. Such large volumes of people make huge demands in terms of the reliability of the platform door systems. Moreover, the braking systems have to cope with the extreme climatic conditions on the Arabian Peninsula, including high temperatures and wind-born sand,” the company told ISMR.

The first of a total of five new metro lines was scheduled to go into operation this year in time for the Hajj in November 2010. It will reach its full capacity by 2011, by which time it will be capable of transporting at least half a million pilgrims per day. The metro in Mecca is being constructed by the China Rail Construction Company, with the trains supplied by Chinese rolling stock manufacturer CNR Changchun Railway Vehicle Company.

The Dubai Metro is one of the longest fully automated rail system in the world that is currently operational with some 75 kilometres of track. It is truly state-of-the-art and features driver-less train operation. The 87 new five-car trains built by Japanese manufacturer, Kinki Sharyo, for the new system are equipped with Knorr-Bremse EP2002 distributed brake control, Knorr-Bremse tread brakes and bogie equipment and the Knorr-Bremse oil-free compressor. Each train is designed to carry over six hundred and forty passengers. All trains will be fully air conditioned.

The first train was originally delivered to Dubai in March 2008 from Japan and is fully automated and configured for a minimum headway of 90 seconds. Maximum speed of the trains will be 90 km per hour. In 2010, when 51 trains are in service, the line has a peak-hour capacity of 11,675 passengers per hour in each direction. The theoretical maximum design capacity is 25,720 passengers per hour which would require 106 trains.

Building on the continued success of the World Cup Race held at the Nad Al Sheba Race Track, Dubai Racing Association commissioned a new Main Grandstand to extend the seating capacity at the race track. As soon as the purchase order was placed in December 1998, work began on manufacturing the masts and headframes in Abacus’ factory. Lamps were ordered from Osram in the UK.

To achieve the desired lighting levels, the lighting design used 29 masts to illuminate the entire race track, all of the masts being 35m tall with bases of 720mm square. The masts had to present as small an obstruction to the view as possible and the headframes were required to be as slimline as current lighting technology would allow.

Using advanced manufacturing technology including Abacus’s advanced CAD system; production proceeded quickly and without a hitch. The masts were made from three tapered sections that were a friction fit with each other, and the sections were made up of two halves welded together. Each steel sheet was profiled with a CNC plasma-cutting machine, with the data downloaded directly from the CAD system.

The profiles were then formed into the half-polygon troughs using a press brake of 1300 tonnes’ capacity with a 15m long bed. Pairs of troughs were welded together with a laser-guided automatic welding machine; finally, all of the masts and headframes were galvanised.

## AN EYE ON CONSTRUCTION

“The decrease in confidence in the regional real estate sector will see a significant change in the types of projects driving construction activity, with government-funded infrastructure and building projects set to provide the best opportunities for PPP,” Deloitte reported, pinpointing opportunities from efforts this year to develop schools, hospitals and affordable housing in Saudi Arabia, Qatar and Abu Dhabi.

Qatar’s construction industry grew at an exceptional rate between 2006 and 2008. However, despite the historic high growth trend, Qatar’s construction industry contracted in 2009, with industry value falling to QAR25.78bn (US$ 7.08bn), a real contraction of 21.78%. Despite these figures, BMI is still optimistic that Qatar will outperform other countries in the region - in the short term, the low base effects from 2009 will drive high growth in 2010 (forecast at 17% y-o-y). Over the next five years, BMI expects growth to average 9.9% between 2010 and 2014.

For the MENA region in Lebanon, for example, real estate values are projected to rise by 10 to 15 per cent each year until 2013, helped by the marked increase in investments by Arab property investors. Domotex Middle East has also noted that a construction boom is expected in Syria as well following the Government’s plan to spend billions on infrastructure projects over the next five years, while in Jordan the construction sector rebounded in the fourth quarter of last year, growing 12.8 per cent from a year earlier.
