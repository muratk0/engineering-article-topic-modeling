# ANEYEONINDIA

[TARİH: 01.07.2010 ISMR]

According to a report by KPMG and CII (the Confederation of Indian Industry), “India survived the recent global financial crisis better than most large economies. GDP growth is expected to be in the range of 7-8 per cent per annum for the next couple of years. Development of infrastructure to support the growth is important and a significant portion of the capital required to fund this growth could come from PE (private equity). Hence, PE is being increasingly recognised as an important asset class in India.”

N. K. Dhand, President of IMTMA (the Indian Machine Tool Manufacturers’ Association), outlined proactive strategies that India could deploy to combat the effects of the economic downturn.

“Faced with a sudden and steep downturn in domestic demand for its products due to the economic slowdown in financial year 2009-2010, the Indian machine tool industry has had to execute a number of manoeuvres to stay afloat. Minimising costs; putting off expansion; rationalising manpower; maximising revenue collection; focusing on core products; reducing inventories etc. are all are being attempted.

“However, these defensive tactics are aimed at survival. What the industry has to do in addition is deploy proactive strategies which will lead to enhanced strength in the future. One of these is building up exports. The Indian machine tool industry has certain inherent strengths, such as a large pool of design manpower and cost-competitive manufacturing, which can enable it to capture a significant share of the global market.”

Laser cutting offers many advantages to part manufacturers. Lasers do not mechanically stress the work piece, they offer extreme flexibility (a wide range of forms and contours can be cut) as well as high-precision processing. The heat-affected zone of the material is very small. Lasers also offer rapid cutting, high quality and either reduce or eliminate re-finishing work for better overall productivity.

## NEW LAUNCHES

TRUMPF’s TruLaser 1030 is a space-saving, entry level machine. Simple operation is key to this model and it introduces a special concept that allows the customer to choose between ‘beginner’ and ‘professional’ levels. For those new to the technology, the touch panel only shows the keys that are needed for fast component cutting. The user is guided intuitively through the programme with technology tables running automatically in the background.

The TruLaser 1030 combines a rugged drive system and machine construction with a TRUMPF TruCoax 2kW diffusion-cooled RF laser resonator. With a simultaneous axis speed of 85m/min, the TruLaser 1030 can cut mild steel in thicknesses of up to 15mm. TRUMPF has also expanded its TruDiode Series. These diode lasers are now available with output power of 800, 1000, 2000 and 3000 watts for welding, brazing, hardening and heat treatment.

Bystronic’s Byspeed laser cutting system enjoys great popularity on the market. At the MACH 2010 exhibition recently, Byspeed was equipped with a new nozzle changer which was launched on the market just a few months ago. With this option, nozzles can be exchanged within 15 seconds without any manual action being necessary.

“The user benefits through the greater autonomy of the laser cutting system, since fewer operator interventions are required. And, as the optimal nozzle is automatically selected for every cutting plan, the flexibility is increased, while the process reliability and the quality of the parts are also improved. Furthermore, a monitoring function ensures trouble-free nozzle changing,” added Bystronic.

## NEW LASER DEVELOPMENTS

Both disk and fibre laser technology ensure a much better level of beam quality and faster cutting processes. Fibre laser technology is an area of growing interest. The demand for fibre lasers with very high beam quality and power outputs below one kilowatt is very promising but still small relative to the entire market.

One laser for two different machines is the concept behind TRUMPF’s new TruMatic 3000 Fibre punch/laser combination machine. It shares a fibre-guided TruDisk laser with the TruLaser Robot 5020 cell in the TRUMPF LaserNetwork, an arrangement that significantly increases the utilisation rate of the beam source.

With the TruMatic 3000 Fibre and TruLaser Robot 5020 systems working in parallel, the fibre-guided TruDisk 3001 (with its 3 kilowatts of laser power) can switch from one application to the other in milliseconds. The machines are supported by the intelligent LaserNetwork and are able to automatically make use of the laser beam as required.

The BLM-ADIGE LT Fiber is a CNC tube laser cutting and profiling system that uses the latest technology fibre laser source to machine materials with highly reflective exterior surfaces that previously proved difficult or impossible to cut using conventional laser sources. It achieves this, says BLM, with less than half the installed power requirement.

“Crucially, the shorter wavelength of the fibre laser and the consequently simplified optic path enables the LT Fiber to cut highly reflective materials such as copper and brass previously considered impractical in terms of laser cutting as back-reflection could significantly damage a conventional laser source. Maximum sheet thickness when cutting brass and copper with the LT Fiber is 3mm, while polished aluminium of up to 5mm thickness can also be cut safely and effectively,” BLM told ISMR.

Fibre lasers (1-5kW) have excellent beam quality so they can be focused to relatively small diameters and the extra intensity should lead to faster cutting and better cutting quality. However, there is still little evidence that CO2 lasers will be displaced by fibre lasers in the next few years, a laser cutting expert told ISMR. If anything, it will almost certainly be the traditional Nd:YAG laser - those that you find in the fine cutting industry – that are more likely to be partially displaced by the highly competitive fibre laser.
