# MIDEST puts Africa in the spotlight

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

ONE of the world’s largest and most comprehensive subcontracting events, MIDEST, takes place in Paris from 4 to 7 November 2014. Despite slow economic conditions across Europe, MIDEST has attracted over 1,700 exhibitors, as OEMs turn to outside suppliers to manage costs and volatile demand.

Special features at this year’s event include a focus on Normandy, a region where strong trading links already exist in sectors including energy, automotive, aerospace and food. This year’s featured country, Tunisia, offers a low-cost supply base, with high-level capabilities, that is just a short step from Europe’s manufacturing heartland. With a 3.6 per cent growth rate and 11m inhabitants, Tunisia’s mechanical engineering and electrical sectors account for 35 per cent of exports.

The free MIDEST conference programme will feature dedicated content highlighting the strengths of Normandy and Tunisia, as well as a strong focus on aerospace and sessions on plastics, composites and additive manufacturing. MIDEST 2014 will be held in conjunction with MAINTENANCE EXPO, the total maintenance solutions show. It will also present the forthcoming MIDEST MAROC, the industry event in Morocco, which will run from 10 to 13 December 2014.

Despite its size and the wide range of technologies on offer, the way that MIDEST is organised into themed areas makes it very easy to find exactly what you want. There are dedicated villages for all the major processes, including forming and fabrication, casting and forging, turning and machining, plastics and composites, electronics and product finishing.

There are also dedicated villages for some less mainstream technologies, including wood processing, micro-technologies and industrial services. This year’s show also sees the launch of two new focus areas – technical textiles and special machines.

A highlight of the MIDEST exhibition is the chance to see the subcontracting strength of the French regions. 15 of these regions have pavilions at the show, with French companies making up around 60 per cent of exhibitors. There are also 18 national pavilions from countries outside France, and a total of 35 countries represented among the exhibitors.
