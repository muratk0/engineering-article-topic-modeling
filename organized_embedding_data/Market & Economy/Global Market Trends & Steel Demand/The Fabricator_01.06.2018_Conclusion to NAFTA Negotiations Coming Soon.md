# Conclusion to NAFTA Negotiations Coming Soon?

[TARİH: 01.06.2018 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

The assertion is that some kind of deal is close as far as NAFTA is concerned. It has been the stated goal of the Trump administration that a new deal be in place by the end of the year, and Congress needs time to get in the game.

Four scenarios seem to be making the rounds. The first is a quick but partial deal focused on the automotive sector. This has been the most critical sector for Canada and Mexico as well as for the U.S. auto sector as a whole. The deal seems to be focused on increasing the content requirements for cars sent to the U.S., but it is not nearly as draconian as had been originally discussed.

The second option is extending the deadline from May 17 to later in the summer. This presumably still leaves time for Congress to act but more time to hammer out some details regarding everything from extending the steel tariff exemptions to farm sector adjustments.

The third option is continued hyperbole and drama as Trump keeps calling the deal the "worst ever." This is the tactic that has consistently kept businesses on edge. There has been less of this invective over the last few weeks as Trump’s attention has been focused on Iran, North Korea, and China. The details seem to be staying in the hands of the negotiators.

The fourth option is holding out for a total rewrite, and this option has no support at all from either Canada or Mexico. It has been labeled a "nonoption" by even the U.S. at times, but it is still threatened now and then.

The odds right now seem to favor option two with the assertion that deals on automotive and agriculture be promulgated. The U.S. currently sees some reason to do a deal sooner rather than later as there is now a good chance the next president of Mexico will be Andres Manuel Lopez Obrador. AMLO is a hard-liner on the U.S. and has often expressed some very hostile positions regarding this country. It would be far better for the U.S. to conclude a deal before he wins the election, which is scheduled for later this summer.
