## Construction market trends

According to ASME (originally the American Society of Mechanical Engineers), there are several ongoing construction market trends such as:

Single-design models: To save time and money, there’s a shift to all in-house from engineering to coordination, creating a one-stop shop;

Material use: Going to prefabrication off-site for construction to improve schedules – ‘Instead of joining one piece of duct work you can join 20 feet of duct together’;

Energy efficiency: Systems that recover energy through heat wheels and occupancy sensors are becoming vital;

Permanent modular construction: This fits particularly well in a time crunch, from fast food restaurants that need to go up quickly to army barracks for military deployment.

Skilled engineers: Well-rounded mechanical engineers who can go beyond calculations to fitting into these coordinated engineering design models.

The Society of Chartered Surveyors Ireland Construction Outlook 2014 shows a stabilisation in construction output for the first time in several years since the significant decline in the construction industry as a result of the economic downturn in 2007.

## Surface heating in milliseconds

THERE IS a demand for fast and targeted heating and cooling. This challenge can be met with the use of an in-line high-temperature process that provides targeted surface heating in a matter of milliseconds. The Activation Line 300 was specifically designed to fill this need. With its high electro-optic energy efficiency of over 40 per cent, the most powerful L³ LIMO Line Laser (a CW output of over 13kW with a homogeneous line focus of 300mm in length) heats even very thin functional layers (thicknesses of over 0.1µm to more than 100µm) with an intensity of 40kW/cm² in a few milliseconds. Because of the low thermal capacity, the layers also cool down again very quickly.

“The laser energy thus serves to functionalise the layer and, as a result, add to its value,” concluded Dirk Hauschild, director of Thin-Film Activation at LIMO Lissotschenko Mikrooptik GmbH in Dortmund. “Thanks to the targeted irradiation using the laser line profile, the user can uniformly temper large surface areas. The Activation Line 300 thus enables customised annealing, crystallisation and sintering processes with fully closed surfaces.” ■

## Welding seams in thick-walled aluminium

WHEN LASER welding aluminium, the weld pool is distinctly fluid. Material expulsions and holes can occur, and these can affect weld quality and bonding. Visibly good results can be achieved using a coaxial seam tracking system and the precisely adjustable effective width of the laser spot.

“It is not only thin components – generally the case with conduction welding – that can be welded. The ScanTracker also enables conduction welding, with scan function, of thick-walled aluminium components. There is no need for expensive and complex post-treatment, above fall for visible seams,” explained the manufacturer, Precitec GmbH & Co. KG.

During the welding process, the ScanTracker measures the weld joint transversely, and the distance in the Z-direction just a few millimetres before the TCP coaxially via the YW52 welding optics. The welding head is fully autonomous and controls the laser spot two-dimensionally and precisely at the joint without additional axes. The minimal lead-time of under a tenth of a second and the programmable distribution of linear energy across the width of the seam enure short cycle times. See www.precitec.de ■
