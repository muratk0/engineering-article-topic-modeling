# Is the data center boom more of a balloon?

[TARİH: 01.11.2025 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

For metal fabricators serving that market, it doesn’t matter; right now, they have parts to deliver

DAN DAVIS

W

atching the explosion of the data center market makes me think of other buildups in recent U.S. history—and the collapses that came with them.

The dot-com balloon popped in 2000, after years of crazy market valuations and growth for a lot of web-based companies. The recreation areas and free breakfasts and lunches for employees weren’t enough to keep those online firms around. Only seven years later, the real estate market in the U.S. collapsed as overextended home owners couldn’t make mortgage payments, which led to mass foreclosures and the devaluation of housing-related investment products, like mortgage-backed securities. Economists suggest that U.S. households lost trillions in net worth in the ensuing recession.

Many are now wondering if we are in the midst of another balloon. Companies, such as Google, Amazon, Meta, and Microsoft, are investing billions in artificial intelligence and the data centers and infrastructure that will power these energy-hungry, super-computing products. Other companies responsible for making AI happen, such as chipmaker NVIDIA and data analytics platform builder Palantir, have seen their stock market values rise as well.

Where is all of this headed? On one hand, some believe that AI-enhanced systems will deliver a new wave of productivity, allowing few to do so much more than they can now. There is already speculation that early iterations of AI-powered software are making it hard for new college graduates to find entry-level jobs. On the other hand, you have economist Daron Acemoglu of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology telling AP that he expects a "nontrivial but modest" 0.7% boost in productivity related to AI over the next decade.

From the perspective of metal fabricators currently in supply chains tied to the data center market, it doesn’t matter. They are more worried about getting parts out the door.

To do this, these shops have invested in new machine tools and automation that allow them to produce quality parts at a rapid rate and in a consistent manner. Data centers, in a way, have been a major force in the U.S. manufacturing base, which is embracing advanced technology in a way that it really never did before.

In 2022, The Fabricator ran a story, "The job shop helping to maintain the information superhighway" (December 2022, p. 48), which detailed how Nevada Precision Sheet Metal was using a TRUMPF TruLaser 7000 tube cutting machine and a Sunrise APS-30080M automatic punching and shearing system for flat bar from Trilogy Machinery to help with the fabrication of racks. Gianni Ygnelzi of NPSM explained that these racks were destined for a 50,000-sq.-ft. space with 32-ft.-high ceilings. That’s just for one data center.

Data centers, in a way, have been a major force in the U.S. manufacturing base, which is embracing advanced technology in a way that it really never did before.

Imagine how many racks will go into the Open-AI Stargate Project slated for 8 million sq. ft. in Abilene, Texas. The scale of these projects is mind-blowing.

Meanwhile, some metal fabricators are sitting on the outside of this hyperactivity. The lesson for metal fabricators coming out of the Great Recession that began in 2008 was customer diversification, but even today, some manufacturers still maintain a significant customer concentration in one sector because of history or just opportunity. For those heavily linked to sectors like agriculture or automotive, business has been slower than they would like.

But there’s always another sector to serve, and the data center boom looks like a good one to target. Some, likely spooked by memories of the web bust of the early 2000s, see impending doom, but they need to realize that this is just another investment cycle, this time inspired by AI’s potential. There will be some winners and some losers, some consolidation of efforts and some that fall too far behind and exit. But the investment levels are real because the players are well funded. Those watching the data center market expect global growth to continue at a compounded annual rate of 10% to 2030.

What will be the impact of an AI-empowered future? Will such a future be worth the environmental impact in terms of power generation and water usage? Will the cost to power these super-computing entities cause stress for both the electrical grid and taxpayers? Frankly, metal fabricators don’t have to worry about those questions. They are fabricating the future as determined by those visionaries with the means to build it.

A more interesting point to ponder is what happens after the data center boom winds down, which some suggest might be around 2030. A lot can change nowadays in that short window of time. What we do know is that metal fabricators are well equipped to produce quality and cost-effective parts and assemblies for whatever the future holds after investing to serve current customers. That’s a solid foundation from which to attack future opportunities.
