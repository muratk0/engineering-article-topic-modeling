# Italian machine tool orders

[TARİH: 01.09.2019 ISMR]

IN THE SECOND quarter of 2019, the UCIMU index of machine tool orders highlighted a 31.4% downturn compared to the same period in the previous year. UCIMU SISTEMI PER PRODURRE is the Italian association of machine tools, robotics and automation.

"The global result was due to the strong decrease registered for orders in domestic and overseas markets. In particular, the orders collected by manufacturers in the domestic market showed a 43% fall compared to the period April-June 2018. On the overseas front, Italian manufacturers reported a remarkable reduction in orders, down by 28.5% compared to the second quarter of 2018," explained the association.

"This drop in domestic orders," pointed out Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, "proves that the Italian market, after the positive effect generated by Industry 4.0 provisions, is returning to its normal status. However, even if we expected a pace change, this normalisation process has turned out to be particularly abrupt in the first few months of the year, which is also due to the lack of clarity on the applicability of competitiveness measures that the Italian Government should have put at the disposal of SMEs right away.

"Only now," he continued, "with the recent approval of the Growth Decree which re-established Super-Depreciation, again accompanied by Hyper-Depreciation, have Italian manufacturers been given a clear schedule of the provisions at their disposal to better define their investments in new machines. We therefore expect that the third quarter index will be positively influenced by the reactivation of this measure, which is a key measure to foster the technological upgrades that the Italian manufacturing industry still needs."

He further urged the Italian government to abandon the "logic of intermittency" by which (to date) the applicability of all measures in favour of manufacturers has been defined, as well as implement an overall Package for Enterprise Growth. This, he said, should include all tax benefits relating to investments in Research & Development and to Super- and Hyper-Depreciation for investments in new machinery, software and automation, creating a 'long-term, global plan'.

## Training and marketing

"Alongside the issue of innovation is professional education and training 4.0, for which we ask that the applicability of the tax credit provision be extended to 2020 and reviewed in such a way as to include the cost of external trainers, the most onerous expense item for SMEs in the calculation of tax credit," he added.

In overseas markets, Carboniero highlighted 'the atmosphere of political instability, the unrest all over Europe and fears connected with the worsening of the trade conflict between the United States and China. He also drew attention to decreased imports from Germany (a key Italian market) which have been affected by automotive sector issues, such as changes linked to the spread of electric motors.

"We welcome the decision of the Government authorities to establish a tax credit for Italian companies that take part in global exhibitions held abroad. However, it is important to identify leading events with the help of the entrepreneurial organisations, so as not to waste available resources," he explained.
