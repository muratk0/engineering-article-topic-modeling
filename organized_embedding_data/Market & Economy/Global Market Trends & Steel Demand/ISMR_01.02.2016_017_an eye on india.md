# AN EYE ON INDIA

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 ISMR]

We highlight trends, forecasts and opportunities in the Indian economy through to 2025.

Industrial production in India is expected to be 3.15 per cent by the end of this quarter, according to Trading Economics global macro models and analysts’ expectations. It estimates industrial production in India to stand at 1.79 in 12 months’ time. In the long-term, it is projected to trend at around 1.41 per cent in 2020, according to its models.

“India has a very large domestic market, and resilient private consumption is a major driver of economic growth. A vast supply of inexpensive but skilled labour has turned India into the ‘back office of the world’. Despite numerous problems, India has generally managed to avoid hard authoritarian rule or military coups, which have happened in many other developing countries including India’s neighbours, Bangladesh, Myanmar and Pakistan,” commented analyst, BMI, who believes it will become the fastest growing economy in Asia.

“India’s labour market offers many potential benefits for prospective investors. As one of the most populated countries in the world, the labour market is immense and further benefits from a low minimum wage and relatively few labour market restrictions. There are also, however, considerable risks such as the weak quality of education which suffers from large regional disparities and limited female participation in both education and the labour force.”

## Positive medium-term outlook

According to the HSBC Global Connections’ Trade Forecast, India’s low unit labour costs and a competitive currency are expected to keep it well positioned to take advantage of bottoming commodity prices and fastgrowing Asian markets. However, established trading partners such as the US and the UAE (the latter being a regional trading hub) are likely to remain dominant destinations for exports.

“As the global economy strengthens, we expect cyclical sectors such as transport equipment and metals to be the key drivers of India’s projected export recovery. Weak external demand and declining commodity prices have weighed significantly on Indian exports, almost 35% of which is commodity-linked. Given that much of this can be attributed to a cyclical slowdown in growth rather than loss of competitiveness, we think that the medium-term outlook for India’s exports is positive,” said the forecast.

“A gradual recovery is underway in India, supported by lower oil prices and expansionary monetary policy. Although the jury is still out on the true state of the recovery, economic momentum should pick up speed in the coming months on the back of better monetary policy transmission, higher public investment and strengthening urban consumption. Thus, India is likely to continue outperforming other emerging markets in the short-term, not least because of its lower vulnerability to slower Chinese growth. India is a net importer of commodities and has modest trade linkages with China, which is likely to mute the spill-over impact of the Chinese slowdown on the Indian economy.

However, petroleum products and minerals will continue to be important imports. Along with industrial machinery, they are projected to contribute around one third to India’s import growth between 2015 and 2030.
