# Strong year for Italian machine tool industry

[TARİH: 01.02.2017 ISMR]

Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation association

THE ITALIAN MACHINE tool, robot and automation manufacturing industry closed 2016 with positive economic indicators and is readying itself for a year of further growth.

"In 2016, production recorded a 5% upturn against the previous year. A positive trend was reported re deliveries by Italian manufacturers in the domestic market, 20.5% more compared to the previous year. On the other hand, exports decreased (-3.3%) due to the weakness experienced in some major markets for Italian products," commented Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation association.

Based on ISTAT data processing by UCIMU, in the first nine months of 2016 (latest available survey), the main markets for “Made in Italy" products turned out to be: Germany at 260 million (-1%); the United States at 247 million (-9.1%); China at 222 million (-9.7%); France at 158 million (+23.6%); Poland at 90 million (+3.5%); Mexico at 76 million (+37.1%); Spain at 75 million (+8.5%); Turkey at 64 million (-4.4%); United Kingdom at 58 million (-33%) and Russia at 57 million (-60%).

Consumption registered a 10.1% increase - the third consecutive year of double-digit growth and a testament to the recovery of the Italian market which has finally started to invest again in production technologies.

"2017 will also be positive for the Italian industry sector. According to the forecasts, there will be growth for all the main economic indicators. Production will rise +4%, approaching the record-breaking value of 6 billion euros registered in 2008. Exports are expected to grow by 1.7%. The positive consumption trend will also continue – it should rise by 6.9%, driving manufacturer deliveries as well as imports," added Carboniero.

"Now, with the Industry 4.0 Plan included in the already approved Budget Law 2017, the Italian Government has given the country a well-structured and comprehensive programme of industrial policy. Confirmation of Super-Depreciation at 140%, introduction of Hyper-Depreciation at 250%, re-financing of the New Sabatini Law and an increase from 25% to 50% of tax deductions for R&D activity are the pillars on which this programme is built. With this plan, Italian companies can rely on a series of measures aimed at increasing their competitiveness. These measures can further boost the consumption of machinery and innovative technologies for factory digitisation."■
