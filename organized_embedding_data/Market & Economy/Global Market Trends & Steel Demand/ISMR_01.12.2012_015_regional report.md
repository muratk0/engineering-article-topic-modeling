# REGIONAL REPORT

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

According to the World Steel Association (worldsteel), the recovery of steel demand in the MENA region has been slower than expected due to continuing political instability but it forecasts that apparent steel use in the region will still increase by 4.9 per cent in 2012. In 2013, it expects the growth rate to accelerate to 6.7 per cent and steel demand to reach 66.9 Mt.

## Gulf infrastructure hots up

Important sporting events, such as the 2022 Football World Cup to be held in Qatar, will speed up construction programmes and boost property prices in the Gulf state. Qatar will spend some US$ 100bn over the next five years as it rushes to prepare for soccer’s biggest tournament. Over the next five years, Qatar will build a US$ 25 billion rail network, a US$ 11 billion new airport and a US$ 5.5 billion new deep water seaport. The Gulf state will also spend billions more on 12 air-conditioned football stadiums.

Other projects include a US$ 1 billion crossing linking the new airport with projects in the northern part of the capital, Doha. It will also spend an additional US$ 20 billion on new roads. These huge projects are quite unprecedented in the history of the region and are set to provide rich pickings for the steel fabrication sector in the form of bulk demand for equipment, new technology and expertise.

The GCC is steaming ahead with railway projects worth US$ 106.2 billion, research from Ventures Middle East revealed in the build-up to the CityBuild Construction Summit in Abu Dhabi. Saudi Arabia leads the way, with a total of 23 projects valued at US$ 25.6 billion, including the US$ 6 billion Makkah-Madina railway link which is currently under tender for construction.

In the UAE, eight railway projects valued at US$ 20.6 billion are underway, including the US$ 11 billion Emirates railway project. Meanwhile, the US$ 25 billion Qatar national rail scheme will link to the GCC railway network, the US$ 30 billion, 2,200km system that will connect all six GCC states by 2017.

Other major regional railway developments underway include Kuwait’s national railroad network and metro system, worth a combined US$ 17 billion; Oman’s national freight and passenger railway, valued at US$ 10 billion and Bahrain’s rapid transport network at an estimated US$ 8 billion.
