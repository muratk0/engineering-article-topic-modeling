ISMR SAYS: "Analysts foresee another year of recession for Latin America"

AN EYE ON LATIN AMERICA

We highlight trends and economic forecasts for the Latin America region in 2016 and 2017

Growth in Latin America and the Caribbean has been marked down further and is likely to contract for the second consecutive year in 2016, said the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

The IMF's latest Regional Economic Outlook for the Western Hemisphere, released on 27 April 2016 in Mexico City, projects that the region is set to contract by 0.5 per cent in 2016 – marking two consecutive years of negative growth for the first time since the Latin American debt crisis of 1982-83. This rate, however, masks the fact that many countries continue to grow, modestly but surely, whereas a small number of economies - representing about half of the region's economy - face recession largely as a result of domestic factors.

"The deceleration in activity reflects weak external demand, further declines in commodity prices, volatile financial conditions and, for some, important domestic imbalances and rigidities," the report said. "At the same time, many countries have continued to experience large exchange rate depreciations, mainly as a result of deteriorating terms of trade and external demand."

For 2017, the IMF expects the region to bounce back to 1.5 per cent growth.

## Regional mix

"Brazil is mired in a deep recession with growth contracting by 3.8 per cent – the same rate as in 2015 – due to economic and political problems. Chile's growth is expected to slow to 1.5 per cent in 2016, reflecting subdued confidence and sluggish investment in the mining sector. Argentina and Venezuela face output contractions of about 1 per cent and 8 per cent in 2016, respectively. But Argentina's medium-term growth prospects have improved noticeably as a result of an ongoing transition to remove domestic imbalances and distortions and correct relative prices. On the other hand, Peru's economy has strengthened and growth is expected to rise further in 2016 to 3.25 per cent, primarily boosted by ongoing mining investment," said the IMF.

“Given the continued recovery in the United States, the growth outlook for Mexico and Central America remains relatively robust. Mexico is expected to grow at a moderate 2.4 per cent in 2016. Countries in Central America have benefited from low oil prices and solid U.S. growth. Output growth for the region is projected at 4.5 per cent in 2016."

The regional outlook is subject to various downside risks. Latin America, pointed out the IMF, remains particularly vulnerable to a stronger-than expected slowdown in China – the destination for 15 to 25 per cent of exports from Brazil, Chile, Peru, Uruguay and Venezuela – and to further declines in commodity prices, the report said. A slowdown in China would contribute further to lower commodity prices and increase corporate risks.

The World Bank has forecasted that the Latin America and Caribbean region will contract by 1.3 per cent in 2016 after a 0.7 per cent decline in 2015, the first back-to-back years of recession in more than 30 years. It is projected to begin expanding again in 2017, gradually gaining momentum to around 2 per cent in 2018.

Prospects vary across the region: South America is anticipated to contract by 2.8 per cent this year, followed by mild recovery in 2017. In contrast, supported by ties to the United States and strong exports, output in the Mexico and Central America sub-region, and the Caribbean, are expected to grow at 2.7 per cent and 2.6 per cent respectively in 2016, and by more in 2017 and 2018. Brazil is forecast to contract 4 per cent in 2016 and its recession is expected to carry over into 2017, amid attempts at policy tightening, rising unemployment, shrinking real incomes and political uncertainty.

## Economic forecasts

According to Focus Economics, Latin America remains trapped in recession.

"Economic weakness persisted at the beginning of the year following two consecutive periods in which the economy contracted in the third and fourth quarters of 2015 – the sharpest fall in six years was registered in Q4. The region's aggregate GDP is estimated to have decreased 1.2% year-on-year in the first quarter, which continues to suggest overall weakness in the region. However, this data mask the fact that some economies within Latin America are managing the region's recession better than others and are growing, while others are facing a severe economic deterioration," said the analyst, pointing out that the regional GDP aggregate is mainly being held down by Brazil, Ecuador and Venezuela.

Venezuela remains rooted in a profound political and economic crisis, which shows no signs of ending soon.

"Meanwhile, in Mexico, economic activity continues to grow, although at a tepid pace. Colombia's economy, which was once a regional star, began to show signs of cooling at the beginning of the year. In fact, Peru is the only economy where growth remains close to its potential, despite a challenging regional and global economic scenario. Peru will also retain its prudent, business-friendly economic policy framework in the next years, following the results of the presidential election in which Pedro Pablo Kuczynski won by a narrow margin," continued the analyst.

Latin America did begin to see signs of improvement in Q2, reflecting the current external conditions and global developments. After experiencing heightened volatility in financial markets earlier this year, the region transitioned to a smoother period in the second quarter which prompted a stabilisation in exchange rates. A positive evolution in global commodities prices is alleviating concerns for many of the region's commodity exporters.

Heading into the second half of the year, the region's economic activity is expected to stabilise and is seen improving gradually in the coming quarters. Nevertheless, risks to the short-term outlook persist. Analysts foresee another year of recession for Latin America. The expected contraction in Latin America's GDP this year reflects a projected contraction in Argentina, Brazil, Ecuador and Venezuela, which together account for 50% of the region's GDP.

"This month's (June's) regional GDP growth forecast was revised up by 0.1 percentage points over the previous month's projection and stemmed from an increase in the forecasts for Bolivia and Brazil. Meanwhile, the forecasts for Chile, Colombia, Mexico and Peru were left unchanged, while the projection for the remaining five economies surveyed was cut," concluded Focus Economics.

"Colombia's real GDP has been expanding recently, strongly aided by improving domestic demand and a gradual jump in non-residential infrastructure-linked construction activity. The hydrocarbons sector continues to suffer from the ongoing structural transformation affecting the global energy industry and persistent evidence of excess supply. Despite oil-related public sector budget restraint, robust domestic consumption has become a primary driver of economic activity in Mexico, also supported by improved labour market conditions and persistently low interest rates in the USA," commented Scotiabank in its Spring 'Latin America Regional Outlook'.

"The Mexican economy offers a promising economic outlook for 2016-17. Domestic consumption remains a key growth driver. Improving conditions in the USA due solid employment, systemically sound banks and monetary stimulus are some external factors supporting Mexico's outlook. The Mexican economy will likely expand by 3.1% in 2017 up from 2.3% in 2016. Mexico remains sensitive to the structural transformation affecting global energy markets as well as oil price gyrations. Energy sector structural reforms were unable to prevent a sharp reduction in crude oil production. The construction sector will continue to grow, but will remain sensitive to public sector investment adjustments and credit flows to residential housing. Overall, private consumption and the services sector will remain key domestic drivers of economic growth in 2016-17."

We preview FABTECH 2016 which will be held in Las Vegas from 16-18 November 2016.

North America's largest metal forming, fabricating, welding and finishing event heads to the Las Vegas Convention Center in Las Vegas, NV USA from 16-18 November 2016. FABTECH 2016 is expected to cover more than 550,000 net square feet and anticipates hosting over 28,000 attendees and 1,300 exhibiting companies.

"FABTECH provides a convenient 'one stop shop' venue where you can meet with worldclass suppliers, see the latest industry products and developments and find the tools to improve productivity, increase profits and discover new solutions to all of your metal forming, fabricating, welding and finishing needs," the organiser explained.

Admission to the show floor is complimentary if visitors register by the deadline (11 November 2016). After the deadline, the on-site fee is US$ 50.

## Visitors in focus

The event provides educational sessions and expert-led presentations covering the latest trends and technologies in the metal forming, fabricating, welding and finishing industries.

"The post-show report from our last show in Las Vegas tells us that international visitors made up 18% of our attendees for that year. It may take a little extra planning, but experiencing FABTECH 2016 will definitely be worth it," the organiser told /SMR.

"Visitors tell us that the number one reason they come is to see and evaluate new products and technologies. Being able to compare equipment and processes against one another in a real-time situation is a huge part of the experience. Keeping up with the very latest technologies and trends in metal fabrication, forming, welding and finishing is also important to our attendees."

More than half of attendees at FABTECH represent small-to-medium companies looking to connect with knowledgeable people who can give them a competitive advantage in their markets. From job shops to OEMs, FABTECH celebrates metal manufacturing as an industry.

The show will encompass three halls at the Las Vegas Convention Center - the North Hall, Central Hall and Paradise Center. Attendees will find a variety of technology pavilions dedicated to Forming & Fabricating, Tube & Pipe, METALFORM, Welding and Finishing.

The five FABTECH co-sponsors include SME; the American Welding Society (AWS); the Fabricators & Manufacturers Association International (FMA); the Precision Metalforming Association (PMA) and the Chemical Coaters Association International (CCAI). Together, these associations bring technical proficiency and industry insight to FABTECH.
