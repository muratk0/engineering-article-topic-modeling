## INDUSTRY REPORT

Shipbuilding is an important and strategic industry in a number of countries. Shipyards often play a significant role for the regional industrial infrastructure and, with military shipbuilding, for national security interests.

The shipbuilding industry is embedded in a value chain consisting of designers, equipment manufacturers, yards, ship owners, operators and their clients, as well as supporting parties such as classification societies and research institutes. Shipbuilding is favoured by many developing nations (e.g. South Korea and China) as a means of building their industrial base with supportive incentives offered by governments.

## Global trends

The current global downturn has intensified pressure on the worldwide shipbuilding industry, as it has resulted in reduced demand. For the short term, this puts pressure on the global shipbuilding industry including shipyards and marine equipment manufacturers. Reduction of demand appears strongest in mass segments like bulkers, tankers and container ships (segments in which European players are less active). On the other hand, drivers like fuel prices have also triggered new demand in these segments, mainly to replace smaller and/or less efficient ships for new, larger ones. This demand is benefiting Asian yards dominating these segments, says a recent EC shipbuilding study. Secondly, says the study, the reduction in demand in the mass segments has triggered these yards – faced by depleting order books – to challenge other segments.

According to a May 2012 presentation by the Korea Iron and Steel Association, there was a 6.8% increase in shipbuilding in 2011, but it was forecasted to decline by more than 5% due to the global downturn in the shipbuilding industry and request for delay in deliveries.

New naval programmes have boosted shipbuilding markets, such as the UK Royal Navy’s Type 26 Global Combat Ship, a multimission warship due to come into service.
