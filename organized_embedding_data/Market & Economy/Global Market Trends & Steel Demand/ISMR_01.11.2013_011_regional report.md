THE BATTLE FOR RECOVERY

We highlight economic forecasts and manufacturing activity in the US, along with new initiatives to stimulate recovery

ISMR SAYS: “Encouraging signs of revival for manufacturers in the US’s embattled economy are on the horizon”

The past six years have been tumultuous and challenging for the US economy and the government has battled to strengthen the US economic recovery following the global economic downturn. However, the US dollar still remains the world’s premier reserve currency and America's fiscal deficit has been roughly halved in nominal dollar values, whilst the United States remains a diversified and resilient economy that continues to be supported by a stimulating monetary policy.

“In the past few months, economic growth, investor confidence and financial markets have been sideswiped by a number of developments. First, there were growing expectations that the US Federal Reserve would begin to unwind its extraordinary monetary stimulus. Second, escalating tensions surrounding the civil war in Syria had raised the threat of US military intervention. And third, the headwinds facing the larger emerging market economies appeared to be getting stronger,” commented Scotia Bank in its global market update.

“For the most part, these issues have been largely diffused, although not without some residual negative impact on growth internationally due to unsettled capital and currency market developments. The partial shutdown of the US government and the need to extend the government’s borrowing authority risked undermining the forward momentum of the US economy and its global trading partners if a political compromise could not be reached quickly….”

## Looking to the future

In its forecast, Scotia Bank warned that the US economy could become increasingly handicapped if the anticipated revival in business investment does not materialise. Any meaningful back-up in borrowing costs, it said, would further aggravate already cautious business spending, thereby contributing to a deeper and more protracted slowdown. Instead of creating jobs, businesses would reinforce the emerging slowdown by consolidating operations and cutting back their workforces. Consumer spending and residential activity would increasingly be at risk.

Nonetheless, its base case forecast for the US economy included a political compromise that would raise the federal debt ceiling (which did, in fact, happen). It saw a relatively small impact on US consumer and business investment expenditures in the current quarter, although the anticipated weakness is not anticipated to be sufficiently large to lower its annual real GDP estimate of 1.6%. It maintains its view that the US economy will gain some traction in 2014, with output advancing by an average 2.6%. A slightly weaker economic hand-off into the New Year will be offset by a lessening in fiscal drag and the Fed’s recent decision to delay the tapering of its massive monthly bond-buying programme in a bid to keep downward pressure on longer-term borrowing costs, it added.

Increasing exports and business investment should reinforce a slightly stronger pace of growth in 2014, building upon the improving performance expected in the United States, and the encouraging signs that some of the larger emerging market economies, like China, are stabilizing and underpinning the demand and prices of commodities. Similar performances are anticipated in Mexico and in other Latin American countries whose industrial activity and tourism are closely tied to US developments.

## Manufacturing activity

Economic activity in the US manufacturing sector expanded in September for the fourth consecutive month, and the overall economy grew for the 52nd consecutive month, say US supply executives in the latest Manufacturing ISM Report On Business®.

Highlights from the third quarter include improved optimism for the world economic outlook among US industrial manufacturers; the US economy remains a growth driver with moderate revenue growth expected in 2013; investment spending plans rise with an emphasis on R&D and new product introductions; and hiring plans reach a five-year high, while the lack of qualified workers is slowing gaining.

In a recent Baker Tilly survey, where three hundred senior executives of US manufacturing companies participated, the downturn in the US economy was thought to be tougher on small manufacturers, with a sizable minority struggling to make it through.

“Executives from larger companies are more likely to report that they are using tools such as tax planning and negotiating vendor discounts. Larger businesses are also more likely to use foreign markets to diversify their revenue sources,” commented Baker Tilly.

The US Precision Metalforming Association (PMA) released its latest Business Conditions report in September this year.

“PMA’s member companies are optimistic about the trend for fourth quarter shipments, in spite of a modest decline in incoming orders being anticipated over the next three months,” said PMA President, Bill Gaskin. “Last year at this time, expectations for both fourth quarter shipments and orders were soft. This year, momentum in shipments seems to be building toward a modest fall rally, which is generally consistent with the most recent ISM report.”

## The skills gap

Caterpillar Chairman and CEO and National Association of Manufacturers’ (NAM) Board Chair, Doug Oberhelman, recently announced the formation of a task force of business and manufacturing leaders that will tackle the workforce challenges hampering the continued growth of US manufacturing competitiveness in the global marketplace.

Two of the most pressing issues, which the task force will address, are the mounting skills gap between the preparedness of workers and the capabilities that manufacturers need as well as the scarcity of graduates in the science, technology, engineering and mathematics fields.

“A strong manufacturing base is a critical foundation for strengthening the American economy and, to be successful, a strong manufacturing economy requires a highly skilled workforce,” said Oberhelman.

“It is an issue that will help to define our potential growth and shape manufacturing for decades to come. This multi-industry task force will supply the necessary policy solutions that should provide a road map to follow so that we can have the best workforce in the world.”

The task force will launch in January with a hearing-style discussion to identify common problems across industries and explore solutions. The group will work with public- and private-sector representatives throughout 2014 to develop concrete proposals on how the private sector and government at all levels can work more effectively together to ensure that the United States has a globally competitive workforce. The task force also will deliver a detailed advocacy plan so that the NAM can work with governments to implement these proposals and develop the workforce that meets the needs of modern manufacturing.

Workforce development is one of the key challenges that manufacturers face to remain competitive in today’s global economy, and this year’s FABTECH exhibition in Chicago will examine this issue with a special panel discussion. FABTECH, North America’s largest metalforming, fabricating, welding and finishing event, will be held at McCormick Place in Chicago from 18-21 November 2013. ■
