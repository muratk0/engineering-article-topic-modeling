# TESTING TIMES

[TARİH: 01.08.2012 ISMR]

Trends, users and growth prospects in test and measurement markets are highlighted

The vision measuring machine market rebounded strongly in 2010 caused by the revival of the manufacturing and automotive sectors in North America and Europe and continued strongly in 2011 with unit sales of 8,000 to 10,000. In between 2010- 2011, most participants witnessed a marked increase in sales, and many reported up to 60 to 70 per cent more than average sales.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan, "Analysis of the Vision Measuring Machines Market", finds that the market earned revenues of US$ 346.6 million in 2011 and estimates this to reach US$ 456.7 million in 2016. In this research, Frost & Sullivan's analysts examine the following markets: measuring microscopes, profile projectors and multi-sensor systems.

## Measurement in focus

The outlook for every market segment is positive, said the company, especially for multisensor systems which currently account for 69.9 per cent of total sales. The escalating competition among end users has made them keen to offer the best quality of products, creating a significant market for advanced and expensive metrology equipment.

"The major end users of non-contact metrology systems include the electronic and industrial manufacturing segments," says Frost & Sullivan research associate, Vigneshwaran Shanmugam. Acknowledging the demand for sophisticated equipment, vision measuring machine manufacturers need to keep raising the bar on technology development. Currently, there is minimal technological progress relating to hardware with most changes restricted to aspects of specification such as accuracy, resolution and speed of measurement, said the company.

On the other hand, software manufacturers are making rapid strides in introducing novel algorithms to their solutions portfolio for better speed and performance. To work around this issue, product vendors could collaborate with customers by tailoring their offerings to suit their needs.

"Due to the long shelf-life of the majority of these products, new equipment purchases are few and far between," notes Shanmugam. "Providing software upgrades with sizeable functional upgrades and working closely with end users to develop customised solutions products will help improve sales."

## Materials testing

The material testing market is currently undergoing a period of recovery and stabilisation after significant losses sustained during the economic downturn. The recovery of its key traditional end-user industries has been pivotal in driving this revival.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan, "Analysis of the Material Testing Equipment Market", finds that the market earned revenues of US$ 495.6 million in 2010 and estimates this to reach US$ 591.1million in 2015.

Market recovery has been fuelled by demand from traditional end-user markets. Although the impact of the downturn continues to linger, the recovery has been remarkable, says the company.

"2009 was one of the most difficult periods for the material testing market," explained Frost & Sullivan programme manager, Vijay Mathew. "However, demand from traditionally strong users of material testing equipment such as automotive, aerospace, primary metals and plastics have helped the industry to bounce back."

In addition to traditionally large end-user sectors, novel applications in the fields of biomedical, composites, medical devices and nanotechnology are expected to boost the market in the coming years.

While new opportunities are emerging, a key challenge remains the presence of established participants in the market. This has limited the business opportunities for smaller companies.
