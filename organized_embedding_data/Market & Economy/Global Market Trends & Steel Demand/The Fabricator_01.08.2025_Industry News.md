Travelers releases 2025 Injury Impact Report

Workers’ compensation insurer The Travelers Companies Inc. has published its 2025 Injury Impact Report, which compares workers’ compensation data from the five years leading up to the COVID-19 pandemic with the next five years. The analysis of more than 2.6 million claims submitted during that time found that while the number of workplace injuries overall continues to decline, the costs associated with them are climbing.

"Over the past decade, we’ve seen three trends intensify: increasing retirement ages, ongoing employee turnover, and longer injury recovery times," said Rich Ives, senior vice president of business insurance claim. "Our aim with this report is to provide employers with insights on these dynamics that are contributing to growing claim severity so they can better navigate these workforce challenges, protect their employees, and keep their businesses running."

The report found that the frequency of workplace injuries overall has declined over the past decade. Travelers examined 1.2 million workers’ compensation claims received during the past five years, down from 1.4 million from 2015 through 2019.

There were many shifts in the workplace over the last 10 years, including continued job churn during and after the pandemic. This created a steady stream of new employees, who are among the most vulnerable to injury. The report found that employees in their first year on the job accounted for approximately 36% of injuries and 34% of overall claim costs during the last five years. This is an increase from the prior five years, when 34% of injuries and 32% of overall claim costs were attributed to new employees.

During the past five years, employees aged 50 or older made up 41% of the injured employee population, and those 60 and above represented 16%. This is up from 39% and 13%, respectively, when compared with data from 2015 through 2019. This trend is significant because older employees, while typically injured less frequently than their younger counterparts, tend to require longer recovery times and have more costly claims.

From 2020 through 2024, employees missed an average of 80 workdays per injury, an increase of more than seven days when compared with the previous five-year period. Injured employees 60 and older were out of work because of workplace injuries for nearly 97 days, almost 17 more days than the overall average and an increase of 14 days from pre-pandemic years.

ESAB Corp. to acquire EWM GmbH

ESAB Corp., a focused industrial compounder in North Bethesda, Md., has signed a definitive agreement to acquire EWM GmbH, a Germany-based provider of heavy industrial arc welding equipment and advanced automation.

Brighton’s parent company acquires König + Co. GmbH

Cincinnati-based cold-form tank head manufacturer Brighton Tru-Edge has announced that its parent company, HBH Holdings Inc., has acquired Germany-based hotform tank head producer König + Co. GmbH.

Brighton Tru-Edge and König + Co. will remain separate operating entities and brands, but collectively they become the largest cold- and hot-form tank head manufacturer in the world, according to Brighton.

Otto Aviation announces new Florida manufacturing facility, headquarters

Otto Aviation has announced plans to establish an advanced manufacturing and production facility at Cecil Airport in Jacksonville, Fla. The plant, which will occupy 80 to 100 acres of land at the airport, will serve as the primary site for the final assembly of the Phantom 3500 next-generation business jet.

Production of flight test vehicles is slated to start in 2026. The project is expected to create hundreds of manufacturing and engineering jobs.

Otto plans to begin operations in Hangar 825, originally built by the U.S. Navy for aircraft fleet operations, and will subsequently develop a dedicated manufacturing plant to produce the Phantom 3500. The company’s headquarters also will relocate to Jacksonville. It plans to begin flight tests by early 2027 and aims to achieve certification and entry into service in 2030.

Online fabricator provides instant quotes for welded sheet metal assemblies

RMFG, Fort Worth, Texas, an online metal fabricator offering laser cutting, CNC bending, tapping, and welding services, has unveiled an instant-quote service for custom welded sheet metal parts. The platform enables engineers and procurement teams to upload STEP files, annotate weld locations, and receive pricing in seconds, with no minimum order required, the company states.

The service debuts with MIG welding capabilities and supports quick-turn production with lead times as fast as 10 days. All parts are welded by certified professionals using precision fixture tables and the company’s proprietary quoting and workflow software. TIG welding support is expected to launch soon.

Vaughn Industries to build 120,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility

Vaughn Industries has announced plans for a new 120,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility in Carey, Ohio. The company is a contractor offering multiple skilled trades in electrical, technology, mechanical, HVAC, high-voltage substation, high-voltage transmission and distribution, and renewable energy.

The nearly $18.5 million facility will bring together the company’s virtual design and construction and fabrication processes under one roof. The expansion is expected to create 126 jobs.

"We have outgrown our current facilities and need to expand our manufacturing facilities to maintain our current business as well as the future growth of our company," said John Barnes, business development manager. "The open layout of the new manufacturing facility is critical to ensure efficiency and logistics in all of our fabrication processes."

According to Barnes, the new facility will allow for added machinery such as a coil line, spiral duct machine, waterjet table, and unistrut extruder. This provides the opportunity to manufacture products onsite rather than purchasing from outside Ohio, Barnes said.

The company expects to begin operations in Q2 2026.
