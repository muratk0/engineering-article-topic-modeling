# SKILLED WORKERS NEEDED FOR CANADA

[TARİH: 01.11.2011 ISMR]

CANADA’S STEEL INDUSTRY will need between 5,000 and 10,800 skilled tradespersons, and as many as 29,000 new workers overall, in the coming five years says a report released by the Canadian Steel Trade and Employment Congress (CSTEC). The CSTEC report, 'Human Resources in the Canadian Steel Sector', recommends that industry and government work together to bolster human resources planning at the sector level, ensuring that individual companies have the tools they need to build a strong training culture in their workplaces.

“The Canadian steel sector has a solid future. CSTEC’s report emphasises new opportunities for the industry with thousands of good, well-paying jobs in the coming years,” said Ron Watkins, President of the Canadian Steel Producers Association. “These jobs are a part of the modern steel industry that requires high-tech skills and advanced training.”

The CSPA is the national voice of Canada's C$12-14 billion steel and steel pipe and tube industry, employing some 25,000 people with steel mills in five provinces. In the manufacturing supply chain, the country’s steel companies directly employ 25,000 people and support another 100,000 Canadian jobs. For the future, there is the dual need to replace retirees from the existing workforce and to invest in developing the skilled workforce capable of meeting the sophisticated needs of advanced manufacturing.

The steel industry also has a vital role to play in the emerging green economy. Since 1990, the industry has slashed its energy consumption by 25 per cent, streamlined its operations to cut greenhouse gas emissions by 17 per cent and produces the most recycled material in the world.

Key recommendations in the CSTEC report include benchmarking training and development; establishing workplace advisory committees on training and development; implementing a career information strategy; developing national strategies for skilled trades and steel technologists; expanding its essential skills training policy and enhancing energy conservation in production. TRUMPF INSTALLS 20,000TH CO2 LASER

THE TRUMPF GROUP has marked another milestone in its history with the installation of its 20,000th TruFlow laser. The 7kW high-powered CO2 laser has been integrated in a TruLaser Cell 1100 at the Fischer Group in Germany where it will be continuously welding tubes and profiles.

“One of the most significant features of TruFlow lasers is their wear-free, zero-maintenance system of gas circulation using magnetically-suspended turbo blowers combined with the high frequency excitation of the laser gas. This solution minimises maintenance and operating costs while maximising availability,” TRUMPF told ISMR.

Despite a degree of pessimism in the laser industry, TRUMPF continues to believe in the future of CO2 lasers as Dr.-Ing. Christian Schmitz, who heads up laser development and production at TRUMPF, explains: “The idea that CO2 lasers are somehow on the way out is a simple misconception.”

“The wavelength of CO2 lasers continues to offer major benefits for certain applications. It is still the best option for customers who are looking to process the full spectrum of sheet thicknesses, and remains the number one choice for welding thick plates,” he continued.

TRUMPF supplies TruFlow lasers with output powers ranging from 700 to 20,000 Watts. The open interface architecture of the laser control system makes it easier to integrate the laser within a higher-level control system, so that it can be used in challenging environments.
