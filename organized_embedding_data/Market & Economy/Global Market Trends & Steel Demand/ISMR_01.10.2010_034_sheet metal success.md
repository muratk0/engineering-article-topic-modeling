# SHEET METAL SUCCESS

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

It is Mumbai at two in the afternoon. The glistening façade of a high-rise mirrors the shanties of the slums. In the midst of it all is a jumble of people, vehicles and dust. A hand extends from the rear of a dark luxury sedan to pass a coin to a street vendor. Countless wildly honking cars, three-wheeled tuk-tuks, motorcycles bearing small families, brightly coloured Tata trucks and battered buses vie for the pole position at a crossing. They all want to move along as fast as possible. A spirit of new departures is in the air.

Many overseas companies are also hoping to claim a good starting position in one of the world’s largest growth markets. They are flocking to the subcontinent having invested more than thirty billion US dollars in the 2008/2009 fiscal year. The prospects are too tempting to ignore. At an average growth rate of about nine per cent, India is on the road to transform itself into a powerhouse of the global economy. The local economy felt the effects of the global financial crisis for only a short period of time and once again is expanding at a rate of eight per cent. By the middle of this century, the country will have climbed to become the third largest economic power.

## SHEET METAL FABRICATION

“In this vibrant business environment, sheet metal fabrication has become a booming industrial sector and the need for high quality sheet metal products is enormous,” reports Rajendra Kothari, managing director of Crish Metal Works. His sheet metal fabrication operation, serving numerous industries, offers the complete production solution, including powder coating for switchgear cabinets, covers, housings, claddings, consoles and noise abatement elements. With approximately 120 employees, the firm generated revenues of more than six million euros last year.

A strategic decision made in 2005 gave him new impetus. He relocated the firm to a new production site on the eastern edge of Mumbai and, since then, has continuously upgraded his equipment.

“I wanted the best CNC technology available. And there was simply no alternative to the TRUMPF punch presses,” said the graduate electronics engineer. “When my customers learned about my initial investment in 2005, orders demanding high quality production poured in — almost on their own.”

Two years later Rajendra Kothari purchased his second punch press, followed last year by a laser cutting system, all made by TRUMPF. At the same time, he set up a powder coating system and an assembly and packing department, achieved ISO 9000/9001 certification and successfully positioned himself as a specialist for complete solutions, following the ‘one-stop shop’ principle. His customer list reads like a Who’s Who of Indian industry. Awards for outstanding business achievements grace his office.

“Our prime selling point is our machinery. With that we were able to achieve significant growth even in the crisis years — 2008 and 2009,” the ambitious businessman reports. By 2020 he, as an export-oriented manufacturing specialist with his own product line, aims to generate a turnover of more than 70 million euros and is seeking joint venture partners.

Such goals appear to be thoroughly realistic in view of the country’s booming industries with their ravenous hunger for sheet metal components. The automotive market is just one example: India is already the fourth largest producer of automobiles and boasts a rapidly growing export share. The IBEF — India Brand Equity Foundation — reports 57 per cent export growth in 2008/2009 alone.

The Indian Tata Group has sparked an international sensation with its low-priced Nano model. Major manufacturers such as Toyota and Honda manufacture in India. Volkswagen, in conjunction with Suzuki, intends to grab a piece of the pie.

Steel consumption is a further growth index and the figures are considerable: 53 million tons in 2009 while forecasts for the next five years predict an average growth rate of 16 per cent. Ship and aircraft construction, civil engineering, heating and climate control, and power generating technologies are driving consumption upward.

According to IBEF estimates, the Government of India is also planning to invest US$ 180 billion in the next five years to bring the country’s infrastructure up to par. A growing middle class, now numbering about 300 million, has also developed a great appetite for consumer and luxury goods. Those are grand prospects for industry-independent job shops such as Crish Metal Works.

## LEVERAGING GROWTH INDUSTRIES

But sheet metal fabricators serving a specific industry have also been dealt a good hand. It was in 2008 that Taikisha Engineering set up its own sheet metal fabrication facility at the edge of the industrial center of Pune, just three hours by car, along a new road, from Mumbai. These new buildings are situated in a verdant area, far from the chaos of the city.

“The low dust levels in the air have a beneficial effect on production quality,” explains Deputy CEO A.S. Prabhu about the lavish landscaping around the work halls, which features banana plants and rose beds. It was only in mid-2008 that this Japanese-Indian joint venture — specialising in the engineering and manufacture of turnkey, robot-equipped painting lines, command and control units, and monitoring systems for the automotive industry — put its own sheet metal manufacturing operations into service. Before, all the sheet metal components had been bought in.

“The quality standard at Indian job shops is rising but in many cases it has not yet reached the level we’d like to see,” explained Vice President H.N. Makwana. His father, N.A. Makwana, who manages Taikisha Engineering India, explained the strategic background.

“We want to apply our vehicle engineering expertise in other growth industries in India — such as railway rolling stock, aviation engineering and subway construction. Taikisha India holds to international production standards when manufacturing painting systems and has become certified for these target sectors. To let us guarantee top quality to our customers, we are now manufacturing all the sheet metal parts ourselves.”

The plant does not just produce sheet metal components for systems destined for the Indian market. It has also positioned itself as an international supplier within the global Taikisha Corporation. Idle capacities are used for job shop orders.

“We profit from site advantages such as a superbly skilled labour force, letting us deliver high quality at an affordable price. Thus, we are taken seriously as a competitor on the world market,” says A.S. Prabhu.
