# CECIMO announces positive forecasts for 2017

[TARİH: 01.08.2017 ISMR]

Filip Geerts, CECIMO Director General

THE CECIMO GENERAL Assembly that took place in Rotterdam, this June, has confirmed the following:

Based on solid booking numbers, European production of machine tools is set to grow by almost 4% in 2017 and exceed a volume of 25 billion euros.

European businesses in the MT (Machine Tool) sector coped well within the negative international environment. Their exports suffered much less than global ones, and are expected to accelerate in 2017.

Industrial data is context-sensitive and often contains trade secrets. Policy-makers should refrain from designing strict regulations on access to and sharing of industrial data.

Bilateral contracts between businesses work sufficiently well for now. Contracts provide the legal certainty and freedom to companies who are exchanging data.

"According to preliminary forecasts, machine tool production in Europe is projected to grow at an annual rate of close to 4% and recorded more than 25 billion euros in 2017, after exceeding 24.3 billion euros last year," commented CECIMO, the European association representing the common interests of machine tool industries globally and at an EU level. It brings together 15 national associations of machine tool builders.

"Although exports from the European machine tool industry were 3% lower in 2016, CECIMO members were outperforming internationally as the global MT trade decreased by around 7%. Global machine tool consumption contracted by 6-8% in 2016. CECIMO countries exported machine tools worth around 18.3 billion euros in 2016, accounting for the clear majority (96%) of the European exports in the sector and for almost one of every two machine tools sold in overseas markets. It is difficult to make a forecast for 2017, as plans for international trade barriers seem to emerge. However, if the business climate remains unchanged, CECIMO exports are likely to grow to a level of 19 billion euros."

Amongst the reasons for this positive trend, Dr. Frank Brinken, Chairman of the CECIMO Economic Committee and Vice-Chairman of Starrag Holding AG, pointed out that "digitisation and future proven high-quality standards increase the attractiveness of the European MT portfolio in foreign markets."

On the domestic European side, modernisation of production equipment and previously postponed investments for capital goods will probably contribute to a rise in machine tool sales, said CECIMO. It expects European consumption to pass 18 billion euros in 2017 and retain a solid market share in global MT consumption of above 26%. Such an increment relates to an annual market expansion of nearly 3.5 per cent.

However, warned CECIMO, levels of investment, necessary to modernise manufacturing with digital-ready equipment, are still insufficient. However, it added, the growing acceptance and speed of digitisation in the industry support the economic recovery in Europe, while political stimuli and a positive sentiment among European businesses should drive investment upwards in the MT sector.

"We are glad to see that European policy-makers put connectivity, in an industrial context, at the forefront and that they foresee a dialogue with trade associations for an EU framework on machine-generated data. Yet, we remind policy-makers that industrial data mirrors processes, products and systems, so it is context-sensitive and often contains trade secrets. The goal is not to implement new premature rules, but rather to increase our companies' technical and organisational capacity. Together with policy-makers, we should generate a common understanding fit for the digital era," said Filip Geerts, CECIMO Director General.

"Businesses should negotiate and decide freely the extent of the industrial data to be shared," added Luigi Galdabini, CECIMO President. Policy-makers should rather provide long-term certainty, raise awareness of new business models and support high-speed internet across Europe, with a focus on SMEs.
