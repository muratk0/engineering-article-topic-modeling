# CEOs welcome robotics revolution

[TARİH: 01.05.2016 ISMR]

“The US economy is one of the front-runners in the global automation race. By 2018, the number of industrial robots sold to 2018 will, on average, rise by at least five per cent per annum, to a new record of 31,000 units (2014: 26,202). About half of these will be installed by car makers and their suppliers,” continued the IFR, citing the 2015 World Robot Statistics that it issues.

“Viewed according to robotic density - meaning the number of industrial robots per 10,000 employees - the US automotive industry, with 1,141 units, already ranks third in the world's national economies after Germany (1,149 units) and Japan (1,414 units).”

In the most recently published annual economic report of the US President to Congress, the White House underlines the central role played by robotics in the economic growth of the United States. Therefore as far as technology and innovation are concerned, it is the increased deployment of robots – alongside digital communications technology – that will prove to be the decisive factor of success in the robust growth of production in the USA. The economic experts see great potential in various economic sectors.

“The automobile industry is currently displaying the fastest rate of modernisation within NAFTA (the USA, Canada and Mexico). 55 per cent of the total demand for industrial robots comes from the auto sector alone. One fourth of these units is installed by carmakers – the greatest demand is, by far, found in the supply industry. The drive towards automation has been running at full pelt for five years now. This is enabling car manufacturers and suppliers in the US to supply the world's largest domestic market and to produce the greatest number of cars and light commercial vehicles after China. The USA is witnessing very intense investment in the technical renewal of its domestic manufacturing, aimed at improving competitiveness and partly at regaining capacities from abroad,” added the IFR.

The automotive supply industry is providing a strong impetus to the deployment of robotic technology in North America. Investments within NAFTA have increased by about 40 per cent every year since 2010, and are forecast to continue rising in coming years. The primary drivers of industrial robot investments among automotive suppliers are new quality standards, more energy-efficient production and new materials.

"Clearly the automotive industry continues to drive innovation in the automation arena and need for optimisation, quality, adaptability and flexibility,” said Joe Gemma, President of the International Federation of Robotics. "The need from OEMs has filtered down to the supplier base to work on improved quality, flexibility and process optimisation fostering the requirement for automation technologies to meet demands as well as quicker deliveries and adapting to a dynamic environment."

## CEO market forecasts

"In the age of Industry 4.0, the automotive industry is taking a leading role when it comes to flexible and state-of-the-art automation solutions and the direct collaboration between humans and robots,” said Stefan Lampa, President and CEO, KUKA Roboter.

"Moreover, the demand for automation solutions is constantly growing in nearly all sectors. New markets are evolving where automation has not been a major topic until now. A particularly important market for us is the electronics sector: the electronics industry is confronted with an increasing number of new products, an accelerating pace of development and short product life cycles. Here too, flexibility is the ‘be-all’ and ‘end-all’ in the industry."

"The growth in the world robotics market is being driven forward at a rapid pace,” added Olaf C. Gehrels, CEO of FANUC Europe Corporation. "Along the way, two technological milestones have been set: collaborative robots, based upon standard robots equipped with tested and proven controllers, have demonstrated their readiness for routine use in the industrial environment and the introduction of high-payload robots, capable of handling items weighing up to 2,300kg, now allows the employment of totally new manufacturing concepts."

Investment in robotics continues to grow – venture capital companies committed around US$ 172 million in 2013 while technology leaders are already buying into robotics technology. Consequently, CEOs around the world are positively welcoming this technological revolution, according to PwC’s latest CEO Pulse on robotics, which explores CEOs’ current perceptions of how robotics in the workplace is shaping their businesses today and in the near future. Cost-effective and productive: CEOs agree that robotics will make their companies more efficient, with up to 94% of those who have already adopted robotics claiming it has increased productivity in their business. Driver of innovation: In addition, 64% of CEOs are confident that robotics will bring new innovations to their business models. Growing role of robotics in IT, sales and consumer service: While manufacturing and production are the areas where most CEOs have traditionally adopted robotics, they have identified IT, sales and customer service as three key areas that will also feel the robotic touch in the near future.

In addition, polled CEOs also expect that, over the next five years, almost a fifth of their workforce tasks will have an element of robotics to them. As the ability and role of robotics grows, companies will be also forced to confront a series of challenges, including:

• The big ‘employment debate’: While a general fear persists as to the extent to which robotics and computerisation will impact the workforce, 69% of CEOs polled disagreed that robotics will make some high skilled jobs obsolete. Instead, there is a wider belief that robotics will help create new and exciting opportunities for their workers by automating repetitive tasks.

• New skills: As robotics becomes more integrated throughout the business, employers are going to have to invest in reskilling many parts of the workforce to adapt to working alongside and managing robotic processes and even colleagues.

• Insuring against risk and protecting intellectual property: As companies start to automate processes and/or introduce more artificial intelligence into white collar ‘thinking’ tasks (like knowledge management, analytics, business services and law), this new automated knowledge workforce will pose real problems for companies in terms of insuring against risk (for instance software crashes, cyberattacks or systemic fragility) and protecting intellectual property.

## GOING WITH THE FLOW

We highlight a selection of new and innovative waterjet technologies for sheet metal manufacturers

Waterjet technology uses a thin stream of water traveling at extremely rapid speeds and under very high pressure (2000 - 6000 bar) to “erode” the material being cut. A major advantage of waterjet technology is its ability to cut virtually any material as well as fibre-reinforced materials, reflective materials, uneven surfaces and stacked layers of different materials. It does not superheat the area adjacent to the cut, so material integrity is left intact. No frayed edges or burrs are left after cutting, producing excellent material edge quality and largely eliminating the need for secondary finishing processes.

Waterjet is considered a “green” technology – it produces no hazardous waste, reducing waste disposal costs. Large pieces of reusable scrap material can be cut which might have been lost using traditional cutting methods. Parts can also be closely nested to maximize material use, and the waterjet saves material by creating very little kerf. Many job shops add waterjets to their operations alongside other cutting technologies such as EDM, laser, milling and plasma.

New waterjet innovations Waterjet cutting is especially advantageous for cutting complex shapes. Materials can be cut into almost any shape. Sharp corners, bevels, pierce holes and shapes with minimal inner radii are all possible. Stacking, nesting and tabbing optimises material and can significantly reduce cutting times. Below is a selection of the latest new and innovative waterjet technologies for sheet metal manufacturers.

Swiss manufacturer, Bystronic, has introduced a twin-head ByJet Flex for waterjet cutting, which can accept up to two newly developed, 3D cutting heads for producing bevelled edges on components. Upgrading from 2D to 3D technology takes less than an hour, said the manufacturer.

“The ByJet Flex platform can be integrated into a wide variety of production environments and used for both 2D and 3D applications, operating with two CNC heads for straight or bevelled cutting. The basic version is equipped with all prerequisites that allow it to be upgraded with functions such as 3D cutting heads, drill spindles, automatic parts holding during cutting (FixMaster), precise positioning of the cutting head (PositionPointer) and intelligent fine adjustment for rapid, multi-head cutting (PrecisionAligner),” Bystronic told ISMR.

Eckert’s COMBO waterjet cutting machine uses two modern cut technologies - plasma and water. The system allows up to ten times higher cutting speeds and a 70% reduction in costs, according to the manufacturer. ECKCAM Professional Plus is modern software for design offices which allows users to choose the preferred cutting technology and decide which elements will be cut by water technology and which by plasma.

Eckert’s OPAL waterjet is available in working widths from 1000 to 4500mm and working lengths from 1000 to 10000mm (pressure of 4130 bar). Optional use of the PRO-X 3D cutting head widens the design capability of the machine for 3D cutting operations.

Flow International Corporation has launched a new website, available in 14 languages, at www.flowwaterjet.com. Flow offers a comprehensive line of Ultra High-Pressure (UHP) abrasive waterjet system solutions for manufacturers of all sizes and budgets. It develops 5- to 11-axis UHP abrasive waterjet cutting systems as well as other multi-process applications such as inspection, measurement, touch probe/verification, precision routing and drilling in sizes of up to 20ft x 50ft x 5ft.

The company brought its waterjet software, FlowXpert 2015, to FABTECH in November last year. It also featured live cutting demonstrations on its Mach 4 and Mach 2 line of waterjets. At FABTECH, the Mach 4 combined Flow’s HyperJet® pump, rated at 94,000 psi and Dynamic Waterjet® XD, highprecision flat stock and 3D cutting technology.

In August 2015, the company announced the grand opening of the new Flow Waterjet Customer Technology Centre in Kent, Washington (USA).

At last November’s FABTECH, Hypertherm launched a new waterjet abrasive management system comprised of two products—the PowerDredge™ abrasive removal unit and its EcoSift™ abrasive recycling unit. The PowerDredge increases waterjet productivity and profitability by automating costly abrasive clean-outs. This allows companies to reduce both labour costs and system downtime.

“The EcoSift helps companies to recycle their used abrasive. Testing and microscopic analysis shows that up to 60 per cent of abrasive sitting at the bottom of a waterjet table actually meets the specifications for brand new abrasive and can be re-used without impacting cut quality. As up to 60 per cent of waterjet operating costs are attributed to the purchase of new abrasive, this can lead to significant cost savings for companies,” the manufacturer told ISMR.

Its HyPrecision Waterjet, a complete line of waterjet products, includes pumps ranging from 15 to 150 horsepower, precision cutting heads and abrasive delivery equipment. The systems feature Advanced Intensifier Technology to extend maintenance intervals, reduce maintenance time and maximise performance. In April 2016, Hypertherm was selected as a ‘2016 World’s Most Ethical Company®’ by the Ethisphere Institute for a second year.

Established in 1984, Jet Edge is a global designer and manufacturer of waterjet systems for precision cutting, surface preparation and coating removal. Its EDGE X-5® 5-axis waterjet system is designed and built for heavy duty industrial use in waterjet shops. The workhorse waterjet system cuts precise sophisticated 3D parts such as impellers and bevels up to 50°. An optional second cutting head can be added to double productivity. In February 2016, the manufacturer announced the launch of its new aftermarket waterjet parts division, Choice Waterjet Parts, Inc.

At last November’s FABTECH exhibition in Chicago, Jet Edge demonstrated live 5- axis abrasive waterjet cutting demonstrations on its X-Stream-powered EDGE X-5® 5-axis waterjet with Aquavision Di control and Closed Loop Filtration System. The waterjet manufacturer also showcased its new BOSS-CUTTER™ 3-axis waterjet system, its new 55,000 psi (3800 bar), 30HP iP60-30 waterjet intensifier pump and its X-Stream pressure intensifier pumps.

As a manufacturer of plasma and oxy-fuel cutting solutions, Kerf Developments has now extended its portfolio with the introduction of a new range of waterjet cutting machines. The Optima waterjet series has the option of a 3-, 4- or 5-axis cutting head. The Optima is offered with an X-axis of 2, 3 or 4m and a Y-axis span of 1-, 2-, 4- or 6m with bespoke dimensions available upon request. The Z-axis stroke enables cutting of material up to 180mm thick with precision levels of 0.1mm.

The PRO model from KMT Waterjet Systems’ new modular STREAMLINE SL-VI high-pressure pump series enables waterjet cutting at up to 6,200 bar. With a pump output of 125 HP, users can employ several cutting heads – this combination of extremely high pressure and powerful motor results in what KMT calls ‘the fastest waterjet cutting system in the world’. Waterjet cutting at 6,200 bar has many advantages – the most obvious is probably the higher cutting speed compared to conventional 4,000 bar applications. Depending on the material, the speed can be increased by up to 50% or more.

“Purchasing the pump was absolutely worth it. There are orders that we otherwise would never get. For example, we cut 5mm thin aluminium in 3-4 layers in continuous operation at 5,300 bar – with a 4,000 bar pump, which we run in continuous operation with 3,600 bar. For us, this means a speed increase of 50% compared to the smaller pump,” said Jochen Schäfer, owner and managing director of the company.
