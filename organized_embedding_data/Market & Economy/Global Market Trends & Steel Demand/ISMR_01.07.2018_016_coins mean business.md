# Coins mean business

[TARİH: 01.08.2018 ISMR]

IN GERMANY, AUSTRIA and Switzerland, 89 per cent of the population used coins as a means of payment in the last 48 hours. This is the result of a survey contracted by press manufacturer Schuler, with more than five hundred participants. The percentage is the same in all three countries and is even slightly higher (90 per cent) among those of between 18 and 39 years of age.

"The fact that people in German-speaking countries do not want to eliminate euro and cent coins, and franks and rappen, as a means of payment is also demonstrated by another finding: 86 per cent of those surveyed think it is important to have coins, and in Switzerland it is 89 per cent. Of those surveyed, 88 per cent say cash gives them "a good feeling" and seven of ten (71 per cent) have a sense of security when paying with coins.

"New developments, such as the five-euro collector coin with a polymer ring minted with Schuler technology, offer a variety of methods for integrating additional easily identifiable security features. The third issue ("Subtropical Zone") appeared a few weeks ago, and two more are planned in the next two years. Such tri-material coins represent an innovation that almost half of the respondents (49 per cent) miss with the present metallic currency. Nevertheless, two-thirds (66 per cent) called it attractive."

Schuler is a leading supplier of systems/presses for the production of coins. A press from the company is used in the manufacturing process of almost every currency worldwide, it told /SMR. The results of the survey were compiled by the Conversio market research company on the basis of five- to seven-minute telephone interviews from December 2017 to January 2018.
