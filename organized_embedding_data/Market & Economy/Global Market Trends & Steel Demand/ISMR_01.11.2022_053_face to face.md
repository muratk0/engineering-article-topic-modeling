## FACE TO FACE

Grégory Conraud, AMB Picot's owner and CEO, met ISMR at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition to unveil new technology initiatives in roll-bending markets, as well as an innovative new venture in the straightening market.

ISMR: Which new technologies are you showcasing at EuroBLECH in Hanover this year?

Grégory Conraud (GC): Over the years, we have expanded our range to adapt to the technical requirements of our customers. Our roll-benders can incorporate two, three or four rolls. All of them are equipped with digital control systems and software that enable the operator to achieve very precise rolling levels.

At EuroBLECH this year, we are showcasing our 100% electric roll-bending machine, specialising in thin sheet-metal processing. It is the world premiere of this system which is optimised for energy consumption, rolling accuracy and simplicity of maintenance. It is equipped with a touch-screen industrial PC controller and the latest version (V4) of the Easyroll© rolling assistance software.

Easyroll software

The EasyRoll package contains five main modules that help the user learn to use the machine very quickly via an ergonomic interface.

■ Touchscreen console, with CNC electronic control and a roller control interface developed by Picot.
■ Manual mode with display of rollbending diameter.
■ Automatic/program mode.
■ Material calibration mode for precise calculation of bending dimensions.
■ Program generation.
■ Capacity checking module.
■ Module to calculate roll inclination/cone forming.
■ Maintenance (online option).

The consoles are designed for plug & play operation so that no action is required on the machine.

The CNC rolling assistant, EasyRoll, includes five major modules that enable the user to become very easily familiar with the Picot three-roll bending machine through an ergonomic interface. This means intuitive and efficient rolling that will accommodate both experienced and unexperienced users. Our new range handles 1-5mm material which ensures up to 80% savings in energy consumption.

Version 4 of this patented software, launched in June 2022, uses artificial intelligence to provide powerful assistance to the user by calculating any necessary corrections of the rolling program. This feature, based on the effectively measured rolled profile entered by the operator, automatically updates the program and fine-tunes the dedicated material database.

Easyroll is now fully integrated into all our machines (from smallest to largest). We are now working on the V4 model with AI (which will be ready by the end of this year). The new version V5 (with more ergonomy and new program technology) will be ready in the second quarter of next year.

At the FABTECH exhibition in Atlanta, USA, this year, we will showcase our digitalisation capabilities by operating a roll-bending machine, based at our dealer's showroom, from our booth at FABTECH.

The machine software can be controlled through a tablet.

ISMR: What other recent investments have you made? What is your strategy for the future?

GC: We strengthened the team recently with a new technical manager, a young engineer, who has now come on board. We have also increased our office space and invested around 800,000 euros in new machine tools for our own production (turning, punching and straightening machines).

This investment is also linked to an exciting new cooperative initiative for us. We formed a Joint Venture with Lisse to develop 'EASYFLAT' straightening machines. It is bringing its experience in straightening and flattening machines, while we contribute our experience in software development; standards; machine design, assembly and distribution.

In the next couple of years, we aim to own our own factory for EASYFLAT production. We believe that the straightening market will grow and has a lot of potential. There are two technologies: levelling (the Advantage machine) and straightening (the Rapide machine) from coil to address material deformations after laser cutting and before polishing etc. Large companies will increasingly need straighteners and levellers in a market where there are only a handful of major players.

We have resized the machines and used electric-drive technology to make them more energy-efficient. The straighteners of 1-15mm (1.5m machine as standard) and there are three machine sizes. They follow the same principle as the roll-bending machines, using lots of smaller rollers to flatten the material.

ISMR: Apart from these new technologies and investments, what else would you count among your key achievements this year?

GC: The COVID-19 pandemic has been a challenge to navigate but, despite this, our business has been very good. It is not the first global crisis we have had to navigate. I bought the business in September 2008, just a week before the financial crisis.... Every crisis focuses the mind on what really matters and makes us think about our processes and ways in which we could improve our systems and services. It keeps us from being complacent. We decided to review our product portfolio; our communication policy; our position in the market; investment plans etc. during the pandemic. We are also finalising our digitalisation plan.

I am quite happy with the supply strategy that we have followed for the past 20 years. We have a strong supply base in France and across Europe. This stood us in good stead during the pandemic and secured our supply, making us more flexible.

When the pandemic struck, our sales were split 50% from overseas and 50% from France. We then sold only to French manufacturers during the pandemic and doubled our sales figures. We are now growing at 15% per year. The French government's tax incentive programmes for capital equipment acquisitions also played a part in this.

ISMR: Which trends and movements do you see in global roll-bending markets?

GC: I see a growing trend towards remote diagnostics and real fabrication support. It suits customers and suppliers. In 30 minutes, for example, you can log into a machine, see what is happening, consult remotely with the customers and quickly diagnose and remedy any operational fabrication issues. It reduces any downtime and keeps machines running.

Roll bending is a complex process which requires a great deal of expertise so, in a climate of worker shortages, we have tried to improve and simplify the operator experience for our customers. Our challenge is to make an unexperienced operator proficient on our machines. It is why we focus and invest so much on simulation; updated algorithms; new software versions and increased ergonomy. Improvements must always have a practical focus. The new features that we develop are geared towards improving our customers' processes.

It is easier for us, as a small and agile company, to invest in making this process easier for customers. We test a lot, fix any bugs that arise and are not afraid of making mistakes. All our customers receive new software versions for free. Our focus is always on continuous improvement.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
