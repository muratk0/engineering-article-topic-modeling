# China drives aviation growth

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

CHINA’S aviation market, one of the world’s fastest growing, is going through dramatic changes. Regulatory and policy reforms, low-cost carrier (LCC) and other innovative business models, new technology aircraft, and evolving consumer behaviours are driving airlines to launch additional direct flights and develop more point-to-point networks.

In its China forecast, Boeing projects that the current growth trend will continue over the next 20 years, with passenger traffic increasing 6.9 per cent and air cargo traffic increasing 6.7 per cent annually. The majority of the growth, approximately 65 per cent, will be within China. About 16 per cent of the growth will be international traffic to destinations within the Asia Pacific region. The remaining 19 per cent will be long-haul international traffic. To support this growth in demand, China will need 6,020 new aircraft valued at US$ 870 billion.

“Over the past 20 years, airlines in North America and other aviation markets have moved from flying widebody aircraft to flying single-aisle aircraft on domestic routes. In 1993, widebody aircraft supplied approximately 20 per cent of capacity in North America. Today, that number has dwindled to 5 per cent. We have also seen this trend in China, where almost 30 per cent of capacity was on widebody aircraft in 1993, compared with 9 per cent today,” Boeing explained.

“Single-aisle aircraft, such as the 737-800, provide the efficiency and network flexibility airlines need to be competitive in short-to-medium-haul markets, where quick turnaround and airplane utilisation are essential. LCCs and new entrants will stimulate traffic growth in China, as they have around the world. The LCC business model depends heavily on passenger demand for point-to-point service, which avoids connections at hubs and shortens travel time. Point-to-point service will help alleviate congestion at major hubs, such as Beijing, Shanghai and Guangzhou. New LCCs, coupled with increased growth in established airlines, will drive a need for 4,340 new single-aisle aircraft,” added Boeing.

New-technology widebody aircraft, such as the 787 and 777, are helping Chinese airlines to expand their global networks and compete more effectively with international carriers. In 2013, Chinese airlines opened 10 new long-haul markets. Over the next 20 years, this expansion is expected to continue as traffic to Europe grows 6.1 per cent; to North America, 6.3 per cent; to Oceania, 6.6 per cent and to Africa, 7.4 per cent. China will need 1,480 new widebody aircraft to support this market growth, according to Boeing. ■
