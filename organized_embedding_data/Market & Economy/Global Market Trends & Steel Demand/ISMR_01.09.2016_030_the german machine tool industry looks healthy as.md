# The German machine tool industry looks healthy as major projects boost mid-year figures.

[TARİH: 01.09.2016 ISMR]

It ranks among the five largest specialist groupings in the mechanical engineering sector. It provides production technology for metalworking applications in all branches of industry, and makes a crucial contribution towards innovation and enhanced productivity in the industrial sector as a whole.

Because of its key role in industrial production, its development is an important indicator for the economic dynamism of the industrial sector. In 2015, with around 68,500 employees (annual average for 2015, firms with more than 50 staff), the sector produced machines and services worth around 15.1 billion euros.

## Order bookings in focus

"In the second quarter of 2016, order bookings in the German machine tool industry rose by 16 per cent compared to the preceding year's equivalent period. Domestic orders were up by 19 per cent, while demand from abroad increased by 14 per cent. The Eurozone contributed growth of 37 per cent, and non-Eurozone nations [registered] a 10 per cent rise in orders. In the year's first half, order bookings increased by a substantial 12 per cent compared to the preceding year's figure. While domestic order bookings rose by 10 per cent, orders from abroad were also up, by 13 per cent," said VDW, the German Machine Tool Builders'Association.

"The year's second quarter, too, signals a green light for an overall rise in orders during the current year. Our business is running significantly better than we expected at the beginning of the year. The sector can point to a sound, balanced performance over the year's first half," added Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW.

The higher level of demand results primarily from good domestic business, and in particular from automotive projects in China and Mexico. The substantial rise in orders is therefore equally attributable to domestic orders and exports. For the first half of 2016, one-off effects are primarily responsible for the auspicious order situation. Abroad, machining centres and milling machines are benefiting, particularly from large-scale orders. All other metalcutting technologies, plus significant areas of forming technology, however, are performing less well in terms of orders from abroad, said the VDW.

On the domestic scene, in contrast, the picture is somewhat different. To quote Dr. Schäfer: "Metal-cutting is two per cent up, and the current situation gives [us] reason to hope that a broad spectrum of technologies can benefit from this."

Turnover in the first half of 2016 was slightly better than break-even.

"In view of the encouraging development of orders in the first six months of the year, and the range of order backlog now recovered to over seven months, we are anticipating a perceptible rise in turnover for the forthcoming period," explained Dr. Schäfer. This, he added, is conditional upon demand levels [also] remaining stable in the year's second half, particularly in the domestic market. Germany's industrial sector is in good shape, he said, and exhibits impressive pricing structures and competitive efficacy. The ifo Business Climate Index, for instance, contrary to analysts' expectations, recovered again in July.

"Despite Brexit, the business cycle is holding up well, and showing no significant signs of deterioration," explained Dr. Schäfer.

Employment remains at a high level. This is an indication that German machine tool manufacturers, despite cyclical fluctuations, are keeping themselves fit to face the challenges of the future.

"The sector is entering the year's second half in improved condition, and is continuing to invest in qualified staff," emphasised Dr. Schäfer. In May 2016, almost 69,000 people were employed in the machine tool industry – a slight increase of around one per cent over the preceding year.

## Opportunities and challenges

Germany boasts a sound tradition of key sheet metal, metalworking and machine tool trade shows, exhibitions, conferences and seminars. These include events such as GrindTec; Stanztec; EuroBLECH (see page 27); EMO; METAV; Blechexpo; AMB and LASYS.

Increased automation and the drive towards smart manufacturing, flexibility and digitisation are key trends in today's production environments. This new industrial age is widely expected to usher in new opportunities, as well as challenges, for manufacturing. As a result, collaboration between the technology and manufacturing sectors will gain in strategic importance, particularly in driving productivity, innovation and growth With increasing product variety and decreasing batch sizes, manufacturing processes in sheet metal working require highly efficient and flexible solutions.

Industry 4.0 and connected manufacturing is both a challenge and an opportunity for manufacturers. In July, the German Academic Society for Production Engineering (WGP) unveiled their position paper on Industry 4.0 to the public, and warned that only one in ten of Germany's manufacturing companies was intensively addressing this issue in an operative context. The WGP's stance is intended as a wake-up call for the SMEs, urging them to make a change.

Additive manufacturing is also a muchdiscussed topic among international production specialists. Traditional vendors are also positioning themselves in this field with hybrid systems. A recent study commissioned by the VDW shows that 3D printing in production operations is admittedly a niche phenomenon at present, but the sector is growing exponentially, and will continue to do so in the years ahead.

"So firms would do well to engage with the new production technology in mechanical engineering too, and to keep advances under observation in each specific sector," advises Carl Martin Welcker, General Commissioner of EMO Hanover 2017. But the technology should on no account be seen as a danger for the machine tool industry. On the contrary, he says, it's an opportunity. "Manufacturers should most definitely engage with new components for their own products. But they should also keep the market under observation with regard to additive processes and, in cases of doubt, factor in changes to their customers' products, and modify their corporate capabilities appropriately," he warned.

## An eye on the economy

According to the National Association of Manufacturers (NAM) in the US, manufacturers contributed US$ 2.17 trillion to the US economy in 2015. Manufacturing accounted for 12.1 per cent of gross domestic product in the US economy that year. There are currently 12.3 million manufacturing workers in the United States, accounting for 9 per cent of the workforce.

Over the past 25 years, US-manufactured goods exports have quadrupled. Foreign direct investment in manufacturing exceeded US$ 1 trillion for the first time ever in 2014. However, says the NAM, over the next decade, nearly 3.5 million manufacturing jobs are likely to be needed, and 2 million are expected to go unfilled due to the skills gap.
