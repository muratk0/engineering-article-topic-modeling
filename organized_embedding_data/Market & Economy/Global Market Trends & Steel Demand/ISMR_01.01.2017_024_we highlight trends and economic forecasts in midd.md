# We highlight trends and economic forecasts in Middle Eastern markets for sheet metal manufacturers

[TARİH: 01.01.2017 ISMR]

According to Expo Centre Sharjah (organiser of the SteelFab event), there are strong business drivers for growth in the Middle East, despite low oil revenues in the region.

"Manufacturing, construction, infrastructure and emerging industries, such as aerospace, are keeping the order books ticking for the regional steel fabrication and metal working industry. Continuous spending in the real estate and infrastructure sectors and long-term strategic spending are projected to keep the core sectors stable. Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Kuwait and Qatar are expected to award US$ 140 billion-160 billion in contracts per year until 2019, with cancellations, reprioritisations or deferrals unlikely since most states have commitments due to upcoming mega events," it explained.

"Major projects include Saudi Arabia's US$ 15 billion Al Mozaini Riyadh East Sub Centre, a US$ 8 billion project for metro lines B & C in Mecca, the UAE's US$ 32 billion extension of Al Maktoum International Airport, Dubai World Central and the US$ 20 billion Al Gharbia Chemicals Industrial City. Qatar's QRail, Kuwait's refinery projects and other projects related to World Expo 2020 and Fifa World Cup 2022 are also in the spotlight.

"The aerospace industry is booming in the UAE. Leveraging its airline and airport companies, the country has expanded into manufacturing and servicing airplane components, gaining partnerships with the world's biggest aviation players. Experts anticipate steel demand to increase up to 55 million tons during 2017, with Arab investments of US$ 100 billion."

Expo Centre Sharjah will host the 13th SteelFab, a leading steel-working exhibition in the region. Supported by the Sharjah Chamber of Commerce and Industry (SCCI), the event's 13th edition will take place from 16-19 January 2017. SteelFab will present modern technologies in steel forming, metal-cutting, finishing, milling and heavy machinery, as well as other related engineering applications. The event attracts world-wide attendance and participation of over 350 global firms and over 1000 leading international brands in the field.

## Oil price impact

Demand for machine tools is expected to increase significantly in the Gulf countries such as the United Arab Emirates, including Dubai. Saudi Arabia is one of the largest and most influential economies in the Middle East and has initiated a series of multi-billion-dollar projects. According to Euromonitor's 'Machine Tools in Saudi Arabia' report, the industry was set to see 4% CAGR over 2014-2020 to reach SR 140.6 million in the latter year, stimulated by strong construction activities and the expected rapid growth of the Saudi automotive industry. However, falling oil prices and austerity measures have hampered growth in 2016 and Focus Economics panelists expected to see the economy grow 1.1% in 2016, accelerating to 1.2% in 2017.

The Middle East constitutes 43 per cent of the world's total proved gas reserves. However, the Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) market in the Middle East is undergoing transformation, according to analyst Frost and Sullivan.

“As a hub of oil and gas, some Middle Eastern countries are experiencing low production levels of gas. This lack of availability of domestic gas has stimulated LNG imports in some Middle Eastern countries. With new LNG suppliers such as the United States and Australia, the Middle East is expected to sign long-term LNG contracts in the coming years to cater to the increasing gas demand. Qatar has been the largest LNG supplier worldwide. However, with new competition from the United States and Australia, Qatar's supremacy will be challenged," said the analyst.

"Most of the oil exporting economies in the Middle East and African region, barring Qatar and the United Arab Emirates due to their well-diversified economy, ended 2015 with low economic fundamentals, due to the drop in oil prices."

"In 2016, low oil prices and deepening conflicts continue to weigh mightily on the economies of the MENAP region. Oil exporters are facing another year of heavily reduced oil export revenues, and require ongoing fiscal consolidation and reforms to cope with these losses and to diversify their economies away from oil. Oil importers are experiencing uneven and fragile growth, and need to adjust to the challenges of spillovers from their oil-exporting neighbours and the threat from conflicts," added the International Monetary Fund in 2016.

"Economic activity in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) strengthened in Q3 on the back of accommodative monetary policies in most countries in the region, more stable financial and exchange rate markets, a slight increase in oil prices and stronger crude production," commented Focus Economics.

"According to our estimates, the region's aggregate GDP expanded 2.5% year-on-year in Q3, up from Q2's 2.1% growth. Nevertheless, the region appears to have entered Q4 on a weaker footing as an uncertain global outlook, doubts about the deal among the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries (OPEC) to cut crude production and harsh fiscal adjustment processes are exerting downward pressure on growth. In this context, the PMI for the non-hydrocarbon sector in the majority of MENA countries deteriorated in October."

The Egyptian Central Bank announced the free-floating of the EGP on 3 November 2016. This move prompted the IMF to approve a US$ 12 billion loan to the country and is expected to alleviate Egypt's financial needs.

## Iran in focus

"Iran, which is benefiting from its reintegration into the global economy and stronger oil exports, is expected to be the best performer in 2017. Of the rest of the major economies in the region, Egypt and Qatar will likely grow the fastest, with projected expansions of 3.5%," explained Focus Economics.

"With a gross domestic product of around $ 1.383 billion in 2016, Iran is already close to the GDP level of Turkey and, with a forecast annual growth of around 5%, will overtake it in the near future. Imports to Iran will increase by about 11% in the next five years. So now is exactly the right time to grab the opportunity to present and establish products and services in this growing market," the organiser of the IIMTE (Iranian International Machine Tools Exhibition) told ISMR. The event will take place from 28 February – 3 March 2017 at the Shahr-e-Aftab exhibition area in Tehran, Iran.

"After the end of economic sanctions, experts expect a doubling of Iran's trade volume in the next three years and, over the next five to seven years, another tripling."

A reported need for high-quality machine tools and production expertise is attracting sheet metal manufacturers to this Middle Eastern market. The three-day AMB Iran 2016 event was organized by the German Machine Tool Builders' Association (VDW) and Messe Stuttgart International, a leading German trade fair organizer. Germany is taking preliminary steps to regain its foothold in Iran, which was once a very rewarding market for German businesses.

The first edition of AMB in Iran was run from 30 May – 1 June 2016, attracting 111 exhibitors. The next event will be held in Tehran from 23- 26 May 2017.

In addition to modernising the oil industry, it is primarily the automobile construction, the chemical industry and the health sector that offer enormous market potential in terms of machine and plant engineering," said the AMB Iran organisers.

"Iranian imports are expected to increase by as much as US$ 20 billion between 2015 and 2017. It has been calculated that 20% of these will involve machinery and equipment. The number of registered vehicles is set to increase significantly. Currently, 90% of vehicles sold in the country are manufactured locally. The production capacity of Iranian manufacturers currently stands at 2 million vehicles (2014/15), but the actual utilisation is just 55.5% (1.11 million vehicles). The automobile parts industry in Iran is also very well developed, nevertheless there is still a high demand for imports. There are some 15,000 production sites in Iran. Yet, 60% of components for the automobile industry are still being imported."

In the wake of the Vienna Agreement, experts are expecting the revitalisation of the formerly good economic relations between Iran and the industrial nations – particularly in production engineering. Muchneeded modernisation measures are planned in the oil industry, which will provide new opportunities for the machine and plant construction sector. Also in focus are automotive production, the chemical industry and the expansion of renewable energies.
