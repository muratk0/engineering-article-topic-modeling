INDIA IN FOCUS ISMR SAYS: “Detailed analysis of key sheet metal sectors in India comes under the spotlight”

Core sectors such as construction and automotive will provide huge boosts to India’s economy and sheet metal consumption

According to industry analyst, Frost and Sullivan, India will be at the core of global business and commercial activity with projections for the largest working-age and youngest population in 2020. India will be home to 864 million middle class households by then, which will spur demand for white goods and compel companies to experiment with the low-cost segment.

India is set to become the second largest producer of crude steel by 2015 with expanding construction and infrastructure sectors set to further boost steel demand. Indian finished steel production in 2011-12 stood at 70.32 million MT and is forecast to reach 92.29 million MT in 2015-16.

Frost and Sullivan has highlighted trends and forecasts in major Indian sheet metal sectors, which ISMR has summarised here.

## Construction sector forecast

The Indian pre-engineered steel buildings (PEB) market is experiencing robust growth fuelled by infrastructure development and the increasing popularity of PEB systems in the industrial sector. Sectors such as automotive, power, logistics, pharma, FMCG and capital goods provide huge growth opportunity for PEB in India.

F&S’s ‘Analysis of the Indian Pre-Engineered Buildings Market’ finds that the market earned revenues of INR 52,970 million in 2012 and estimates this to reach INR 136,120 million in 2016, growing at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 26.6 per cent. The structural steel buildings market in India was around 5.0 to 5.5 million MT in 2011, of which around 9.5-10 per cent i.e. 537,176 MT accounted for the PEB market in India. It is expected that the market in terms of volumes will reach 900,000 MT by 2016, growing at a CAGR of 11.1 per cent.

"New potential application areas for PEB in the next few years will include power plant structures, factory buildings, commercial buildings (offices and retail malls), warehouses and metro stations," noted Frost and Sullivan's senior research analyst.

The Government of India has set a massive target for developing infrastructure in the 12th FYP (2012-17) at an estimated cost of INR 40,900,000 million, of which nearly 33-34 per cent have been allocated to the power sector, with an additional target of 90,000 MW. Currently the thermal power capacity stands at 140,976 MW, with the proposed capacity addition of 50,000 MW during the 12th FYP and will provide huge growth opportunity for PEB in this sector.

The commercial building sector in India is estimated to reach a target of 20.44 billion square feet by 2030 from 7 billion square feet in 2010.

With the onset and speedy progress of the Metro rail system in the next ten years, the scope of PEB is immense. The heightened emphasis on green building construction is also poised to push market expansion. For instance, to increase energy efficiency, many PEB manufacturers have developed high-efficiency windows and insulation for roofs, walls, ceilings and floors. The use of sky lights and solar panels on rooftops will meet daylight and captive power requirements, whilst reducing emissions and gaining carbon credits under the clean development mechanism (CDM).

The integrated building management systems (IBMS) market in India is riding high on the wave of energy consciousness and building owners’ desire to increase their green footprint. New analysis from Frost and Sullivan, “Analysis of Integrated Building Management Systems Market in India”, finds that the market earned revenues of more than INR 25.86 billion in 2011 and estimates this to reach INR 51.55 billion in 2016, at a compound annual growth rate of 14.8 per cent. The rising impetus from the Indian government to reduce CO2 emissions and energy consumption, and reforms to aid foreign direct investments has significantly aided the IBMS market.

Traditionally, construction and infrastructure sectors together have had the highest demand for steel (more than 60 per cent) in the country.

"Among steel-intensive industries in India, construction registered the highest growth rates over the past consecutive five years. Manufacturing is the second highest steel consumer segment followed by automotive, both of which are poised to witness impressive growth over the future," noted F&S.

## An eye on automotive

The small and light commercial vehicles market is set for robust growth in India, reveals Frost and Sullivan analysis, due to macro-economic parameters such as infrastructure development, forthcoming Special Economic Zones (SEZs) across the country and urbanisation. According to Frost and Sullivan, alongside these key parameters, future demand for these vehicles is likely to be driven by the following factors:

• The abundance of small lanes in urban, semi-urban and rural areas in India where it is easier to manoeuvre SCVs and LCVs.

• SCVs are being used as last-mile transportation modes in many rural and semi-urban areas where public transportation is scarce. An SCV’s performance is better in terms of driving in rough terrain, passenger comfort and number of customers ferried, compared to a three-wheeler.

• Indian cities are moving towards hub-and-spoke models so SCVs and LCVs are allowed inside the city, whilst heavy vehicles are allowed only outside city limits.

“The Indian SCV and LCV market is the fastest growing segment in the world. With a market size of 353,620 units in 2011, it is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 18 per cent in the next five years to reach 0.83 million units by 2016,” said F&S.

"At present there are immense opportunities to be explored in this segment, and many international OEMs are setting up their plants in India to tap this segment. Both Indian OEMs and multinational entrants are planning new products/variants with feature and price differentiations," observed F&S analyst, Turab Ali Khan. "There are huge gaps in vehicle availability with regards to the Gross Vehicle Weight (GVW), pricing and power currently. Hence, this situation can be exploited to tap further growth in this market."

The Government of India's budget for FY 2010-11 clearly points towards heavy investments in the Indian infrastructure segment, bringing cheer to the commercial vehicle (CV) market. Acknowledging the rising need for green initiatives, the Government also mandated the use of compressed natural gas (CNG) for the registration of CVs in the metro cities. The entry of global CV majors to the market will significantly raise the bar on technological innovations and impel domestic manufacturers to refine their designs and engineering, especially in the bus segment.

## Defence in view

Between 2011 and 2015, India's defence budget is expected to grow at more than eight per cent year-on-year. India is projected to spend approximately US$ 54 billion on foreign arms and equipment procurements, which will also generate offset opportunities worth US$ 18 billion.

The massive increase in the armed forces inventory, with the procurement of medium multi-role combat aircraft (MMRCA), helicopters, naval vessels and submarines will create avenues for maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO), enterprise asset management (EAM) and product lifecycle management (PLM) solutions. ■
