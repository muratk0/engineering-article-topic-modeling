# GROWTH WILSON TOOL

[TARİH: 01.09.2011 ISMR]

ISMR: WHICH MARKET REGIONS DO YOU FEEL OFFER THE MOST POTENTIAL FOR GROWTH FOR YOUR PRODUCTS AND WHY?

CB On the European side, we feel that the existing major European markets are where we will get the most growth this year as they rebound from the recession. Western and Northern Europe are strong regions for us. Eastern Europe is gradually moving back to 2008 levels at different rates (Russia, for example).

BR We believe that North American business will remain strong. Our Canadian acquisition will bring us greater market share in Canada and North America. South America is also a major focus for us – countries such as Brazil and Argentina. Asian markets are more difficult and we have a smaller footprint there but we continue to see double-digit growth year-on-year in China.

The rest of Asia has also bounced back strongly with 30 per cent growth numbers – Malaysia, Singapore, Vietnam, India etc. The percentages are higher but the overall numbers are a little lower – business levels there are probably not quite as strong as the levels we predict in Europe and North America for the balance of 2011/2012.

ISMR: WHAT IS YOUR STRATEGIC AND TECHNICAL FOCUS THIS YEAR? ARE THERE ANY AIMS, OBJECTIVES OR INITIATIVES THAT YOU CAN DISCUSS?

BR We want to gain market share, ensure strong customer service and deliver quality products. These have been our chief targets throughout the years and we continue to push these.

CB In Europe, press brake tooling is a major focus for Wilson Tool. We are working at extending our press brake tooling range and we are adding manufacturing facilities to be able to make some of this tooling. In 2009, we added manufacturing plant and we are now looking at the potential of extending that plant to make even more tooling.

We have seen steady growth in this market in Europe. The US press brake tooling market is more mature. We are trying to ensure in Europe that we have a full range of tools to supply to customers and we now feel that we are close to this point.

ISMR: HOW MANY CUSTOMERS DOES WILSON TOOL HAVE WORLDWIDE?

CB We have over 20,000 customers worldwide and they tend to be quite diverse. Anywhere you see a piece of metal under 6mm thick, it would have started life as a flat sheet of metal, worked by many of our customers, who are involved in virtually every industry sector.

ISMR: WHICH TRENDS DO YOU SEE DEVELOPING IN THE TOOLING INDUSTRY AND WHERE DO YOU SEE THE GREATEST OPPORTUNITIES?

BR One of the trends that we have been experiencing from our large, multi-national global customers is increasing pressure to treat and service them, and their global facilities, as one customer worldwide.

This is, of course, is an opportunity for us to potentially gain more of their business on a global basis but business is done differently around the world and there are pricing issues, transfer pricing and service elements that have to be taken into consideration.

It’s developing an understanding of what they are looking for but it is a difficult metric to balance – some of these companies have multiple facilities around the world and they all want the same service elements or pricing structure. The intention is to move more towards a ‘partnership’ principle from the vendor supply chain to bring their costs down.

CB From a technology trend perspective, our customers are trying to do more on their punch presses (i.e. joining components in assembly, tapping etc.) thereby removing the need to go to secondary operations. Another trend we are seeing is tool identification such as data matrix codes on tools to identify them. Prima Power and TRUMPF now have tool identification systems and we expect to see more of this in the future.

A main customer focus has always been on rapid tool changeover and lean programme implementation. That has also been a strong consideration for us, especially in our R&D operations. Our tooling has always fitted the principle of lean programming and lean set-ups.

BR Our tooling is focused on helping customers to reduce secondary operations. How can we help customers process the sheet while it is still on the punch press? The use of our deburring and tapping tools, for example, means they don’t have to go to a secondary operation. We also have our own lean seminar programme in place to help introduce customers to the benefits of lean manufacturing.

Another key trend we see is on the service side. Customers want tools more rapidly (3 days delivery instead of 5 days etc.) so we have done a lot of work on reducing our lead times, especially for special tooling (1 or 3-day deliveries). We are also looking at other service options for customers. Some customers want more training so we are looking at how we can help them become more efficient in their operations, increase their machine operator skill levels etc.

ISMR: WHAT IS YOUR APPROACH TO TOOL MATERIALS OR TOOL COATINGS?

BR Our engineering staff is always experimenting with new and improved coatings or variations of existing coatings. If there is a customer application problem, we can offer four or five different coatings to try to overcome specific customer challenges ie. stripping etc. We also offer a wide range of material grades for tool steels, depending upon customer requirements. We work directly with steel mills to try to find solutions for specific customers for punch press and press brake tooling.

ISMR: PLEASE GIVE DETAILS OF ANY NEW PRODUCTS THAT YOU LAUNCHED THIS YEAR. ARE THERE ANY NEW PRODUCT LAUNCHES ON THE HORIZON THAT YOU CAN DISCUSS?

CB To bring in completely new sets of customers, we have been concentrating on extending our range of replacement punches and strippers and now have a complete range of assemblies and replacement tools for Amada ABS assemblies. We also now offer two tool grinders to the market.

We launched a new deburring tool this year. In the US and Europe, we have now launched into hydraulic and manual press brake clamping as well as tooling markets. We are now offering hydraulic and manual clamping options – we exhibited the hydraulic clamping option at the Blech EXPO exhibition in Germany recently. In terms of new products, we are planning a lot of new product launches around the FABTECH exhibition in November this year.

ISMR: PLEASE GIVE US AN OVERVIEW OF YOUR PRODUCTION FACILITIES AND ANY SHEET METAL OPERATIONS/ACTIVITIES. DO YOU PLAN TO ADD TO OR AUGMENT YOUR PRODUCTION FACILITIES IN ANY WAY?

CB As well as our UK capacity expansion, we have added the two Exacta facilities in Canada to our production facilities worldwide.

BR In Toronto, Exacta Precision makes the punching tooling. We also have the Exacta Fabtool facility which makes conventional press brake tooling. So, from a product perspective for bending (in North America), we have completely rounded out all styles and options of press brake tooling products (product lines that we did not have before the acquisition of Exact Fabtool). So, we can now offer all our customers in North America any product style or product range of press brake tooling.

Wherever we feel there is a long term opportunity for our product range, anywhere in the world, we will continue to pursue (either logistically or service-wise) our capabilities in those markets.
