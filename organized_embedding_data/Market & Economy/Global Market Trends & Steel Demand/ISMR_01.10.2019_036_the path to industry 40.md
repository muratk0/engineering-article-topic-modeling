# THE PATH TO INDUSTRY 4.0

[TARİH: 01.10.2019 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Industry 4.0 isn't just about investing in new technology and tools to improve manufacturing efficiency — it's about revolutionising the way your entire business operates and grows.

The Industry 4.0 market was valued at US$ 66.67 billion in 2016 and is expected to reach US$ 152.31 billion by 2022"

In 2011, the term 'Industry 4.0' was introduced during the Hannover Messe trade fair. From the invention of the steam engine (First Industrial Revolution) to the introduction of ICT-automated production systems (Third Industrial Revolution), the emphasis has been on mass and serial production. As new innovations have been introduced over the last five years (Big Data; cloud computing; Internet of Things; self-learning robots; mobile; 3D printing; Augmented Reality; holography etc.), one-on-one production has become reality," commented the organiser of the Industrial Design 4.0 conference.

The Fourth industrial revolution, Industry-4.0, has emerged over the past few decades. This takes the emphasis on digital technology from recent decades to a whole new level with the help of interconnectivity through the Internet of Things (loT), access to real-time data and the introduction of cyber-physical systems. Industry 4.0 offers a more comprehensive, interlinked and holistic approach to manufacturing. It connects physical with digital, and allows for better collaboration and access across departments, partners, vendors, product and people. Industry 4.0 empowers business owners to better control and understand every aspect of their operation and allows them to leverage instant data to boost productivity, improve processes and drive growth.

"Industry 4.0 refers to a new phase in the Industrial Revolution that focuses heavily on interconnectivity, automation, machine learning and real-time data. Industry 4.0, also sometimes referred to as IloT (Industrial Internet of Things) or smart manufacturing, marries physical production and operations with smart digital technology, machine learning and Big Data to create a more holistic and better-connected ecosystem for companies that focus on manufacturing and supply chain management. While every company and organisation operating today is different, they all face a common challenge-the need for connectedness and access to real-time insights across processes, partners, products and people," commented Epicor.

## A revolution and a disruption

“Industry 4.0 isn't just about investing in new technology and tools to improve manufacturing efficiency – it's about revolutionising the way your entire business operates and grows,' added Epicor.

"The rise of new digital industrial technology, known as Industry 4.0, is a transformation that makes it possible to gather and analyse data across machines, enabling faster, more flexible and more efficient processes to produce higher-quality goods at reduced costs. This manufacturing revolution will increase productivity, shift economics, foster industrial growth and modify the profile of the workforce – ultimately changing the competitiveness of companies and regions," explained BCG.

The idea behind Industry 4.0 is to reconcile the conflict between maximum efficiency and minimum cost (the driving forces of mass production) on the one hand, and maximum flexibility, customised offers and rapid product lifecycles on the other. Since its inception, Industry 4.0 has spread beyond the factory walls to encompass a broader digital transformation that spans processes, functions and industries. This technology-driven transformation could change the way that many organisations make sense of information and act upon it to make decisions that could impact operations, deliver greater customer value and improve performance.

“Industry 4.0 will be a revolution and a disruption. Its ultimate target is to achieve individual production down to batch-size one at costs close to mass production. To do that requires a tight value-network integration within the production line but also with suppliers via de-centralised control of production processes. IoT and Artificial Intelligence (AI) are mandatory to delivering that outcome," commented Fujitsu.

The Industry 4.0 market was valued at US$ 66.67 billion in 2016 and is expected to reach US$ 152.31 billion by 2022, at a CAGR of 14.72% between 2017 and 2022. The increasing adoption of the industrial Internet and the increased focus on efficiency and cost of production are major drivers for the Industry-4.0 market.

## Implementing the right strategy

"There's a lot of hype around the 4th Industrial Revolution (4IR), or Industry 4.0, but do you really know what it is and how it will impact you? 4IR represents a transformative opportunity for industry. It is clear that, when implemented well, digitally-based technologies and systems can improve productivity and quality whilst reducing costs in manufacturing. The new global transformation and implementation of cyber-physical systems, brought to us through 4IR, will enable companies to remain competitive," explained Innovate UK.

“Manufacturers have raised concerns about 4IR in terms of confusion, resource concerns, uncertain benefits and the language of 4IR, all of which create key barriers for SMEs. A study carried out by the Knowledge Transfer Network into 4IR highlighted SME concerns around:

Which advanced technologies are manufacturers using now?

There is a lack of a common understanding of definitions and standards.

■ There is no common glossary of terms readily available.

■ "Where do we start?"

■ "Where can I see it?"

■ “What is government doing?"

The most profound challenge for manufacturers looking to implement a technology-driven transformation process is clearly understanding where they are currently in terms of solution maturity. Once they have a clear starting point, then they can realistically, and strategically, begin to advance. ABI Research, a global tech market advisory firm, has just released its Industry 4.0 Maturity Assessment to help manufacturers gain this clarity and insight.

The Interactive Industry 4.0 Maturity Assessment aims to help manufacturers to benchmark and baseline the maturity of their plant or operation today and put together a plan for the future. It evaluates maturity progress in the seven critical disciplines below:

■ Robotics & Automation.

■ Manufacturing process.

■ Software.

■ Control.

■ Data Management & Analytics.

■ Connectivity.

■ Worker Enablement.

Ninety-nine percent of middle market manufacturing executives today are at least moderately familiar with Industry 4.0, according to BDO's Middle Market Industry 4.0 Benchmarking Survey. But while more than half (54 per cent) of middle market manufacturing executives have either developed or are in the process of developing an Industry 4.0 strategy, only five per cent are currently implementing one. Another 25 per cent plan to develop an Industry 4.0 strategy, but have yet to get started on it.

“Over time, Industry 4.0 will radically transform how manufacturers derive and deliver value,” said Eskander Yavar, Industry 4.0 co-leader and national leader of BDO’s Management & Technology Advisory Services. “But the window of opportunity is closing fast. Manufacturers who don’t invest in 2019 risk falling behind the curve. The middle market is no exception.”

In conjunction with these concerns, the term “Big Data” often used when speaking about 4IR can be viewed very negatively by companies who do not really understand its meaning and benefits. The biggest business concerns facing companies digitising their manufacturing processes, according to Innovate UK, include:

1 Legacy systems/integration is the single greatest digital challenge.

2 Identification of new business models for vendors to make middleware and targeted software more accessible by manufacturing SMEs allowing them to embrace 4IR.

3 Building competence and confidence within the company where there are skills gaps.

4 Companies of any size need to be in a fit state to benefit from adopting new technologies (machines or software). Failure to do so will compromise predicted benefits and productivity gains, rendering a high ROI an almost unachievable ambition.

5 Access to easily accessible specialist, localised programmes to help manufacturers navigate this complicated landscape and find a result that is tailored for them.

6 Support to help manufacturers understand the terminology generated through 4IR.

7 An approach that works for many manufacturers is – led from the top – driven from the factory floor.

The areas where manufacturers will gain significant benefit from focusing their resources for digitisation, says Innovate UK, include:

- Factory floor and production systems.
- Additive manufacturing.
- Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT).
- Sensors.
- Systems integration.
- Digitally assisted assembly.
- Robotics and automation.
- Simulation.
- Cyber security.
- Flexible manufacturing.
- 3D visualisation.
- Digital processes (rather than paper and human time-consuming processes).
- Predictive maintenance.
- Energy and water efficiency.

It is crucial for companies to develop new skills and acquire new tools to be able to leverage Industry 4.0.

## The digital transformation

As a result of Industry 4.0, in the future billions of machines, systems and sensors worldwide will communicate with each other and share information. This will not only enable companies to make production significantly more efficient, it will give them greater flexibility when it comes to tailoring production to meet market requirements.

The digital transformation of industry isn't advancing simply because of its ability to reduce costs. Instead, it serves as a basis for new business models that can pay huge dividends in terms of quality and documentation.

The Fourth Industrial Revolution will be transformational with technologies such as Augmented Reality, robotics, Big Data and the Internet of Things changing the way that we manufacture.

"Industry 4.0 is an industrial ecosystem, which among everything else is forcing a change in the role of people in the industrial value chain. It is crucial for companies to develop new skills and acquire new tools to be able to leverage Industry 4.0 properly," concluded analyst, Roland Berger.

OMAX FEEL THE FORCE Corporation

As OMAX® Corporation celebrates 26 years of waterjet technology, its General Manager, Mike Ruppenthal, outlines its strategic approach.

Dr. John Cheung and Dr. John Olsen founded OMAX in 1993, after finding a way to revolutionise the waterjet machining method. A new type of motion control technology and proprietary software ushered in the development of what they termed 'the first truly affordable waterjet system to offer fast, precise and consistent results'. Today, OMAX Corporation offers diverse, advanced product solutions with a broad range of table sizes, pumps, accessories and software.

OMAX's abrasive waterjet machines cut simple to complex shapes from virtually any material and thickness. The cold-cutting process of waterjet machining eliminates thermal stress in the workpiece material, preventing the hardening and warping that often occurs during conventional cutting processes.

In 2019, OMAX merged with Hypertherm. This merger combines OMAX’s direct drive pumps, software and applications technologies with Hypertherm’s portfolio of intensifierbased pumps, abrasive recycling systems and AccuStream consumables.

## Technical innovation

"Last year, OMAX celebrated its 25th anniversary. The anniversary celebrated a history of customer-focused innovation and new products, such as the ProtoMAX and GlobalMAX, as well as reinvigorating our commitment to maintaining thought leadership in the machine tool market," Mike Ruppenthal, OMAX's General Manager, told ISMR.

"Since Dr. John Olsen developed and patented a motion control system for waterjet cutting systems in 1993, we have endeavoured to provide precise, easy-to-use, cost-effective, standardised, reliable and high-quality abrasive-jet machining systems to the manufacturing industry, with strong aftersales services."

Owner of the OMAX, MAXIEM, GlobalMAX and ProtoMAX brands, the company provides a comprehensive selection of JetMachining Centers that feature intuitive software controls and incorporate efficient pump technology. In developing its machines, the company designs, manufactures, assembles and tests components as a complete system to ensure optimum performance. The company also has a comprehensive service and support network in the waterjet industry to keep its customers ahead of the manufacturing curve.

Following the merger, Hypertherm has confirmed that it does not plan to make significant changes to OMAX's operations, management structure or distribution channels. OMAX's offices and manufacturing operations in Kent, Washington, where the majority of its 367 associates are located, will remain intact, with no consolidations or reductions planned. Hypertherm also anticipates continuing both the Hypertherm and OMAX waterjet product offerings and brands as, it told ISMR, 'the companies technologies and business models balance each other'.

## Strategy and focus

Approximately 65% of OMAX's sales are in North America (Canada and the U.S.A.). The strongest market for OMAX outside the U.S.A. is Europe. Its primary customers are fabricators and job shops (in automotive and architectural sectors, for example) who need waterjet-cut parts. It also serves the engineering educational sector (technical schools, aspiring manufacturers etc.).

Through its distribution network of 30+ partners worldwide, OMAX Corporation has expanded its global reach and increased the availability of its precision-engineered technology. In line with this, Mike Ruppenthal has a key strategic focus for spreading the waterjet message more widely.

"Our focus for the future is expanding OMAX’s reach into new markets. There are industrial manufacturing markets that often don’t know about waterjet or who are sceptical of the technology. Complementary machining, cutting without heat-affected-zones, ease-of-use … we're looking at how to educate those potential customers on the benefits that we know OMAX technology can bring to their shop," he explained.

"One market trend is that the waterjet has now also become a tool for the maker movement, artisans and tech schools. This has been driven by the development of more personal waterjets – our ProtoMAX is an example of a personal abrasive waterjet machine. The compact machine footprint, combined with its versatility, makes the ProtoMAX a suitable abrasive waterjet for prototyping, educational applications or as a complement to a larger machine shop," he told ISMR.

"The other trend emerging in the waterjet industry is micromachining. Since waterjets cut without heat-affected zones, and there is little side pressure (compared to traditional CNCs), the technology is beginning to be used to manufacture intricate telecommunication, aerospace and medical parts. Often these parts are made from a lightweight composite or speciality metal that doesn't cut very well using laser or plasma cutting technology."

A further indicator that he highlighted to ISMR was the continuing struggle (common across the engineering industry) to find good, trained operators which has led OMAX to focus on building products that are easy to use and program for relatively inexperienced operators.

"A knowledgeable machinist is invaluable. Men and women with years of experience working on CNCs, waterjets or other machine tools are in a good place. As a profession, many of the machinist trades can weather economic uncertainty based on the fact that their skills are 'sought after'. For as long as many of us have known, trade schools and careers in manufacturing have declined in popularity, but I think we're seeing that trend turn around. I think we see more opportunities in trades education now," Ruppenthal explained.

Along with the trends towards miniaturisation and personalisation that are creeping into the waterjet sector, the move towards automated and smart systems is another key trend highlighted by OMAX. It continues to develop its software systems and waterjet cutting systems with a 'Waterjet 4.0' goal in mind.

## Challenges and opportunities

Issues of prime importance at OMAX are understanding the difficulties in hiring qualified employees, challenging economic environments across the globe and the evolution in digital manufacturing.

"By ensuring that our software controller, IntelliMAX, is extremely user-friendly, we've helped customers reduce the learning curve when onboarding new personnel to work on their machines. Customers are also looking to get the most out of their waterjets and we continue to invest in application development to help them do that. In addition, we’ve highlighted our waterjets as one of the most versatile machines on the market. We have several success stories of OMAX customers who purchased a waterjet for a specific application, but found the versatility opened doors to new products and new markets," Ruppenthal told ISMR.

"As a company, OMAX is great at fostering innovation. We’re an engineering-led company and, because of that, we can let our people explore different avenues of waterjet machining. For example, our monitoring program, IntelliVISOR, stemmed from letting our engineers have the space to create. The challenge then becomes; how do we bring these amazing products to market and how do we share what we are excited about with the machining world?"

## A focus on innovation

OMAX lines of abrasive waterjets can cut almost anything, including aluminium; brass; bronze; carbon fibre composite; ceramic; copper; fibreglass; glass; granite; Kevlar; marble; stainless steel; titanium; tungsten and more.

At EMO 2019 in Hannover recently, the company presented the OMAX 5555 JetMachining Center® with a 40hp EnduroMAX® pump and Tilt-A-Jet®, as well as its latest in personal waterjets, the ProtoMAX®.

"The OMAX 5555 is a perfect fit for shops needing an industrial machine with a smaller footprint capable of cutting 1397mm x 1397mm. With a completely sealed and protected ball screw drive system, this robust and reliable workhorse is suitable for shops cutting projects needing high precision. The Tilt-A-Jet lets your waterjet achieve virtually zero taper with most materials. The Tilt-A-Jet can position the nozzle at an angle calculated by the software to exactly offset the taper from the jet. Taper doesn't disappear – it just gets moved to the scrap part of the material, leaving your part with exactly square edges," commented Mike Ruppenthal.

In addition to its industrial machines, OMAX also demonstrated the new ProtoMAX personal abrasive waterjet system at EMO Hannover. ProtoMAX is a compact, selfcontained cutting system that is suitable for prototyping and low-volume cutting of almost any material, up to approximately 26mm thick. With all the versatility benefits of large abrasive waterjet cutters in a sleek package, the ProtoMAX is suitable for small job shops, engineering classrooms, 'makerspaces' and personal use. It also makes a useful addition for large shops and fabricators that need occasional waterjet capabilities to complement their other cutting systems.

"Energy-effective direct drive pumps power both large machines. OMAX direct drive pumps use less electricity and water while reducing component fatigue. These pumps also run quietly and cleanly, making them the ideal solution for shops that want to do more with less," concluded Mike Ruppenthal.

On stand 5010 in Hall 5 at Blechexpo in Stuttgart this November, OMAX will be demonstrating its OMAX 60120® abrasive waterjet. Featuring the IntelliTrax® highprecision drive system, OMAX waterjets create exact parts of up to 0.0254mm.

"This robust and reliable system requires little maintenance and is as easy to use as a conventional machine tool," Ruppenthal told ISMR. "Our GlobalMAX line is an example of being more accessible and enabling more endusers to incorporate waterjet. The GlobalMAX line offers all the benefits of cutting with waterjet at a price point that is more favourable to fabricators who are new to waterjet or who would like expanded capabilities without larger capital investment."
