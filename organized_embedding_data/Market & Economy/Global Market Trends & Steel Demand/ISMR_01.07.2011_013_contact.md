While the recent global recession took a heavy toll on many industries, evident in waves of layoffs, lost revenue, bankruptcies and a general sense of uncertainty about the future, the aerospace and defence industry as a whole continues to buck the trend, finishing 2010 with record results. Revenues increased by 2 per cent compared with 2009, while operating profit was up 19 per cent over 2009, said analyst PWC.

“Perhaps the most notable attribute affecting results was better programme performance. Also affecting 2010 performance was a strong rebound in commercial aviation, which drove better results in the aftermarket. While overall industry results were at record levels, not all news was cheery. Pressure has continued to mount on global defence budgets and on contractors to improve productivity. Many defence contractors have already taken actions to reduce payrolls and consolidate operations in preparation for leaner times ahead.

“The outlook for defence is filled with uncertainty and challenge. There are palpable tensions between the dynamic need for global security and a scarcity of economic resources. Security, however, is not a luxury but a necessity. So the defence industry, with a proud tradition of innovation, must become leaner and more productive. Policy makers can support the industry by preserving funding to maintain critical skills and competencies and by reducing regulation and promoting more free trade.”

## REGIONAL PROCUREMENT

The shift of economic power from West to East creates a changing competitive landscape. Advanced technologies and new operational concepts will drive procurement in a US$ 244 billion warships market over the next 10 years, says defence analyst Forecast International. Visiongain calculates that, in 2011, the global market for warships and naval vessels will amount to US$ 75.4 billion.

Countries such as the US, China and India are major defence spenders. Latin American defence spending is expected to grow from approximately US$ 63 billion in 2011 to more than US$ 65 billion by 2014, according to Forecast International’s “The Military Market for Latin America” report. The market leaders are Brazil, Colombia, Venezuela, Chile and Mexico accounting for 90 per cent of total defence spending in the region. The Middle East defence spending binge is set to grow further in coming years as Gulf Arab nations embark on military modernisation programmes. In its annual Middle East market analysis, Forecast International projects that defence investment across the region will expand by 14 per cent over the next five years. The region's continuation as a leading global area for military expenditure is being spurred by Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates.

## AEROSPACE AND UAV MARKETS

According to the “Aerospace Economic Report and Outlook 2010” published by Embry-Riddle Aeronautical University (Barr et. al.), major OEMs and primes such as Boeing, Lockheed Martin, EADS and others forecast the near and long term future of aerospace manufacturing in the US on the growth of Gross Domestic Product (GDP).

“Small and medium-sized aerospace manufacturers took a greater hit from the recent recession than larger firms but, as in the past, will be even quicker to recover. Due to the increase of air travel and cargo traffic, idle aerospace manufacturing capacity, when utilised, will quickly spark growth in 2011,” it said.
