[TARİH: 01.09.2017 ISMR]

In the second quarter of 2017, order bookings in the German machine tool industry fell by seven per cent compared to the preceding year's equivalent period. Domestic orders were down by 27 percent, while export orders rose by four per cent. In the first half of 2017, total order bookings shrank by one per cent, with domestic orders decreasing by 15 per cent. Order bookings from outside Germany were up by six percent. Forming technology is performing better than metal-cutting machinery.

"With the mid-year figures, we're well on course for our expectations," commented Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director, VDW. Export orders continued their upward trend, with the Eurozone nations still the principal drivers. Their orders rose twice as steeply as orders from the rest of the world. Following substantial growth last year, driven primarily by project business with the international automotive industry, the high level achieved is predicted to continue through 2017, according to the VDW.

"Domestic demand, however, was a disappointment in the year's first half," said Wilfried Schäfer. This remained weak, due not least to the base effect owed to high growth achieved during the first half of 2016. This effect, however, is now coming to an end. For the second half of 2017, significantly better figures are anticipated by the VDW. This reflects, it told ISMR, the optimistic mood in the business community, rising cyclical indicators for Germany and macro-economic forecasts, which pundits have only recently revised upwards.

"Moreover, we're anticipating a huge boost from the forthcoming EMO Hannover 2017 exhibition," concluded Schäfer. The metalworking exhibition, he added, will be showcasing numerous innovations in all technical categories and will provide a major boost to capital investment.

## German automotive industry

According to the VDA (the German Association of the Automotive Industry), the German passenger car market remains robust and added nearly two per cent in July to reach 283,100 new registrations.

"Almost 2.1 million new cars have so far been registered this year, which is a rise of three per cent. As expected, the share of diesels among all new car registrations last month was down on the previous year. It came to 40.5 per cent, compared with 47.1 per cent in July 2016. For the German group brands, a good 45 per cent of new passenger car registrations were of diesel vehicles – both in July and during the first seven months of this year," it explained.

Exports of passenger cars from plants in Germany totalled 307,700 units (-8 percent) in July. From January to July, German car makers supplied 2.5 million passenger cars to customers around the world (-3 per cent). The decline in exports also affected production; last month, 371,700 new vehicles were built in Germany (-9 per cent). Since the beginning of 2017, 3.3 million passenger cars have been produced, i.e. four per cent less than in the same period last year.

There has been a huge rise in electric car registrations in Germany during the first half of the year. A total of 22,465 new cars with electric propulsion were registered – a leap of 113 per cent. The proportion of electric vehicles among all new passenger cars has more than doubled compared with last year, from 0.6 per cent to 1.3 per cent. The German group brands market share continued rising in the first six months of 2017 – to 61 per cent.

The increase was even greater in June. Growth of 175 per cent to 4,702 electric vehicle registrations took the figure to a new monthly record. It includes Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs), plug-in hybrids and vehicles with fuel cells. The trend is similar for plug-in hybrids (+189 per cent) and the BEVs (+164 per cent). In June, electric cars accounted for 1.4 per cent of the overall market.

## Looking to the future

According to the Kiel Institute for World Economy, the German economy is already in the fifth year of a moderate upturn. According to the GD joint economic forecast prepared by Germany's five leading economic research institutes on behalf of the Federal Government, capacity utilisation is gradually increasing, and aggregate production capacities are now likely to have slightly exceeded their normal utilisation levels. However, cyclical dynamics remain low compared to earlier periods of recoveries, as consumption expenditures, which do not exhibit strong fluctuations, have been the main driving force so far. In addition, net migration increases potential output, counteracting a stronger capacity tightening.

"Gross Domestic Product (GDP) is expected to expand by 1.5% (1.8% adjusted for calendar effects) and 1.8% in the next year. Unemployment is expected to fall to 6.1% in 2016, to 5.7% in 2017 and 5.4% in 2018," said Oliver Holtemöller, head of the Department of Macroeconomics and vice president of the Halle Institute for Economic Research (IWH) – Member of the Leibniz Association.

According to the Institute, inflation is expected to increase markedly over the forecast horizon. After an increase in consumer prices of just 0.5% in 2016, the inflation rate is expected to rise to 1.8% in 2017 and 1.7% in 2018. The public budget surplus will reduce modestly. Public finances are slightly stimulating economic activity in the current year and are cyclically neutral in the year ahead.
