# Positive signs for Italian machine tool industry

[TARİH: 01.09.2017 ISMR]

## ACCORDING TO UCIMU-SISTEMI

PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tools, robot and automation association, the 2016 outlook for the Italian machine tool, robot and automation manufacturing industry was positive, registering increases for all main economic indicators, except for exports.

"Fifth among manufacturing countries, Italy now takes third place among exporters and has gained one position in the consumption ranking, where it came in fifth, as a testimony to the liveliness of its domestic demand," said the association.

It expects the positive trend to continue throughout 2017 – the year in which production, export and consumption is expected to grow, also supported by measures from Italy's National Plan Industry 4.0. It will drive both the deliveries of manufacturers in the domestic market and imports, said the association.

"In particular, in 2016, production increased by 6.4%. This was due to the positive trend in manufacturers' deliveries in the domestic market, which grew by 25.7%. However, exports suffered a setback. After dropping by 4%, the value of exports returned to levels seen in 2014, wiping out the increase achieved in 2015," commented UCIMU President, Massimo Carboniero.

In 2016, major markets for Italian products were: Germany (+1%); the United States (-9.4%); China (-6.9%); France (+ 23.2%); Poland (+7.2%); Spain (+14%); Mexico (+29.3%) and Turkey (-5.1%).

A strongly positive performance was recorded on consumption, which registered a double-digit increase for the third consecutive year, 15.3% more than in 2015.

According to forecast data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU, Italian production will go up by 6.7% in 2017. Consumption will increase by 10.1% more than in 2016, driving manufacturers' deliveries in the domestic market – expected to grow by 12% – and exports (+7.5%).

Exports are expected to enjoy a 3% upturn. The index of machine tool orders, collected in the second quarter of 2017 by UCIMU, registered a 17.2% increase compared with the same period in 2016.

"Nevertheless, even by the end of 2017, according to the forecasts, we will not have recovered all the ground lost in the recession years. The relaunch of Italian manufacturing SMEs' competitiveness has just begun and more time is needed for them to adopt provisions and start their investment plans," cautioned Carboniero.
