# German clients boost demand for machine tools

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 ISMR]

ORDERS RECEIVED BY the German machine tool industry in the first quarter of 2014 were 10 per cent higher than the previous year. Domestic orders were 20 per cent higher than last year, whereas orders from outside Germany increased by 5 per cent.

“The domestic market is a major source of demand for the machine tool sector right now,” said Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association), commenting on the result.

“Order levels began to rise at the end of 2012 and this ongoing trend confirms just how optimistic German clients are at the moment and how the domestic reluctance to invest is now well and truly over. This is also reflected by the sentiment levels in industry which have risen again recently,” added the VDW.

The machining equipment sector, with its broad client base, has moved ahead to take a leading position, in contrast to last year. Orders have risen by 14 per cent. Forming equipment orders were 4 per cent up on the previous year. Many orders were received, in particular, for sheet metalworking machines designed for a wide range of applications. Following last year’s boom, the demand for pressing equipment has declined slightly, explained the association.

No clear increase in demand has yet been noted from customers outside Germany. Orders from the Eurozone, in particular, were down by ten per cent. “We are pinning our hopes on Asia and America,” explained Schäfer. The 9 per cent increase seen in orders from outside the Eurozone in the first quarter, in contrast, was much stronger.

“The first quarter has given the green light to increased production in the current year,” confirmed the VDW executive director. All assumptions, however, are based on there being no negative impact from international developments, such as the conflict with Russia. In 2013, the German machine tool industry produced machines and services worth around 14.5 billion euros and employed about 71,600 people (annual average for 2013, firms with more than 20 staff). This corresponded to growth of 2 per cent. ■
