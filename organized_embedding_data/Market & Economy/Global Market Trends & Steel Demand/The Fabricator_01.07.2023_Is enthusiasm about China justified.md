# Is enthusiasm about China justified?

[TARİH: 01.07.2023 The Fabricator]

At the start of the year, many held considerable doubt regarding the future of the Chinese economy. It was still mired in the pandemic lockdown, and prospects for economic growth were dimming by the day. The backlogs at the Chinese ports were setting records, and the majority of the world’s supply chain managers were actively seeking alternatives to China despite all the advantages possessed in terms of infrastructure and production costs.

The mood shifted as Xi Jinping ordered the relaxation of the lockdown, and the country started to resume its more traditional pace. As the COVID crisis ebbed, there was a surge of enthusiasm on the part of Chinese authorities as well as western investors and business people. Was China really back now? Some assert this enthusiasm is not necessarily justified.

The assertion is that China will manage growth of 5.0% this year, and the bulk of that expansion will be due to the surge in domestic consumption now that the lockdown has been lifted. This is the Xi plan – reduce China’s dependence on exports by substituting domestic demand—only it has not worked out that way. According to analysts and historical patterns, corporate revenues need to grow by over 8.0% to sustain growth at that 5.0% level, and the latest numbers indicate that these corporate revenues have grown at just 1.5%.

The Chinese consumer has not come through as expected, but this should have been anticipated given that millions of Chinese were denied income for almost three years. The Chinese government has consistently ignored the fact that millions of Chinese routinely supplement their official wages and salaries with independent businesses. Nearly all of these were shuttered during the lockdown and have been very slow to start back up. Without that extra income, the average consumer doesn’t have much to work with.

Imports fell by over 8.0%, and that is another sign of weakened consumer activity. The MSCI stock index fell by 15% and consumer-centered stocks have fallen by 25%. The government analysts dismissed these numbers with an assertion of "seasonal adjustment," but that seems to indicate that nobody expected the arrival of spring this year.

The debt situation is worsening as well. Chinese consumers are now holding debt that is 30% of their disposable income (in the U.S., it’s 10%). The U.S. managed to accumulate excess savings that are 10% of the national GDP during the pandemic while China accumulated less than 3.0%.

All this is not to assert that China is headed for some kind of recessionary retreat in 2023, but it does clearly indicate that everything is not going to plan.
