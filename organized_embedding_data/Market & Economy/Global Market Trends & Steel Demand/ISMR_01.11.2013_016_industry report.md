## Middle East

The construction boom and rising investments in real estate in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA) have resulted in phenomenal growth for building technologies such as heating ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC), lighting and mechanical-electrical-plumbing (MEP) systems in recent years. As all these systems operate on electricity, KSA’s energy consumption has inevitably surged rapidly over the past few years.

KSA is projected to account for over 50 per cent of the Gulf Cooperative Council’s (GCC) district cooling operating capacity and 33 per cent of overall revenue by 2016, at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 21.7 per cent. KSA is the highest waste generator in the GCC which was recorded at approximately 22 million tonnes in 2011. The availability of cheap fossil fuel and low landfill rates, however, have been hindering growth of waste-to-energy and the recycling market in the country.

The rise in energy consumption must be addressed by developing new techniques for energy efficiency. Some techniques that can support energy-efficiency measures are HVAC, lighting, integrated building management systems (IBMS), performance contracting and recycling.

KSA is spearheading the region’s emerging trend towards adoption of alternative and renewable sources of energy. With a strong renewable energy resource base and constant technological developments, KSA has ambitious plans to improve energy efficiency by effectively using existing systems and encouraging the use of energy-efficient products.

In 2010, the Government of KSA established the Saudi Energy Efficiency Board (SEEC), as energy efficiency was identified as a national priority. Amongst others, one objective of the SEEC was to propose a national energy-efficiency plan to rationalise energy usage in the country. Thus, KSA’s first grid-connected solar power plant was inaugurated in 2011. KSA offers over USD 1.35 billion of energy-saving potential and out of this about USD 1 billion is an immediate addressable market potential for energy efficiency. Currently, the KSA accounts for USD 1.44 billion of the untapped energy-saving market, which translates to about 37.1 bn kWh of energy saved.

“Greener equipment should start making inroads into the KSA market. If Saudi Arabia implements energy-efficiency programmes as proposed by the electricity advisor, demand for products such as programmable thermostats, energy-efficient HVAC equipment and other associated services and solutions is expected to increase,” said Frost & Sullivan environmental and building technologies industry manager, Kumar Ramesh. ■

Powerful entry level sheet metal CAD/CAMsoftware, Radan, has doubled production on an ageing Nisshinbo punch press, proving to be a smart investment for a company manufacturing a range of commercial and industrial heating and air conditioning systems.

## First year hurdles

During Air Source Ventilation’s first year, it used a commercial software brand inherited with its 25-year-old Nisshinbo MTP 1000F punch press but found all aspects of it to be too slow.

“Every tool had to be placed manually on the part,” explained engineering director, Andy Pinchbeck. “We had to select which tool we wanted to use and tell the software which line we wanted it to go on then crop the end short so that it tagged the part. That had to be done manually for every single line.

“There was manual nesting too. The machine was probably designed for repeat sheets to be run off hundreds of times so was very cumbersome for the constantly changing nests needed for our three or four-offs. Even with the software package we were using, it was a huge workload just getting it ready to start punching.”

And the company’s software issues didn’t end when punching started…

“The software sequenced punches in such a way that it threw up a program error on around one in ten sheets which had to be fixed or recovered, taking up to half an hour every time. The only way around it was to manually sequence each sheet, selecting which punch to do and in which order. It was taking far too much time…,” explained Pinchbeck.

## Choosing a solution

As it approached its first anniversary, the company was growing and the number of parts it was manufacturing was increasing so it needed to considerably reduce machine downtime.

## FOCUS ON CAD/CAM

Machine set-up, tooling lists, nest graphics, part identification and machine program are all automatically produced directly from the nest schedule.

Rather than invest in a new machine, it opted to upgrade the software with Radan’s entrylevel system, Genesis. This gave it traditional Radan CAM functionality and ease of implementation, swiftly resolving any production problems.

“Machine set-up, tooling lists, nest graphics, part identification and machine program are all automatically produced directly from the nest schedule,” said Andy Pinchbeck. “From the outset, I thought we’d have to be buying newer machines by now, to be where we are today, but two years after installing Radan we’ve still not had to upgrade to expensive machinery.”

## Doubling punch production

Operating with 25 employees out of 13,000-square foot premises near Manchester, Air Source Ventilation counts call centres, health centres, offices and council departments amongst the customers for its mainly roofbased systems comprising renewable energy heat pumps, ventilation equipment and heat recovery units.

All sheet metal components – such as the double-skinned panels which provide the casework for the equipment; heavy duty steel base frames; internal blanking and structural parts; control panel back plates, enclosures and roofs – are now produced through Radan Genesis. The number of sheets going through the Nisshinbo has doubled and, according to the software manufacturer, machine downtime, through problems, is non-existent.

“The machine now runs for 13 hours per day and it’s no longer a full-time job for someone to be sitting at the computer getting the job tooled and nested,” said Air Source Ventilation.

The Nisshinbo’s limited memory means that it cannot hold large programs, but Genesis overcomes this by creating a number of sub-routines.

“We use sheets of up to three metres, which can often contain 20 or 30 different complex parts, for some jobs so the programs can be particularly large. And, where there are just too many dissimilar parts even for sub-routines to handle, it’s so easy with Genesis to split the job into a few different programs and send them to the machine in three or four sections,” the subcontractor explained.

There were a couple of unexpected bonuses along the way which have improved productivity even further. Because all parts are labelled, the punch operator was manually typing out 15,000 labels over a six-month period.

“It was taking longer to produce the labels for a job than it was to punch the sheets,” added Andy Pinchbeck. He mentioned this to Radan’s area manager who organised a bespoke macro, automatically producing the labels directly from information on the nester. Its second macro was for sheet slitting, which has now been included in the main release.

“This demonstrates how Radan listens to what customers want their system to do, creates that bespoke functionality for them and then, if it is useful for everyone, it becomes a standard feature,” outlined Andy Pinchbeck.

He added that, overall, Radan Genesis is one of the key factors contributing to the company’s success.

“As a start-up company three years ago, we needed to keep the initial outlay to an absolute minimum and we couldn’t have got the punch output to where it is today without Radan Genesis. So, for us, it’s been a smart investment by increasing our output without the need for additional expensive hardware and it’s enabled us to concentrate on growing the business because we don’t have to spend our time just producing the parts,” he concluded. ■
