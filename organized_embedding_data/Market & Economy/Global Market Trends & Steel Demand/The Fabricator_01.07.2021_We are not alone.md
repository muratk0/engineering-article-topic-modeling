THE SUPPLY CHAIN HAS BEEN A WRECK FOR A YEAR AND NOW IT STANDS TO GET EVEN WORSE.

This may sound like the opening for a documentary on UFOs but it really refers to the fact that American manufacturers are not suffering the problems of rapid growth in any kind of isolation. This has been a global phenomenon and that only adds some complications for the U.S. manufacturer. A year ago, the issue was recession inspired by a lockdown and companies were trying to figure out survival. Now the issue is growth and all the accompanying challenges of higher commodity costs and scarce supply of labor.

At the start of the year, it was just the U.S. that was facing these challenges but now Europe is growing just as fast due to their own loosening of the protocols. China has an acute labor shortage as its economy has modernized and they are just as short of essential commodities. The high prices here are the same as the high prices there.

The implications for the U.S. are significant as it means there is even more competition for these scarce resources. We need steel, copper, aluminum, lumber, and oil — so does Europe and so does Asia. The demand for skilled labor is high everywhere and therefore has been driving wages up everywhere. The supply chain has been a wreck for a year and now it stands to get even worse as there will be higher levels of demand and that usually triggers intense competition for the attention of the suppliers. Just look at what has been happening with the supply of semiconductors.

There is a shortage and it has forced dozens of automotive industries to shut down. The auto sector is not the only consumer of these chips and the electronics sector has been prepared to pay a higher price for the chips they need. They get the supply and the auto sector gets to wait. The nature of shortage competition is predictable.
