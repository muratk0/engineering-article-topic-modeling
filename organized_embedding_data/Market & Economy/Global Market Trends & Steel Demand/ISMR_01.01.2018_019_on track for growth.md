India is expected to become the third largest commercial aviation market by the early 2020s. ## Market outlook 2017–2036

Boeing's long-term outlook projects that network carriers will add over 18,000 new aircraft, with the vast majority of demand – 11,000 units – in the single-aisle category. Widebody passenger aircraft at network airlines are forecast to increase by 6,500 units, with 54 per cent in the small widebody category, which will continue to fragment and grow the number of markets served by the global network carriers. About half of forecast deliveries to large network carriers will be for growth, to open new markets and to add frequencies in existing markets, while the remaining half will replace retiring aircraft.

"Demand in the commercial market is forecast to more than double over the next two decades. To meet this demand, we forecast the number of jet aeroplanes will nearly double to 47,000 aeroplanes, at an average annual growth rate of 3.3 per cent. To support this fleet growth, we forecast a need for more than 41,000 new deliveries, valued at over US$ 6 trillion, for growth and replacement over the next 20 years," said the Boeing report.

"Single-aisle aeroplanes command the largest share of new deliveries at more than 70 per cent, with airlines needing more than 29,500 over the next 20 years. These new aeroplanes will continue to stimulate growth for low-cost carriers and will provide required replacements for older, less-efficient aeroplanes. In addition, more than 9,100 new widebody aeroplanes will be delivered to allow airlines to serve new markets – passenger and cargo – more efficiently than in the past."

The report also forecasts that by 2036, approximately 40 per cent of all new aeroplanes will be delivered to airlines based in the Asia region. An additional 40 per cent will be delivered to airlines in Europe and North America combined, with the remaining 20 per cent delivered to the Middle East, Latin America, CIS and Africa.

## Aerospace fasteners' market

Technavio's market research analysis predicts that the global aerospace fasteners' market will grow at a CAGR of around 7% from 2017-2021. Aerospace fasteners, including bolts, nuts, screws, rivets and pins, are extensively used in commercial and military aircraft, ballistic missiles, rockets and associated aftermarket services.

"The application of temperature-resistant titanium fasteners, which have a high strength-to-weight ratio, and growing investment in cabin interior products are propelling growth in this market. Both civilian aircraft and military programmes use titanium aerospace parts because of their lightweight properties and compatibility with carbon fibre-reinforced composite structures. Also, with growing innovations in cabin interior products, such as customised cabins, wireless in-flight entertainment and connectivity (IFEC), interactive windows and lightweight seats, the demand for fasteners will increase significantly over the predicted period," said the report.

With technology innovations driving product acceptance, the introduction of software such as ASRP5 that allows the simulation of complex assembly processes is expected to drive the overall market. The new software assists in viewing the manufacturing process during aircraft assembly. Software experts and assembly line engineers examine and visualise the assembly process, which helps them to maximise the use of fasteners to eliminate stress. It also decreases the time required for complex joining processes and improves the production rates of single-aisle and wide-body aircraft. At present, ASRP5 is being used in the assembly of two new Airbus aircraft, A350 XWB and A320neo.

The global aerospace fasteners' market is highly competitive, where vendors contend based on reliability, cost, product quality and aftermarket service. Aerospace fastener manufacturers are increasingly focusing on emerging economies such as Mexico, Brazil, India and North Africa owing to their high requirement for commercial aircraft. Such immediate needs have opened huge growth opportunities for third-party companies to collaborate with Original Equipment Manufacturers (OEMs) and open several maintenances, repair and overhaul (MRO) service centres in these regions.

"The commercial aerospace fasteners' segment dominated the market in 2016 and will continue to maintain the lead over the forecast period. The development of low-cost airlines to provide economical air travel is a major growth driver for this segment as manufacturers are increasing passenger capacity and cutting back in-flight comfort and facilities. The high number of deliveries, orders, backlogs of low-cost carriers and more electric aircraft (MEA) will further fuel the demand for fasteners during the forecast period," added the report.

"The Americas is the largest region in the global aerospace fasteners market and accounted for the highest market share during 2016. North America leads the aerospace fasteners market in the Americas, in terms of expenditure and technological advancements. Much of the region's growth is due to the rapid increase in the production of long-range aircraft and military aircraft programmes, which will fuel further investments in the market."

## Aerospace and defence (A&D)

According to a research report by Deloitte, the global aerospace and defence industry was likely to experience stronger growth in 2017. The report indicates that the industry has experienced multiple years of a positive but subdued rate of growth and, in 2017, the Aerospace and Defence (A&D) industry was estimated to grow by about 2.0 per cent.

The global A&D sector revenue rebound is attributed to various factors in the commercial aerospace sub-sector and the defence subsector. Defence sub-sector revenues, said the report, were likely to grow at a much faster 3.2 per cent in 2017. This is due to stronger defence spending in the United States, which has returned to growth after several years of declines in defence budgets. The report by Deloitte indicates that future growth was expected to be driven by the new U.S. administration's increased focus on strengthening the U.S. military.

"Rising global tensions have led to increasing demand for defence and military products in the Middle East, Eastern Europe, North Korea and the East and South China Seas. This is in turn resulting in increased defence spending globally, especially in the United Arab Emirates (UAE), Saudi Arabia, South Korea, Japan, India, China and Russia," said the research.
