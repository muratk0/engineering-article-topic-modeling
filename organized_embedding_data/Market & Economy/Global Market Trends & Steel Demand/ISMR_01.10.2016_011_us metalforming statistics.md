# US metalforming statistics

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO THE August 2016 Precision Metalforming Association (PMA) Business Conditions Report, metalforming companies expect an improvement in business conditions during the next three months. Prepared monthly, the report is an economic indicator for manufacturing, sampling 122 metalforming companies in the United States and Canada.

The August report shows an upswing in economic activity, with 30% of participants predicting that activity would improve in the next three months (up from 22% in July), 54% anticipating no change (compared to 56%) and 16% believing that economic activity will decline (down from 22% in July).

Metalforming companies also forecast an improvement in incoming orders during the next three months, with 38% expecting an increase (up from 32 % in July), 44% predicting no change (compared to 50%) and 18% anticipating a decrease in orders (the same percentage reported in July).

However, average daily shipping levels dipped in August. Only 29% of participants reported that shipping levels are above levels of three months previously (down from 36% in July), 36% reported that levels are the same as three months ago (compared to 37%) and 35% reported a decrease in shipping levels (up from 27% in July).

The percentage of metalforming companies with a portion of their workforce on short time or layoff dropped to 11% in August, down from 13% in July. The August 2016 figure is the same as it was at this time last year.

"Optimism about general business conditions is at the highest level since February," said William E. Gaskin, PMA President. "A seven-month slide in the trend for incoming orders also reversed itself in August, perhaps in response to a rising equities market, lack of action by the Federal Reserve and a calming of fears about near-term side effects from Brexit. Also, PMA's separate orders and shipments surveys indicate that for the period of January to June 2016, average new orders for metalforming companies have been dead-flat year-over-year, while average shipments declined by 3% over the same period. Higher costs for steel due to import duties assessed in the past six months have also negatively impacted metalforming companies who are not on automatic price-adjustment programmes with their customers. So, profitability is also being challenged."

PMA is the full-service trade association representing the US$ 137-billion metalforming industry of North America—the industry that creates precision metal products using stamping, fabricating, spinning, slide forming and roll forming technologies, and other value-added processes.■
