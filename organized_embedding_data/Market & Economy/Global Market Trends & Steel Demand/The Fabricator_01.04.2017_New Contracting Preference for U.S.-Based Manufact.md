resident Donald Trump’s rhetoric about rejuvenating U.S. manufacturing hits reality as Congress begins work on converting the president’s "Made in America" emphasis into legislation that commands the support of both Republicans and Democrats, which is not necessarily an easy feat these days.

Some interesting bills that already have been introduced seem to be high-visibility targets for bipartisan support. One is the Retain Act, H.R. 1185, which would establish a "preference" for federal contract bidders that submit a certification that confirms the contractor and its subcontractors will not relocate jobs from the U.S. to foreign countries during the period of performance of the contract and will use products substantially manufactured in the U.S. and services from domestic companies.

Preferences already exist in the law for small businesses, woman-owned contracting companies, and companies owned by minorities, so why not include companies that keep jobs in the U.S. and purchase goods and services from other U.S. manufacturers? Rep. Tim Ryan, D-Ohio, co-chair of the House Manufacturing Caucus who introduced the bill, thinks it makes sense to provide this type of preference.

"Companies that receive federal contracts paid for with taxpayer funds should keep their operations in the U.S. That is common sense," he said. "Rewarding companies that ship jobs overseas with lucrative contracts sets a bad precedent that will only work to further erode our American manufacturing base, and the families it sustains."

But his office did not respond to a query as to whether the bill had attracted Republican support or whether Ryan has talked with anyone in the Trump administration about support for his bill. So far all House co-sponsors are Democrats. A companion Senate bill has not emerged either.

The National Association of Manufacturers did not respond to a query asking whether it supports the bill.

Upgrading Visibility of Manufacturing Within the White House

Another bill Ryan is co-sponsoring does have bipartisan support already. That bill would establish the position of U.S. chief manufacturing officer in the West Wing.

Rep. Tom Reed, R-N.Y., the other co-chairman of the bipartisan House Manufacturing Caucus, is an original co-sponsor. Sens. Chris Coons, D-Del., and Susan Collins, R-Maine, introduced the companion bill in the Senate.

The U.S. chief manufacturing officer (CMO) will be responsible for coordinating manufacturing-related policies and activities across agencies. The CMO also will develop a national manufacturing strategy to revitalize the manufacturing sector, spur economic growth, and expand U.S. competitiveness.

"A chief manufacturing officer in the Executive Office of the President that sets a strategy based on rapid innovation is the best support our government can give American manufacturers as they respond to the opportunity and challenges of an increasingly connected, automated world," said Douglas K. Woods, president, The Association For Manufacturing Technology.

Other major trade associations have come out in support of this move. Those groups include American Society of Mechanical Engineers, Automation Federation, International Society of Automation, Information Technology & Innovation Foundation, Intelligent Systems International, Manufacturing Alliance of Communities, National Center for Defense Manufacturing and Machining, and National Council for Advanced Manufacturing.
