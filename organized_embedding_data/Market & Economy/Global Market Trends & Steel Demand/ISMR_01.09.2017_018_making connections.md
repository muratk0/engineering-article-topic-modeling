# MAKING CONNECTIONS

[TARİH: 01.09.2017 ISMR]

We highlight some of the major trends and developments affecting the growth of global consumer electronics and home appliance markets, with a focus on the rise of ‘smart' technologies.

The rebuilding of cities and fast pace of life is driving new technologies to enter and shape people's living environments.

"People are looking for smarter, smaller and more efficient appliances for their homes to improve space efficiency. More smart devices are already entering people's homes and they are increasingly adopting the Internet of Everything. As the population ages, more efficient homes and services are needed to better serve the needs of an aging population," commented Matthew Crabbe, research director of APAC at analyst, Mintel.

## A global view

According to analyst, Futuresource Consulting, Consumer Electronics (CE)

market revenues returned to growth in 2016 following 2015's 'blip'. Growth was modest, however, at 1.2% (to reach US$ 680 billion), it said, as 'emerging' markets faltered, mobile revenues slowed and categories (such as small-screen TVs and cameras) continued to be squeezed by convergence. Despite the challenges, however, several hotspots of innovation are fuelling growth in CE including VR & AR, connected cars, voice assistants and smart homes, with stability returning to TV and PCs.

Representing almost one in five dollars spent on CE, 'traditional' categories such as TV, audio and video equipment stabilised last year, thanks to improving TV sales and the continued growth in wireless speakers, headphones and digital media adapters (which includes the likes of Amazon Fire TV Stick, Roku, Apple TV etc.), offsetting declines in set-top boxes and Blu-ray/DVD equipment.

"Mobile grew 5% to represent half of total CE revenues for the first time, at US$ 338 billion, according to the analyst. Consumer PC sales fell marginally, but look to be relatively stable going forward, helped by the boom in online gaming, growth in 2-in-1 tablets and Chromebook. Consumer tablet sales fell 16% as shipments of 'phablets' surged. Game consoles fell 9% to US$ 10.5 billion, but within this handheld games fell 40% as a result of competition from mobile apps. VR will boost this market in 2017," said the analyst.

"Imaging equipment declined 16% to US$ 11.4 billion in 2015, and will continue to decline before the traditional camera market starts 'bottoming out' over the next 2-3 years. Hotspots like high-end ILC, video-drones and instant print 'Polaroid-like' cameras offset some of the decline in this category. Despite a significant market slowdown in 2016 for wearables, the market is expected to recover as smartwatch designs improve and applications emerge. VR will fuel headsets for PC, console and mobile gaming. The market for fitness wearables remains relatively robust at the enthusiast level; however, the volume end of the market is softening as the novelty of activity tracking wears thin."

Despite not living up to expectations to date, Futuresource Consulting expected the smart home market to grow 30% this year to achieve US$ 3 billion in sales across diverse product categories. The market, it said, is skewed to the U.S. which will represent more than 50% of spend this year, driven by convenience lifestyle, technology awareness, affluence and a high level of security and safety awareness. It estimates that up to 40% of connected home devices are now supplied by service providers, notably security and communications firms.

"The impact of Amazon's success with Echo will open up a scenario where smart home might 'piggy-back' as added value on a primary acquisition driver (audio in the case of Echo)," it said.

Emerging markets, which now represent over half of the worldwide CE market, have badly weakened since 2014, largely due to weakening economies in important regional hubs such as Russia, Brazil and more recently Nigeria, Africa's largest economy and market. The falling price of oil, unrest in the Middle East and currency fluctuations brought about by sanctions on Russia and, more recently, Brexit are contributing to price pressure and slowing demand. North America remains strong at 21% of global CE demand, driven by affluence and avid demand for new technology.

Futuresource's 5-year market outlook for consumer electronics is 1% CAGR, with revenues reaching US$ 710 billion by 2021, subject to a relatively stable world economy.

Persistence Market Research indicated staggering growth prospects for the global consumer electronics market. It expected smartphones to remain the dominant consumer electronics product type and Asia-Pacific to continue to be the leading region over 2016-2020.

"Growing awareness of the benefits of electronic devices will also be a key factor strongly supporting market growth. Rising popularity of the connected homes concept is likely to push the demand for smart home devices over the forecast period. Smart mobile devices, kitchen appliances, HVAC systems and intelligent security systems will especially gain traction within the next few years. With rising government support for digitalisation of a variety of services, the market is likely to witness surging promotion and the adoption of consumer electronics. Moreover, technological advancements and consistent R&D leading to new product launches will boost market growth during the forecast period," said the analyst.

## Accelerating demand for home appliances

The home appliances sector accelerated in 2016, as global shipments grew by 4% to 247 million refrigeration and laundry units, according to the latest 'Worldwide Home Appliance Market Report' published by Futuresource Consulting. Continued volume growth is expected over the next five years, for a market that is worth US$ 85 billion per year.

"Whilst growth in the established markets of North America and Western Europe was 3%, this solid performance was outstripped by emerging markets in Asia and Africa, where some consumers are acquiring devices for the first time. However, Latin America underperformed for the second year in a row due to economic headwinds," said the analyst. The rapid roll-out of appliances with 'smart' features is now expected to be a priority for the industry.

"Manufacturers are now focusing on the inclusion of smart features and we anticipate that half of all home appliances that ship in 2021 will offer online connectivity," said Filipe Oliveira, market analyst at Futuresource Consulting. "Connectivity has the potential to open the door for manufacturers to provide services linked to the major domestic appliances that they sell. These could include diagnostic and maintenance services, management and ordering of consumables, improved fabric care or even enhanced cooking experiences."

In laundry, a trend towards high-capacity models across all regions is noticeable. Dryers are experiencing a period of strong sales growth in Southern Europe, where historically uptake has been low. In refrigeration, multi-door devices and premium features such as ice dispensers are making their way into the middle range of the market in the mature markets of Western Europe and North America.

"The average retail price of major appliances is still declining but a move towards higher-end devices and the uptake of smart features in mature markets should help average prices stabilise. Whirlpool, Electrolux and Haier continued to dominate the major domestic appliances global market in 2016, with a combined unit share of over 40%," added the analyst.

"The global major home appliance (white-goods) market, estimated to be nearly 640 million units in 2016, is forecast to reach 800 million units in 2020," added analyst, IHS.

The electric household appliances market share is poised to surpass US$ 600 billion by 2024, according to a new research report by Global Market Insights, Inc. Global industry shipments are anticipated to reach one billion units by 2024.

"With a rise in environmental and social consciousness, many consumers are looking to diminish the impact on the atmosphere. Driven by the desire to contribute towards the environmental ecosystem, consumers are seeking products which are energy-efficient and cost-effective, thereby driving the electric household appliances market," explained the analyst.
