# Manufacturing flashes strong comeback signs

[TARİH: 01.07.2026 The Fabricator]

Tube & Pipe Market Update

Pipe and tube sectors join the ascent out of the doldrums in latest data

By

Rick Preckel

MANUFACTURING PERFORMANCE

The durable goods manufacturing output index improved in April by 1.2% after being flat from February to March, according to data from the U.S. Federal Reserve.

Pipe- and tube-consuming categories were broadly positive, with improvements in motor vehicles and parts (+3.7%), electrical equipment and appliances (+0.4%), aerospace and miscellaneous transportation equipment (+1.6%), fabricated metal products (+0.6%), and machinery (+0.6%). These gains were partially offset by declines in furniture and related products (-1.8%) and miscellaneous manufacturing (-0.2%).

Durable goods manufacturing in April was 3.2% above year-ago levels. In addition, durable goods manufacturing capacity utilization in April was 75.1% compared to 74.2% in March.

The May Manufacturing PMI Report, as published by the Institute for Supply Management (ISM), indicated that economic activity in the manufacturing sector, as measured by the Manufacturing PMI, remained in expansion for the fifth consecutive month with a reading of 54.0%. (Growth in the sector is indicated by a reading above 50.) This is 1.3 points higher than April and is the highest reading since May 2022.

Looking at the index components, the New Orders Index expanded for the fifth straight month in May, moving (strongly) to 56.8% from 54.1% in April. The New Export Orders Index reading of 50.6% was 2.7 percentage points higher than the April reading, and the Backlog of Orders Index registered 52.2%, up 0.8 percentage point from the April level. The Production Index for May remained in expansion territory at 54.3% and represented a 0.9 percentage point increase from April levels.

The Pricing Index in May came in at 82.1%, which indicates rising prices, but at a slower pace than April, as the Index contracted by 2.5 percentage points.

"In May, U.S. manufacturing activity remained in expansion territory, growing at a faster pace compared to the month before," said Susan Spence, MBA, chair of the ISM Manufacturing Business Survey Committee. "Of the five subindexes that make up the PMI, the New Orders Index indicated faster growth compared to the previous month. The Supplier Deliveries Index stayed the same, the Production Index grew at a faster rate, and the Employment and Inventories Indexes remained in contraction, though both improved."

While activity levels improved, there were more negative than positive comments in the survey—mainly over the Iran war, tariffs, and pricing volatility.

Tubing-intensive manufacturing industries reporting growth in May were electrical equipment, appliances and components, miscellaneous manufacturing, furniture and related products, machinery, transportation equipment, and fabricated metal products. For more information, visit

www.ismrob.org

.

DOMESTIC PIPE, TUBE, AND STEEL MILL NEWS

U. S. Steel announced in May a $1.9 billion investment to build a new direct reduced iron facility at Big River Steel Works in Osceola, Ark.—the first of its kind in the United States. The new facility will leverage U. S. Steel’s 2022 investment into direct reduced-grade pellet capabilities at its Minnesota Ore Operations Keetac plant, creating a direct link among mining operations, EAF feedstock creation, and steel production at Big River Steel Works.

SHIPPING AND IMPORTS

Preliminary pipe and tube shipments for April for the sectors of the market that we cover increased from March by 1.0% and were 1.0% lower than a year ago. Manufacturing and construction product shipments in April increased from March by 0.6% and were 5.1% higher than a year ago.

April preliminary shipments of mechanical tubing products decreased by 0.5% from March and were 3.1% lower than April of last year.

OUTLOOK

Mechanical and hollow structural section pipe and tube product prices have continued to rise with additional price increase announcements in April and May. These are largely driven by rising hot-rolled band (HRB) prices, which have continued their upward trend. Most analysts suggest that weak HRB imports are the primary driver of higher domestic HRB prices.

The United States Trade Representative’s structural excess-capacity Section 301 investigation, which covers China, the EU, Japan, Mexico, South Korea, Vietnam, India, and other nations, has moved into the post-hearing/rebuttal review phase. Public comments closed in mid-April, and hearings were held May 5-8. The probe targets multisector manufacturing, including steel and machinery, where remedies could include new tariffs layered onto existing duties, potentially 7.5% to 25%. No determinations have been issued yet. The 10% Section 122 tariffs expire on July 24.

An April 2 presidential proclamation restructured how Section 232 tariffs apply to steel pipe, tube, and derivative products by clarifying the assessment methodology.

Effective April 6, Section 232 duties on steel articles and steel-derivative products are applied to the full customs value of the imported product, eliminating prior metal-content allocation and breakout reporting.

Transportation and fuel costs remain elevated because of the Iran war. As of early June, an agreement to end the war had not been reached.

FLAT-ROLLED STEEL

According to data from the SteelBenchmarker, the index for base domestic HRB prices moved to 1.39 in April from 1.36 in March and from 1.06 in September 2025. According to other sources, prices have continued to increase since the last reading. Sources tell us that lead times for hot-rolled coil and cold-rolled coil in the U.S. are about eight weeks.
