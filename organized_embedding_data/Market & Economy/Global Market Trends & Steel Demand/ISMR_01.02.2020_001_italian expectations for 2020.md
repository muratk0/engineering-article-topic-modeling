# Italian expectations for 2020

[TARİH: 01.02.2020 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tools, robotics and automation systems manufacturers association, 2019 marked the end of the positive trend kick-started in 2014 in Italy.

“Nevertheless, the registered decrease is very moderate and shows that main economic indicators are returning to normal levels, after the implementation of measures supporting Industry/ Enterprise 4.0. This slowdown will continue in 2020," explained Massimo Carboniero, President of the association.

“As evidenced by the preliminary year's data, processed by the Studies Dept. & Business Culture of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in 2019 production recorded a 4.9% decrease compared to the previous year. This was due to the reduction in deliveries by Italian manufacturers to their domestic market, down by 8.1%, as well as to negative trends affecting exports, which decreased by 2.3%. Based on our ISTAT data processing, in the first eight months of 2019 (latest available survey), the main destination countries for Made in Italy products by the sector were: United States (+15.1%); Germany (-4%); China (-13.4%); France (+17.6%) and Poland (-16.2%), said UCIMU.

“On the domestic front, in 2019, Italian consumption of machine tools, robots and automation dropped by 7.2%. The export/ production ratio registered a slight increase, changing from 54.1% in 2018 to 55.6% in 2019. In 2020, the Italian industry sector should report a further slowdown, despite remaining at very high levels (equal to those in 2017). Production will go down (-8.4%), negatively affected by exports, and is expected to decrease by 5.3%. Consumption, i.e. Italian user demand, will stop at (-10.1%) The partial downsizing of the domestic market will have repercussions both on the deliveries of Italian manufacturers, which will drop to (-12.2%), and on imports (-7%). The export/production ratio will gain about two percentage points, accounting for 57.5%," continued the association.

Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, commented: “The analysis of data processed by the Studies Dept. & Business Culture of the association shows that the international situation is clearly influencing the Italian machine tool, robot and automation industry, which registered its first hiatus after five years of continuous growth. The decrease reported in 2019 and expected for 2020 is likely to be moderate. However, these results, which are very close to those recorded in 2017 (a decidedly positive year) highlight a slow repositioning of Italian industry back to normal levels. We can trace normal levels back to 2016, before the effect of Industry/Enterprise 4.0 incentives implemented by the Government authorities. This is confirmed by applications to exhibit at 32.BI-MU, the biennial machine tool exhibition taking place at Fieramilano Rho from 4-17 October 2020. Currently, the surface area booked by exhibitors is equal to that booked in the same period for the 2018 show.

Carboniero emphasised the importance of digitisation and the digital transformation of production on the Italian manufacturing industry. He urged the industry to focus on this to both maintain and increase Italy's competitive advantages.

“The new measures for innovation introduced by the Italian government encourage us to continue in this direction. I am referring to the three tax credits. The first is for R&D activities, the second is a replacement for Super-Depreciation, with a 6% rate to purchase capital goods applied to investments up to a maximum of two million euros. The third is a replacement for Hyper-Depreciation, for those who purchase 4.0 equipment, with a rate of 40% for investments up to 2.5 million euros and 20% for investments between 2.5 and 10 million euros. Nevertheless, we still fear that tools put at the disposal of the Italian industry, such as Super- and Hyper-Depreciation, together with new ones included in the Budget Law 2020, may cause confusion in companies accustomed to the previous competitiveness measures," he added.

He welcomed confirmation of the provision for professional education and 4.0 training for staff, allowing them to adapt to the new requirements of digitalised factories. However, he also urged the inclusion of costs for teachers and trainers, key expenses for SMEs. He also called on the Italian government to facilitate the international reach of Italian SMEs, especially to far-away countries, hoping that ’appropriate resources will be allocated to the ICE-Italian Trade Agency's project for overseas buyers attending international trade shows taking place in Italy'. He also encouraged government authorities to support 'the creation of enterprise networks for the study and analysis of specific areas or sectors.

THE A-PILLAR and sill of the new Volkswagen ID.3 electric car are being produced on a hot stamping line by supplier Snop in Klášterec in the Czech Republic. The ID.3 rolls off the line in Zwickau (Germany). Outer body parts for the ID.3 are also being produced on Schuler machines, such as the press line which Schuler will install at the Volkswagen site in Zwickau this spring.

Commissioning of the hot stamping line took place in summer last year. The 1,200-metric ton press hardening system comes with blank loader; oven to heat the blanks to 930 degrees Celsius; three-axis twin feeders for rapid transport and dies to form and cool the parts. Schuler's PCH ("Pressure Controlled Hardening") technology offers short cycle time of up to eight seconds for lightweight as well as high-strength components.

The ID.3 is based on the modular electric drive matrix (MEB) developed by Volkswagen. The special model ID.3 1ST, with power of 107kW and a top speed of 160km per hour, has a range of up to 420km and will be delivered in summer 2020. In addition, there will be models with a range of 330 and 550km.

Schuler offers technology in all areas of forming – from networked presses to press shop planning. The product portfolio also includes automation and software solutions, dies, process expertise and service for the metalworking industry. Presses from the Schuler Group mint coins for more than 180 countries. The company is majority-owned by the Austrian ANDRITZ Group.
