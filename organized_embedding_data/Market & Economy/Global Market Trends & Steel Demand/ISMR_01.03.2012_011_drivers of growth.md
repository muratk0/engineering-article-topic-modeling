# DRIVERS OF GROWTH

[TARİH: 01.03.2012 ISMR]

We analyse growth trends and patterns in Asian automotive markets

2011 was a volatile year for the global automotive sector, due to unforeseen natural disasters (such as the Japanese earthquake) and global economic uncertainty.

China has been the fastest-growing economy, expanding at 10 per cent annually, driven by exports and investment. The Chinese automotive industry has emerged as a driver of economic growth, supported by government initiatives and increased investment. The recent development plans of the government aim to develop energysaving and electric vehicles. Opportunities exist in power battery technology, infrastructure development, emission reduction and system integration technology.

As India is evolving, so are its mobility woes. Challenges in India, such as inadequate roadway accommodation for buses and non-motorised mass transport systems, and unplanned haphazard development at the suburban fringe without adequate infrastructure, transport and other public services etc. are likely to have a huge impact on future automotive demand in India.

## Asia in view

Industry analyst, Frost & Sullivan, expects Indonesia’s total automotive industry volume (TIV) to increase at a moderate pace of 6.5 per cent year-on-year to 948,500 units in 2012 supported by a stable local economy, positive consumer sentiment and the launch of new car models.

Vivek Vaidya, Vice President, Automotive Practice Asia Pacific at Frost & Sullivan said that 2012 will witness the launch or facelift of about 25-30 new car models in Indonesia, which is likely to increase vehicle sales.

“Japanese OEMs, mainly Toyota, Honda and Suzuki, are likely to be front runners in terms of new model introduction as they race to launch a combined 15-20 models in 2012,” he said, adding that Ford, Hyundai, Proton and BMW are likely to introduce more than 2 models in 2012 to make their mark in the Indonesian market.

Mr. Vaidya said that vehicle sales in 2012 will be driven by the 4x2 segment with a 6.5 per cent year-on-year growth to 606,100 units, driven by key model launches. He added that the sedan segment is likely to decline 4.5 per cent year-on-year to 25,520 units in 2012 as consumers’ preferences switch to sports utility vehicles (SUVs) and compact city cars.

He also projects the 4x4 segment to grow 12.3 per cent year-on-year to 6,380 units, supported by popular models such as Pajero, Ford Ranger and a few other premium branded models.

Indonesia has overtaken Thailand as the largest automotive market in ASEAN in 2011.
