# Manufacturers could benefit from One Big Beautiful Bill

[TARİH: 01.07.2025 The Fabricator]

Around Washington

Some senators look to extend key provisions beyond the five-year period proposed by the House

OMAR NASHASHIBI

F

ollowing a narrow 215-214 vote in the U.S. House of Representatives on May 22, the U.S. Senate has begun to move on the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1). This budget reconciliation package, which passed entirely along party lines in the House with no Democratic support and dissent from two Republicans, includes tax and spending provisions as well as a $4 trillion increase to the federal debt limit.

The reconciliation package revisits and reconfigures several provisions from the 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (TCJA) that had either expired or were on the verge of expiration. In doing so, it cements a number of tax policies vital for manufacturers along with those championed by President Donald Trump’s administration while also providing increased funding for defense and border security. Key tax and economic provisions of the House-passed One Big Beautiful Bill Act include:

•

Bonus depreciation

. Extends the 100% bonus depreciation through 2029, with retroactive effect from Jan. 1, 2025. This allows businesses to immediately deduct the full cost of qualifying capital investments, boosting short-term cash flow and long-term investment planning.

•

R&D expensing restoration

. Reinstitutes full expensing for research and development costs through 2029 by eliminating depreciation’s previously required amortization for tax years 2025 to 2029. This change aims to accelerate technological innovation and maintain a competitive edge in high-tech industries.

•

Section 163(j) interest deduction overhaul.

Changes the interest deduction calculation back to an EBITDA standard, effective retroactively from Jan. 1, 2025, alongside increasing the gross receipts threshold from $31 million to $100 million. This adjustment is intended to ease the tax burden on businesses by aligning deductions more closely with operating performance.

•

Production property depreciation incentive.

Introduces a groundbreaking 100% depreciation incentive for qualified production property that applies to construction commencing between Jan. 20, 2025, and Dec. 31, 2028—with assets required to be placed in service by Dec. 31, 2032. This measure directly targets the manufacturing sector, encouraging investments in new facilities and equipment.

•

Permanent Section 199A deduction

. Makes the pass-through business deduction permanent while raising the maximum deduction to 23% of qualified business income, providing a more stable tax environment for small and medium-sized enterprises.

•

Section 179 and small-business enhancements.

Increases the Section 179 expensing limit to $2.5 million—with phase-outs beginning at $4 million—and raises the small-business gross receipts threshold from $25 million to $80 million, thereby broadening access to simplified expensing rules for companies across various sizes.

•

Estate and gift tax reforms

. Permanently enhances estate and gift tax exemptions, setting the deduction at $15 million from 2026 onward, with automatic adjustments for inflation—a change poised to affect longterm wealth planning for high-networth individuals.

•

Educational and workforce investments

. Expands Pell Grant eligibility starting July 1, 2026, to include students pursuing short-term, workforce-focused credential programs; broadens the scope of 529 plans to cover qualified postsecondary credentialing expenses; and permanently extends Section 127 employer education assistance (including tax-free employer payments on student loans) with inflation-adjusted limits. These elements reflect a broader commitment to fostering a skilled workforce for the future.

In their revisions, several senators have signaled interest in extending key provisions—such as bonus depreciation, R&D expensing, and the EBITDA standard for interest deductions—beyond the five-year period proposed by the House. These expansions may make critical elements permanent or extend them for an entire decade. With Senate efforts targeted for completion by July 4, any substantial modifications will require further House review before the legislation can be forwarded to President Trump for signature.
