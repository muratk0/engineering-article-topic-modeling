# Domestic surge for Italian machine tool orders

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

IN THE THIRD quarter of 2016, the index of machine tool orders, processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian machine tool and robotics association), recorded a 5.8% decrease compared to the same period in the previous year. This was due, said the association, to negative trends in overseas markets.

"The index of overseas orders registered a 6.8% drop, compared to the period July-September 2015, showing the weakness of international demand, explained UCIMU. “According to our processing of ISTAT data for the first seven months of 2016 (the latest available survey), exports of Italian machine tools decreased by 4.7%. A sales increase was reported in Germany (+9.6%), France (+37.4%) and Poland (+5.8%), whereas sales slowed down in the United States (-7.9%), secondary outlet markets and in China (-16.8%)."

On the other hand, the positive trend continued for orders collected by Italian manufacturers in the domestic market, increasing by 11% in the period. This is the thirteenth consecutive quarter of growth in the Italian market, according to the association.

The 30.BI-MU/SFORTEC INDUSTRY, the biennial machine tool exhibition, took place at the beginning of October in Milan. Over 62,000 visits (6% more visits by professional operators compared to the previous show), 1,076 exhibiting companies and 90,000 square metres of total exhibition area characterised this year's event.

Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, commented: "Of course, we are not pleased with the slight slowdown shown by the index. Decreases were reported in some important overseas markets, such as the United States. Other markets slowed down (e.g. China) and others had difficulties (e.g. Russia). However, history teaches us that Italian manufacturers are capable of re-orienting their products towards profitable areas.

“The demand for machine tools in Italy is high due to the need to modernise production plants in the country, supported by several measures such as the New Sabatini Law and Super Depreciation. The effectiveness of these measures, also evidenced by a survey we carried out, convinced the Italian Government authorities to transform them into the 'pillars' of the National Plan 'Industry 4.0', included in the Stability Law for 2017.

"Confirmation of the 140% Super Depreciation for machinery purchases; re-financing of the New Sabatini Law; the introduction of the 250% Hyper-Depreciation for investments in innovative technologies and the increase of tax credits from 25% to 50% for investments in R&D - these are the key points of the Plan, at last bringing the manufacturing industry back into the spotlight for Italian economic strategy."

## PMF Industries expands manufacturing facilities

PMF INDUSTRIES HAS announced the completion of a new, 14,000 sq. ft. expansion that includes a hot-spinning operation and space for a Class 1000 cleanroom as well as the company's existing mechanical polishing, electropolishing and passivation functions. Along with its new flowforming equipment, which can produce flowformed parts of up to 48 inches in diameter, the expansion positions the company for increased business in the aerospace, food and beverage, pharmaceutical, microelectronics and defence industries as well as the energy and transportation sectors.

PMF's new hot-spinning operations, which involve the heating of an axially symmetrical, metal tube spun on a lathe at high speeds and the use of various tools to shape it, will produce canisters able to withstand up to 5,000 psi of pressure to hold compressed natural gas, oxygen and other gases used for transportation, pharmaceutical or military applications.

The company is in the process of relocating its mechanical polishing, electropolishing and passivation operations to the new site, which offers better air filtration and a brighter, more spacious work environment and includes clerestory windows. The new electropolishing and passivation area, where metal-worked components will be physically and/or chemically stripped of free iron and elements such as chromium when placed in a series of chemical baths and rinses (some while undergoing an electrical charge), will feature new tanks, an overhead hoist system and the addition of a citric acid bath for use with more environmentally-sensitive applications.

As an enhancement to its core business of producing flowformed parts, PMF also recently expanded its capabilities with the addition of two new machines, a hot-spinning machine and a flowforming machine, featuring four rollers instead of three, allowing the company to flowform parts of up to 48 inches in diameter. Flowforming is a cold-extrusion, metal-forming process that produces seamless, high-precision, rotationally symmetrical cylindrical, conical or contoured shapes. It offers an economical method of producing hollow shafts used in many industries. The process offers design flexibility and can produce complex, near-net-shaped components from preforms or blanks, resulting in significant material savings and dramatically lowering costs.
