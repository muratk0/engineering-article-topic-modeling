SLIVERS OF SUNSHINE FOR TUBE AND PIPE AS MANUFACTURING CONTINUES TO DRAG

By

Rick Preckel

MANUFACTURING PERFORMANCE

Data from the U.S. Federal Reserve indicates that the durable goods manufacturing output index improved in September by 0.1% after a similar improvement in August.

Pipe-and tube-consuming categories were mixed, with improvements in fabricated metal products (+1.2%), electrical equipment and appliances (+1.8%), aerospace and miscellaneous transportation equipment (+1.4%), and furniture and related products (+0.3%). These gains were offset by negative month-to-month comparisons in machinery (-0.1%), motor vehicles and parts (-2.2%), and miscellaneous manufacturing (-0.5%).

Durable goods manufacturing in September was 3.0% above year-ago levels. Durable goods manufacturing capacity utilization held steady in September at 74.0%. As of this writing, government data remains delayed because of the recent shutdown.

The November Manufacturing PMI Report

®

, as published by the Institute for Supply Management

®

(ISM

®

), indicated that economic activity in the manufacturing sector, as measured by the Manufacturing PMI, remained in contraction territory at 48.2%. This represents the ninth consecutive month of contraction. Growth in the sector is indicated by a reading above 50.

Breaking it down, the New Orders Index contracted for the third straight month in November to 47.4%, which is two points below the October reading of 49.4%. The New Export Orders Index, at 46.2%, was 1.7 percentage points higher than the 44.5% registered in October. The Production Index for November moved into expansion territory at 51.4%, 3.2 points higher than October’s reading of 48.2%.

The Pricing Index continued to indicate rising prices with a reading of 58.5% as compared to 58.0% in October. Meanwhile, the Backlog of Orders Index registered 44.0%, down 3.9 percentage points from the October reading of 47.9%.

Susan Spence, MBA, chair of the ISM Manufacturing Business Survey Committee, noted, "Looking at the manufacturing economy, 58% of the sector’s gross domestic product (GDP) contracted in November, matching the previous month’s figure, and the percentage of GDP in strong contraction (registering a composite PMI of 45% or lower) decreased slightly, at 39% compared to 41% in October. The share of sector GDP with a PMI at or below 45% is a good metric to gauge overall manufacturing weakness."

SHIPPING AND IMPORTS

Preliminary pipe and tube shipments for October for the sectors of the market that we cover decreased from September by 2.1% and but were 8.1% ahead of a year ago. Because of a data lag remaining from the federal government shutdown, import volumes for September and October are based on licenses. Manufacturing and construction product shipments in October decreased from September by 2.9% and were 5.6% lower than a year ago.

October preliminary shipments of mechanical tubing products, those most closely associated with manufacturing activity, increased by 2.6% from September but were 6.2% lower than October of last year.

OUTLOOK

On Nov. 10, the U.S. and China reached a trade deal. The deal suspends for one year the implementation of a 100% tariff increase that the U.S. government had threatened, while China has agreed to suspend retaliatory tariffs and export controls on rare-earth minerals.

China also committed to buying at least 25 million metric tonnes of soybeans from the U.S. annually through 2028, while the U.S. agreed to extend Section 301 tariff exclusions through Nov. 10, 2026.

The Federal Reserve decided to lower short-term interest rates by 25 basis points at its meeting in October, and odds were high for another 25-basis-point reduction at its meeting in early December. Economic data continues to be mixed with the most recent inflation data showing an uptick, while the September jobs data (the latest available) was stronger than expected.

FLAT-ROLLED STEEL

According to data from the SteelBenchmarker, the index for base domestic hot-rolled band prices moved to 1.09 in November from 1.06 in October. According to other sources, prices have continued to increase since the last reading. For this cycle, the reading peaked in April at 1.22 and hit 1.06 in September.
