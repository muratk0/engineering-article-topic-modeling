If there has been any other story other than the impact of the pandemic affecting business of late, it has been that of the labor supply. There is not an industry sector or part of the country that has not been affected by the labor shortage. There are not enough skilled workers and not enough unskilled workers. There are not enough professionals and too many retirements. There are people unwilling or unable to work and there are more demands from the workforce than ever as they suddenly seem to have the leverage they have lacked in the past. What has happened to elevate this issue to the top of business concerns? It’s not just one thing but many factors that have all added to the cumulative impact.

There are not many immediate solutions to the labor shortage for the manufacturer. The people that will be coming off the unemployment roll in the next couple of months will not be taking these jobs in the industrial sector — they came out of the service sector, and they will return to the service sector. The manufacturer must have trained and skilled workers and that requires a pipeline that goes from the trade schools, training centers, and community colleges to the industry and there are simply not enough people coming through. In the past, there was reliance on immigration but the vast majority of those seeking entry into the U.S. lack the required skills. There has been an attempt to delay people’s retirement or to lure people out of that retirement but this is far harder to do with a manufacturing worker than for someone working in an office. For most manufacturers, the only short-term option is to poach somebody else’s worker but luring people away with more money and perks can be expensive.

COMPANIES HAVE BEEN REPORTING THEY’VE BEEN FORCED TO TURN AWAY BUSINESS BECAUSE THEY SIMPLY CAN’T STAFF UP TO MEET THAT DEMAND.

What does this mean for manufacturing? The impact of the labor shortage is about as obvious as it can be. Companies are reporting they’ve been forced to turn away business because they simply can’t staff up to meet that demand. This crisis is not ending with the relaxation of pandemic protocols — the problem existed before all this and will persist after the pandemic issue fades.
