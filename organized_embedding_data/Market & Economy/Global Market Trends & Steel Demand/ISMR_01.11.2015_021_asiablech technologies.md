ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in Asian sheet metal markets?

DT: Automation is a key trend right now and China has just become the largest market consumer of robotics. The Chinese government decided to subsidise automation production in China and there was a rush to market of low cost technology for that. This focus on automation was driven, I believe, by rising wages, higher labour costs generally and even the prospect of labour shortages. So the adoption of automated solutions is one way to boost productivity.

However, as demand slowed with the economy, so did the appetite for cheap and quick supply. Users, at the moment, are not so much looking for low cost equipment but complex and high-end technology which will provide a competitive edge. There appears to be a trend towards some Chinese manufacturers utilising more components from outside China, that is, the “best in class” components from Europe or elsewhere. China is not such a low wage economy any more – the standard and cost of living has risen somewhat with growth and wealth creation, so skilled labour is at more of a premium. High quality automated machinery pays in this market.

Underpinning all this, demand is still growing a rapid pace, especially in certain sectors. Manufacturing growth rates of 5 or 6% are forecast for the next few years. But, for example, the Chinese kitchen appliance market experienced an 11% increase year-on-year in 2014. There is still room for enormous growth as urbanisation continues – growth in the market for kitchen appliances is expected to increase by at least one percentage point per year. We will certainly be targeting this manufacturing sector during the visitor promotion phase for AsiaBLECH next year.
