# FOSTERING GROWTH

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

ISMR: Please outline developments in the Spanish market for machine tool manufacturers, metal formers and fabricators? How has Spain been affected by the global economic downturn and what do you forecast for the future?

KA: Spain is the third largest manufacturer and exporter of machine tools in the European Union.

Machine tool production in Spain reached a figure of 745.1 million euros in 2009 (definitive data) which is a 29.5% drop with respect to the 1,056.7 million euros that the sector had earned at the close of production in 2008. This is the lowest figure in the last ten years, and we need to look back to the mid-1990s to find similar production levels.

In 2008, production equalled that attained in 2007 and marked the start of a change in the trend after three years - 2007, 2006 and 2005 – in which turnover had grown significantly every year.

Exports, despite suffering heavy setbacks, have fared relatively better and are close to the level attained in 2006. More specifically, the export figure is 551.9 million euros, which represents a drop of 25% with respect to the 736 million euros exported in 2008. The high degree of internationalisation that characterises the machine tool manufacturing companies has become a key element for their sustainability and the export/import coverage ratio has grown by an impressive 265%. In 2009, almost 75% of machine tool production was sold to foreign markets, and Spain maintains its position in the ranking as the ninth machine tool exporting country worldwide.

Overall, we note that the tendency towards change is consolidating, with order intakes growing each quarter compared to the previous one. But, despite this, the increase is still insufficient. As regards turnover, we anticipate a decrease of the same in 2010 that could range between 15 and 20% due to the very low levels against order books over 2009.

ISMR: What are your thoughts on current European markets for metal formers and fabricators, in the wake of the global economic downturn? How do you see European markets developing over the next six months? Will there be any specific regional pockets of growth?

KA: In the 2009 country rankings, Germany remained in the lead as the main destination for Spanish exports, receiving 24% of the same. France was next, dislodging Italy as the second country in the ranking with 8.2%. China was in third place, in the highest position that the Asian giant has ever held, with 7.7%. Italy was fourth, with 7.5%, having undergone a sharp decline in its quota after the 20% it reached in 2008, and another BRIC country was in fifth place – India - with a percentage of 7.1%.

The next five countries, completing the top ten Spanish export destinations, were Russia, Portugal, the UK, Mexico and the USA. 73.7% of sales abroad were to these ten countries. The progress of the BRIC countries over the last five years - China (3rd), India (4th), Russia (6th) and Brazil (11th) – is of particular note.

Regarding orders from foreign markets, we have observed that both China and India are performing well, some improvement in Italy and France has also been noted and, with regard to Germany, recovery is taking place extremely slowly with the consequences that this has on our sales since it is the main destination of Spanish exports. Although the outlook is positive, we have not assessed the results of the sales figures shown by the German market.

ISMR: Please outline current metal forming and fabricating trends, technologies and developments in the sector.

KA: At present, our companies are developing advances in areas related to fast hot-forging (Loire Safe and Fagor Arrasate), rotary forging (DENN), variable-section profiles (Ona Pres), reduction of energy consumption in presses, synchronisation of energy consumption on a press line or reducing press structure mass, especially servo presses which offer higher energy consumption.

## FACE TO FACE

AFM gears its efforts towards promoting the competitiveness of the machine tool industry, defending the manufacturers’ interests and fostering the sector’s growth

ISMR: Please give us some background history on AFM. Who are its members, how many do you have and how do you help them? Please also outline the benefits of membership.

KA: AFM, the Machine Tool Manufacturers’ Association of Spain, was created in 1946 and represents almost a hundred companies that account for practically the entire production capacity of the Spanish machine tool sector (94% of its turnover for 2009).

AFM gears its efforts towards promoting the competitiveness of the machine tool industry, defending the manufacturers’ interests and fostering the sector’s growth. Its activities contribute to strengthening the internationalisation of its members and deepening its constant commitment to technology innovation.

AFM is much more than an association, as it also has its technology unit, INVEMA, the Machine Tool Research Foundation. Its job is to promote technological development and improve the industrial management of the sector´s companies, in close collaboration with its R&D&i, quality and safety units. INVEMA coordinates and backs member companies participating in numerous research projects in the autonomous community, nationwide and at a European level.

AFM thus promotes a full range of services and activities, which include the following:

■ Promotion of competitiveness

■ Internationalisation

■ Technology

■ Marketing and communication

■ Industrial management

■ Training and educational knowledge transfer

■ Competitive intelligence

■ Institutional relations.

ISMR: What are your main objectives and targets over the next few years? How do you intend to achieve them?

KA: The Strategic Plan for the 2009-2012 period has led to the identification of three strategic challenges that the sector must tackle over the coming four years:

■ Geographic growth of the activity

■ Growth geared to market sector/niche

■ Improvement of company’s competitiveness/profitability

Meeting these three strategic objectives will allow the general objective of the machine tool sector to be met, which consists in the medium and long term of the sustainability of associated companies. Sustainability is understood in terms of business and employment continuity, improved profitability, increased market share, technology development or improvement of the general image of the sector. In short, improved competitiveness.

ISMR: Please outline AFM’s main successes and achievements over the last two years, as well as any current or ongoing initiatives that are particularly noteworthy.

KA: AFM-INVEMA’s participation in the projects and initiatives relating to machine tool innovation and technology development occupies a relevant place in the sector’s R+D+I strategy, both in the international sphere and via initiatives for promotion and participation in technology projects developed by the Spanish Government and Autonomous Community Institutions. Our association encourages both projects of a horizontal type benefiting a large number of companies by catering for a specific need, and projects participated in by sector companies and managed by the association itself. Some of these projects are as follows:-

## CENIT PROJECT

Among the numerous R+D+I projects presented to the Public Administrations of the central and autonomous governments, the response of our sector to the CDTI’s call with the CENIT programme is of particular note. The objective of this scheme is to finance major projects for generating new knowledge that could be of use for the creation of new products, processes or services, or for the integration of technologies of strategic interest, thus contributing to improving the positioning of the Spanish production sector.

The CENIT Machine Tool project entitled “eEe – Advanced Technologies for Manufacturing Equipment and Processes in 2015”, which was recently finalised after four years of intense research work, is the largest project ever embarked upon by the sector. With a budget of almost 30 million euros, a total of 22 companies and 16 research organisations participated in the scheme with the objective of researching and developing technology to provide the basis for machine tools in 2015: eco-friendly, efficient as regards both precision and productivity, able to perform new processes in an integrated, multi-functional manner and including advanced electronics to make them intelligent and user-friendly.

## OTHER RELEVANT PROJECTS

The Association’s priority is to promote, vitalise and encourage ambitious R+D+I projects, and we are currently working intensively on this in collaboration with sector companies. As a result of this action, the following projects have recently been approved: “Hyperion” (led by Soraluce with participation from firms such as Etxe-tar, Goratu, Danobat and Zayer, and financed by the Basque Government within the framework of the Etorgai Programme), and “Impeler”(led by Ibarmia Innovatek with participation from Nicolás Correa, Juaristi, Fagor Automation and Ona Electroerosión, and financed by the CDTI as an Integrated Project).

## CHINESE-SPANISH AND MALAYSIAN-SPANISH INSTITUTES

Training is one of the strategic focus areas for the progress and growth of the machine tool sector. With the aim of enhancing the sector’s technological image from its grass roots and training new machine tool users in the use of our equipment, AFM, the Proschools Consortium and the IMH Machine Tool Institute implement initiatives for educational knowledge transfer through vocational training for emerging markets. This has been done through schemes such as the Chinese-Spanish and the Malaysian-Spanish Institutes, together with new projects currently being developed.

Apart from the educational, professional and technical significance of these types of international projects, the Institutes can also function as efficient platforms for supporting machine tool exports and the spread of business. They are a channel through which a qualified workforce is made available in the countries where the Institutes are located, with workers skilled in the operation and benefits of the Spanish machines, as well as contributing to a greater appreciation of the quality, technology and general image of the country that promotes and backs this type of initiative.

Moreover, the sector is developing a new and very innovative project called ASMAOLA. This is the creation of an innovation centre in manufacturing processes for industrial SMEs. With a calling to help small business in practical innovation projects of short duration and limited budget, and to network with companies, research institutes and universities, it offers a unique added value as its proximity to the Machine Tool Institute enables it to return to the industry the knowledge gained working for companies, in the form of training, courses, etc.

ISMR: Please let us know the latest news on AFM’s events and exhibitions.

KA: Every two years, we organise the Machine Tool and Manufacturing Technologies Congress which is set to be held for the 18th time at the Kursaal Palace in San Sebastián on 10, 11 and 12 November 2010.

Since 1976, this Congress has encouraged the exchange of knowledge and technology between machine tool manufacturers and researchers from technology centres and universities, favouring its application to production processes and facilitating companies’ adaptation to the growing technical demands of a constantly evolving market, thus increasing their competitiveness.

AFM is the co-organiser, together with the BEC (Bilbao Exhibition Centre), of BIEMH, the Spanish Machine Tool Biennial, the world’s fourth most prominent trade fair for the sector and the third most important in Europe thanks to years of consolidated progress as a pioneering monographic event dedicated to the machine tool sector. The twenty-seventh BIEMH will be held in Bilbao from 28 May to 2 June 2012.

ISMR: What are the main concerns of your members at the moment? How are you helping to alleviate those concerns?

KA: In these difficult times caused by the extreme crisis in the demand that has affected all countries and all markets, the main concern of our business is that there is a consistent recovery in demand. Here at AFM, we are working to help find those sectors or markets where there is a faster recovery and thus allow the normal pace of production to be recovered more rapidly.

There is another aspect, more structural than cyclical, which is a real challenge for the Spanish machine tool sector. This is the size of its companies, mostly SMEs, which is an important area for improvement in tackling future projects. Our mission is to facilitate collaboration between companies in terms of finding common areas and encouraging partnerships or even company mergers.
