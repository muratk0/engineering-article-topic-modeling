Manufacturers show resilience amid economic uncertainty, but challenges persist

Accounting and advisory firm Wipfli has released its 2025 North American manufacturing benchmarking study. Conducted annually since 2016, the study provides insights into performance trends, operational benchmarks, and strategic priorities across sectors, including metal formers, die casters, tool builders, contract machinists, and plastics processors. This year’s survey analyzed responses from 285 locations of 249 companies, examining finance, operations, HR, and sales performance.

"Manufacturers are doing their best to hold on in a tough environment," said Laurie Harbour, Wipfli partner. "While some are hiring, it’s often out of necessity rather than growth. The industry must act swiftly to improve operations and navigate uncertainty."

Manufacturers entered 2025 with cautious optimism, but sentiment declined sharply in Q2. The most frequently cited concerns included raw material tariffs, inflation, recession risk, and the rising cost of doing business. While inflation and wage pressures remain, their severity has lessened compared to 2024.

Despite margin pressures, manufacturers continued to demonstrate strong operational discipline. Efficiency among top performers averaged $139,800 per employee in 2024, only slightly below the 2023 peak of $143,593. Median EBIT for top performers held steady at 7.8%.

Debt-to-earnings ratios showed that 46% of manufacturers remain in a bankable position, 23% identify as somewhat bankable, and 31% fall into the "questionably bankable" category.

Labor and SG&A costs remain problematic, and responses around hiring reflect the uncertain market. While 37% of manufacturers reported hiring for growth or open positions, this activity is occurring despite persistent market challenges and flat profitability. The fact that 38% maintain current staffing levels underscores a cautious approach, with many companies opting to avoid additional labor costs until economic conditions improve.

Forecast capacity utilization for Q2 2025 was 63%, but actuals came in at 53%—the largest delta observed in recent years. This gap reflects a broader wait-and-see approach as manufacturers delay new product launches and inventory increases.

Capital expenditure plans also reflect this caution. While manufacturers invested in automation and digital technologies in 2024 despite high interest rates, plans for 2025 are more conservative as companies await clarity on trade and tariff policy.

Sales performance remains a challenge, but quote activity is on the rise. Hit rates across all manufacturing sectors averaged 11.2% (by number of quotes). This increase in quoting suggests that customers are exploring reshoring options and testing new supplier relationships, but not necessarily committing to new orders.

McAlpin Industries has announced plans for an expansion in Webster, N.Y. As part of a phased-in project, the company plans to construct a 300,000-sq.-ft. facility to support its light manufacturing operations.

Work at the site, scheduled to be operational in late 2026, will include laser cutting, forming, welding, finishing, and assembly. The project is expected to create up to 52 jobs.

Atlas Manufacturing opens 48,000-sq.-ft. facility in Wisconsin

Precision sheet metal fabricator Atlas Manufacturing has announced the acquisition of a 48,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility in Lake Hallie, Wis. This expansion significantly increases Atlas’s production capacity and consolidates its western Wisconsin operations. The move more than doubles the square footage of the company’s previous setup in the region and provides space for future expansion, additional production lines, and enhanced prototyping capabilities.

The company serves clients across the high-performance computing, medical device, industrial equipment, and commercial OEM markets. Its vertically integrated model includes design for manufacturability support, quick-turn prototyping, high-precision sheet metal fabrication, powder coating, and complex mechanical assembly.

Hitachi Energy invests $106 million in Tennessee expansion

Hitachi Energy has announced a $106 million investment to expand its transformer component manufacturing facility in Alamo, Tenn. The expansion will boost production of transformer bushings, including dry bushings for high-voltage direct current and alternating current transmission systems up to 800 kV.

The components manufactured at the site will support the growing need for long-distance power transmission to enable the rapid expansion of data centers, AI infrastructure, and other electrifying industries.

The project adds more than 60,000 sq. ft. to the facility, including more than 35,000 sq. ft. for manufacturing, 20,000 sq. ft. of warehouse space, and 5,000 sq. ft. of new office space. The expanded site is expected to be operational by mid-2027 and will create more than 100 jobs.

The project includes the implementation of an integrated logistics center and vertically integrated machine shop, enabling JIT delivery, reducing external warehousing needs, and enhancing operational efficiency.

Lockwood Manufacturing Co. acquired by Brattle Technologies

Livonia, Mich.-based Lockwood Manufacturing Co. has been acquired by Brattle Technologies, a family-owned holding company of specialty businesses in Cambridge, Mass.

Lockwood manufactures aluminum-and steel-based equipment for the industrial, laboratory, food service, hospitality, and health care industries. With products including racks, cabinets, carts, heat units, and material handling equipment, the company also serves as the OEM partner for companies selling industrial and scientific appliances. Its capabilities include in-house design, laser cutting, robotic welding, powder coating, and electrical component assembly.

Matt Phillips has been promoted from president of Lockwood to CEO, succeeding David Lamson, former owner/CEO, who has joined the board of directors.

Mountain Man Welding and Fabrication adds 30-kW fiber laser

Denver-based Mountain Man Welding and Fabrication had added a Peddinghaus PeddiLaser Plate 6225. The machine replaces the company’s old plasma table, allowing for faster, cleaner processing of thicker plate.

The 30-kW fiber laser has 0.002-in. positioning accuracy, 0.0007-in. mechanical repeatability, and the ability to cut material up to 3 in. thick. It offers up to 45-degree beveling and cutting speeds up to 200% faster than traditional plasma, according to the company.

With two 8- by 20-ft. cutting tables, the machine processes carbon, stainless steel, aluminum, brass, and copper.

Leeco Steel orders 50-kW fiber laser for Texas facility

Lisle, Ill.-based Leeco Steel, a supplier of steel plate, has purchased a 50-kW fiber laser cutting system from Cutlite to be installed at its Alvarado, Texas, facility.

The machine achieves tolerances as tight as ±0.005 in. on plate parts up to 1 in. thick and ±1/32 in. or better on thicker materials up to 4 in. It also can process internal features and holes as small as a quarter of the material thickness.

"Our customers are increasingly looking for suppliers who can do more than just deliver raw material," said Denton Nordhues, president/CEO of Leeco Steel. "They need a partner who can augment their business with ready-to-fabricate parts. Our investment in the 50-kW laser is a direct response to that evolving market demand."
