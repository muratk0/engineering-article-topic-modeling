# What Really Matters in this Presidency

[TARİH: 01.02.2017 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

Here we are, at the beginning of another presidential term. We have been through many of these, and they all have had their share of drama. What can we reasonably expect from the 45th president as far as the manufacturing sector is concerned? In the interests of full disclosure, I have to admit that Trump was not my choice — I was a Kasich supporter to the bitter end. That said, there is much in the policy discussion now that was part of the Kasich plan, as well as the plans of many others in the GOP and even some in the Democratic Party. As the new administration gets underway, it strikes me that three areas will be of most significance: trade policy, regulatory policy, and tax policy.

During the campaign, much was made of the issue of trade and the impact of imports. Many companies have been forced out of business by foreign competition, and many jobs have been lost to these same competitors. At least that was the case in the ’80s and ’90s; over the last few years the jobs have been lost to technology and robotics. The comments by Wilbur Ross, nominated to head the Commerce Department, provide the best indication of future intent. The plan is to enforce the rules that already exist to ensure that U.S. companies get the access promised and that other countries play by the existing rules.

Regulations rank as the biggest concern for many manufacturers, who have heard pledges to reduce the burden for years. Trump and his team seem to mean it, but the reality is that changing regulations can be a very slow and tedious process as many of them were imposed by law and it will take a change to that law to change the regulation. Bureaucracies move very slowly.

Tax policy is ultimately in the hands of Congress, and this discussion has been underway for years. Everyone favors change in principle but differ sharply on the specifics. Once again, the process will be slow and halting as each group pushes their preferred agenda. The consensus is that taxes should be cut, but the question is who gets that reduction.
