## INDIA IN FOCUS

India has been one of the world's fastest growing large economies, with growth averaging about seven per cent over the past five years.

ISMR SAYS:

"India aims to increase its industry competitiveness and adopt new technologies such as Industry 4.0"

According to the OECD, economic growth in India will slow somewhat but remain robust, at close to 7½ per cent in 2019 and 2020.

"Higher oil prices and the rupee depreciation are putting pressure on demand, inflation, the current account and public finances. However, business investment and exports will be strong, as past structural reforms – including the new Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code, smoother implementation of the Goods and Services Tax (GST), better roads and electricity and bank recapitalisation – are paying off," it said.

"Monetary policy will need to be tightened as inflation expectations are trending up and there are several upside risks to inflation. Containing the relatively high public debt-to-GDP ratio would require controlling contingent liabilities, such as those stemming from public enterprises and banks. Further subsidy reform would help make social spending more effective. Improving public banks' governance is also key to avoid a new wave of non-performing loans and to support the investment recovery."

## An eye on the economy

"India's economy is gaining momentum, thanks to the implementation of several recent noteworthy policies – such as the enactment of the long-awaited goods and services tax, and the country opening up more to overseas investors. Therefore, we expect economic growth to pick up to about 7.3 per cent for fiscal year 2018/19 – meaning the year that runs from April 2018 through to March 2019 – from 6.7 per cent in the previous year Meanwhile, inflation has edged higher, in part due to a reduction of economic slack," said the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

"To sustain and build on these policies and to harness the demographic dividend associated with a growing working-age population (which constitutes about two-thirds of the total population), India needs to reinvigorate reform efforts to keep the growth and jobs engine running. This is critical in a country where per capita income is about US$ 2,000 dollars, still well below that of other large emerging economies."

The IMF also pointed out that India has been one of the world's fastest growing large economies of late, with growth averaging about seven per cent over the past five years. Important reforms have already been implemented, it said, but it feels that more reforms are needed going forward to sustain this high growth. Policy priorities include:
- accelerate the clean-up of banks and corporate balance sheets; 
- continue fiscal consolidation (at centre and state levels) and broadly maintain the reform momentum in terms of structural reforms in factor markets, labour, land reforms and further enhancement of the business climate to achieve faster and more inclusive growth.

"Economic growth in India slowed in the third quarter of fiscal year 2018, which ran from October to December, on a weaker expansion in private consumption owing to tighter financial conditions. Nevertheless, fixed investment increased at a faster rate in Q3, while export growth accelerated and import growth decelerated. The differing export and import dynamics can partly be attributed to the large currency depreciation, followed by the announcement of new import restrictions on some goods, in the immediate run-up to the quarter," explained analyst, Focus Economics.

"Turning to the January-March 2019 period, the economy will likely grow at a steady pace, with survey data for the private-sector economy pointing to robust growth in both the manufacturing and tertiary sectors through February. Meanwhile, India's electoral commission announced that the forthcoming general election will run from 11 April till 19 May 2019, with the final vote count due on 23 May.

## Improving competitiveness

Atul Chaturvedi, Additional Secretary, Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT), India said on 13 March 2019 that the debate around embracing Industry 4.0 and the job losses that it could entail had made the case for new and more jobs that the new industry paradigm would create. This had emboldened the government to give a fillip to adopt and assimilate best standards from the expertise available from within and outside the country to improve the competitiveness for manufacturing and services.

Speaking at a symposium on 'Productivity Growth with Industry 4.0 Standardisation', organised by FICCI, jointly with the National Productivity Council (NPC) and Asian Productivity Organisation (APO), Mr. Chaturvedi said that India is going to be US$ 5 trillion economy in the next five years and the aim is to achieve it faster by increasing the competitiveness of industry and adopting new technologies such as Industry 4.0. It was time, he said, to skill and re-skill the workforce to align their expertise with the requirements of Industry 4.0.

Dr. Amita Prasad, Director General, National Productivity Council, India, emphasised the need to adopt manufacturing standards that are commonly agreed and not just copy global standards unilaterally. Security and safety of data was another area that had to be carefully monitored and respected, she added.

Hikaru Horiguchi, Director, Asian Productivity Organisation, said that the organisation was focused on raising the technological capacity of SMEs and environmentally friendly growth. The accent was on developing awareness and capability in adopting Industry 4.0 standards.

Under the 'Make in India' initiative, the Government of India aims to increase the share of the manufacturing sector in the gross domestic product (GDP) to 25 per cent by 2022, from 16 per cent, and to create 100 million new jobs by 2022.

## Construction equipment

According to a new report by ResearchandMarkets.com, the Indian construction equipment market has been on a major upswing since 2015 with the sale of construction equipment growing by 24% and crossing the 90,000-unit sales milestone for FY 2017-18. It has been propelled, said the report, by significant tailwinds from the current government's focus on infrastructure development whilst a broad-based macroeconomic push, backed by the pursuit of a favourable policy regime, has led to India becoming one of the fastest infrastructure developing countries in the world with almost 27km of highways being developed on a daily basis across the country.

"The infrastructure spending level stood at 9% of GDP for the 12th five-year plan (FY13-17). The latest budgetary allocations for FY2019-2020 indicate a continued growth momentum with the inclusion of a 4.56 lakh crore outlay towards infrastructure development focused on railways, roads, aviation and waterways. There has been a 21% year on year increase in funds allocation towards railways infrastructure development, along with a strong focus on rural roads development for FY2019-2020 which is likely to further drive the sales of construction equipment in India going forward," commented the analyst.

"Construction equipment sales in India have traditionally been dominated by earthmoving equipment led by the backhoe loader, followed by crawler excavators. Equipment sales peaked at 90,000 units in FY2017-18 with a favourable overall environment and strong momentum for infrastructure development. FY2018-19 is likely to be another record year for the industry with the reduction in GST rates on construction equipment from 28% to 18% in late 2017, providing a major boost to equipment sales in 2018."

## Sheet metal in India

Building on the success of its five previous shows, the sixth edition of BLECH India will take place from 25–27 April 2019 in Mumbai. Inter Ads-Brooks Exhibitions Pvt. Ltd., organiser of this key exhibition for India’s sheet metal working industry, has announced that the Bombay Exhibition Centre (BEC) in Mumbai will be the venue for BLECH India. It is one of the largest exhibition centres in India and hosts a variety of international trade exhibitions. BLECH India encompasses all core sectors of sheet metal working technology such as sheet metal; finished and semi-finished products; forming; separation and joining technology; machine elements; handling; surface treatment and tools/dies.

BLECH India attracted 151 exhibitors from 10 countries in 2017. International exhibitors were from Germany; Italy; UK; Turkey; China and Spain. 60% of exhibitors were from India. A highly qualified audience of 4,568 attended the three-day show.

"The latest edition of BLECH India looks set to double in size, compared to 2017's exhibition (2,755.5 sq. m.), representing the largest ever BLECH India. This growth, set against a buoyant Indian market, highlights India's potential as a manufacturing location of global status. BLECH India 2019, with its specialised exhibition profile, targets specific trade visitors from industry sectors including mechanical engineering; sheet metal products; the automotive industry and its suppliers; iron and steel production; household appliances; steel and aluminium construction; rolling mills as well as shipbuilding and aerospace. Many returning companies to BLECH India 2019 have booked larger stands and will present a variety of live machine demonstrations," the show organiser told ISMR.

Co-located, once again, with BLECH India 2019 is Fastener Fair India 2019, the trade show exclusively dedicated to fastener and fixing technologies. Taking place for the seventh time from 25-26 April 2019, the exhibition covers a wide product range of industrial fasteners and fixings; assembly and installation systems; storage and logistics services; fastener manufacturing technology and construction fixings.
