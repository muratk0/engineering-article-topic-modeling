Fab shop consumption trends in the age of trade wars

Not long after tariffs started to flood the news cycle in February, metal fabricators began reporting significant price spikes and renegotiations for raw material, both for steel and aluminum.

At this writing in mid-March, Section 232 national security tariffs went into effect March 12, charging 25% on steel and aluminum imports from all sources, including derivative products. A variety of other tariffs have been implemented, proposed, or delayed— the news changes by the day. (you can find more details in Steel News, p. 22.)

Fabricators face both challenges and opportunities. Some hedged last year with strategic raw material purchases. Many are in close contact with customers, ready to respond to changes in demand.

Fabricators face both challenges and opportunities. Some hedged last year with strategic spot buys. Many custom fabricators are in close contact with customers, ready to produce should supply chains shift, orders change, or reshoring opportunities arise.

To that end, fab shops are investing in software and technologies that will help them be more responsive. This becomes evident when digging into the latest responses from the "Forming and Fabricating Job Shop Consumption Report," published by the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association.

The survey tracks fabricators’ greatest needs in equipment and software—and in the latter category, the need for quoting software dominates. Almost 43% cited it as their greatest software need, followed by scheduling at 34%.

Most telling is the survey’s insight on automation. More than 30% cited software and information processing as their greatest automation need, while welding and bending were tied at 28%. FMA released this report in February, and the economic news has certainly changed since then. Trade and material price uncertainty isn’t ideal, but it’s the new reality. In the meantime, the industry’s best operations stand ready to respond.

Registration opens for 2025 Great Designs in Steel symposium

Registration is now open at

www.steel.org/great-designs-in-steel/register

for the 2025 Great Designs in Steel symposium, hosted by the American Iron and Steel Institute on May 21 at Suburban Collection Showplace in Novi, Mich.

For more than 20 years, GDIS has showcased the latest steel trends and material applications in new cars and trucks. Attendees at the 2025 symposium will have access to a full-day technical agenda organized in three tracks that focus on vehicle architecture, manufacturing, and materials and joining. The preliminary agenda includes presentations from OEMs including GM, Nissan Motor Co., and Rivian, as well as Tier suppliers and academic research institutions.

Lourenco Goncalves, chairman/president/CEO of Cleveland-Cliffs Inc., and Lyndon Lie, executive vice president/CEO of Volkswagen Group of America, will deliver keynote addresses to kick off the event. The symposium will include three body-in-white and vehicle architecture presentations from Volkswagen, General Motors, and Rivian, along with two hot-topic panels discussing stamping of advanced high-strength steels and AI in automotive steel.

More than 20 technical presentations will cover such topics as forming parts with third-generation steel, a new all-steel rocker assembly, the correlation between numerical analysis and hardware testing, hot-vacuum stamping technology with electrogalvanizing, laser hardening of cutting dies, tubular hydroforming, multipart integration, resistance spot weld simulation, influencing factors of liquid metal embrittlement, deepdraw forming of steels, and repairability of third-generation AHSS.

Attendees will view vehicle displays and technology exhibits from OEM and supplier companies, and they’ll have the opportunity to network with industry peers at the reception culminating the event.

Nu-Way Industries opens new headquarters in Illinois

Precision metal fabricating and assembly company Nu-Way Industries has moved its headquarters from Des Plaines, Ill., to a 260,000-sq.-ft. building in nearby Elk Grove Village, Ill. With capabilities including custom laser welding, complex fabrication and assembly, and remote laser welding, the company plans eventually to add 10 to 30 jobs.

Nu-Way has invested more than $9 million to modernize and upgrade the facility, including a revamped layout designed to optimize part flow through the shop floor.

Workcells are organized according to the order in which a typical part would visit them.

Isuzu to establish production base in South Carolina

Collier’s International

Anaheim, Calif.-based Isuzu North America Corp. is establishing a new production base in Greenville County, S.C., to expand its vehicle supply capabilities in anticipation of the electrification of commercial vehicles in North America. The company has purchased a 1 million-sq.-ft. facility on more than 200 acres of land that it is converting into an Isuzu-run assembly plant.

The new assembly plant will introduce a variable-model, variable-volume production system that produces both internal combustion engine vehicles and EVs. The $280 million facility will enable Isuzu to consolidate all production-related operations under one roof.

Wirefab Inc. marks 70 years in business

Wirefab Inc., a manufacturer of custom wire and metal products, is celebrating its 70th anniversary. Founded in 1955 as a small wire shop, the company has grown into an 84,000-sq.-ft. facility in Worcester, Mass., with more than 200 machines.

The employee-owned company’s capabilities include wire forming; resistance, MIG, and TIG welding; laser cutting; tube bending, cutting, drilling, and forming; sheet metal fabrication; and CNC machining.

Eaton expands South Carolina footprint with new manufacturing facility

Power management technology provider Eaton has announced it is expanding with new operations in Jonesville, S.C. The $340 million investment will create 700 jobs and establish a new manufacturing facility for three-phase transformers.

The 861,000-sq.-ft. facility will be the company’s 11th facility in South Carolina and its third manufacturing facility for three-phase transformers in the U.S.

Production and hiring at the new plant are expected to begin in 2027.

HII’s Ingalls Shipbuilding opens virtual reality welding lab

HII’s Ingalls Shipbuilding division, along with representatives from the U.S. Navy, Accelerate Mississippi, and Mississippi Gulf Coast Community College, have opened a virtual reality (VR) welding lab at the shipyard. The facility is designed to help welders hone their skills easily and safely and to enhance HII’s ability to increase the number of proficient shipbuilders with this essential skill set.

"By embracing virtual reality technology, Ingalls is not only equipping future shipbuilders with critical skills but also setting a new benchmark for how industries can leverage technology, local training providers, and state resources to train workers," said Courtney Taylor, executive director of AccelerateMS.
