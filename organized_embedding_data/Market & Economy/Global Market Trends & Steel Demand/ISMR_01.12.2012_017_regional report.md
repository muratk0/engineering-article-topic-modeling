## An eye on Saudi Arabia

“The Kingdom of Saudi Arabia has become one of the favourite destinations for the steel majors due to a thriving construction sector and soaring steel demand. The construction sector in the country is considered as the largest and the fastest growing market in the Gulf region, with huge growth potential. As the appetite for steel is growing in the Kingdom, its production will also grow at a CAGR of around 9 per cent during 2012-15 estimates our latest research report,” said RNCOS.

According to the RNCOS report ‘Saudi Arabia Steel Industry Forecast to 2013’, steel consumption in Saudi Arabia has grown rapidly in the past few years on the back of rising construction activities, growing investment in railways, and cheap and reliable energy supply. In the next five years, the Kingdom is expected to sustain its leadership in construction activities in the entire Middle East, says RNCOS.

“Our report discusses how the Kingdom showed its resilience to the downturn in the construction industry, which affected all Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries. On current trends, real GDP is projected to grow by six per cent in 2012. The private sector is again expected to lead the way, reflecting the increased role of the private sector in the economy, a clear break from the past. While there has been progress on diversification, the Saudi economy remains dependent on oil exports and growth has been driven mainly by factor accumulation.

## UAE in focus

Abu Dhabi is looking to increase the role industry plays in its burgeoning economy, stepping up investments in leading manufacturing sectors with an eye to building export markets in the medium term and laying the foundation for a more advanced industrial base in the longer run. Under its long-term development plan, Economic Vision 2030, it is hoped that industry will account for 25 per cent of GDP by 2030. As Vision 2030 foresees a quadrupling of GDP – from US$ 100bn in 2008 to US$ 400bn by the end of the plan’s term – this will entail a rise in the sector’s share of the overall economy to US$ 100bn by 2030.

Manufacturing is the second strongest economic growth driver among non-oil sectors in the UAE. In terms of its GDP contribution, it raked in 12.9 per cent share in 2007 and 12.2 per cent in 2008, becoming second only to the hydrocarbon sector in both the years. This consistent performance of the sector reflects the government’s heavy investment in the manufacturing industry. The Global Competitiveness Report 2011/2012, issued by the World Economic Forum, ranked the UAE 27th globally for competitiveness. The report classified the UAE as an innovation-driven economy, the highest stage of economic development that a country can achieve.

Its economy is being propelled onto a whole new level with the building of four integrated economic cities.

“After analysing the industry-specific statistics, it was found that the steel industry in Saudi Arabia is highly import-oriented. According to our calculations, apparent steel consumption in Kingdom reached around 12.1 million metric tons in 2011. Due to such a strong demand and soaring domestic steel prices, the share of imported steel is expected to witness an upward trend in the coming years," added RNCOS.

According to the International Monetary Fund, economic performance has been exceptionally strong and the outlook remains buoyant. Oil production has been raised to ease pressures on global oil prices, and increased government spending coupled with accommodating monetary policy has supported continued acceleration in non-oil activity.

“Overall real GDP is estimated to have grown by 7.1 per cent in 2011, with 8 per cent growth recorded in the non-oil sector — the highest since 1981 — and private sector growth at 8.5 per cent,” said the IMF.

“Saudi Arabia has provided important support to the global economy during a period of high global uncertainty, including through its actions in stabilizing the global oil market. Higher imports and increased workers’ remittances linked to the strong growth of the Saudi economy, together with expanded financial assistance have exerted a positive spillover and helped support other economies in the region and beyond."

Emirates Steel believes that construction projects in the GCC region will be the key driver supporting the steel industry’s growth in 2012 and 2013, followed by oil and gas, petrochemicals and other infrastructure projects.

“Demand for steel products [in the UAE] have surged immensely over the past few years, backed by construction boom, growth in the real-estate investment, and rising income levels. The apparent consumption of finished steel products is expected to reach more than eight million metric tons by 2014-end. The UAE steel industry in the past has been highly import-oriented. Steel imports grew around 15.5 per cent to about 6.7 million metric tons in 2010. With the reduction in import tariff by the GCC, imports of steel are expected to increase in the years to come. However, some major expansion plans of steel players in the country will increase domestic steel output further," said RNCOS.

Its research reveals that real-estate projects in Dubai and Abu Dhabi are currently the key driving forces behind the growth of the UAE steel industry. Even government support towards diversifying the economy from being an oil-based economy is playing a critical role in promoting investment and growth in the steel industry. ■
