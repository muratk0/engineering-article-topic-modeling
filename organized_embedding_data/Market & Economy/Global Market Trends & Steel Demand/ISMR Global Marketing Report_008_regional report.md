Spotlight On Russia

ISMR turns its focus on sector growth opportunities in the Russian Federation for sheet metal users and manufacturers

ISMR SAYS: “Russian investments in railways, shipbuilding and construction are creating opportunities for manufacturers”

Russia continues to play an important role in the global economy. Due to its abundant natural resources, it has become one of the leading steel producers and exporters in the world and remains a key market with strong potential growth in steel consumption.

According to the Ernst and Young September 2012 report on Russia’s attractiveness, in 2011 Russia took a step forward in the global competition to attract FDI. A boost in consumption, a strong labour market and an increase in investments are the prime drivers of this growth, it says.

Russia’s attractiveness as an investment destination increased by eight percentage points over 2011, the largest growth of any region. In 2011 particularly, there was a boost in investors’ confidence in the country, positively affected by its accession to the WTO from mid-2012. Strengths that make Russia attractive for investors include:

## • National resources

• Market opportunities – according to industry estimates, the country is poised to become Europe’s largest consumer market by 2018

• Higher educational advantage

• Balanced/lower labour costs and skills

• Solid telecommunications infrastructure – Russia has the fourth largest number of operational land lines and cellular phones in the world. In 2011, Russia also surpassed Germany to become the largest internet user in Europe.

The 10th edition of the annual Doing Business report also finds that since 2005, Russia has implemented a total of 17 institutional or regulatory reforms that improve the business regulatory environment for domestic firms.

Russia’s areas for improvement include the political, legislative and administrative environment; transport and logistics infrastructure as well as innovation and a culture of entrepreneurship.

## Sector forecasts

Key sheet metal users in Russia include the automotive, aerospace, construction, domestic goods, shipbuilding, energy generation, railway and defence industries.

According to a recent speech by Dmitry Medvedev, Prime Minister of the Russian Federation, 1.6 million vehicles were manufactured over the first nine months of 2012 in Russia. This volume increased by virtually 12 per cent on the same period of 2011. The industry is posting impressive growth because the domestic market has not yet been completely saturated, he explained. At the same time, all automotive sectors, including production of passenger cars, lorries, buses and light commercial vehicles, continue to grow. Foreign concerns/companies have become an integral part of the Russian automotive industry. Foreign cars being assembled in Russia now account for almost half (49 per cent) of all cars being sold there.

On 8 November 2012, the government discussed its draft programme for the Development of Shipbuilding for 2013-2030 aimed at restoring Russia’s position in the global shipbuilding industry. Funding within the programme is planned at over 600 billion roubles, and more than 50 per cent of it, or 330 billion roubles, is to be provided from the federal budget. Priority tasks include technological advancements necessary for the creation of prospective marine and river vessels, the renovation of industrial capacity of research institutes and design bureaus and the development of the industry’s human resources potential.

Objectives include increasing the production of civilian vessels five times over by 2030, achieving a similar labour productivity growth (or at least by 350 per cent) plus creating and modernising over 100,000 highly qualified jobs. The government wishes Russia’s share of naval ships on the global market to exceed 15 per cent, and its share of civilian vessels to be at least two per cent. Its current share is less than 0.5 per cent.

According to the Exporail organisers, Russia’s exhibition of railway technology and services, Russian Railways (RZD) continues to implement its ‘Strategy for Developing Rail Transport in the Russian Federation’. Approved by the government in 2008 and covering the period up to 2030, investments that could reach RUR13.8 trillion (€335 billion) in infrastructure and rolling stock are being made as the company modernises and upgrades the existing railway and expands the network to respond to new traffic opportunities.

“More than RUR2.8 trillion (€68 billion) of this is being invested between 2010 and 2015, with RZD aiming to increase freight traffic by more than a quarter and passenger volumes by one tenth over this period. Under the programme, some 20,700 route-km of new lines could be constructed – up to 1,500 km of these for highspeed passenger services. And to meet freight traffic forecasts, priority is being given to developing a fast transit corridor between Asia and Europe and to building new lines to move Russia’s abundant natural resources,” said the exhibition organiser.

## An eye on construction

The construction industry remains one of the key catalysts of economic growth in Russia. Its role is expected to strengthen in the years to come as the government aims to undertake multi-trillion rouble investments to modernise and expand the country’s infrastructure.

According to PMR’s latest report, entitled “Construction sector in Russia 2012 – Development forecasts for 2012-2015”, the Russian construction industry is still struggling to gain momentum. After 9.2 per cent y-o-y growth in the first two months of 2012, mostly on the back of the unseasonably warm winter, the value of construction work carried out in Russia increased by only 3.2 per cent in the January-August time frame. This deceleration of growth rates was certainly fuelled by the high base effect and by the economic slowdown.

For 2013 PMR analysts forecast an acceleration of market growth to nine per cent, propelled by easing global macroeconomic unrest and the speeding up of state-funded target programmes, especially relating to the residential and road construction segments.

The construction and modernisation of the fifteen stadiums for the needs of the 2018 World Cup in Russia will cost approx. RUB 162-199bn ($5.1-6.2bn). PMR analysts estimate that 2013 and 2014 will be the peak period for the construction of stadiums when works on thirteen stadiums in Russia will be carried out. The majority of construction is planned to start in 2013. The construction of seven stadiums will also begin in 2013 and be completed by 2017, with six stadiums to be completed in that year.

In August 2012, the government approved the Construction Road Map plan designed to improve the business climate in the construction industry. According to the plan, the number of procedures which will be needed to obtain a building permit should be reduced from the current 51 to 15 by 2015 and to 11 by 2018, and the period needed for all of these mandatory processes will be reduced from the current 423 days to 130 days by 2015, and to 53 by 2018. This includes more rapid approval of site-related documentation. In addition, the Residential Construction Support Fund will, through tenders, use its reserves to provide land, free-of-charge and for a fixed period, for the construction of economy class homes. Sites will be awarded to the company offering the lowest sale price for its proposed properties.
