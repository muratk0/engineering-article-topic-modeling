# INDUSTRY REPORT

[TARİH: 01.05.2011 ISMR]

## COMMERCIAL PROPERTY MARKETS

The RICS Global Commercial Property Survey for Q4 suggests that, while there remains evidence that emerging markets are generally outperforming the more established markets, there are some notable cases where the divide is closing. Countries where respondents were most upbeat were, once again, China, Hong Kong, Singapore and Brazil, though there were marked improvements in Germany and Russia. Market conditions generally appear worse in ‘peripheral’ European regions, the UK and Japan.

Occupier markets have firmed, with 75 per cent of the reporting countries indicating greater demand from tenants than in the previous 3-month period. Significantly, respondents suggest that rental expectations are picking up; expectations for rents improved compared to the previous quarter with just under two thirds of countries now expecting rents to rise in Q1 2011.

Elsewhere, strong results came from Russia and Germany as both countries saw the fortunes of their respective real estate markets bounce on more fertile economic conditions. Both occupier and investment markets improved markedly in Russia, while in Germany it was the turnaround in capital values that was most prominent; improving from +19 to +45. The pick-up in rental expectations was more modest as it moved up from -4 to +8. Respondents also suggested that conditions are becoming more favourable in Poland. Results from China and Hong Kong suggest that, despite the ongoing attempts from authorities to cool the market, activity remains buoyant. Meanwhile, sentiment continues to deteriorate in the UK and Japan as property professionals expect further rental declines. ‘Peripheral’ European countries such as Portugal, Ireland, Spain and Greece are seeing the net balance drop for most indicators as debt burdens remain most pronounced.

## SUSTAINABLE HOUSING DESIGN

Traditional building methods must make room for cost effective, sustainable designs if housing crises are to be alleviated, according to an independent RICS research report.

“By making use of off-site construction methods, recycled materials and innovative structural design, homes can be constructed swiftly and cheaply with less impact on the environment. Many of the designs can be easily segmented and extended, eliminating the need for costly renovation work, and enabling them to evolve as the needs of the occupiers change,” says the report.

“More innovative, modern off-site and modular designs are not only extremely cost effective, but can be constructed with ease in a very short period of time. Furthermore, many major mortgage providers are already willing to lend against these structures, which has been a problem in the past, meaning that first-time buyers could find them a highly practical way of getting onto the property ladder.”

Emerging Asia leads the world in the implementation of sustainable real estate solutions, finds a RICS sustainability survey. The region ranked first across many property sectors and occupier types in the survey, with agents reporting the highest rises in demand for sustainable real estate solutions in the government, bank and commodity sectors.

Comments from respondents in the region indicate that sustainable real estate is a growing movement in Emerging Asia with governments introducing incentives for the initiation of green technology in the construction sector and the latest building trends focusing on ‘environmentally friendly’ design and ‘green’ features.

Overall, demand for sustainable real estate solutions looks set to remain strong in the region as respondents gave the most positive indication that sustainability has moved up the corporate agenda as a result of the Gulf oil spill (net balance of 62%) and that sustainable construction will not be overshadowed by issues of government debt (net balance of 33%).

In terms of global sectors, the survey found that most governments around the world increased their implementation of sustainable real estate solutions, from a net balance of +36 to one of +34, Q1 2010 vs. Q3 2010. Implementation in the manufacturing sector is increasing modestly but continues to lag the other sectors in general.

Of particular interest is Latin America, where the net balance improved to +37. Across all sectors globally, the survey found that implementation of sustainable solutions is generally on the rise compared to last quarter, with professional firms and governments continuing to lead the way.

In terms of sustainable mandates across global occupier categories, the most substantial change came in the large occupier sector (>49 employees), which rose from a net balance 21 percent in Q1 2010 to 31 percent in Q3 2010.

That said, there has been some improvement among smaller occupiers as well, with 7 per cent more respondents indicating growth in sustainable mandates compared to -4 in Q2. Interestingly, agents report that hoteliers – particularly in Africa and the Middle East, Latin America, Developed Asia, and Emerging Europe – posted substantial growth in demand for sustainable real estate solutions. ■
