# Indian automotive sector growth

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 ISMR]

THE Indian automotive industry has shown healthy growth from FY’08 to FY’13, says analyst Frost & Sullivan. However, high inflation, fuel prices and unfavourable interest rates, resulting in high cost of ownership, affected car sales in FY’14. Domestic sales of the Passenger Vehicle (PV) segment declined by 6 per cent, Commercial Vehicle (CV) segment sales declined by 25.3 per cent and Three-Wheeler (3W) sales declined by 10.9 per cent when compared to FY’13. The Two-Wheeler (2W) segment, on the other hand, has shown positive growth of 7.3 per cent in FY’14.

“Despite the fall in FY’14, over the next few years, solid but cautious growth is expected due to policies announced by the Indian Government in the interim budget and with improving affordability, rising incomes and untapped markets,” explained the analyst.

“The PV market is primarily aspiration-driven and the launch of new models will spur the growth of this segment. This, combined with the advent of technology-inclined Gen Y, means (that the) future will see many high-end passenger vehicles. The launch of technology-loaded models with attractive optional features will be the key to growth in this segment.”

India’s Commercial Vehicle segment is likely to grow at a CAGR of 6.4 per cent, said F&S, backed by the growth of Small Commercial Vehicles and Intermediate Commercial Vehicles (ICVs). The two-wheeler market has emerged as the most vibrant and transforming segment of the overall Indian automobile industry, witnessing unprecedented growth. The two-wheeler domestic market is likely to grow at a CAGR of 5.3 per cent over the next five years, said F&S.

India is the world’s foremost producer, consumer and exporter of three-wheelers. Frost and Sullivan expects the three-wheeler industry to report a moderate volume CAGR of 3.6 per cent over the next five years. Long-term sales growth will be the highest in the passenger carrier segment, and lowest in the goods carrier segment due to intense competition from SCVs. ■
