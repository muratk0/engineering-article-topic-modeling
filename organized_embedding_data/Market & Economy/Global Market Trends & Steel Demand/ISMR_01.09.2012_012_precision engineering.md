# PRECISION ENGINEERING

[TARİH: 01.09.2012 ISMR]

The discrepancy between Italian domestic and overseas demand is shown even more clearly from the forecast data prepared by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE.

In 2012, at the current forecast level, production will increase by 4.9% over the previous year, to 4,995 million euros. Exports will reach a record level of 3,660 million euros, 12.3% more than in 2011. On the other hand, however, Italian manufacturers’ deliveries on the domestic market will register a decline of 11.1% remaining at 1,335 million euros, penalised by consumption contraction which will drop by 4.2% to 2,445 million.

“As far as exports are concerned, in 2011, China proved to be the main target market of the “Made in Italy” branding in the field, followed by Germany, United States, France, Brazil and Russia. The last available export survey for the period from January to March 2012 shows China ranking steadily in first place among the Italian target market countries in the sector, with an increase of 30.2% over the same period of the previous year, followed by the United States (+96.4%), Germany (-13.3%), India (-2.1%), Russia (+92%) and France (+0.8%),” commented the association.

“From the analysis of the data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE,” said Giancarlo Losma, previous chairman of the association, “it is clear that the positive results are due only to the excellent performance of exports. However, what worries Italian manufacturers most is the reduction of sales in the domestic market: the small businesses in the sector, those sized to operate only in the local area, risk collapsing.”

“Do not forget” he added, “that poor investment in production in our country means a set-back of the Italian manufacturing system at all levels in the production chain. In addition, it is a dangerous phenomenon when compared with the strong stimulus for development implemented by emerging or emerged countries. For this reason, we welcome the strong reform programme passed by the government as well as the effort put into the drafting of the decree for development, particularly where it enables SMEs to have new debt instruments, but we believe that the latter should be integrated with measures which could immediately bring about the recovery of domestic consumption of manufacturing technology.”

Appointed to succeed Giancarlo Losma to the UCIMU chairmanship is Luigi Galdabini, CEO of Galdabini SpA, a leading machine tool company for metal processing and mechanical tests based in Cardano al Campo (Varese). Luigi Galdabini, vice-chairman since 2005, will lead the association from 2012-2013.

Lamiera, Italy’s premier sheet metal/metal forming exhibition held in Bologna from 9-12 May this year, recorded 18192 visits (of which 787 were from outside Italy). 451 exhibitors, 42% of which were from outside Italy, took up a total exhibition area of 40,000 square metres.

## The order index

During the second quarter of 2012, the machine tool order index, processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, recorded a 20.6% decrease, compared with the same period last year, with an absolute total of 71.5 (2005=100). The result, says the association, is due to the negative performance of Italian manufacturers in domestic and export markets.

In particular, the domestic index of orders shows an 11.5% decrease compared with the same period of the previous year, with an absolute total of 52.4. This result demonstrates the weakness of domestic demand and what has now, says UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, become a structural crisis of the Italian market which has drastically reduced its levels of investment in production technology. The export market index of orders shows a 23% downturn compared with the April-June 2011 period, bringing the absolute total down to 78.5.

The 2012 forecast produced by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE reveals that, at the end of the year, export sales will show a 12.3% increase, when compared with the closing figure of 2011.

Luigi Galdabini, new President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, said: “Italian manufacturers of machine tools are extremely worried about the current situation. What is even more alarming is the trend of internal demand, where reduction has now become structural. However, although significant, the decrease in export orders is still acceptable if we consider that these orders are being compared with the excellent results achieved during the second quarter of 2011.”

“This figure, together with the export/production level result which will reach 73.3%, shows the importance of activities by manufacturers outside Italy, and the impact of foreign demand on the business of Italian companies and not only as far as the machine tool sector is concerned,” said the association.

“In this respect,” stated Luigi Galdabini, “it is impossible to understand the choice of the Government Authorities to include in the Spending Review Legislative Decree the decree (art. 12, par. 70) calling for the elimination of funds destined for activities to support the ‘Made in Italy’ branding around the world. The resources provided by the fund ensure appropriate support for Italian companies. For this reason, in addition to requesting the immediate elimination of the provision included in the decree of 6th July 2012, we ask for a provision for the abatement of IRAP on personnel, for an amount equal to the export/invoicing ratio of the business, with the aim of favouring the more virtuous companies and stimulating the process for the internationalisation of those that are still strongly dependent on the domestic market.”

As far as Italy’s domestic market is concerned, the association noted that consistent decrease in demand calls for immediate and direct intervention to ensure that the loss of competitiveness is reversible.

“Considering how serious the situation is, and the de-industrialisation risk for a country which has stopped renovating its manufacturing structures,”said Luigi Galdabini, “the government authorities should plan the introduction of a decisive measure, like a subtraction from taxable income equal to 50% of the value of investments on new machinery. However, and in the knowledge that in a period of cost reduction this may not be a sustainable measure for the Treasury, manufacturers should at least ask for the introduction of free depreciation of capital goods. This measure, which would allow users to distribute the depreciation of purchased goods over shorter periods in the medium term, would have no impact whatsoever on the Treasury as the taxes due would simply be postponed.” ■
