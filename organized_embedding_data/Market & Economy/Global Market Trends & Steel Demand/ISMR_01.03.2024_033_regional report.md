IN THE SPOTLIGHT

We highlight macro-economic forecasts, business surveys and trade projections for manufacturing in France for 2024 and beyond.

ISMR SAYS:

"Machine tool markets and industrial activity in France come under the spotlight."

As a republican state and a parliamentary democracy, France has a Parliament that is made up of the National Assembly (Assemblée Nationale) and the Senate (Sénat). There are three sub levels of governance in France: the Regions (régions), the Departments (départements) and the Municipalities (communes). It is an advanced, high-income EU economy with overseas regions/territories in the Americas and the Atlantic, Pacific and Indian oceans.

"The country plays an influential global role as a permanent member of the United Nations Security Council; NATO; the EU; G-7; G-20 and other multilateral organisations. Much of France's population is concentrated in the north and south east, although there are many urban agglomerations throughout the country. Paris is the largest city," outlined the CIA World Factbook.

"After a strong economic recovery from the COVID-19 pandemic, France was hit by an energy shock driven by Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Inflation rose and economic activity slowed. Nevertheless, the economy has remained resilient and inflation is well below other EU countries due to more limited reliance on Russian gas and a strong policy response—including price controls on gas and electricity, tax reductions on fuel products, transfer payments and measures to support businesses. This cushioned the impact of the energy price shock but has been costly for the government and not well targeted," commented the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in February 2023.

## Business surveys

Examples of strong industry sectors in France include aircraft manufacturing; machinery; automotive; electronics; pharmaceuticals, chemicals, food processing and metallurgy. It is transitioning to a green economy via the "France 2030" strategy.

The Banque de France publishes a range of monthly and quarterly economic surveys that provide a snapshot of the French economy in the form of business climate indicators and short-term forecasts.

According to the business leaders that it surveyed (approximately 8,500 companies and establishments between 28 November and 5 December 2023), activity continued to rise in market services and the finishing works of the construction sector, while it was almost stable in industry and declined in structural works.

"Forecasts for December suggest that activity in the services sector will continue to grow, while it will ease in industry and contract in construction. Order books continued to weaken in industry, and even more so in the structural works sector of the construction industry," said the bank.

"Selling prices continued to moderate. According to business leaders, raw material prices continued to fall while finished goods prices remained virtually unchanged. ## Industrial activity

EVOLIS represents manufacturers of French machine tools and capital goods who actively contribute to the competitiveness and efficiency of French industry through the modernisation, digitalisation and decarbonisation of production in the main industrial sectors.

According to an economic survey carried out by EVOLIS among its members, published in December 2023, industrial activity was expected to slow down in 2024.

"As indicated by the sudden weakening of industrial opinions on order books over the coming months, the slowing of the activity outlook will particularly affect the construction equipment sector which will see its turnover decline by 6%," said EVOLIS.

"In the fluid equipment sector, the year 2024 will be characterised by a weak increase in turnover of around +2%, including +3% on the domestic market and +1% for export. Activity in the industrial equipment sector is expected to increase by +1.7% in 2024, driven mainly by the export market. With the order book falling, workload in the design offices would be reduced compared to the expectations of business leaders. Finally, in the handling equipment sector, manufacturers remain more optimistic but still anticipate a slowdown with expected growth of just under 5%," it added.

## Machine tool market

EVOLIS reported in October 2023 that the French machine tool market had grown by 18% over 2022.

"Demand for machine tools was mainly driven by the recovery of the aerospace and automobile industries. The aid released as part of the 'France Relance' plan has also supported manufacturers in their investments in machine tools. Finally, the surge in raw material prices was, at least partially, passed on by manufacturers in their selling prices, which accentuated the growth of the market in value," explained the association.

"Domestic demand was largely met by overseas supply, with a 24% increase in imports. Flows from Germany, France's leading trading partner (22% of imports), increased by over 15% in the 2022 financial year," it continued.

According to EVOLIS, after a mechanical rebound in 2021, French production of machine tools decreased by almost 1% in 2022. Production reached €798 million: machining machine tools represented 63% of machines produced and forming machines represented 37%. The production capacity utilisation rate in the machine tool manufacturing industry remained below pre-crisis levels throughout 2022. French production, it said, also suffered from the disengagement of China, which reduced its imports from France by 39% in 2022. As a result, the French trade balance in the sector deteriorated in 2022.

"The year 2022 was characterised by two major movements in the distribution of demand for machine tools by customer sector. Firstly, the general mechanics and subcontracting sector gave way as the leading customer sector to the aeronautics industry, which represented 27% of total machine tool sales in 2022 (compared to 17% in 2021). At the same time, the level of concentration of demand within traditional customer sectors reduced. To cope with the fall in demand from the automotive sector, which weighed heavily on the sector during the COVID-19 crisis, machine tool companies have strived to reduce their dependence on traditional markets by seeking new sectoral outlets," explained the association.

During the first half of 2023, demand for machine tools showed signs of slowing. However, some machine tool-consuming industries benefited from improved supply chains. Ultimately, EVOLIS expected investment growth in machine tool client sectors to slow to 2.5% in 2023. Demand for machine tools was expected to increase by around 6% in 2023.

According to its 2023 outlook, published in September 2023, manufacturers anticipated a slight growth in 2023 despite a weakening order flow. Among EVOLIS manufacturers (manufacturers of equipment for handling, construction, fluid treatment, production machines and industrial robots) recruitment difficulties, the high level of raw material prices and supply problems fade very gradually. As a result, delivery times and stocks were improving.

"As supply constraints are resolved, new demand constraints emerge. Impacted by the rise in inflation, more restrictive access to credit and a less favourable cash flow situation for companies, it is now expected to slow down. The flow of new orders is slowing, as evidenced by the decline observed in order books. However, these are still at a high level which bodes well for a smooth 2023, despite a more difficult second half," confirmed its report.

## Consumables and equipment for machine tools

According to EVOLIS analysis in February 2024, French demand for consumables and equipment for machine tools recorded double-digit growth in 2023.

"Sales of consumables for machine tools increased by 11% over the year, following an increase of 10% observed in 2022. Sales of equipment experienced very robust growth of around 30% in line with growth levels for 2022. Although price increases can partly explain this growth, these advances demonstrate activity that has remained sustained in French industry and the resilience shown by productive investment in 2023," outlined EVOLIS.

## Construction materials

“The 2023 construction equipment market should end virtually stable. Unit sales, all equipment combined, should be, identical to those recorded in 2022," added EVOLIS.

On the other hand, a decrease in sales of materials of around 10-15% is expected in 2024.

"Purchases of materials for the construction, improvement and maintenance of buildings will be down, in line with the fall in activity predicted by the construction sector for this year. Investment by rental companies will also decline. Conversely, investment by public works companies and, in general, public clients (municipalities, départements and regions) could be maintained and thus limit the anticipated decline in the market," it commented in January this year.

## Earthmoving equipment

Sales of earthmoving machines in the French market were very dynamic in 2023.

"2023 was therefore a very good year for manufacturers, who were able to sell their very large stock of orders accumulated since 2021 throughout the year. Sales of large equipment (with the exception of tracked tractors) should show a drop of almost 8% while for compact earthmoving equipment, sales could show an increase of around 6% in 2023, driven largely by mini excavators (+7%)," explained EVOLIS.

## Arc welding equipment

According to a survey carried out by EVOLIS in June last year, the French market for arc welding equipment reached €190 million in 2022. After strong growth during the first half of 2022, sales of arc welding equipment slowed down in the second half of the year.

In connection with the unfavourable economic situation which negatively impacts certain customer segments in this sector in France and, in particular, the Building & Public Works sector, the first quarter of 2023 seemed to be characterised by a slight slowdown in demand, compared to 2022. Over 2023, we expect the French welding market to stabilise. Activity will remain supported by new opportunities brought about by the 'France 2030 Plan' and projects linked to the decarbonisation of industry," outlined EVOLIS.

## Macro-economic forecasts

According to the European Commission's macro-economic forecast for France (published in February 2024), French GDP is estimated to have grown by 0.9% in 2023 (driven by strong growth in the second quarter). This is marginally below the Autumn 2023 forecast.

"High inflation and tighter financial conditions weighed on growth throughout the year despite government support measures and a very favourable labour market, accompanied by dynamic wages that preserved households' purchasing power. While net exports contributed positively to growth, these were fuelled mostly by a decrease in imports of goods, as the growth of domestic demand was limited," said the report.

In 2024, it expects it to grow moderately, by 0.9% annually (a downward revision of 0.3 percentage points compared to the Autumn Forecast). The revision can be mainly attributed to a lower carry-over from a weaker than expected second half of 2023. Economic activity is expected to gather momentum in the second half of 2024. Private consumption is set to drive GDP growth on the back of rapidly declining inflation.

"Investment is expected to remain subdued until the second half of the year due to still restrictive financing conditions but is then set to start recovering. Net exports are projected to make no contribution to GDP growth given that imports are set to rise—pushed by strong demand—while exports of goods are only expected to progressively catch up with their pre-crisis level," said the forecast.

"In 2025, GDP growth is forecast at 1.3%, marginally below the Autumn Forecast. Economic activity is expected to be further driven by private consumption as inflationary pressures dissipate, and households saving rate declines gradually towards its historical average. Investment from both households and corporations is projected to recover progressively. Net exports are set to make a limited contribution to growth, with strong export growth offset by rising imports as domestic demand expands," it continued.

After peaking at 7% in the first quarter of 2023, HICP inflation decreased throughout the year to reach 4.2% in the fourth quarter, largely thanks to declining energy and commodity prices. The progressive phasing-out of energy-related measures and the increase of the electricity tax in February 2024, to roughly two-thirds of its pre-crisis level, is expected to lead to an increase in electricity prices of close to 10%. Meanwhile, wage increases are still set to feed underlying price pressures.

"However, the downward trend in inflation is set to continue over the forecast horizon. After 5.7% in 2023, inflation is expected to reach 2.8% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025. For 2024, this is slightly lower than in Autumn, while the forecast for 2025 remained unchanged," concluded the European Commission's forecast.

## Banque de France projections

According to the Banque de France's macroeconomic projections in December 2023, the French economy will gradually emerge from inflation without suffering a recession.

"In the short term, the latest information available suggests a slight downward revision of growth for 2023 to 0.8%. However, we are maintaining our growth projections for 2024 (0.9%) and 2025 (1.3%) unchanged, and we are expecting growth of 1.6% in 2026. This acceleration in 2026 assumes that the effects of recent shocks to the French economy (external tax shock, tightening of monetary and financial conditions) will have abated by that time," said the bank in December 2023.

"Economic activity is expected to remain sluggish in 2024 before strengthening subsequently. In 2024, growth should be driven to a greater extent by household consumption, due to the fall in inflation, which will benefit the purchasing power of wages, and to the lower saving ratio. In 2025, growth should also benefit from enhanced private investment as the impact of tighter monetary and financial conditions will be weaker. In 2026, these trends should strengthen to generate a strong recovery," it added.

Headline inflation (HICP), which peaked in early 2023, was expected to continue to subside. After averaging 5.7% in 2023, the bank anticipated inflation to fall sharply to 2.5% in 2024.

"Headline inflation should decline substantially over the forecast horizon, helped by falling energy prices; core inflation (i.e. HICP excluding energy and food) should also come down, albeit more slowly. Provided there are no new shocks to commodity import prices, headline inflation should gradually come back towards 2% by 2025 at the latest, and then remain at a slightly lower level," it confirmed.

Over the next few quarters, following a time lag, employment is expected to adjust to the economic slowdown observed since late 2022, with only a partial recovery of past productivity losses.

"Consequently, the rate of unemployment should continue to rise, although it should stay under 8% in 2025 before resuming a downward trajectory as economic activity recovers," the bank concluded.
