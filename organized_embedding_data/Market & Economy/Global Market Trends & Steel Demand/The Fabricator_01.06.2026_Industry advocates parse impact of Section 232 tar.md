# Industry advocates parse impact of Section 232 tariff adjustments

[TARİH: 01.06.2026 The Fabricator]

Steel News

Steelmakers claim enough domestic capacity exists already

By

Kristen DiLandro

A

process that could reduce Section 232 steel and aluminum tariffs for mills and smelters in Mexico and in Canada garnered mixed reactions from steel and metals producers and supply chain associations.

On April 23, the U.S. Department of Commerce highlighted the procedure for Canadian and Mexican steel and aluminum producers to request tariff adjustments tied to new U.S.-based production commitments. The update alters details of President Trump’s earlier Proclamation 10984 measure, which reasoned that tariffs must be applied to Canadian and Mexican medium- and heavy-duty vehicles, parts, and buses on the basis of national security.

As of the DOC’s latest April 23 update, Canada and Mexico are eligible for tariff relief on these goods provided that the products qualify under United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement origin rules. USMCA origin rules state mills melt and pour steel in Canada or Mexico and aluminum producers smelt and cast in either country.

Eligibility for the tariff relief program hinges on assessment of the applicant’s investment commitment in the U.S. To be considered, detailed investment plans demonstrating new domestic production capacity tied to automotive or industrial capacity are required. Companies also will be subject to ongoing reporting and production milestone mandates, according to the DOC.

Additional documentation requirements include project descriptions, capital investment plans, timelines, projected capacity increases, and evidence of direct connections to automotive and truck supply chains in the U.S.

Quarterly reports on construction and production milestones and compliance to investment commitments will be assessed by the department on an ongoing basis. Compliant companies may find Section 232 tariffs reduced from 50% to no less than 25%.

STEEL INDUSTRY REACTION

Philip K. Bell, Steel Manufacturers Association president/CEO, emphasized the sizeable investments domestic steel mills have already made to meet the automotive sector’s steel requirements.

"There is no shortage of domestic steelmaking capacity for automotive-grade steels. SMA members continue to make record investments to ensure the optimal mix of automotive steels that are safely and sustainably produced in facilities that are modern and efficient," Bell said.

"After reviewing the president’s executive order, we find it difficult to see that there are any companies that can take advantage of the proclamation," he added. "If there are, it would be interesting to see which companies come forward who believe that there is something that they can carve out of this executive order that benefits them."

American Iron and Steel Institute President/CEO Kevin Dempsey also emphasized ongoing efforts from U.S. producers to meet the demands of domestic automotive manufacturers.

"In recent years, American steel producers have announced significant investments in new and upgraded facilities, including advanced steelmaking furnaces and state-of-the-art finishing lines, many of them specifically designed to produce the high-strength, lightweight steels that modern vehicles demand," Dempsey said.

"These mills will further expand our ability to supply automakers with the full range of steel products they require," he said.

WAIT-AND-SEE MODE

The American Metals Supply Chain Institute, which represents steel and metals consumers, traders, and logistics companies, said it was still assessing the rule change.

"The American Metals Supply Chain Institute will continue to monitor developments and assess potential impacts on the metals and broader supply chain sectors. We welcome perspectives from across the industry on how these evolving dynamics may affect your operations," AMSCI Executive Director Alexandra Jopp wrote in a LinkedIn post.
