# AN EYE ON MEDICAL

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

ISMR analyses trends in global medical device markets and pinpoints growth sectors for manufacturers

New technologies and longer life spans are driving the growing medical device and medical electronic manufacturing markets around the world. Large medical device companies are developing strategies to increase their share in the market by expanding their distribution and manufacturing channels into emerging markets such as China, India and Brazil. Analyst Visiongain has determined that the value of the Indian medical device market alone in 2023 will reach US$ 11bn.

“In 2011, the medical device market worldwide was worth US$ 266bn, with high single-digit growth from the year before. Despite being a vast and mature market, there are a number of sectors likely to experience fast growth during the next 10 years. Much of this growth is linked to the prevalence of a number of diseases worldwide, as well as an ageing population. The rise in neurological disorders will drive both the diagnostics market and the neurological device market. Changes in wealth and lifestyle globally, particularly in the emerging markets, will lead to a rise in obesity and coronary diseases,” says Visiongain.

International firms are seeking regulatory approvals for their medical devices while focusing on international sales, joint ventures, mergers and acquisitions. Medical device manufacturers are often challenged with getting products to the market faster whilst lowering production costs. Supply chain collaboration is therefore highly critical to their success in emerging markets.

## Emerging market forecasts

Strong innovations are also emerging in the global market for medical devices, says analyst Global Research, Inc. “New market research forecasts medical devices in China to reach US$ 30.4 billion by 2018, while the global market for hearing aid devices is expected to hit US$ 11.3 billion in that same time and surgical equipment is currently on pace to reach US$ 7 billion,” it said. “The medical devices market in China is forecast to grow at a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 11 per cent, from US$ 14.3 billion in 2011 to reach US$ 30.4 billion in 2018. China’s medical devices market is dominated by the ophthalmic devices and In Vitro Diagnostics (IVD) sectors. The IVD market in China is driven by a large patient pool that requires large volumes of tests. With the implementation of the government-backed insurance schemes, and a subsequent increase in the reimbursement ratio (the ratio of the total amount reimbursed) there is expected to be a significant demand for the adoption of ophthalmic surgery devices."

The increasing volume of surgical procedures being carried out is creating higher demand for surgical equipment, says the company, such as surgical sutures, electrosurgical devices and hand instruments. The global market for surgical equipment, says Global Research, Inc, is therefore forecast to exceed US$ 7 billion by 2018, following growth at a Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR) of 4.2 per cent during 2011-2018.

Modern surgery is hardly conceivable without minimally invasive procedures. New techniques and methods are being introduced all the time. These bring about major benefits for the patients but, at the same time, impose the highest possible demands on surgeons and the medical instruments manufactured to support them.

## Steel for medical

Equally crucial to the design and functionality of a medical device is the material used to create it. Soldering to some of the most widely used metals in the medical market, including Nitinol and stainless steel, can be challenging. Removal of the tenacious surface metal oxides must be completed before soldering can take place.

For example, Grade 304 Stainless Steel provides a versatility and durability that suits medical applications. The steel also possesses good post-welding characteristics. No strengthening is required after the steel is shaped, welded and cooled. This greatly reduces the risk of breakage. In addition, the steel can be safely sterilized and re-used, where other materials could cause a potential risk of re-infection if re-used. 304 Stainless Steel contains 18 per cent chromium and 8 per cent nickel. This combination lends itself well to the manufacture of a wide range of medical devices. It is not magnetic and so will not cause complications in the operating room or inside the human body. Its heat resistance allows it to withstand varying temperatures whilst being used in manufacturing, as well as medical device application, without changing shape.
