# FOCUS ON CHINA

[TARİH: 01.02.2011 ISMR]

While the repercussions of the downtrend in the US economy and the global economic downturn were felt in European markets, countries such as China and India currently offer more growth potential compared to their western counterparts. China is witnessing high growth in its steel and furniture industries and production of goods such as semi-conductors is increasing.

China has now become the biggest exporter in the global economy ahead of Germany and the US. It now accounts for about 11 per cent of world trade in goods. More than half of China’s exports are currently produced by foreign-invested enterprises (processing trade).

Many countries in Asia, including China, are also increasing their defence expenditures. Led by China, countries such as India, Australia, and Singapore are increasingly expanding their military capabilities. These countries usually have defence budgets worth approximately 2.3 per cent of their respective GDP. According to Frost & Sullivan’s Asia Pacific Consultant of Aerospace & Defense Practice Kunal Sinha, Asia Pacific’s military aerospace procurement revenue will grow to a total of US$70.64 billion by 2016, at a CAGR of 5.8 per cent from 2010 to 2016.

“Attracted by low labour costs, global aerospace and defence manufacturers and contractors are shifting their manufacturing activities to China, India, Malaysia, Singapore and other Asian countries,” says Frost & Sullivan Consultant Kunal Sinha. “Even after accounting for transportation and others costs associated with managing the supply chain, total savings of 20 to 30 per cent can still be achieved.”

## MACHINE TOOLS IN CHINA

In 2009, affected by the economic crisis, the global machine tool industry suffered heavy losses, and the gross industrial output value and sales revenue fell by 32 per cent and 33 per cent respectively from the previous year. Stimulated by a series of policies for expanding domestic demand, China’s machine tool industry thrived and the industrial output value grew by 7.6 per cent year-on-year, surpassing Germany and Japan to rank No.1 in the world for the first time. The sales revenue for China’s machine tool industry rose by 0.5 per cent year-on-year, becoming the largest consumer of machine tools all over the world for a successive ninth year.

According to Research in China, China’s machine tool industry has made new progress in aspects including output, localisation, machine numerical control and demand/requirement structures. The import substitution rate of China-made machine tools was 55 per cent in 2007, 61 per cent in 2008 and 70 per cent in 2009. CNC machine tools saw rapid growth in China in recent years.

“The demand for ordinary and economical types of CNC machine tools has shrunk, whilst the demand for popular and high-end type of CNC machine tools continues to grow. It was expected that the demand for CNC machine tools would reach over 100,000 sets in China in 2010, of which 40 per cent would come from medium and high-end models – the market size will be between RMB6 billion – 7 billion,” confirmed Research in China.

## SHEET METAL MARKETS

Sheet metal forming is a large industry in China. The machinery building industry uses 60 – 70 per cent sheet/plate, tube and section metal per year. Based on this, there are over 30,000 sheet metal forming companies in China. Most of China’s sheet metal forming companies mainly employ hand-operated production lines, which influences the productivity and production quality of parts produced, especially in the automotive industry.

“It is very important for car body parts-forming plants to install a multi-station press and automatic production lines,” CCMI’s sheet metal forming committee explained. “Many sheet metal forming plants are the workshops of OEMs. According to our investigations, there is very strong competition in stampings for electronics and home appliances. China’s sheet metal forming industry needs to increase its precision stampings’ production, as well as the number of automatic production lines and multi-station stamping press lines.”

“Although the European and US markets will continue to present challenging conditions and limited growth potential for the oil & gas industry throughout 2010, the Middle East and Asia are expected to drive demand of equipment. The shift in demand from the US to China will affect equipment manufacturing resulting in a higher number of orders covered by Asian manufacturers. For this reason, China and the Far East are widely seen as the motor for growth across the next few years for midstream operations, and Europe and the US for LNG and gas infrastructure,” Frost and Sullivan reported recently.

## AUTOMOTIVE AND TRANSPORT MARKETS

In the first half of 2010, China’s automobile sales surged 30.45 per cent year-on-year to 7.18 million units, with passenger vehicle sales amounting to 5.42 million units, up 25.58 per cent year-on-year.

According to statistics and analysis from the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM), China’s automobile production reached 1,754,800 units in November, an increase of 13.67 per cent compared with the previous month, and up 26.11 per cent compared with the same period in 2009. Among the main automobile categories, production of passenger cars was 1,364,500 units, higher than the previous 13.83 per cent and up 26.97 per cent year-on-year; commercial vehicle output was 390,300 units, higher than the previous 13.12 per cent, and an increase of 13.23 per cent compared with the same period in 2009.

From January to November 2010, China’s automobile production reached 16,400,100 units, an increase of 33.71 per cent compared with the same period in 2009. Production of passenger cars was 12,451,900 units, up 35.28 per cent year-on-year; commercial vehicle output was 3,948,200 units, an increase of 28.96 per cent compared with the same period in 2009.

The development and expansion of airports have been rampant across Asia Pacific countries, especially in China, India, Thailand, Indonesia, Australia, Malaysia and the Philippines. Frost & Sullivan expects that the airports industry will maintain its positive outlook, especially in China and India, which are the two primary nations that will continue to drive the industry forward. Over the next 10 years, Frost & Sullivan’s research shows that more than 350 new airports will be developed in the Asia Pacific region to cater to the growing demands and convenience of air travel, both with business and leisure travellers.

Mumbai, Shanghai and Ho Chi Minh City will likely be the top three investment destinations for urban rail in the next 20 years. Urban rail development is a key priority for China. Shanghai, with a population of over 20 million, also offers significant opportunities to various rail technology companies due to its plans to build 20 metro lines within the next 10 years. Chronic congestion is a major concern and the current public transportation system is heavily over-utilised. Urban rail projects planned are aimed at addressing the existing congestion issues and ease public movement across the city, especially the central business districts.

Despite the global economic crisis, the steel industry in China has enjoyed good levels of growth and Chinese steelmakers are forging ahead with growth plans and partnerships to increase market share. ■
