The G7 comprises the U.K., U.S., France, Germany, Italy, Canada, and Japan — the seven largest industrial economies in the world. Many issues were brought forward during the recent meeting, but three stood out as the most serious and responsible for the biggest rifts.

The top of the list was the ongoing trade war with China. This confrontation has escalated to the point of no return, and the impact on the global economy has been profound. The previous incarnation of this policy could be interpreted as hardball negotiating, but it has now become an open confrontation that leaves no room for a reduction in tension as both sides have taken steps that will be nearly impossible to back down from.

The second major point of contention was Iran. The U.S. position has been to put intense pressure on the Iranian regime with an eye to forcing some kind of regime change. The position of the other G7 nations is opposed to that of the U.S., and it seems likely that plans to subvert the U.S. plan will go forward despite threats of retaliation from the U.S. through yet another round of tariffs.

The third area was the Amazon. The G7 nations are critically concerned about the policies of the Bolsonaro regime in Brazil. The Amazon has been set ablaze by illegal farming operations that want to replace the forests with crops. The value of the Amazon as a carbon sink and producer of oxygen is unparallel, and its destruction is extremely detrimental to the entire world.

It was more than interesting to see what commodity markets did with the news from the G7 meetings. Given all the attention on Iran and the continued tension in the Middle East, it would have been expected that oil prices would have jumped, but they didn’t. In fact, they fell as the U.S. continues to sit on a glut of supply that would easily off set any reduction in world output. Steel prices are down along with demand, and so are most other metal prices.
