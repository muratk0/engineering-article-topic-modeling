# PREPARING THE WAY

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

Last year was a tough one for metal formers and machine tool manufacturers, in the wake of the global economic downturn. European economies were badly affected.

ISMR spoke with Dr Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association) for his assessment of current trends in German metalforming markets and his view of the future for German manufacturers.

“The German economy was heavily affected by the global economic downturn, especially as it is very export-oriented. This accent on exports is helping us today to recover faster than other regions because the upswing, to a large extent, in the machinery sector is driven by growth in Asia and South America. The automotive market in Germany, in particular, was strengthened by governmental measures in 2009. Today, it is picking up again internationally and there are several new projects in discussion. Manufacturers in cutting and forming are seeing positive trends in this market right now.

“In 2009, overall machine tool manufacturers lost over 55% in orders and 30% in turnover. In the metal forming sector, there was a drop of 40% in demand and 25% in turnover. We saw an upswing at the end of 2009 and orders from January to July 2010 showed an increase of 67% in total machine tools and 36% in metal forming. Machine tool orders in total are expected to rise by more than 65% over 2010.”

“Despite the upswing in orders for the first half of this year, a turnover deterioration of 19% was evident (due to the time lag between order intake and corresponding sales as well as special and large metal forming machine tools). Total machine tool export orders in Germany for the first half of 2010 are up by 55% and, in Europe, we saw an increase of 40%. We are seeing improvements in European markets but not in all countries across Europe.

Overall, the European machine tool market saw 36% fewer exports in 2009, he told ISMR.

“Economic data for German machine tool manufacturers was better in the first half of the year than expected over the year. Demand is growing again. Capacity utilisation and the order book are moving upwards. The fall in exports stopped in the second quarter and the cut-back in staff is bottoming out. However, German machine tool production will fall once more in the current year.

“In the first half of 2010, the order book grew by 58 per cent in comparison to the same period in the previous year. Here, domestic incoming orders increased by 51 per cent; foreign demand grew by 61 per cent.”
