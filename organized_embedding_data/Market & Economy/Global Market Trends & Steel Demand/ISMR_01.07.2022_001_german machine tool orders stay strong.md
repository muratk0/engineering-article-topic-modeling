# German machine tool orders stay strong

[TARİH: 01.08.2022 ISMR]

Orders received by the German machine tool industry in the first quarter of 2022 were 44 per cent up on the same period last year. There was a 44 per cent increase in orders from within Germany and also from overseas.

"Orders for our industry continued to flow in during the first quarter of this year," commented Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director, VDW (German Machine Tool Builders Association). "They were only eight per cent below the figures posted in the record year of 2018," he continued. However, the effects of the Russia-Ukraine war are expected to become more pronounced in the coming months, he said. This is evidenced firstly by the most recent growth rates, which have been significantly lower. Secondly, supply bottlenecks remain the greatest challenge and are far from being resolved. “This issue will be with us for quite a few months," Schäfer feared.

Supply bottlenecks are also the reason why production continues to lag far behind orders. In the first quarter, production rose by a disproportionately low six per cent. In response, the VDW has lowered its production forecast for the current year from 14 to eight per cent growth.

“At present, the new machines are simply ending up in storage, as many of them cannot be put into operation because of missing parts," explained Schäfer. However, these machines do not appear in the production figures until they are loaded onto trucks or ships. China is currently the source of a great deal of uncertainty. The lockdowns in numerous major cities and industrial centres are preventing customers from taking delivery of machines, and service personnel from travelling to their customers.

"As with production, there is currently little momentum behind exports. There was a slight increase of one per cent in the first quarter. Europe and the Americas have posted slight declines of two per cent and one per cent, respectively. Asia has grown by eight per cent, with Central and South Asia and Southeast Asia growing much more strongly than East Asia. At four per cent, exports to China, the largest sales market, grew only moderately at the beginning of the year. China accounts for a good 19 per cent of German exports, while the U.S. market takes a much smaller share of 13 per cent. Exports to the USA fell by six per cent in the first quarter," explained the VDW.

“Italy ranks third among the ten most important sales markets, accounting for both an increase and a share of six per cent. After the poor performance of the previous year, business with Switzerland bounced back, with 70 per cent growth in the first quarter. This returns the country to fourth place in the rankings. Exports to Austria were lower compared with the very strong first quarter of 2021. Exports to Poland were also down slightly on the previous year's level. The Netherlands' figures remain buoyant, with the country taking 11 per cent more machine tools in the first quarter than last year. France has continued its downward slide, falling by 18 per cent. Mexico and Turkey complete the top ten, posting sizeable double-digit growth rates of 27 and 26 per cent respectively," it added.

Total imports to Germany have grown by 20 per cent, driven by Switzerland, Japan and China, whose shipments have seen significant increases.■
