Steel conduit use to remain strong in 2018, says Steel Tube Institute

The Steel Tube Institute (STI), Glenview, Ill., predicts another year of high demand for steel conduit. This strong outlook is based on discussions at recent meetings with industry leaders, contractors, and engineers.

At the December 2017 meeting of the Construction Sustainability Council, the American Iron and Steel Institute presentation showed that ongoing U.S. economic improvement will continue to boost growth in jobs and business investments throughout 2018. Predictions also showed an increase in the use of steel, as well as a 2 percent increase in nonresidential building construction. These trends signify that the demand for and use of steel conduit will remain strong, specifically in the construction of new buildings.

Presenters at the 2017 Greenbuild International Conference and Expo and other industry gatherings have noted a growing trend in designing more resilient buildings. The Industry Statement on Resilience, co-authored by the American Institute of Architects and the National Institute of Building Sciences, defines resilience as "the ability to prepare and plan for, absorb, recover from, and more successfully adapt to adverse events." Electrical systems are a significant component of any building design, and steel conduit is the most resilient product for protecting physical conductors, according to STI. Logic suggests engineers and architects will continue to specify steel conduit in the coming year as part of the push to improve community resilience.

In preparation for this growth, STI has hired Dale Crawford as director for steel conduit. He is responsible for activities, strategies, and programs involving North America’s leading steel conduit manufacturers.

Hypertherm celebrates 50 years in business

Hypertherm, a Hanover, N.H.-based manufacturer of industrial cutting systems and software, is celebrating 50 years in business. Events this year will include the unearthing of a long-buried time capsule and celebrations at all its global locations with company founder Dick Couch.

Couch and Professor Bob Dean started the company in 1968 in a small, two-car garage, where they discovered that radially injecting water into a plasma cutting nozzle would form a narrow arc, making it possible to cut metal accurately and quickly while nearly eliminating heavy dross and double arcing.

Today the company designs and manufactures industrial cutting products for use in a variety of industries such as shipbuilding, manufacturing, and automotive repair.

Beckwood Press announces two contracts

St. Louis-based Beckwood Press Co., a manufacturer of custom hydraulic presses, automation systems, and forming equipment, has announced it has been awarded two contracts.

One contract was awarded by Dahlstrom Roll Form, Jamestown, N.Y., to design and manufacture a 75-ton, high-speed punching press. With a 30- by 50-in. forming area and a custom-designed hydraulic circuit, the press can punch aluminum and steel sheet material up to 60 times/min. Programmable fixed stops limit ram retraction and eliminate overshoot, while a through-hole in the bed expedites scrap removal and helps increase throughput.

Beckwood also was awarded a contract to manufacture two 13-ton, servo-driven, electromechanical presses for use by a footwear manufacturer in a trimming application. The 40- by 50- by 158-in. presses feature a monolithic frame, quick die clamps, 13-in. stroke, and a knockout cylinder mounted inside the ram weldment to strip the part after it is trimmed. Using a Parker XFC electromechanical actuator to drive the main ram and a load cell to monitor force feedback, the presses will have an average cycle time of 2.5 sec. or less.

Dallas Industries delivers feed line to Orick Stamping

Dallas Industries, Troy, Mich., a manufacturer of coil handling and press feeding equipment and controls for the stamping industry, has delivered a feed line to Orick Stamping in Elida, Ohio.

The feed line employs the Dallas LoopSelect compact coil line concept, which allows for two material loops based on material thickness. For lighter-gauge materials, a front loop is developed between the reel and feeder/straightener. For heavier-gauge materials, a back or paddle loop is developed below the mandrel. SyncLoop technology monitors the coil OD and loop depths and adjusts payout speeds based on the line speed average, coil OD, and loop depth.

The compact line includes a servo feed with a piloting pull-through straightener that enables both the upper feed and straightener rolls to lift off the material momentarily for proper pilot registration.
