s of press time in early December, the domestic steel mills have announced four flat-rolled price increases totaling a minimum of $140/ton ($7.00/cwt) going back to October 21.

As of early December, the U.S. steel mills were looking for base price levels for new orders being placed for the first quarter of 2017 to be a minimum of $600/ton ($30.00/cwt) on hot-rolled and $820/ton ($41.00/cwt) on cold-rolled and coated (galvanized/Galvalume). These prices are before any extras, such as coatings, need to be added to the base price to get the final FOB mill transaction pricing on any specific product.

On Dec. 6 Steel Market Update (SMU) market analysis found flat-rolled price transactions were averaging $580/ton ($29.00/cwt) on hot-rolled. We also published cold-rolled base prices as averaging $790/ton ($39.50/cwt), galvanized averaging $790/ton ($39.50/cwt), and Galvalume averaging $790/ton ($39.50/cwt) for new orders placed that week for future delivery in early 2017.

At the time that this is written, we are now six weeks into the current up cycle for steel prices, and in the opinion of Steel Market Update, pricing momentum continues to be with the domestic steel mills. Our expectation is for steel prices to continue to increase over the next 30 to 60 days.

Benchmark hot-rolled coil bottomed during the middle of October at $470/ton ($23.50/cwt); and at $580/ton, it is now $110/ton higher and was expected to continue to move toward the $600/ton level by the end of 2016.

During the recent Heating, Air-conditioning & Refrigeration Distributors International conference in Colorado Springs, Colo., we discussed the pricing charts for galvanized steel, which had price movements similar to the hot-rolled products (see Figure 1). We found there was confusion about what the index numbers that buyers see on a daily basis mean to those actually buying steel in the spot markets.

When Steel Market Update looks at pricing, we gather data that gives us a high and low from which we determine our average. It is the average price most people hear in the marketplace, but you need to be aware there are differences from customer to customer and supplier to supplier.

Second, when looking at these numbers, remember that they represent spot transactions being conducted out of the domestic steel mills. Spot sales are those being taken now for future delivery based on the mill’s lead times. (Lead time is related to the amount of time it takes the mill to make the steel.) The mill lead time on hot-rolled at this time is three to five weeks. Therefore, the numbers in Figure 1 are actually apply to product delivery in January 2017.

For other flat-rolled products for which more processing is required (such as cold rolling, annealing, pickling, and coating with zinc), the lead times are running anywhere from four to nine weeks. As a result, you could have product being delivered in February at the prices being quoted the week of Dec. 5.

This is important to remember if your company buys product from a service center, as it may or may not be passing its replacement cost along to you. One of the reasons publications such as Steel Market Update exist is to provide information to those buying steel from service centers so they can plan for price adjustments (up or down) based on how our price index is moving and the momentum of the market. (To gauge that momentum, we provide a Price Momentum Indicator to our clients.)

Historical Averages for Hot-Rolled

SMU also believes steel buyers need to be aware of the historical averages and what was behind those numbers. Figure 2 shows the annual average price for hot-rolled coil during the calendar years 2009-2015, as well as 2016 year-to-date (YTD). We picked this timeline because 2009 and 2010 were both considered to be during the Great Recession.

Based on the historical averages, steel buyers should not be surprised to see prices reach into the mid $600s in early 2017. If we get a spike in demand, we could see the number push up against the $700 to $750/ton level next year. This is, of course, subject to any impact a black swan event could have on the market. (It should be noted that most black swan events, like the unexpected interruption of steel production at a mill, tend to push numbers higher rather than lower.)

There is one thing you can count on during calendar year 2017: It will be tumultuous.
