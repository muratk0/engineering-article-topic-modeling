# Good News Indicators, Some Clouds on the Horizon

These are good days for the industrial community as a whole, at least according to much of the data that has been released of late. The Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI) has been very comfortably in the mid-50s and the New Orders Index has been in the 60s. The manufacturing sector of the Credit Managers’ Index has been a little volatile but has stayed in the mid-50s as well, and just like the PMI, anything over 50 indicates expansion. The latest iteration regarding capacity utilization shows numbers at a three-year high and closer than ever to the bottom end of ideal. It is not quite at 80 percent, but at 78 percent it is getting there. Now you can add the data that is coming from the "Forming & Fabricating Job Shop Consumption Report" (FFJSCR) to that list of good news indicators. Some clouds remain on the horizon, but for now there is reason for optimism.

The level of operating capacity is reported as increasing by around 43 percent of the respondents, and about 55 percent report this number as stable; less than 10 percent report a decrease. In general, the capacity numbers reported by the FFJSCR are roughly 10 percent lower than they are for the entire industrial sector, and that gap is consistent. The companies the FFJSCR represents generally operate under different restrictions. They do not have the luxury of competing for business without having the capacity to immediately respond if they win it. They essentially have to invest in capacity on faith so that they can be ready if the opportunity comes. As a result, there is generally always a certain amount of slack built in. The good news is that capacity numbers have been improving quarter by quarter, and that means more of these investments are paying off and faster.

Even better news comes from the report on new orders, as they are indicative of future activity that at this point would likely carry the company through the bulk of the year. A whopping 47.1 percent report that new-order activity is increasing, and 42.4 percent report that it is stable; only 10.3 percent report that new-order activity has been declining. This is on a par with what was reported last quarter, and that is a trend worth reinforcing. This is also consistent with the new-order activity the PMI has reported.
