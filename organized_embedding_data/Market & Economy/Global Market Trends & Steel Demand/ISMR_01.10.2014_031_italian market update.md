# ITALIAN MARKET UPDATE

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

We examine the latest metal forming and industrial robotics’ statistics for the Italian market

With 400 companies, and a production value of 4.5 billion euros (2013 data), the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation system manufacturing industry has a high economic importance for the country as a whole. It contributes 7.2 billion euros to the national GDP (if production, sales of parts, tools and numerical controls, as well as machine servicing, are taken into account). Ranking fourth in global production and third in export production, the Italian industry has always featured highly on the international scene.

Particularly prone to export activities (for which 75% of its overall production is destined), the Italian industry sees the US as one of its most prosperous and thriving markets. With 400 million machines sold there, the United States became the second most important export market for the Italian machine tool, robotics and automation industry in 2013.

Italy, said UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robots, automation systems and auxiliary technologies), will register a remarkable increase in machine tool consumption, with growth rates above the European average. In particular, it expects domestic consumption to go up 2.5% in 2014, 5.6% in 2015, 7.3% in 2016 and 8.3% in 2017.

## Metal forming in focus

According to UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robots, automation systems and auxiliary technologies), Italy produced forming machine tools to a value of 1,962 million euros in 2013, a 7.9% decrease compared to 2012. Italy, it said, now ranks third among international manufacturers for these systems, after China and Germany, but ahead of Japan, Korea and the United States.

“According to the 2013 final balance, Italian exports of metal forming machines decreased by 7.4% when compared with the previous year. Particularly high is the level of production numbers exported, equal to 66.5% of total sector production. Imports reached 111 million euros. The sector generated a positive trade balance, to a value of 1,194 million euros, equal to almost half of the total machine tool trade balance,” explained the association.

Among the most exported Italian machines, first place goes to curving and bending systems, followed by presses. The main export markets for Italy’s forming sector are the United States (an 11% increase when compared with the previous year), followed by China (+12.5%). Italian exports went down to countries such as Germany (-19.8%), Russia (-24.5%), Brazil (-24.4%), India (-20%), France (-20.3%) and Poland (-29%). However, Italian exports increased to Turkey (+13.6%) and Mexico (+4.8%). The total percentage for the Italian metal forming sector (machine tools and production systems) exports was 40.9%.

## Industrial robotics in Italy

From the analysis of data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, it appears that, after the recession in 2012, the Italian robotics sector has started to grow again. In 2013, Italian production of robots reached 490 million euros, 5.2% more than in 2012. The result was driven both by positive results in exports, which grew by 8.3% to 195 million, and by delivery performance on the domestic market, which grew by 3.1% to 295 million. Italian consumption also showed positive signs, said the association, +4.9% at a value of 515 million euros. The export-toproduction ratio also increased, from 38.6% in 2012 to 39.8% in 2013.

About half of all robots produced by Italian companies in 2013 were destined for overseas markets. In 2013, in Italy, the main areas of application for robots were material handling and welding which represented, in terms of units, 68.3% and 17% of total consumption respectively. The total number of robots installed in Italy in 2013 was 94,790, said UCIMU. The Lombardy and Piedmont regions each host over 30% of the companies in this sector. In the comparison between the two regions, as far as production units are concerned, Piedmont is ahead of Lombardy for contribution to sector turnover, with 64% of the total (against 6.3% from Lombardy, which is also behind Emilia Romagna with 23.8%).

Material handling includes all those functions connected with forming, die casting, handling of parts, measurement and control. Welding is generally connected to the activity of joining metal parts, and relates mainly to the automotive sector.

“Of particular interest is the analysis of distribution by type of robot. Particularly popular are articulated robots capable of carrying out complex operations, thanks to the flexibility of movement ensured by the arms which operate as anthropomorphic tools. In 2013, Italy produced 1,935 articulated robots, used for welding and painting applications. Second in terms of production were Cartesian robots (612 units), used in pick-and-place processes, for handling workpieces,” explained UCIMU.

## LAMIERA ends on right note

The seventeenth edition of the LAMIERA sheet metal exhibition in Italy, which took place at BolognaFiere from 14 to 17 May 2014, ended on a positive note. The number of visits officially recorded at LAMIERA, one of the main international exhibitions dedicated to machine tools operating with metal forming technologies, reached 18,620 (826 of which were from outside Italy).

Approximately 370 companies exhibited (40% of which were from outside Italy), occupying an overall exhibition space of 35,000 square metres. The international profile of the event, promoted by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robots, automation systems and auxiliary technologies), was confirmed by the strong presence of exhibitors from: Germany; UK; Spain; the USA; Switzerland; Holland; Austria; France; Turkey; Belgium and Ireland as well as Australia; China; Denmark; Finland; Japan; Greece; Monaco; Romania and Taiwan.

The spotlight at LAMIERA was on sheet metal forming machines; welding machines; cutting and oxy-cutting machines; bar; tube and profile processing machines; presses; shearing and punching machines; treatment and finishing; components and accessories.

The LAMBDA project, which this year focused on sustainability, was a main feature of the exhibition. The main attraction in this area was the Alfa Romeo 4C, designed by the Centro Ricerche Fiat, as the representative object of the new production techniques. The conference programme, run alongside LAMIERA, was attended by approximately 170 operators.

400 bilateral meetings at the event involved about 40 Italian companies and operators from overseas delegations. Invited by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE and ICE-Agenzia, the event was attended by representatives from: Brazil; India; the United States and Turkey.

Also invited independently by the association were delegations from: Canada, Croatia and Serbia. These were joined by the delegation from Russia, the country of honour for LAMIERA 2014, invited under the auspices of the ‘Machines Italia in Russia’ project, promoted by the Italian Ministry for Economic Development and FEDERMACCHINE.

The next LAMIERA exhibition will take place at BolognaFiere from 11-14 May 2016 (www.lamiera.net). EMO MILANO 2015, the global exhibition dedicated to the metal working industry, will be held in Milan from 5-10 October 2015. ■
