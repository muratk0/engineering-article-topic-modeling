## INTERNATIONAL GROWTH PROSPECTS

Dr Schäfer is optimistic that, by the end of the year, things will be looking much better for German machine tool manufacturers. He notes that many German manufacturers have invested in new product developments to position themselves for the eventual upturn. Creativity and innovation is still strong, he told ISMR.

A view of international machine tool demand shows that it has increased by almost three quarters in the first half of the year, Schäfer explained. Sales are roughly at the same level as last year. Orders in Asia have also sharply increased, he adds – the main players for Germany are Japan and South Korea. Orders in France, Spain, Switzerland, Germany and Great Britain have moved up two figures, says Schäfer.

“German manufacturers are well-positioned in terms of international competition and they have done their homework in recent years,” he explained. “This concerns the intensification of research and development, improvement to products e.g. through their energy-efficient design, the optimisation of procurement and production processes, as well as improvements to cost structures. In addition, a more flexible labour market and the regulation on shorter hours has helped.

“German machine tool providers often focus their production on special solutions. This way they are immediately sought after again when demand increases because there are clearly fewer procurement alternatives.”

“In July, German machine tool capacity stood at 76.3 per cent. The all-time low of the same month in 2009 was a good ten percentage points less. The order book is slowly beginning to climb again, most recently at 6.9 months in June of this year. Unfortunately, the employment figures dropped once again in June and July to 63,100 after experiencing slight increases in the previous two months. We assume, however, that the cutback on core staff is slowly bottoming out.”

Schäfer indicates that the driving force for the pick-up in demand for machine tools in the first half of the year was demand from abroad. According to VDW’s preliminary figures, all the BRIC countries i.e. Brazil, Russia, India and China, are registering growth again. Substantial investments were also made again in South Korea and Japan. Europe, Germany’s largest market, also contributed to the order boom. In particular, Switzerland, Austria, Poland and Italy have moved forward, says Schäfer.

“The driving forces in Asia were China, which further strengthened its position as by far the most important market, and South Korea. America, in particular the USA, was still regressive while Brazil registered a plus of 17 per cent. In contrast to this, exports to Europe were weak, which were around a quarter below the volume of the previous year. Rays of hope were reflected in Turkey and Belarus.”

“The economies in the emerging markets are growing at an above average rate in the current and coming years,” he added. “The development of the industry is progressing everywhere in quantum leaps. For this, production technology is required, which is sourced in large quantities from Germany. In the previous year, this area represented over one quarter of all machine tool exports.

“Even today, it is foreseeable that the demand is moving towards Asia. Today, China alone represents more than one quarter of German exports and Asia overall accounts for 40 per cent. In Asia, the Japanese are strong and the Chinese are catching up. It is the German providers who are facing big challenges if the domestic market of Europe no longer yields the high growth of the past. It is not easy for medium-sized companies with limited personnel and capital to be present everywhere on the international stage. Here, concentration on particularly promising sales markets and better cooperative ventures with German manufacturers abroad could be the order of the day.”

Schäfer does, however, warn of continuing risks in global finance markets which have not yet been averted and the danger that they may erupt again. He highlights concerns in the banking sector for members who try to get finance.

“The fast upswing has to be financed and it is the responsibility of the banking sector to provide enough capital to manufacturers to support this. We hope that our industry will be properly supported in the necessary way to realise this upswing.

“If there are no more shocks from the financial markets, then German machine tool production should be back on a growth path from next year,” he adds.

## SUSTAINABILITY AND ENERGY EFFICIENCY

“With the Blue Competence® brand, the German machine tool industry has proactively taken the issue of sustainability fully on board, and has thus positioned itself ahead of its international competitors”, explained Dr. Schäfer.

Blue Competence®, however, is not solely market-driven. Within the framework of the EUP Directive, Europe’s environmental policymakers aim to define specific requirements for the energy-efficient machine tool. The plan is to ensure continuous and quantifiable initiatives for improvement. For actual implementation, the directive expresses a preference for self-regulation by the industry.

The VDW is a strong supporter of the energy-efficient machine tool, because the sector has put all the preconditions in place for harnessing its capabilities as much as possible. However, the measures involved have to be practical, warns Dr Schäfer, otherwise ‘a fully functioning market will be destroyed’.
