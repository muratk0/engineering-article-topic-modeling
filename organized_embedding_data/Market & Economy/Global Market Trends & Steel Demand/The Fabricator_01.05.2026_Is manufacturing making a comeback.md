# Is manufacturing making a comeback?

Tube & Pipe Market Update

Tube shipments, key indices rise sharply—along with prices

By

Rick Preckel

MANUFACTURING PERFORMANCE

Data from the U.S. Federal Reserve indicates that the durable goods manufacturing output index improved in February by 0.1% after increasing by 1.1% in January.

Pipe- and tube-consuming categories were mixed. Motor vehicles and parts (+1.7%), electrical equipment and appliances (+1.1%), and aerospace and miscellaneous transportation equipment (+0.3%) all increased but were partially offset by declines in furniture and related products (-0.7%), fabricated metal products (-0.3%), machinery (-1.2%), and miscellaneous manufacturing (-1.1%).

Durable goods manufacturing in February was 2.4% above year-ago levels, and durable goods manufacturing capacity utilization held steady in February at 74.2%.

The February

Manufacturing PMI Report

®

, as published by the Institute for Supply Management

®

(ISM

®

), indicated that economic activity in the manufacturing sector, as measured by the Manufacturing PMI, remained in expansion for the second consecutive month with a reading of 52.4% (growth in the sector is indicated by a reading above 50). This is only the third positive reading in the last 40 months.

Looking at the index components, the New Orders Index expanded for the second straight month in February, moving to 55.8% from 57.1% in January. The New Export Orders Index reading of 50.3% was 0.1 point higher than the January reading. The Backlog of Orders Index registered 56.6%,

up a full 5 percentage points

from the January level and reaching its highest reading since May 2022 (58.7%).

The Production Index for February remained in expansion territory at 53.5% but was 2.4 percentage points lower than the January reading. The Pricing Index in February came in at 70.5%, which indicates rising prices and represented an 11.5% increase from January.

"In February, U.S. manufacturing activity remained in expansion territory, although growing at a slower pace than the month before," said Susan Spence, MBA, chair of the ISM Manufacturing Business Survey Committee. "Of the five subindexes that make up the PMI, two (New Orders and Production) indicated slower growth compared to the previous month. Three demand indicators (the New Orders, Backlog of Orders, and New Export Orders indexes) are in expansion, and the Customers’ Inventories Index remains in ‘too low’ territory,

signaling a positive outlook for future production

."

The tubing-intensive manufacturing sectors reporting growth in February were machinery electrical equipment, appliances, and components; fab-ricated metal products; transportation equipment; and miscellaneous manufacturing. Visit

Keep in mind that Federal Reserve data and ISM data are based on different metrics and may appear contradictory.

DOMESTIC PIPE, TUBE, AND STEEL MILLS

John Deere

announced a major expansion of its U.S. operations, with plans to open an excavator manufacturing plant in North Carolina. A new $70 million factory will take over production of next-generation excavators previously built overseas. Company leaders expect further U.S. investment over the next decade.

E&H Tubing

, a mechanical tube manufacturer in Jackson County, Ind., has launched an expansion of its manufacturing operation to a former industrial site in Washington County. E&H Tubing specializes in high-carbon steel tubing for agricultural, home and garden, and construction applications, producing A513 and A500 tubing in rounds and shapes from 0.706 to 3.5 in. OD.

SHIPPING AND IMPORTS

Preliminary pipe and tube shipments for February for the sectors of the market that we cover increased from January’s readings by 5.8% and were 4.0% higher than a year ago. Manufacturing and construction product shipments in February increased by 6.1% in that period and were 8.2% higher than a year ago.

Stronger yet, preliminary mechanical tubing product shipments in February increased by 10.1% from January and were 2.9% higher than February of last year.

OUTLOOK

Mechanical and hollow structural section pipe and tube product prices have continued to rise, with additional price increase announcements in February and March. These are largely driven by rising hot-rolled band (HRB) prices, which have continued their upward trend. Most analysts suggest that weak HRB imports are the primary driver of higher domestic HRB prices.

On Feb. 20, the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that President Trump’s IEEPA tariffs are illegal. The Trump administration immediately implemented temporary 10% Section 122 tariffs on all countries and initiated Section 301 investigations to determine if longer-term tariffs are appropriate. Section 232 tariffs on steel and steel derivative items remain in force.

A geopolitical conflict in Iran has disrupted the oil and natural gas market, which has raised the price for transportation fuels to the highest level since mid-2022. When combined with other repercussions from the war, those increases are creating economic concerns.

FLAT-ROLLED STEEL

According to data from SteelBenchmarker, the index for base domestic HRB prices moved to 1.27 in December from 1.23 in January. According to other sources, prices have continued to increase since the last reading. The index hit a low of 1.06 in September before starting to climb.
