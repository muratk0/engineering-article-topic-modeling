Sometimes the best data is the most local. It manages to isolate conditions in a sector of the country and avoids some of the noise that can skew more national indicators. Given that many of the FMA members claim the middle of the country as home to some degree, there is some very encouraging data coming from this part of the world.

The data from the Mid-America Business Conditions Index has been making steady progress since collapsing in April of last year. This is another of the studies conducted by Ernie Goss at Creighton University and matches up very well with some of the national indices such as the Purchasing Managers’ Index (PMI). Both have an index where anything over 50 signals growth and under 50 suggests contraction. In April of last year, the index fell to an 11-year low (numbers not seen since the Great Recession) but for the last nine months, it has been gaining momentum. The index numbers for January rose to 67.3 from a 64.1 reading in December. This is an index for nine states in the Midwest.

IN APRIL OF LAST YEAR, THE INDEX FELL TO AN 11-YEAR LOW (NUMBERS NOT SEEN SINCE THE GREAT RECESSION).

Looking at Kansas as an example, the index showed similar gains as it went from 55.3 in December to 62.0. The breakdowns in the survey told a positive story. New orders came in at 72.8, production or sales came in at 81.3, delivery and lead time came in at 63.3, employment at 53.3, and inventories at 39.3. The Goss study identified a gain of some 800 manufacturing jobs in the state.

What makes all this the more encouraging is that it matches national trends. The latest PMI fell a little from the December numbers but remained strongly in the expansion zone with a reading of 58.7 as compared to the 60.5 notched in December. The results of the Credit Managers’ Index were even stronger with a reading of 59.7 as contrasted with the 57.8 noted in December. All of this points squarely at continued growth in the manufacturing sector.

Demand for goods remains very strong. Choosing Kansas as the target of this deep dive is not just because it is my home state but because Kansas is typical of a state that many do not think of as a manufacturing state despite the fact it is the second biggest industry (behind agriculture).
