# Let’s prepare for the reshoring wave

[TARİH: 01.06.2025 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

If you believe the U.S. manufacturing renaissance is near, you need to support funding for the Manufacturing Extension Partnership

DAN DAVIS

I

n early April, The Fabricator conducted a survey of its subscribers and members of the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association, with one question asking them "Do you see these tariffs being good for bolstering U.S. manufacturing in the long term?" Forty-five percent of respondents said "yes," and 16% said "maybe."

That’s a significant share of the metal fabricating community believing that manufacturing in the U.S. can grow, enabling the country to become a self-sufficient maker of goods. Based on anecdotal evidence of reshoring activity turning into increased quoting levels for job shops, you understand some of the optimism for President Donald Trump’s trade decisions.

If this American manufacturing renaissance is to happen, it makes sense that small and medium-sized manufacturers will need all the help that they can get to prepare for this new work. That’s why it’s imperative to continue funding of the Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) programs across the U.S. and Puerto Rico.

Started in 1988 in the midst of a trade war with Japan, MEP was created to assist manufacturing companies without ample resources to prepare for and implement new technologies in the hope of finding new efficiencies. Over the years, MEP centers have established footholds all over the country, helping more than 150,000 manufacturers and leading to an estimated $152.2 billion in new sales and $34.2 billion in cost savings, according to the MEP National Network. A report by Summit Consulting and the Upjohn Institute found that MEPs generated a substantial impact in the domestic manufacturing sector, more specifically an economic and financial return ratio of more than 17-to-1 for the $175 million in funding invested in fiscal year 2023.

If you aren’t familiar with the MEP in your area, you’re missing out on a very helpful resource. Are you wondering how to kick-start a lean manufacturing program? Do you want to upgrade your enterprise resource planning software but don’t know where to start? Do you need assistance in targeting new sectors to expand and diversify your customer base? The MEP likely has the expertise to help you with such questions and, likely, a lot more.

In fact, in an annual MEP client impact survey of 8,700 manufacturers conducted in 2024, 64% indicated that ongoing continuous improvement and cost reduction efforts represented the greatest strategic challenge facing their organizations in the near term. Considering 95% of the survey respondents represented small and medium-sized companies that employ fewer than 500 people, they likely could use the help. (For comparison’s sake, about 70% of the subscribers to The Fabricator work in companies that have fewer than 100 employees.)

If this American manufacturing renaissance is to happen, it makes sense that small and medium-sized manufacturers will need all the help that they can get to prepare for this new work.

Support for MEP has always been bipartisan in Washington, D.C., and that was never more evident than in early April when reports leaked that the Trump administration was pulling funding for MEP agencies in 10 states. Followed by a couple of weeks of intensive phone calls and meeting with lawmakers, the White House altered course and restored funding for the manufacturing consultancies.

But is this support only temporary? Will the slash-and-burn approach to reducing the size of the federal government ultimately take down one of the few government-funded programs that has made a true impact on strengthening the domestic manufacturing base?

That funding question will have to be answered soon as federal money for 14 other state MEPs ends on July 1 and for another 13 on Oct. 1. (The 10 states that saw funding dry up as of April 1 have been given a reprieve through the end of fiscal year 2025, which is Sept. 30.)

"Eliminating federal support for MEP centers would hamper American small and medium-sized manufacturers. We urge you to take immediate action to protect the MEP program and the manufacturers that rely on it," wrote U.S. Sen. Tammy Baldwin, D-Wis., and Maria Cantwell, D-Wash., in a letter to Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick.

Now is not the time to knee-cap the backbone of U.S. manufacturing. Small and medium-sized companies, who already lament that they can’t find enough workers to support current job levels, require assistance if they are to absorb the additional work that is to return to this country’s shores.

For those who contend that these MEP operations can support themselves by charging for consulting services or relying more on state-level funds, you are being overly optimistic. Shops want to work with the MEPs because they don’t require the same monetary investment that a private consultant might. Also, state governments are already going to have to deal with the evaporation of federal dollars for other areas, such as health care and education. MEP support isn’t going to be a top priority for those governors and state lawmakers.

The Department of Government Efficiency claims to have saved taxpayers $165 billion as of May 8. Taking $200 million of that to support domestic manufacturing through the MEPs is an investment that far outweighs any savings that might come with the demise of this important program.
