NORTH AFRICA (MENA) MIDDLE EAST

EASTERN PROMISE

We highlight infrastructure and industrial opportunities in Middle Eastern and North African (MENA) markets

ISMR SAYS

“Market stabilisation came on the back of resilient growth among the countries that are integrated into the Gulf Cooperation Council)”

According to FocusEconomics, growth in the Middle East and North Africa (MENA) market stabilised in Q2 following sharp decelerations observed in Q1. The region expanded 2.6% annually in the second quarter, matching the result tallied in the first. The stabilisation came on the back of resilient growth among the countries that are integrated into the Gulf Co-operation Council (Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates), while activity in oil-dependent nations softened. In Q2, oil-export-driven economies benefited from strong crude supply and higher oil prices.

“The pronounced downward trend seen in oil prices in the second half of the year, which drove prices to hit a six-year low in November, along with less buoyant government spending due to falling revenues, is likely to have impacted economic activity among these economies in the July – December 2015 period. Moreover, rising geopolitical risks and a protracted global economic recovery are likely to have weighed on MENA’s growth,” said the analyst.

## MENA region dynamics

“Economic dynamics in the MENA region have been dominated in 2015 by the low-oil-price environment, an uncertain global economic scenario and rising geopolitical threats. The sharp downward trend in oil prices that started in mid-2014 continued in 2015. Although prices recovered slightly in Q2, they remained at low levels and, in November, crude prices even hit a six-year low,” said FocusEconomics.

“The global economic outlook has weakened gradually throughout this year as a result of a fragile economic recovery in the developed world and, in particular, due to concerns about the health of the Chinese economy. This situation reverberated across many emerging-market nations, including those in the MENA region. Finally, the presence of the Islamic State (ISIL) in the region, widespread terrorist activity and rising political tensions in some countries have all put additional pressure on MENA’s economic performance this year.”

While expected to improve slightly, economic dynamics in the Middle East and North Africa will remain broadly stable in 2016, according to the analyst. Oil prices are expected to remain low next year, thereby putting a dent in the region’s economic growth, particularly in oil-export-driven countries. Moreover, oil-reliant economies will have to embark on a sizable fiscal adjustment to correct 2015’s excessive budget deficits.

“The security situation will be precarious next year as ISIL’s activity remains strong and its influence is expanding in the region. The main positive news for next year would stem from the likely reintegration of Iran into the global economy. While this could imply a notable increase in global oil supply and lower crude prices, the lifting of economic sanctions will certainly mean that Iran’s demand for goods and services will increase, thereby generating opportunities for growth in the region. Globally, while the region is in relatively good shape to cope with the expected monetary policy normalization in the United States, this has the potential to increase volatility in the financial markets, in particular in countries with a lower level of foreign exchange reserves,” concluded FocusEconomics.

## MENA’s economic outlook

The 2016 economic outlook for the Middle East and North Africa was cut in December 2015 after the stable forecast observed in the previous period, as low oil prices and geopolitical threats continue to weigh on the region’s outlook. FocusEconomics’ Consensus Forecast panellists cut the region’s growth forecast for 2016 by 0.1 percentage points to 3.1%. The revised forecast was driven by worse projections for seven of the sixteen countries in the region (Bahrain; Egypt; Iraq; Israel; Qatar; Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates). Panellists kept their projections unchanged for Algeria; Iran; Jordan; Kuwait; Lebanon; Morocco; Oman; Tunisia and Yemen. Growth in the MENA region was expected to be 2.7% in 2015.

“Qatar is expected to be the best performer in 2016, followed by Iran which is expected to benefit from the removal of economic sanctions. At the other end of the spectrum, Saudi Arabia will be the worst performer, followed by Algeria and then Kuwait. Among the rest of the major economies in the region, Egypt and the United Arab Emirates will likely grow the fastest, with a projected expansion of 3.8% and 3.2%, respectively,” explained FocusEconomics.

The World Bank projects overall MENA region GDP growth to be less than 3% for the third year running—about 2.8% for 2015. Iran’s economy is expected to grow faster from 2016 onwards, following the agreement to limit nuclear development and allow more inspections of its nuclear sites.

SteelFab, a forthcoming UAE trade event for the steel fabrication and metal working industry, is looking at leveraging rising demand from core sectors of the industry in the country and the region. Experts point out that United Arab Emirate’s prudent economic policies, aimed at increasing diversification, have resulted in key sectors such as construction; manufacturing, infrastructure; oil & gas and pipelines maintaining positive growth levels despite a decline in oil prices.

“This has rubbed off positively on the regional steel fabrication industry, which is now looking at acquiring cutting-edge equipment, advanced technologies, new products and applications for all their metal forming, fabricating, welding and finishing needs,” the show organiser told ISMR.

The 12th SteelFab 2016, to be held at Expo Centre Sharjah from 17-20 January 2016, is set to become a sourcing and gathering point for fabrication units from across the region and beyond.

“As proof of the success of the government’s diversification programmes, the non-oil sector’s contribution to the UAE’s gross domestic product now stands at 69%, leaving only about a third to the oil sector. According to a recent study, GCC countries are spending an estimated US$ 172 billion on infrastructure and capital projects in 2015 – the highest on record to date. Among the other core sectors, the manufacturing sector was set to grow at a CAGR of 4.32% over the next five years, with the total investment in GCC jumping from US$ 222bn in 2010 to about US$ 380bn in 2014,” added the show organiser.

The construction sector is also flying, said the show organiser, with the total value of contracts in 2015 standing at US$ 194bn. The pipeline industry is also expanding, with around US$ 18bn investments being pumped into the sector over the next four years. The GCC is also expected to add more than 21,000 km to its current pipeline network over the next five years, which is about 14% of the global planned pipeline network of 161,000km.

Before the installation of its new line, Dubai-based United Metal Coating LLC produced 25,000 tons of pre-painted aluminium per year but the company has now started its second painting line since September 2015. With both lines, production capacity will be 50,000 tons of prepainted aluminium coils. Products are shipped to UAE, GCC, Middle East, North Africa and Europe. The new line was designed to be operational from November 2015.

“With more and more new markets and increased demands from our customers, the management of UMC has taken a decision to increase present capacity of 25,000 tons to 50,000 tons. UMC will also add a new slitting line to serve the new coating line,” the company explained.

## MENAP forecast

The International Monetary Fund (IMF) expected a modest recovery in the MENAP region (Middle East, North Africa, Afghanistan and Pakistan) despite a slump in oil prices, regional conflicts and the lingering uncertainty of the post-Arab Spring transitions.

“Despite a sharp decline in oil prices, growth in the oil-exporting countries is projected to remain steady at 2.4 percent in 2015, with inflation subdued. Faced with large oil revenue losses, most countries are expected to use accumulated financial buffers and available financing to cushion some of the impact on growth while gradually slowing their fiscal spending, so that they can share the now reduced oil wealth equitably with future generations and rebuild buffers for dealing with oil price volatility. Specific policy announcements would help reduce uncertainty about how medium-term fiscal consolidation plans will be carried out,” stated the IMF.

“In the oil-importing countries, growth is expected to strengthen from 3 per cent in 2014 to 4 per cent in 2015, supported by a gradual recovery in the euro area, improved domestic confidence and more accommodative fiscal and monetary policies. Lower oil prices are helping, although their impact on near-term growth has been moderated in many countries by incomplete pass-through to retail fuel prices. Consequently, the benefits are mainly in the form of improved fiscal/quasi-fiscal positions and external vulnerabilities rather than stronger growth. Solidifying recent subsidy reforms will help lock in the gains, which can help reduce fiscal and external vulnerabilities where needed and, in other countries, make space for increased growthenhancing spending.” ■
