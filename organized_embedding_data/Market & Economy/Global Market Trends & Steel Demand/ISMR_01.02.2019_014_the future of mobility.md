[TARİH: 01.02.2019 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"If they want to remain successful, manufacturers and suppliers will have to offer user-oriented innovations"

The future of mobility is 'eascy' - electrified, autonomous, shared, connected and 'yearly' updated. Mobility is in the midst of transformation. The pace of investment in future mobility technology increased nearly six-fold, from US$ 4.3 billion to US$ 25.3 billion per year from 2010-2013 to 2014-2017 said McKinsey.

"The comprehensive and rapid reorganisation of the automotive sector after 2025 will have far-reaching consequences for the entire industry and its value chains. Elementary structures and attitudes will have to change fast to cope with developments by 2030 and beyond. If they want to remain successful, manufacturers and suppliers will have to offer user-oriented innovations. All these trends are likely to become increasingly apparent between 2020 and 2025 - which means that these are decisive years for manufacturers and their suppliers," commented PWC.

According to analyst Technavio, the global electric car market size will grow by almost six million units during 2019-2023. It also expects the electric motorcycle market to grow at a CAGR of nearly 42 per cent by 2021. The global connected car market is anticipated to grow rapidly and will post a CAGR of more than 32% from 2018-2022, according to Technavio. In this article, we identify trends, forecasts and patterns in dynamic global automotive markets, for sheet metal manufacturers, that will shape the industry now and in the future.

## Top ten automotive trends

Pathbreaking trends in the automotive industry will be setting the stage for a lot more innovation and investments from stakeholders in the industry. According to analyst Technavio, the following top ten trends defined automotive business strategy for 2018:

Crossover vehicles – For a generation that expects the comfort of an SUV with fuel and operational cost-effectiveness, crossover vehicles have become a big hit. A mix between regular Sedans and SUVs, crossover vehicles have emerged as one of the most practical ways to commute without compromising on comfort and style. Technavio expects big investments and experimentation, alongside the discovery of newer markets for the sturdy crossover vehicles.

Car sharing – Industry experts have found that, over the past couple of years, emotional attachment to cars has decreased significantly. This explains why car sharing has become very popular, even in emerging economies in the APAC and the EMEA regions. The fact that, by 2030, one out of every ten cars sold is estimated to be a shared vehicle, reflects the growing awareness to 'go green' and reduce carbon footprints.

IoT sparking the growth of connected cars - IoT and connected devices have now made their way into the automotive industry. It is expected that connected cars will step out of developmental obscurity and become an important personal item to be connected to the internet. While the technology in itself isn't new, Technavio expects the adoption rate of connected vehicles to rise steadily over the next few years.

- Baby boomers and multi-terrain vehicles – millennials rule almost every market segment but when it comes to the automotive industry, baby boomers lead the way. As a majority of this generation move closer to retirement, being able to travel on their own is a critical requirement. Multi-terrain vehicles provide the much-needed comfort and ease of operation in difficult terrains. Their five-door utility, heated leather seats, high-end audio systems and frugal fuel consumption are very appealing to baby boomers. This explains the spike in sales of multi-terrain vehicles over the past few years, and the trend is expected to continue into 2018.

## - Cloud-powered automotive industry –

One major automotive industry trend is the use of cloud technology. Industry 4.0 has transformed every aspect of automobile production, from design and operation to the servicing of physical systems. Cloud also enables scaling of different processes and services, thereby reducing costs and eliminating any scope for waste. The automotive industry will take more determined steps to integrate their engineering and design activities with the cloud.

- Improved pricing with blockchain capabilities – one of the major challenges for the automotive industry is dealing with counterfeit parts. Blockchain capabilities have emerged as a key possible solution to rectify this problem. By simplifying the methods to identify and remove the counterfeit parts, blockchain brings transparency into the system and improves overall pricing strategies. In fact, industry experts believe that blockchain will eventually enable fair pricing in the car market.

- 3D printing – 3D printing in the automation industry has come a long way since Urbee was designed. While major credit goes to Local Motors for breaking barriers and launching Strati, other major names in the industry have also developed various 3D car prototypes. Due to the safety benefits and cost-effective nature of 3D printed cars, Technavio expected to see larger investments from big-name manufacturers in this sector.

- Electric cars – by the beginning of 2018, sales of electric cars had reached close to 10 million units, which explains why stalwarts like General Motors, Daimler AG and VW have entered this lucrative market with their own models. The declining reservoir of non-renewable energy sources is a major push for the production of electric vehicles, which is reflected in the increased market share of electric cars in the European market.

- Driverless with autonomous vehicles – Although fully autonomous vehicles will take some more time to make their way onto the roads, autonomous vehicles are definitely worth watching. With benefits such as reduced accidents, better traffic management and increased passenger comfort, the autonomous vehicle is the 'crème de la crème' where future automotive technology is concerned.

## - Big Data's increasing role in the automotive industry – The U.S. Department of Transportation has mandated that all new cars must digitally communicate, starting in 2020. Ford and Toyota have already teamed up to launch open platform infotainment systems that will connect to popular mobile phone operating systems, such as Android and iOS. With these developments, 2018 witnessed a higher number of cars with interconnected features that will allow app developers to adapt their offerings to cross platform users on mobile, tablet and the web. Digitalisation is finding a relatable example in the automotive industry.

## Automotive software and simulation markets

In view of the increasing importance of software in the future of mobility, the automotive software market was projected to grow at a CAGR of 18.12% to reach US$ 60- billion by 2025 from an estimated US$ 18.7- billion in 2018 (according to analyst Markets and Markets).

"The demand for enhanced safety, convenience and comfort features in automobiles is growing steadily. These rapid changes compel manufacturers to alter their designs repetitively. The continuous use of electronic content in powertrain, HEV/EV, chassis, ADAS and infotainment systems are expected to drive the growth of the automotive software market. The passenger vehicle segment, by vehicle type, is estimated to hold the largest market share during the forecast period," commented the analyst.

The communication systems segment of the automotive software market is estimated to be the fastest growing segment, added the analyst. As people across the globe want their vehicles to be safer and advanced, OEMs are manufacturing vehicles with more electronic content. For the proper functioning of electronic content, it must communicate in a seamless manner. For the right communication, software is being used extensively. On the back-end of autonomous driving, AI and HMI, the automotive software market for communication systems is growing.

According to Markets and Markets, Asia Pacific is projected to be the largest market for automotive software from 2018 to 2025. The market growth in this region can be attributed to increasing vehicle production, changing in-vehicle electronics architecture, increasing in-vehicle services connected to the external cloud and the expansion of leading OEMs and automotive component suppliers. Market growth in the region can also be attributed to the increasing sales of connected vehicles and government initiatives regarding the use of environmentally-friendly electric vehicles.

The automotive simulation market is projected to grow at a CAGR of 11.1%, to reach US$ 2.9 billion by 2025 from an estimated US$ 1.4 billion in 2018, said Markets and Markets. The on-premises segment, by deployment, is estimated to hold the largest market share during the forecast period. The services segment of the automotive simulation market is estimated to be the fastest growing segment, by component, from 2018 to 2025.

Asia Pacific is projected to be the largest market for automotive simulation software from 2018 to 2025.

## Metalforming opportunities

The global metalforming market for automotive was valued by Markets and Markets at

US$ 217.69 billion in 2017 and is projected to grow to US$ 269.01 billion by 2025, at a CAGR of 2.83% during the forecast period. The global metalforming market for automotive is booming because rising vehicle production, along with stringent emission norms and fuel economy regulations, are driving demand for lightweight materials in the automotive industry. Metalforming techniques, such as hot forming and hydroforming, are also making metalformed components stronger.

The metalforming market for automotive is expected to grow at a CAGR of 2.83%, by value, during the forecast period. The market was estimated to be US$ 221.22 billion in 2018 and is projected to reach US$ 269.01 billion by 2025, according to Markets and Markets. The driving factors include growing vehicle production and the increasing trend of vehicle lightweighting.

"In 2017, according to OICA, passenger car production in the Asia Oceania region was around 44.96 million units. China, Japan and India had a share of 55.2%, 18.6% and 8.8% of the passenger car production in Asia respectively. Passenger cars are, therefore, estimated to be the largest segment of this market," said the analyst.

"Aluminium is expected to show the fastest growth over the forecast period, by material type. It is replacing steel in many automotive applications such as Body in White (BIW) and chassis. The usage rate (%) of aluminium for forming applications is higher in Europe and North America, when compared to that in Asia Oceania. Consumer spending power and vehicle costs are higher in countries such as the U.S. and Germany, compared to the developing economies. Automakers are increasing the use of expensive lightweight materials, such as aluminium, which helps to reduce the overall vehicle weight, thus improving performance and fuel efficiency. However, it is 35-40% costlier than steel."

By technique, added the analyst, stamping is expected to be the largest market over the forecast period. Stamping is one of the most common forming techniques used in the automotive industry. Many structural components are manufactured through the stamping process. These include closures (doors, bonnet and truck) and components of BIW such as A pillars, B pillars, cross beams and roof liners.

"The cold forming market is estimated to be the largest market in the forming type segment and is expected to maintain its position in the metalforming market for automotive. Cold forming is one of the most conventional manufacturing processes in which components are formed using different types of forming techniques at room temperature and do not require additional handling and carrying. Because of this, the overall cost of the cold forming process is low, compared to the hot forming process. Owing to such cost advantages, cold forming is still the major preference of OEMs across the globe," it said.

Because of large vehicle production in the Asia Oceania region, it is estimated to be the largest metalforming market for automotive over the forecast period. China is the largest contributor, in terms of vehicle production, for the Asia Oceania region. Japan is the second largest vehicle-producing country and contributes approximately 8.4% to global vehicle production.
