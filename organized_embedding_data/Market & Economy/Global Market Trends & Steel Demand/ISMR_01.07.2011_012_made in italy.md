# MADE IN ITALY

[TARİH: 01.07.2011 ISMR]

The Italian machine tool industry is seeing a resurgence in manufacturing with machine tool orders doubling in the first quarter of 2011, a 19.1 per cent increase compared to the same period in the previous year, according to the latest figures from UCIMU-Sistemi per Produrre, the Italian Machine Tools, Robots and Automation Manufacturers’ Association.

While the numbers indicate a rebound, the association says that there is still a gap in growth between the domestic market, which is still weak, and the export market which is spurring the recovery.

The top export market for Italian machine tools is China, representing 14.2 per cent of total exports, followed by Germany (10.5 per cent), US (5.8 per cent), India, (5.6 per cent), France (5.6 per cent), Russia (4.8 per cent) and Brazil (4.7 per cent).

Preliminary statistics indicated that Italian industry output in 2010 was about 4.23 billion euros, compared with growth of 3.3 per cent over the same period in 2009. The data from January to September 2010 show that Italian machine tool exports to China were worth over 270 million euros, surpassing Germany to become the Italian machine tool industry’s largest export market. The United States was ranked third.

It is noteworthy that the Italian machine tool exports/sales to traditional markets such as Germany, the United States and France are declining, but India (up 83.8 per cent), Russia (16.4 per cent), Iran (312.5 per cent), Brazil (47.7 per cent) and other emerging markets show significant growth for Italian machine tool exports.

The association works to support the industry and to promote the growth and spread of an entrepreneurial culture through the supply of services that are constantly updated to meet the requirements of the companies operating in this particular sector. The members of the Association produce around 70 per cent of the output of the industry and UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE provides expert support for all aspects of corporate activities, including the special skills of its subsidiaries.

From 1 January 2011, UCIMU has decreed that environmental sustainability of the production cycle will become a key criteria for Italian manufacturers to receive the UCIMU blue mark of quality in addition to commercial reliability, financial strength, attention to safety/function testing and customer care (monitored through Key Performance Indexes).

## MARKET STRUCTURE

In 2009, Italy was the fifth largest machine tool consuming country in the world, registering 2013.3 million euros worth of purchases. It was fourth in the world rankings for machine tool production and third for exports. However, the Italian machine-tool, robot and automation industry has certain features that distinguish it from the industries of other countries.

“The differences become apparent if we consider the structure of the industry, which is much more fragmented than in other countries,” explained UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE. “Indeed, most of the 500-odd companies in Italy are small or medium sized. In a typical company, management is firmly in the hands of the owner and normally no more than 70 people are employed, compared with the 200 that are the average in Germany and Japan, where, in addition, management has a highly vertical structure.

“Italian companies exploit to the full the limited size of their companies, successfully penetrating the markets worldwide with their flexibility and quick response. It is no coincidence that one of the strengths of the Italian machine tool industry lies in supplying custom-made machines and systems - in other words, machines and systems produced to meet the specific requirements of individual users. As to the quality of products, it is significant that almost 70 per cent of the machine tools produced in Italy are electronically controlled, and 20 per cent of these form part of integrated systems (cells and systems made up of more than three machines).

“Italian companies are also ‘special’ because of the marketing policy they have adopted, based on the conviction that technology has to be shared. Consequently, they present themselves on world markets with an approach marked by readiness for dialogue and co-operation,” concluded the association.

## GLOBAL SHOWCASES

The BIMEC biennual exhibition for Mechatronics and Automation will be held at the Rho-Pero exhibition centre in Milan, Italy from 16-19 November 2011. Focusing on assembly, handling, moving, robotics and industrial vision, BIMEC is organized by EFIM-ENTE FIERE ITALIANE MACCHINE. Technologies on show will include lines and machines for assembly, industrial robots, vision systems, measure and control, software and hardware for system and product development (PLM), logistics, engineering, safety and subcontracting.

Promoted by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, BIMEC enjoys the patronage of AIDA, the Italian Assembly Association, IMVG, Italian Machine Vision Group, CIS, Comitato Interassociativo Subfornitura and SIRI, the Italian Robotics and Automation Association.

The biannual BIMU/SFORTEC exhibition was held in Milan, Italy last year from 5-9 October 2010. With 1225 participating companies and around 60,000 visitors, it included the World of Welding, the World of Assembly, the World of Surface Finishing and the World of Dies and Moulds. The machine tool show was flanked, as usual, by SFORTEC, the technical subcontracting, components and structural processing exhibition promoted by CIS-COMITATO INTERASSOCIATIVO SUBFORNITURA. The next BIMU/SFORTEC event will be held from 2-6 October 2012.

Italian visitors at the BIMU trade fair have included automotive giants such as Alfa Romeo, Ducati, Ferrari, Lamborghini, Maserati, Moto Guzzi, Piaggio, Pininfarina and Pirelli. Household appliance giants have included Gruppo Merloni, Miele and Zanussi. Aerospace sector visitors have included AgustaWestland, Alenia, Avio Group and Piaggio Aero Industries.

The Lamiera sheet metal exhibition in Bologna, Italy closed in May 2010 with over 18,000 visits registered. 441 exhibitors, 30 per cent of whom were from outside Italy, took up 37,000 square meters of space. It returns to Bologna from 9-12 May 2012. ■
