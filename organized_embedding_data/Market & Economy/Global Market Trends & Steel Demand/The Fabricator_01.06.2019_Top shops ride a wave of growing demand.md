# Top shops ride a wave of growing demand

[TARİH: 01.06.2019 The Fabricator]

Demand outpaces headwinds of tariffs and material prices

By Tim Heston

Delivering the FAB 40

The FAB 40 list is prepared with the help of metal fabricators willing to share their revenue numbers and company information with

The FABRICATOR

and its readership. Emails were sent to about 1,200 subscribers within the fabricated metal products sector. Companies were asked to provide their 2018 revenue, 2019 projected revenue, as well as their number of locations and employees. Those companies that choose to submit information are then ranked according to their 2018 reported annual revenue.

This list is created as a reference for the metal fabricating market. It is not intended to act as an official benchmark for the industry because participation is limited and independent verification of reported revenue from private companies is not possible.

lance at some of the top custom fabricators on the 2019 FAB 40, and you’ll see some eye-popping revenue numbers. Many companies shot past their sales forecasts, and most projected their revenues to continue to climb this year. Revenue boosts from acquisitions aside, most don’t expect 2019’s organic growth to be as dramatic as last year’s. Many have been seeing material prices stabilize and fall a bit, which points to waning demand. All the same, few expect the growth to subside in a major way in the near future. Though economic conditions may have downshifted globally, the demand for U.S. metal fabricated products continues in high gear.

Overall, managers of this year’s FAB 40 described a year that required some complicated strategizing. Many are growing thanks to strong domestic demand from various industries, but most continue to face challenges. A few top fabricators have more than 50 or even 100 open positions that aren’t easy to fill in an era of record-low unemployment.

Materials purchasing has required some serious navigation, too, considering the metal pricing roller coaster of the past year. And high material prices have certainly contributed to rising revenue numbers. For some entries you’ll notice that relatively few employees produce a large amount of revenue. But that always happens in shops that process a lot of material, particularly those that handle a fair amount of structural and heavy industrial work. The material pricing trends have just made the effect a bit more significant.

(Editor’s note: As noted, material prices and the volume of material a shop processes tend to skew the revenue-per-employee metric. A more telling metric is the value added per payroll dollar, which removes material from the equation. While the FAB 40 does not track this metric, another study from the Fabricators & Manufacturers Association does: the annual "Financial Ratios & Operational Benchmarking Survey," available through the FMA store at

That said, some shop managers said their revenue numbers would have been even higher had it not been for some smart material purchasing strategies that allowed them to offer their customers a better price. That’s a case where lower revenue is actually better for metal fabricators and their customers alike.

Just five years ago, revenue numbers for the bottom half of the FAB 40 (starting at No. 20) topped out at just $24 million in sales. This year, with many of the same companies participating, that figure is $45 million.

The FAB 40 gives a peek into a custom metal fabrication industry dominated by private companies, many of them family businesses. A few are public. MEC, No. 1 on the FAB 40 for many years running, just went public on the NYSE on May 9. And BTD, No. 3 on the list, is a subsidiary of Otter Tail Corp., which is listed on the NASDAQ exchange.

But those are the exceptions. Metal fabrication is largely a private company arena, which makes the FAB 40 a valuable resource. It’s a telling statistical slice. Just five years ago, revenue numbers for the bottom half of the FAB 40 (starting at No. 20) topped out at just $24 million in sales. This year, with many of the same companies participating, that figure is $45 million. Yes, a little of the boost comes from higher material prices, but the majority of that boost has come from growing opportunities.

Again, though, many shop managers in the FAB 40 don’t expect a repeat of 2018’s spectacular growth. Some said they expect business to stay steady, others expect weaker demand as 2019 ends and 2020 begins. The trade battles with China have had some negative impacts too, especially if a fabricator’s assembly operations source parts from China, often because the component simply isn’t available anywhere else.

Those headwinds aside, the growth numbers and projections speak for themselves. The most dramatic growth has come from Mayville Engineering Co. (MEC), the No. 1 company on the FAB 40 for many years. In 2010 MEC reported revenues of $111 million; today, after its acquisition of Defiance Metal Products, MEC is projecting year-end 2019 revenue to be more than $562 million. Still, even before that major acquisition, MEC’s 2018 revenue was more than $350 million—almost triple what it was five years ago. That fact alone says that, for companies that can deliver in this industry, opportunities abound.
