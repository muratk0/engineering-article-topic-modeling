# The Minimum Wage Debate Is Back Again

[TARİH: 01.07.2014 The Fabricator]

Does the debate over the minimum wage really matter much to the manufacturer? Yes and no. It is important to understand why this issue is so durable despite the fact that so few people are affected by it. There are some 76 million people in the U.S. who are paid by the hour. Of that 76 million, only 3.6 million earn the federal minimum wage or below. That is about 4.5 percent of all wage earners. Of the 3.6 million, 65 percent (or 2.34 million) are between the ages of 16 and 20. This would not seem to be a major issue as far as economic growth is concerned — the bigger problem would seem to be the 20 million people who are out of work. Clearly, the minimum wage battle is politically motivated.

Both parties tend to benefit from this contest as it motivates the voter base for both the Democrats and Republicans. The Democrats can position the issue as striking a blow for the common man, and the Republicans can get their supporters riled up over the impact on small business. What stake does the manufacturer have in this fight given that very few jobs in the sector are anywhere near minimum wage levels?

The most relevant aspect for the manufacturer is that focus on the minimum wage distracts from the real issue of the skills gap. The people that are trying to make a living and raise families working at a fast-food joint don’t progress very far with an additional few dollars an hour; they need to enter the workforce through a career that promises growth and development. The job shortage in manufacturing should not exist given the rate of joblessness, but it does because insufficient attention is paid to training. The way to take people out of the minimum wage trap is to take them into new careers and vocations, but that process is far more challenging than just mandating a higher hourly wage.

The business community in general opposes these arbitrary wage hikes at the bottom end of the scale as they tend to drive up wages across the board without considering the cost structure of the business. Seems simple on the surface, but the issue has more twists and turns than your favorite summer roller coaster.
