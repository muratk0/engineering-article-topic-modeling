# What to Expect from the U.S. Dollar

[TARİH: 01.02.2015 The Fabricator]

The renaissance in U.S. manufacturing has been volatile to say the least. While some solid evidence of nearshoring and onshoring is apparent, operations still set up overseas to take advantage of lower costs. The one thing the U.S. manufacturer has been able to count on for the last few years has been a competitive dollar position. Previously the dollar gained so much strength against other currencies that it was almost impossible to sell into foreign markets. There are signs that this unfortunate situation may be returning.

The Swiss central bank shocked Europe and the global financial markets a few weeks ago by suddenly cutting loose from the euro. The franc soared, and the ability of Switzerland to export was put in jeopardy. Many wondered what they were thinking, and this became obvious in the days that followed. They were counting on the dollar to gain strength and therefore attract those that were seeking a safe haven from the collapsing euro. That has been the case, and the dollar has been getting stronger and stronger with every passing week. If the Federal Reserve follows through with plans to raise interest rates, the dollar will surge even more.

The dollar has now been trading against the euro at levels not seen in over a decade, and many expect recovery in Europe to take years. The U.S. is losing its export edge very rapidly, which will create issues all year. It is going to be very hard to sell into Europe and Japan, which is not new, but these nations will have an easier time selling against a dollar that has gained momentum. Suddenly the U.S. will struggle to sell to Latin America, Asia, Africa, and everywhere else.

The Federal Reserve has not mentioned the dollar much, but it may have to start considering the impact of its planned interest rate policy. If the dollar is already surging, the Fed may be reluctant to throw gasoline on the fire with an interest rate hike that will make the U.S. that much more attractive to investors but less attractive to the buyers of her products.
