Every time there is any sort of economic shift, there will be legions of commentators that seek to dazzle the world with their dire predictions and extreme interpretations. These days we are getting the inflation hysterics. "Hyperinflation is right around the corner". "Stagflation is due by next Tuesday". The reality is far more prosaic.

The definition of hyperinflation is a rise of over 50% in prices month after month. We are currently looking at 5.0% inflation. Hyperinflation requires a neartotal collapse of an entire economy. Given that we are growing at nearly a 5.0% rate for the year, the U.S. is not in that position. Equally idiotic is the assertion we are facing stagflation. This is when there are both high levels of inflation and high levels of unemployment. Usually, one development makes the other unlikely. Inflation means a hot economy and that generally means high rates of employment. The current rate is 4.7% and that is as low as was prior to the lockdown. The fact is that 6.0% is the normal rate of joblessness. There has been no stagnation in the economy — just the opposite. There is growth that is nearly twice the average of the last twenty years combined with very low rates of unemployment and businesses desperate to hire. If anything, we are in the grip of hypergrowth that has overwhelmed the supply chain.

There is not a shred of evidence to support the assertion and these statements do nothing but panic people. The U.S. is facing good old-fashioned inflation that stems from excess demand, ample supplies of money, and a supply chain that was caught unprepared for the pace of growth coming off an unprecedented period of economic shutdown. Remember, for a while, we were sporting 9.5% growth in Q2 of this year. Much of the inflation we see right now is really transitory — as the Fed has been asserting. There will be lingering inflation from wages and even money supply but nothing approaching the dire warnings some are issuing.
