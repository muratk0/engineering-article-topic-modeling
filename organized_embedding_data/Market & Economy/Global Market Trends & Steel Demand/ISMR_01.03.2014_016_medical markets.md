# MEDICAL MARKETS

[TARİH: 01.03.2014 ISMR]

“The medical market calls for highly complex, value-added parts manufactured to very high spec. and quality levels”

The global medical device, technology and equipment market is forecast to be worth over US$ 440.5 billion by 2018.

Medical technology is a collective noun for medical devices in vitro diagnostics, medical imaging equipment and e-health solutions. Billions of patients worldwide depend on medical technology. Ageing societies, higher disposable income, better medical care and rising pressure on costs in the health care sector are the salient features of the business environment for medical technology companies. The market calls for highly complex, value-added parts manufactured to very high spec. and quality levels from sheet metal manufacturers.

The market for medical technology products remains an attractive one. It will continue to grow, as will the challenges, says the VDW (the German Machine Tool Builders’ Association). Medical technology is characterised by a constant flow of innovation, the result of a high level of research and development within the industry, and of close cooperation with users. Products typically have a lifecycle of only 18-24 months before an improved product becomes available.

Medical product requirements include extreme precision and perfect surface finishes along with high process reliability and traceability of all manufacturing steps. Surface protection is a key issue for metals and alloys used in the medical sector.

## Metal meets medical

“In medical technology, production operations are run with very different batch sizes,” explained Simon Voit, Product Manager Medical Technology, Marketing, Control Systems for Machine Tools at Dr. Johannes Heidenhain GmbH in Traunreut, Germany, identifying one of the tasks that daily confront manufacturers of medical technology.

“Whereas, in dentistry, the production of one-offs is the everyday norm, for surgical instruments the sheer diversity of variants entails small batches. Hip and knee joints, in contrast, are manufactured in a cost-efficient process-oriented series production operation. Irrespective of the batch size involved, the regulatory requirements for NC programs are very stringent.”

Heidenhain was one of more than 20 participants in the “Metal Meets Medical” special show at METAV 2014 – the international trade fair for manufacturing technology and automation held in Düsseldorf from 11-15 March 2014. Under the slogan “Smart Solutions – More Efficiency”, it demonstrated how production technology contributes towards more efficiency and more precision in medical technology manufacturing. Other participants included 3D Laser; AHC Oberflächentechnik; Fuchs; Paul Horn; Renishaw Sandvik; Siemens; SolidCAM; TRUMPF plus the FEP research institutes from Dresden and IFW, IW, IFUM and Laser Centre from Hanover.

“People are looking for more flexible, more efficient production solutions while retaining a consistently high standard of quality”, explained Claus Bieder, Senior Manager Business Development, Siemens in Erlangen, Germany. In this context, efficiency primarily means speed. Faster translation of specific requirements into digital product prototypes, rapid creation of CNC programs, rapid set-up and change-overs for the machines when handling different batch sizes, and not least expeditious machining, are the salient challenges in the production process.

Other keynote topics at the exhibition were the materials used such as chromium-cobalt; stainless steels; ceramics; plastics or magnesium (some of which are less than easy to machine). Other topics included stringent hygiene guidelines; maximised precision in the µm range and coatings made of metal, plastics or ceramics which have to be wafer-thin and sturdy. METAV showcased solutions for this in Hall 15, covering the entire process chain, from the machine, the tools and the components all the way through to the metrological instrumentation for dental technology, implants, surgical instruments and mould construction.

The global surgical equipment market is divided into three categories: electrosurgical devices, hand instruments and surgical suture. The surgical equipment industry is made up of mechanical devices (such as lasers; electro-surgery devices; disposables; heavier cutting tools; generators and accessories), handheld instruments (blades; scalpels; clamps and scissors) and wound closure products such as sutures (non-absorbable and absorbable).

## Market forecasts

China’s orthopaedic instrument industry has seen rapid development over the years, with total market size rising from 3.28 billion yuan in 2006 to 9.85 billion yuan in 2012. Trauma products, spine products and joint products constitute the three major market segments of the Chinese orthopaedic instrument industry,” explained China Market Research Reports.

“In 2012, the three principal products accounted for an aggregate market share of about 82.2%, of which trauma products showed the highest (approximately 35.5% with market size up 18.24% YoY). Spine and joint products ranked second (up 12.6% YoY) and third (up 12.4% YoY) respectively.”

The global medical device, technology and equipment market is forecast to be worth over US$ 440.5 billion by 2018. The medical device contract manufacturing market is expected to total US$ 49.45 billion by the end of 2014. The outsourcing of medical device manufacturing is forecast to increase over the next few years as companies seek to target expanding markets in Asia Pacific and Central and South America.

From 2014 to 2024, the medical device contract manufacturing market is forecast to expand rapidly. The leading companies operating in this market include Accellent; Flextronics; Symmetry Medical; The Tech Group (West Pharmaceutical Services); Greatbatch; Nortech Systems; Jabil Circuit; Benchmark Electronics; Vention Medical and Creganna-Tactx Medical.

“The medical device industry is relatively highly consolidated compared to the medical device contract manufacturing market, where the majority of companies are small. As a result, for contract manufacturing, the smaller companies are heavily dependent on the larger multinationals revenues. The purchasing power therefore lies with the large medical device manufacturers. As the healthcare industry consolidates over time, competition to provide products and services to prospective clients will become increasingly intense,” explained Companies and Markets.com

According to market research reports, increased healthcare expenditure, improving standards of living and longer life expectancies are the major factors driving the growth of the surgical equipment market. The growing demand of outpatient surgeries, increase in the number of healthcare centres, rise in surgical procedures performed globally, technological innovations and a growing preference for minimally invasive surgeries are also driving the growth of the market.

The METAV 2014 exhibition also focused (for the first time) on additive processes which are becoming progressively more important for industrial mass production operations as well as for manufacturing medical technology products. Not only were additively manufactured medical products showcased (such as implants, OP templates or prosthetics) but also the progress made in deploying 3D layer-formers; reconstruction methods; (bio-materials) and how to machine them. In additive production processes, there is huge potential for faster, more cost-efficient and more sustainable manufacturing in medical technology.

“Additive processes enable components with new functions to be created that could not be produced using conventional metal-cutting methods. Processes of this kind expand the options available for manufacturing components,” added Dr. Matthias Luik, Head of Research and Development at Paul Horn, Tübingen, Germany. ■
