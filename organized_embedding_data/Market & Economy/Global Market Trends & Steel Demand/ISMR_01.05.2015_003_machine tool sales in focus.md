# Machine tool sales in focus

[TARİH: 01.05.2015 ISMR]

THE SPANISH AFM MACHINE tool association has confirmed a slight decline of 3.2 per cent in the overall 2014 turnover of its machine tool and manufacturing technologies sector in terms of global sales.

“Despite the uncertainty in 2014, the sector as a whole has managed to more or less maintain its production level. With the exception of metal forming, which suffered a marked decrease of 19.6 per cent, the other subsectors have produced a positive annual balance,” it confirmed.

The President of AFM, Antxon Lopez Usoz, commented: “We have weathered the storm of a year of great uncertainty where some of the markets, where we had received important orders over the last few years, have been exposed to tension and changes that have caused a destabilising effect. This is the case in Russia and, to a lesser extent, Brazil.

“In the Asiatic continent, we experienced a slowdown that affected China and India. The German market, too, has been affected by the Russian crisis. However, other markets have livened up our sales. The US market is once again growing, with a 40 per cent increase impact on our exports. In Europe, most noteworthy is the recovery of the United Kingdom, Portugal, Italy and France, despite the fact that the macro data of these latter two markets are not so favourable.”

“Analysing machine export data up to December, it can be observed that China once again heads foreign sales in 2014 (19.9 per cent), followed by Germany (11.2 per cent) and the US (7 per cent), and by France (5.5 per cent) and Portugal (5.3 per cent). The next five countries are United Kingdom (5.2 per cent), Mexico (5.2 per cent), Brazil (4.1 per cent), Italy (4 per cent) and India (3.7 per cent). Orders in 2014 grew by 2.1 per cent with a good final quarter of the year, marking a stable start to 2015 insofar as activity is concerned,” added the AFM.

Spanish consumption of machine tools showed growth in machines of 39 per cent which, although not at the required pace, offers scope for improvement.

“The national market is finally starting to look hopeful. Moreover, we trust that the necessary re-equipment of the Spanish industry will take place. The support plans that the Basque Government has put into motion have been and are a good incentive for companies to consider investing in advanced manufacturing technologies to improve their production capacity. We hope that this will be adopted at a national level in 2015,” said Lopez Usoz.

The AFM President predicts that “despite not knowing the consequences that the recent financial injection, the evolution of the Euro with respect to the Dollar, or the oil price trend may bring about, 2015 will be a year of stability on demand, where we will be able to grow, although only slightly, over the 2014 production figure.”

He also pointed out the increasing popularity of the Industry 4.0 concept and is seeking active collaboration with those who can provide knowledge and experience in information and communication technologies and their application in the industrial world such as the GAIA Cluster of ICTs.

Antxon Lopez Usoz is also President of ADDIMAT, the recently created Spanish additive manufacturing association which now has 64 companies as members. ADDIMAT has encouraged, with the BEC (Bilbao Exhibition Centre), the creation of a special exhibition area dedicated to additive manufacturing at the Subcontracting Fair of Bilbao (26-29 May 2015) with a conference on additive manufacturing on 27 May 2015. The new association will hold its first General Meeting at BEC on 28 May 2015.

Additive manufacturing will also be a “nuclear” issue at the twentieth Congress of Machine Tools that the sector will hold in San Sebastian from 10 to 12 June 2015, with North American guest speaker, Terry Wohlers, an expert in additive manufacturing and 3D.

## Growth in UK for TRUMPF Group

The laser manufacturer SPI Lasers, a member of the TRUMPF Group, acquired JK Lasers with effect from 15 April 2015. With almost 100 employees and annual sales of 17.6 million euros, JK Lasers is one of the world’s leading manufacturers of high power fibre lasers for industrial use.

JK Lasers, until now a member of the GSI Group, has more than 40 years of experience in laser development. The company’s core competence is in fibre lasers with outputs from 100 watts to 4 kilowatts, as well as combiners for joining laser sources and other beam guidance components. The most important target groups of JK Lasers include machine manufacturers for laser cutting applications and for additive manufacturing.

JK Lasers is based in Rugby, UK, a few hours’ drive away from the headquarters of SPI Lasers in Southampton. Rugby will act as an additional production and development centre for fibre lasers and optical components.
