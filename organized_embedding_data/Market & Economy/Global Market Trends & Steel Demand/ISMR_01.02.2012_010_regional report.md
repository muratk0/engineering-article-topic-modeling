## Germany In Focus

The German economy has grown strongly over 2011. ISMR examines sectors that show particular promise for sheet metal manufacturers.

ISMR SAYS: “Despite the Eurozone crisis, prospects look strong in the German market.”

In 2009, Germany experienced a serious post-war recession, when GDP suffered a historic decline of 5.1%. 2010, however, was characterised by rapid economic recovery (+3.7%). The German economy grew strongly in 2011. The price-adjusted gross domestic product (GDP) increased by 3.0% compared with the previous year, according to calculations by the German Federal Statistical Office (Destatis). In the course of 2011, price-adjusted GDP again exceeded its pre-crisis level. The economic recovery occurred mainly in the first half of 2011.

All this bodes well for sheet metal manufacturers, despite the current crisis in the Eurozone.

## Automotive market

The automotive industry is still the driving force in sheet metal working technology and prospects in this sector continue to look bright, in spite of current concerns over the Eurozone. This sector is becoming increasingly efficient, with sheet metal working playing a vital role in this context. High-tensile thin steel sheets, formed via press hardening, now constitute lightweight design on the mass market and a new trend is increasingly coming to the fore - materials combining sheet metal and plastic.

According to VDA, the German Association of the Automotive Industry (VDA), the German passenger car market in 2011 amounted to 244,500 newly registered vehicles in December – a rise of 6 per cent over the previous year. In 2011 a total of slightly more than 3.17 million new passenger cars were registered in Germany, which was a year-on-year rise of 9 per cent. In December 2011, domestic incoming orders also showed another increase of 4 per cent.

Matthias Wissmann, President of the VDA, stressed, “The German passenger car market in 2011 proved to be more stable in a difficult situation than many had supposed. Despite the ongoing discussion surrounding the debt crisis in Europe, the model campaign by our manufacturers with their fuel-efficient new passenger cars has succeeded in winning over the customers. Our market share has climbed to over 70 per cent, which indicates good prospects for 2012.”

Throughout 2011, German manufacturers exported more than 4.5 million passenger cars (+7 per cent). The dramatic slump seen in 2009 has been compensated for within two years and a new record set. Wissmann underlined, “Exports are still the mainstay of vehicle production in Germany. We have increased our market share in almost all the important markets – which has also consolidated production at home.”

Incoming overseas orders showed a year-on-year rise of 10 per cent, continuing the positive trend in December. Passenger car production in Germany reached nearly 5.9 million in 2011 as a whole, also setting a new record.

## Machine tools in Germany

The German machine tool industry is once again sailing into calmer waters, says the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders Association). In the third quarter of 2011, order bookings rose by 29 per cent. Domestic orders rose by 33 per cent, export bookings by 27 per cent. Overall, demand was up by 74 per cent during the first nine months of 2011. Domestic customers’ demand was up by 71 per cent on a like-for-like basis, while the corresponding figure for customers outside Germany was 76 per cent.

“The increase in order bookings for the ongoing year continues to be excellent,” said Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director at the VDW. Nonetheless, he adds, the growth dynamic is slowly decelerating on a quarterly comparison. Following months of two-to-three figure growth rates, he continues, a normalisation trend had been foreseeable.

The official production result for Germany’s machine tool industry, according to provisional figures, rose by 36 per cent in the first nine months of 2011. “Over the year as a whole, we look like doing better than hitherto expected,” explains Schäfer. The VDW was predicting production output to grow by one-third for 2011.

## Eurozone debt

However, in view of continuing uncertainties on the financial markets, and not least the euro’s debt crisis, companies expectations for the months ahead are gloomier across the board.

Schäfer expects the effects on turnover developments in various companies to depend very closely on the structure of their product portfolio and their customer base. For vendors of customised machines, in particular, and those operating in project business with long lead times, the high order backlog, together with the continuing need for capital investment in the automotive industry plus strong demand from energy utilities and aircraft manufacturers, all combine to produce a stabilising effect, he says.

The global economy is what determines the continued development of the German machine tool industry. However, the global economy will continue to grow despite slowed rates. Investment budgets for the machine tool industry’s major client sectors remain high internationally. Particularly large clients such as the automobile and aircraft industries are planning numerous strategic investments in new markets, new technologies and new models. The machine tool industry will benefit. The VDW therefore expects further growth in German machine tool production in 2012.

Passenger car production in Germany reached nearly 5.9 million in 2011 as a whole.

## EuroBLECH in focus

The sheet metal working industry is a highperformance industry sector; with companies ensuring their technological advance through expertise and continued innovation. EuroBLECH 2012 is the leading international exhibition for sheet metal working – it will take place in Hanover, Germany on 23 - 27 October 2012. EuroBLECH 2012, the 22nd International Technology Exhibition for Sheet Metal Working, will occupy eight halls at the Hanover Exhibition Grounds: halls 11,12,13,14,15,16,17 and 27.

It predominantly targets global sheet metal working specialists at all management levels in small and medium-sized companies as well as large enterprises: i.e. design and production engineers, quality managers, buyers, manufacturers, technical directors and experts from research and associations. At EuroBLECH 2010, 1,455 exhibitors from 43 countries put a wide range of innovative solutions, cutting edge technology and an enormous amount of live machine demonstration on display. In total, 61,500 trade visitors from 98 countries came to visit the world’s leading event for the sheet metal working industry.

EuroBLECH 2010 did set some important records: the show was more international than ever. Forty six per cent of the exhibitors and 38% of the visitors came from outside Germany. The event welcomed considerably more visitors from the USA, India, Israel, Australia, China, Brazil, Canada, Japan and the United Arabic Emirates. However, top visitor countries were once again European countries: Germany, Austria, the Netherlands, Switzerland, Belgium and Italy. Almost every second visitor came to the show with a definite intention of buying.

EuroBLECH 2012 is planning to launch a special exhibition area focusing on “Protection against Product Piracy, Trademark and Patent Law”. For the Centre of Excellence on Hot Sheet Metal Forming Technology CHS2, established between the University of Kassel, Germany, and Lulea University of Technology, Sweden, it has already become a tradition to organise its hot stamping seminar during EuroBLECH in Hanover. In contrast to the CHS2 Conference Series, which addresses hot sheet metal forming technology from a more scientific point of view, this seminar series is dedicated to industrial application of press hardening and/or hot stamping.

See www.euroblech.com for further details.
