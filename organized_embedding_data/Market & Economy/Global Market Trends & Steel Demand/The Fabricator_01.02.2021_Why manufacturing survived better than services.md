To be honest this is something of an overstatement. It would be more accurate to state that some manufacturers weathered 2020 better than others and better than many service sector businesses. By this time we all know the gist of the story. The recession was a lockdown inspired crisis and the target of the lockdown was the service sector as the goal was to isolate and quarantine the population. One of the unexpected developments was a surge in consumer demand for goods. Prior to the imposition of the lockdown the consumer spent roughly 65% of their disposable income on services and suddenly these were not available so spending shifted to things. RV sales have never been higher, car sales jumped, demand for appliances and lawn equipment surged. The consumer has to consume, and when denied their trip to the coffee shop, bar, concert, or sporting event, they have to find another outlet.

The manufacturer that was in that stream watched their sales perk up. It might have been those that actually make these products but there were also gains made by the companies that manufacture steel shelves for Amazon distribution centers. Those manufacturers that oriented towards sectors such as aerospace or the oil and gas industry did not fare as well. The question now is how long this consumer pattern lasts. Will there be a vaccine-led recovery that allows an end or at least a reduction of the lockdown protocols?

THE CONSUMER HAS TO CONSUME, AND WHEN DENIED THEIR TRIP TO THE COFFEE SHOP, BAR, CONCERT, OR SPORTING EVENT, THEY HAVE TO FIND ANOTHER WAY.

There has been talk of the "Great Divergence" and this refers to a resumption of pre-pandemic patterns. This will have significant implications for the manufacturing sector. The general idea is that when the lockdowns end there will be a significant level of pent-up demand for the services that have been denied to the consumer over the last year. People will be able to attend events, eat at restaurants, hang out at bars, and generally resume their old service-centered habits. They will suddenly stop buying things. Demand for manufactured goods would slip. That would affect carmakers, appliance makers, and the like. On the positive side, there would be more demand for aircraft and the oil sector would see a boost as people started to drive more. The manufacturer will have to deal with 2021 with trepidation and watch patterns closely to avoid too much inventory or too little.
