# Who is Controlling the Dollar?

[TARİH: 01.03.2015 The Fabricator]

The dollar is gaining, or at least this is what it looks like. In truth, it is almost an optical illusion, akin to thinking that you are moving forward because you see someone in your rearview mirror losing ground. The dollar has not so much gained strength as the other currencies are getting weaker. This is more than just a different way to state the problem. If the dollar was gaining because of something the U.S. was controlling, the U.S. could do something about it. But the U.S. has almost no control over what is going on.

The euro is staggering as the region tries to contemplate life after the "Grexit" (Greece’s exit from the eurozone); Japan is mired in an endless loop of Abenomics (fiscal measures introduced by Japanese Prime Minister Shinzo Abe); and China is looking at the slowest rate of growth seen in over 15 years. The central banks of the world are slashing their interest rates to record lows so that their economies can get out of their stall. Only the U.S. is contemplating a rate hike — even though there is no inflation problem to whip.

The manufacturer was nearly driven out of the U.S. in the 1980s by a ridiculously strong dollar. It was next to impossible to sell overseas, and everything hitting U.S. shores was cheap. These were good times for the consumer and the American traveling abroad, but a disaster for those trying to sell to other nations. Today the dollar is nowhere close to those record highs, but it is gaining despite the fact the U.S. has done nothing to bolster its value.

That is not going to be the case for much longer. If the Federal Reserve goes through with its strategy of hiking interest rates by the middle of the year, the dollar is very likely to take off like a rocket as the world’s investors will start to stampede into the U.S. currency as a safe haven and as a place to make some returns. This will have a profound impact on exports and imports alike. It will once again be tough to sell to other nations at the same time that consumers in the U.S. will be snatching up the cheap imports that will once again flood this country.
