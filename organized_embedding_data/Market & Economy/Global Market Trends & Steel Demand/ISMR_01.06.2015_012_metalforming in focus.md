# Metalforming in focus

[TARİH: 01.06.2015 ISMR]

ACCORDING TO the May 2015 Precision Metalforming Association (PMA) Business Conditions Report, metalforming companies expect softening business conditions during the next three months. Prepared monthly, the report is an economic indicator for manufacturing, sampling 126 metalforming companies in the United States and Canada. PMA is the full-service trade association representing the US$ 113-billion metalforming industry of North America.

The May report shows that 24 per cent of participants predict that economic activity will improve during the next three months (down from 33 per cent in April), 60 per cent expect that activity will remain unchanged (compared to 58 per cent in the previous month) and 16 per cent believe that economic activity will decline (up from 9 per cent in April).

Metalforming companies also forecast a decline in incoming orders during the next three months, with 35 per cent predicting an increase in orders (down from 41 per cent in April), 50 per cent anticipating no change (compared to 47 per cent in April) and 15 per cent expecting a decrease in orders.

Current average daily shipping levels also dipped in May. Thirty-six percent of participants report that shipping levels are above levels of three months ago (compared to 46 per cent in April), 43 per cent report that levels are the same as three months ago and 21 per cent report a decrease in shipping levels (up from 11 per cent in April).

The percentage of metalforming companies with a portion of their workforce on short time or layoff increased to 9 per cent in May, up from 7 per cent in April. The May 2015 figure marks a slight increase from this time last year, when 7 per cent of companies reported workers on short time or layoff.

“PMA’s Business Conditions report for May reflects a typical seasonal adjustment in orders and shipments that generally occurs as we approach the end of Q-2,” said William E. Gaskin, PMA President. “PMA’s confidential Monthly Orders & Shipments Report of a sample of PMA members detailed that actual Q-1 orders and shipments grew by 3 per cent and 2 per cent respectively, versus 2014, year-over-year. This reflects the slow growth pattern evidenced in GDP and Federal Reserve projections. The automotive sector continues to lead a general economic recovery in manufacturing in the United States.”
