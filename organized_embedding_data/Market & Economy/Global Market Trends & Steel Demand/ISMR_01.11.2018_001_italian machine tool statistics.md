# Italian machine tool statistics

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

THE INDEX OF Italian machine tool orders registered stability in the third quarter of 2018, as processed by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian machinetool, robots, automation systems and ancillary products manufacturers' association). The index grew by 0.2% compared to the same period in the previous year, to a value of 109.2 (base of 2010 = 100).

"Italian machine tool orders from overseas registered a positive trend: they increased by 6.8% versus the period July-September 2017, with the absolute value of the index reaching 122.4. However, a decrease in orders was noted from the domestic market, where the index reduced by 15.3% compared to the third quarter of 2017, with an absolute value of 56," commented the association.

"The downturn of orders in the domestic market," stated Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, "shows a certain slowdown of investment in new technologies by Italian users, but this result should not mislead us. The outcome registered in the period July-September 2018 is being compared to a record-breaking quarter; it is difficult to do better than we did last year..."

"What we can say," he continued, "is that orders from customers in Italy are still satisfactory at present. This comes from results obtained at 31.BI-MU/SFORTEC INDUSTRY, the machine tool sector's exhibition that closed in Milan several weeks ago and was a great success in terms of visitors and contacts. Many of our customers waited for BI-MU to define their investment plans determining, at least partially, a resizing in order collection for the quarter in question."

Carboniero added that a key worry for Italian manufacturers and, on closer view, the entire Italian manufacturing industry, is the uncertainty around the incentive provisions included in the Italian Budget Plan 2019 which, he said, 'is likely to lack important measures to support growth, especially for the SMEs that make up the framework of our industrial system'.

## Depreciation and tax credits

The results of the survey, conducted by Fondazione UCIMU-Eumetra and presented last June, show that more than 50% of metalworking and mechanical engineering enterprises did not make any investments over the last year and a half. It was mostly the smallest businesses that did not take part in the renewal process or even in the digital upgrade of machinery and factories.

"If SMEs are at the centre of the economic agenda, as the Italian Government authorities affirm, then the Italian Budget Plan has to include an update of depreciation coefficients for machinery as well as confirmation of the 50% tax credit on the incremental value of investments made by businesses in R&D plus Professional Education and Training 4.0," said Carboniero. "We welcome the proposal to include a system of Hyper-Depreciation coefficients in classes, in favour of SMEs: a higher coefficient for small investments, decreasing as the value of new purchases increases. A positive element is also confirmation on the re-financing of the New Sabatini Law. Even if amended, compared with its first version, since 1965 it has allowed SMEs, which often have low capitalisation, to pay for their investments in new technology, thanks to the results of their business activity."

However, he said, the replacement of Super-Depreciation with the introduction of a "MINI IRES" (reduced Corporate Income Tax) at 15%, to encourage investments in new manufacturing technologies, is not enough to guarantee continuous machinery upgrades in Italian factories, if this is not accompanied by a review of depreciation coefficients, now dating back to 1988. The world has changed, he added, and so have the needs of manufacturers. Technology obsolescence times are becoming shorter and shorter, he explained, 'so we can no longer consider the coefficients defined 30 years ago as up-to-date...

"With reference to the tax credit for investments in Research & Development, we ask the Italian Government to keep it at 50%, as it is currently, to ensure the maximum push of companies towards innovation, an activity that, more than others, may guarantee competitiveness of product offerings in the sector.

"Finally," he concluded, "we urgently need a system of incentives for Professional Training and Education 4.0. Besides the current 40% tax credit, it should also include a reimbursement of costs for professional trainers, which is usually the heaviest expense for small- and mid-sized businesses. Also, thanks to the Industry 4.0 Plan, we now have better performing and upgraded factories than we had some years ago. However, now we must train our staff to fully take advantage of this technological upgrade." See www.ucimu.it
