## Connected cooking

The growing popularity of induction cooking technology, in the wake of rising fuel prices and the changing lifestyle of consumers with increasing number of nuclear families, has increased the dependence of users on household devices and is expected to significantly drive the home appliances market.

According to Grand View Research, the global household cooking appliance market was valued at US$ 80.74 billion in 2018 and was poised to progress at a CAGR of 6.5% from 2019 to 2025. A growing number of nuclear families, improved standards of living in developing countries and the availability of energy-efficient equipment are some of the crucial factors expected to drive the industry during the forecast period. The increased focus of leading players on innovation as a way of being more competitive has led to the introduction of smart kitchen gadgets, connected cooking facilities and cordless cooking devices. A smart kitchen comprises tech-enabled kitchen equipment designed to provide control, efficiency and convenience.

"Connected cooking, which involves the inter-connection of kitchen appliances through the Internet of Things (IoT), facilitates cooking with minimal intervention by controlling these appliances remotely. For instance, the Bailey Weber Kitchens thermometer uses Bluetooth connectivity to keep a check on the set temperature and alerts when the dish reaches its ideal temperature. June Life Inc.'s intelligent oven can detect the type of dishes inside the oven using its built-in camera, automatically controlling and setting up the temperature and time required for cooking," explained the analyst.

Another determining factor for the market is the price and life span of the product. The lifespan of cooktops depends upon usage and quality, which directly impacts sales. The quality has a bearing on the pricing of such equipment, which varies as per the budget of a buyer. Single students, bachelors or lower-middle-class families often prefer less expensive or economical products that can last up to two or three years. Expensive and premier cooking appliances, on the other hand, are preferred by upper-middle-class and upper-class families.

Apart from leading to technological advancements and innovations, changing consumer preferences and trends have also compelled manufacturers to experiment with product design, allowing them to expand their portfolios and more effectively cater to the demands of their customers. The product expansion strategy allows them to acquire a larger customer base and increase their market share. For instance, Klove, an IoT-enabled stove knob, can be replaced by the knob of a common gas oven or stove. It is connected to the internet and has sensors to monitor what is cooking. Klove's Artificial Intelligence (AI) powered app can parse through recipes and can also use Amazon's Alexa for voice interaction with users.

"The growing perception that contemporary household devices for food preparation are a necessity for preparing different dishes quickly and more efficiently has resulted in the evolution of the cooking devices industry. Moreover, low-to-medium replacement cycle periods for household cooking appliances, ranging from one to five years, helps generate faster demand, which also makes the market highly competitive. In the era of dynamic customer demand, market players are making constant efforts to offer innovative and high-quality household cooking appliances by providing product in different colours, styles and shapes," added the analyst.

"Additionally, growing consumer spending on expensive and connected devices for preparing food in urban areas is expected to provide an impetus to market growth. Manufacturers have started to target the middle-class, young and high-income groups residing in urban areas by introducing innovative, durable and easy-to-use devices. Strong economic growth, especially in countries such as China, India and Brazil is further expected to contribute to market growth in the near future."
