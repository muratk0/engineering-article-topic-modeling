# REGIONAL REPORT

[TARİH: 01.05.2019 ISMR]

ITALY IN FOCUS In 2018, machine tool, robot and automation consumption in Italy grew by 25.9%.

According to the OECD, GDP growth in Italy was projected to be 0.9% in 2019 and 2020.

"Private consumption will moderate, as lower employment growth and rising consumer price inflation temper real household disposable income gains and offset the positive effect of expansionary fiscal policy. Business investment will slow as domestic and external demand growth weakens. With weak domestic demand, the current account surplus will remain around 2.5% of GDP," it said.

"Fiscal policy will turn expansionary in 2019, widening the budget deficit to 2.5% of GDP in 2019 and 2.8% in 2020. Public debt, which has been gradually falling in relation to GDP, will instead stabilise at a high level. Government bond yields have surged by 185 basis points since mid-2018. Policies should ensure that social spending is sustainable, effective and intergenerationally fair."

## Italy's economic outlook

According to ISTAT (the Italian Institute for Statistics), in 2018 Italy's GDP was expected to increase by 1.1 per cent in real terms. For 2019, it estimated GDP to increase by 1.3 per cent in real terms driven by the contribution of domestic demand (1.3 percentage points).

"In 2018, exports will increase by 1.6 per cent and imports will grow by 2.6 per cent; both are expected to accelerate in 2019 (3.2% and 3.5% respectively). Residential household consumption expenditure is expected to grow by 0.9 per cent in 2018, accelerating in 2019 (1.2%). The stabilisation in employment and the wages increase will support households purchasing power. Investment is expected to progressively decelerate both in 2018 (+3.9) and in 2019 (+3.2%)," said ISTAT.

"Labour market conditions will improve over the forecasting period. Employment growth is expected to stabilise at 0.9 per cent in 2018 and in 2019. At the same time, the rate of unemployment will decrease at 10.5 per cent in 2018 and at 10.2 per cent in 2019."

However, in January 2019, The International Monetary Fund revised down its forecast for Italy's economic growth this year, warning that any new tensions over public finances may hurt the nation's output, borrowing costs and banking system.

The fund's new estimate for 2019 matches revised projections that the Bank of Italy published in its quarterly economic bulletin. Deputy Premier Luigi Di Maio, of the populist Five Star Movement, criticized the central bank over the weekend and questioning the timing of its report.

The euro region's third biggest economy is seen growing 0.6 per cent in 2019, down from an October estimate of one per cent, the IMF said in an update to its World Economic Outlook. The gross domestic product is forecast to rise 0.9 per cent in 2020, unchanged from the prior projection, the Washington-based fund said.

The export-reliant country's slowdown is part of a broader weakening in the euro region, with powerhouse Germany's economic prospects also revised down. Italy's projected 2019 growth is the lowest among the nations listed individually in the IMF overview of its latest projections. Italy is also singled out for the risks related to its public debt which, at more than 130 per cent of output, is the euro region's second-biggest ratio after Greece.

The Italian government last year clashed with the European Union over its budget targets that were eventually revised in a last-minute deal.

The tense talks with the EU's executive arm prompted an increase in the nation's borrowing costs, which had a limited impact on the loan rates for families and businesses.

## Record-breaking year for Italian machine tools

According to UCIMU, 2018 was a record-breaking year for the Italian industry for manufacturing machine tools, robots and automation systems, which registered double-digit increases for all economic indicators. 2019, it said, will instead be characterised by general stability.

As shown by the preliminary year's data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in 2018 production grew to 6,900 million euros, highlighting a 13.4% upturn compared to the previous year. This was the fifth consecutive year of growth and, in terms of absolute values, is the new record for the Italian industry sector.

"The outcome was due both to the excellent performance of deliveries by Italian manufacturers in the domestic market, which increased by 21.1% to 3,270 million euros, and to the positive trend of exports, up by 7.2% to 3,630 million euros," explained Massimo Carboniero, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tools, robots and automation systems manufacturers' association.

"Based on the ISTAT data processed by UCIMU, in the first eight months of the year (latest available survey), the main destination countries for Made in Italy products in the sector were the following: Germany 246 million euros (+11.6%); China 237 million euros (+7.1%); United States 223 million euros (+9.5%); Poland 143 million euros (+49.8%) and France 135 million euros (-4.6%). Thanks to these increases, Italy strengthened its role internationally, highlighted not only for the competence expressed by the industries in the sector (measured by production and export data) but also for the dynamism of its demand, supported and stimulated by incentive provisions for competitiveness, such as Super- and Hyper-Depreciation, included in the Industry/Enterprise 4.0 programmes."

In particular, in 2018, machine tool, robot and automation consumption in Italy grew by 25.9% to 5,620 million euros, said Mr Carboniero. Italian users' propensity to invest in new technology, as well as in digital products, led Italian manufacturers to focus on the domestic market, shown by the decrease in the export/production ratio, which changed from 55.6% in 2017 to 52.6% in 2018.

"In 2019, however, the Italian industry sector should reinforce its 2018 performance, enjoying a positive trend with deliveries in overseas markets expected to increase. A clear slowdown is shown in the domestic market, whose growth trend seems to have lost the momentum we were used to seeing over the last few years," explained Massimo Carboniero. "We are satisfied with the 2018 result: the year closed with excellent results, both in Italy and abroad. Moreover, considering sales overall i.e. not only of machines, but also the production of parts, tools and numerical controls (which are not included in the total of Italian machine tools), we can confirm that turnover exceeded nine billion euros.

"For 2019, although we are confident about maintaining the results of 2018, we are noticing a slowdown of investment in new machines by Italian users. Of course, we understand that the growth we were used to cannot continue uninterruptedly with the same pace and intensity. However, it is important to keep in mind that a recent survey conducted by FONDAZIONE UCIMU and EUMETRA shows that only 50% of Italian metalworking and engineering organisations made investments in the period between 2017 and the first part of 2018."

## Sheet metal in Italy

The only event in Italy solely dedicated to the sheet metal field, LAMIERA attracts visitors from all major user sectors. The international biennial exhibition dedicated to the metal forming machine tool industry and innovative technologies relating to the sector has attracted over 450 companies and will be held at Fieramilano Rho, from 15-18 May 2019, in halls 13 and 15. A new hall 18, the Innovation Hall, is also part of LAMIERA this year.

The exhibition area is already 30% larger compared to the space booked by exhibitors in February 2017 (40,000 square metres of total exhibition area). Exhibitors will display products and technology relating to sheet metal forming machinery; bar-; tube-; section-working machines; presses; blanking and punching machines; lasers; robots; automation; moulds and dies; heat treatments; surface treatment and finishing; wires and metal carpentry as well as subcontracting.

"The number of direct exhibitors has already grown by 10% to date. Around 27% of organisations who sent in their applications will take part in LAMIERA for the first time or will come back and exhibit again after several editions of the event," explained UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian machine tools, robotics and automation association) and show organiser CEU-CENTRO ESPOSIZIONI UCIMU.

Start-ups and SMEs will be able to talk about their projects before key European and international steel players at Made in Steel, which will also take place at fieramilano Rho (Milan, Italy) from 14-16 May 2019. It is organised by Siderweb.

"Messaging apps and chatbots to improve the client service experience, Big Data analysis, cybersecurity, real time production control, tests and inspections to guarantee constant quality are only a few examples of the needs of steelmaking today, that can be fulfilled by digital start-ups, crossfertilising the steelmaking industry with 4.0 technologies, that in some cases have been developed in other industries and still are little known in ours," declared Emanuele Morandi, Chief and CEO of Made in Steel. "With our international event, we aim at creating a match between the steel industry request for innovation and environmental sustainability and the new services and technologies that make them possible."
