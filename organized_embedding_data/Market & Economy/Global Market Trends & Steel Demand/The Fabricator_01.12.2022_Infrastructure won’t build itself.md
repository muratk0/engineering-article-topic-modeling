# Infrastructure won’t build itself

[TARİH: 01.12.2022 The Fabricator]

From The Editor

To build a better tomorrow, the world is going to need structural steel fabricating talent

Dan Davis

, Editor-in-Chief

dand@thefabricator.com

W

hether you like it or not, the U.S. is scheduled to get an economic shot in the arm with the investment of federal funds to repair, improve, and modernize this country’s infrastructure.

As part of the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act, signed into law in November 2021, billions of dollars are going to be spent on road, bridges, and rails ($110 billion); airports ($25 billion); ports ($17 billion); water treatment ($55 billion); and broadband networks ($65 billion). In many instances, the investments will cover delayed maintenance and improvements in these areas, but in others, they will deliver amenities that many communities have done without.

But this future won’t be a reality without people—structural steel fabricators—to create it. You can always put up a kiosk in a store to replace a sales clerk, but that kiosk is not going to do much good in the shop or on the construction site.

In early November, President Joe Biden launched the Infrastructure Talent Pipeline Challenge, a push for employers, unions, schools, state and local governments, and trade associations to come together and make commitments to support workforce development. The scope of the effort is commendable, but the focus isn’t placed squarely on developing fabricating and welding talent. As is so often the case, the responsibility for developing that next generation of skilled workers is going to fall to fabricating shops.

Visiting a school on Career Day and arranging for student visits on Manufacturing Day are good things, but that’s not going to move the needle in terms of making a structural steel fab shop a desired employment destination.

That requires company owners and mangers to get out of their comfort zone—the confines of the shop. They need to go out and meet the folks running the vocational training programs at the local community college, high schools, and even middle schools. Visiting a school on Career Day and arranging for student visits on Manufacturing Day are good things, but that’s not going to move the needle in terms of making a structural steel fab shop a desired employment destination. Fabricating company representatives need to get to know the students through multiple visits, guest lecturing, donating material for coupons, and even acting as an advisor to these programs. Only then can meaningful relationships with the administrators, teachers, and students be formed—and that’s where the benefits start to appear.

If these schools know you, they know and trust the opportunities with your company. This doesn’t guarantee a steady supply of new employees, but it does greatly enhance the chance that young people might actually fill out an application and entertain the idea of working for you.

The sales pitch of building a new future for the U.S. is exciting and should appeal to a younger generation that feels undervalued. This is their future, so they should play a part in creating it. Structural steel fabricators just need to show them the way.
