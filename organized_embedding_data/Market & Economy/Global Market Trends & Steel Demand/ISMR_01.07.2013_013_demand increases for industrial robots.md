# Demand increases for INDUSTRIAL ROBOTS

[TARİH: 01.08.2013 ISMR]

“2012 was quite satisfying for the global robot industry and robot sales reached the second highest level ever,” stated Dr. Shinsuke Sakakibara, President of the International Federation for Robotics (IFR), in July 2013. Between 2008 and 2012, he said, robot sales increased by 9% on average per year.

“We are very pleased that, in 2012, we reached the second highest level of robot sales ever recorded in the history of industrial robots. More than 159,000 units were sold, almost the same level as in 2011,” added Dr. Andreas Bauer, Chairman of the IFR Industrial Robot suppliers’ group.

“Demand for industrial robots is increasing due to the accelerating trend towards automation all over the world. We estimate that robot installations will reach a similar level again in 2013. In the last three years, investments in robots have been strongly supported by the automotive industry. In other industries, general industry sales are increasing.”

Around 70% of total robot sales in 2012 went to Japan, China, US, Korea and Germany. In 2012, robot sales to Japan continued to increase slightly to 28,700 units. The electronics industry reduced robotic investments considerably, while the automotive industry continued to increase robot orders by 31%. However, the sales volume of industrial robots is still far below the peak level of 2005 (44,000 units).

In 2012, China was the second largest robot market in the world after Japan. Although robot sales to China only slightly increased in 2012 to about 23,000 units, it is the most rapidly growing market in the world. Between 2005 and 2012, sales of industrial robots have increased there by about 25% on average per year. Thailand is also becoming a more important robot market.

“The trend towards automation is continuing due to the increasing industrialisation of huge growing consumer markets, the necessary modernisation of existing production facilities and the need to be competitive in a global market. Energy efficiency and lightweight construction are the main challenges for the manufacturing industry. The potential use of industrial robots in general industry will substantially grow when robots can collaborate with workers and when robotic integration becomes easier,” concluded the IFR.
