# FOCUS ON ASIA

[TARİH: 01.05.2010 ISMR]

ASEAN or the Association of South East Asian Nations, formerly ASA, has been promoting regional, economic and social growth amongst the non-communist countries in south east Asia since the late 1960s. The association, which includes both Thailand and Vietnam, currently has over ten member states and has emerged as a rapidly growing market for metal formers and fabricators.

According to the Foreign Affairs and International Trade of Canada (FAITC), ASEAN has a combined gross domestic product (GDP) of $1.4 trillion and is one of the fastest growing economic regions. In its 'Global Commerce Strategy Priority Market' report, it states that ASEAN is a 'regional economic force and quickly becoming the free trade hub of Asia.' It also adds that 'ASEAN enjoys several free trade agreements with China and Korea and is negotiating others'.

According to China Knowledge this week, China is favouring Asia and trade with the Association of South East Asian Nations has grown from just US$8 billion in 1991 to US$ 2.1 trillion in 2009. Dave Forest from Pierce Point explains that the ASEAN block is now China's fourth largest trading partner after America, the EU and Japan and ASEAN trade accounts for 10% of China's total import and export value. He goes on to say that, from China's point of view, doing business with the ASEAN nations makes sense as the region is close and within the Chinese sphere of political influence.

David Cowan, senior analyst at Rothman Research, recently confirmed that mining companies are expanding into Asia and the Pacific, due to the increase in demands for raw materials especially from emerging economies.

"The growth of Asian steel industries have precipitated mining companies to expand into the region especially since, without the raw material they supply, their strong growth cannot be sustained. With the steel mills' demands to produce more, industrial metals and mineral companies are more than happy to supply their needs, at the same time sustaining a strong and stable market," he said.

In May 2009, Chris Oliver from Marketwatch reported that Japan's finance and economy Minister, Kaoru Yosano, had called upon Asian nations to shift their focus from exports to promoting domestic consumption. The assumption was that regional economies should try to restructure their economies to better withstand financial shocks.

In its 'Global Economic Prospects and Policy Challenges' report in February this year, the IMF noted that the 'recent global recovery in emerging economies have been driven by strong domestic demand in major emerging economies, notably in Asia.' It adds that 'overall growth in emerging economies in 2010 is projected at 6 per cent on annual average and 6.4 percent on a Q4/Q4 basis.

## MORE MANUFACTURING JOBS

A study undertaken by the Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India (ASSOCHAM) has projected 87.37 million new jobs in India by 2015 with a very significant share of 32 per cent by the manufacturing sector, followed by the trade and construction sector.

The chamber study on 'Emerging Future Jobs' has revealed that the next most important source of new employment is expected to be trade with 24.24 million new jobs followed by construction with a figure of 15.13 million.

Within the manufacturing sector, textile products, food products, beverages, transport equipment, metal products, leather products and machinery are expected to contribute the major part of this increase in employment, points out the report.

According to ASSOCHAM, manufacturing will be the highest employment potential sector because, after agriculture, it accounts for the largest share of employment at 12.5% among different divisions of economic activity. The report suggests that if manufacturing GDP is made to grow at 10 per cent per annum, its employment will grow at over 5 per cent.

The Associated Chambers of Commerce and Industry of India (ASSOCHAM) President, Dr. Swati Piramal, said that the 16.7% figure of the Index of Industrial Production for January 2010 shows that the recovery in India's economy is becoming gradually more pronounced and visible. The ASSOCHAM Chief added that if the current trend continues, industrial production will go up in subsequent months and India would be able to achieve the GDP growth rate of 7.5% in current fiscal and 9% by the end of the Plan period.

## STABILITY PROSPECTS

To further seal the developmental gap, the ASEAN nations aim to set up the ASEAN Economic Community (AEC) by 2015.

The AEC aims to 'find a balance in terms of coherence and support among the ASEAN Member States towards economic integration'. This will support the initiative for ASEAN Integration (IAI) launched in 2000 that was aimed 'at narrowing the development divide, enhancing ASEAN's competitiveness and accelerating economic integration in ASEAN markets,' according to the ASEAN fact sheet.

The IAI was primarily set up to help the younger members of ASEAN, namely Cambodia, Lao PDR, Myanmar and Vietnam and their plans have been devoted largely to soft infrastructure. However, 'development of transport and communication infrastructure networks, completion of the road, rail, air and sea linkages within ASEAN' are now key focus areas.

For some of ASEAN's nations the future is bright. The Iron and Steel Institute in Thailand, the largest country producer and consumer of steel products in South East Asia, has estimated steel demand to grow by around 6.5% to 7% p.a. in the next ten years.

Vietnam's imports of steel have risen over the last few years, exceeding 9 million tonnes in 2009, according to the World Steel Review in March this year, making it about the tenth largest importer of steel. In its report, it stated that 'Asian crude steel production for the five major countries in January was 21.8% up. China's production increased by 18.2% to 48.7 million tonnes, Indian steel production rose by 14.8% to 5.4 million tonnes, while Japanese production jumped by 37% to 8.7 million tonnes. South Korean production was up 32% to 4.5 million tonnes and, in Taiwan, steel production showed a 71% increase to 1.7 million tonnes.'

According to the FAITC's report, ASEAN's growth is due to the 'growing middle class, its regional integration of economies and its proximity to the Chinese and Indian markets.' The report goes on to conclude that 'the region is an increasingly important global value chain player and a preferred production base for many multinational companies exporting to East Asia, North America and Europe, due to the fact it has an important air and sea transportation gateway and a critical access point to China.'

South East Asia is a vital driver of manufacturing growth – a market that offers superb potential for further development.
