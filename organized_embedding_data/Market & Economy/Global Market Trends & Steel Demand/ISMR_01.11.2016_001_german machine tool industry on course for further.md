# German machine tool industry on course for further growth

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

IN THE THIRD QUARTER OF 2016, the German machine tool industry's order bookings rose by 8 per cent, compared to the equivalent period in 2015. Domestic orders were down by 14 per cent, while demand from abroad increased by 22 per cent. In the first nine months of 2016, order bookings were up by 11 per cent compared to the preceding year. Domestic orders showed a slight rise of 2 per cent whilst overseas demand increased by 16 per cent.

“In this year's third quarter, our sector has been able to build on the encouraging business trends of 2016's first half. The good order situation is being crucially boosted by demand from abroad. This applies to the Eurozone and, to an even greater extent, to the non-Eurozone, explained Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders' Association). In the Eurozone, the Southern European markets of Italy, Spain and France are helping to drive the order upturn forward. Outside Europe, orders from the USA have been particularly buoyant, mainly due to the rising level of orders from the nation's automotive industry.

The third quarter exhibited an increase of 7 per cent for metal cutting equipment, and a rise of 10 per cent for forming machinery. While, for both these technologies, orders from outside Germany showed a uniform double-digit rise, metal-cutting machines figures showed minus 23 per cent in domestic orders during the July-to-September period. For the year's first nine months, order bookings displayed a similar picture: an increase of 12 per cent overall, while domestic business shrank by 7 per cent. Export orders, in contrast, were up by 22 per cent.

In terms of forming technology, orders from both inside and outside Germany showed a balanced picture, with export orders (up 11 per cent) almost level pegging with domestic orders (up 10 per cent). In the first nine months of 2016, the overall growth of 9 per cent in forming technology was strongly supported by domestic orders, up by 24 per cent. This massive rise is mainly attributed to large-scale project business involving presses for the German automotive industry. In the same period, export orders showed a modest increase of 1 per cent.

Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director, VDW (German Machine Tool Builders' Association)

“In contrast to the preceding months, developments in the third quarter of 2016 were not dominated by large orders. Rather, we're seeing a balanced picture over the various product categories involved, which are proving very successful on the global market," commented Wilfried Schäfer.

Capacity utilisation in the German machinetool industry had risen from 86.6 mid-year to 90.2 per cent in October 2016.

“This indicator confirms that, despite cyclical fluctuations, the sector is firmly on course for further growth," is the verdict of the VDW Executive Director. See www.vdw.de ■
