# Italian robotic sector growth

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 ISMR]

UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian association of manufacturers of machine tools, robots and automation systems, and SIRI, the Italian association of robotics and automation, presented final 2013 data for the Italian robotic sector at LAMIERA, the exhibition dedicated to metal forming technology machine tools in Bologna this May.

From the analysis of the data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, it appears that, following the recession in 2012, the Italian robotic sector has started to grow again. In 2013, Italian production of robots reached 490 million euros, 5.2 per cent more than in 2012. The result was driven both by positive export results, which grew by 8.3 per cent to 195 million, and by delivery performance in the domestic market, which grew by 3.1 per cent to 295 million. Italian consumption also showed positive signs: +4.9 per cent, for a value of 515 million euros. The export-to-production ratio increased, from 38.6 per cent in 2012 to 39.8 per cent in 2013.

“In terms of units, Italian companies in the sector produced 2,781 robots in 2013. Of these, about half were destined for overseas markets. Domestic consumption, which totalled 4,679 robots, took up 72.8 per cent of imports,” said UCIMU. “As in previous years, in Italy the main areas of application for robots in 2013 were material handling and welding which represented, in terms of units, 68.3 per cent and 17 per cent of total consumption respectively.”

In 2013, Italy produced 1,935 articulated robots for welding and painting applications. Second in terms of production were Cartesian robots (612 units), used in pick and place processes, for handling workpieces. Following the survey carried out by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the total number of robots installed in Italy in 2013 was 94,790. ■

## Leadership team changes

HYPERTHERM, a manufacturer of plasma, laser and waterjet cutting systems, has confirmed two leadership changes. Effective immediately, Aaron Brandt assumes the role of ‘Vice President of Engineering’ while Hidde van Hoeven becomes ‘Business Team Leader for Mechanized Plasma’.

In his new role, Mr Brandt will oversee Hypertherm’s team of plasma, laser and waterjet engineers. Currently, more than 10 per cent – or 150 Hypertherm associates – work in engineering. In his new role, Mr Brandt will place even greater emphasis on research and development and create additional synergy among Hypertherm’s engineering teams.

Aaron Brandt is Hypertherm’s new VP of Engineering

Mr Brandt’s successor is Hidde van Hoeven, a nine-year associate, credited with making significant contributions to Hypertherm’s US and Europe, Middle East and Africa operations. In his role, Mr van Hoeven will lead development of the next generation of high precision plasma products, such as the HyPerformance HPRXD, and will work closely with CNC, THC and CAM software teams on the development of new Built for Business Integrated Cutting Solutions applications. ■

## Accent on quality

FINE TUBES, the UK-based manufacturer and global supplier of precision tubes, has appointed Phil Adderley as technical and quality director. As part of his new role, Mr Adderley will have overall responsibility for Quality Assurance and Compliance, Quality Control, Technical and Laboratory Services.

With 19 years of experience spanning metallurgy, Quality Assurance and Quality Control as well as senior technical management roles, Mr Adderley has a B. Eng in Materials Engineering and an MSc in Engineering with Management (Materials). He joins Fine Tubes from his previous position as technical director for Firth Rixson Forgings.

Recent projects for which Fines Tubes has supplied specialist tubing include the Airbus A380 and A350 series, the Sukhoi Superjet 100, Skylon (the world’s first reusable space plane) the Gemasolar power station and CERN’s Large Hadron Collider. ■

## New Lotus CEO

PROTON Holdings Berhad has announced the appointment of Jean-Marc Gales as the new Chief Executive Officer of Group Lotus plc, effective from 1 May 2014. Lotus is a wholly-owned subsidiary of PROTON and a member of the DRB-HICOM Group.

Gales, a Luxembourgian, holds an MSc in Management from London’s Imperial College, and an MSc in Mechanical Engineering from the University of Karlsruhe in Germany. A known industry player, he has held several significant positions with numerous global automotive manufacturers.

Before his new appointment at Lotus, Gales was Chief Executive Officer of the European Association of Automotive Suppliers (“CLEPA”) since 2012, where he played a key role in turning around the association through various strategic initiatives, including streamlining working processes and increasing corporate members from 82 to 112. Gales also established a lobbying strategy with the European Parliament and European Commission, and helped define the Industry Research & Innovation Strategy and Roadmap for 2025.

Aslam Farikullah, current Chief Operating Officer of Group Lotus plc, remains as Member of the Board of Lotus Cars Limited and will continue to contribute to the growth of the business in the future. ■
