# ITALIAN MACHINE TOOL TRENDS

[TARİH: 01.09.2010 ISMR]

The positive trend in new orders for Italian machine tools continues and, for the second quarter of 2010, showed an increase of 66.4% compared to the same period in the previous year, for an absolute index value of 79.7 (base 2005=100). Although this seems to be an impressive improvement, the absolute index is still at a very low level, equal to the result for the second quarter 2003.

Data processed by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tool, robot, automation and auxiliary technologies manufacturers’ association, indicate that the turnaround seen by Italian manufacturers since the end of 2009 is further confirmed by these results, which highlight a recovery in both domestic and international demand. Despite this, says UCIMU, the order book index is still distinctly below average levels.

“For the international market, the index of orders received by Italian manufacturers highlights growth of 60.5% compared to the same period in the previous year. Consequently, the absolute value of the index is 70.6. On the domestic front, the growth trend seen by manufacturers since the end of 2009 continues. The index of orders for domestic demand in the second quarter of 2010 grew by 75.6% compared to the same period in the previous year, for an absolute index of 89.4,” confirmed UCIMU.

“On a six-monthly basis, the increase in the order book is 34% (for an absolute value of 87.3) compared to the period January-June 2009, thanks to the increase in orders received from domestic (+39.2%) and international (+32.3%) markets.”

“This data,” said Giancarlo Losma, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, “confirms the turnaround. The increase in new foreign orders encourages hope for the near future, in the expectation of a recovery in demand from traditional markets and a significant increase from emerging countries.

“On the other hand,” he continued, “we are still concerned about the domestic market. The decision not to extend the de-taxation of profits reinvested in high tech machinery until the end of 2010 is a short-sighted and hazardous choice. This decision risks a slowdown in the domestic market’s recovery.”
