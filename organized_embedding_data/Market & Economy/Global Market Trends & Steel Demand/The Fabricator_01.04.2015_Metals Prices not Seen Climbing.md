# Metals Prices not Seen Climbing

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 The Fabricator]

Metals News

Icy winter does nothing to help cool a consuming market

By

MetalPrices.com

staff

Steel market prices were continuing unrelenting declines as the end of winter approached.

Collapsed oil prices and tubular goods markets have added to high import volumes and steel service center inventories, driving lower transaction values. Buyers recently have been reluctant to commit to large purchases, pushing steel manufacturers to keep lowering prices and resulting in some predictions that a steel industry turnaround could still be at least a few weeks away.

Domestic U.S. hot-rolled coil prices have begun to break below the $500/ton level—free-on-board Midwestern mills—down $100 or more from where they were at the start of winter, and as much as $200 from the start of autumn last year. Cold-rolled coil averaged $640/ton, down from $745/ton in mid-December and $790/ton in August. Domestic mill prices were approaching or undercutting those of imports in some cases, and stocks of unsold imports were rising as U.S. steelmakers responded to import volumes that reached a 32 percent market share in January, according to the American Iron and Steel Institute.

While falling oil country tubular goods sales and consumption of flat steel were driving much of the price decline, steel consumers in other sectors like automotive production and nonresidential construction were reporting generally strong trends. Talk of possible trade actions and increased consolidation of domestic steel production also were seen by some as putting a possible floor under current pricing trends, although sharply lower raw materials costs—with iron ore and other input prices down almost 50 percent in the past year—were still a factor.

Also contributing to midwinter steel market uncertainty was the potential for a repeat of last winter’s North American cold weather and unprecedented ice coverage impinging on Great Lakes shipping well into the spring. According to the Great Lakes Environmental Research Laboratory, the total ice coverage on the lakes as of March 5 was 80.4 percent compared to 91 percent on the same day in 2014. The opening of Great Lakes shipping was delayed into April last year, and cargo shipments fell by 7 million tons with lost business revenue estimated at over $700 million by the Lakes Carriers Association. Trade usually starts on the lakes around March 1.

Ferrous scrap prices plunged to five-year lows in the first quarter and could continue to decline in the second quarter. Scrap dealers got a stay of execution in March when prices for shredded scrap and other obsolete grades were unchanged. This came on the heels of an $80/ton plunge in these prices in February.

A Look at Nonferrous Metals

On the aluminum front, further announcements from the auto industry projecting the expansion of aluminum used in cars and trucks prompted many major mills to add more capacity or diversify their book of business. However, the mills have not yet seen to fill this capacity. Several key factors, which were viewed as a threat to the Midwest aluminum premium, have become reality.

Falling European and Asian premiums have allowed some traders to move material to the U.S. This influx of material in addition to Chinese shapes has resulted in an easing on what was thought to be a tight primary market. In addition, and possibly the most significant factor in shrinking Midwest premiums, is the availability of scrap in North America. Most major mills are able to book orders out as far as three months.

Market participants at this point have no confidence the Midwest premium can sustain the downward pressure. Most believe the market is headed toward an 18 to 19 cents/lb. bottom, but some are suggesting the "real market" has already hit that level and premiums will continue to erode into the second half of 2015.

Stainless steel scrap prices have been disappointing since the beginning of the year, falling below 55 cents/lb., putting pressure on a price recovery. Nickel prices on the London Metal Exchange dropped below $14,000/metric ton in March, the lowest level since more than a year ago and occurring while LME nickel warehouse stocks are at historic highs. Stainless steel scrap prices and volumes traditionally have been strong in the first quarter, with most mills replenishing inventory due to a strong demand from service centers.

"Demand for stainless scrap in North America remains poor, and it doesn’t look like it will pick up anytime soon," one source told

MetalPrices.com

. Many are expecting prices to continue to fall because of the impact of lower ferrous scrap values, which fell as much as $20/ton in early March; chromium pricing fell another 3 to 5 cents/lb., and molybdenum (for 316 grades) fell nearly $1/lb.

"People are very cautious with inventory, and nobody wants to be stuck with inventory they can’t move quickly … while others are holding onto inventory due to depressed scrap prices," a second source said.

Copper is a wild card, and several dynamics are influencing its prices this year. Most will have short- and long-term implications, determining if copper prices will eventually find a floor. Copper’s price recovery hinges on the strength of the dollar, energy markets, global economic growth, and if the ratio of supply to demand fundamentally balances itself. Investors are less willing to make bets without inclination and having good reason to jump back in. Players will have to wait until midyear to get a better picture, and then there will be as much uncertainty.
