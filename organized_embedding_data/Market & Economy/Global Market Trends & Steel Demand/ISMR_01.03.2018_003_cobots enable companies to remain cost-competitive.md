# Cobots enable companies to remain cost-competitive

[TARİH: 01.03.2018 ISMR]

THE LONG-STANDING industrial practice of outsourcing manufacturing operations to low-cost parts of the world to keep manufacturing costs down has recently taken a turn. A report from Boston Consulting Group has shown that 'the share of executives saying that their companies are actively reshoring production increased by 9% since 2014 and by about 250% since 2012. This suggests that companies that were considering reshoring in the past three years are now taking action.'

"With rapidly rising labour costs in traditional low-cost countries such as China, manufacturers are now finding that the financial advantages of manufacturing overseas are being seriously eroded. When they realize that offshoring also usually involves complex logistics, added shipping times and difficulties in reacting quickly and nimbly to changes in customer requirements, there are clear reasons why many manufacturers should be looking for new ways to reduce manufacturing costs and increase productivity," said Trelleborg Sealing Solutions.

Trelleborg Sealing Solutions – part of Trelleborg Group – spent years researching ways to optimise its production to meet customer demand. It was not interested in moving its production to one of its own factories in lower wage countries because customers were demanding faster and more flexible delivery. Instead, Trelleborg Sealing Solutions found a suitable solution using collaborative robots - cobots - from Universal Robots. The lightweight cobots have been a part of the workforce at Trelleborg Sealing Solutions since 2012.

"We use our CNC machines much better with cobots, and our product has never been more uniform. Basically, this means that we produce at a much more competitive price than before," commented production manager, Jesper Riis, at Trelleborg Sealing Solutions.

Universal Robots was formed as the result of many years of intensive research at Denmark's robot cluster in Odense. Its product portfolio includes the collaborative UR3, UR5 and UR10 robotic arms named after their payload in kilos. See www.universal-robots.com

Adding robots does not only optimise production. Contrary to popular beliefs, the increase in automation has a positive impact on employment as well, said operator Dragan Jovanivic: "After adding robots to production, we increased incoming orders to such an extent that we had to hire 50 new employees."
