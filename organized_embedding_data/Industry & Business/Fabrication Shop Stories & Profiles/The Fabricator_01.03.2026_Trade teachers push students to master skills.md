# Trade teachers push students to master skills

[TARİH: 01.03.2026 The Fabricator]

From The

FABRICATOR.COM

A welder reflects on her own experience with an old drill press

By

Jo Mackiewicz

This past summer, I got the chance to serve as a judge in the Harbor Freight Tools for Schools (

hftforschools.org

) competition. To win part of $1.5 million in prize money, high school teachers of skilled trades respond to questions about their strengths in teaching and trades and the outstanding features of their programs. They also submit a video, which gives them the chance to show off their shops and their students’ projects. While assessing the applications, I read about the ways the applicants—highly effective trades teachers—scaffold their students’ learning, moving them from less to more difficult concepts and tasks.

Substantial research in education, including trades education, shows that scaffolding of this sort works when it targets the upper limit of the learner’s capability. In this region, the learner stretches just past their current level of competence. Most education researchers have called this the zone of proximal development, or ZPD. This term came from the work of Lev Vygotsky, a cognitive psychologist who studied learning assessment and instruction. Describing the ZPD, Vygotsky wrote, "Instruction is only useful when it moves ahead of development."

At the shop where I work, I’ve recognized the ZPD of my own trades learning. For example, when I first started at Howe’s Welding and Metal Fabrication in January 2022, I didn’t know how to use the big drill press that rests like a small mountain in our machine shop. The drill press has two levers that control its speed. Up-down combinations produce four general speeds—slow, medium-slow, medium-fast, and fast. But there’s another lever—one with nine additional speeds that can further modulate each of the four general speeds.

During my first year or so at the shop, my boss reminded me to use the up-up combination for a ¼-in. bit and the down-up for a ½-in. bit. He tried to tell me about the other lever, the one with the nine speeds, but everything he said about it sounded like the speech of adults in a Peanuts cartoon: "Wa wa wa. Wa wa wa wa wa wa." My brain couldn’t interact with that information yet because it was too busy attending to all the other variables.

But a few months ago, as I was preparing to drill a hole to tap, I was finally ready to use the speed-nuancing lever. Seemingly all of a sudden, that lever didn’t seem like too much to grapple with. It seemed useful. It finally fell into my drill-press ZPD.

Reading those applications for the Harbor Freight competition, I saw the extraordinary effort that excellent skilled-trades teachers put into sequencing the learning of their students. Through appropriately challenging—and often community-engaged—projects, they scaffold their students learning and inspire the rest of us.

Jo Mackiewicz

is a professor in the English Department of Iowa State University and a welder at Howe’s Welding and Metal Fabrication in Ames, Iowa.
