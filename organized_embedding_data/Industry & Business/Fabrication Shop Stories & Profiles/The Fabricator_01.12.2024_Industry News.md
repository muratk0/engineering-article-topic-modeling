BlueForge Alliance, Learning Blade launch submarine manufacturing curriculum

Learning Blade, a STEM career exploration platform for grades 5-9, has announced the launch of a new mission, "Beneath the Waves: Building Submarines," to address critical workforce shortages. The manufacturing-and engineering-focused curriculum is available through Learning Blade to educators and students, and access has been fully funded in Alabama, Arkansas, Florida, Idaho, Illinois, Louisiana, Massachusetts, Michigan, Missouri, Ohio, South Carolina, Tennessee, and Wisconsin.

The U.S. manufacturing sector faces a massive skills shortage. Over the next decade, 4 million manufacturing jobs will likely be needed, and 2.1 million are expected to go unfilled. America’s submarine industry alone will need to hire 140,000 skilled employees over the next 10 years to meet the Navy’s growing demand for submarines. Learning Blade’s mission-based approach helps address this crisis by introducing students to more than 100 STEM careers and sparking interest in essential industries like naval manufacturing.

"The state of the industrial workforce is in crisis, and employers are desperate to find qualified workers with the technical skills needed to grow their operations," said Jerry Wooden, CEO of eDynamic Learning, the parent company of Learning Blade. "By introducing students to in-demand STEM careers, state leaders and district administrators are helping to introduce young talent to critical industries and are paving the way for the workforce of tomorrow."

The submarine mission arose from a unique partnership among the Navy, BlueForge Alliance (a nonprofit integrator supporting the U.S. Navy’s Submarine Industrial Base program initiatives), and Learning Blade. Together, they’ve created a curriculum that promotes STEM career awareness, demonstrates academic relevance, and fosters interest in submarine manufacturing—an industry that is vital to the nation’s security.

"In the classroom, students often ask, ‘When am I going to need this in real life?’" said Carrie Curtis, PhD, education programs project manager, Maritime Industrial Base Program Office. "Learning Blade’s mission-driven approach bridges academic gaps by integrating various contents to tackle real-world challenges. At a pivotal stage in their personal growth, students are exposed to emerging technologies and career possibilities, allowing them to take charge of their educational paths moving forward. This strategy is essential for ensuring our nation remains competitive and ready to meet future workforce needs."

In the "Beneath the Waves: Building Submarines" mission, students imagine they’re part of the Young Submariners Program, a special project launched by the Navy. Their adventure begins with a trip to a WWII museum where they see an old submarine called the USS Razorback. Throughout the mission, students learn about various roles in submarine manufacturing, including naval architects, marine engineers, welders, and quality control inspectors. The program ends with students presenting their own designs to a fictitious naval program director, showcasing their understanding of modern manufacturing techniques.

Hynes Industries earns Excellence in Manufacturing Award

The Business Journal has reported that custom metal fabricator Hynes Industries, Youngstown, Ohio, is the recipient of the 2024 Excellence in Manufacturing Award from the Youngstown/Warren Regional Chamber and partner Magnet. The award recognizes companies based on business longevity, growth, product innovation, and achievement.

The manufacturer, which serves markets including automated material handling, truck trailer, solar, and general industrial, employs more than 200 people at three locations.

Gladwin Machinery acquired by Vermilion Group

Gladwin Machinery Solutions, a provider of new and used metal fabrication, automation, and welding equipment based in Fridley, Minn., has announced its acquisition by Vermilion Group.

With additional locations in Wisconsin, Illinois, and Missouri, the company serves customers in end markets including contract manufacturing, construction, HVAC, automotive, agriculture, and aerospace and defense.

The company’s management team will remain involved in day-to-day operations as shareholders and leaders.

Epicor acquires Acadia Software

Epicor, a provider of industry-specific enterprise software, has acquired Acadia Software, a provider of connected worker systems that provide step-by-step guidance to frontline workers in manufacturing and other supply chain industries.

"The acquisition of Acadia furthers Epicor’s commitment to helping businesses across the make, move, and sell industries move beyond simply telling workers what to do, but showing them how to do it effectively to drive stronger productivity and efficiency," said Epicor CEO Steve Murphy.

By integrating Acadia’s capabilities, Epicor customers can provide their frontline teams with real-time data, digital work instructions, and task management tools.

Flow partners with Netflix’s "Car Masters: Rust to Riches" for Season 6

Kent, Wash.-based Flow Intl. Corp., a manufacturer of ultrahigh-pressure waterjet cutting systems, has announced its collaboration with the Netflix series "Car Masters: Rust to Riches."

In the current season, which premiered on Oct. 23, the featured team at Gotham Garage is using a Flow Mach 500 waterjet to transform classic cars into custom builds. The machine is equipped with the Dynamic Waterjet XD cutting head, powered by Flow’s HyperJet pump, for complex cutting.

Birdon announces $37 million expansion in Alabama

Birdon, a U.S. Government defense prime contractor specializing in the design, production, upgrade, and support of marine vessels, has announced plans for a $37.55 million expansion at its Bayou La Batre shipyard.

Construction is scheduled for completion by Aug. 31, 2027. The project is expected to create 136 jobs over the next three years, bringing the company’s total workforce at the facility to more than 200 people.

This expansion follows Birdon’s acquisition of the Bayou La Batre shipyard on March 1, 2024, as part of its efforts to support the U.S. Coast Guard with Waterways Commerce Cutters (WCCs). The company was awarded a $1.187 billion contract in 2022 to design and build 27 WCCs over a 10-year period. The vessels are designed for critical operations such as driving piles, building towers, and performing substantial marine construction tasks.

In addition to the WCC contract, Birdon will continue the repair and maintenance work currently underway at the shipyard.

DeGeest, LestaUSA, Eurosider partner to help manufacturers close automation gap

With the recent acquisition of the North American rights to patented technologies and equipment from Eurosider, DeGeest Corp. is now able to bring European finishing systems to manufacturers in North America, regardless of where they are on their path to automation.

Eurosider technology was formerly available through Coating Delivery Technology, which was recently acquired by DeGeest.

"We’ve been partnering with Lesta and its self-learning robotic technology for years, so it’s exciting to add Eurosider to the mix to be able to bring a well-rounded solution to more customers," said Derek DeGeest, president of DeGeest Corp. and LestaUSA.

Eurosider’s Nitrotherm and Polifluid technologies use nitrogen as a carrier instead of compressed air to deliver a consistent finish to liquid and powder finishing applications, respectively. The systems eliminate variables such as temperature and humidity in the finishing process, the company states.

According to DeGeest, manufacturers can add these technologies to their existing manual operations with no interruption or change to current processes.

"Eliminating variables is an important step in preparing for full automation later, when technology such as Lesta robots can be added to be able to finish more parts without hiring," he explained.
