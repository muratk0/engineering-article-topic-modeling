Obama administration loses out on large apprenticeship program push

The House and Senate finally agreed on legislation that could mean major changes for the Workforce Investment Act (WIA), the 1998 law that has long needed some updating. The WIA specified a number of programs that have outlived their usefulness and also mandated the structure of many local workforce boards that are now considered slow-footed and encumbered in the current economy.

The compromise bill is called the Workforce Innovation and Opportunity Act (WIOA), which is on its way to becoming law after the House passed the bill by a resounding 415-6 vote on July 9. The WIOA kills 15 WIA programs.

What the bill doesn’t do is create a new $500 million apprenticeship program that the Obama administration proposed earlier this year. That apprenticeship program would have been one element in a new $1.5 billion job-driven training fund focused on community colleges, which would have replaced the Trade Adjustment Assistance Community College and Career Training (TAACCCT) grant program. (The TAACCCT program was

not

one of the discontinued job-training efforts.)

Ron Painter, CEO, National Association of Workforce Boards, said the WIOA makes several operational improvements and some clarifications that are important. He noted these significant advances:

• Eliminates an administrative bottleneck called “sequence of services,” which required a series of steps before an individual could be referred for training

• Applies “common performance indicators” to all funding to gauge outcomes across multiple programs

• Places emphasis on not only “demand” occupations, but also credentials that are industry-recognized and portable—while following input provided by local Workforce Investment Boards and their industry sector partners

• Allows a certain percentage of funds to be committed to “pay-for-performance” training contracts for adults and youth

• Provides for more flexibility in transferring funds to better “localize” investment strategies

• Reduces the size of the Workforce Investment Boards

Manufacturing Initiatives Made at Maker Faire

The Obama administration announced new manufacturing initiatives in June. But, unlike the call for manufacturing research centers that involve federal funding, this recent announcement attempts to leverage existing resources, primarily federal laboratories.

The key initiative that came out of the White House Maker Faire in June is a promise to allow manufacturing companies access to more than $5 billion worth of research, prototyping, and testing equipment and expertise spread over 700 federal research and development facilities. That access will come via some refinements to

www.data.gov/research

, including machine-readable data.

(The first Maker Faire occurred in California in 2006. Its organizers describe the event as “part science fair, part county fair,” and it welcomes any type of “maker”—hobbyists, artists, engineers, tinkerers, and commercial exhibitors. These types of individuals make up the “Maker Movement,” which is a do-it-yourself, grassroots collection of people bringing creative ideas to realization.)

A second announcement at the Maker Faire focused on the Mayors Maker Challenge, which ties into an initiative from a group called the Manufacturing Alliance of Communities (MAC). The alliance, founded in 2008, started as a group of mayors from mostly Midwestern states joining forces to fight the closing of automotive assembly plants. The group expanded its mission in 2012 to support manufacturing more broadly, including reaching out to cities and towns with small welding and fabrication operations.

Matt Ward, the MAC policy director, said more than 100 local officials from around the country are involved in this effort. All of them have committed to take steps to boost and support the innovation and entrepreneurship in manufacturing. Such a step could be something as simple as designating a current city or county official as a liaison to the Maker Movement or as detailed as assisting in the development of a new training program for a local business.
