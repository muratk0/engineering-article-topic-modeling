Business partners Kristen Reinhardt and Barbara Foster make up the driving force behind Aesthetic Metals, a custom fabrication shop that specializes in privacy screens, indoor and outdoor, and other metal products for outdoor living spaces.

I

f you were to spend a few minutes in conversation with Barbara Foster and Kristen Reinhardt, you’d think they were lifelong best friends. The conversation proceeds seamlessly, one picking up where the other leaves off, and their enthusiasm is palpable. A main topic of discussion: How they work with customers to design decorative and functional projects for residences and businesses.

"Many customers come to us because they’re interested in a partition or a privacy screen, but they don’t know where to begin," Reinhardt said.

"We have a pattern library, and we have photos of most of our projects," Foster said, so they have a good starting point. Once they get a customer into a conversation, it doesn’t take long to learn what the customer likes and doesn’t like, and before long, they move from idea to concept to plan.

Neighbors and workout partners, they are the creative force behind Aesthetic Metals, a small company in Rockford, Ill., that specializes in all manner of indoor and outdoor products: privacy screens, sun screens, and room partitions; tables, pedestals, and other furniture; fire pits, fire rings, and fire tables; and planters, trellises, and garden sculptures (see

Hold the Pickles, Hold the Lettuce

For decades fast-food joints were known for efficiency and rigid consistency—that is, no options—until Burger King’s 1974 jingle stressed that "special orders don’t upset us." This is Aesthetic Metals’ niche. Foster and Reinhardt enjoy discussing ideas, offering choices, and taking great care to develop designs that thrill their customers, and every order at Aesthetic Metals is a custom job. Quite a bit of time and effort go into creating and optimizing a good design, blending the customer’s interests with the surroundings at the installation site (see

Figure 4

).

Located less than 100 miles from Chicago, Aesthetic Metals has many installations in and around the Windy City—highend restaurants, the museum campus, and rooftop decks. One diehard Chicago Cubs fan wanted a sun screen for a pergola and incorporated a baseball in the design. Although the baseball in the sun screen is small, the result is dazzling: a large shadow that works its way across the deck as the sun travels across the sky.

This doesn’t mean that Chicago is its sole market. Although the company is just a few years old, it has shipped products from coast to coast.

Earning an A in Art Class

Reinhardt, an art teacher by profession, found a strong complement in Foster, who has a strong aptitude for design. Both are also strong in soft skills and spend as much time as necessary on the design phase, especially when working with customers who aren’t too artistically inclined.

"We try to guide them gently," Reinhardt said. In some cases, they proceed surreptitiously and surprise the customer with an extra detail or feature.

"We did a screen in a pine cone motif, and hidden throughout we placed the family’s initials," Foster said. The customer was thrilled about this additional and subtle personal touch.

Although Reinhardt and Foster are knowledgeable about metals and fabricating, it’s clear that their focus isn’t the nuts and bolts of fabricating, but delivering projects that make the world a nicer-looking place.

"We get to make beautiful things," Foster said, summing up Aesthetic Metals’ ethos.
