President Obama announced the two newest manufacturing institutes, one of which will develop next-generation lightweight metals and train workers to work with those products.

The American Lightweight Materials Manufacturing Innovation Institute (ALMMII) is led by The Ohio State University, the University of Michigan, and the Columbus, Ohio-based Edison Welding Institute (EWI). This will be the third institute in President Obama’s much ballyhooed National Network of Manufacturing Innovation (NNMI). The fourth institute will be called the Digital Lab for Manufacturing, to be located in Chicago. Obama has positioned these institutes as engines of job creation in new industries based on new technologies developed within the NNMI.

The ALMMII is expected to open this spring in Detroit. It will receive $70 million in federal funding over five years, matched by another $78 million from the consortium partners themselves. A University of Michigan press release announcing the grant alluded to the institute creating 10,000 jobs in the metal stamping, metalworking, machining, and casting industries that are dominant in the Midwest. But that job number estimate appears to represent jobs that are already available in the Midwest but lack qualified applicants.

Rebecca Gurk, EWI’s corporate communications and marketing manager, explained that a needs assessment conducted by the ALMMII team prior to the NNMI award determined that there were 245,000 job postings in the ALMMII region related to industries associated with “light-weighting” over the period of a year. About 5 percent of the total postings were in job categories related to materials, including material engineers, material scientists, and various metalworking trades. (Five percent of 245,000 is apparently where the 10,000 estimate came from.)

“ALMMII’s education and workforce initiatives will be geared toward growing a workforce that has the skills and knowledge to meet the worker demand that already exists and is rising,” she said.

“The challenge is in optimizing component designs and developing the advanced processes to manufacture [specialty metals] robustly on a large scale at affordable cost. And each material needs its own tailored process.”

—Alan Taub, American Lightweight Materials Manufacturing Innovation Institute

Besides its apparent primary job training role, the ALMMII also will have a research focus.

“Many of the materials for weight reduction already exist, such as high-strength steels, aluminum, magnesium, and titanium,” said Alan Taub, a University of Michigan professor of materials science and chief technology officer of the new institute. “The challenge is in optimizing component designs and developing the advanced processes to manufacture them robustly on a large scale at affordable cost. And each material needs its own tailored process.”

Federal Reserve Looks at Metals Warehouses

In part out of concern about price spikes that could affect industrial users of metals, the Federal Reserve Board is reviewing its policy on what kind of commercial practices financial holding companies (FHCs) can engage in with regard to physical commodities such as metals.

Last July an executive from MillerCoors told the U.S. Senate Subcommittee on Financial Institutions and Consumer Protection, chaired by Sen. Sherrod Brown, D-Ohio, that aluminum warehouses owned by subsidiaries of companies such as Goldman Sachs were refusing to release aluminum and other metals MillerCoors had already paid for, forcing the company to pay premiums and penalties for the warehousing of those already-paid-for supplies. Brown, at hearings in January, said aluminum users had paid an extra $3 billion in 2013.

The advanced notice of proposed rulemaking the Federal Reserve issued in January asks whether it ought to impose restrictions on the commercial commodity activities of FHCs. But Brown said the Fed proposal is “a timid step” and was “too slow in coming.”
