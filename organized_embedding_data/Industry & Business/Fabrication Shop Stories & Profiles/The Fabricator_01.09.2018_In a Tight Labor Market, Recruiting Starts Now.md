# In a Tight Labor Market, Recruiting Starts Now

[TARİH: 01.09.2018 The Fabricator]

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

This summer sported the lowest rate of youth unemployment in years–just a hair shy of 10 percent. This number had been climbing every year, but this last summer those who were seeking a job were very likely to have found one. The bad news is that there were substantially fewer young people looking than in past years. This has been a trend for some time now as people under the age of 20 are either attending school or simply living at home with few financial obligations.

This has implications for fabricators and manufacturers. It is not that these kids are likely to pick up jobs during the summer in the operations we run. Most younger workers employed by fabricators will be starting a new career of some kind, but before a person is ready to make that kind of decision, they have to been exposed to the world of work.

The good news for the economy as a whole is that there is a generation that now has some work experience under their belt. The employer ranks that kind of experience as high or higher than education, as this is what assures the new boss that the employee has an idea what the demands of work are–like timeliness and team work.

The bulk of these summer jobs were in traditional areas such as retail, food service, and entertainment, but there were some new entries such as health care and outdoor work. The number of young people taking parttime jobs in hospitals doubled from five years ago, and lawn and garden services drew a lot more young workers than in past years.

Fabricators have to look at their workforce needs in a different way than in the past. Recruiting has to start very early, while kids are still in school and learning what their options might be. Companies that plan on hiring will face hurdles they didn’t before—like fewer kids with any of the usual skills involved with modern manufacturing—and that will mean more training by the employer and more hiring of attitude rather than aptitude. That summer job looms larger and larger as the first test for a prospective employee.
