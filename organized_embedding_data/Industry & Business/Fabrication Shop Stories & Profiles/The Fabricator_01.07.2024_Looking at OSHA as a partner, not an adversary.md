# Looking at OSHA as a partner, not an adversary

[TARİH: 01.07.2024 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

Such a relationship can prove beneficial, especially with so much at stake

Dan Davis

"Folks that are out there working … see that it was really hard to get us VPP-certified. They think that the company must actually care. That goes a long way to not only supporting safety efforts, but also production quality and compliance."

—Chris Chell, Nova Group

One of the most attractive perks of being a part of a VPP is that it exempts participating companies from programmed inspections. If companies maintain stellar safety metrics, they only have to be re-evaluated every three to five years, depending on who is administering the program.

Now keep in mind that unprogrammed inspections can still occur. They are triggered by an employee death, catastrophic injury to three or more employees, a report of an imminent danger, or an employee complaint. Having said that, being in the VPP suggests that a company has a work environment in which most of those incidents aren’t likely to happen. Discipline in safety practices typically helps companies avoid penalties related to safety mishaps.

The timing might be right to consider joining a VPP. During his presentation, "OSHA and Trends," at the FMA Safety Conference in late spring, Eric Hobbs of Ogletree, Deakins, Nash, Smoak & Stewart law firm stated that OSHA is gearing up to add up to 200 new compliance officers in the coming years. (In 2021, for the first time in OSHA’s history, the agency actually had fewer compliance officers than when the agency was first created in 1973.)

Those new officers will be replacing veterans who are heading for retirement. The replacements won’t have as much experience and likely will lack proper mentoring because of the turnover at the agency. When these new compliance officers show up for an inspection, they are in the midst of on-the-job training, and that adds unpredictability to an already stressful event.

In the meantime, OSHA is interested in getting more manufacturing companies to be a part of a VPP. It announced a push to "modernize, expand, and enhance" VPP in early 2023, as the programs lost a lot of participating companies after the pandemic. One of the ways it is looking to re-energize the programs is to make a way for companies to obtain VPP status while also securing ISO 45001-2018 certification. This would eliminate duplicated efforts and help companies separate themselves from their competitors.

OSHA wants manufacturers to join a VPP. Metal fabricators appear to benefit when they participate. It seems to be a win-win scenario for keeping workers safe.
