FMA changes foundation name to SparkForce

The Fabricators and Manufacturers Association, the leading trade association for the metal fabrication industry, has changed the name of its charitable foundation. On Jan. 1, 2025, Nuts, Bolts & Thingamajigs became SparkForce, The FMA Foundation.

This new name embodies FMA’s commitment to support the future of manufacturing and attract the next-generation workforce—the same commitment that has driven the organization since it formed the foundation in 1990.

Why the change? FMA President/CEO Ed Youdell shared, "Our mission is to spark young people’s interest in manufacturing careers, and today’s manufacturing industry is much more than nuts and bolts—it’s advanced, cutting-edge technology. The new SparkForce name shows how we’re intensifying our efforts to empower the next generation of manufacturers while establishing a much clearer connection with the FMA workforce lifecycle."

As always, SparkForce will be working to address the industry’s labor shortage crisis by fulfilling its mission to engage, nurture, and excite people of all ages in the pursuit of careers in manufacturing through scholarships, summer manufacturing camps, and inclusion camp programs.

To date, the FMA Foundation has given away more than $1.6 million in scholarships to students pursuing careers in the manufacturing industry. SparkForce will continue to award scholarships twice a year to high school graduates and returning students who are enrolled in manufacturing-related degree and certificate programs. In addition, the foundation has awarded more than $3.1 million in grant funding to support its successful manufacturing camp programs at partner colleges, schools, and community organizations throughout the country. The program has significantly expanded over the last 20 years, and in 2025, more than 5,000 youth and young adults will be inspired to pursue manufacturing career pathways by attending SparkForce Camps.

Thyssenkrupp Materials NA acquires Cobotix Mfg.

Thyssenkrupp Materials NA, Southfield, Mich., has announced its acquisition of Cobotix Mfg. Inc., a precision metal fabricator specializing in copper and aluminum in Port Coquitlam, British Columbia. Cobotix offers automation and robotics capabilities including fully automated manufacturing, enabling high-precision production with minimal human intervention.

With this acquisition, thyssenkrupp Materials NA strengthens its capabilities in automated manufacturing and precision metal fabrication and expands its service offerings, particularly in automated copper and aluminum bus production, for key markets including power generation, electric vehicle infrastructure, and telecommunications.

Prima Industrie acquires Sistec AM

Prima Industrie SpA, through its Prima Power brand, has acquired Sistec AM, a provider of industrial automation. The acquisition, part of Prima’s "Evolve by integration" strategy, broadens the company’s automation capabilities and enhances its sheet metalworking technologies portfolio with advanced automation technologies and customized flexible manufacturing systems.

Based in Pordenone, Italy, Sistec AM is a designer, developer, and manufacturer of robotic and customized automation systems for production processes, including robotic lines, cells, and assembly and control systems.

BTX Precision acquires A-1 Jay’s family of companies

Chicago-based BTX Precision has completed the acquisition of a family of precision component manufacturing and processing providers—including A-1 Jay’s Machining, A-1 Jay’s Sheet Metal, Inotech Laser Processing, Bay Area Grinding, and Silicon Valley Precision Cleaning—from the Machathil family in San Jose, Calif. Under BTX ownership, these companies ("the business") will eventually merge into a single brand, A1J Technologies.

Jensen Machathil continues to lead the business as business unit president within BTX. Founder James Machathil continues to support the business in an advisory capacity and remains involved as an investor/partner in BTX Precision.

The business is recognized for its ability to support complex and sophisticated requirements. Capabilities include complex machining, EDM, grinding, lapping, sheet metal fabrication, and laser micromachining, as well as precision cleaning and cleanroom assembly.

Civan Lasers launches two demonstration labs in the U.S.

Civan Lasers has opened advanced demonstration labs at AMET in Rexburg, Idaho, and Photon Automation in Detroit. Both locations offer hands-on demonstrations and tailored process development for clients interested in Civan’s Dynamic Beam Lasers for welding. The expansion allows the company to offer comprehensive systems and support to help customers integrate the technology into their operations.

Dynamic Beam Laser technology provides control over beam shaping to address welding of challenging materials and thick sections, including thick copper components, thick stainless steel, and large wind tower sections—applications in which traditional welding methods can be slow and complex.

Highfield Boats to open facility in Michigan

Wright & Johnson N.A., dba Highfield Boats, a manufacturer of aluminum rigid inflatable boats and tenders, has announced plans to expand production capabilities in Cadillac, Mich. The expansion is expected to generate more than $3.1 million in capital investment, creating 110 jobs.

This new facility will be a hull and assembly production operation. At full capacity, it will employ 75 hourly employees, including welders, fiberglass specialists, PNC operators, painters, engine installers, and electricians, as well as 35 salaried positions.

Bullet Proof Diesel expands into metal fabrication services

Mesa, Ariz.-based Bullet Proof Diesel has announced the expansion of its operations with the launch of Neal Technologies Inc., a division that offers precision laser cutting, CNC machining, metal prototyping, and welding services in Phoenix.

Positioned to serve industries from automotive and aerospace to construction and creative prototyping, the division will deliver tailored services designed to meet the unique needs of its clients.

Magna Intl. to increase South Carolina footprint

Automotive supplier Magna Intl. will establish a new satellite operation and expand its existing operation in Piedmont, S.C. The combined investment of about $200 million will create 200 jobs across both locations starting in 2025. Operations at the new 625,000-sq.-ft. facility are expected to begin in Q1 2025.

Both locations will produce automotive components for cars, light trucks, and commercial vehicles.

Venture Steel purchases slitting line from Athader S.L.

Venture Steel has purchased a high-production slitting line from Athader S.L. (of The Bradbury Group) for installation at its newly expanded manufacturing facility in Ramos Arizpe, Mexico.

This line will have the capability of slitting 0.250- by 62-in. by 80,000-lb. coils with 110,000-PSI tensile strength running up to 600 FPM. It will include automation, a double eccentric slitter head with capstan and automatic slitter tooling changeover, quick-change separators, dual tension rolls with hydraulic lockup, automatic overarm separator changeover, and a slit coil packaging line.

Venture Steel is a processor and distributor of metals with a focus on flat-rolled products.
