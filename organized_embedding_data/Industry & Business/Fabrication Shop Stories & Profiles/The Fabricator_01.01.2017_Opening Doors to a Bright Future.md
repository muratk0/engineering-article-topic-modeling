oving can be a pain, but it also can be cathartic. While you break your back packing up, moving gives you a chance to take a break as you unpack feelings and memories tied to a collection of physical goods. It’s an opportunity to purge the clutter and move ahead free of "valuables" that really have no value other than sentimentality.

The Fabricators & Manufacturers Association (FMA) officially made its move from Rockford, Ill., on Dec. 9 and settled into its new home in Elgin, Ill., during the following weeks. It’s a modern and sleek new facility that should give FMA a better opportunity to respond to the needs of its members; readers of its publications; and industry partners, such as magazine advertisers and FABTECH

®

exhibitors.

In packing up, the FMA staff ditched a lot of relics from an analog era. That manual on safeguarding mechanical shears, while somewhat relevant given the amount of old iron in shops, really didn’t have a large audience. The workbooks on the basics of CO

2

laser cutting looked a bit dated compared to the technology producing laser-cut blanks at lightning speed. And the CDs about press brake basics might have been somewhat useful, if people had a means to play them.

The purging doesn’t mean that FMA doesn’t have a well-stocked stable of reference material, though if your fabricating operation is in need of training materials, you should really check out the online bookstore at

www.fmanet.org

. (Click on "Store" at the top and it will take you to the online repository of educational materials.)

FMA’s new home was designed for a modern organization. It has a conference center to hold member training events, while also providing informal work areas to accommodate small group meetings or individuals who need a place to conduct business. Large-screen video-conferencing is also available if, for example, a group of metal fabricators is looking for instruction and wants to be connected with a subject matter expert in another location.

FMA will host an official open house, tentatively scheduled for May 18. The first official educational event to be held at the new facility will be FMA’s annual Safety Conference April 19-20. If you would like to attend one of these scheduled events or just stop in for a visit, we’ll be here to welcome you.

While we didn’t move too far from our Rockford origins, only about 40 miles southeast on I-90, the move to Elgin does get us closer to Chicagoland, which is home to a heavy mix of precision metal fabricators, stampers, structural steel fabricators, and welding shops. Of course, with that collection of metal manufacturers clustered in one area, plenty of machine tool builders, software developers, and service providers can be found nearby as well. The hope is that perhaps they might use FMA’s headquarters as a meeting point.

FABTECH is held in Chicago every other year for a reason. The city is within driving distance of a majority of metal fabricators doing business in the U.S. In 1990 most states in the Midwest, Northeast, and even the Southeast looked at manufacturing companies as being the leading employers within their respective borders, according to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics. The rise of the health care economy and demise of manually intensive manufacturing jobs have changed the job market greatly since then, but even in 2015, manufacturing was the leading employer in Indiana, Michigan, Wisconsin, and Iowa. Manufacturing still matters, and FMA wants to be closer to the heartbeat of the U.S. manufacturing base.

For those not within a short drive, the new FMA headquarters is 25 miles northwest of O’Hare International Airport. The drive to Elgin from the airport is shorter than the time it takes a plane to move away from the jet bridge and taxi into position for takeoff.

This new building is an investment that FMA is making in metal fabricating’s future. Following the establishment of the FMA Metal Fabrication Lab at Harper College in nearby Palatine, Ill., where the association already has held several hands-on training workshops, FMA is doubling-down on its bet that manufacturing can expect a bright future.

If you are in the area or planning to visit, feel free and stop by the new building. It’s our new home, and we’d like the metal fabricating community to feel the same way.
