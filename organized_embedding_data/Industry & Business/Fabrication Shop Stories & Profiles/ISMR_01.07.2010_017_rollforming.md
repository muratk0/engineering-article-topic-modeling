Formits’ order book, since May 2009, has included, among others, coil process lines and rollformers to produce steel cladding, metal furniture, tubes, crash barrier and plasterers’ angle bead. This brought the total order book to well over a million pounds.

“Our customers (mainly in the construction industry) were very badly hit by the economic downturn in 2009,” Bain told ISMR. “Factories, house-building etc. were substantially reduced but the situation is improving as customers have now used up their stock, inventories are low and they need to re-stock. House-building, in particular, seems to be improving which is good for the rollforming industry overall.

“Because we are a small outfit, and our overhead is low, two or three large contracts can keep us going for a few months. We have a small, highly skilled staff and use subcontractors when we have a lot of work – we also subcontract all our machining work. We had a high level of enquiries at the MACH exhibition in the UK, which has just finished.”

## OBJECTIVES AND TARGETS

“Our main objective is to specialise in rollforming – that is our niche market,” Bain told ISMR. “There are other coil processing machinery manufacturers who do not do rollforming – they just use standard roll forming machines with different numbers of rolls/tooling. We specialise in roll forming machinery and aim to become the leading UK manufacturer in this field over the next three years.

“We want to consolidate our position by making special purpose applications so that customers come back to us for future business. We put the end product (ie the final product of the line) into our COPRA CAD software and determine how many passes the product needs. We will then build the machine around the product, rather than putting new tooling on an existing standard machine. We start with the finished product and work backwards, if you like. We always develop the products (the section profile) in close consultation with our clients and often offer suggestions to reduce cost and increase efficiency on a line.”

The main advantages of using its rollforming lines, Bain says, are for users who need high volume production using minimum labour requirements to produce consistent components (either fully or partly finished) with minimum secondary operations. The company is looking to expand into larger premises to fulfil its current order book.

“Most of our customers are primarily end users in the construction and steel furniture industries – they tend to be Tier 1 producers, rather than subcontractors,” Bain told ISMR. “Our main business is in the UK (around 90%), but we do have equipment (ordered by parent companies in the UK) in Scandinavia, Poland, USA, Romania and western Europe. We do not deliver anything into Asia, at the moment, but we are hoping that will change – I am in discussions with a potential Indian customer at the moment. We will also be visitors at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover.”

## LINE INNOVATION

“I started Formit in 1989,” Bain explained. “In 1999, we became part of a large engineering group which lasted for five years. In 2004, the present directors (myself included) bought the remaining shares to make ourselves fully independent again. Our speciality is rollforming machinery (around 80% of our business). Our rollforming equipment is bespoke – customised to requirements for special applications.

“We are particularly proud of a CNC control garage door production line, worth over £600,000, that we installed in the north-east of England in 2008. It was a unique rotating roll former – we developed it to create a different profile on the edge of the garage door. The basic idea was to rotate the heads so that only one set of coil handling equipment etc. was needed and the overall cost was greatly reduced. That had never been done before and the customer was very pleased with it.

“We are currently making a special lockseam tube line, worth over £600,000, for a new customer in western Europe that will be installed in four months’ time,” continues Bain. “Earlier this year, we installed a rollformer on a fluorescent light production line for a UK customer (worth around £160,000). We also supplied a CNC control rollformer for cladding for portable buildings in the north of England last year (worth around £300,000). It generally takes 6-8 months for us to deliver a line.”
