Four-wheeled metal bin manufacturer uses cloud-based system to drive growth

SITUATION

The Egbert Taylor Group, Droitwich, England, produces build-to-order fourwheeled metal bins in more than 1.5 million different combinations of sizes, colors, lids, and end customer logos.

The company was using a legacy ERP system that was limiting its growth. Over the course of 15 years, the system had become extensively customized with many undocumented modifications. This began to restrict new product development and time to market, inhibiting the ability of the company to respond quickly to changes requested by new and existing customers.

"Our legacy systems were reaching the end, and as such we required an effective system that enables us to have greater visibility of the business and its processes," said Andrew Davies, finance director of Egbert Taylor Group. "Our objectives included improving working capital management through streamlined production processes, as well as the benefits of integrating our quoting process into a single cloud-based solution."

RESOLUTION

Following a thorough review of the market, the bin manufacturer selected Infor CloudSuite Industrial to track stock levels and provide delivery schedules. The company chose the system for two main reasons. First, it enables a tightly integrated business process between customer sales ordering and procurement, in keeping with Taylor’s customer service improvement objectives. Second, the completeness of functionality for build-to-order businesses in the application suite has allowed Taylor to use the system with minimal customizations.

The company chose to deploy via the cloud to capitalize on the lower upfront costs, help reduce risk, support business continuity, and ensure that the business always has access to the latest version of the application suite. The cloud infrastructure also will enable the company to roll out the system to its field sales team with minimal disruption.

Conveyor eliminates chip-jamming downtime for auto parts manufacturer

SITUATION

A Detroit-area manufacturer and Tier 1 supplier of parts to the automotive industry operates many automated twin-spindle CNC turning machines. The mild steel being turned creates a very gummy, stringy, entangled nest of chips.

Because the firm’s conveyors were jamming and failing to remove these large balls of chips, they were suffering damage. At least one conveyor incurred a daily downtime that lasted for an entire eight-hour shift.

RESOLUTION

The automotive supplier decided to install a Munchman II conveyor from Jorgensen Conveyors. The conveyor uses two belts through the lower curve, incline, and discharge sections. This enables successful removal of the large, stringy balls of chips that had been jamming the single-belt conveyors.

The conveyor also incorporates Jorgensen’s UVS-EcoLogic

®

control, which provides constant load monitoring to protect the conveyor motor and drivetrain components from damage in a conveyor jam condition, which can cause excessive torque on the conveyor. The control also features an automatic jamclearing sequence of conveyor operation for increased uptime.

"For this application, we removed the inverted V cleats on the upper belt to make sure the conveyor could pass a machined part through in case it accidentally falls onto the conveyor," said Kaytlen Rinaldi, lead engineer at Jorgensen.

After a successful installation and runoff, the automotive supplier decided to put this type of conveyor on all of its existing machines and advised its machine tool suppliers that it wanted the conveyor on any new machines it ordered in the future.

"We’ve never had a Munchman II jam up or cause a downtime that was the fault of the conveyor itself," said the automotive supplier’s maintenance manager. "We currently have 20 in-house and have six more on order. When we’ve added the conveyor onto all our machines, we’ll have more than 50."
