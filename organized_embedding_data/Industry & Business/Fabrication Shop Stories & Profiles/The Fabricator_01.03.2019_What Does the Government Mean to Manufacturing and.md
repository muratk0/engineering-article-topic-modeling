It is the inalienable right of all Americans to gripe about their government. We know all the jokes by now – the comment that there are no more feared words than "I’m from the government and I’m here to help you." When we enumerate the challenges of running a business, two government-related issues are at the top of the list – regulation and taxes. Is there anything positive that can be said about the government’s role? Did the government shutdown do any real harm, or was it a relief to have a little less activity for a month or so?

Broadly speaking, the government plays a major role in the growth of manufacturing or any business through the provision of the infrastructure that everyone depends on. The transportation system relies on that infrastructure, and everyone relies on transportation. There is also the legal infrastructure that protects our contracts and sets the parameters within which competition operates. When one considers the full list of government activity, it is apparent that a lot comes from various levels of the system – everything from research and development to education to export promotion, and so on.

Did the government shutdown affect manufacturing and fabricating in the U.S.? Yes and no. As usual it all depends on the individual company. Those that are among the 22,000 companies that sell to the U.S. were directly affected. Others were hurt because they have been waiting for government approval or they have been inhibited from exporting. Still others have been affected because some 800,000 people were not getting paychecks and business was losing upwards of $20 million a day.

Past government shutdowns have been triggered by disputes over budgets and spending – the shutdowns were an attempt to keep the proverbial can from being kicked down the road. This dispute became highly personal and was a clear power struggle between the president and the House of Representatives. The antagonism was intense, and neither side showed any desire whatsoever to yield on even the smallest point. This heralds a period when every political battle will be intense and cooperation will be banned. This is what hurts manufacturing and every other business. The leaders of the country are not focused on what the economy needs while engaged in this kind of intense partisan warfare.
