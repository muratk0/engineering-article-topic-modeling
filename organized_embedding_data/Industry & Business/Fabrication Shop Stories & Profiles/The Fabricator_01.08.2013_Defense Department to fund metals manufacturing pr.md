# Defense Department to fund metals manufacturing program

Around Washington

New effort funded with existing federal dollars

sbarlas@verizon.net

.

The Department of Defense announced in May that it intends to fund a new Lightweight and Modern Metals Manufacturing Innovation (LM3I) Institute with a budget of $70 million a year.

A solicitation has not gone out yet from the Office of Naval Research, which will be handling applications. Once created, a federal interagency group will run the program.

This is one of the two newest such institutes. The other is the Digital Manufacturing and Design Innovation Institute. The first was the National Additive Manufacturing Innovation Institute. All three are essentially “pilot programs” setting the stage for what the Obama administration hopes will be a $1-billion-a-year national network for manufacturing innovation.

But Congress has shown no inclination to provide the $1 billion in prior years and is not likely to do so in fiscal 2014, which begins Oct. 1. The pilot programs like the LM3I, on the other hand, are being funded out of already appropriated federal funds. In the case of the LM3I, DOD is providing $50 million, and the other $20 million is coming from other federal agencies. An award to the organization that will host the LM3I is expected in late 2013.

The rationale for the new LM3I rests on the fact that U.S. development of new alloys has lagged. Because of strict reliability concerns and a hyper-competitive global marketplace for metals, U.S. industry has focused on production efficiency in recent years instead of expansion into new applications and markets. Until recently this has resulted in a significant reduction in new alloy development and applications, particularly in lightweight metals.

That is a serious shortcoming when the DOD, for example, tries to reduce the weight of the Joint Light Tactical Vehicle (JLTV), the presumed successor to the High Mobility Multipurpose Wheeled Vehicle (HMMWV or Humvee). One variant of the JLTV has been eliminated because it was too heavy to meet the weight requirement of being transportable by Army and Marine Corps helicopters.

Some of the $20 million in non-DOD dollars for the LM3I is being provided by the Advanced Manufacturing Technology Consortia (AMTech) program at the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). The AMTech itself is a new program at NIST funded for the first time in the current fiscal 2013. Its budget is $14.2 million.

Obama Administration Seeks Supply Chain Research Funding

Speaking of new programs at NIST, President Obama’s budget request for fiscal 2014 includes $25 million for Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) Manufacturing Technology Acceleration Centers (M-TAC). The MEP has offices in every state and is dedicated to helping small and mid-sized U.S. manufacturers create and retain jobs, increase profits, and improve production efficiency. This new M-TAC component is expected to focus on supply chains in technology-intensive manufacturing sectors.

The NIST published a request for information on June 12 that seeks input on how these M-TACs should operate and exactly what they should focus on. Generally, the program will seek to provide small and midsized manufacturers with the same access as large manufacturers to the universe of emerging technologies and processes being created around the globe.

These M-TACs will be located at MEP offices, but it is not quite clear yet what they will be doing that current MEP technical experts are not already doing. Moreover, Congress must appropriate $25 million for this program, and that is far from a done deal.

Meanwhile the proposed budget for the MEP program in 2014 is $119 million, about $10 million less than its budget for this year. Neither the House nor Senate Appropriations Committee has started any work on any federal department or agency budget for fiscal 2014. So it is too early to get a read on whether the $25 million for the M-TACs is in the cards.
