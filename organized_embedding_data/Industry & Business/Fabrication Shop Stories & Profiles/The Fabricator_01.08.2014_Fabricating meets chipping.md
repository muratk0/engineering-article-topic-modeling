# Fabricating meets chipping

[TARİH: 01.08.2014 The Fabricator]

IMTS 2014 PREVIEW

With metal fabricators showing more interest in machining technologies, IMTS 2014 likely is a destination of interest

In 2013

The FABRICATOR

conducted its biannual survey of its readership and found that metal fabricators were doing a little more than bending, cutting, and welding sheet metal. More shops are jumping into machining—and attending tradeshows like IMTS 2014 in Chicago, Sept. 8-13.

The publication’s readership survey, which was sent to 9,749 subscribers and had a 2 percent return rate, revealed that 78 percent actually had on-site machining capabilities. When asked to gauge the amount of machining activity compared to the previous year, 56 percent indicated that it had increased, and 36 percent said it had remained the same. Fabricators are increasingly unafraid of letting the chips fall where they may.

Of course, that doesn’t mean these fab shops yearn to be machine shops. These are companies looking to expand their manufacturing capabilities so that they may become a one-stop shop for customers wanting to cut one invoice, instead of using multiple sources to deliver one metal component. These fab shops are machining small parts that then will be welded onto a large metal assembly. They aren’t chasing machining work for the most part; they are supplementing their core fabricating work with chipping.

It’s one of the reasons that attendance at IMTS continues to grow. The 30th iteration of the International Manufacturing Technology Show expects to welcome more than 100,000 attendees from more than 110 countries. Exhibitors will occupy over 1.2 million square feet of space, showing the latest metal cutting technologies, tooling advancements, and software developments.

Fabricators who will be attending the six-day event will have the chance to visit with some manufacturers of waterjet (see Figure 1), plasma, and laser cutting equipment and welding power sources and accessories, which comprise one of the product category pavilions. The other pavilions are Metal Cutting, Tooling and Workholding Systems, Abrasive Machining/Sawing/Finishing, Controls and CAD/CAM, EDM, Gear Generation, Industrial Automation, Machine Components/Cleaning/Environmental, and Quality Assurance.

Figure 1 Waterjet cutting is a technology that spans both the fabricating and machining worlds. The abrasive cutting technology removes material, like in machining, but a majority of the work is focused around 2-D materials, like in sheet metal fabricating.

What’s New in 2014?

This year IMTS 2014 welcomes Hannover Messe’s Motion, Drive & Automation event to North America for the first time. Companies involved in the power transmission, motion control, and fluid technology industries will be exhibiting alongside IMTS vendors.

Also, Local Motors Inc., an organization that specializes in low-volume vehicle manufacturing based on open-source designs, will build a prototype vehicle completely with 3-D-printed parts right in the Emerging Technology Center (Booth N-650). At IMTS 2012, a Local Motors crew built its Rally Fighter from the ground up over the course of the week (see Figure 2).

Figure 2 In 2012 a crew from Local Motors built a vehicle with parts created with traditional manufacturing technologies. In 2014 the company will attempt to build a vehicle relying on a 3-D printer to make the parts.

FMA Communications (Booth N-6074) also will be at the show. Fabricators who have immersed themselves in the metal chipping world while walking the IMTS show floor can stop by and pick up the latest issue of

The FABRICATOR

just to remind them that their world still revolves around sheet metal.
