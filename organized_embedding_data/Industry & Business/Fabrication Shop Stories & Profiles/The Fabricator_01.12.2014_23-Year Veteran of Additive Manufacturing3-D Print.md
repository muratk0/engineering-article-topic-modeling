He currently sits on the board of the Additive Manufacturing Users Group as its AM industry adviser, is a founding adviser for the 3D Printing Association, and has served as chairman of the SME community for additive manufacturing.

Grimm’s presentation will address what fabricators and metals processors need to know to further examine and invest in 3-D printing. He will demonstrate how this technology is upending “business as usual” with examples from auto and aerospace technology, and will share his experiences about the many industrial products that have benefited from the process. Grimm’s high-energy delivery and unique 3-D printing expertise gained as both a systems expert and small-business user of the technology will both entertain and inform.

2014 Election Postmortem

It may be safe to watch TV again, but is it any safer to be in manufacturing after the “red tide”? The congressional gains made by the GOP may be better interpreted as losses for the Democrats as the average voter didn’t so much vote for the Republicans as they voted against the Democrats. The prevailing mood was anger and frustration, and the only real message is that people are tired of a “do-nothing” Congress. This could play well to some of the goals of manufacturers.

Traditionally, more is accomplished when the presidency is held by one party and Congress is controlled by the other. The two sides must find a middle ground or nothing at all gets done. Assuming that there is desire to accomplish something, this might be an opportunity for the middle-of-the-road crowd to assert itself.

Manufacturers have an interest in three issues that are also considered potential areas of focus for Congress and the president. The first is trade. There are rumblings that this election may have cleared some room for progress on the Trans-Pacific Partnership and the new pact with Europe. The second area in which progress might be made is infrastructure development. Fewer budget hawks that would oppose any spending at all and more pragmatists that want to see some progress as far as hiring is concerned could lead to some new big projects and repair efforts. The third area for potential cooperation would be tax reform, as nobody sees the current system as anything but broken. The problem is that the two parties are miles apart regarding the shape of that reform.

The other big issues as far as manufacturing is concerned will not lend themselves to interparty comity. The Bank Reform Act has been a major drain on the availability of capital for manufacturing growth, and most manufacturers want to see some changes in the Affordable Care Act. Unfortunately, these are both sacred cows to the Democrats and changes will be bitterly opposed. In addition, any real progress on the issue of immigration is unlikely as the two sides have very different sets of priorities.
