Congratulations!

Today is a day of change.

You’ve got more value-added offerings!

Don’t let it make you deranged!

You have machining in the front.

You have painting in the back.

You have to keep those eyes peeled to keep everything on track.

You’re a one-stop shop.

And everyone should know that behind that smile, you are about to blow.

I

thought about Dr. Seuss’ "Oh, the Places You’ll Go!" more than a couple of times in December after several conversations with metal fabricators. Each had a separate story, as is normally the case, but they all had one thing in common: They had added, or were thinking about adding, to their roster of capabilities.

The motivations are pretty clear in moments when fab shops are looking to add services such as machining or powder coating. There are always concerns about lead times. Just because a metal fabricator’s main customer needs something in a week doesn’t mean that a custom coater can turn the job around that quickly. That powder coater has other clients that expect their finished parts to be delivered on time, and the metal fabricator more than likely is going to have to wait until openings become available on the line.

Fabricators also are worried about parts being transported to and from contract partners. Even if all parties are made aware of the need to keep parts from being scratched or dented, the likelihood of damage is great because so much handling is occurring before, during, and after transit. Scrapping and reworking parts are headaches that should be avoided if possible.

But perhaps the No. 1 reason that a shop looks to add a new value-add service is because a customer asks for it. Companies aren’t really interested in having a bloated supply base, so if they can find one fab shop to deliver a finished part or assembly, they’ll jump at the chance.

There’s also the fact that metal fabricators have a hard time saying "no." Most like a challenge and understand that a happy customer is probably going to be a repeat customer.

Dynamic Metal Innovation, Emmaus, Pa., and Ludlow Manufacturing, Waukegan, Ill., both have added machining capabilities to their typical fabricating offerings. Their journeys are covered in "More fab shops open the doors to machining" in this issue.

"If you want to play in our space and not just be a fabricator that just laser-cuts, forms, and does a little bit of welding, [machining] gives you something different that a lot of other shops don’t do," said Todd Ludlow, founder and CEO, Ludlow Manufacturing.

Ludlow has turned a small activity into a key part of his business. He said it’s helped his company take on more work from existing customers, supercharging sales. Without the machining option, he said, Ludlow Manufacturing would have likely seen growth cut by about two-thirds.

— Todd Ludlow, founder and CEO, Ludlow Manufacturing

Dynamic Metal Innovations jumped back into machining in 2025, after having dabbled with it shortly after opening in 2019. Today, machining represents about 20% of the company’s overall revenue, and Scott Hartrick, founder and president, said he expects that to grow in the coming years.

Adding machining seems to have really paid off for both shops, but getting to this point wasn’t easy. Success in the area for both companies didn’t really occur until they found the right professionals to lead the charge.

"It’s good to be going in the right direction, but I think in a nutshell that the success of a machine shop in any sheet metal shop is dependent on the people you put in place for it," Hartrick said. "You can have rows of DMG Moris and if you don’t have the right people running them, you have a lot of half-million-dollar paperweights."

Simply stated, machine shops and metal fabricating shops have remained distinct types of manufacturing businesses for a reason. The disciplines both involve metal, and that’s about where the similarities end.

"I have a newfound respect for well-oiled machine shops, no pun intended," Hartrick said. "They are just humming along, making parts and running efficiently.

"It’s really hard, and knowing what I know now, I’m really impressed by shops that can do that."

Despite the learning curve, fab shops are going to push forward, taking on new processes in the name of customer satisfaction, and the requests will keep coming. Customers tend to put metal fabricators in uncomfortable positions because shops routinely deliver on requests—no matter how outrageous they might be.

Customers don’t really understand or care about the challenges fab shops face when adding capabilities like machining or powder coating. But they are more than willing to put their money behind those shops that can figure it out.
