n late December, the U.S. Department of Labor announced the launch of its American Manufacturing Apprenticeship Incentive Fund, designed through a cooperative agreement with Arkansas, to support expansion of advanced manufacturing registered apprenticeships nationwide.

The department’s $35.8 million cooperative agreement with the Arkansas Department of Commerce’s Division of Workforce Services will incentivize employers nationwide to develop, expand, or join existing advanced manufacturing registered apprenticeship programs through a pay-for-performance model. Through the program, employers will receive $3,500 for each new apprentice hired.

Administered by the Employment and Training Administration, the American Manufacturing Apprenticeship Incentive Fund supports America’s Talent Strategy, as well as several of President Donald Trump’s executive orders related to expanding registered apprenticeships.

Later in the same month, the DoL announced the availability of $98 million in funding to support the development of pre-apprenticeships in high-demand industries including construction, advanced manufacturing, information technology, and health care. Funded through the department’s Youth-Build program, the grants will help provide academic support, occupational skills training, and employment services to young people, ages 16 to 24, in communities where many youths are not participating in the labor force.

In early January, the DoL also released a forecast notice announcing the upcoming availability of $145 million in funding to support an incentive payments program to further expand the national apprenticeship system. The initiative is built on a pay-for-performance model, ensuring taxpayer dollars deliver measurable outcomes.

The DoL will award up to five cooperative agreements for a four-year period of performance focusing on the expansion of newly developed registered apprenticeships, as well as the growth of existing programs across industries. The program also will place an emphasis on incentivizing industries with a well-established registered apprenticeship program infrastructure.

USMCA UP FOR RENEWAL; MEXICO TARGETS CHINA

The U.S.-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) undergoes its first mandatory review in July 2026. Representatives of the three countries are required to state their USMCA intent—whether they would like to alter or extend the agreement—by July 1.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer told lawmakers in December that the U.S. will renew the USMCA if key issues are resolved. Those issues include:

Mexican labor rights.

The U.S. trade deficit with Canada and Mexico.

Disputes over energy, dairy, technology, rules of origin, and critical minerals.

Trump has indicated that he would like bilaterial talks with Mexico and Canada to replace the multilateral approach. Greer later added that the bilaterial approach might come to fruition if trilateral resolution cannot be achieved.

If the three countries can decide to move forward with an altered USMCA agreement, it’ll be extended for another 16 years. If the parties do not agree to a long-term extension, annual reviews occur until a final decision is made. The agreement automatically terminates in July 2036 if there is no decision to extend it.

Meanwhile, Mexico’s legislature approved tariffs on 1,463 imports from China and other countries. The tariffs, ranging in scale from 5% to 50%, took effect on Jan. 1.

The government cited several reasons for the tariffs:

Protect domestic industry

Respond to import surges

Align with strategic partners, including the U.S., ahead of USMCA review

Sectors targeted by the tariffs include automotive parts, electronics, appliances, textiles, metals, plastics, and footwear.

AGENCY WORKS TO CUT RED TAPE FOR SMALL BUSINESSES

In mid-December, the U.S. Small Business Administration announced it has approved its first round of 7(a) Manufacturers’ Access to Revolving Credit (MARC) loans—already delivering $3.5 million in working capital to four manufacturers. The loan program, launched just before the federal shutdown in late 2025, is the first-ever SBA loan offering dedicated to manufacturers, designed to provide maximum flexibility and minimal red tape for small businesses in NAICS 31-33.

The first MARC loans approved demonstrate the wide range of working capital needs that the product was engineered to support, from a $1.5 million line of credit for a welding and fabrication firm to a $250,000 facility for a porcelain enamel manufacturer. These initial transactions were supported by community and regional banks that deployed the MARC loan program to expand their working capital solutions and support local manufacturers.

The MARC program complements the SBA’s core 7(a) and 504 loan programs, providing a flexible new line of credit to manufacturers and lenders. MARC loans can be used in combination with SBA and conventional commercial loans.
