# Innovative apprenticeship programme

[TARİH: 01.01.2020 ISMR]

THERE'S A CRISIS facing the manufacturing industry; the lack of skilled workers. To help combat this challenge locally, PMF Industries, a U.S. metal fabricator specialising in flowforming combined with other manufacturing processes, has established a ground-breaking apprenticeship programme.

Working closely with the Pennsylvania Department of Labour, Keystone Development Partnership and Central PA Workforce Development Council, PMF developed a strong, innovative, state-approved Computer Numerical Control (CNC) programme that is discovering – and then delivering – exactly what skilled workers are looking for in today's career opportunities.

"With an ageing workforce, we knew that if we didn't think outside the box and use innovative ideas to attract new skilled workers, we'd be in serious trouble," said PMF President & General Manager, John Perrotto. “We were firm on the idea of establishing an apprenticeship programme. But the bigger question was, how would it work? Then we had a breakthrough."

PMF realized that the key was to truly understand the interests and behaviours of their target audience – younger workers. Focused on this, they started to formulate a strategy around the things they knew would resonate with younger workers who might have a stigma against a career in manufacturing: technology, growth opportunity, no student loan debt and more.

Armed with this insight, PMF developed an apprenticeship consortium with five local high schools that offer Career & Technology Centre (CTC) programmes: Williamsport High School; Jersey Shore High School; Keystone Central; Sun Area Tech in New Berlin and CPI in Bellefonte. Other Williamsport area manufacturers have since joined the effort.

"Currently, eight apprentices are actively participating in the CNC apprenticeship programme. These young people are earning while learning, and the educational aspect doesn't cost them a dime. The three-year programme includes 2000 hours of paid, on-the-job training per year; 144 hours (one evening per week) of classroom instruction per year and no educational costs for the student. Upon completion of the state-certified programme, the apprentices earn three years of bonified work experience and a good wage with great benefits. There is no student debt and a state-certified Journeyman designation, explained PMF Industries.

"This programme offers quite a positive leap into adulthood," added PMF Executive Vice President & Director of Engineering, Ken Healy. "We are creating viable career opportunities for young workers who have determined that college is not in their plans, and we're attracting the new workers we wanted. It's a real win-win."

As a flowforming specialist, PMF can produce component shapes beyond the means of conventional metal forming at, it says, a more economical price. It can make more than just traditional cones and cylinders and can form one-piece component shapes. It takes its customers from design through to the finished product■
