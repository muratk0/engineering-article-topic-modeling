# HR Managers and Technology Educators will Meet to Tackle Manufacturing Skilled-Labor Crisis

[TARİH: 01.05.2015 The Fabricator]

The most recent study by Deloitte and The Manufacturing Institute on the skilled-labor shortage shows that over the next decade, 3.5 million manufacturing jobs will be needed and 2 million will likely go unfilled due to lack of qualified candidates. This crisis will affect every manufacturer in the U.S. and their ability to sustain and grow their business.

The upcoming 4th annual FMA T.E.A.M. Summit will bring together technology educators from community and technical colleges and HR and operations personnel from manufacturing to explore "out-of-the-box" ideas on how to reduce this labor shortage. The conference will be June 3-5 at Manchester Community College in Manchester, N.H.

Manchester was selected as this year’s conference site because it offers locations and speakers to inspire nontraditional thinking and problemsolving. The opening keynote event will be held at the headquarters of the FIRST Robotics program. The keynote speaker will be Don Bossi, president of FIRST, whose message will inspire attendees to look outside the accepted practices for recruiting and training. The group will tour FIRST Place and the SEE Science Center, both of which, like FIRST, were founded by inventor and manufacturer Dean Kamen.

A second tour will take attendees to Dyn, a cloud-based Internet performance company. The company faces similar challenges in hiring, training, and retaining employees as those faced in manufacturing. Honored by Inc. and Business New Hampshire magazines, Dyn is the fastest- growing small business in the state. T.E.A.M. Summit participants will hear how this company has approached its skilled-labor shortage and view the results.

A final tour will take attendees to Hypertherm’s newest production facility and technical training center, where machine operators are trained for companies throughout the state.

Other sessions include presentations by Jason Scales, welding educator for Lincoln Electric, and Fred Young, CEO of Forest City Gear.
