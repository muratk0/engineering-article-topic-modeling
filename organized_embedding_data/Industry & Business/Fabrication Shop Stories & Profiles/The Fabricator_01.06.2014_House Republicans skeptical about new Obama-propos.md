# House Republicans skeptical about new Obama-proposed training programs

[TARİH: 01.06.2014 The Fabricator]

AROUND WASHINGTON

$500 million apprenticeship program likely to be rejected

By Stephen Barlas, Contributing Writer

Republicans in the House are unlikely to approve new job training programs President Obama wants to fund in his proposed fiscal 2015 budget (starts Oct. 1). That was the message key GOPers on the House Education and the Workforce Committee sent Labor Sec. Thomas Perez at a hearing recently. The president’s fiscal 2015 budget proposal includes more than $10 billion for six new job training programs. Among them are a $500 million apprenticeship program that appears to be part of a new $1.5 billion Community College Job-Driven Training Fund. It would replace the longstanding Trade Adjustment Assistance Community College and Career Training grant program, which has been funded at $500 million a year for the last four years.

There is a crying need for apprenticeship programs in the metalworking industry, of course. The National Institute for Metalworking Skills (NIMS) already has obtained Department of Labor (DOL) certification for an apprenticeship program. Jim Wall, the executive director of NIMS, said he gets calls every week from companies wanting to start programs. But because metalworking companies are small and medium-sized, they need some sort of regional effort in which they can share costs and resources, such as the apprenticeship program in Pittsburgh organized by the National Tooling and Machining Association (NTMA).

The NTMA contracts administration of that apprenticeship program to New Century Careers in Pittsburgh. Paul Anselmo, executive director at New Century, said 60 companies participate in his apprenticeship program, which currently is training 150 apprentices. A few companies get a dribble of federal funding from the DOL’s H-1B grant program, which is focused on immigrants. But New Century itself gets no federal funding, nor do most of the companies.

Anselmo believes federal funding could help him double the size of the machining apprenticeship program. To do that, however, he said he would have to spend considerable time educating potential users—again, mostly small and medium-size businesses that are focused on getting work in and out of their shops and not on learning how apprenticeship programs can help them. In addition, many of those target companies aren’t eager to have the federal government involved in their business. “There is some hand-holding that would be needed to be done,” he said, “and it wouldn’t hurt to have federal funding to do that with.”

However, it appears unlikely that House Republicans will approve a $500 million appropriation for fiscal 2015 for Obama’s new apprenticeship program.

“The president wants to pile more training programs onto the more than 50 duplicative and ineffective programs that already exist, making a confusing maze of programs even more difficult for workers to navigate,” said Rep. John Kline, R-Minn., chairman of the House Education and the Workforce Committee.

The Government Accountability Office has issued repeated reports criticizing the maze of overlapping DOL training programs. Obama often has talked about consolidating them so they work better. With regard to the apprenticeship program, Perez argued, “We are taking it to scale.” But it is unclear what existing DOL apprenticeship grant programs currently exist and are being scaled up. The press office at the DOL’s Employment and Training Administration did not return a phone call.

The Obama administration already has announced a $100 million apprenticeship program in the current fiscal 2014. That will be funded out of reprogrammed DOL funds already approved by Congress. That $100 million is coming out of funds originally designated for the H-1B technical skills training grants program
