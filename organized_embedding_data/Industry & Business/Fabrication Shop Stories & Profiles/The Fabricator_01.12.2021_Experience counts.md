# Experience counts

[TARİH: 01.12.2021 The Fabricator]

From The Editor

Structural steel fabricators who know what’s happening in the shop and at the site are valuable resources

Dan Davis, Editor-in-Chief

dand@thefabricator.com

I

n an age when the structural steel fabrication industry is more frequently embracing advanced technologies, such as augmented reality inspection technologies and automated beam welding lines, are oldtimers being squeezed out? Maybe. But it’s probably their own choice.

Technology advancement continually leaves generations behind. For my dad, it was with the advent of the mobile phone. He has one, but he uses it the way the name of the device suggests: engaging in phone conversations. He has no desire to access the computing functions, even though smartphones now can be directed by voice. (It makes no sense to me that he spent a lifetime cramming newspaper clippings of sports facts in a wallet to win a future debate, but he frowns on the ability to access a mobile computing device to access any sport-related fact he could ever think of.) He stopped trying to stay current.

That’s the choice. You keep moving forward or you check out. It’s not really a right or wrong decision, but if you want to remain employable, you really need to embrace change, even if it’s at a slower pace than those just entering the structural steel industry.

It’s also important because the industry needs experienced fabricating professionals. They know how the world works beyond the computer terminal.

Read "Detailing the steel detailer’s rise to prominence" (p. 5) to find out why Ron Palma of Vancouver Community College took his students on a field trip to a new Amazon facility being built in downtown Vancouver, B.C. He wanted the students to see the scope of the project, observe how things were joined together, and learn the necessity for structural steel to be fabricated correctly so it won’t disrupt the construction process. There’s a lot riding on these projects to keep them on schedule and within budget. Experienced personnel in the fab shop can make that happen.

Justin Auciello, president, Auciello Iron Works, featured in "A dying church no longer" (p. 12), praises the young people who have joined his family’s company in recent years, but he also acknowledges that long-timers helped him make the transition when he joined the business in 2010. He didn’t have a background in handrail manufacturing, but those that had developed the fabricating chops over the years kept things moving.

These fabricating veterans also are incredibly helpful in providing an education that simply can’t be replicated in a vocational classroom. A recent story in

focused on two generations of press brake operators in the same fab shop. The two older operators went to a vocational school where they learned the basics of sheet metal fabrication, including how metal is formed, and later put that training to work on the shop floor. One of the operator’s sons joined the same company more than 10 years ago, but he didn’t come in with the same educational background. That’s where the two experienced brake operators filled the gap. They caught him up on some of the basics of bending and worked with the older press brakes.

Ironically, the son is sometimes now the educator when it comes to working with the company’s newer press brake with its slick touchscreen control. His two older colleagues have learned enough to work with the new equipment, but they certainly recognize that their younger colleague has the expertise when it comes to the latest CNC devices. Those experienced operators are both valuable and relevant. That’s the whole point of this discussion.

Fabricators embrace chaos every day. New technology is there to help. Just give it a chance.
