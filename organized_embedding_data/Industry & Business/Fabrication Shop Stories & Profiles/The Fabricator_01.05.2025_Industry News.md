Study reveals PPE management generally strong, yet compliance issues persist

J. J. Keller & Associates Inc. and the International Safety Equipment Association recently joined forces to conduct a comprehensive survey of those responsible for purchasing and/or managing PPE across a range of industries about their top challenges.

"With the study, we examined PPE program development, purchase, training, and compliance," said Bob Larsen, vice president of research & development with J. J. Keller. "We believe that understanding this 360-degree view of PPE pain points over time is important to continuous improvement as an industry."

The resulting report, "2025 PPE Pain Points Study: Top Trends and Challenges," reveals that 95% of survey respondents completely or somewhat have the time and resources needed to assess PPE needs properly, and 70% talk with their employees regularly about PPE.

However, more than 75% of respondents reported that at least some of the time, they have difficulty getting employees to wear PPE. More than one-third of respondents prioritize durability, followed by comfort, emphasizing the need for PPE that workers can and will consistently wear. Nearly half of respondents struggle with training employees on critical PPE usage aspects, including when PPE is necessary, how to properly wear it, and its limitations.

While 75% of respondents have not experienced problems buying PPE in the sizes they need, those who have had issues find difficulty locating larger and smaller sizes. And although more companies are prioritizing PPE designed for women, nearly one-third of respondents still face challenges in finding properly fitting options.

"Safety professionals are committed to protecting workers, but this research shows that persistent challenges remain—especially in compliance and training," said Cam Mackey, president/CEO of ISEA. "By leveraging these insights, safety managers, manufacturers, and industry leaders can take meaningful steps to improve PPE programs, ensuring workers have access to properly fitting, high-quality PPE and are consistently trained to use it correctly."

Lexicon expanding headquarters, fabrication operations in Arkansas

Lexicon, a provider of construction management, steel fabrication, erection, mechanical installation, and plant maintenance for heavy industrial, commercial, and bridge projects, has announced plans to expand its corporate headquarters and steel fabrication operations in Little Rock, Ark. The $37.6 million project is expected to create 60 jobs.

The two-year, four-phase expansion will include adding 5,000 sq. ft. to the corporate office, remodeling a separate 40,000-sq.-ft. facility, building a new 6,165-sq.-ft. production facility, and expanding an existing fabrication facility by about 46,000 sq. ft.

HSG Laser names North America president

HSG Laser, a provider of 2D and 3D high-power lasers, as well as bending, automation, welding, and software systems, has appointed Rakesh Kumar as its new North America president.

Kumar has more than 30 years of experience in the U.S. fabrication market, leading teams in manufacturing, sales, parts, and service, as well as with products. He has previously served as senior vice president and general manager at Cincinnati Incorporated and as general manager sales at AMADA AMERICA.

Allied Steel Buildings installs Kaltenbach machines at new Texas facility

Allied Steel Buildings, a provider of prefabricated metal building projects for the commercial, manufacturing, and cold storage markets, has installed three Kaltenbach machines at its new manufacturing facility in McGregor, Texas.

Allied uses the 3D Blaster 1508 shot blaster, the Eco Blaster 2506 shot blaster, and the Top Coating 2512 painting system from Kaltenbach to preclean raw steel, apply protective treatments to fabricated components, and deliver the Allied Blue finish coat.

Kalamazoo Outdoor Gourmet to relocate manufacturing operations

Following a merger with Mon-treal-based Urban Bonfire, Kalamazoo Outdoor Gourmet has announced it will relocate its manufacturing operations currently in Montreal to Kalamazoo, Mich. This relocation requires an expansion at its current Kalamazoo facility to accommodate the new production lines. The $3.8 million project will create 22 new jobs.

Kalamazoo Outdoor Gourmet is a manufacturer of appliances and cabinetry for outdoor cooking and entertaining, producing customized modular outdoor kitchen systems.

Vulcan Tool earns ISO 9001:2015 and SDVOB certification

Vulcan Tool Co., Dayton, Ohio, has announced that its management system has been assessed and certified to meet the requirements of ISO 9001:2015 for engineering services, precision machining, welding and fabrication, machine repair, and machine and die building. The company also has been certified as a Service-Disabled Veteran-Owned Business by the National Veteran Business Development Council.

Vulcan Tool Co. offers precision machining, welding and fabrication, machine repair, and custom design and build services. It also manufactures tube cutting machines, dies for trimming deep-drawn parts, end trimming machines, and a line of air spindles for high-RPM applications.
