The U.S. Department of Commerce has named one of its first manufacturing communities—and it happens to be in President Obama’s hometown of Chicago.

Actually, Chicago—or Chicago’s Metro Metal Consortium Manufacturing Community, as it is formally known in this federal program—is one metalworking community among the dozen the DOC announced as part of its Investing in Manufacturing Communities Partnership (IMCP) initiative. The program’s objective is to accelerate the resurgence of manufacturing in communities nationwide by supporting the development of long-term economic development strategies that help communities attract and expand private investment in the manufacturing sector and increase international trade and exports. The other 11 communities are: Southwest Alabama; New York Finger Lakes region; Southern California; Southwestern Ohio; Northwest Georgia; The Tennessee Valley; South Kansas; Washington Puget Sound region; Greater Portland, Maine, area; Greater Milwaukee area; and Southeastern Michigan.

The Commerce Department’s Economic Development Administration (EDA) is running the new program. Its announcement was made simultaneously with the appointment of a new administrator of the EDA, Jay Williams. He worked in the Obama White House for a time and prior to that was the mayor of Youngstown, Ohio, from 2006 to 2011.

But exactly what the 12 communities will receive in the way of federal help is up in the air. Each regional entity will get a designated federal liaison at 11 federal agencies, all of which have economic assistance grants to hand out. But there is no guarantee that any of the 12 communities will get any of those funds, which the Commerce Department estimates to be worth $1.3 billion.

Sec. of Commerce Penny Pritzker announced the first 12 communities on May 28. Seventy communities applied to the program. They had to submit manufacturing resurgence plans and compete with other cities before being named as one of the first participants. More specifically, the communities had to demonstrate the significance of manufacturing already present in their region and develop strategies to make investments in six areas:

1. Workforce and training

2. Advanced research

3. Infrastructure and site development

4. Supply chain support

5. Trade and international investment

6. Operational improvement and capital access

The grants available through the IMCP are mostly promising to upgrade worker training efforts, in one way or another. However, many of those efforts are already underway, so critics of the program question what this initiative will be doing

additionally

.

Chicago’s “manufacturing community” is different from the other geographic areas in that local manufacturers are mainly focused on general metalworking. The other areas are dominated by either aircraft or automotive manufacturing.

Chicago’s Metro Metal Consortium Manufacturing Community will be led by the Alliance for Illinois Manufacturing and Illinois Manufacturing Excellence Center (IMEC). They will work with metal manufacturers to assess business operational capability and identify key areas for improvement. Sustainability efforts will be coordinated by the Cook County Department of Environmental Control with participation from Illinois Sustainable Technology Center and Elevate Energy. The region has more than 3,700 firms in the metals industry and supply chains that employ more than 100,000 people and generate more than $30 billion in revenues.

David Boulay, IMEC president, noted that Cook County is taking the lead in pulling all the Chicago metalworking participants together.

“They have assembled a great team,” he said. “But how this plays out … well, that is always a matter of execution.”

While he hopes that the designation as a manufacturing community will help the Chicago metals economy get more federal resources, Boulay said the local manufacturing economy will benefit even if those funds never arrive.

“… [I]f all the designation does is help us focus existing resources, that is an excellent path in the right direction,” Boulay added.

Later this year the Obama administration plans to launch a second IMCP competition to designate additional communities and convene the 70 communities that applied for designation to share best practices in economic development planning.
