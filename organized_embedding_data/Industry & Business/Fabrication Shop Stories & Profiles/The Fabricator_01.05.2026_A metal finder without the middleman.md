# A metal finder without the middleman

[TARİH: 01.05.2026 The Fabricator]

Technology Spotlight

A metal distributor turns an internal tool into an industrywide sourcing platform

By

Dan Davis

Unlike other sourcing platforms that act as a mediary between the buyer and the seller, Sparky’s Source Finder was designed to put buyers in direct connection with suppliers from all over the U.S. Buyers can search more than 700 metal suppliers by material type, shape and style, and grade.

A

s the third generation of his family to be a part of the metal supply business, Greg Nolan wanted to make his own mark in the industry, and he’s doing that by turning what was once an internal metal sourcing tool into an industrywide platform for all to use.

Nolan’s grandfather Jack co-founded Murphy & Nolan, a metal distribution business, in 1953 in upstate New York. John, Jack’s son and Greg’s father, joined the business in the early 1970s and eventually formed his own company, Nolan Supply Corp., in 1976. Over the years, John explored ways to minimize inventories, instead relying on a pool of metal partners to provide the materials, which allowed him to pass the cost savings to his metal-buying customers. Today, Sparky’s Metal Supply carries on that supply approach as an internet-based metal sourcing firm.

The move from a brick-and-mortar metal supplier to an online business is a big transition. That didn’t stop Greg from thinking there was room for improvement.

"Over the years, I’ve watched from the outside and saw the inefficiencies in the business, not just in in the way he did things, but in the industry as a whole. So I’ve have been trying to find ways to modernize it," he said.

One of the main things Greg thought might make sense was a digital tool that would help his father source metals more responsively and efficiently. If proven worthwhile, the tool then possibly could be sold to other businesses.

But as he built the internal database and investigated how people actually went about purchasing steel and other metals, Greg found it to be very inefficient. Buyers might do a little bit of research, but they tended to rely on their small pool of sources. If they wanted to increase that contact list or had to search outside their familiar circle for something like a specialty alloy, they had to rely on general internet searches or third parties that acted as middlemen between the entity sending out the RFQ and those metal providers responding. And that didn’t take into account the need to follow up with each of the new potential sources, which aren’t even vetted in most cases, with individual communications.

The internal tool—a database filled with vetted supplier contacts and their offerings, including material type, size, and format—grew very useful for Sparky’s Metal Supply. Other supply houses, which happened to be part of the extended Nolan family, thought similarly.

"Once I finished building it, we had family members that are also in the industry with their own businesses start using it," Greg said. "It quickly became very clear that this would be valuable to anyone who’s buying and selling online."

Now Sparky’s Metal Source Finder (

www.sourcefinder.us

) is available to most of the metal-buying public in the U.S. (Source Finder is not currently available to companies based in New York because of an existing noncompete restriction that remains in effect through September 2027.)

WHAT CAN IT DO?

A visitor to the Source Finder website will find 700 metal suppliers. Most of the companies, according to Greg, have been longtime suppliers to Sparky’s Metal Supply. Others have been sought out and vetted to ensure that they are legit metal providers, something that is not necessarily the case for some online sourcing platforms that only require a website and proof of being a business of some sort.

All of the metal suppliers are reviewed on a quarterly basis to ensure that the contacts are current. Greg said that email updates, sent in by users or people from the listed companies, act as a crowdsourced quality check as well.

He added that suppliers on the website are not charged for inclusion in the database. That’s a major difference from other sourcing platforms, on which metal buyers have access only to those suppliers that have paid to be showcased on that platform.

To get started, a buyer visits Source Finder and begins a search by supplying information for any of these categories: material type, shape/style, and grade. The database covers 48 material categories, more than 90 shapes and styles, and more than 1,000 grades.

When a list of relevant suppliers has been identified, the buyer can choose sources to contact and quickly generate a ready-to-send RFQ within the user’s native email environment. No software installation or change in communications workflow is needed, according to Greg.

The website also includes an AI-powered agent, called SCOUT, that will go beyond the Source Finder database and scour the internet for those searches involving hard-to-find materials or specialized grades. Greg said that SCOUT evaluates potential matches against the user’s specific RFQ inputs and assigns a confidence score to help indicate likely fit.

Human expertise is available as well. The Material Expert Helpdesk can be used when buyers run into obstacles searching for a specific material type, have technical questions, or need guidance on possible material substitutions.

Greg said that the most obvious benefit with Source Finder is the time saved in sending out RFQs. But when asked if such a tool can devalue long-term relationships between some metal fabricating shops and metal service centers, he responded by saying that it also can be used to see if those supply chain partnerships stand up to real world metals pricing. It’s a matter of doing a simple search to see what type of disparities in pricing might exist in the marketplace.

"For example, I can do a search for 4140 [medium-carbon chromium-molybdenum alloy steel], and I see a quote for $7,600 and another for $6,600. There’s the savings right there, and maybe the legacy guy might have been that $6,600," he said. "At least you know you are on the low side of the range."

TRYING IT OUT

Metal buyers can try Source Finder for 30 days with unlimited access without logging in credit card information. If after those 30 days, if the buyer wants to continue to use the platform, three membership tiers are available: Basic, which gives the user 10 searches a month; Pro, with its 50 searches a month; and Unlimited, which provides unlimited searches, multiple accounts, SCOUT usage, and access to the Material Expert Helpdesk.

"It’s been really interesting to have that kind of validation we’ve seen with users," Greg said. "We saw this pain point. We experienced it firsthand, and we built something to solve it for ourselves. I thought other people would like this as well."
