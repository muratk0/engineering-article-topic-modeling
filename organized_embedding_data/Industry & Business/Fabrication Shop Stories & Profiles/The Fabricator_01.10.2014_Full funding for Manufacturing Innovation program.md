# Full funding for Manufacturing Innovation program inches closer to reality

AROUND WASHINGTON

Now the Manufacturing Extension Partnership gets a close look

By Stephen Barlas, Contributing Writer

There looks to be a good chance that Congress finally will pass legislation authorizing a program to build a national network of Manufacturing Innovation Institutes.

The Obama administration has created four institutes: America Makes (founded as the National Additive Manufacturing Innovation Institute), Youngstown, Ohio; Next Generation Power Electronics Manufacturing Innovation Institute, Raleigh, N.C.; Digital Manufacturing and Design Innovation Institute, Chicago; and the Lightweight and Modern Metals Manufacturing Innovation Institute, Detroit. They have been put together in a kind of piecemeal fashion, carving limited funding out of the existing budgets of various federal agencies, primarily the Department of Defense. Despite President Obama’s rhetorical support in the past few years for a centralized, national program with dedicated funding, Congress has declined to respond. That may now change.

Bills establishing a national program probably will be going to the floors of both the House and Senate this fall now that the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee has passed its version of the Revitalize American Manufacturing and Innovation (RAMI) Act (H.R. 2996). The committee passed the bill in late July—and by a voice vote, meaning it was noncontroversial.

The Senate Commerce, Science, and Transportation Committee passed its version (S. 1468) in April.

The RAMI Act would direct the secretary of commerce to support the establishment of institutes across the country dedicated to improving U.S. competitiveness in manufacturing, increasing domestic production, and accelerating the development of an advanced manufacturing workforce. Each institute will be a public-private partnership between industry, academia, and other relevant entities. They will be required to focus on a specific and unique manufacturing process, technology, or methodology.

Both the House and Senate bills authorize a Manufacturing Innovation Institute program within the Commerce Department’s National Institute of Standards and Technology.

Both the House and Senate bills authorize a Manufacturing Innovation Institute program within the Commerce Department’s National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) and allow for a one-time $600 million appropriation. The funding will occur only if both the appropriations committees in both houses vote to provide that $600 million or some reduced figure.

The House already has passed the Commerce Department appropriations bill for fiscal 2015, which starts Oct. 1. That bill did not include a $600 million appropriation. Likewise, the Senate Appropriations Committee has not provided $600 million, although it has appropriated nearly $100 million for three institutes to be run out of the Department of Energy (DOE). For example, the DOE has committed to spend $70 million in the next five years to fund the Next Generation Power Electronics Manufacturing Innovation Institute.

Currently the four existing institutes are being funded with a smidgen of NIST money, which apparently was taken out of the budget for the agency’s Industrial Technology Services program. That shifting of funds didn’t sit well with the House Appropriations Committee. In the report accompanying its fiscal 2015 Commerce appropriations bill, the committee slapped NIST’s wrist by saying: “The Committee is aware that prior year funds under this account have been used for NIST contributions to interagency support for Manufacturing Innovation Institutes. Any such contributions in fiscal year 2015 shall be proposed as a reprogramming of funds under the procedures in section 505 of this Act.”

“This is all pro forma and should not be construed as an indication of displeasure,” NIST spokesman Mark Bello said in reference to the report’s comment.

Almost all of the NIST’s Industrial Technology Services budget goes to the Hollings Manufacturing Extension Partnership (MEP) program, which provides funds to state offices dispensing technical help to small and medium-size manufacturers. But questions have emerged about how much of the congressional appropriation—the House would fund the MEP at $130 million in fiscal 2015—the NIST is spending on administrative overhead.

The same report accompanying the 2015 appropriations bill stated: “The Committee is aware of recent efforts by MEP to examine ways to reduce administrative costs and provide more direct assistance to the Centers. Accordingly, MEP shall provide a report within 60 days of enactment of this Act detailing the amount of funds to be maintained at headquarters and the uses of those funds.”
