NBT Inclusion Camp connects campers with manufacturing work possibilities

If FMA’s Nuts, Bolts & Thingamajigs foundation wanted an ideal partner that aligned with its mission to enhance the manufacturing industry and students’ skills, it would be hard pressed to find a better candidate than Valley Packaging Industries.

VPI is truly unique in its hometown of Appleton, Wis., starting with the ".org" suffix on its web address. The 68-year-old repackaging company’s mission is "to promote the dignity and worth of individuals who have disabilities or are disadvantaged and to assist them in developing their optimum level of social, vocational and economic independence in the community."

VPI does that through its core business—repackaging and assembly services for many kids of clients. One major service is assembling corrugated cardboard point-ofpurchase displays for retailers. Others include shrink-wrapping, labeling, and bag packaging. And it does this with a workforce overwhelmingly comprised of people with some form of disability.

It makes sense, then, that this is the third year in a row that VPI has hosted an NBT Inclusion Camp, a four-week introduction to manufacturing and job skills that exposes disabled people in the Fox Valley to multiple jobs at the company’s 400,000-sq.-ft. facility. Time at camp includes classroom sessions, working on the assembly floor, and also visits to nearby manufacturers that give campers a look at other future work possibilities.

One day near the end of this year’s camp, participants on one side of the floor were repacking individual oatmeal packets for a large retailer, while students on the other side were creating point-of-purchase boxes for another.

"Four weeks of camp really gives you a good sense of what they’re good at, what they like, and how we can help them advance that," VPI CEO Tim Riebau said. "Day one, it’s like the first day of school. They’re … a little bit scared, not really understanding what they or their parents signed them up for. And really, by the end of camp, as you see them on the production floor, they’re more confident in the jobs they’re doing. They’re communicating better on the production floor.

Some campers indeed do become VPI employees, and for many, it’s the first time they’ve ever had a job or a paycheck.

One high school student at this year’s camp, 16-year-old Gavin Cummings of nearby Kaukauna, Wis., said he found out about the camp from his parents and his county social worker. Over the course of the camp, he did multiple jobs: pallet-jacking and carting boxes of tissue packs, stapling boxes, building box covers, and packaging paper plates and napkins. But there’s one thing he likes best.

"Making the money," Cummings said. "But [aside from] making the money, meeting new people and getting out."

Cummings said he’s already thinking about post-high school life and listed joining the Marine Corps and working as a welder at nearby fire truck maker Pierce Manufacturing.

"I like the hands-on stuff—moving around and doing stuff," he said. "Not like sitting there and waiting, but more being hands-on and moving stuff. That’s what I like to do."

Riebau noted that Pierce is one of several area manufacturers with which VPI collaborates for student tours. He feels that giving students that added exposure to those companies not only has expanded campers’ view of the world, but also expanded those companies’ view of what’s possible for their workforces.

"To me, one of the most critical parts of this is … every week, they have at least one or two tours where they go to other manufacturers in the community," Riebau said. "We coordinate them, and they go through and learn about other manufacturers. So, it really exposes them to these other companies.

"More of these companies now see [students] as recruiting opportunities. They’re doing it for us and these campers, but they’re also doing it for themselves to promote the jobs they have. I think that the transition is happening, and these companies are getting much closer to being able to bring in individuals with a variety of abilities or disabilities and work with them.

Paul Mueller Co. plans $22 million expansion in Missouri Paul Mueller Co., a manufacturer of stainless steel tanks, components, and equipment, will expand in Springfield, Mo., investing $22 million and creating 133 jobs. This first phase of the expansion includes the establishment of a new facility that will increase its capacity for the assembly of large production modules.

The facility will add more than 100,000 sq. ft. of space, including 60,000 sq. ft. of clear-span, climate-controlled production area. It doubles the company’s R&D square footage as well.

ARKU opens contract leveling center in South Carolina

ARKU Inc. has opened a new subsidiary in Greer, S.C. With 1,500 sq. ft. of office space and 13,000 sq. ft. of production, this is the company’s second contract leveling center, with the first in Cincinnati.

Located between Atlanta and Charlotte off I-85, the location includes loading and unloading via the dock or side. Customers in the Southeast can send their parts, sheets, and plates directly to this new facility, which also functions as a showroom for potential machine buyers.

Leveling and stress-relieving services have documented flatness checks showing tolerances as low as 0.01 in. The facility handles materials from 0.012 to 1.10 in. thick and up to 78 in. wide and 150 in. long. Maximum piece weight is 2,000 lbs., and maximum skid weight is 5,000 lbs. There are no minimum quantities.

Honda NCM adds ATV weld and paint operations

Honda North Carolina Manufacturing (NCM) has opened weld and paint operations for ATVs at its Swepsonville, N.C., facility. These improvements are part of a $21.5 million investment that will complete the establishment of NCM as the exclusive manufacturing facility for Honda ATVs in North America, as ATV production transfers there from Honda South Carolina (SCM) in Timmonsville, S.C.

Current NCM ATV production includes the FourTrax series and TRX sport models.

Caster Concepts adds advanced engineering area to Michigan facility

Caster Concepts, a manufacturer of heavy-duty casters and industrial caster wheels, has expanded operations at its Albion, Mich., facility to include a 3,000-sq.-ft. advanced engineering area. This follows a 16,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing expansion in 2023.

"This expansion is all about attracting new engineering talent to the Caster Concepts team," said Bill Dobbins, president. "Our engineers will have even more advanced precision machinery and the newest tools needed to meet the unique challenges our customers bring to us every day."

The new area will include the space and tools needed to develop solutions to unique material handling challenges using 3D solid modeling, EA analysis, rapid prototyping, quality assurance testing (such as dynamometer and static load testing), and design review and collaboration.

Ice Industries to establish roll forming facility for solar industry

Ice Industries Inc., an industrial manufacturer and supplier, announced it is investing $6 million to establish a new production facility in southwest Louisiana. The company’s first venture in the state is expected to begin production in early 2025, initially focused on roll forming steel back rails for photovoltaic solar panels that First Solar Inc. expects to start manufacturing in New Iberia in the second half of 2025.

The facility, expected to create 70 direct jobs, complements a similar operation in Bowling Green, Ohio, that also provides roll formed products to the solar energy market.

In January, First Solar awarded Ice Industries a long-term contract supplying components to its Iberia Parish facility that is expected to create more than 700 direct new jobs in Acadiana.

Ice Industries has six facilities in Ohio, Mississippi, and Mexico that offer manufacturing, stamping, fabrication, welding, and assembly services. The company expects to begin installation of new equipment into the retrofitted 150,000-sq.-ft. facility in October and commence production in early 2025.

Mabin Holdings acquires Martelli Companies

Mabin Holdings has announced its acquisition of Martelli Companies, a provider of metal processing, fabrication, trucking, sandblasting, painting, assembly, installation, and warehousing services. The acquisition increases Mabin Holdings’ capabilities in fabricating, delivering, and installing steel products and in serving a variety of industries, from transportation to construction.

The company says the addition of Martelli Companies to its portfolio, which already includes PennFab, a fabricator of railroad catenary systems, rail stations, and bridges, will make Mabin Holdings the largest steel fabricator on the East Coast.

Co-Line breaks ground on 70,000-sq.-ft. building addition

Co-Line Welding Inc. has begun site preparation for an expansion to its north manufacturing facility in Lynnville, Iowa. This expansion project—the company’s seventh—will give the firm 442,000 sq. ft. total of manufacturing functions in four separate facilities.

With the space constraints in its south fabrication facility, Co-Line plans to relocate numerous fabrication pieces to the new 70,000-sq.-ft. addition, scheduled to be fully enclosed by December.

The job shop offers metal fabrication services including laser cutting, sawing, stamping, bending, manual and robotic welding, machining, paint and powder finishing, and assembly.

Morgan Steel adds machining center

Morgan Steel, a steel service center offering fabricating and processing services, has added a Flex Machine Tools GM-20-06 machining center to its equipment fleet.

The company offers stock carbon steel products and quick-turnaround stainless and aluminum products, as well as flat laser, tube laser, plasma/oxyfuel, sawing, shearing, press brake, CNC machining, fabrication, robotic and manual welding, powder coating, and assembly services.

Nix Industrial relocates coatings operation, acquires Huncilman Sheet Metal Fabrication

Nix Industrial, a fifth-generation family business, has moved its coatings operation adjacent to its custom fabrication and advanced machining operations in Poseyville, Ind. The move doubles the company’s space from 15,000 to 30,000 sq. ft.

Offering services both in the shop and in the field, the custom manufacturing and industrial repair company specializes in metal fabrication, machining and gears, coating and finishing, and industrial products sales.

Nix recently acquired majority ownership interest in Huncilman Sheet Metal Fabrication of New Albany, Ind., also a fifth-generation family-owned business. Huncilman’s primary capabilities include laser cutting, precision forming, welding (including robotic welding), and e-coat painting.

This is Nix Industrial’s largest acquisition to date. Combined, the companies make one of the largest metal fabricating businesses in Indiana, employing more than 200 people.

Yaskawa Motoman expands Ohio headquarters

Robotics manufacturer Yaskawa America Inc., Motoman Robotics Division (Yaskawa Motoman), has completed an expansion to its headquarters in Miamisburg, Ohio. The 185,000-sq.-ft. addition nearly doubles the size of the company’s current production space.

The additional manufacturing space is used to engineer, develop, integrate, and build robotic systems and provide additional space for the retrofit and rebuild of previously deployed robots and systems.

"This expansion represents the first phase of Yaskawa’s planned $200 million investment in the United States to support the company’s growth initiatives and its commitment to driving additional development and production in the Americas," stated Steve Barhorst, president/COO.

The company celebrated the opening of the new addition with an event for employees and their families in early August.
