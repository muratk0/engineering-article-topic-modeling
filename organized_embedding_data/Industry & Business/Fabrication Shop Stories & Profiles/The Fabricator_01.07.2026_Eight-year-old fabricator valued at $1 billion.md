# Eight-year-old fabricator valued at $1 billion

[TARİH: 01.07.2026 The Fabricator]

Tech Talk Caleb Chamberlain

The potential of a transformative business model

N

ews broke in May that SendCutSend, an online custom part manufacturer, raised $110 million at a $1 billion valuation. Those numbers may seem surprising to the manufacturing community at large, given that SendCutSend was founded just eight years ago. I find the announcement intriguing and indicative of a market trend I’ve been writing and speaking about for years.

Full disclosure: I am not completely unbiased on this topic. Those who’ve read my previous articles know that I run a similar company called OSH Cut. Most of our customers would consider SendCutSend to be one of our primary competitors. My perspective is influenced by macro trends and similarities in our service offerings and growth trajectories. CEO Jim Belosic has set out to democratize access to manufacturing services. That resonates with my own professional passion, and this latest round of funding seems to signal that entire industries are ready to embrace this transformative model.

THE JOURNEY TO AN EYE-POPPING CAPITAL RAISE

As reported on the company blog, early on SendCutSend raised $6 million in a "friends and family round," but most of its growth was enabled by reinvesting cash flow. Exact figures aren’t available, but in that same blog post, Andrew Reed, partner at Sequoia, identified the fabricator as a "high-growth, profitable company." And as reported by The Fabricator, Belosic said annual revenues are "well over $150 million." That kind of organic, profitable growth is impressive.

This latest round of funding involves big backers like Sequoia, Paradigm, and Patrick and John Collison (the founders of Stripe, an online payments platform). For context, manufacturing business valuations are usually driven by EBITDA multiples, but investors like Sequoia aren’t in the habit of valuing businesses that way, per se. Sequoia famously funded companies like Apple, Google, YouTube, NVIDIA, Oracle, Stripe, and many others, usually well before they were profitable.

Sequoia invests in companies that disrupt existing markets or create entirely new ones. Through that lens, SendCutSend’s high valuation signals that investors are starting to see a revolution in the manufacturing space. I agree with that assessment, and I’ll explain why.

THE BUSINESS MODEL

The fundamental mission of SendCutSend is to democratize access to manufacturing services. Belosic has said that the secret to a great business is to "just provide an amazing customer experience." For the long tail of small-volume sheet metal buyers, that means responsive quoting, fast service, and good quality. Customers can simply upload a model, place an order, and have it delivered to their door quickly and with minimal friction.

Under the hood, SendCutSend and competitors like OSH Cut use software to make that happen. It begins with a customer-facing web app that provides instant prices, manufacturability feedback, and simple selfserve ordering flows.

Software drives the fulfillment engine as well. Democratizing access to prices and ordering means millions of people can now place orders online, whether they are ordering a single part or thousands. To deal with the high-mix complexity, software automation enables efficient purchasing, inventory management, scheduling, machine programming, process flow, part tracking, shipping, and customer communication.

Software is the through-line in this kind of business, and it requires a new kind of manufacturing company, one where in-house software development is a tightly integrated core competency. Customized software enables a highly curated, systemized, end-to-end experience for buyers—and they love it.

THE SILVER TSUNAMI

Rapid growth has also been enabled by ongoing demographic shifts in the U.S. In a recent publication, McKinsey reported that by 2035, owners of 71,000 manufacturing businesses in the U. S. will be exiting. More than a quarter are either unsure of their succession plans or intend to close their businesses.

An ongoing wave of business closures will create a capacity vacuum, with demand growing but capacity shrinking. And it’s not just lost capacity that creates bottlenecks. As seasoned operators retire, we lose critical experience that isn’t easy to regain.

In spite of the challenge, I called this "the opportunity of a century." If there’s a large market need, great businesses will thrive. This will be a tailwind for every manufacturer that sticks around, but I believe that the new online manufacturing model will scale to absorb a significant percentage of the demand.

Throwing an instant quoting engine online is generally not enough. Quoting is the easy part. For high-mix businesses to work well, they must have manufacturing systems that enable scale, to the tune of tens of thousands of unique SKUs produced every week. A system that can grow to meet that kind of high-mix need is positioned perfectly to absorb demand as traditional shops exit.

Put simply, when customers hunt for new vendors, they’ll gravitate toward fast, easy solutions that can handle the demand. Scalable businesses of all kinds are in a perfect position to fill the capacity vacuum.

RESHAPING MARKETS

We live in the on-demand era. Many young adults in the workforce today never knew a world without online ordering and fast delivery of virtually any product.

Software, the internet, and an aggressive integrator—Amazon—made this on-demand world possible. Amazon paired new technologies with vision and world-spanning physical infrastructure to create an entirely new business model and retail reality.

To date, that hasn’t really happened in manufacturing. Yes, there are a handful of tech-heavy players like Xometry, Proto Labs, and SendCut-Send. But collectively, they account for less than a thousandth of the total manufacturing output of the U. S.

The manufacturing marketplace remains highly fragmented, with tens of thousands of independent manufacturers that aren’t benefiting from scale. Software is now allowing new manufacturers to aggregate volume and benefit from scale economies.

Vertical integration could allow SendCutSend and others to restructure the supply chain, eliminating middlemen, passing savings on to customers, and creating a scale economic moat that enables even more growth and less competitive pressure. If investors think SendCutSend is beginning a new wave of manufacturing consolidation, it could motivate the billion-dollar valuation on its own.

THE AI BET

Ten years ago, if someone told me that AI agents would be writing functional software, I’d have laughed in their face. But today, that’s exactly what is happening. At OSH Cut, our software development and deployment flows leverage AI tools heavily. For the first time ever, we are finishing development tasks faster than they are created.

I believe that AI agents have only been this good for single-digit months, and that they’ll only get better from here. Meanwhile, there are many startups working hard on applying "agentic" intelligence to CAD and design. This is significantly more difficult than software development, in part because software provides instant feedback: Code works, or it doesn’t, allowing for ultrarapid development and self-correction. It’s hard to close the loop on manufacturing processes. But after what we’ve seen so far, I would be surprised if AI didn’t eventually get there, and maybe soon.

At OSH Cut, we expose first-principles manufacturability feedback instantly, right in the web browser. For customers, that means they can upload a sheet metal part and watch a simulation of it forming on our press brakes and tools, knowing right away whether we can make the part for them, and if not, why. While smart AI agents can already use a browser, AI integration can be streamlined using MCP, or model context protocol, which defines how AI agents can connect to external tools and services. By automating design for manufacturability via software and exposing that to AI agents, the design, test, and training loops for CAD-focused AI can be accelerated. Online direct manufacturers are uniquely positioned to provide this kind of rich, instant manufacturability feedback. It’s not enough to write the software; companies have to understand manufacturing from first principles, bone deep.

In the future, if new things are designed at 10 or even a thousand times the pace of today, manufacturing will become a significant bottleneck. This could create a manufacturing boom unlike anything the world has ever seen. With that backdrop, a billion-dollar valuation on a massively scalable, software-driven direct manufacturer may make more sense. This feels a lot more like a classic venture capital bet with possible venture-scale returns.

THE MARKET IS BROAD AND DIVERSE

Manufacturing is far too diverse to be dominated by a single player, or even by dozens of players. Some consolidation will likely take place. Perhaps in the future, the U.S. will have thousands of manufacturers, instead of tens of thousands. But there are just too many ways of doing business, too many specialties, for one manufacturer to push everyone else out.

Even within specific markets, business practices can vary widely. I expect that in the online, on-demand world, a dozen different scaled sheet metal companies could exist, all offering different combinations of capability, quality, conformance, inspections, traceability, industry-specific requirements, speed, and price.

I am encouraged and optimistic about SendCutSend’s capital raise, even as a direct competitor. We need as many manufacturers as we can get.
