HVAC product fabricator resolves quality issues with new material positioner

SITUATION

United Enertech Corp. is a family-owned and -operated business supplying louvers, dampers, shutters, roof curbs, and many other air movement and air control products. The HVAC company is located in Chattanooga, Tenn.

The company’s goal is to meet customers’ needs for quality and speed while maintaining flexibility to produce products with custom specifications. This is a difficult trifecta to accomplish. It requires a highly organized manufacturing process.

"Our niche market is being able to do quick turnarounds of just about anything. We have a rush program that allows customers to specify how fast they need something," explained Jason Price, United Enertech’s manufacturing engineer.

In addition to its normal product line, the company also manufactures a new line of blast-resistant products, some of which are sold to civil defense and petrochemical companies. With such a diverse product line, accuracy in its various manufacturing processes is critical for getting products out the door in a timely manner.

Such accuracy wasn’t always the case, however. Occasionally quality control discovered a product with an incorrect measurement.

"So that means the whole thing would have to come back apart, and have to be resawed. Anytime you are resawing, you are losing money," said COO Andy Schwall.

Not only were products cut inaccurately, setup was taking a tremendous amount of time. That meant throughput on the saws was extremely low, according to Schwall.

Both Price and Schwall agreed that manually measuring parts for cutoff with a tape measure was flawed. Achieving product conformity was difficult, and a great deal of time and labor went into correcting miscut parts.

RESOLUTION

This situation led United Enertech’s local machinery distributor, Wakefield Equipment, to suggest the company purchase a TigerStop material positioner.

"Because of TigerStop we realized that everyone’s tape measure was different and had a different amount of wear. Having a guy out there with a tape measure, measuring each individual piece, or moving a mechanical stop every time, just isn’t cost-effective or efficient," Schwall said.

This is especially true when using thousands of linear feet of extrusion per year. Nonconforming products pile up rapidly.

"All of your quality control starts with the positioner. You know everything else down the line is going to be correct," Schwall said.

Integrating new equipment into a process can be a daunting task. Ensuring employees can quickly learn how to use new equipment is not always an easy feat. Price said that wasn’t the case with the material positioners.

"…Within an hour, I was able to program it to do whatever I needed to do and be able to help support the guys out there fabricating," he said.

The positioners have allowed United Enertech to save money on labor and rework, and they have prevented a large amount of scrap from making its way to the trash bin.

"Because the TigerStop can be retrofitted to an older machine, it’s easier to justify that purchase because you’re not having to throw away your older machine. You can just add it to your old machine," Schwall said.

The use of the material positioner isn’t limited to one type of machine. For instance, once United Enertech saw the success of the positioner on its cutoff equipment, shop floor employees started using it for accurately punching parts as well (see

Figure 2

).

"The versatility of the positioner, being able to attach it to a hydraulic press or a mechanical press to do repeated patterns, has helped us save a lot of time," Price said.

Thanks to the equipment’s advanced interconnect kit, United Enertech has created nearly autonomous operations with the material positioners. For example, when an operator verifies the job and programs it into the material movement control, the positioner’s advanced interconnect kit allows the machine to run the program and punch the pattern without a need for a machine operator to stop and verify each hole.

"A lot of our products have a very fixed spacing for the blades. A manual machine with a manual index can get that done, but it’s not the quickest way to do it. That’s why we use the TigerStop for that process. Without making a mechanical index that basically has an infinite range of adjustability, the positioner is the only way to do that," Price said. "There’s a lot of labor savings there."

Storage, sawing, and sorting equipment eliminates bottlenecks

SITUATION

AHP Merkle GmbH is a manufacturer of hydraulic cylinders based in Gottenheim, Germany. Its products include standard, block, and stamping cylinders; push and core pull units; clamping elements; and rotary drives.

The production process is designed to be lean and efficient. All manufacturing operations are performed inhouse and linked by short transport routes to help ensure short delivery times. The company keeps some types of cylinders in stock and ready for shipping at all times. Other products are made to order based on a modular construction system.

The company purchases raw materials for its hydraulic cylinders as bar stock, usually in the form of 9.84-ft.-long rods. The most frequently used materials are steels and aluminum.

"To ensure that we can react quickly to incoming orders, it is an advantage for us to keep as many sizes and materials as possible in stock," explained Paul Gutmann, plant manager. "This is because the procurement time through to the delivery of certain materials can be as much as half a year."

So when building its new headquarters, the company looked for a new way to store and access those materials.

RESOLUTION

AHP Merkle incorporated a Unitower tower storage system from Kasto Maschinenbau GmbH into its new plant. With 94 storage locations and a height of 59 ft., it is a compact way to store bar stock.

The loads are supported by cassettes, each of which has a capacity of 3.3 tons. They are filled at an input station at the front of the tower, either manually or using a gantry crane. An operating gantry crane with loading traverse handles the load supports. It is not necessary to move the entire stock, but only the relevant cassette.

When AHP Merkle expanded capacity in 2015, it implemented a second tower, doubling its number of storage locations. The cassettes are transported along two roller tracks into the saw mill, where the company uses a KASTOvariospeed C 15 fully automatic, CNC circular saw for cutting solid materials, sections, and tubing. The saw can be operated simply and intuitively via a FUI. It is fed from a universal magazine that can hold up to 10 of the 9.84-ft.-long rods, and once the machine is loaded with input stock, everything else runs automatically. As a result, the saw can run unattended for long periods.

The sawing system is combined with a KASTOsort automatic sorting system equipped with two robots mounted to minimize space requirements. After the sawing operation, the first of the two robots picks up the cut part from the machine and inserts it into a clamping device. The second robot then uses its pretensioned milling head to remove all the burr. The cut parts then are placed in one of up to four containers. The robot can stack parts in separate dedicated containers or combine them as required for each specific order.

"Compared to our previous sawing technology, we have been able to quadruple our throughput," said Gutmann. "What used to be the bottleneck in our manufacturing operations now has the greatest capacity."

Plate bending machines produce more than 200 exhaust system silencers per hour

SITUATION

Magneti Marelli, an international group owned by FCA Fiat Chrysler Automobiles, designs and produces high-tech systems and components for the automotive sector. With more than 38,000 employees, 89 production units, and 12 R&D centers, the group has a presence in 19 countries and supplies carmakers in Europe, North and South America, and Asia.

Magneti Marelli Exhaust develops and produces exhaust systems with silencers that reduce the noise generated by the pressure waves of the exhaust gases.

RESOLUTION

MG srl provided Magneti Marelli with a system that integrates bending and welding of silencer bodies, which subsequently are delivered to Honda in Japan. The CNC system’s memorized programs can be recalled for the entire production of several different silencers and catalysts.

After the delivery of the first system, Magneti Marelli commissioned two more identical systems from MG. The machines manufacture more than 200 pieces per hour. A loading device with vacuum pads lifts the plate and positions it on the motorized table, where a row of rollers carry the metal smoothly to the bending rolls. A sensor recognizes the plate thickness and weight, ensuring that only one sheet is lifted at a time. Two arms on the motorized table square the plate, centering it to allow the bending cycle to be completed correctly. The pneumatic ejector then unloads the piece into a nodding platform.

The entire bending cycle is controlled by the CNC Touch Command EVO control. The closed-loop CNC has a continuous dialogue with the sensors on the machine board, registering the received values and, if necessary, correcting possible errors in real time. The control adapts to different materials and to any shape when basic parameters are dialed in. It controls the top and side supports, motorized table, squaring arm, and loading/unloading systems.

Safety features include modules for emergency systems, multibeam security barriers, security carpets, bumpers, and laser scanners.
