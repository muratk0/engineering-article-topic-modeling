# Picture this: Mural commemorates manufacturing’s importance

In its 90th year of business, Rocklin Manufacturing continues to uphold the manufacturing legacy celebrated on its headquarters’ façade

Dan Davis

This 112-year-old building on Jennings Street in Sioux City, Iowa, manufactured parts to support armed forces in both World War I and World War II. Today, it’s the home of Rocklin Manufacturing Co.

A mural depicts 1940s-era manufacturing activities at Rocklin Manufacturing’s headquarters in Sioux City, Iowa. Pictured in the mural are company founder I.J. Rocklin (in the gold sports coat in the middle section) and his son Jim Rocklin (to I.J. Rocklin’s right).

W

hen Ross Rocklin drives to work, he has a visual reminder of his family’s manufacturing legacy. His grandfather, I.J. Rocklin, and his father, Jim Rocklin, are there on the side of the Rocklin Manufacturing Co. building for him and all of Sioux City, Iowa, to see.

A mural, first painted in 2005, commemorates the family business, which dates back to 1934 when the grandfather founded the company, which focused mostly on manufacturing products for helping farmers to be more efficient. The building, which dates back to 1912, was once the home of Sioux Tools. Ross Rocklin said it’s believed to be the last surviving building in Iowa that housed manufacturing activities supporting U.S. armed forces in both World War I and II.

"We have a long 90-year history of manufacturing, and we’re very proud of that history," Ross said.

I.J. Rocklin purchased the building in 1941. Not too long after, Rocklin had to expand the facility to keep up with manufacturing demand related to the war. At the time, about 250 employees worked around the clock fabricating products for the war effort.

That era of the company history is reflected in the mural. Paul Chelstad, a local artist with a following that extends well beyond Sioux City’s borders, approached Jim Rocklin in the early 2000s about the idea of a mural for the building, which at the time was vacant. (After the war, the company had consolidated its operations in two newer buildings that had been constructed during World War II, leaving the original 1912 building empty.) Chelstad knew the Rocklin family, and he liked the idea of honoring the history of manufacturing in Sioux City. Jim Rocklin was excited about the idea and provided Chelstad and his creative partner on the project, Mark Kochen, another local artist, with a treasure trove of old photos from the 1940s.

"I had seen the Diego Rivera mural in Detroit for Ford Motor Co. before we laid this plan out with all these photos. So we decided to do a cross section of the building featuring people from this time," Chelstad said.

Leveraging Kochen’s computer skills, the artists developed a mock-up of what the mural would look like. Chelstad then had huge stencils created of all the people and equipment to be featured in the mural so that the artists could apply them to the building as an outline. Chelstad handled the painting of the people, and Kochen focused on the machinery and equipment.

The artists included I.J. Rocklin in that original mural. Jim Rocklin joined the mural in 2018.

That’s when the company decided to move back into the venerable building, which had sat vacant for almost 40 years. But before any move could take place, a thorough renovation had to occur. That meant replacing windows, power washing the exterior, and generally shoring up an old building that, while built to last, still needed some attention.

When the exterior work was completed, Chelstad and Kochen were invited back to touch up their handiwork. Chelstad, looking back on the rehabilitation project, recalled almost a complete rework of the original mural. But it did give them the opportunity to add Jim Rocklin to the picture, right next to his father. (Jim Rocklin died in 2017 after a two-year battle with pancreatic cancer.)

"I think people love the building and they love the mural," said Ross, now the company president.

Rocklin Manufacturing is no longer making farm-related implements. The company manufactures and distributes portable equipment that helps metal fabricators with everyday challenges. This includes I.J. Rocklin’s invention, the Rocklinizer, which applies hard and wear-resistant material onto tools and dies, and the latest product, the MobiLase, an industrial fiber laser marker. In 2024, the Small Business Administration recognized the company as the Small Business Exporter of the Year for Iowa and the Regional Exporter of the Year for Region 7, which includes Iowa, Kansas, Missouri, and Nebraska.

"The mural has always been visible, but now the building is getting noticed too," Ross said. "It’s a great place to come to work—a state-of-theart facility within 112-year-old walls."

Here’s one final tidbit about the mural: If you look closely at the flame coming from the welding scene at the top of the mural, you’ll notice a smile in the flames just above the company name. That smile belongs to Fern Rocklin, Jim’s wife and Ross’ mom.
