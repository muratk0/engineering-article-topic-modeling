New Fabricator Increases Flexibility, Energy Efficiency with Compressed Air System

SITUATION

George and Angie Williams, co-founders of metal access door and cabinet supplier Williams Brothers Corp. and fire extinguisher and cabinet supplier Strike First USA Corp. of America, spent 25 years developing their distribution businesses. Recently the husband-and-wife team decided to branch out from solely distribution into manufacturing, launching AG Laser Technology, a full-service sheet metal fabricator producing products such as metal doors and cabinets, in Front Royal, Va.

"Becoming a manufacturer meant that our team had to adopt a completely different mindset," said Angie, who serves as CEO and president of the new company. The owners had to switch their focus from branding and shelving to building an end-to-end precision laser operation that could rapidly take in customer order specifications and engineer, fabricate, finish, and deliver high-quality products.

The Williamses reorganized their 60,000-sq.-ft. facility into an operation that now includes both distribution and 22,000 sq. ft. of fabrication, with laser machines, a punch press, bending presses, and robots.

Nash Hale, vice president of engineering for AG Laser Technology, noted, "There’s no room for error. When an order comes in, we need to respond quickly, configure the machines, and start production."

This can be a complex task for a company with such a large portfolio of products, many of which can be manufactured in numerous finishes and sizes. Some orders are standard, while others require distinct specifications. The company needed to find ways to achieve the flexibility and efficiency required to complete this variety of tasks.

RESOLUTION

To help meet these demands while also avoiding downtime, eliminating air contamination, and reducing energy costs, the company acquired an Ingersoll Rand R45n 50-HP, contact-cooled rotary air compressor equipped with variable-speed control.

The system delivers 222 ft.3/min. of compressed air power to the fabricating and powder coating equipment, robotics, and multiple workstations, allowing the company to produce up to 2,000 units in an eight-hour shift.

"Our compressed air system has been very important to keeping our operation running clean and smoothly," said Hale. "It also has allowed us to significantly reduce use of nitrogen gas for laser cutting, since we’re able to use air as an assist gas."

Oil and moisture can have detrimental effects on laser cutting and powder coating. Particulates in the gas mixture also can cause damage during laser cutting. The air system features a Polysep™ oil-water separator, and its Nirvana™ refrigerated dryer eliminates moisture at a 37-degree dew point. The filter system eliminates particles larger than 40 microns.

Piping also was important to help ensure constant flow and reduce air contamination. A full-loop layout comprising 1,000 ft. of SimplAir™ aluminum piping keeps the air clean from dirt or particulates that could reduce end product quality.

V-Shield™ technology for the piping also helps eliminate air leaks, which can cause high energy loss in compressed air systems. All critical fittings are secured with distortion-free O-ring face seals, and polytetrafluoroethylene braided stainless steel hoses reduce leaks to increase system efficiency and keep utility costs down.

The compressor system’s variable-frequency drive helps ensure that the motor runs at the right speed for the power demand, increasing system control and reducing energy use.

Structural steel fabricator invests in equipment to eliminate manual tasks

SITUATION

In 2014 Wilson’s Structural Steel opened for business in Fenton, Mo., to fabricate structural steel principally for commercial buildings in the St Louis area and the surrounding Midwest region. Although it can be looked upon as a somewhat new firm, its principal, Ken Wilson, is certainly no stranger to the St. Louis area and to the structural steel fabrication industry. He has been involved in structural steel fabrication for the past 45 years.

There was a short break, however. In 2010 Wilson decided to retire from the business only to find that retirement was not really the next chapter in his career. During those days in retirement, he often thought about what he had learned over the years and decided that he wanted to give it one more shot. He wanted to implement his years of experience in setting up what he was to consider as the most cost-efficient fabrication facility possible.

The mission started with finding the right facility. In the past the physical plants that Wilson worked in always had some layout limitations. In relatively short order, he was able to find an existing facility in Fenton that was wide open and could accommodate a logical flow of material.

Soon after the business started, developers were knocking on the company’s door with work, as Wilson has historically been known as a quality structural steel fabricator with competitive pricing. Understanding that low cost gets you the job but the repeat orders arrive only if you prove your ability to perform from a delivery and quality standpoint, Wilson’s Structural Steel was conservative with its delivery commitments. In line with this gradual startup, the company slowly added staff.

During this time, Wilson also knew he needed to stay on top of innovative processes. A visit to the North American Steel Construction Conference in St. Louis in 2013 provided him the opportunity to see new structural steel fabrication technology that automated common manual tasks on the shop floor.

RESOLUTION

At this show Ficep Corp. was exhibiting its latest drill line, which had movable spindles to permit not only drilling and scribing, but also milling. The ability to move the spindles in the length axis independently gave the drill line machining center flexibility. It was more productive than other structural steel processing lines, as the three spindles could operate independently.

During this trade-show both Wilson and Ficep engineers started to put their heads together to explore how this new technology could be developed further to give a fabricator an all-in-one manufacturing cell. The added ability to perform high-speed milling and scribing in multiple surfaces simultaneously would hold the potential to provide fabricators with generous time savings on the shop floor.

Traditionally, copes, rat holes, and weld prep that are located on the ends of structural steel sections were manually laid out and required hand-held torches to make the cuts. In some shops, burning took place automatically on a beam processing line, which required even more shop space and another operator.

In both these cases, extensive manual grinding was required to remove slag from the cutting process and to also provide additional metal removal, as a cutting torch could not cut flush to another surface.

During the past year Ficep has worked with Wilson’s Structural Steel to generate and maximize different high-speed milling routines to produce these cutouts (see

Additionally, when the workpiece leaves the drill line system, it is ready for fit-up and welding. Wilson’s Structural Steel reports that, when floor-to-floor time is considered, it now eliminates up to 80 percent of the time required in the generation of these cutouts when compared to manual work needed to produce similar results.

Even simple block copes can be generated quickly with high-speed milling, which also eliminates cleanup after burning.

Ficep’s software also is useful for processing information from the 3-D building models. All the intersecting coordinate locations for subsequent fitting of detail items (see

Wilson’s Structural Steel also has taken steps to improve workflow processes. It is one of the first structural steel shops to go from a 3-D CAD model to a fabricated section ready for shipment without paper on the shop floor.

Since startup in 2014, Wilson’s Structural Steel has created 31 new jobs, and the fabricator anticipates hiring an additional 12 over the next 12 months to achieve the facility’s optimum capacity.
