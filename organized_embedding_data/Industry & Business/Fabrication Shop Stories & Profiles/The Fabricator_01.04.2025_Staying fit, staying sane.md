# Staying fit, staying sane

[TARİH: 01.04.2025 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

A good working environment can maximize a company’s productivity

DAN DAVIS

T

he metal fabricating business is stressful enough without having to worry about hikes in metals prices and disruptions in supply chains. But that’s where the domestic manufacturing base finds itself, trying to stay the course in a sea of change aggravated by inconsistent governmental actions and retaliatory actions from other countries.

To disconnect from the stress, some look to lose themselves in their work. For example, a welder can have a rhythmic soundtrack running through the ear buds as he lays beads. But not everyone has that luxury. The reality is metal fabricating is a collaborative effort. Your attention is required for any number of tasks or meetings.

That’s why working in a welcoming environment can go such a long way. Hard work doesn’t have to be as hard when someone actually wants to be there doing it.

Employees at Maguire, Sioux Falls, S.D., find this out each day they show up for work. Maguire’s new 70,000-sq.-ft. water tower and storage tank fabricating facility opened in August 2024, and earlier this year, it earned Fitwel certification. This means that employees walk into a building that’s literally designed to maximize their well-being.

"We wanted to be the employer of choice in the region for manufacturing," said Brian Cooper, Maguire’s vice president of business development. "So we looked at [building this facility] 180 degrees differently than we might have done otherwise." (You) can learn more about Maguire in "New facility means new efficiencies for water tank fabricator" on p. 38 of The Fabricator’s October 2024 issue.)

Fitwel, launched in 2016, is the creation of the U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the General Services Administration. It represents a certification that gauges a company’s commitment to providing a work environment that improves the mental and physical fitness of the people working there. Today, the Fitwel certification is administered by the Center for Active Design.

Fitwel can be used to address building environments of all types, not just manufacturing facilities. It rates a facility across 12 categories: location, building access, outdoor spaces, entrances and ground floor, stairwells, indoor environments, workspaces, shared space, water supply, cafeterias and prepared food retail, vending machines and snack bars, and emergency procedures.

Knowing that it wanted to pursue this certification when designing the new building, Maguire management set out to make the shop floor as hospitable as possible. Two levels of windows, which run the entire length of the building, let in sunlight. Meanwhile, solar tubes, which capture natural light from the roof and reflect it to an internal apparatus inside the building, bring outdoor light into the indoors. Precast concrete walls maintain comfortable indoor temperatures more efficiently than the walls of a common metal building. An air quality system automatically flushes out contaminated air when sensors are triggered, providing a consistent level of clean air throughout the day.

That’s not all. Maguire has a gym on campus for time-starved employees and locker rooms for changing clothes. There also is a walking path for those looking for some less-strenuous exercise. Even the vending machines have healthy options, such as trail mix and nutrition bars.

"We have all of these things that are built for our team members to have the best employment experience possible," Cooper said.

If this type of work environment sounds unique, it’s likely because it is. For example, Maguire is the first company in South Dakota to attain this type of certification.

In the metal fabricating world, these amenities are almost nonexistent. Most shops are struggling to provide a refrigerator where someone can keep some salad dressing cold.

This might seem overkill for someone who’s spent a majority of his life working in a dark shop where the only light is provided by some old-school fluorescent bulbs, but that’s where modern manufacturing is headed. If companies want to compete for talent, they need to come out of the shadows.

"As we continue to grow, we want to set a precedent for how a business can thrive while creating an environment that benefits its most important asset: its people."

— Scott Jones, Maguire

Maguire has not only embraced that challenge, it’s made it part of its mission. It wants to excel in business while simultaneously creating a workplace that prioritizes the health and well-being of its employees.

"As we continue to grow, we want to set a precedent for how a business can thrive while creating an environment that benefits its most important asset: its people," said Scott Jones, Maguire’s president.

Having a place where you can hit the ground running at the beginning of a shift and then hit a treadmill or a walking path right after work is a great benefit for anyone in manufacturing. It keeps the body going and the mind clear. It’s also a prescription for staying sane in today’s crazy manufacturing economy.
