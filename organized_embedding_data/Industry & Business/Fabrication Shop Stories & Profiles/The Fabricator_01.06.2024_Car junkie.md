Craig Kennedy discusses Kennedy Coachworks’ capabilities, projects

Rafael Guerrero

C

raig Kennedy’s first car was a two-door 1975 Plymouth Scamp. It was this car where he learned how to work on vehicles.

Fast forward to today, and some of the vehicles he’s working on—a 1969 Chevrolet Camaro, a 1973 De Tomaso Pantera, and a replica of a 1960s Alfa Romeo—may draw more attention.

Such is the way Kennedy’s life has unfolded as the owner and sole employee of Kennedy Coachworks, a fabrication/restoration shop in Sheridan, Colo.

"I enjoy the challenge of it. My favorite part is the fabricating of pieces, making stuff from scratch," Kennedy said. "I really enjoy working on the rarer vehicles where you can’t just go and buy a part or buy a piece. We need to figure out a way to manufacture, whether by making body panels or machining certain parts.

"It’s fun to be able to do that and bring these cars back to life," he said.

Working on Exotic Cars

His 2,500-sq.-ft. shop can only fit so much, given it must store both his projects and an assortment of older and newer sheet metal fabrication equipment. Kennedy tries, though; he typically has two to three projects going at once.

Some small fab projects may take only a week or two. Others, though, may take longer.

"Sometimes some of these projects are 40-50 hours, others can be a couple thousand hours, depending on what the scope of the project is and what work needs to be done," Kennedy said.

A noteworthy project is the 1973 Pantera, which he said will be done in 2024. Another is the 1969 Chevrolet Camaro, which he said will be ready to showcase this summer once it comes back from painting.

Arguably his biggest project, though, is the building of a replica of a 1960s Alfa Romeo. He built a plywood bust to help support the body as it takes shape. He is hand shaping the aluminum body of the car and will then build the entire chassis for it.

Kennedy said this project will showcase his capabilities, "from fabricating a full entire body to chassis work to machine work to everything involved in between.

"It’s a long-term project that will be here for a while," Kennedy said.

People interested in this project and other restoration projects can visit Kennedy’s social media channels. Kennedy said he enjoys this aspect of his job—connecting with people from all over the world and replying to comments that come in.

As of this writing, his Instagram account has just under 42,000 followers. He does note his Instagram handle,

@craig_the_welder

, is a bit misleading as his expertise has expanded beyond welding.

"I’ve been debating on changing it to Kennedy Coachworks, but at this point I don’t know if there’s really a reason to," he said. "Plus, that might confuse some people if I changed my [Instagram] name."

Doing What Others Can’t

Kennedy started Kennedy Coachworks in 2018 after years of working at other vehicle restoration shops. This personal milestone also signified his commitment to Colorado, having grown up in Kentucky (just outside of Cincinnati) and attending school in Pennsylvania.

While he dabbles in custom work—he expressed interest to someday do one-off art projects that aren’t auto related—Kennedy focuses most of his attention on car restorations. His services and capabilities include metal design and fabrication, welding, soldering, and chassis repair and fabrication.

He likes to say it is a shop where he can do things other places cannot.

"Some of my customers are other shops where they just don’t have the capabilities or machinery that I have to fabricate certain parts," he said.

For now, it’s just him. Kennedy joked his only other employee is his border collie. However, he would like to hire someone else in the future.

"There’s a lot of long hours and a lot of blood, sweat, and tears put into getting things off the ground because it’s not the easiest thing to do," Kennedy said of having his own small business. "But it helps that I’m passionate about this; it kind of makes it easier.

"It definitely helps make those long 12-, 14-hour days a lot more reasonable when it’s something you enjoy," he added.
