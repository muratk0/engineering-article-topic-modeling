n 2024, Nova Group won the Rusty Demeules Award for Safety Excellence, the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association’s highest safety honor for the second year in a row. Nova Group’s fabrication shop has now reported seven straight years without an OSHA-reported illness or injury. If you look at the last four years, that’s been more than 400,000 on-the-job hours (89,510 hours in 2020; 106,418 in 2021; 112,142 in 2022; and 110,000 in 2023) without injury.

Nova Group Inc. is a general contractor, working primarily within the Department of Defense/Department of Energy space. Our fabrication facility is geared to support our work around the world with prefabricated materials. As part of Quanta Services, we have the opportunity to partner with and support many of our sister companies in the same way.

We also have an in-house engineering department, where we do our own drawings, calculations, and work plans. We outsource a bit when we need help. We take the plans and specs and turn them into living, breathing models before we go out in the field and try to build.

I have the privilege of spending a lot of time in our fabrication facility with our team. It’s right across the parking lot from our main office. The president of the company sits 100 ft. from the fab shop and participates in our programs. He also can be a part of the group when we recognize safety milestones.

We’re not a huge shop. We’ve got about 20 people on the floor. We also are a very manual shop because we build interesting and custom oneoff projects. It’s hard to automate when you are doing custom fabrications like that.

What Is VPP?

We are part of the Cal OSHA VPP, also formally known as the California Voluntary Protection Programs. This is a program designed to recognize employers and their employees who have implemented safety and health programs that effectively prevent and control occupational hazards. These programs go beyond meeting the minimum Cal/OSHA standards.

In California right now, there are 66 total star sites, companies that meet all program approvals, and three sites that are working on achieving that status. When you think about the number of companies and facilities that are in California, 66 is a small number. But for us to be a part of this program, being the only metal fabricator, it makes a lot of sense for Nova Group to stand out.

Cal/OSHA VPP utilizes these core safety elements: management commitment and employee involvement, hazard prevention and control, safety and health training, work site analysis and audits, and continuous improvement. Continuous improvement is really the core of what makes VPP a positive thing for our company.

Why Join VPP?

Why would you embark on such a venture? When I started, I was told that we wanted to be a part of this program. Early on, the most obvious benefit was that it eliminates the potential for random inspection. In reality, that’s not the most valuable part.

One of the things that happens is that you get audited, like you might get audited by your insurance company. The alternative is that you pay somebody to come and audit you. With the VPP arrangement, you are getting a true audit with people asking hard questions. It’s free and provides you with accountability.

What gets tracked gets done, right? When you have somebody coming in to recertify you every couple of years, your paperwork’s going to be right. Your site is going to be clean. You know that they’re coming because you’ve invited them.

So, the third party provides accountability, but they also help. They come in, and they’ll answer questions. They help to strengthen your programs with recommendations and resources.

They also provide you with peer groups. I sit on a committee within Cal VPP concentrating on employee engagement. It provides me with ideas on how to keep the ball rolling and how to keep people engaged in safety. Every quarter we report back to the group, and we make presentations at conferences.

Engagement with employees is so important in a plant because it helps everyone to live and breathe safety all day long. It becomes a part of the culture.

Our participation in VPP also is seen as an investment in employee safety. When folks see how hard everyone worked to become VPP certified, they realize that management actually cares. That goes a long way to creating a safe work environment, but also feeds efforts related to production, quality, and compliance assurance.

They also recognize that it’s a significant expense to even start on the journey. They see the company investing in fixing things and spending time to specifically address safety.

To participate in a continuous improvement effort like VPP, you don’t have to create everything from scratch. You have huge resources. If you don’t have some sort of safety program going on in your shop that VPP officials might want; you can go get it from somebody else and just tweak it to match your facility.

How To Get Involved

To be a part of VPP, a shop needs excellent safety numbers. You have to be in the top 10% in your North American Industry Classification System code. So you basically need to have no injuries or few injuries. You also have to be in the top 10% every time you reapply, which reinforces the continuous circle of improvement.

You have to reapply and requalify every three years. It’s basically going through the whole process that got you qualified in the first place again.

The process begins with an on-site evaluation. The first one that we did in 2017 was very traditional. They brought two OSHA employees and what they call a special team member—somebody like me from another company who has knowledge on safety. They examine the shop floor and take a look at your books. It’s very thorough.

In the end, they tell you everything that they see as an impediment to maintaining the safety of employees. You get their audit report, and then you work through the findings and what needs to change, update, or be added to your programs.

It takes time, and OSHA officials want to get it open and shut. But you still have to run your business. You need to devote a lot of time to ensure that meeting the requirements of the final audit is both realistic and a worthwhile investment in worker safety.

In our case, the first audit revealed a lot of stuff that was valid. We needed to improve on the paperwork and keep better records. There wasn’t a whole lot that we needed to actually fix physically with the plant, like guarding for example, because we had addressed this in previous efforts.

Our latest audit, in 2021, was in the middle of the pandemic. As a result, we had a hybrid approach. They wanted to see our paperwork, and we made it available to them. They went through it, and then six months later came for a visit. One person did a tour around the shop for nearly two hours and then sat down for a review of their findings.

Celebrate the Accomplishment

After you wrap up your audit, you get your certificate. That’s a big deal. It’s time to have a celebration.

We had a big barbecue party. We were joined by the local state senator, the area supervisor, and the mayor, to name a few. They all wanted to be a part of it.

We got the newspaper out to cover the event. We took good pictures to share. We definitely made sure that word got out.

Now, a lot of people know about Napa Valley. It’s known throughout the world for wine, of course. But, in reality, it’s a small town. We have about 75,000 people in the city limits. Due to our participation in programs like this, mentoring local youth, and recruiting within our community, wearing a Nova shirt or hat to the grocery store often ends up in a conversation about what we do. It’s great to have interest in the fabrication industry within Napa.

You could say that being a part of VPP has helped to make Nova Group a more attractive place to work.

For instance, I work in the middle schools as part of our career development outreach efforts, and it’s not uncommon that I’m talking to a kid and a mom about what we do. She might ask, "Is it safe for my kid to be a welder?" and I can say, "Yes." And I’m not saying that because it’s my opinion. I’m saying that because it’s a fact. We do welding and fabrication every day, and no one is getting sick or injured because we’ve done the work to make it a safe job.

Safety Tools

How do we keep the fabricators and the welders safe? Here are some of the tools that we use.

Every job starts with a pretask analysis, what others might refer to as a JHA, a job hazard analysis, or an AHA, an activity hazard analysis. That is then incorporated into another tool, RED (recognize, eliminate, discuss) Books. These folding notebooks fit in a pocket. They are very effective tools for safety because they act as regular safety reminders. Say you finish what you’re doing and you are going to join another crew, you have to review that crew’s hazard analysis and sign off that you read over it and know what to expect. If a truck shows up that we didn’t know about and you need to leave that crew to unload it, you stop and consult that RED book all over again.

So it’s really just about planning, not running off and just doing stuff. We also do a lot of training. It’s expensive, but it works.

Within the first year of an employee coming on board, he attends an OSHA 10-hour hazard awareness course and gets CPR, first aid, and respirator use training. This goes for the entire company, not just the fab shop.

Equipment training also is part of the mix. We document every piece of machinery that someone is authorized to use. That includes general hand tools, like a grinder, and equipment like a band saw. If you trained on that machine, it’s documented.

And, obviously, the longer people stay, the more training they get.

Good Catch Reports are another useful tool. There are different terms for this, and a lot of people have different versions of it. At Nova Group, we use the program like a suggestion box. Anyone can write whatever they want to share. The important part is the tracking and trending of these reports. We don’t just take the suggestions and say, "Good job. Here’s a reward for turning in a suggestion."

The shop management team meets with the executive committee on a weekly basis and part of the discussion are these Good Catch Reports.

Employees are incentivized to submit these suggestions. For example, we play bingo every Thursday in our shop safety meeting. If you turn in a suggestion, or a Good Catch Report, you get an extra bingo card.

These suggestions aren’t limited to safety issues. They could be reports on quality, production, or environmental issues. They really can be anything that an employee wants to talk about. This is a vehicle for them to make their thoughts and concerns known. It’s a powerful tool when they know the president is going to hear about each one, and they are tracked to closeout resolution.

We also do a weekly audit of our shop. So rather than just the safety guy or me walking around the shop, we pick two people and charge them with looking around certain areas of the facility. Anything that they find that can be fixed right away, they are asked to make things right. If it’s a bigger issue, it’s noted and addressed later.

We track all those notes until they are fixed. Again, these audit notes are reviewed by a team on Fridays. On Monday mornings, the plant manager goes over what was found the week before. It keeps safety a part of the conversation.

Lastly, on-the-spot employee recognition is another way to promote positive safety behavior. If a supervisor witnesses someone taking the time to reduce the risk of injury, he can give out Nova bucks. We have the Nova store that has a lot of cool stuff in it. It’s got things like kayaks, fishing poles, tents, and sleeping bags, and household goods. It’s like the stuff you can get off Amazon.

It’s a great pat on the back for employees staying safe and trying to keep their co-workers safe.

Clearing the Air

One of the things that was a real challenge for us with VPP was dealing with industrial hygiene issues. It was very important during that initial VPP audit, and we didn’t have a lot of experience dealing with these types of issues.

During this time, our corporate owners asked what they could do to help us out, and we suggested bringing in an industrial hygienist. We could have contracted with one, but the company ended up hiring an expert in this area. Honestly, it’s helped improve the entire corporation because everyone has access to his expertise.

Recently, we had him out to take a look at fumes coming off our new plasma cutting machine that was used for pipe beveling. We thought we needed to test the fumes because it was a new process. He conducted his tests, and we ultimately changed our work process which helped to reduce the amount of fumes the operator is exposed to.

In another instance, we had him do some testing on welding fumes. We had switched from MIG welding to TIG welding for most of our piping. After he completed testing, we ended up reducing our PPE thresholds because we have the data to back it up, making the welders happy with less cumbersome equipment.

A Partnership That Works

Being a part of the VPP has worked out well for Nova Group. It would be good to see more companies, particularly metal fabricators, participate. It’s helped us to keep our employees safe, and it’s proven to do the same for other companies as well.
