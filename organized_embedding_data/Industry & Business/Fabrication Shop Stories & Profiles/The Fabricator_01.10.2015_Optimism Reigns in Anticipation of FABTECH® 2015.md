how organizers brace for another record-breaking event By Amanda Carlson, Contributing Editor Some things you can control and some things you can’t. It’s usually the things that business owners can’t control—for example, stock market surges and plunges, foreign economies, gas prices, and the weather—that tend to keep them up at night.

For example, metal fabricators can’t necessarily control the fact that they aren’t having the type of gangbusters year that they did in 2014. According to the Manufacturing Outlook Index, released by the National Association of Manufacturers (NAM), manufacturing as a whole has slipped a bit compared with where it was even at the start of the year. As of late August, the index was steady at 51.7, down from 59.9 in March.

Despite those numbers, the "Manufacturing Economic Report," released by NAM in late August, states that manufacturers maintain a cautiously optimistic outlook here in the U.S. as business continues to expand, albeit slowly.

Here’s what metal fabricators

can

control: investing in technical training, staying on top of technology advances, networking with peers, and learning more about how to run an efficient business.

That’s where FABTECH

®

2015 comes in.

Co-organized by the Fabricators & Manufacturers Association Intl. (FMA), SME, American Welding Society (AWS), Precision Metalforming Association (PMA), and Chemical Coaters Association Intl. (CCAI), the show is a one-stop shop for metal fabricators to browse metalworking tools and technology; network with other manufacturing professionals; and learn about technical and leadership concepts during educational conferences, panel discussions, and forums.

This year’s show also is a strong indicator that, despite slower business and an unpredictable stock market, the industry is optimistic and, most importantly, no one is pushing the panic button.

More than 1,500 exhibitors and 40,000 attendees will occupy more than 725,000 sq. ft. of space at Chicago’s McCormick Place this November.

FABTECH 2013—the last time the show was held in Chicago—smashed existing records for attendance, number of exhibitors, and exhibit space for an odd-year show. And last year in Atlanta, the show was the biggest ever held in an even year. This year show organizers are bracing themselves for a massive turnout of attendees who will peruse metal fabricating equipment in three separate halls.
