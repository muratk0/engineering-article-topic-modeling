The Importance of MFG Day

Editor’s Note: In October Gary Weldon, business development manager, Staub Manufacturing Solutions, Dayton, Ohio, posted a link on the Fabricators & Manufacturers Association’s Linked-In group discussion board to his contribution to the Made in Ohio blog. In his blog entry—

http://madeinohio.us/one-question-a-mfg-day-story-he

describes how the answer to one question changed his view on Manufacturing Day

.

[On Oct. 3] the scene at our metal fabrication company was like that at many manufacturing companies around Ohio (and the country) on MFG Day. A lone yellow bus parked alongside our building, and a stream of high school students filed down the steps and made their way inside.

In the crowded conference room, our company president welcomed them, showered them with a barrage of industry facts, and gave them a broad overview of what we do. The looks on their faces told me that they were having a hard time drinking it in. This world of manufacturing was all brand-new.

Next it was my turn. I hoped that the story of my journey might help them look at a manufacturing career differently. I told them that, out of high school, college was not an option for me. I started in my first machine shop at 18, emptying the trash and making minimum wage. Two and a half years later, I was making almost double as a machinist. I left that position to enter a tool and die apprenticeship program, and four years, more than 8,000 work hours, and 52 college credit hours later, I had doubled my wage again.

I shared with them the wide variety of positions that I’ve held and many of the neat things that I’ve been able to do in my 25-plus years in the industry. I mentioned how I’ve been able to make a very good living, buy a nice home, and raise a family all without a four-year degree (and the accompanying debt).

I don’t know if they were faking it, but the students did a pretty good job of not appearing bored. When I finished my story, I answered a few of their questions and then asked one of my own. I was curious to see how they looked at manufacturing and whether open houses, like the one we were doing, actually made a difference.

“I know that this is not scientific, but my experience with the students … leads me to believe that there may be a connection between being familiar with manufacturing and the likelihood of pursuing a manufacturing career.”

So, this is what I asked them: “Prior to today, who has considered manufacturing as a career option?”

I was stunned at their response. Out of a group of more than 20 students, only one raised his hand. Yes, only one. Interestingly, when I probed the students further, this young man was the only one who had been exposed to manufacturing before. (His mother works in the office of a local manufacturing company.) All of the others didn’t even know anyone personally who worked in manufacturing.

I know that this is not scientific, but my experience with the students … leads me to believe that there may be a connection between being familiar with manufacturing and the likelihood of pursuing a manufacturing career. I guess it just makes sense. Doesn’t it?

Well, the good news is that because of MFG Day, thousands of students from all walks of life now have seen a glimpse of what today’s industry is like. And when the time comes for them to make a career decision, manufacturing might just be the option they choose.

Gary Weldon

Staub Manufacturing Solutions

Dayton, Ohio
