# The power of reaching for the stars

[TARİH: 01.10.2024 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

Returning to the moon represents the next great chapter in mankind’s history

Dan Davis

M

y dad was a curious guy. Upon meeting you, he might hit you with a series of questions in rapid succession. "Where did you go to school?" "Where did you buy your groceries?" "Did you know [insert famous athlete’s name] born in the area?"

It sounded like a comedy bit, but he was collecting information. (Having lived with the same approach to intelligence-gathering, I knew the drill.) The answers to those questions gave my dad an idea of just how to connect the interrogation subject to the area and if the person paid attention to the details. They also provided my dad an opportunity to compare life in some other part of the U.S. to his own upbringing in Louisiana.

Interestingly, for someone that was not only curious enough about people and their backgrounds, but also history, sports, and nature, he never much had an interest in space. He once mentioned that he used to watch "Flash Gordon" as a kid, but he never really spent time reading books or watching any film associated with space exploration or even science fiction.

When I asked him why that was the case, he replied, "I hate space," with a dramatic pause between each word. Again, upon hearing the exchange, my wife thought it was the funniest thing she’s ever heard. Of course, I recognized the humor, especially given the delivery, but I also knew the person delivering the statement. He saw space as cold, unforgiving, and devoid of life. He also likely wondered why the federal government spent so much money exploring such a place when those same dollars could be spent to the betterment of life on Earth.

Given all of that, I carry my dad’s thoughts with me. What’s the big deal with space exploration?

Then we had Sharon Cobb, the associate program manager of NASA’s Space Launch System (SLS) program at the agency’s Marshall Space Flight Center in Huntsville, Ala., on The Fabricator Podcast. (The episode was recorded at the International Manufacturing Technology Show in Chicago on Sept. 11. Cobb was a keynote speaker that morning.) She provided an update on the rocket system that will play an integral part in establishing the infrastructure that will allow man to not only set foot on the moon again, but to also work and live there, proving out processes and technology that might make a visit to Mars possible one day.

Back when the program was announced in 2017, I thought this was a feeble attempt to recreate an important event from the early lives of many baby boomers. But, as Cobb described, this is a lot more.

NASA and its program partners are creating the foundation for future space exploration with the construction of the SLS; the Orion spacecraft; the Lunar Gateway, the space station that will orbit the moon; and the Human Landing System, the spacecraft that will ferry astronauts to the moon and back to the space station. It’s literally like hearing about the few opening scenes to a new "Star Trek" spinoff.

In the podcast discussion, Cobb touched on several things that will be addressed as part of the Artemis program: refueling in space, building living and working environments on the lunar surface, and figuring out how to maximize material usage without the convenience of unlimited trips to the nearby Home Depot for supplies. The latter ties into using additive manufacturing to build what you need and trying to figure out what resources on the moon might be able to be used for some of these endeavors. It’s science fiction come to life.

Unlike my dad, I always enjoyed a good science fiction tale. I honestly look at the genre as a sort of guide to what might come. In many instances, science fiction authors are correct. Their imagination syncs well with human desires and capabilities, albeit many years, even decades, down the road.

That’s why the Artemis program is so inspiring. It’s laying the groundwork for exploration that man once thought impossible.

And who is making it possible? The very people reading this publication. They—

you

—are making parts and assemblies that will play vital roles in delivering fearless souls to the stars and beyond.

You’ll really enjoy The Fabricator Podcast with Cobb, which is tentatively scheduled for release in early October. (Check out

thefabricator.com/podcast

for the latest episodes.) She does a good job of explaining some of the challenging work that needs to occur before man actually returns to the moon’s surface, but she also foreshadows the excitement that lies ahead.

When the time comes for astronauts to take another big leap onto the moon, I hope I’m around to watch it. It’s not about replicating the past. It’s about the future.

My dad might not have liked futuristic space content, but he loved history. I think he would have been inspired by these developments, as I’m sure most of us are.
