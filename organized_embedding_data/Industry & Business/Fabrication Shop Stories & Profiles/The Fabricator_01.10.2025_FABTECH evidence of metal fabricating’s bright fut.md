# FABTECH evidence of metal fabricating’s bright future

[TARİH: 01.10.2025 The Fabricator]

Editor’s Corner

DAN DAVIS

A

s FABTECH 2025 came to a close in Chicago on Thursday, Sept. 12, it was hard to ignore the previous three days of excitement. A near record number of attendees walked more than 850,000 sq. ft. of exhibit space to see the technology that is going to enable shops to thrive in the face of a promising manufacturing future and a very competitive labor pool.

It shouldn’t come as a surprise. These are truly uncharted times.

President Donald Trump just signed off on The One Big Beautiful Bill, which provides manufacturers with tax benefits, including 100% bonus depreciation for equipment and property and full expensing for research and development, which should spur investment. The tech titans, like Amazon, Apple, Google, Meta, and Microsoft, are spending millions building the infrastructure to run the data centers necessary to power their AI-enhanced products and services. Multinational companies also are bringing manufacturing back to the U.S., as noted in a recent survey from the Reshoring Initiative where 40% of contract manufacturers revealed that customers actively had taken steps to reshore production.

Meanwhile, tariffs remain in place, and inflation is sticking around. Also, some sectors like agriculture and automotive are struggling.

But that hasn’t done much to tamp down metal fabricators’ overall enthusiasm. When your livelihood is tied to fabricating the future, you don’t get caught up with what that future might be. Today, it’s all about data centers and power generation for those server farms. Tomorrow, it might sustainable energy and electric cars, which the rest of the world remains committed to making a reality.

After talking with various fabricators and those that serve the industry at FABTECH, I don’t blame them for the excitement.

AI promises to streamline front office operations.

So many words have been dedicated to covering automation on the shop floor that they overshadow what can be accomplished in the front office. Thanks to AI, what typically took estimators and part designers hours to accomplish might now be done in minutes. AI will be able to scan past orders and files to find similar jobs and use those as references for new quotes and drawings.

Companies no longer have to rely on longtime employees who might or might not be able to recall those older jobs. Institutional knowledge is now available for everyone.

Of course, in a world where "first to bid" can often translate into "first to win," speed is of the essence. AI allows a metal fabricator to obtain that speed without sacrificing accuracy.

Digital connectivity empowers the small and medium shop.

Real-time visibility into processes has long been linked to large manufacturing operations, where supply chain partners demanded accountability and stressed financial discipline. Those pressures have crept down the supply chain, and now the same expectations are being asked of metal fabricating companies of all sizes. Fortunately, tools that provide this type of glimpse into shop floor operations are now readily available and part of many cloudbased shop software offerings.

Companies no longer have to rely on the owner’s gut feeling. They can see where capacity is available and what machine tools are being underutilized. Data can feed decisions, which can lead to a reduction in wasteful spending.

Metal fabricating is in the spotlight as young people look for alternatives to college.

AI is already making an impact as unemployment figures for new college graduates are actually higher than the overall U.S. unemployment rate. A lot of entry-level roles are being consolidated as workers are being asked to use AI as part of their jobs. This shift in the economy is forcing young people to consider AI-proof careers, specifically something that requires working with your hands.

Metal fabricating is a great alternative career path for students, even those who didn’t take shop class in high school. An entry-level job can lead to numerous other jobs within a growing company, and many times those organizations offer tuition reimbursement programs that allow workers to add to their skills or pursue a degree. The temptation to learn on someone else’s dime makes a lot of sense after hearing tales of people being held back by college debt.

All of those trends point to a manufacturing sector ready to tackle opportunities ahead. Some uncertainty lingers, but industry players don’t really seem too concerned. Metal fabricators are ready to get to work.
