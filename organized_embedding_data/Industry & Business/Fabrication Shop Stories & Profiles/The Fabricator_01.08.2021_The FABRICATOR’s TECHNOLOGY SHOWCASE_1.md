Introducing GEKA USA

W

ith the metal fabricating market in North America primed to grow as society emerges from the pandemic, a 100-year-old Spanish machine tool manufacturer is poised to take advantage of the economic expansion, working from its new facilities in the U.S.

While plenty of companies pulled back investment dollars over the past year, family-owned GEKA established an administrative office in Maryland and a warehouse in northern Illinois. The name should be familiar to manufacturers, as the company’s Ironworkers have been sold in the U.S. since 1978.

The FABRICATOR

spoke with Jon Larrañaga, the company’s director of the U.S. and Canada, to find out just what the company’s formal presence in North America means for the metal fabricating community.

What thinking led GEKA to establish a presence in North America?

The U.S. is our second home. We have sold machines in the U.S. since 1978, and we wanted to be closer to our customers and partners. Being located in our key market gives our customers the chance to see that we are focused on helping them as much as we can. Our customers will see this critical support in different ways—through good customer service, excellent technical support, new technology developments, first-rate delivery terms, etc.

GEKA is developing new product lines and applications, and our goal at GEKA USA is to learn where our new product developments are most needed in the U.S. market. We want to listen and work closely with our customers and partners to develop the best solutions for their needs.

The market is moving us towards a service and solutions-oriented industry. GEKA has great products. We are well-known for the quality of our machines. They are robust and reliable. The best proof of that is that we continue delivering parts and consumables for machines manufactured in the ’70s and ’80s.

The new team’s mission is to ensure GEKA USA leads the market thanks to its first-rate support as well as the high quality of its products.

Why locate in Maryland and Illinois?

In Maryland we have our service team, which is led by Ryan Vorsteg, our chief operating officer. He has a lot of experience working with GEKA products. Besides, the southeast and northeast are very important areas of the country for us: we have a lot of customers and business that demand high-value products and have projects that require highly focused service attention and customized solutions.

In Illinois we have our warehouse. Illinois is a great location. It’s in the Midwest, very well-located to serve the rest of the country. It’s also an industrial state, which helps us from an infrastructure and supply point of view.

Will you have dealers?

Yes, we are working with dealers and agents that helps us to sell ironworkers, CNC solutions, and consumables to our customers.

In the U.S., GEKA is more recognized for its traditional ironworker machines, but you also reference other product lines, like the CNC equipment. How important is that CNC equipment market for GEKA USA?

It’s very important. Every year we have more sales and installations of our CNC automation lines in the U.S., and we expect to see even greater demand for our machines in the future because GEKA USA takes care of everything: design, fabrication, delivery, installation and commissioning, and service. So the customers just need to tell us about their need and we will offer the best solution for that.

It’s also important to highlight that GEKA is highly focused on developing customized applications for specific industries or niches. One example is the solution we designed, built, and installed last November for Fisher Engineering’s snowplow business.

What other technology, outside of ironworkers and CNC automation lines, are part of the GEKA USA lineup?

We have plasma cutting systems, and we are working on new drilling solutions. Additionally, at FABTECH, we are going to introduce two new concepts on our ironworkers.

Our plasma cutting tables are like our ironworkers: high-quality, robust, and reliable. We are already working with our partners in the U.S. on the best way to introduce our technology to the market.

The GEKA Drill is a new development. We are planning to launch this new product by the end of the year in Europe and in 2022 in the U.S.

As I said before, at FABTECH in Chicago, Sept. 13-16, we are presenting two new concepts for the Ironworkers: our new Bendicrop-130 machine and a new evolution for our Semipaxy machine. Both are going to see great demand. Our customers told us that they are looking forward to seeing these new concepts: the higher capacity unit of the Bendicrop series and the latest Semipaxy evolution, which is designed to be modular and plug-and-play when it comes to installation.

What do you see for GEKA USA in the future?

GEKA USA is going to become a service and solutions company, so we are expecting to grow intensively in this area. Therefore, our service team will grow as well and right now one of our priorities is to attract the right talent for our company.
