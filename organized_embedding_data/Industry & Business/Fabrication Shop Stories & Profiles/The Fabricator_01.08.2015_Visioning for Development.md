# Visioning for Development

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 The Fabricator]

Willpower is "a thing."

Thank you for following these columns. I feel honored that you find these useful. The last few columns were focused on

visioning

into the future. I’d like to address something a bit different this time:

self-development.

Most leaders are serious about self-development. After all, our followers are always watching to see if we remain smart enough to keep leading. If they perceive we’re not—that we’ve stopped growing—they begin to wonder if it’s smart to keep following.

Too many people I’ve met apply the "drill instructor" model of self-development. First they yell at themselves when they mess up—even beat themselves up—and then try to improve using bruteforce willpower. Execute, fail, repeat.

Willpower is "a thing." If you’ve cultivated your will for a long time, it’ll work for you. Most people these days neglect their willpower and so it becomes weak. Thankfully, there’s an alternative to willpower that actually works better.

Last time I noted that

visions create self-fulfilling prophecies.

That’s the engine we’re going to put in motion. The fastest way to develop any feature in yourself is to spend three to five minutes every morning and evening picturing yourself perfect at whatever you need to improve. Every Olympian and professional athlete does it.

It works.

It also works in every part of life, such as in your leadership role. Just picture yourself perfect at the aspect of leading you most need right now.

This is an exercise in self-programming, not self-delusion. There’s a big difference between visualizing perfection while knowing you’re not, and thinking you’re perfect and being wrong. If you practice this for a few minutes twice a day, in two weeks you’ll see a difference, rationalizing it away. In four weeks you won’t be able to rationalize it away. In two months you’ll have made so much progress that you’ll think you’ve got it knocked, stop visioning, start to deteriorate, and remember I told you so. It’s like overseeding a lawn—the old grass has roots that run deep. You need to help the new grass overcome the old. In three months you’ll have improved so much you’ll be able to move on to envision other things to develop. Try it out until our next column. Have some fun with it.
