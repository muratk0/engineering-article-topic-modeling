etalworking is more than just machinery, process Xs and Os, procedures, and PPE when a community of people gather to teach, learn, help one another, and appreciate the different talents everyone brings forward.

For Abbey Hunter, founder of The Hot Spot Metals Studio in Grand Rapids, Mich.—a collaborative space for metalworking, metalsmithing, and welding—it’s all about the community.

From Metalsmith to TIG Welder

An artist at heart, Hunter fell in love with metalsmithing while studying art and design at Grand Valley State University in Grand Rapids, Mich., where she learned how to manipulate metal to make jewelry.

While becoming a welder wasn’t her primary ambition, that’s exactly what happened.

"I picked up a part-time job at a brewing company. The lead welder at the time knew I had graduated with a metalsmithing degree, so he pretty much gave me a hood and a jacket and told me I was going to be helping him. He taught me everything I know about welding," Hunter explained.

Even though she lacked formal weld training, he felt she had all the qualifications necessary to be a great TIG welder—she was good with her hands, she had fine-motor skills, and she was patient.

She was such a quick study that the lead welder wanted Hunter to join the maintenance team full time. Not everyone was on board.

"Apparently the guys upstairs weren’t sure how they felt about a woman working on the machines. So the lead welder went upstairs and pulled their heads out of their asses and I ended up getting the job as a welder. Being there full time and learning everything I did on the job really spurred a love for welding."

One of Hunter’s favorite things is witnessing a welding student’s aha! moment—when a concept clicks—and the confidence they build as a result.

It wasn’t easy work either. It was all sanitarygrade stainless steel TIG welding.

"I learned to run before I could walk with welding," Hunter said.

Bringing a Metalworking Community Together

Even though Hunter knew she was never going to be a career welder, the new skill set allowed her to expand upon her artistic abilities. In addition to jewelry maker, she also was a metal sculptor. This broad range of abilities came in handy when it was time for her to open The Hot Spot Metals Studio.

First and foremost, the space was meant to create a safe and comfortable environment for people to try their hand at metalsmithing, metalworking, and welding. It was also meant as a gathering place for talented local artists who might not have the money to purchase their own equipment or a space safe enough to house it.

"I wanted to create this space that was safe and have access to tools without the need to spend a ton of money. But it was also about a community of people who can come together. We have dedicated teachers there, but we all learn from each other and lean on each other," Hunter said.

The business offers memberships for individuals to come in and use its collection of metalworking and metalsmithing tools alongside skilled professionals; classes and workshops from jewelry making to fabrication and TIG welding; and a retail shop with metal necklaces, earrings, rings, bracelets, and home décor made by local metal artisans.

"For jewelry making, we’ll do focused workshops where people spend two or three hours and have a finished project to take home at the end, like a ring or a pendant. We have longer, broader classes that teach them how to use the tools, the techniques, and the handiwork that goes into working with metal jewelry. I offer a couple of different TIG welding workshops—some where people come in and learn a little bit each week and others that are project-based. People like making things they can take home with them and say that they welded it, like a planter or a table," Hunter explained.

It’s been a rewarding experience for Hunter to see people working together, sharing knowledge, and enjoying learning something new. While 2020 was difficult, she made the appropriate adjustments to provide a safe working environment amid COVID-19.

Abbey Hunter is an artist, welder, and business owner who is using her broad expertise to build a community promoting and advancing metalsmithing, metalworking, and welding.

In addition to appreciating the community The Hot Spot brings together, Hunter loves witnessing someone’s aha! moment.

"I get so excited when it clicks with my students. Maybe their welds aren’t perfect, but you know they’ve got it and are really into what they’re doing. I just tell them to keep going, stay in that moment, and remember what your hands are doing; remember the pace at which you’re laying the bead, and what your posture feels like, and keep on rolling."

The Hot Spot offers a variety of classes and workshops, and Hunter noted that the most popular are the ones from which people can take home a finished project, like this metal planter.
