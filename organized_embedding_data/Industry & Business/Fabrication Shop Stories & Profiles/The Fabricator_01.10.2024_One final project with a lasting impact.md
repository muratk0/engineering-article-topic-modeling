Massachusetts teen builds bench in honor of iconic bridge

Rafael Guerrero

L

aynie Coates graduated from high school last spring and managed to leave an indelible mark not too far away from her alma mater, Franklin County Technical School (FCTS), Turners Falls, Mass.

Just a few miles east of the school is one of the area’s most famous attractions, the steel truss arch French King Bridge, which has allowed travelers to cross the Connecticut River since 1932. Just east of the bridge sits the "Gateway to the Mohawk Trail" bench Coates made for her capstone project during her senior year of high school. The bench, with design elements that resemble the architecture of the nearby bridge, seats four and serves as a culmination of all the fabrication skills she learned at FCTS. It’s also a symbol of the kind of work she’d like to do in the future.

"I’m glad to put up something positive about the bridge," said the 18-year-old Coates.

An Ideal Fit

The capstone projects at FCTS can reflect the many interests and capabilities of its high school seniors. They include an aircraft engine stand for the school’s aviation program and a fabricated tubular steel frame for a linesman chute to be used during football practices.

Coates didn’t select this particular capstone project on her own, she said. She pondered potential project ideas—projects that would take up the bulk of her senior year and require design, fabrication, and documentation, and would make for an interesting end-of-the-year presentation. At the same time, the town of Erving, Mass., was looking for a community-minded project to enhance the French King Bridge and its surroundings.

"The French King Bridge is pretty iconic," said John Passiglia, FCTS welding and metal fabrication instructor. "It’s a big truss steel bridge, a place where people stop a lot and look at the river."

Sometimes students will come up with a project idea all on their own, Passiglia said. Other project ideas may come from somewhere else. In this case, the town reached out about the bench.

When it came to who would make sense for this project, Coates was the ideal fit for the bench, Passiglia said.

"One of my jobs as a teacher is connecting the right kid for the right project," he said.

Coates studied the bridge in photos and in person and brainstormed design ideas. The school, local government, and the state’s transportation department were involved as well, as all three agencies had to give the final approval. For instance, the town of Erving sent blueprints of what they would like to see.

But Coates wanted to take it a step further and get creative.

"I didn’t want to do just a normal bench," she said.

Ultimately, they OK’d a proposal for the bench to replicate the bridge’s design but on a much smaller scale.

Gateway to the Mohawk Trail

The bench is a 7-ft.-long by 2-ft.-deep mini-replica of the much-larger 782-ft.-long bridge. It is made from 2- by 2-in. square tubing, while the bench seating and back rest are 10-ga. steel.

The bench’s truss pieces, meanwhile, are ½- by ½-in. solid square stock. Coates said the same number of beams in the bench truss can be found in the actual bridge.

Coates also painted the bench, opting for bright green. While she likes the color now, that wasn’t always the case. She originally envisioned the green to be more like the actual bridge’s.

"In the shop, [the bright green] stuck out a lot and I was very worried about it," she said. "But when we put it out on the bridge with the grass and everything, I think it blended in well."

Coates worked on the bench from October 2023 through April 2024, juggling the project with other school and personal commitments. In some cases, she would work on the bench for a week and then not touch it the following week.

In addition to the plasma table, some of the other equipment Coates had at her disposal at the FCTS shop included a MIG welding machine, ironworker, band saw, bending machines, grinders, and paint.

Local Celebrity

Coates’ reputation grew locally following the bench’s installation last spring.

"When we were installing it, people were constantly honking horns, people were calling me … I honestly felt like a local celebrity," she said.

Not only is the bench something the community can appreciate for many years to come, it also demonstrates Coates’ passion for fabricating. She especially loves welding. And to think, welding almost never happened for Coates, who originally enrolled in interior design.

"For some reason, I absolutely loved it. You can do so much with it," she said about welding.

The bench is located next to the iconic steel truss bridge.

Coates hopes this experience will lead to more jobs and opportunities to weld. She’s already found one job at a fab shop—she hopes more will come.

"I’m definitely not letting my welding career go," she said. "I want to stick to welding, whether it’s in a shop welding dumpsters, plows, making railings for people, garden art, even another bench."
