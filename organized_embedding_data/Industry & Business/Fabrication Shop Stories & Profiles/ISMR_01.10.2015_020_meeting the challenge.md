# MEETING THE CHALLENGE

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

Ed Youdell, President & CEO of the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association technical focus to ISMR

Based in Rockford, Ill. (USA), the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association (FMA) is a professional organisation with nearly 2,500 members working together to improve the metal processing, forming and fabricating industry. Founded in 1970, FMA brings metal fabricators and fabricating equipment manufacturers together through technology councils, educational programmes, networking events and the FABTECH® exhibition.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to the FMA and explain your mission and objectives?

EY: FMA’s mission is “to advocate for the growth and sustainability of the North American metal processing, forming and fabricating industries”. Our objectives include:

- To be the members’ indispensable resource for professional development and knowledge exchange.

- To be acknowledged by stakeholders and customers in the manufacturing community as their principle knowledge and networking resource.

- To grow awareness of manufacturing as a desirable career path.

- To be the leader in facilitating collaboration among organisations serving the metal fabricating and manufacturing community.

FMA also has two technology affiliates, the Tube & Pipe Association, Intl. FMA's publishing arm, FMA Communications, also publishes six magazines focused on the needs of professionals in the industry.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal and cutting markets?

EY: Advances in fibre and disk laser technology continue to drive cutting speeds higher and higher. Electric press brakes are quickly gaining market share as job shop owners demand higher operating efficiencies and environmentally sustainable processes. Software that talks across brands and platforms is something that continues to be a request from shop owners.

The industrial internet and the use of big data are on the horizon as standard business operating tools that shop owners will be faced with understanding and utilising to their best advantage.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements with the reasons for this?

EY: One of the things that we’re most proud of is the tremendous acceptance and growth of Manufacturing Day. FMA is one of the four coproducers of this annual celebration of manufacturing across North America. Manufacturing Day, officially the first Friday in October, is one day of the year when we ask manufacturers to open their doors to their local community and demonstrate all the wonderful things that manufacturing is in the 21st century.

It started as a way to give manufacturers the opportunity to take charge of changing the public perception of the industry and showing young people that manufacturing offers great career opportunities. We started in 2012 with just 240 open house events in about 35 states. In 2015 we saw that grow to more than 2,400 events in all 50 states, plus five Canadian provinces, Mexico and Puerto Rico. By the time the attendance tally is complete, we anticipate that more than 500,000 people will have participated this year.

If there is anything that may help to overcome the skills gap in manufacturing, we think Manufacturing Day could be the solution.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your members at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

EY: Finding qualified employees is always at the top of the list of primary challenges facing job shop owners. FMA recently made a US$ 500,000 pledge to Harper College in Palatine, Ill. to name its new metal fabrication lab. The objective is to equip the lab with the latest technology and provide the instructors with the tools they need to educate the next generation of manufacturing professionals. Once the lab is fully operational, Harper has programme enrolment benchmarks which they are committed to reaching to receive the entire pledge amount.

We will be inviting the area job shop owners to tour and engage with the lab and students with the goal of having them hire the students graduating from the two-year programme. Going forward, I expect that we will replicate this model in different parts of the U.S.

An issue that nags at our members is the continued ineffectiveness of the U.S. Congress and the current White House administration. This creates an environment of uncertainty which contributes to shop owners running their businesses in a cautious manner. The fact that we don’t have an approved highway infrastructure bill is inexcusable. This programme will create jobs as well as drive business forward for metal fabricators that are part of the heavy equipment and construction industry supply chain.

The continued delay in the renewal of the Section 179 provision in the tax code for accelerated depreciation for capital investment also hurt our industry. This is the rare legislation that helps all sides of the purchase equation - the job shop owner who gets additional incentives to make investment that keeps his operation competitive, and the capital equipment supplier who is then able to grow and maintain a capital-intensive business.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

EY: FMA is focused on credentialling the metal fabrication industry’s workforce. We have developed several different certificate and certification courses in disciplines such as laser cutting, press brake, roll forming, coil processing and precision sheet metal operations.

Growing membership is always an emphasis as we seek to continuously refine our benefit offerings to help job shops to maximise performance and competitiveness.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

EY: The greatest challenges and opportunities exist on the personnel side of manufacturing. It’s no secret any more that there is a skilled labour gap in the industry and, with 10,000 baby boomers retiring each day in the United States alone, replacing the institutional and technical knowledge and the technical skills that these workers possess is really a challenge.

The opportunities are plentiful for manufacturers to introduce themselves to their future workforce and inspire young people to want to enter the various fields of manufacturing. The most visible way to do this is for manufacturers to participate in Manufacturing Day, the North American celebration of the industry. The Fabricators & Manufacturers Association is one of the four co-producers of this event annually.
