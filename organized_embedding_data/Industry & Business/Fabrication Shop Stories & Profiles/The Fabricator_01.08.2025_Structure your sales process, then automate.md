f you work at a custom fabricator, you face a growing challenge: how to scale outreach, generate more qualified leads, and close more business without expanding the sales team or exhausting internal resources. The answer, increasingly, lies in the intelligent use of automation and AI.

Today, you have the chance to modernize your sales and marketing processes using AI and automation, but the technology only works if the foundation is strong. A well-defined, repeatable sales process is the core structure that will allow you to take business development to the next level—creating the conditions where AI can be implemented to deliver results.

The Power of Process

Every successful sales organization begins with a process. Without one, even the most powerful AI tools can’t help you scale. Think of the production floor: Raw materials come in; value is added by cutting, bending, welding, coating, and assembly; and then the end product is passed to shipping to be sent to the customer. Employees at each stage follow a step-by-step process. In some cases, a dedicated department (such as shipping or quality) offers its own set of subprocesses.

Even if they’re paper based, transactions on the production floor are measured through a system. This measurement allows for visibility, down to the millisecond and the penny. You can put those same process principles in place for sales and new business development.

Once a customer sends a request for quote (RFQ) and you win a bid, you probably have an established procedure to process the order. But what about everything that happens

before

the RFQ? As a custom fabricator, you might track your bidwin ratio. The exact percentage depends on how you measure the ratio and who’s included (new customers, established accounts, or some combination). You also might measure your revenue concentration. Keeping an eye on these metrics can be extremely helpful. That said, what do you do to improve them? Without a formal sales process, you end up playing a guessing game based on hunches and personal experience.

Think of everything that happens before the RFQ as the "fuel" that keeps your business running. The higher-octane gas you consume, the better the ride. Sure, during busy times, you might have more fuel than you can consume, but what about the slow times? Having no established sales process is like running on low-octane gas: The business moves forward, but in fits and starts—ups and downs, feasts and famines.

Herein lies the value of establishing a formal sales process. I like to use the framework of a

presales funnel

. Within this funnel,

five distinct steps

help narrow prospects to a manufacturer’s addressable market.

1. List Building.

Create a list of industries, revenue ranges, and characteristics of the companies that could buy what you sell. Use these criteria to identify individual companies in your addressable market.

2. Account Qualification.

Prioritize and score all prospects added to the list. Qualify prospects based on how good of a match they are for you in the future, not their current readiness to buy.

3. Contact Verification.

After scoring the prospect, identify the key decision-makers. Gather their email addresses, direct dial, and cell phone numbers.

4. Nurture the Relationship.

Isn’t it nice when you call someone for the first time and hear, "I’ve been waiting for you to call! Our biggest supplier just went down and I have a massive PO for you." Let’s be real—this

never

happens.

A presales funnel helps structure the sales process, starting with broad research and ending with a highly focused list of concrete opportunities.

In reality, the sales relationship starts first by gaining permission to keep in touch. You build a relationship with the key contacts in the organization. This leads to small wins, some of which can grow over time.

5. Identify Opportunities.

Recognize when a buyer has pain, interest, or need, and book a more in-depth conversation to move from a working relationship to a trusted partnership.

Automation and AI in Action: Step by Step

Each step in this funnel creates a simple framework and structure. This is what allows for automation and AI to plug in seamlessly. Now that you have a clearly defined, step-by-step sales process, you can identify where you can apply automation and AI effectively.

1. List Building.

Before AI, sales organizations could use various platforms and tools to build targeted prospect lists, typically segmented by industry, geography, and company size. AI enhances this step significantly, especially during the brainstorming and research phase.

Before AI, you might search manually for something like "nonautomotive industrial manufacturers in the Midwest with over $50 million in annual revenue." That was helpful—but limited. Now, with AI, you can run much more nuanced prompts. For example: "We currently manufacture a high volume of fabricated metal legs used to support conveyor systems. List specific companies that might purchase similar components and suggest other sub-industries—beyond conveyor manufacturing—that would also use these types of parts."

By prompting your favorite AI tool with questions like this, tailored to your business, you’ll uncover highly relevant leads and fresh industry segments you may have otherwise overlooked. The difference is night and day.

2. Account Qualification.

To qualify accounts, I like to use a simple A-B-C-D score. The score is assigned based on a single factor: How much of what you sell does the prospect buy? Each threshold for scoring is determined in advance.

A sales organization can use AI tools like ChatGPT to research specific products a prospect might be manufacturing, as well as estimating volumes. While there is some benefit to AI and automation in this step, I still find that the old-fashioned way works best: Call the company and ask.

3. Contact Verification.

Once you have a refined list of qualified companies, you need to find the people involved in making the decision to source from outside suppliers. This is where AI and automation begins to play an integral part of the sales process.

Today, sales organizations are starting to use custom-built and third-party software to identify people, titles, email addresses, and phone numbers for the team to contact. Once you verify the contact is correct, you can move on to the next step.

4. Nurturing Relationships.

This is where it gets exciting. AI can help write industry-specific messaging, generate personalized follow-up emails, and schedule touchpoints across channels like email, LinkedIn, and even phone calls. You can use AI-generated introductions tailored to the person’s role and industry. AI also can help you score leads based on how they respond—or don’t respond—so you can follow up more aggressively with hot leads and check in more gently with cooler ones.

5. Opportunity Identification.

As soon as the prospect indicates any type of pain, interest, or need, you can qualify an opportunity. From here, you can book a call or a meeting to learn more about the specific problem or project. AI and automation can help you find common time for the call using tools like Calendly, or prompt questions to ask the customer with ChatGPT. In terms of communication, of course, it’s best to let the humans take over at this stage, so they can truly begin to build the relationship.

Too many companies try to jump straight to AI, hoping it will fix a broken or undefined sales system. But AI isn’t a magic wand—it’s a precision tool, and it only works when aimed at a structured target.

That’s why creating a process must come first. Without clearly defined stages and handoffs, there’s nothing for automation to support and nowhere for AI to plug in. It’s like trying to automate a factory without a production line.

Automation After the Sale

Often overlooked is how AI can continue delivering value after the first order. From onboarding to upselling, the same principles apply. AI-driven surveys gather customer feedback and detect dissatisfaction before it escalates.

Email sequences prompt reorders or cross-sell recommendations based on purchasing behavior. And customer relationship management (CRM) integrations ensure that customer insights flow to support, operations, and finance. This kind of holistic visibility keeps customers happy and revenue predictable.

Don’t Forget About the Humans

Recently, one midsize metal fabrication company landed its largest customer after a sixmonth nurturing process. AI wrote the outreach and flagged the opportunity, but it was the vice president of sales who closed the deal over lunch. Here’s the truth: AI doesn’t close deals; people do.

AI helps to get you in the door. The humans are the ones who build trust.

Buyers, engineers, and decision-makers want to feel understood. They want to work with someone who knows their industry, their pain points, and their goals. AI helps to get you in the door. The humans are the ones who build trust.

That’s why we never automate the most important moments: discovery calls, proposal reviews, and postsale support. These are critical conversations. The future of sales in fabrication isn’t about replacing people with AI. It’s about

enabling

people through AI.

A strong, repeatable process gives fabricators the structure to automate with purpose, implement the right tools, and apply AI in ways that scale outreach without losing the human touch. When done right, technology doesn’t replace relationships—it enhances them.
