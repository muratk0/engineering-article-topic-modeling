Inline storage system manages huge variety of parts for stainless steel equipment producer

The Uniline inline storage system has a compact, narrow footprint.

SITUATION

Jörg Schneider Edelstahlverarbeitung, based in Weimar, Germany, manufactures stainless steel plant equipment in accordance with strict hygiene standards. It makes products to order for use in sectors including institutional catering, the food and pharmaceutical industries, microelectronics, and machine manufacturing. Production processes include laser cutting, bending, welding, and surface finishing.

The 40-person staff, working in a production area of 43,000 sq. ft., has to manage a huge variety of parts, which poses a big challenge for internal logistics.

"We produce some 800 assemblies each month, most of them single items ordered by customers," said Jörg Schneider, owner and managing director. "Depending on the order situation, we have 4,000 to 6,000 different sheet metal parts in circulation."

An efficient and transparent internal material flow is essential, especially since all production is based on the JIT principle. "This means that each part has to be in exactly the right place at exactly the right time," explained Schneider. "We work as a rule on a project-by-project basis, and in about 20 to 30 percent of the projects, the customer changes its schedule. That makes order picking even more difficult for us."

The company formerly relied on a manually operated cantilever racking system with random storage for its raw material. A second manual storage system was used for order picking of prefabricated components for assemblies. However, this system was unable to deal with the increasing complexity and rising production volume.

Unmachined and semifinished parts frequently were delivered to workstations days before they were needed. "That took up valuable space and made it hard for us to keep track of the material," said Schneider.

The company decided to combine raw material supply and order picking in a single storage system to help increase capacity and optimize logistics.

RESOLUTION

Schneider contacted Kasto Maschinenbau GmbH & Co., a provider of semiautomatic and fully automatic storage systems and automated handling equipment for metal bar stock, sheet metal, and cut-to-size parts, and explained his requirements. Together they decided on a Uniline inline storage system, designed for supplying material for machining and for order picking.

Kasto built the storage system as a silo structure with roof and wall panels right next to one of Schneider’s existing factory buildings. With a system height of 47 ft. and a compact, narrow footprint, it provides up to 750 storage locations for bar stock, flat goods, and bulky goods up to 19 ft. long and 3 ft. high.

Three types of system pallets, with capacities up to 5.5 tons, are used as load carriers. In addition to euro-pallets, crates, and wire boxes, they can handle a variety of sheet metal and profile material. The inline storage system has three output stations inside the building and two input stations outside, which are loaded and unloaded by a storage and retrieval machine (SRM). Schneider keeps 8,000 to 10,000 different articles in the storage system, from raw materials to completed assemblies.

Users enter storage and retrieval commands via a computer terminal at each transfer station. The system pallets are moved to and from the storage locations automatically by the SRM, with a lifting speed of nearly 79 FPM and a traveling speed of almost 197 FPM. Lateral transport carriages and chain conveyors at the transfer stations move the load carriers in and out of storage. Employees remove material manually or with a forklift.

The output stations also have material deposit racks so workers can pick articles from one pallet while another storage or retrieval process is taking place.

The KASTOlogic warehouse management system coordinates all movements of the SRM and keeps track of the stocks. Bar codes on the system pallets allow continuous tracing of each article. The system records the height of each pallet and automatically looks for a free place with this height. Kasto also integrated its storage management system into the company’s production planning and control system to create transparency and a standardized flow of information and material.

The storage system has been in successful operation at Schneider since late 2013. "We were able to significantly increase our capacity, shorten our transport paths, and make our inventory much more transparent," said Schneider. "Our goal was to double our revenues by purchasing the new storage facility. We’ve already achieved this, and without increasing our factory space." The company is now in an excellent position for continued growth because the Kasto storage system still has additional capacity.

Industrial attachment manufacturer reaches air quality goals with fume extractors

Craig Mfg. now enjoys clean air in the weld shop.

SITUATION

At Craig Mfg., welding is a critical function in the production of heavy industrial attachments. For this third-generation company, capturing weld fumes is a challenging task because of the varying product types and sizes.

The company had a unique opportunity to reinvent itself after a devastating fire in May 2005, which destroyed its plant in Hartland, N.B., Canada. John Craig, then president, emphasized to his rebuilding team the need for a state-of-theart facility that included the best air quality for all welders and plant staff.

The company decided to replace its existing air cleaning system. But despite assurances the system would provide equal or better air quality than Craig’s previous system, the new equipment did not deliver. The supplier honored its guarantee and removed the equipment.

RESOLUTION

Craig Mfg. then approached Atlantic Air Cleaning Specialists Ltd., the United Air Specialist (UAS) local distributor, and tapping into the experience of the UAS application engineering team, developed a system using SmogHog

®

equipment that would clean and change the air in the newly expanded plant every six minutes.

With the cold winters this Canadian manufacturer faces, exhausting and heating replacement air is extremely expensive. One of the reasons the company purchased this equipment was that the ROI usually is within two to three years working eight hours a day—and this is on heat savings alone. In addition, there are no replacement filters with a scheduled maintenance program.

Although the units require occasional checking, they can be set up for selfcleaning. "Our employees believe the SmogHog cleans the air in the welding shop better than any other equipment we have used," said Craig. "Government regulations weren’t the driver; we did it because we want our employees to be safe."
