# Metal raccoon now calls Millvale, Pa., park home

A 12.5-ft.-tall sculpture becomes centerpiece of community’s first playground

By

Dan Davis

R

accoons in urban areas aren’t too unusual. These resilient creatures have adapted quite well to city living. There’s a reason some of them are best described as

chonky.

In Rainbow Raccoon Park in Millvale, Pa., across the river from Pittsburgh, you can find the chonkiest raccoon of them all. The 12.5-ft.-tall, almost 5,000-lb. raccoon arrived on Oct. 14 and hasn’t left. Given the welcome it got from local residents, the raccoon really has no reason to leave. A local knitting group even made a giant scarf for the sculpture to wear during a debut party held a few days after installation.

"The feedback has been amazing. The community loves it," said Filip Agren, the creative force and metal fabricator behind the project.

Agren is a 2011 graduate in architecture from Carnegie Mellon University. Leaning on his past woodworking experience and welding skills he picked up working in the university lab, he started Standard & Custom, a designer and fabricator of architectural products and custom furniture, in 2013. The raccoon, however, was his first real foray into large outdoor sculptures.

"Someone just sent me the RFP. I wasn’t aware of it on my own, but somebody kind of tipped me off about it," Agren recalled. "I thought it looked like a cool opportunity, so I went ahead, did my design, and submitted it. Then I ended up winning the competition."

Filip Agren works on the head of the raccoon sculpture.

Laser-cut triangles of weathering steel make up most of the large racoon body that now stands in Rainbow Raccoon Park in Millvale, Pa. Filip Agren of Standard & Custom created the sculpture. Triad Metals, Pittsburgh, donated the steel for the project; BelleFlex Technologies, Ford City, Pa., laser-cut the steel; and West Penn Laco, Millvale, donated welding supplies. The Henry L Hillman Foundation provided funding for the project.

The Millvale Community Development Corp. was looking for a centerpiece for its new park, which formerly was a plot of land littered with junk. With the park already bearing the name of everyone’s favorite nocturnal neighbors—thanks to a vote by local students—the raccoon structure made sense.

CONSTRUCTING THE RACCOON

Upon being notified that his design won the contest in February 2025, Agren began the fabrication process. He used 0.25-in. weathering steel, a corrosion-resistant metal that develops a brown oxidation layer as it is exposed to the elements over time. Agren noted that he chose steel not solely for durability but also as an homage to Pittsburgh’s steelmaking heritage.

From a design standpoint, he wanted to go with a polygon mesh model approach, a nod to video gaming. (A polygon is a 2D closed geometric shape formed by three or more straight, connecting sides. Video game developers like poly mesh design because 3D objects created with a limited number of polygons help mobile games and virtual reality run smoothly, being easier to process than more realistic models.) As a result, most of the 600 parts used in the raccoon structure are triangles.

"This project was essentially digitally fabricated," Agren said. "We had a 3D model, and I could lay out all of the pieces flat. We had all of the pieces laser-cut, and I had a numbering system to help me piece them together like a puzzle. "I wanted to start off with the smallest stuff first, so I built the hands and the feet in the beginning. Then came the ears, the snout, and the mouth. From there, I did the head and the tail," he continued. "I gradually built up all of these parts and then finally could start putting the body together, which was by far the most challenging part."

Agren used MIG welds to tack up the different body parts and then followed that up with full welds on exterior joints and some interior welds for reinforcement.

"Luckily, when you work with triangles, they kind of triangulate their own final position," he said.

LOOKING FORWARD TO MORE PROJECTS

The raccoon project came at an opportune time for Agren. In 2021, he opened Gallery AFK in Braddock, Pa., to connect more with his creative side and showcase the work of other artists. He’s now hoping to pursue other metal sculpture work if new opportunities arise.

Those interested in finding out more about Agren’s skills can just check in with the giant raccoon at 533 E. Ohio St. in Millvale. It’s not going anywhere anytime soon.

"It should outlive me," Agren said. "I’m pretty confident it will."
