# Stakeholders weigh in on the USMCA

[TARİH: 01.01.2026 The Fabricator]

Around Washington

Predictable market access remains top of mind

By

Omar Nashashibi

T

he Office of the U.S. Trade Representative conducted a three-day public hearing in early December 2025 to collect stakeholder input for the upcoming six-year joint review of the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA). Initially scheduled earlier, the hearing was postponed to December after the USTR received an unexpectedly high volume of submissions, including approximately 1,500 written comments and around 170 requests to testify. The delay provided the agency additional time to assess the materials and accommodate a broader range of participants. The USTR will incorporate the testimony, comments, and follow-up rebuttal filings into its report to Congress, which will help shape the U.S. position for next year’s trilateral review.

More than 100 stakeholders appeared during the rescheduled hearing. They represented business groups, labor unions, agricultural producers, civil society organizations, and policy and academic experts. Testifiers offered a broad assessment of USMCA’s implementation and identified areas where revisions or clarifications may be appropriate. The wide participation reflected sustained interest in how the agreement is functioning and what priorities should guide the U.S. during the 2026 review.

Industry representatives expressed strong support for continuing the agreement, emphasizing its role in maintaining predictable market access and supporting integrated North American supply chains. Many urged USTR to extend USMCA beyond its initial term and highlighted the benefits it has provided for manufacturing, agriculture, and cross-border investment. While some business groups recommended targeted updates— such as refining enforcement tools or adjusting technical provisions—they also cautioned that significant renegotiation could undermine stability for producers and investors.

Analysts, policy advocates, and civil society groups presented a wider mix of views. Some noted economic gains under USMCA, while others pointed to areas where enforcement or worker protections could be strengthened. Several recommended enhancing labor and environmental mechanisms or addressing gaps affecting small businesses and consumers. At least one Democratic lawmaker encouraged USTR to balance industry perspectives with broader public-interest concerns.

The hearing also included remarks from U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer, who noted that withdrawal from USMCA remains a possible outcome under the agreement’s review and sunset provisions. Although this reflected the structure of the agreement itself, the comment introduced additional uncertainty for stakeholders, who largely support continuation. As USTR prepares for the 2026 joint review, the hearing record highlights both the broad support for maintaining the pact and the varied proposals for targeted revisions that could shape the next phase of North American trade policy.

PUBLIC COMMENTS ON USMCA AUTOMOTIVE PROVISIONS

USTR has issued a formal request for public comments to support its upcoming review of the USMCA as it relates to trade in automotive goods. The notice, published Dec. 5 in the Federal Register, initiates the third biennial assessment required under the USMCA Implementation Act, which directs USTR to report to Congress on the agreement’s automotive provisions by July 1, 2026.

USTR is seeking input on how the automotive rules of origin and related requirements have operated since the agreement entered into force. The agency specifically requests feedback on the functioning of regional value content formulas, steel and aluminum sourcing rules, labor value content thresholds, and other criteria that determine whether a vehicle or part qualifies for USMCA preferential tariff treatment.

USTR will use the public comments, along with data and discussions from prior implementation processes, to prepare its required 2026 report to Congress. Written submissions will help inform USTR’s analysis of compliance trends, operational challenges, and the overall effectiveness of the agreement’s auto-specific provisions.

The review is part of the ongoing monitoring framework built into the USMCA to evaluate how key industry-specific chapters—in this case, automotive goods—are performing within the trilateral trade agreement and comes against the backdrop of the broader review of the USMCA.

SECTION 232 PRODUCT INCLUSION DECISIONS DELAYED

The Bureau of Industry and Security has indicated that decisions on the latest proposed steel and aluminum product inclusions under Section 232 will be deferred until 2026. The inclusion process allows stakeholders to petition for additional steel and aluminum products to be brought under the existing national security tariffs.

BIS’s decision to delay action means that importers, domestic producers, and downstream manufacturers will not receive additional guidance on the status of those requests until sometime in 2026. The agency noted that the regular schedule for the process remains in place, and the third inclusion window is set to open this month.
