Wyoming man fabricates butterfly sculpture inspired by his son to raise autism awareness

RAFAEL GUERRERO

T

he blue metal butterfly that sits outside of the Sheridan County YMCA building in Sheridan, Wyo., is meant for everyone to enjoy.

But more specifically, it’s Henry’s butterfly, as the plaque reads.

But who is Henry? Ask the 11-year-old’s father, Nick Wright.

The 41-year-old father of three shared this about his oldest son, which inspired the quote, "I got this," that appears on the plaque installed next to the sculpture.

"He says it at school all the time—he doesn’t want your help, he wants to do it himself," Wright said. "He’s very independent that way, and we’re proud of that. We may run late or it’ll take us longer to do something, but he’s going to do it."

Wright built "Henry’s Butterfly" not only as a gift for Henry but also to raise awareness of adults and children like him who are autistic.

"My whole goal was to spread awareness. There’s so much about autism that’s unknown to those not exposed to it," Wright said.

Making It Happen

The 10-ft.-tall sculpture’s most notable features are the large jigsaw puzzle piece that acts as a base and the blue butterfly flying with wings open over it. The plaque on the sculpture details what the two symbolize.

"This piece donated to the Sheridan County YMCA [2024] is to promote awareness of autism spectrum disorder," the plaque reads. "Jigsaw puzzle piece symbolizes that we are all unique and can fit together. Butterfly symbolizes the beauty of diversity and continued development."

The Wright family stands in front of "Henry’s Butterfly," installed outside of the Sheridan County YMCA building in Sheridan, Wyo.

Wright, a local fabricator who co-owns LJ Wright Ironworks & Design, came up with the idea for the sculpture just over a year ago. Since moving to Sheridan years earlier, Wright had always wanted to raise awareness of local autism resources, so the idea of an autism-themed project just made sense to him. The question then became: Would anyone be interested in an autism-related sculpture? When Wright, whose family frequented the YMCA, heard the facility was working on an autism sensory room, he thought he might’ve found the right location.

"Hopefully they were onboard because I sensed this was the perfect fit," he said.

"I showed them the pictures, the CAD drawings; I had the model. They were 100% onboard," Wright explained, adding that administrators didn’t even feel the need put it to a vote. They told him to just go ahead and do it and to let them know when it was done.

Nick Wright designed and built the "Henry’s Butterfly" sculpture to raise awareness of autism resources. Pictured are Henry (left) and Charlie (right), two of Wright’s three sons.

Images: Nick Wright

Equally enthusiastic were Imperial Powder Coating and Total Concrete Works, two local businesses Wright sought out for the sculpture’s powder coating and concrete work.

"A couple of days later, I went back to [Imperial Powder Coating] with a different project," he said of his conversations with the business. "They told me they couldn’t quit thinking about the butterfly and what I was doing. They wanted to donate their time and material and be onboard. I was very thankful for that."

Ultimately, both businesses donated their time and material to the "Henry’s Butterfly" project.

Praise All Around

After months of work, the sculpture was installed outside the YMCA building in April 2024; organizations and others worldwide use April to celebrate autism awareness and acceptance.

The sculpture is meant to be interactive, Wright said. The plaque, for instance, provides an opportunity for passersby to learn about its significance. The color acrylic on the wings is another interactive feature of the sculpture, as the sunlight passing through it gives shadows a stainedglass look.

The Sheridan County YMCA touted the piece when it was unveiled in the spring.

"This is a colorful and interactive piece of artwork that we invite adults and children alike to enjoy. It’s a soothing sensory experience that holds the promise of acceptance," the Sheridan County YMCA wrote in a Facebook post last spring.

People have given the sculpture high marks, Wright said, adding that some even have come up to him asking for hugs once they realized he was the one behind the project.

At the shop, customers asked questions as the butterfly sculpture began to take shape.

"They would get emotional because they knew somebody in their family, a friend, a friend of a friend, who had autism," he said. "They were thankful in what I was trying to do."

But perhaps most important of all was that Henry loved the sculpture. On the day it was unveiled, "He was grinning all day. His friends were all saying how famous he is now. It was a big highlight for him," Wright said.

"I know at the YMCA, if he’s had a hard day, I know they walk out there and walk around it and look at it. And he’ll come back around and he does better."

As for Wright’s thoughts on how it turned out?

"The more I worked on it, the more I saw it come together, the prouder I’d get," he said.

Wright is the co-owner of Sheridan, Wyo.-based LJ Wright Ironworks & Design.
