Former FMA leader dies

John Paul Nandzik, 88, the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association’s longest-tenured leader, died on Sept. 11. That also happened to be the last day of FABTECH, one of the most successful manufacturing tradeshows in the U.S., which he helped to launch.

After graduating from Riley High School in South Bend, Ind., Nandzik earned two certificates in electronics from Acme School of Die Design in South Bend and DeVry University in Chicago. In 1959, he joined the Air Force and served for six years at Bunker Hill Air Force Base in Indiana, maintaining equipment for electronic measures in B-58 bombers.

When he first moved to Rockford, Ill., he worked in manufacturing until 1974, when he was hired by FMA. During this time, Nandzik earned an associate degree from Rock Valley Community College.

He acted as president and CEO of FMA from 1984 until his retirement in 2002. While working for FMA, he led the charge of establishing FMA’s official headquarters in Rockford. During his tenure, Nandzik also was involved in forming an FMA foundation—now known as SparkForce—to award scholarships to students pursuing careers in manufacturing-related fields.

"FMA’s cultural DNA was created and nurtured under John’s leadership," said current FMA President and CEO Ed Youdell. "John led the organization’s growth and development—from starting in a garage to building an office and establishing roots in the Rockford community. The creation of FABTECH and The Fabricator magazine have emerged as the bedrocks on which FMA’s value proposition is founded.

"We stand on the shoulders of the many who have come before us, and John’s shoulders are among the most significant in our history," he continued. "We are saddened by the news of his passing but will find comfort in his lasting legacy at FMA." Nandzik also was a proud and active member of the American Society of Association Executives, taking on many leadership roles with the organization.

Nandzik was born Jan. 22, 1937, in South Bend to Marie and Paul Nandzik. He is survived by his wife, Sharon; his daughters, Terri Skiba and Kelly Hart with her husband Jason Snebold; grandchildren and great-grandchildren, Tyler and Wendi Skiba (Tristan), Dana and Bryce Cushman (Beckett, Lyza, and Evie), Bailey and Adam Dutko (Bryn and Atlee), Miranda and Mitch Machmuller (Jaxon), Jordan Hart and fiancé, Ben Childers.

Steel Warehouse adds hybrid leveling system

Steel Warehouse Co., a distributor of steel coil, sheet, and plate, has completed construction of a next-generation hybrid leveling system at its facility in South Bend, Ind.

Combining decoiling, leveling, cutting, and packaging, the $20 million system can process an array of steel grades and thicknesses.

The company provides hot- and cold-rolled materials to a variety of industries, including aerospace, agriculture, automotive, construction, energy, and transportation.

Masaba opens new metal fabrication facility

Masaba Inc., a manufacturer of custom-designed bulk material handling systems and processing equipment, has opened a new 147,500-sq.-ft. metal fabrication and office facility in Vermillion, S.D.

The new building, located adjacent to the company’s existing 211,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility, includes 120,000 sq. ft. of fabrication space and 27,500 sq. ft. of office and ancillary areas. The expansion represents a major investment in advanced steel fabrication technology, with more than $15 million in new equipment installed to centralize and automate steel processing.

Production began at the facility in May. By September, all plate and structural steel processing had fully transitioned into the space. The new facility has enabled the company to repurpose its former steel processing area into additional welding and assembly bays.

Bodor Laser establishes global compliance framework for laser equipment

Bodor Laser has announced it has established a global compliance framework and earned several international certifications as regulators in major markets introduce tougher laser equipment safety rules covering electrical, radiation, and protective standards.

The manufacturer has set up a dedicated compliance research team to monitor regulatory updates worldwide and built an early-warning system to respond quickly as new standards take effect. It designs and manufactures its products in line with FDA approval in the U.S., CE certification in Europe, and UL/CSA compliance in North America.

Salvagnini to exclusively distribute new Metevo panel benders

Salvagnini, an Italy-based manufacturer of sheet metal fabrication systems, and Lanhao Intelligent Technology, a provider of proprietary control systems and panel bending machines in China, have announced a cooperation agreement aimed at broadening access to panel bending systems.

Under the agreement, Salvagnini will exclusively distribute out of China a new range of panel benders developed by Lanhao specifically for Salvagnini and marketed under the new Metevo brand. These machines will initially be launched across Asia, the Middle East, and South America.

RK Industries to expand in Colorado

RK Industries LLC, a provider of construction, manufacturing, advanced fabrication, and building services, has announced plans to expand its operations in Aurora, Colo. The 154,488-sq.-ft. facility will house the company’s headquarters and four of its eight businesses—RK Industries, RK Mechanical, RK Service, and RK Electrical—along with RK Foundation.

The second-generation, family-owned business expects to create more than 1,700 jobs over the next eight years. It also offers accredited apprenticeship programs in seven skilled trades, from electrical and plumbing to HVAC services and welding, to address the skilled labor shortage.

WEG announces $77 million expansion

Global transformer producer WEG has announced plans to expand in Washington, Mo., investing $77 million and creating 50 jobs. The expansion will provide additional capacity to support growing utility and clean energy demands.

Comprising five main businesses—Motors, Energy, Transmission and Distribution, Automation, and Coatings—the company has manufacturing units in 12 countries and is present in more than 100 countries. It serves such industrial segments as oil and gas, mining, infrastructure, steel, pulp and paper, and renewable energy.

Woodward to build 300,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility

Woodward Inc. has announced plans to build a precision manufacturing facility in Greer, S.C. The site is expected to become operational in 2027 and will progressively scale production and hiring in subsequent years to meet market demand.

The 300,000-sq.-ft. facility will produce servo-hydraulic actuation systems, which are critical components used in aircraft flight control. Initially, most of the factory’s capacity will be dedicated to producing spoiler actuation for the Airbus A350.

The $200 million facility is expected to create about 275 jobs in manufacturing, engineering, and business operations, with potential for workforce expansion in the future.

QuaLex Manufacturing announces Pennsylvania expansion

Sheet metal fabrication company QuaLex Manufacturing LLC will invest $1.3 million to expand its operations in Franklin, Pa. This project will create at least 35 full-time jobs over the next three years and retain 63 existing full-time positions statewide.

The company, which also has a location in Chambersburg, Pa., will lease an additional 30,000 to 40,000 sq. ft. of space at its Franklin facility.

QuaLex Manufacturing produces stainless and aluminum products that include fire truck cab assemblies, step ladders, and construction equipment.

Mate Precision Technologies names new president

Mate Precision Technologies, a manufacturer of punch press tooling, press brake tooling, and laser consumables, has appointed Joe Schneider as its new president. He succeeds Kevin Nicholson, who has announced his retirement after 25 years with the company, the past 19 serving as president.

Schneider has more than 31 years of experience with the company, developing a strong working knowledge of the firm and the industries it serves, as well as a comprehensive background in marketing and product development. He started at Mate GmbH in Germany in 1994, then returned to the U.S. in 1996 and held progressively responsible roles in engineering and management. He was appointed vice president of marketing in 2006.
