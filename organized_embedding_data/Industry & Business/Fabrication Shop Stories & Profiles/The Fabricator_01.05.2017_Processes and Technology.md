Geographic diversity boosts peak capacity

Joe Amico Vice President, EVS Metal

Riverdale, N.J.

Plenty of fabricators

diversify their customer base not only to make their business more sustainable, but also to try to mitigate the ups and downs of demand. If a shop serves a lot of industries, chances are some sectors will be up while others will be down.

Custom fabricator EVS Metal has done this for years. No one customer makes up more than about 8 percent of revenue. It also has a kiosk product line, which helps diversify sources of revenue even further. EVS’s touchscreen kiosks are sold across various industries. You can use them to order at a fast food restaurant; find the store you’re looking for at the mall; or, as a "virtual receptionist," call or text the person you’re trying to reach in the front lobby of a business. You’ll find such a kiosk in front of EVS’s Riverdale plant.

The fabricator also concentrates on long-term contracts. "We’re always looking to get blanket orders, and make it advantageous for the customer to give us one," Amico said. "We look at demand variation, and [consider] building and holding inventory. The extra space we have allows us to do that. That’s a big advantage we have, and it helps us win business."

But the fabricator has also done something else that has helped mitigate demand variation. It opened facilities in Texas, New Hampshire, and Pennsylvania.

Each plant has its specialty. The Pennsylvania plant focuses on large parts; the New Hampshire plant focuses on tight-tolerance products, like the frames for metrology machines; and the Texas facility, being near Austin, concentrates heavily in the semiconductor sector.

Having multiple plants has long been a strategy for the industry’s larger custom fabricators like EVS, which has more than 250 employees. But it’s not just to gain a footprint in more regional markets. At EVS, it gives each plant a higher peak capacity. If, say, semiconductor work is going full-tilt, the Austin facility can tap resources from other plants, if they have capacity to spare.

"Not all facilities are busy at the same time. Having multiple plants lets us move work back and forth between them," Amico said. "Working in different geographic areas helps, because the upticks in demand are often geographical."

Ultimately, when it comes to part quality and delivery performance, the customer shouldn’t detect any difference, no matter which EVS facility fabricated and shipped the product.

This does take coordination in scheduling and delivery—otherwise, the strategy would fall apart. "But if it’s done right," Amico said, "from the customer’s perspective, all the logistics should be invisible."
