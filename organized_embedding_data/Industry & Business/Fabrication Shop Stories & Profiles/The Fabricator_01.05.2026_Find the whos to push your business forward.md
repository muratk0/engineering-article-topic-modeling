# Find the "whos" to push your business forward

Executive Perspectives

Don’t let milestones guide you— hire the right people to do that

By

Lisa Wertzbaugher

or me, the hardest part about being a small business owner is wearing many hats and not getting to spend all my time on selling, which is what I love the most and is my greatest strength.

I’ve said many times that I’m much better with big companies than small companies. I prefer managing more people and a bigger customer. It’s true that large companies also come with large challenges, but that is more comfortable for me than trying to be a jack of all trades on a small team.

However, I own a small business. And while we are growing, we will not add a lot of head count until late 2026 or early 2027. Recently, I read "Who Not How" by Dan Sullivan and Dr. Benjamin Hardy, and it has me rethinking our strategy.

Our current head count accumulation plan is based on adding positions as we hit revenue milestones and build out departments. But the theory of "Who Not How" takes a different approach. It focuses on identifying the critical challenges and barriers to goal achievement and then hiring a qualified person to tackle them.

According to Sullivan and Hardy, you don’t need to build out the department, create the road map, or develop the processes. You need to hire someone experienced who can do all that for you independently. It will take investment, but the ROI should be significant if you are freed up to focus on your strengths and areas in which you bring the most value.

IDENTIFYING BOTTLENECKS

The first step in finding the "whos" to take your company forward is identifying the critical bottlenecks to goal achievement.

Goals can be many things, but in our business, it’s growth. We are small, and we need to get bigger. The bottleneck is the time I spend on quoting and project management instead of sales. Every day, I spend time on activities that I’m not the best at—but we have no one else on staff with the skills to do the job, so I do it.

To mitigate that shortcoming, I’ve been training myself, outsourcing part of the work to a contractor, and developing a process that can be documented once we hire someone.

I’m not getting far. So, I’ve decided to work with an HR consultant to create a job description and start interviewing for someone to take this problem off my plate.

To identify your bottlenecks, consider the following questions: What am I doing each day that detracts from what I’m best at? What problems are slowing down goal achievement? If I could hire one person to be a game changer in my business, what would that person look like?

POSTING AND INTERVIEWING

Once the problems are identified, you need a job description to attract the right talent.

I made a list of all the tasks I need to offload and built them into one job description. Will we find someone qualified for all of them? I do not know, but it will cast the widest net on talented candidates.

As our HR consultant screens applicants, she is communicating that the job needs further definition from whomever we hire. We are communicating the challenges we are facing and asking candidates to articulate how they would solve them. We need someone who can provide analysis and recommendations to us, not someone who’s just looking to us for direction.

I know this process will take an investment on our part, and it is sooner than we planned, but we are moving forward with posting. It typically takes a while to find the right candidate, but there is no commitment in interviewing, only advantages for us during the discovery process.

TRAINING

Our training will focus on our company, our culture, our leadership expectations— not the actual job.

I am doing the job now, but I’m confident the right person can do it much better. I do not plan to develop a task-oriented training plan. We will have a 30-/60-/90-day onboarding process, but it will focus on supporting, not leading, the employee.

PLANNING FOR NEW HEAD COUNT

Who is next? As we continue to grow, we will continue to look for the right "whos" to solve our problems instead of depending on milestones to be our cues.

Each milestone in the business will present unique challenges we have never faced before. Instead of spending time trying to solve problems and develop plans, we will focus our energy on adding experienced team members to lead efforts. As owners, my husband and I must focus as much as possible on what we are best at and evaluating new opportunities for expansion. For example, developing a process for project management is not our strength, and we will not do as well as we could if we don’t hire the right person for the job.

KEEPING GOOD TALENT

Once you have the right team in place, you have to keep it intact. These teammates you hire will be so capable and independent that they can be easy to neglect.

The employees who are the most troublesome usually get the most attention. The high performers also rarely bring frustrations to their bosses’ attention. They do the work and do not complain but will quietly start looking for other opportunities.

Make sure you have a plan to engage your leadership team and support your best employees as much as possible.
