Parts leveler bumps up business for contract fabricator

Situation

“We used to cringe when we had a customer requirement for really flat parts.” That’s how Joe Arceneaux, manufacturing engineering manager at O’Neal Manufacturing Services’ (OMS) Greensboro, N.C., plant, summed up his position at the time the company was competing for a long-term contract for construction machinery parts with a tight flatness spec.

OMS is one of the largest contract fabricators in the U.S., with 10 plants and 1,300 employees across the country. The ISO-certified, family-owned company, which has 1.5 million sq. ft. of manufacturing space, is located in an area with a concentration of construction equipment OEMs, so it is experienced in producing large assemblies and components.

The contract requiring flat parts involved 0.39-in. laser-cut HSLA steel with 65,000-PSI minimum yield. The parts measured about 3 by 7 ft., and the customer required 0.07 in. flatness over the entire length.

“This tough material has a lot of inherent stress in it to start with, so even if it is as flat as the mill can make it—which would still not meet the customer requirement—there will be distortion from stress relief during laser cutting,” Arceneaux explained.

“We had a gantry flattening press we used for prototyping these parts, and it took 15 to 60 min. per piece, often requiring hits from both sides. And the parts are large enough to make material handling an issue in the process,” he said.

Resolution

After processing several orders of prototypes through Arku’s Cincinnati leveling center, the company purchased a FlatMaster 88 from Arku’s shop floor. The parts leveler handles a variety of materials and thicknesses, as well as the variable requirements of processing different part shapes.

It uses servohydraulics to set the leveling gap, maintain it, and adjust forces to compensate for parts with cutouts, perforations, or odd shapes such as crescent or triangle. Servohydraulics also protect the machine from damage if it is overloaded accidentally. The leveling rollers can be changed and the unit thoroughly cleaned in minutes to prevent surface blemishes on parts or contamination when running dissimilar metals.

The leveler processes parts from 0.080 to 0.910 in. thick and widths up to 78.7 in., depending on the model. In addition to leveling, the machine also stress-relieves the part. Level parts make it easier to set up and achieve consistent results with automated or robotic welding processes downstream. Stress relief ensures the parts don’t warp during welding or bending.

“We put the machine in line with our laser out-feed, where it’s also accessible to the press brake. After a few days of training for our lead operator, we were ready to run,” said Arceneaux. “In a few seconds, one pass through the FlatMaster brings the part into spec and relieves inherent stress.

“As we look ahead at new business opportunities, we see the leveler as a competitive advantage for us. It is a real game-changer to have this capability in-house, because flat parts, fully stress-relieved, always improve downstream processes and quality.”

Manufacturer of painting equipment cuts programming time in half with software

Situation

Luterbach AG in Hildisrieden, Switzerland, manufactures painting and coating equipment for the aeronautics, aerospace, and automotive industries.

The company launched a study on the feasibility of replacing its programming system for the TRUMPF® 200R punching machine. Its software was DOS-based and offered only limited network compatibility, meaning that files had to be generated and stored locally. Programmers were making significant errors, and nests often took hours to create. Data integrity was a major concern, with bugs often rendering programs useless.

The review also explored an investment in a new 3-D CAD system.

Resolution

The company purchased the Jetcam Expert 1 software for its suitability to the company’s smaller, one-off jobs, as opposed to systems that specialized in mass production. It has since replaced its original punching machine with a TRUMPF 600L punch/laser combination machine.

Operations Manager Herbert Wyss said, “The investment in Jetcam in combination with a 3-D CAD system was a massive improvement, cutting programming time and error rates significantly. One of the key factors was [the software’s] ability to work with a broad range of CNC punch, laser, and combination machines from different manufacturers, as we wanted to leave our options open for the selection of a sheet metal processing machine to be purchased down the road.”

Nesting was initially set up manually. Luterbach eventually upgraded to Expert Premium with free-form nesting, which immediately reduced waste materials significantly. The software’s built-in collision avoidance logic allows the system to be set up to avoid forms and clamps automatically, either by lifting the head over the area or avoiding the area altogether. Once it is set, the programmer does not have to intercede again, as the logic is applied automatically.

The company identified that further savings could be achieved by streamlining processes between the 3-D CAD system and the software. In-house training with Jetcam Composite GmbH answered the company’s questions and ensured a smooth process from CAD file to nest. Features such as single component automatic processing (SCAP) allow CAD files to be imported automatically and tooling applied so that they are available immediately for nesting.

The company later expanded its software to include the Jetcam Orders Controller (JOC) Premium order management module. This provided a remote interface to the software’s order and nest databases, allowing the company to create dynamic work lists of parts to be queued for nesting. A CSV API was developed to allow direct import of job lists from Excel. Luterbach also commissioned several other add-ons to simplify the handling of jobs. As a result, a recent order containing more than 1,250 parts that previously took 34 hours to process was completed in 8 hours—more than a 400 percent improvement.

“The acquisition of JOC Premium has reduced programming times alone by 40 to 60 percent because of its automation of CAD import and nesting,” said Marco Brun, workshop manager. “It was undoubtedly the best financial decision our company has ever made.”
