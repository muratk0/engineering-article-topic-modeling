s I wrapped up 2025, I reviewed the strategies we deployed in our business—what worked, what did not, and what needs adjustment.

I always write a business plan with KPIs and metrics for each department. This gives us a roadmap by which to guide the business throughout the year, but also something to analyze as weclose one 12-month period and ramp up for the next. One of the most successful— and helpful—strategies we deployed in 2025 was hiring a business coach for our team. We initially hired her as a coach for one of our employees but quickly added executive coaching for my husband, who serves as president of the company, and several other staff who were new to their management roles.

I recently heard the term "plasticity of the brain." It refers to the human brain’s ability to reorganize its neural connections, structure, and function throughout life in response to learning, experiences, injury, or environmental changes. This allows our brains to adapt, form new memories, develop skills, and recover from damage, fundamentally revealing that the brain isn’t fixed but is rather constantly rewiring itself.

In a nutshell, at every moment, we can be a better version of ourselves in whatever way we define that. It takes intentional and strategic work, and it will not be easy, but I am convinced it’s worth the effort.

I am also convinced that having a mentor increases the odds of success in this arena.

FIND ONE

Some companies have excellent mentoring programs that pair senior and junior staff. I participated in one early in my career. However, as you scale to the top (or own your own business), finding a coach will be your responsibility.

Joining an association is a great place to start. We have been FMA members for more than a decade now, and I cannot imagine my career without it. We attend the events, network, and join user groups. I also write this column.

I have met many accomplished professionals who have had a significant impact on my career. Every month, someone reaches out in response to my column and shares helpful insights and success stories. In fact, an email I received last month inspired me to write this month’s column.

In addition to national associations, there are excellent local groups like your area chamber of commerce, manufacturing-specific organizations, and business fraternities. You may also have colleagues in your industry who do not compete with you and would be willing to meet.

PAID COACHING

Finding a mentor informally by joining groups and looking locally is one option. There also are many options for paid coaching either through individual consultants or business organizations.

Paid coaches provide the same benefits as personal trainers. They have a tested plan, a schedule of activity, and experience that shortens your success timeline.

The work gets done, and this is why we went the paid route in 2025. I was a business consultant and coach for seven years myself, so I am always an advocate of investing money to bring in expertise you do not have on staff.

Some of my best experiences have come from working for a variety of companies. If you have staff who are (or yourself have been) in the same position or at the same company for many years, a consultant is a great way to bring your employees an outside perspective.

GROUP EXPERIENCES

Sometimes a one-on-one mentoring relationship is not realistic or affordable. Maybe there isn’t anyone in your area, or you are unsure how to find someone.

I know one busy business owner who meets with four or five other CEOs every month for coffee.

A company I am doing sales work for recently started a business book club, which I have joined.

Serving on boards is another way to learn from a group of professionals. I have served on the FMA board of directors for 10 years, and the amount I have learned from fellow board members is unquantifiable. It has been a tremendous experience. I have also served in many volunteercapacities that have exposed me to some of the best business leaders in my area.

PASS IT ON

Consider the benefits of mentoring for your entire team.

You can implement a program at your business. Maybe you have a few key personnel you would like to invest in as part of their development plans. Finding good help is difficult, and retention is critically important. Giving your team access to an outside objective coach gives them a safe space for voicing concerns, frustrations, bottlenecks, and questions.

We all work hard to be the best leaders we can be. Find someone who can help you be better—and find someone who can help your team. If everyone improves even a little, the whole company benefits exponentially.

If you have ideas and success stories to share, please send them my way. I love hearing from readers and learning about the wins in this wonderful industry.
