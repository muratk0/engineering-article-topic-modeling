# KMF PRODUCTIVITY SHARE SCHEME

[TARİH: 01.06.2011 ISMR]

INTEGRATING EMPLOYEES INTO the day-to-day success of a business is a guaranteed way of improving the performance of both parties. This is certainly proving to be the case at KMF (Precision Sheet Metal) where a productivity share scheme is celebrating its first anniversary.

KMF is continually looking to develop its people and systems to create a better and more productive working environment which benefits the company, employees and its customers. In creating its Productivity Share Scheme, which is open to all employees with at least 12 months’ service, the Newcastle under Lyme-based company is seeing productivity improve, employee morale rising and customer satisfaction increasing.

Initiated in April 2010, the scheme invites employees to participate either through personal development or by the more tried and tested “new ideas” method. In the case of the former, there are numerous opportunities, with some workshops lasting 30 minutes while others require a greater time commitment.

At the heart of KMF’s scheme is an openness and transparency that ensures that all participants (membership is not compulsory) receive a fair share of the increased profitability that results. For example, an apprentice is not expected to contribute the same level of productivity saving as, say, a team leader but if a member of the scheme does not contribute to any measurable increase in productivity, then they will not benefit from the ‘bonus pot’ at the end of the 12-month period. Membership of the scheme is restricted to those employees that sign up to one of the scheme’s two productivity routes, these being personal development or suggestion.

To level the playing field so that every employee has a fair chance to deliver quantifiable and qualifying improvements to the scheme, KMF has graded the results for the suggestion route. For example, a Team Member has to identify savings of at least £1000/year, while a Team Leader is expected to create savings of £3000/year to share in the year-end bonus. So far these suggestions are delivering good levels of savings and the scheme is on target to meet its budgets. Examples include over £35,000 of savings on product costs from two teams from the shop floor – a 4th year apprentice has also suggested a different way of programming a CNC punching machine that has generated savings of over £1000.
