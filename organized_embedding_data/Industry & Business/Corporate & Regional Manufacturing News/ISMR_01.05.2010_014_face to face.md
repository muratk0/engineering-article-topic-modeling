Bystronic attaches great importance to continuous and independent product development – every tenth employee in the company is involved in the design and development of products and services.

Toengi has a three-point plan for future product investment, which he shared with ISMR

“Firstly, of course, we will continue to invest in our high-end product development because that will remain a large market. However, we do need to become much stronger in the low-mid range machine market both for China and for western countries. We believe that the low-mid range machine market is growing much more rapidly, at the moment, than the high end market. The third area of investment for us will be fibre laser technology."

## Countries such as China and India will increasingly manufacture their own products, and will not be as reliant on European products

Increasing Bystronic's profile and market share in low-mid range machine markets means, in effect, that Bystronic will have to develop and target new products for these markets – which is where R&D facilities in China will be useful. Toengi's strategy, to resolve the time issues, is to use a combination of in-house R&D/technical development and alliances with chosen partners to move product quickly to market.

Laser cutting remains a priority for Bystronic, particularly CO2 laser cutting, but Toengi believes that there is a strong position for fibre lasers in future cutting markets, particularly for thinner materials (up to 5mm).

## FORECASTS FOR THE FUTURE

When the worldwide economy has fully recovered, says Toengi, business forecasting will become much easier.

However, he was prepared to share his thoughts on future growth with ISMR.

"Certainly, we believe that there will primarily be growth in sheet metal markets in China and certain Asian countries. This will be followed by growth in emerging markets – certainly much stronger growth than in 'older’ European markets. We already have sales and service offices in Brazil, India and Russia. In other emerging markets, such as Vietnam or Indonesia, we might need to develop a stronger presence," Toengi told ISMR.

He also highlights projected growth in fibre laser markets for thinner material and for very high-end equipment, which will be boosted by increasing demand for automation and 'lights-out' manufacturing in countries with high labour costs. CO2 laser cutting markets, he believes, will remain strong – it is a proven and flexible technology, he told ISMR – as will waterjet markets (waterjet is a versatile technology that can process a wide range of materials - with waterjet cutting, the cut material is influenced neither by thermal nor processing forces).

Toengi also highlights a trend in manufacturing volumes that greatly affects European operations.

“Countries such as China and India will increasingly manufacture their own products, and will not be as reliant on European products" he explained. “We still have customers in Europe who are exporting parts to new/emerging economies and I cannot see that pattern continuing in the long-term. Labour costs play a huge part in this.'

Toengi also points out that the number of sheet metal machine tool manufacturers will increase 'mainly from low cost countries such as China, Turkey and India', he told ISMR.

"We are therefore facing more competitors in the market," he explained. "These are new types of competitors, producing lower cost machine tools. Our challenge is to design and manufacture low cost machine tools in response – and it is a challenge. We cannot use Swiss and German engineers for this – they have a mindset that is geared towards high-end machine tool development. We have therefore set a new strategy to recruit local R&D engineers in countries such as China for this.

“However, our European customers also face the same challenge. They are competing against manufacturers who can produce parts more cheaply in lower cost countries. They therefore need to be as productive and competitive as possible, moving towards lights-out production. They also need to move into more complex, value-added parts production and reduce the number of parts used in a product. These are fundamental changes to the structure of manufacturing development in Europe."

Toengi sees more use of aluminium in the future, particularly in automotive markets, but he believes that steel will still be the main material of choice for many years to come. It is simply, he says, currently the most cost effective material in the market for the applications.

“The trend in laser cutting is not so much for more powerful lasers, but for more productive and accurate cutting processes, better surface finish and, of course, higher output," he told ISMR. "That is where we are focusing our efforts."
