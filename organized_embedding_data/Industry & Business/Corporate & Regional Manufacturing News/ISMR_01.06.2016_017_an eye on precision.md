# AN EYE ON PRECISION

[TARİH: 01.06.2016 ISMR]

Last year, plate component manufacturer, Solvaro GmbH, helped a range of projects in agricultural and construction machinery towards series production. More manufacturers are now using Solvaro's development services, accounting for over half of its orders in 2015. In mid-2015, for example, Kubota started series production of its new M7-171 tractor at its French facility in Bierne, using nine components from Solvaro. These were industrial plate components, ranging from grid baskets to heat protection sheets for the exhaust system.

Whereas, in 2006, development work accounted for only 6% of all orders, this figure increased to 69% in 2015 and is still rising. However, Solvaro's growth in 2015 was not restricted to development. It also recorded a major increase in capacities through the purchase of a new press line and the recruitment of more staff.

At Solvaro, a combination of robots and lasers now results in speed and process reliability for the manufacture of deep-drawn components such as air grilles for agricultural and construction machinery. The company's 100m² 3D laser machine features no less than three automated co-workers that handle the uninterrupted processing of the parts which are cut into shape by the laser.

## An eye on precision

"Components that have been deep-drawn can then be trimmed in a 3D laser machine. This offers a wide array of advantages. No expensive cutting tools are needed, and this makes the processing of small batches economical. The setup times are also short when switching between the component types being processed. But the most important factor is the highly accurate cutting execution, which makes the laser machine especially reliable," explained Solvaro.

A 3D laser machine at Solvaro delivers accurate trimming of deep-drawn components

ISMR SAYS:

"Time-consuming and costly corrective work, often required when components are deep-drawn after being cut, can be eliminated..."

As the company often produces assembly parts, this is particularly important as the individual parts must fit precisely when put together.

"The time-consuming and costly corrective work often required when the components are deep-drawn after being cut is completely eliminated by the 3D machine," confirmed managing director, Gerhard Bullinger. The machine is primarily used to manufacture air grilles for agricultural and construction machinery, such as the Kubota M7-171 tractor.

## Robots increase the speed

Three Kawasaki robots, working alternately, ensure that the machine is continuously fed. Once a component has been picked up by a robot, it can be tackled from any conceivable angle. Flexibility is increased even further by a movable cutting head. This moves around so swiftly and delicately that it can even cope with very short operational stages such as cutting holes – without requiring the robots to move.

Up to 300 components are cut into shape during each shift. The 100m² machine is operated by an employee behind a protective wall, who can supervise everything live using a series of cameras.

"We have now added laser finishing to our repertoire, as the perfect complement to our expertise in deep drawing perforated metal," concluded Gerhard Bullinger.
