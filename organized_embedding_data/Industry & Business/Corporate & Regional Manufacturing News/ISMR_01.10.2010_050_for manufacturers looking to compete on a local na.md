## A STANDARDISATION STRATEGY

“During the recession, businesses were under extreme pressure to cut costs and as they now emerge from one of the most dramatic downturns in living memory, it can be easy to keep cutting back and even withhold the investment desperately required for growth as the recovery is still fragile and confidence is yet to return fully. Despite this backdrop, it is vital that they do not lose sight of the whole lifecycle costs and Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) of both machinery and its interaction into plant,” says Andy Hodgson, business sales manager from Siemens Industry Automation and Drive Technologies, as he discusses the automation measures that manufacturers can implement to ensure they succeed post-recession.

“Manufacturers looking for success after the recession should apply a standardisation strategy called Optimised Packaging Line (OPL). This approach will mean that they benefit from higher line efficiency, reduced training costs and less capital tied-up in stock of varying spare parts. Upfront investment to develop such an approach will soon reap rewards as the machines move from an initial CAPEX investment process to an OPEX phase. This approach also ensures downtime is reduced as staff is alerted proactively by email or text as soon as a piece of equipment needs attention. While on-site, staff will also be working more efficiently as they use common operating concepts.

“Manufacturers should also note that investing in a totally integrated solution and standardising on an OPL strategy will reduce whole lifecycle costs substantially. Intelligent diagnostics highlight maintenance needs before they happen, while other efficiency gains include ease of operation, reduced training and production flexibility to ensure a measurable return on investment through a standardised approach.”

Another area to be explored by cost-conscious manufacturers looking for sustainable success post-recession is avoiding exposure to risks posed by obsolete machinery and control systems which cannot be supported. Sites can often feature equipment which has been there over 20 years and, although it may be in sound mechanical working order, it can present a number of significant risks to their business. “For manufacturers looking to compete on a local, national and global basis, both standardisation and reducing exposure to risks posed by obsolete equipment have a clear role to play. Both have been designed to keep downtime to a minimum and productivity levels as high as possible – crucially important for manufacturers looking for success post-recession,” concludes Hodgson.
