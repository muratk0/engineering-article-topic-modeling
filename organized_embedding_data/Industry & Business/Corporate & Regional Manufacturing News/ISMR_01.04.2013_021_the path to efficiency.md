# THE PATH TO EFFICIENCY

[TARİH: 01.04.2013 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Automation plays a key role in Nissan’s Autoloader project that will produce parts for the all-electric Leaf model

“Nissan is building the cars of the future with automation from AP&T”

“The gateway to a brave new electric world” – this is how the 100 per cent electric Nissan LEAF was described when it won the 2011 World Car of the Year award.

Up until now, the car model has only been manufactured in Japan, but production will also commence at Nissan’s plant in Sunderland, UK, this Spring. It will be one of the models built using press equipment that has been upgraded with automation from AP&T.

## Effective material utilisation

Sheet metal that is left over from manufacturing large car body parts is used in the project to produce small car body parts, instead of being scrapped, as had previously been the case. This has improved total material utilisation by up to 10 per cent. Work is also performed more rapidly than before whilst noise levels have reduced by around 15dB.

“The Autoloader project is allowing us to reduce our costs and utilisation of natural resources in the form of metal and energy. It is completely in line with the Nissan Green Programme 2016, as we continue our focus on reducing environmental impact and contributing to a recycling-based society,” said Andy Tatham, planning engineer at Nissan Sunderland Plant.

## Automate to accumulate

Automation from AP&T plays a key role in the Autoloader project. Each operation that was previously performed manually is now done by a blank feeder, a shuttle and two SpeedFeeders that are used as transfers in an existing 600-ton mechanical press. The entire process takes place in an acoustic enclosure which muffles noise very effectively. The equipment was ordered in November 2011 after a very careful selection and purchasing process.

AP&T has now delivered a blank feeder, a shuttle and two SpeedFeeders that are used as transfers in an existing 600-ton mechanical press. The entire process takes place in an acoustic enclosure which muffles noise very effectively.

“In 2010 we started looking for suitable press automation suppliers and that is how we first encountered AP&T. We then initiated a procurement process and AP&T presented the best proposal – a customised solution based on standard modules,” explained Andy Tatham.

“The automation equipment has been used for test production since it was installed in September 2012 but, from Spring, it will be used for the commercial production of parts for the Nissan LEAF and other models. The trials have progressed entirely according to plan. We are very pleased and have actually already started the next cooperative project where we are planning to automate another part of the pressing process.”

“For us at AP&T, it is extremely gratifying to be able to help produce a more effective and environmentally friendly manufacturing process for the car of the future. And, with our experience from the automotive industry, I am convinced we can develop more productive solutions together in the future,” added Peter Karlsson, who is responsible for the transaction on behalf of AP&T.

Production started at the Nissan Sunderland Plant in the UK in 1986. The total investment made and announced since then is over £3.5bn. The 510,572 units manufactured in 2012 represented the first time a UK car plant had made more than half a million units in one year. Eighty per cent of production is exported to more than 100 international markets.

Its current model line-up is Qashqai, Qashqai+2, Juke and Note. Forthcoming production at the plant includes the 100 per cent electric Nissan LEAF, the new Nissan Note, the next-generation Nissan Qashqai and a new compact model under the Infiniti brand.
