Stephen Bruner, VP, Marketing, OMAX Corporation

OMAX Corporation, makers of the OMAX®, MAXIEM® and GlobalMAX JetMachining® centres as well as the new ProtoMAX® waterjet cutting system, is currently celebrating 25 years of waterjet technology. On 27 July this year, it held a special anniversary event at its Kent, Washington (USA) 225,000 square foot facility that featured its latest advancements in waterjet machining as well as live cutting demonstrations and tours of the company's manufacturing factory. Since everything OMAX builds is contained within a single campus, the company can ensure complete integration of all parts, software and aftermarket support.

In 2017, the OMAX Corporation achieved ISO 9001:2015 certification. This latest edition of the ISO 9001 quality management standard covers design, development, production and service and ensures that the company has a process for continuous improvement in all four areas. OMAX's abrasive waterjet machines cut simple to complex shapes from virtually any material and thickness. The cold-cutting process of waterjet machining eliminates thermal stress in the workpiece material, preventing the hardening and warping that often occurs during conventional cutting processes.

Dr. John Cheung and Dr. John Olsen founded OMAX in 1993, after finding a way to revolutionise the waterjet machining method. A new type of motion control technology and proprietary software ushered in the development of what they termed 'the first truly affordable waterjet system to offer fast, precise and consistent results'. Today, OMAX Corporation offers diverse, advanced product solutions with a broad range of table sizes, pumps, accessories and software.

## The value of waterjet

"Economically, things are good now. We are seeing strong manufacturing levels generally and strong demand for our products. Folks are either re-inventing their processes, and getting more efficient with waterjet, or trying to keep up with demand – both are great rationalisations for buying a waterjet. The economy in the U.S. is great right now and manufacturing has been building steam for some time, although it is not without its challenges. It is still a struggle to find good, trained operators in the industry so we have focused on building products that are easy to use and program for relatively inexperienced operators," Stephen Bruner, OMAX's VP of Marketing, told ISMR.

Approximately 65% of the company's sales are in North America (Canada and the U.S.A.). The strongest market for OMAX outside the U.S.A. is Europe. Its primary customers are fabricators and job shops (in automotive and architectural sectors, for example) who need waterjet-cut parts. It also serves the engineering educational sector (technical schools, aspiring manufacturers etc.).

"When they buy a waterjet, our customers require, fast, accurate and versatile cutting. In support of those requirements, OMAX products offer simple part programming, easy and inexpensive part fixturing/holding and the versatility of cutting different material types with no hazard-affected zones. Often, customers rationalise the purchase of a waterjet on a single job and quickly find other opportunities to improve productivity with their waterjet," explained Bruner.

## An eye on technology

OMAX exhibited at the recent FABTECH (Atlanta, USA) and EuroBLECH (Hanover, Germany) exhibitions. It showcased its flagship OMAX 55100 waterjet cutting system as well as a Maxiem 1515 and its new ProtoMAX waterjet cutting system at EuroBLECH. This was the first time it had brought the ProtoMAX to Europe as the system now meets CE requirements. With a cutting window of 305mm by 305mm, it offers clean, quiet cutting with no heat-affected zone and no change to material properties.

"ProtoMAX is a compact, self-installed cutting system ideally suited for prototyping and relatively low volume cutting of almost any material which is around an inch thick. The ProtoMAX brings all the versatility benefits of large abrasive waterjet cutters in a sleek and economic package that's perfect for small job shops, engineering classrooms, makerspaces and personal use," Stephen Bruner told ISMR.

"We are continuing to expand ProtoMAX worldwide - it was recognised recently by IMTS with an honourable mention for Innovative Products at the show. We launched the GlobalMAX waterjet cutting system in early 2017. This year, we also introduced the 10HP pump for GlobalMAX. That product can be paired with any size of GlobalMAX machine. We did this for those fabricators who are more concerned about price. The 10HP pump allows us to offer OMAX technology at an affordable level for customers who are just getting into waterjet and are not too concerned about issues such as cutting times. Our focus this year is going to be about driving awareness of our products and technologies. We have very active R&D and engineering departments and are constantly working on ways to improve our waterjets e.g. to improve accuracy and offer more software features etc.," he added.

Visitors to the OMAX booth at FABTECH 2018 got a first-hand look at OMAX's 80X with a Tilt-A-Jet®, Rotary and 100HP EnduroMAX® pump: a GlobalMAX 1530 with the new 10HP pump and 100lb garnet feed hopper as well as the new compact ProtoMAX. OMAX's easy-to-operate software, Intelli-MAX® has been engineered specifically for abrasive waterjets and no special machine code knowledge is required to use it. It is compatible with more than 90 different file formats, including all major CAD program file types, plus graphics file formats such as .jpg, .gif and .png files. This means that 'almost any 2D or 3D part file can be imported directly into an OMAX waterjet controller and turned into a real part', Bruner told ISMR.

A major trend is towards smaller subcontractors using smaller waterjet cutting systems which are less powerful but more accessible from a pricing and spacing perspective. This style machine is equipped with the company's intuitive Intelli-MAX Software Suite to deliver faster cutting speeds and increased uptime. When combined with the EnduroMAX Pump, the machine is designed to provide high levels of precision.

The MicroMAX® features OMAX's proprietary linear traction drive system that uses optical encoders to provide 0.1 micron positional accuracy.

## Trends and solutions

So, how has OMAX positioned itself to respond to current trends in the waterjet industry and meet its customers' requirements?

"The move towards automated and smart systems is a key trend. The idea is that you can have a shop full of machine tools that can be controlled from one central point. Those machine tools also provide data and analytics for predicting when maintenance is needed or if potential problems are on the horizon. We continue to develop our software systems and waterjet cutting systems with that goal in mind. The Waterjet 4.0 approach intuitively makes sense," explained Stephen Bruner, Omax's VP Marketing.

He also pointed out trends towards more personalised CNC machines and routers in the manufacturing industry, adding that miniaturisation and personalisation are also creeping into the sector.

"We accordingly launched, in 2017, the smaller ProtoMAX personal waterjet cutting system, starting at 2068 bars and 5HP pump, with a 305mm x 305mm cutting area. We are finding it popular with vocational schools looking to train the next generation of waterjet operators because the software and hardware components are analogous with other industrial product lines. It is also valuable, with its lower price point and small floor space, to subcontractors who are just starting to use waterjet cutting techniques and are still not sure of the waterjet value. Larger job shops are also using it for prototyping work for customers, so that they don't tie up their larger waterjets and can save operational cost," he added.

## A strategic focus

Bruner has placed a keen focus on expanding the company's footprint worldwide by lining up the right distribution partners.

"We want to expand our footprint globally and have great distribution partners worldwide. Our goal is to work in step with them to bring our products to market. They are an extension of OMAX and ensure that customers receive a consistent level of service and expertise from OMAX, no matter where they are based. That includes trained service technicians to keep their machines running. We are careful when we select our partners," Bruner told ISMR.

OMAX Corporation now has its GlobalMAX 1530 and ProtoMAX abrasive waterjets at the AMT Shanghai Technology and Service Center. Hands-on software and machine demonstrations as well as application support is provided by OMAX personnel at the site. The size and complexity of the Chinese market for abrasive waterjets has prompted OMAX and its distribution partner, SIMENCO, to provide complementary sales and application support. While the AMT Center features the GlobalMAX and ProtoMAX, OMAX personnel will also support its OMAX and MAXIEM brands.

A year of transition and investment has spurred innovation at SafanDarley, its CEO tells ISMR.

SafanDarley continues to progress towards a factory that is fully controlled and managed digitally, Dutch bending specialist SafanDarley told ISMR. In line with this, at EuroBLECH 2018 in Germany, SafanDarley presented its latest innovations in sheetmetal working, including a complete range of automated solutions. On its stand, pressbrakes were connected to a workstation with all relevant information available in a single overview on a dashboard.

Press brakes were connected to a workstation with all relevant information available in a single overview on a dashboard.

SafanDarley also exhibited its second-generation new R-Brake 130T, with pressbrake and robot integrated into one system, at EuroBLECH 2018. The second automation solution on its stand was the E-Brake 35T Mini Cell for bending smaller sized parts. The semi-automatic E-Brake, the E-Brake iTC, was also showcased, equipped with an automatic tool-changer for small batch production.

A key highlight for the company at EuroBLECH was its 'press brake of the future' a new generation E-Brake concept with a new control drive. ISMR met SafanDarley's CEO, Maarten Coerman, in Hanover for an update on its latest innovations.

SafanDarley CEO, Maarten Coerman, with Marlou Siemes, Marketing, SafanDarley.

## Innovation spurs growth

"2018 has been such an interesting year. The sheet metal business is highly dynamic.

A lot of our customers are in the process of changing from small, medium-sized companies to real process industries and we must adapt accordingly. We have therefore focused on processes and digitisation, remodelling our entire product portfolio, over the past year. We are also moving from a machine-builder to a complete solutions provider, becoming an advisor and partner to our customers, not just a supplier. For example, in our direct sales markets we have no sales managers or sales reps, but sales advisors," Maarten Coerman told ISMR.

"This has necessitated changes in our organisation so, over the last twelve months, we have developed new products and increased our factory output. We have hired a new manager in our operations department, from the automotive industry who is familiar with high-quality, high output production. Strategically, we have also hired new product managers, to build the bridge between our sales and R&D department. We have also increased our staff levels by 15% over the last 12 months. That is a great achievement, especially when skilled resource is hard to find. We have invested in our production and increased our turnover by between 15 and 20% this year."

This year of transition and investment has also spurred new innovation at SafanDarley. The company's renewed R-Brake 130T, which ISMR saw on the booth at EuroBLECH, integrates the press brake and the robot into one system. One of its benefits is a fully automated tool and gripper change that is connected to the machine. Together with the product, they can be programmed offline using SafanDarley's RoboBend offline simulation software.

During EuroBLECH, SafanDarley also showcased a new model of Hybrid press brake. This hybrid machine has been specially developed to achieve higher tonnages on a short working length. The machine is equipped with an O-frame and is suitable for bending small, complex parts in thicker material.

The second automation solution on its booth was the E-Brake 35T Mini Cell. This system is characterised by its compactness, making it suitable for bending smaller-sized parts. The optional automatic tool and gripper change function makes it possible to quickly switch between different products. SafanDarley's semi-automatic E-Brake, the E-Brake iTC, is equipped with an automatic tool-changer and offers a flexible solution for small batch production. The tool-changer is built into the existing machine frame, resulting in a machine that takes up no more space than the standard E-Brake.

"There are three key developments for us here at EuroBLECH. The first is the latest version of our AutoPOL software. We have expanded its features so that our customer drawings will be automatically simulated before going to the shop floor (not just on our standard machines, but also on our robotised machines). So, we can now pre-program the robot with the data from AutoPOL," explained Maarten Coerman.

"Secondly, we are promoting our automation solutions. For the first time, we have all three of our automation solutions here on the booth. These are our semi-automated press brake, that can handle tool-change, the Mini Cell (tool-changing and product handling) and the second-generation R-Brake, a fully automatic electric press brake that can handle tool-change and product handling. This machine can be pre-programmed in the evening to run into the night on its own, especially handy for medium or small-sized shops. During the day, the press brake can be operated manually. We have now decided to choose modular functionality for our systems, and this new R-Brake is modular (there are three versions – A, B and C)," he added.

The third highlight was the first glimpse of SafanDarley's new concept, next-generation E-Brake (electronic press brake) with very slim top beam, a C frame (open on all sides) and a belt system (dual-drive system for high-speed and high force). It combines all the benefits of the H-Brake and the E-Brake for better flexibility. The new servo-electronic drive – which is based on SafanDarley's own dual-drive pulley principle – has been further developed, leading to a completely new machine concept. The new generation E-Brake is equipped with a C-frame, while the back gauge can be used over the entire working length. The machine also has a completely flat front, for maximum bending freedom.

## A strong vision

"The focus for us over the next two-three years is integrated solutions. We have always historically focused on hardware and machine technology, as a machine builder, and now we are moving to integral process thinking. We are a relatively small player in the market, and do not have a very wide portfolio, but our speciality and niche is bending. We do not intend to move into other technologies,' confirmed Maarten Coerman.

"Our software systems need to be open, adaptable and able to work with other OEMs products. Flexibility and versatility are key. This means greater connectivity, especially on the software side - which means Industry 4.0."

This concept, based on Industry 4.0 pillars, was on display on SafanDarley's booth where all the press brakes were connected to a workstation with relevant information available in a single overview on a dashboard.

Its wide range of press brakes is grouped into two product lines. The revised range consists of the "Premium" base line and the high-end "Ultra" line. Machines in the Premium line, said Coerman, are characterised as 'being a basic version, at an affordable price'. On machines in the high-end Ultra line, additional functionalities have been added to the standard version compared to the Premium line. The Ultra line machines are equipped as standard with a CNC-controlled crowning table which automatically compensates for different load situations. It is also possible to equip machines in the Ultra line with a 3D backgauge. This makes it possible, said Coerman, to achieve 'the perfect back gauge positions, even for the most complex products'. For even greater...

accuracy, the Ultra line can be equipped with an E-Bend L- Blue angle measuring system.

"The role of the press brake operator is also changing – there are fewer experienced operators in the market now so press-brake preparation and set-up for a job has changed tremendously in the last two-three years.

Automation and digitisation in the bending process has changed everything. So, on every press brake in the Ultraline series, there are consequently two screens on top of each other. The bottom screen is for setting up and controlling the machine itself and the upper screen displays all step-by-step bending instructions," outlined Coerman.

"So, it's moving two ways; at the moment, physical screens on the machines and, for the future, we see added value for the application of Augmented Reality (AR) glasses for service operations, in liaison with our service staff. We are internally projecting AR more towards support and service - these are the applications it is more likely to have in the future," added Coerman.

SafanDarley has also included WILA's TIPS functionality into its press brake control system so that an operator can see where he needs to place the tools on the upper/lower clamping positions. The TIPS system also indicates if the correct tool is being used and if the tools are in the correct position. There are LED indicators on the clamps so that he can see where the tool needs to go.

## Forward into the future

As SafanDarley moves towards more integrated solutions development, with an eye on innovation, the future looks bright for this Dutch bending specialist.

"We are very positive about industry potential going forward, with some reservations about certain geopolitical currents next year. This year, we have seen examples of macro economic influences in North America and are now seeing it in Scandinavia and in Italy, so we are keeping an eye on these developments. The economic signs are positive so we hope that our political leaders will take wise decisions...," concluded Maarten Coerman.

CYLASER has positioned itself for further growth in new markets. ISMR spoke with Frank Vanin, to see how...

CYLASER has been pioneering the application of solid-state fibre optic laser technology for industrial cutting applications since 2004. Today, and hundreds of installations later, it has refined and improved its original vision of a robust, precise, efficient and productive fibre laser cutting system with two new laser cutting processes, Vortex and Vega, which were on display at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover.

With two head offices in Schio (Italy) and a subsidiary office in the U.S., the family-run company has focused its efforts on fibre laser technology (for sheet and tube cutting) as well as welding technology. As the industry gears up for Industry 4.0, CYLASER systems can be integrated seamlessly with other machinery in the production cycle using CYLASER Open Source software (which can manage most automation systems). Remote and self-diagnosis software applications enable remote control and assistance.

CYLASER has positioned itself for further growth in new markets. ISMR spoke with Frank Vanin, the company's area manager, at the EuroBLECH exhibition this October to see how...

## Quality in focus

"When we introduced our fibre laser machine, back in 2005, the market was a little sceptical and believed that fibre laser could only cut very thin material. However, we have proved year after year that fibre laser can cut thicker material at high quality. I would recommend fibre laser cutting for material of up to 25mm thickness for a guaranteed quality cut," Frank Vanin told ISMR.

"We are here at EuroBLECH to showcase our fibre laser cutting systems (for tube and sheet), as well as our welding systems. Key innovations at this exhibition are our new Vega and Vortex fibre laser cutting processes.

Vega is our latest innovation and uses a new type of generator plus our proprietary cutting head to fill the quality gap with CO₂ laser cutting systems on stainless steel. Vega fills this gap for stainless steel cutting of stainless steel of between 1-6mm thickness. Vortex is our fibre laser cutting process for medium to high material thicknesses (8-20mm). Vega offers up to 4kW of power, whilst Vortex starts from 4kW and above."

Vega, the process developed by CYLASER in 2018 that exploits laser beam diameter and a Programmable Shape laser beam, enables users to automatically select the laser spot dimension with the same optics set-up. It is designed to increase the cutting quality for any metal, substantially reduce the cutting roughness on stainless steel and increase the quality of the cut on thicker materials. This includes enhanced cut quality on stainless steel and aluminium.

Vortex is a process that is designed to enable users to obtain higher cutting quality on >8mm material thicknesses. With the Vortex feature, the user can take full advantage of fibre laser's potential through beam emission frequency modulation and in-kerf gas flow management. Benefits, Vanin told ISMR, include lower roughness on stainless steel cutting edge; reduced gas consumption in comparison to standard parameters; lower material heating so that most complicated patterns can be cut and easier part extraction from scrap.

"Customers continuously demand ever higher cut quality; Vega and Vortex are new cutting processes that can offer excellent cut quality. The cutting head is our own design and we built it from scratch since, when we started the project, there was nothing available in the market at that point. You can now see the fourth generation of this cutting head. As a fibre laser specialist, we know almost everything about the fibre laser cutting process and can therefore fully support the customer across the board. Our philosophy is to be a real partner to our customer and fully understand their needs so that they buy the right solution. We often do time and cost studies for customers to indicate return on investment levels. Buying a laser is an important investment for a customer and we are there to offer advice and be flexible. We often adapt machines to customer requirements and applications," explained Frank Vanin.

Innovative features on CYLASER machines include their unusual 'aeroplane' structure. The cutting head runs on a beam that is mounted to the main structure, which is independent from the table. It is not a gantry system. Being independent from the table means that the system is not subject to vibrations, helping to control cutting quality stability in the long term. The cutting head is also at a higher level than a gantry, so it is not subject to thermal drift.

"We can still do the piercing and guarantee a longer lifetime of the protective screen and the focal length. We also allow easy accessibility to the working area for an operator – the doors fully open on both sides so that it is easy for the operator to perform maintenance on the cutting head or collect samples. This also helps to save time," added Vanin.

## An eye on growth

The second-generation company is now run by Federico Campana and his sister, Martina. With around 35 employees, its HQ at Schio is based in the north east of Italy. CYLASER makes use of a coordinated supply chain in the highly industrialised region there, which includes a lot of specialist companies.

"Italy is our main market but we opened a branch facility in the U.S. (a technical centre) in 2015. We have been in the U.S. market since 2009. In 2010, we sold our first fibre laser machine there when we opened the branch facility which has helped enormously with spares, service etc. (we have now sold about 60 units in North America)," confirmed Vanin.

Recent orders in the U.S. include the current installation of a new fibre laser cutting system in Chicago (9 x 3 metres) for the biggest steel service centre in Illinois. This installation led to another large order of an 8m x 3m laser, again in the U.S.

"We are now ready to announce the opening of a branch office in India in November. We have already received two orders from India, with two more expected at the end of the year. India is a market with great potential. The machine on our booth here has been sold and will be the first machine that we send to Mexico - another sign of our expansion into new markets. The customer is a multinational company with its HQ based in the USA. Future plans may include a focus on the German market, where we wish to open a branch facility," Vanin told ISMR.

He is also open to appointing new distributors in selected countries such as Poland, Russia, Australia, Thailand, Spain.

## On the horizon

In 2015, CYLASER moved into a new factory which is almost double the size of its old one (the former 2500 sq. m. facility now heads up its R&D, engineering and special projects divisions). Vanin is optimistic for sales this year and next year – markets in Italy and the U.S., plus its starts in India, the Czech Republic and Mexico, offer great potential for future sales, he added.

"The Italian market has been doing very well – the economy has been great, spurred by the tax incentive programme on machine tools. I believe that 2019 will still be a good year for us, with U.S. and India market potential. We manufacture a lot of 6m machines and can be very competitive on these larger machines. We offer a wide range portfolio of machines (2.5m x 1.25m to 9m x 3m). We also have a double configuration for the medium-sized machines (a long version and a side version for companies with space issues)," he explained.

"Our R&D department is always busy. We are currently working on a bevel head for our fibre laser tube cutting machine and 2D fibre laser cutting systems. We also plan to present a new fibre laser gantry model, with linear motors, at the LAMIERA exhibition in Milan next year for customers who wish to cut thin material at high speeds."
