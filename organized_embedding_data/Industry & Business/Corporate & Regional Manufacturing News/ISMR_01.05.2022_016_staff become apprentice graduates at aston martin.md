# Staff become apprentice graduates at Aston Martin

[TARİH: 01.05.2022 ISMR]

Coventry University's collaboration with independent luxury car group, Aston Martin, in the UK to provide higher and tailored degree programmes to the company's staff has delivered six new degree apprenticeship graduates, with 11 more in the pipeline.

The seven Aston Martin employees who were studying with Coventry University as chartered manager degree apprentices (CMDA) have graduated with BA (Hons) Management and Leadership at Work degrees.

Jamie Cooper; James Kalas; Alex Bray; Alex Doveston; Callum Chandler and Jack Miles have also achieved Chartered Management Institute (CMI) accreditation and full Chartered Manager status. They joined Aston Martin as engineering apprentices in 2012 before being appointed to permanent roles supporting the company's production, quality and manufacturing engineering departments.

After their engineering apprenticeships, they enrolled with Coventry University as chartered management degree apprentices studying for the BA (Hons) Business Management and Leadership at Work degree. The course is designed to help students establish key leadership skills, from managing people and resources to strategic planning. It focuses on contemporary management issues, giving apprentices the opportunity to develop a wealth of general skills needed to help them progress from an operational level through to leadership.

Reflecting on the apprenticeship journey, Jamie Cooper said: "The chartered management degree apprenticeship has been a fantastic opportunity for us to step away from work one day a week and apply ourselves to become better leaders within our company. We have found the degree incredibly useful and are all very excited to see where it takes us on our career paths. It came with its challenges; however, the course leaders were perfect and dealt with a group of stressed yet talented lads incredibly."

Coventry University is currently training two more cohorts of Aston Martin apprentices.

“The degree apprenticeship scheme offers industry the opportunity to upskill staff via a structured programme that can provide competitive advantage and future growth, while apprentices can gain access to expertise that helps ensure their skills stay relevant and up to date. Learners can gain professional as well as academic qualifications," commented Coventry University.

Karen Botting, Senior Manager, Learning & Development, Aston Martin, added: "We are delighted to congratulate our colleagues on successfully completing their chartered management degree apprenticeship and gaining full CMI accreditation. Their new leadership capabilities to manage people, projects, operations and services will be of great benefit to our business."

In February 2022, and announced during the UK's National Apprenticeship Week, Aston Martin launched a drive to recruit over 40 early career positions at its Gaydon and St Athan sites in the UK.
