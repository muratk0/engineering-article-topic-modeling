# UNIVERSITIES HELP FIRMS TO BECOME MORE COMPETITIVE

[TARİH: 01.02.2011 ISMR]

Companies that collaborate with universities benefit from increased profitability and market share – and are six times more likely to produce innovative products, according to new research.

The findings form part of the £3 million, three-year “Impact of Higher Education Institutions on Regional Economies” initiative coordinated by the University of Strathclyde, which involves researchers from 17 institutions investigating how universities affect life, business and the economy.

Leading academics from Manchester Business School and the Universities of Wales and East Anglia found that collaborations between firms and higher education institutions have a strong positive effect on innovation and productivity, and firms with university collaborations are four times more likely to innovate than those without.

PROFESSOR JEREMY HOWELLS of Manchester Business School said: “Over the last 25 years, there has been a growing interest from Government in encouraging the development of relationships between Higher Education Institutions and the business sector. This has moved beyond a simple focus on the commercialisation of new inventions to a recognition that universities have a wider role to play in supporting innovation, developing capabilities in human capital and knowledge exchange, building networks and in supporting business growth and the wider economy. Our research has shown that collaborations between companies and universities have a strong, positive effect on innovation, productivity and the development of new methods, skills and techniques.” The study found that:

■ A firm is five times more likely to produce innovative processes if it collaborates with a Higher Education Institution

■ Collaboration between universities and firms has a significant positive effect on organisational innovation

■ New firms benefit as much as established firms from university collaborations

■ Firms with university collaborations benefit from broader conduits for networking.

Professor Howells continued: “It is clear that university-business collaborations have a wide range of benefits, and can play an important role in increasing the competitiveness of regional economies.

“However, it is important not to link the number of university-firm collaborations in a region with the quality of these links. Our research found informal collaborations, such as conferences, meetings and workshops, are just as important to innovation outcomes as formal collaborations.”
