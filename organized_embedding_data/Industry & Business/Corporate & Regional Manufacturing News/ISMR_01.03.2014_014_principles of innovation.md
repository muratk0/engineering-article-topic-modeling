# PRINCIPLES OF INNOVATION

[TARİH: 01.03.2014 ISMR]

Dr. Nicola Leibinger-Kammüller, President of TRUMPF GmbH + Co. KG, celebrates 40 years of TRUMPF innovation in the UK and gives ISMR her recommendations for success

TRUMPF UK recently celebrated 40 years of innovation in the UK and invited ISMR to meet its staff and customers for the celebrations. Dr. phil. Nicola Leibinger-Kammüller, President of TRUMPF GmbH + Co. KG, flew over to discuss her views of sheet metal markets in Europe and unveil new technology and innovation developments during the visit.

Since TRUMPF was first established in the UK, in 1974, turnover has increased 100 times (now £45 million for the UK subsidiary). TRUMPF delivered and installed the first TruLaser Fiber machine in the UK – one of the fastest growing national economies in Europe – in March 2011. Since then, it has sold 45 fiber laser machines into the UK. Today, TRUMPF offers a one-stop shop capability – manufacturing and supplying the laser; machine; beam guidance; cutting head; process expertise; technical support and finance, amongst other services.

## A long term view

“We are a family-owned company and have always learned to think long-term – more in terms of generations than of the next day’s share prices. That gives us stability, and perhaps it also gives us a clearer idea of what is required for lasting success. And it is the reason for the second pillar of our corporate culture: as an owner family, we have the luxury of keeping most of TRUMPF’s profits within the company, rather than paying them out in dividends. That enables our company to spend between 8 and 10 per cent of its turnover each year on research and development – that is more than double the average for our sector. Over 1,300 of our 10,000 employees work in R&D,” Nicola Leibinger-Kammüller told ISMR.

“Thinking long term is the basis for having the competitive edge. That is a pre-requisite for success in the market and for satisfied customers – and that, again, is the basis of lasting, long-term success. I’m aware that, during and after the 2008 recession, there was a great deal of international interest shown in the Mittelstand model of German SMEs – small and medium-sized enterprises. Yet the success of many of those companies rests on precisely what I’ve just described.”

## Innovate to accumulate

A key part of TRUMPF’s success is its ongoing commitment to innovation. The company is constantly seeking the technology breakthroughs demanded by its customers in the manufacturing industry.

“Innovation requires what could be termed a "fascination for engineering" – a fascination for creating and for shaping technology. I’m very pleased to notice that, in the UK, this idea is regaining popularity. In the German press, a board member from British industry was quoted a few days ago as saying: ‘We have to ensure that our brightest minds don’t land up automatically in the finance industry’,” explained TRUMPF’s President.

“In the future, we Europeans are going to have to make more of an effort on this than we have so far. In China, about 600,000 engineers graduate from universities every year – in Europe, our graduates amount to a fraction of that. It’s my deep conviction that if we, in the so-called ‘Old World’, still want to maintain our prosperity for a further five, ten or twenty years, we need to be even more aware of the importance of innovation, technological leadership and everything that constitutes our present-day competitive edge over our rivals from the dynamic, emerging regions of the world.”

An example of TRUMPF’s ongoing commitment to innovation is its BrightLine fiber laser development. One single laser cutting machine, using BrightLine fiber, can now process materials ranging from mild steel to stainless steel through to highly reflective materials such as copper and aluminium (1mm to 25mm sheet thickness).

The new TruLaser 5030 fiber with BrightLine fiber function is a breakthrough in 2D laser cutting giving manufacturers high productivity and quality in cutting thin sheet, but crucially with exactly the same attributes for thick sheet. Even stainless steel and aluminium, up to 25mm thick, can be cut cleanly with very fine holes and contours applied; previously, they could only be drilled. The combination of beam generation, guidance and focusing are crucial for the BrightLine fiber function and, as TRUMPF designs and manufactures all the elements, they are designed to work together.

BrightLine fiber eliminates secondary finishing – edge quality and process reliability are assured. A new multi-stage piercing process, used in conjunction with BrightLine fiber, offers additional advantages when cutting stainless steel. It prevents slag from forming when the laser beam pierces the sheet. This means the machine no longer needs to give the cutting point a wide berth and can execute even finer contours. BrightLine fiber also facilitates the removal of parts as they are easier to extract from the scrap skeleton. This saves time during manual unloading and increases process reliability during automated unloading.

Coventry-based HTA Group has just ordered two TRUMPF TruLaser 5040 fiber laser profiling centres with the latest TRUMPF BrightLine fiber technology, LiftMaster Sort unloading and sorting automation and a TruStore storage system. According to TRUMPF, the installation will be the first system of its size in the world.

## Key benefits of BrightLine fiber

- Higher quality (homogenous cutting surfaces, reduced burr formation with no follow-up work needed)
- Increased flexibility (up to 25mm stainless steel and aluminium)
- Higher process stability (reliable cutting of mild steel, material cost savings due to omission of waste products)
- High piercing quality (multi-stage piercing technique, better nesting)
- Minimum contour limits (smaller contours, shorter part times for complex parts, higher percentage of contours can be cut at full feed rate)
- Easier part removal (time saved with manual removal, higher process reliability during automation/unloading)

## FACE TO FACE

“There is clearly a move from CO2 to fiber laser,” explained Dr. rer. pol. Lars Grünert, Executive Vice President of TRUMPF GmbH + Co. KG and CFO of the Laser Technology/Electronics business division. “BrightLine fiber technology makes solid-state lasers more universal than before. The move, however, will take time but I anticipate that, in the next 5 -10 years, solid-state laser will be the dominant laser technology.”

A new TRUMPF option on its TruPunch 3000 punching machine was demonstrated for the first time in the UK to ISMR during its visit. A TRUMPF descending die moves downwards during sheet positioning and eliminates contact between the work piece and the processing station. Downward forming becomes a much more reliable process and the risk of collision, by using forming dies, is eliminated. As the die is no longer an interference contour, the machine is easier to use and the sheet can be positioned in any direction after forming. The descending die also results in quieter production and lower tool maintenance.

To provide similar benefits on its TruPunch 5000 and TruMatic 7000 combination machines, TRUMPF has also developed an active die. This offers all the advantages of a descending die and effectively provides a second punching head, able to perform an active forming stroke from below the sheet. This broadens the range of processing options and tool usage and enables new forming sizes and heights to be achieved. The sheet is no longer lifted by the tool but always lies flat on the machine. This allows forms to be created closer to the work piece clamps and material to be optimally utilised.

## A customer focus

Under its TruServices banner, TRUMPF has developed its SYNCHRO PLUS programme, based on the Lean Manufacturing concept, to bring improved efficiencies to its operation. This new project is restructuring TRUMPF’s internal processes to help bring speedier customer resolution, quicker spare part delivery times and better use of remote diagnostics to help its customers. The company is currently designing a Visual Online Support system (VOS) as an Android and Apple app to improve customer service and customer communications. Audio, image and video files can easily be exchanged with customers and, using it, TRUMPF has so far reported a 27% reduction in customer service visits and a 32% increase in rapid spare part identification.

To enhance customer support, TRUMPF has also introduced MyTRUMPF. This is a portal with integrated e-shops and a variety of other helpful tools and features. It is easy to use and tailor-made, so that customers can view their individual machine base and download all machine-related documentation. Appropriate training courses available for operators and programmers can be accessed via direct links embedded on each machine page. Spare parts and punch/press brake tooling can be ordered online.

The TruServices newly designed customer service agreement is being created by TRUMPF as a modular package with bolt-on options (such as software, week-end support etc.) to suit customer requirements. It will officially be launched at the end of the summer.

“Customer service is becoming a more interactive process,” added Lars Grünert. “Customer service is the future and we are devoting more time to this activity.” He confirmed that TRUMPF is also investigating the possibilities offered by additive manufacturing. ■
