# GM's first assembly plant 'fully dedicated' to electric vehicles

[TARİH: 01.04.2020 ISMR]

GENERAL MOTORS' vision of an allelectric future is coming into clearer focus and gaining momentum with a new US$ 2.2 billion investment at its Detroit-Hamtramck assembly plant in the U.S. to produce a variety of all-electric trucks and SUVs. GM's first all-electric truck will be a pickup with production scheduled to begin in late 2021. This will be followed soon after by the Cruise Origin, a shared, electric, self-driving vehicle unveiled by Cruise in San Francisco recently. Detroit-Hamtramck will be GM's first fully dedicated electric vehicle assembly plant.

"Through this investment, GM is taking a big step forward in making our vision of an all-electric future a reality," said Mark Reuss, GM President, during a press event at the plant with Michigan Gov. Gretchen Whitmer and other local and state officials. "Our electric pickup will be the first of multiple electric truck variants we will build at Detroit-Hamtramck over the next few years."

When the plant is fully operational, this investment will create more than 2,200 U.S. manufacturing jobs. GM will also invest an additional US$ 800 million in supplier tooling and other projects related to the launch of the new electric trucks.

Since autumn 2018, GM has committed to invest more than US$ 2.5 billion in Michigan to bring electric vehicles to market through investments at Orion assembly, the GM battery lab in Warren, Brownstown and the new direct investment in Detroit-Hamtramck. The plant's paint and body shops and general assembly area will receive comprehensive upgrades, including new machines, conveyors, controls and tooling.

GM's joint venture with LG Chem – which is investing US$ 2.3 billion to manufacture battery cells in Lordstown, Ohio – will supply battery cells for the electric vehicles manufactured at Detroit-Hamtramck. A key driver behind GM's decision to make the commitment to Detroit-Hamtramck was the support that this project received from the state of Michigan, U.S.

Detroit-Hamtramck operated on one shift of production and built the Cadillac CT6 and the Chevrolet Impala. Approximately 900 people are employed at the plant. The plant is being idled for several months, as of the beginning of February, as the renovations begin. The plant has built more than four million vehicles since opening in 1985.

General Motors, its subsidiaries and its joint venture entities sell vehicles under the Cadillac, Chevrolet, Baojun, Buick, GMC, Holden and Wuling brands. Its subsidiaries include OnStar, a global specialist in vehicle safety and security services; Maven, its personal mobility brand and Cruise, its autonomous vehicle company.
