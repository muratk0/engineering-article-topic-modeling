# THE DIGITAL REVOLUTION

[TARİH: 01.04.2018 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"Marcegaglia's new business model is based upon flexibility, responsiveness and agility"

Antonio Marcegaglia, Chairman and CEO, Marcegaglia Group

The Marcegaglia Group tells ISMR how Industry 4.0 has impacted its vision, strategy and plans for growth.

Industry 4.0 drives the radical challenges of innovation in terms of the digitalisation of individual processes; reduction of waste; saturation of physical resources; automation of production cycles and logistics; data usage and connection of various processes. The impact of the 4.0 digital revolution on companies has brought more efficiency to resources and production processes; quality maintenance and logistics, including the integration of processes (supply chain, production, marketing and sales) and the evolution of organisational models as well as corporate culture.

Marcegaglia, a global and independent industrial group in the steel processing sector with an annual turnover of €5 billion (2017), 24 factories worldwide and a solid growth path, has made continuous investments in research and development, products, innovation and technology.

As part of a strategy of consistent recent investment in technology across its supply chain, it is following the path of the connected factory by adopting a unified network to enable the re-engineering of processes from an Internet of Things perspective, with a totally digitalised supply chain.

## An eye on Industry 4.0

Today Marcegaglia, with its new plant investments and Industry 4.0 philosophy of digitalisation and automation, is ready to build on the future, aiming to connect all its production tools for real-time interaction.

"We made huge investments just before and during the global economic downturn," said Marcegaglia Chairman and CEO, Antonio Marcegaglia. "We grew because of this. Even though that growth was more modest compared with previous years, it was slightly higher than general growth levels."

In Marcegaglia factories, innovation has driven new solutions aimed at keeping maximum control over all processes, increasing supply chain traceability and highlighting possible issues, sometimes before they occur (with consequent cost and time savings on staff) so that functions can now be delegated to machines, without needing manpower.

For example, at its Casalmaggiore factory in Italy which specialises in the manufacture of welded tubes, Marcegaglia has installed an intelligent and automated system for the storage of tube bundles, moving them to vehicle loading areas when necessary. At its Ravenna production unit, the largest Italian steel metallurgy factory in the group which specialises in the treatment and finishing of coils, plates and pre-painted steel and is the nerve centre for its integrated logistics activity in Europe, technological innovation has led to the creation of a fleet of automated guided vehicles.

## Maximising value

But Industry 4.0 is not just about efficiency and the modernisation of production and supply chains, Marcegaglia told ISMR; only a deep understanding of customer demand, both in terms of quantity and product characteristics for which customers are willing to pay, maximises the value captured by the market.

"The McKinsey Industry 4.0 Global Expert Survey 2015 indicates that 92% of Industry 4.0 suppliers and 74% of manufacturers expect Industry 4.0 to have a direct impact on their business model. Now, a few years later, research shows that a new business model, combining products with services, has had a positive effect on business. Likewise, it is profitable to think strategically and long term for companies offering maintenance services especially with performance-based models," explained Marcegaglia.

"The essence of the digital revolution therefore also becomes the intelligent use of data to create value, sliding from the analogue to the digital factory and shifting from the physical product, overcoming the traditional boundaries of functions, production sites and companies. We are moving towards a new business model, based mainly on flexibility, responsiveness and agility and, therefore, more personalised services for customers," it added.

## Marcegaglia cold-rolled steel for vitreous enamelling

Marcegaglia's experience and expertise in flat steel products has led to the development of a new range of cold-rolled steel for vitreous enamelling. This type of steel is particularly suitable for household and heating technology appliances, for the production of kitchen hobs, oven interiors, washing machine and dryer components, water heater tanks and plumbing systems.

Marcegaglia enamelling steel is manufactured in accordance with EN10209 and supplied in the form of coils, strips, slit strips, sheets and straps. It offers high weldability and is suitable for deep moulding operations. Made in DC01EK and DC04EK grades, it is suitable for conventional single or double-sided enamelling, in thicknesses from 0.5mm to 1.5mm, and a maximum width of 1,515mm.

"Surface appearance guarantees the application of the enamel and its uniformity after application, offering excellent resistance and durability. The surface finish may be normal or rough," Marcegaglia told ISMR.

Marcegaglia's Quality department carries out a hydrogen permeation test on enamelling steels, using the HELIOS-II device (Hydrogen Embrittlement Line Instruments and Operative Sensor) patented by Letomec (a spin-off of the University of Pisa).

But the new digital ecosystem is not just about processes; it filters down through the company, said Marcegaglia, starting with the management ethos.

"It is important to take into account the effect that digital technology also has on management philosophy, in effect a cultural leap in awareness of the power that data wields nowadays in the positive evolution of a business," declared Antonio Marcegaglia. "The digital revolution can be a formidable accelerator for the entire country and for closing the productivity gap. We certainly need investment helped by fiscal incentives, but we need above all to focus on a kind of new mental software, an obsession with innovation, which, even in the most mature or smallest companies can, and must, lead the leaders," explained the Group.

So, according to Marcegaglia, the innovation challenge must become a structural process, a new cultural paradigm, connecting business with the future. And the process of continuous education must be universally accepted across the company, not just by company management. It must cut across all levels of the company as, in Marcegaglia's words, an 'obsession with innovation'.
