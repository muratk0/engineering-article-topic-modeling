# KMF works with schools to ‘inspire engineers’

[TARİH: 01.02.2013 ISMR]

KMF (Precision Sheet Metal) is working with local schools to help inspire the next generation of engineers. The UK subcontract sheet metal specialist is funding and supporting teams of 9 – 11 year olds who are building electric cars as part of The IET Formula Goblin Project organised by The Greenpower Education Trust.

This initiative by KMF is an extension of its existing ‘Young Engineer of the Year Awards’, supported by the UK’s National Apprenticeship Scheme, that are currently underway across North Staffordshire with 20 high schools signed up to take part.

“With the IET Formula Goblin Project we are hoping to encourage even younger students to consider engineering as a career option,” said Gareth Higgins, KMF’s managing director. “We have two schools involved at the moment, but we are actively looking to support other, enthusiastic, teams from the Newcastle under Lyme and Stoke-on-Trent areas. We are happy to purchase the Kit cars for the schools.”

The first school to benefit from KMF’s support was Our Lady and St Werburgh Catholic Primary School whose team of 16, Year Six children and one teacher visited KMF’s factory recently to take delivery of the Goblin Flat pack kit and make use of the KMF apprentice workshop to build the finished electric vehicle and gain a basic understanding of mechanics, before taking it out to test against other schools up and down the country.

The build-time for the Goblin kit car is approximately 15 hours with the children designing their own bodywork. During their time at the KMF factory, the children were supported by KMF’s apprentice trainers Steve Dalton and Nigel Tomkinson, along with third-year apprentice Zachary Peacock and maintenance engineer Glyn Preston.
