# The economic recovery in the tool and die sector is an important step for the progress and development of Europe’s manufacturing capability and sustainability

[TARİH: 01.05.2011 ISMR]

## Major ISTMA directions include:

■ Support and participation in projects promoted by ISTMA-E members

■ Promotion and interaction on joint initiatives with European and National Manu FUTURE Platforms

■ Promotion at ISTMA Europe meetings of the presentation of European R&D projects and their conclusions

■ The improvement of benchmark data in the sector and its introduction as a national benchmark provider

■ An EU directory for different technologies, based on a reference list

■ An ISTMA terminology directory.

On a European level, ISTMA Europe will continue its efforts to promote the relevance and the strategic position of the tooling, mould and diemaking industry for the development and sustainability of the European industry.

ISMR: WHAT ARE YOUR VIEWS ON THE CURRENT BUSINESS CLIMATE? WHEN DO YOU SEE AN END TO THE EFFECTS OF THE ECONOMIC DOWNTURN?

## JP The global economy continues to show some positive signs of global recovery, but:

■ The EU still faces a huge decline (-33%) in new home sales

■ Rising unemployment (many EU countries)

■ There are still falling commodity prices

■ There has been a significant reduction in foreign investment

■ There is a liquidity shortage (a big problem in eastern countries)

■ There are still issues with getting orders and the slow adoption of sources

■ There are budget issues in the public sector

■ There is a general drop in profitability.

Tool factories are SME companies and have an extremely important role in the development chain of every product. Each and every industrial product owes its existence to market demands and the creativity of the people in that market.

But, for an entrepreneurial vision to become a final product, a fundamental link is needed: the tool or tool set. Unfortunately, tool shops have suffered due to the large drop of order income in 2009 and financing problems in 2010 – 11. The operational problems have simply been too big for many companies in the tool and die sector and some national associations have reported the downsizing of the tool and die sector by 20 to 30 per cent.

However, there are now positive reports on market growth and I see more optimistic levels in order backlogs. The best situation at the moment is in the German economy and those companies involved in the automotive industry. The economic recovery in the tool and die sector is an important step for the progress and development of Europe’s manufacturing capability and sustainability.

ISMR: WHICH MARKET REGIONS DO YOU FEEL OFFER THE MOST POTENTIAL FOR GROWTH FOR DIES, TOOLING AND MOULD PRODUCTS AND WHY?

## JP Definitely, the most important regions for growth for tools and dies are BRIC countries because they forecast the highest GDP growth.

BRIC countries are achieving the strategy of rapid growth in the manufacturing sector and, as I said at the beginning, tooling is an essential capability for overall manufacturing development.

The growth of the manufacturing sector is closely aligned to the need for manufacturing equipment such as tools and dies.

Besides the BRIC countries, there is also potential in the EU market, especially the automotive industry which is showing growth.

ISMR: WHAT IS YOUR STRATEGIC AND TECHNICAL FOCUS THIS YEAR? ARE THERE ANY AIMS AND OBJECTIVES YOU CAN DISCUSS?

JP The most important focus for us is the improvement of business performance in the tool and die sector. The sector must employ many new practices and learn how to become more effective and profitable.

I believe that there is a strong opportunity for this in HR and the overall quality of the management process. ISTMA would like to involve more partners in processes which can improve sector power. We understand that strong promotion of the tool and die sector must be made for future prosperity and success.

ISMR: WHICH MARKET AND TECHNOLOGICAL TRENDS DO YOU SEE DEVELOPING IN DIFFERENT MARKET SECTORS FOR DIES/TOOLING/MOULDS AND WHERE DO YOU SEE THE GREATEST OPPORTUNITIES?

JP In terms of trends, I see increasing responsibility in the product development process, improvement and extension of the warranties for equipment produced as well as the employment of more analytical methods to test real processes and forecast process stability and process results.

I also see more involvement in the prototype phase of a product and greater study of new material fabrication.

In my view, the greatest opportunity lies in supporting intensive collaboration between knowledge/expertise providers, tool shops and customers. This respects the importance of the tooling sector and supports long term sustainable development of the sector by all stakeholders.

Toolmakers have to develop strategic transformation of their businesses based on:

■ The supply of Innovative Solutions for Product and Process Engineering (INNOVATION)

■ The constant promotion of Strategic, Technological and Business Partnerships on a global scale (NETWORKS)

■ Constant specialised professional training (EXPERTISE)

■ Consistent technological investment (TECHNOLOGY)

■ Strong financial support.
