# SETTING NEW STANDARDS

[TARİH: 01.08.2013 ISMR]

Stefan Fuchs, CEO of Goldhofer Aktiengesellschaft, unveils his view of global transport markets and explains how his company has revolutionised heavy transport solutions by reducing weight and complexity

ISMR SAYS:

“To meet the challenges of the future, vehicles must combine maximum payloads with minimum deadweight”

Goldhofer is a global transport equipment market leader in general and heavy-duty road haulage and special transport vehicles. With a wide and technologically mature range of products, Goldhofer meets a variety of needs for almost every transport situation. In its 100,000-square metre plant, more than 650 employees work on innovative transport solutions and produce engineered vehicles for payloads ranging between 25 and 10,000 tons for customers around the world.

The history of Goldhofer Aktiengesellschaft goes back more than 300 years to 1705. In 2013, Goldhofer acquired Schopf Maschinenbau GmbH, expanding its product portfolio in civil and military aviation and the mining industry. As it recently acquired an ARKU levelling machine, ISMR quizzed its CEO, Stefan Fuchs, for his views on worldwide sheet metal and transport market trends.

The STZ-VP semi-trailer with Goldhofer’s pendular axle technology combines higher payload with shorter vehicle length

ISMR: What are your thoughts on current markets in the wake of the global economic downturn? How do you see European/global markets developing in 2013? Will there be any specific regional pockets of growth?

SF: The effects of the economic crisis are still felt and many companies are still reluctant to invest. The inhibition threshold has not yet been overcome even if the first positive signs are there. Here in Europe, and also globally, the market is starting to pick up again and some industries are already quite buoyant, such as the power industry that is positively booming.

ISMR: Please outline current trends, technologies and developments in your sector i.e. automotive, construction markets etc.

SF: The trend in transportation logistics is clear – vehicles must combine maximum payloads with minimum deadweight. In this respect, we at Goldhofer have again set new standards and re-defined the limits of performance. At this year’s Bauma in Munich, we presented our new MPA technology for semi-trailers and initiated a revolution in the process. Our STZ-P low loader semi-trailer concept with innovative pendular-axle technology, which was introduced last year, has also made an impact in the market.

## years? How do you intend to achieve them?

SF: Our goal is to consolidate and extend our position as market and innovation leaders. New ideas, strong service and the proverbial Goldhofer standard of quality will remain key to enthusiastic customers. We continue to develop our innovative solutions in an ongoing dialogue with our customers.

ISMR: Please outline your main successes and achievements over the last two years, as well as any current or ongoing initiatives that are particularly noteworthy.

SF: We are proud to have launched a number of industry trends in the last few years. With the development of the STZ-P with pendular-axle technology, we re-defined the payload limits for semi-trailers and moved up into a load class that was previously the preserve of heavy duty modules. The development of the THP/SL-S and THP/SL-L as light versions of our modular systems, with extremely low deadweights, was another important milestone. For this, we developed a completely new frame that enabled us to reduce deadweight to less than 2.5 tons per axle line. At Bauma 2013, we offered a veritable feast of innovations, products and solutions.

## What new equipment have you your association with ARKU and how you are using ARKU equipment?

SF: For our pre-production work, we decided to use a FlatMaster 120. With this machine, we can process much larger numbers of parts and also obtain more accurate results with an annual volume of approximately 300,000 parts cut per year. Detailed analysis shows that the FlatMaster 120 delivers savings of about 600 man hours per year in the production department. We save time not only on the levelling process but also – thanks to the greater precision of the parts – in the downstream production steps.

In the production department, we use mainly plasma- and flame-cut parts, often with complicated geometries. Approximately 65,000 of those parts have to be levelled after cutting, about 95 per cent of which are between 0.5 and 30mm thick. Instead of conventional S355 steels, we are now making increasing use of high-strength steels S690 with higher yield points.

Latin America is likely to become one of the most important markets globally with a forecast gross domestic product of US$ 15.14 trillion by 2025

Latin America is well on its way to becoming an economic force to reckon with by 2025, spurred by transformative and global forces termed ‘Mega Trends’. Rising disposable incomes and a Generation Y population of more than 200 million not only makes this region one of the most attractive investment destinations in the world, but also a source of capital for companies globally.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan, “Mega Trends in Latin America”, identified over 10 key mega trends that will accelerate Latin America’s growth rate to 6.5 per cent per year by 2025. Fuelled by growth in the middle class (461 million), urbanisation (567 million people living in urban areas), increased infrastructure spending (US$ 1.5 trillion) and improved connectivity (1.3 billion connected devices), Latin America is likely to become one of the most important markets globally with a forecast gross domestic product of US$ 15.14 trillion by 2025.

“Increasing urbanisation is expected to bring about spatial changes to cities, and create five Mega-cities, four Mega-regions and two Mega-corridors in 2025,” said Frost & Sullivan research manager, Lorena Isla. “Urbanisation will also generate many investment opportunities in smart infrastructure and new market opportunities for innovative products targeting urban households.”

## Increasing demand for goods and services

The rising inclusion of women will bring this consumer segment into focus due to their higher spending power. Women are expected to represent more than 46 per cent of the total working population, holding around 34 per cent of total decision-making positions by 2025. The empowerment of this demographic will stimulate demand for personal products and services as well as luxury goods.

Meanwhile, almost 70 million people in Latin America will be aged over 60 years by 2020 which could cause healthcare expenditure to reach US$ 580 billion by 2015, giving a boost to associated sectors. Interestingly, most governments across the region are aiming to make Latin America a connected continent. The region is expected to have around 1.3 billion connected devices by 2016, including mobile phones, tablets and other machine-to-machine connected devices in urban and rural areas.

“The proliferation of devices will be driven by the greater broadband penetration and 4G roll-out of 2011,” noted Isla. “The robust growth of mobile subscribers, reaching more than 705 million by 2016, will support new business models designed for emerging consumer segments.”

Electronification of sectors such as governance, entrepreneurship, education, commerce and medicine will provide further impetus to the common goal of connectivity. Six major Latin American economies also plan to implement the analogue TV switch-off in 2019.

Growth in such diverse industries is likely to attract more investments to trailing sectors such as infrastructure. Airport, energy, telecom infrastructure and space jam are all likely to benefit substantially from investors’ interest in end user markets.

## Macro-economic factors

Looking at the region’s finances, the overall situation in the region has improved substantially over the past decade with the 2008-09 crisis doing little to abate this progress.

“In 2010, average external debt in the region fell to just 1.2 percentage points of GDP above the pre-2009 figure. Only in Jamaica and Nicaragua was external debt much higher than in 2008, and in several countries the 2010 figure was lower. Private-sector debt showed similar trends. While the region has continued to integrate into global financial markets (assets and liabilities with the rest of the world), the private sector has brought down its net external debt (IDB, 2012). The region has maintained access to international financial markets whilst keeping country risk indicators relatively low,” outlined the OECD’s Latin American Economic Outlook 2013 report.

The report also points out that, in Latin America, around 70% of GDP is produced by large firms while, in OECD countries, large firms contribute only 40%, with the rest produced by SMEs. So, while SMEs provide many jobs in Latin America, they contribute little to production. This reflects their heterogeneous production structure, their specialisation in low value-added products and the scant contribution SMEs make to exports (less than 5% in most countries). As a result, the productivity gap between Latin America and OECD countries tends to persist over time.

Unless SMEs can overcome these difficulties, says the report, their lack of competitiveness will continue, fuelling a vicious cycle of sluggish economic growth, poverty and slow structural change. Breaking the vicious cycle will require strong policies and institutions to tackle this crucial challenge for the region. Comparison between advanced economies and Latin America shows a sharp contrast in the cost of market entry for new firms.

## Defence procurement

Frost & Sullivan research shows that Latin America will experience high CAGR growth for air modernisation spending at 4.3 per cent for 2011-2020. The Latin American defence market is an attractive market for defence suppliers during the forecast period (2012– 2021). Stable market revenue is mainly driven by the strong need to replace legacy defence platforms. On the other hand, defence procurement projects offer Latin American countries the opportunity to not only increase security but to also improve the capabilities of local defence.

Brazil continues to lead the way in Latin American defence expenditure. Brazilian budgets over the next decade will be higher than those of the next five biggest Latin American spenders combined. New analysis from Frost & Sullivan estimates that the cumulative defence expenditure on new equipment procurement in Brazil, Chile, Colombia, and Peru will reach US$ 69.1 billion between 2011 and 2020. The majority of these procurements will be in Brazil.

Latin American countries’ stocks currently comprise a lot of Cold War and even WWII-era platforms. Long overdue modernisation drives are now being pushed by governments, and the global recession’s comparatively light touch on the region is helping them to do so.

End users in Latin America are extremely price-conscious, in many cases even preferring to buy second-hand platforms and domestically refurbish and upgrade them. There is an increase in the popularity of modular platforms, and demand for upgrade components and packages will remain high.
