# MACHINE TO CLOUD

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Lantek sees a strong future for sheet metal operators who embrace Industry 4.0 principles in their operations.

Lantek is a multinational company which aims to spearhead the digital transformation of companies in the industrial sector of sheet metal and fabrication. It offers its own software solutions in business manufacturing intelligence to connect machines and convert them into smart factories. It rounds off its range with CAD/CAM/MES/ERP solutions for companies that manufacture metal parts from sheetmetal, tubes and profiles, with any cutting technology (laser; plasma; oxycut; waterjet; shearing and punching).

Founded in 1986 in the Basque Country and with its headquarters in Vitoria-Gasteiz (Álava, Spain), it has focused on innovation and a commitment to 'internationalisation'. With more than 21,000 clients in over 100 countries and its own offices in 14 countries, Lantek has an extensive network of distributors with a worldwide presence. In 2016, its international business contributed to 85% of its revenue.

ISMR spoke with its new CEO, Alberto López de Biñaspre Maurolagoitia, to see how it has shaped its core offerings around the potential offered by Industry 4.0 and where it sees the future for sheet metal contractors.

## Industry 4.0 in focus

"We are very proud of the fact that we have already passed the milestone figure of 21,000 customers around the globe in over 100 countries. We are also building a very strong team – we now have over 30 new highly skilled team members. This reflects our success this year. From a business perspective in 2018, we are set for double digit growth in our business. We have also been introducing new digital transformation solutions into the market as it is moving towards Industry 4.0. We re-invest around 15% of our turnover in R&D, R&D is in our DNA and we are constantly launching new solutions into the market," Lantek's new CEO, Alberto López de Biñaspre Maurolagoitia, told ISMR.

Industry 4.0 has been a clear focus for Lantek and a pillar around which it has structured and built its core software and solution offerings.

"Industry 4.0 is our strength and we have therefore accelerated our own digital transformation. We have both the knowledge and the solutions to address this in the sheet metal sector. We can provide solutions from the machine to the cloud. For me, Industry 4.0 means increased competitiveness and exploring the benefits from data (e.g. governing and controlling the performance of the machines that customers have in the workshop capturing all the significant data from the machine's processes and company systems, transforming it into valuable information and then being able to use the data for knowledge-based assistance)," he continued.

"If we think about Industry 4.0, it involves a high level of machine and process control, communication, interoperability and intelligent decision-making. We have a clear view of Industry 4.0 and are focusing a large proportion of our resources into Industry 4.0 solutions for the sheet metal market. We have accordingly opened a new Centre of Excellence in Bilbao and are working with machine tool manufacturers in the sector to help them achieve their own Industry 4.0 goals. They recognise Lantek as a valuable and collaborative partner on this journey."

## Digitisation in the cloud

Sheet metal and fabrication companies are becoming aware of the benefits of digitisation and the competitive advantage it can deliver for their businesses. At the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover this October, Lantek demonstrated its new suite of cloud-based applications, Lantek 360.

"Using cloud-based applications has clear advantages for companies such as data security and data availability. However, some companies are wary of using cloud-based applications, due to concerns about potential disruptions to production. We have addressed these concerns by having critical data available locally at the customer's premises. This hybrid approach offers 'the best of both worlds' ensuring continuity of data availability and manufacture even in extreme circumstances, while utilising the power of the cloud to manage the applications being used as well as the security, availability and reliability of the manufacturer's vital production data," said Alberto López de Biñaspre Maurolagoitia.

For smaller companies, the advantages of cloud working are even more evident, as the cost of suitable computer equipment is high and, more importantly, the skills required to manage such a system are expensive and difficult to find. By moving to cloud-based digitisation, says Lantek, manufacturers can concentrate on their core skills of manufacturing products and reap the benefits that digital transformation can bring, along with the agility that is synonymous with smaller companies.

## Keeping customers close

Lantek sees it as part of its mission to help industry professionals succeed in their digital transition.

"In terms of business, we aim to simplify the digitisation process so that sheet metal companies can concentrate on their core business. In terms of markets, we understand that this digital transformation is a big challenge in the industry. The companies that make this early leap will have a definite competitive advantage and will develop closer relationships with customers and suppliers. In the end, their goal will be to make further efficiencies in their operations," continued Alberto López de Biñaspre Maurolagoitia.

The company has identified three main challenges: optimisation of manufacturing, improved quotation processes and the interconnection of factory systems and areas.

"The optimisation of manufacturing makes our customer more competitive i.e. increasing the productivity of machines, operators and raw material. This is the goal that we have for our products and systems (i.e. advanced nesting, planning tools and advanced data analytics modules). For improved quotation processes, we combined our 30-year expertise of machine tool software with capability, skill and technology in advanced algorithms, Big Data and the development of AI in machines so that we can virtualise the customer's manufacturing process and they can estimate final costs. This affects their profitability," explained Lantek's CEO.

"As regards interconnection and integration without the real Connected Factory, it will not be possible for our customers to work with the maximum benefit of productivity efficiency or even real-time business intelligence. This is essential."

## A vision for the future

Lantek's new CEO sees a strong and stable future for the company as it helps to steer its customers to a more profitable future around Industry 4.0 principles.

"Our main focus is to enable our customers in the sheet metal sector to be competitive. We believe we are the right partner and player for them in this area. This is a revolution in the market and we wish to be a key partner for our customers, to give them the tools they need for success. We see a big trend into moving into complete solutions, rather than just products. We are already in the process of digitising machines, processes and factories, with multi-vendor, multi-plant systems providing more real-time and hybrid cloud solutions," he told ISMR.

"Important trends, such as the move to solutions and Industry 4.0, will impact and shape the market. We also see a move towards a more integrated supply chain across our industry. This will require software solutions to enable customers, manufacturers and suppliers to work more closely together and to share efficiencies across the entire supply chain. Another trend is the gradual consolidation of sheet metal companies, who are reducing in number while they are growing in volume. So, we see increased collaborative opportunities here. These new lighter organisations will also need more specialised software products to run their lights-out production. In the coming months, with our R&D acceleration, we will therefore be introducing more cloud and hybrid products."
