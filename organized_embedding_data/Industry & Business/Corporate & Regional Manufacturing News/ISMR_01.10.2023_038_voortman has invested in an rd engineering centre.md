# Voortman has invested in an R&D engineering centre and hyper-modern Experience Centre, as well as a significant new company acquisition, this year.

[TARİH: 01.10.2023 ISMR]

Family-owned, second-generation company, Voortman Steel Machinery, has enjoyed strong turnover growth in recent years, and made significant investments to its facilities.

Voortman's headquarters in Rijssen, The Netherlands.

For over 50 years, and currently with over 650 dedicated employees, Voortman Steel Machinery has been a leading manufacturer of CNC steel processing machinery and software solutions. Its commitment to automation, quality, and customer service drives its passion to cater to a range of steel processing industries.

Voortman offers a wide range of products for beam, plate, pipe, flat, and angle processing, as well as painting and shot blasting, and has installed numerous systems worldwide through a network of international subsidiaries.

Above: Thomas Krone, Business Development Manager, Voortman Steel Machinery.

## Investing in the future

The company has embraced the digitalisation challenge and pursued production solutions which make use of the latest digital developments. It has poured investment into a new R&D engineering centre and a hyper-modern Experience Centre at Rijssen, The Netherlands, which opened earlier this year. Housing over 250 engineers, the new building combines development of Voortman's machinery and software solutions.

The new sustainable, gas-free and energyneutral building is next to the Voortman Experience Centre and is part of the Voortman Campus. Conceived as an inspiring, creative, green and high-tech working environment for employees, it features open workspaces, lots of light and green surroundings. Engineers in the new building work on technological innovations, implement new technologies and develop/test machine prototypes.

"We acquired a new company, Müller Opladen GmbH (a leading German manufacturer of pipe-processing machines) in January this year. This acquisition expands our product portfolio, by adding profile cutting machines for steel pipes, pressure vessels and structural steel. The integration of Müller Opladen is an important step forward in meeting the needs of our current customers by offering a complete product portfolio for profile, plate and pipe processing, and enabling us to move into new markets," Thomas Krone, Business Development Manager, Voortman Steel Machinery, told /SMR.

"Our three strategic pillars are automation, process optimisation and service. It is very important that our customers' machines are continuously running so that they can deliver products within lead times. We have therefore also invested significant amounts in expanding our global service locations to ensure that our service technicians are always nearby our customers. With seven global locations now, including well-stocked spare parts and consumables service offices, we maximize our customers' uptime," he continued.

## Adding value to processes

Voortman is a key player in the plate-processing industry, serving many plate service centres in recent years. The steel-supply market, which has benefited from disruptions to global supply chains, has become a very important market for the company in recent years.

The Voortman V310 is its most complete plate processing machine. The second version of this was introduced last year. This machine helps plate processors to expand their capabilities if they wish to utilize a full range of processes for plates. The new Voortman V310 performs fully automatic carbide drilling, full contour milling and 3D bevel cutting. VACAM scheduling and buffering allows users to perform all processes in unmanned mode.

"The machine really is a game-changer in the market as its multiple processing capability appeals to plate processors who are constantly looking to add more value to their operations. The plate-processing market is now ready for this type of machine, with easy set-up and start. We will keep adding new features to the machine and offering software upgrades and automation solutions to our customers," explained Thomas Krone.

"In the past, plate processors primarily generated revenue from the sale of steel rather than the cutting process itself such as laser, plasma or oxyfuel. If they add different processes to their operations, they add value to the product for their customers. As end users prefer to buy their products from a one-stop shop, plate processors need to act like a one-stop shop and make sure they can supply everything that their clients need," he added.

He believes that automation is becoming increasingly important, but pinpointed the "logistical inefficiency" in plate processing and the need for better traceability of the process. Voortman offers a range of tools to meet this need.

"Processed plate suppliers need detailed insight into their production processes so that they know where their product is in the overall process and whether they can respect the lead times discussed with their customers. They need to add value efficiently, ensuring that they know the status of orders and are acting in a transparent manner to become a true one-stop shop," outlined Thomas Krone.

## Automation in focus

To propel plate processors to 'one-stop-shop' status, Voortman offers a range of production planning tools, including scheduling and sequencing modules and EVI (Extended Voortman Insights), that display real-time production data.

“Effective production scheduling by utilising the appropriate machines at the right time is key for responding to and winning new contracts, meeting deadlines and maintaining flexibility. Therefore, after automatically generating optimal nesting programs, it is crucial to balance machine workloads and jobs to ensure error-free and on-track production," said Krone.

"To keep the machine running on schedule, ground-breaking functionalities have been added to our VACAM control software. The Operator Dashboard is designed to provide valuable support to operators, informing them of manual operations and plate- or job-loading requirements. Combined with the Unmanned Mode feature to bypass manual intervention for extended periods, operators can add value elsewhere while the machine operates autonomously. The Dashboard indicates when operator intervention is required," highlighted Thomas Krone.

Skilled operator and labour shortages have led Voortman to develop flexible, automated solutions that can run without manual intervention in lights-out mode throughout the night, for example. That means introducing increased process capability to the machines and focusing on software development.

"A great example of this is our new Fabricator automated welding system, which compensates for the global shortage of skilled welders in the steel-fabrication market," Thomas Krone told ISMR. "It performs fully automated tacking and welding of plates to a beam and features a range of robots and welding torches. It also showcases our skills in fully automatic assembly and welding. Operators can choose on the fly between 'Fit & Weld' or only 'Fit'. However, it is not just about optimising and automating the production but also the weld preparation. We therefore developed welding preparation software to run this machine via our DIGI-Steel company in the UK," Thomas Krone told ISMR.

Voortman's VACAM operating software, designed to be user-friendly, is included in all its machines. System component behaviour is the same on all machines running VACAM software, and operators can switch between machines without worrying about where commands and settings on the control screen are located after initial VACAM training. One-button automation is available on Voortman's plate-processing machines.

Voortman's V807 robotic thermal profile cutting system processes profiles and combines all processing functions to offer advantages over dedicated drilling, milling, sawing, punching, shearing and layout marking systems.

“Flexibility is the key. We sell the machine to companies that want to be very flexible to produce any shape in one 'go'. They can load a profile automatically and the machine will start processing all these features," Thomas Krone explained to ISMR.
