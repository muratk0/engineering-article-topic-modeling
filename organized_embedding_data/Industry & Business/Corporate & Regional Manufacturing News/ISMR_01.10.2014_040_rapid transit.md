# RAPID TRANSIT

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

The starting whistle blows in the Arena de São Paolo. The 22 men on the field do their best – as do the 65,000 in the bleachers. The opening match at the 2014 World Soccer Championship in Brazil drew thousands of enthusiasts into the arena – and that was just the beginning. 600,000 foreign fans cheered their teams live, on the spot, over the tournament’s 63 games.

To ensure that fans could celebrate in the twelve stadiums, intelligent logistics were an absolute necessity. In preparation for the games, Brazil adapted and expanded its infrastructure to accommodate the masses of people. “Bus Rapid Transit” was key for this. Dedicated bus lanes, priority at traffic lights and low-floor buses speeded up transit operations in Brazil’s major cities. Thousands of visitors travelled to the stadium in buses made by Marcopolo S.A., based in the town of Caxias do Sul.

## High speed buses

Marcopolo S.A. manufactures the bodies for the vehicles, which were in continuous service for the athletic events.

“Marcopolo buses offer very high standards of comfort and convenience, complying with every requirement and demand for comfortable passenger travel,” explained Nelson Gehrke, Director of Acquisitions and Logistics at Marcopolo. “Even with some delays in the building schedule, Brazil will profit from the infrastructure investments in the long term since the country will then have high quality public transit vehicles that are more comfortable, safer and faster. These are exactly the advantages that Marcopolo vehicles feature to ensure improved mobility in cities.”

The “Made in Brazil” buses are much in demand, and not only in their home country. In 2013, the manufacturer sold 31,000 vehicles – of which 18,000 stayed in Brazil. The remainder was manufactured in ten other countries where Marcopolo has operations. In spite of a weak market, sales in Brazil have risen continuously since 2009. And, in 2013, Marcopolo grew again by 8.6 per cent.

“Our customers want rugged buses, made to last, at competitive prices,” Gehrke noted. The company does its part to turn these wishes into reality. A total of 320 million euros is being invested in the firm’s own factories during the period 2007 to 2016. In 2014 alone, improvements will amount to approximately 50 million euros.

## Modern buses, modern machinery

Part of this extensive investment package includes seven TruLaser 5030 fiber machines.

In the past, the bus-maker used other processes to make up the parts. Marcopolo, however, wanted to adopt a system that would better utilise input materials and be more efficient and less expensive to use. Its choice was the solid-state laser.

“The low operating costs and high working speed convinced us,” said Gehrke. “We invest in processes and machines that enable us to work a variety of materials and let us make changes in the process at short notice.”

Those factors are especially important for this manufacturer, since sustainable mobility is a hot topic around the world. Not only passenger cars, but buses too have to be made ever lighter, more efficient and more eco-friendly. Marcopolo responds to more stringent legislation with thinner sheet metal, more stable alloys and alternative materials.

“Recyclable or recycled materials are being used more and more frequently,” Gehrke pointed out. “The sheet metal alloys and final designs are in a state of transition. That makes for a change in processing, too.”

In his eyes, the laser is the right tool since it can be used for different materials and at various gauges.

At present, Marcopolo is using the 2D laser machines to cut galvanized steel sheet of between 0.95 and 3mm thick, carbon steel from 3 to 4.75mm and aluminium sheet of up to 2mm thick. The batch sizes vary widely. It is no longer enough for those parts to be rugged and exhibit high quality. Nowadays, they often have to be stylish as well.

“The design of our bus bodies is essential,” Gehrke emphasised. The vehicles have to be attractive and modern in appearance. The manufacturer’s own design centre ensures that these goals are reached.

“The new Marcopolo models took the IF Product Design Award in 2014,” Gehrke explained. The creative team developed the Viale BRT Bus for rapid bus travel in major cities and adhered to current trends. Larger windows make for better viewing while interiors are designed for increased comfort – for passengers and driver. These are rounded off by the roof-mounted air conditioner and LED lighting throughout.

## Looking to the future

The market is changing rapidly and Marcopolo uses new technologies to stay in step.

“We concentrate on investments and innovations, whether in employee training or in developing even more sustainable and advanced processes and products,” said Gehrke. “Initial and ongoing training of our labour force is, of course, of critical importance.”

Nelson Gehrke is firmly convinced that Marcopolo will benefit from the investments in infrastructure being made by the Brazilian government. He looks to the future with great confidence as the Olympic Games will be taking place in Brazil in 2016. ■
