# STAMPING SOLUTIONS

[TARİH: 01.01.2023 ISMR]

KLEINER GmbH is a family-owned business in Pforzheim that specialises in precision stamped parts for industry sectors including e-mobility, telecommunications and medical technology.

ISMR SAYS: “KLEINER has 39 Bruderer stamping presses in operation, with none more than ten years old."

A company that embodies the idea of the family business is KLEINER GmbH in Pforzheim, Germany. Located at the heart of the stamping industry in southwestern Germany, it develops and manufactures tools, precision-stamped parts and more, using Bruderer precision stamping presses.

Thomas Kleiner laid the foundations for the business in 1985 in Königsbach-Stein, just outside Pforzheim. The company moved to Eisingen in 1998 in what is now "factory II". Since 2008, the family business has located its headquarters on a 18,000m² site in Pforzheim next to the Pforzheim West motorway exit.

The company offers constructive design of parts and assemblies. Firmly established series processes include cleaning, mechanical assembly and the fully automatic foil bonding of stamped parts.

2D and 3D design; prototyping; EDM/erosion; grinding; milling and toolmaking are all in-house departments, and the company employs its own development engineers for complete flexibility.

A family business

Thomas Kleiner runs the business with fellow managing director Joachim Hartrumpf, as well as Christian Hamann and Frank Chojinski who are authorised signatories. The succession plan is very much in place, with Kleiner's three sons Rico, Daniel and David already well established in the firm.

All three have taken on different roles, based on their respective talents and preferences. Rico, the eldest, trained as an industrial engineer, majoring in business management. After his studies, he worked as a consultant for a reputed SAP business and is now the driving force behind KLEINER's digitalisation. He acquired comprehensive IT expertise during his Masters' studies by spending several semesters abroad, including in the USA, France and Sweden.

The two younger sons are twins. David trained to become a tool manufacturer and studied to the equivalent of Master's level as an engineer and business economist – the ideal preparation for him to fit into KLEINER's production team. Daniel, meanwhile, earned an MBA in Information Systems from the University of Utah, having previously studied purchasing and logistics in Germany. Before the pandemic hit, he worked in sustainability and quality management for a U.S. company.

KLEINER punching grid fitted with EloPin.

"The most important thing for us, though, is that they can do what they want - they're not under any pressure to do anything. We've always made that clear to our sons," explained Jutta and Thomas Kleiner, who have formed a team at work and at home.

Thomas Kleiner is a man who does not mince his words. "We can't say that we're the best because there's no such thing as the best. We're also not the fastest, as we take care in our work. And we're not the cheapest either, as quality comes at a price. We're different, and all we want to do is deliver the best!"

That principle is appreciated by the company's customers, who include leading businesses from the automotive industry and companies right up to Tier 1.

“Our products are now primarily used in electric vehicles, which means that we're in a strong position for the future, despite rising oil prices," Thomas Kleiner explained.

KLEINER offers all key components for e-mobility with a focus on battery contact systems, high-current contacts, capacitor assemblies and bus bars. It develops series products and manufacturing technologies for 48V on-board voltage, battery management and all electric drive concepts in the vehicle. KLEINER has also developed a stamped high-current socket, available as a series product, for charging connectors.

Other industry sectors for the company include electronics, telecommunications and medical technology.

## High levels of automation

KLEINER GmbH's core competency is stamping technology, with operations running almost around the clock in three shifts spread across Pforzheim and Eisingen.

Around three billion workpieces are manufactured each year with material thicknesses of up to 5mm, using machinery offering up to 250 tonnes of capacity, 3,000mm of tool-loading space and 1,500 strokes per minute. Much of this is carried out using Bruderer precision stamping presses. KLEINER has no fewer than 39 of them in operation, with none more than ten years old.

“Modern machinery that can be ideally adapted to the growing digitalisation and automation of our company is one of our strategic goals," Thomas continued. “The impressive re-sale value of Bruderer high-performance stamping presses and high levels of demand supports us in this approach."

A clear illustration of the significant levels of automation can be seen in KLEINER's own in-house manufacturing cell. Technologies from tool- and mould-making, measurement and industrial robotics are combined to enable the fully-automated manufacturing of tool components. The manufacturer even develops and creates individual automation solutions for its customers, such as robot-steered mounting and parts extraction, from the planning stage right through to operations.

## Solution-oriented philosophy

The KLEINER tagline—"We think solutions" —is not an empty promise. The company's spirit of innovation and high levels of precision for contacts with press-fit zones has led to such revolutionary developments as punching grids fitted with Elopin (used under licence) and the in-house-designed KLEINER Flexopin which combines two printed circuit boards with just one pin. Its flexible length means that it can be universally implemented, making it suitable for automated assembly.

KLEINER boasts certifications including DIN 9001 and IATF 16949 for quality management; ISO 45001 for occupational safety management; DIN EN ISO 50551 for energy management and DIN EN ISO 14001 for environmental management.

## Eco and social responsibility

Ecological and social aspects also play a key role in the company and, in this context, it has consistently chosen energy-efficient production methods that focus on the environment and the use of resources, to continue to reduce CO₂ emissions, avoid waste and work towards other energy savings.

“If we're expecting people to switch to low-emissions vehicles, then we should also expect the production of those vehicles to be low-emission," Thomas Kleiner explained.

"We cover over 50% of our requirements with energy that we produce ourselves via photovoltaics and a cogeneration system. The whole building is equipped with underfloor heating, which is fed by the waste heat from our machines. We have also set up an environmentally friendly charging infrastructure for all e-vehicles in the company. We want to lead by example and show the way, rather than just talking about it," he added.

That also applies to the social responsibility that the family business takes for its 280 or so employees and people that live in the area. It is a leading employer and, for decades now, has been committed to supporting regional sporting and cultural associations, helping social projects and building ties with local schools. Around 30 apprentices are in employment at KLEINER.

With a view to the future and further solid growth, KLEINER is investing in component assembly to be able to continue to operate in the market in a targeted manner, oriented towards the wishes of its customers. Other mainstays are contract work and contract manufacturing in toolmaking, the design and construction of entire automation systems as well as product development for third-party companies.

Thomas Kleiner can look forward to a well-earned retirement in a few years' time after 37 years of service, attested Bruderer. Quality is still the watchword, and over decades to come, KLEINER will still be able to declare: "We think solutions."
