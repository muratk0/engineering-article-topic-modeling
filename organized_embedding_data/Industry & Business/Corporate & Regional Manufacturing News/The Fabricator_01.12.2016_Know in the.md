New Members Join FMA & NBT Boards of Directors, New Chairmen Take the Gavel

The FMA board of directors held their fall meeting Oct. 26 in Schaumburg, Ill., and several new members were elected.

Phil Kooima

Owner, Kooima Co., Rock Valley, Iowa

Phil started Kooima Co. in 1988 with one employee in a 1,500-sq.-ft. rented shop. The plant has grown to 150 people and 300,000 sq. ft. of lasers, press brakes, robots, tube lasers, machining centers, assembly, and logistics. Kooima Co. provides fabrications for the construction, agriculture, and recreational vehicle industries.

Carlos R. Mendizabal-Perez

Vice President, Industrias Selbor SA de CV, San Luis Potosi, Mexico

Industrias Selbor performs welding, laser metal sheet cutting, press brake bending, and powder coating. It makes accessories for the material handling industry and electric forklifts. Carlos’ current responsibilities as vice president for planning and development include evaluating new investments in technology and machinery and analyzing new business opportunities.

Jay Phillips

Plate Processing Manager, Valley Iron Fabrication, Fresno, Calif.

When Jay began college at Fresno State he started to work part-time at Valley Iron, a business established in 1958 that has grown to be one of the largest independent, family-owned metal service centers in northern and central California. Valley Iron offers a diverse inventory and has the ability to process flat-rolled products in carbon, stainless, and aluminum.

During the board of directors meeting held on the same date, the chairman’s gavel passed from Edwin Stanley, vice president of sales, procurement, and business development at GH Metal Solutions in Fort Payne, Ala., to Vivek Gupta, president of Texas ProFab Corp. in Carrollton, Texas. Vivek began his first term on the FMA board in 2008.

2016 Las Vegas Is a Knockout

Cutting-edge technologies, expert perspectives, and large industry attendance define North America’s premier manufacturing tradeshow and conference

FABTECH

®

2016, North America’s largest collaboration of technology, equipment, and knowledge in the metal forming, fabricating, welding, and finishing industries, welcomed 1,500 exhibiting companies and a total of 31,110 attendees from over 120 countries in mid-November to the Las Vegas Convention Center (LVCC).

FABTECH’s expansive exhibit space spanned more than 575,000 net square feet, giving attendees ample opportunity to see manufacturing’s latest innovations, interact one-on-one with key vendors, and learn about new industry trends. Throughout the three-day event, attendees also had access to more than 100 education sessions and expert-led panel discussions. A variety of special programming also was featured, including a powerful opening keynote from boxing legend and entrepreneur Sugar Ray Leonard and an appearance from cast members of the hit reality TV show "Counting Cars."

"We are so grateful to our exhibitors, attendees, special guests, and the city of Las Vegas for helping make FABTECH 2016 such a resounding success," said Mark Hoper, vice president expositions & media, FMA. "The enthusiasm and commitment displayed by everyone at the show to improve our industries were contagious. By participating in FABTECH 2016, attendees now have stronger, more diverse business networks and increased knowledge on manufacturing top trends and best practices."

Hundreds of live product demonstrations showcasing manufacturing’s most cutting-edge equipment and technologies took place on the exhibit floor. These demonstrations allowed FABTECH exhibitors to present their current product offerings to targeted buyers and generate quality sales leads.

Special event and panel discussion topics varied and covered important matters relevant to all of today’s manufacturers. They included additive manufacturing/3D-printing, how to attract more women to manufacturing careers, improved product development strategies, and how the 2016 presidential election results could impact manufacturing around the world.

For more information about FABTECH 2016 or FABTECH 2017, taking place in Chicago on Nov. 6-9, 2017, please visit

fabtechexpo.com

.

FABTECH is North America’s largest collaboration of technology, equipment, and knowledge in the metal forming, fabricating, welding, and finishing industries. The event provides a powerfully aligned hands-on, face-to-face business growth experience annually in the U.S., rotating between Las Vegas, Chicago, and Atlanta. FABTECH Mexico also occurs annually and FABTECH Canada occurs biannually. These events are made possible by FABTECH’s five co-sponsors, all of whom represent the varied and diverse makeup of the manufacturing industry. They are the American Welding Society, the Fabricators & Manufacturers Association International, SME, the Precision Metalforming Association, and Chemical Coaters Association International.

FabrinomicsWhat Do We Really Know About Jobs?

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

The latest jobs report was about as expected in some respects and highly unusual in others. It was nice to know that the rate of unemployment fell a bit and even nicer to know that wages are going up at a respectable rate, but several bigger issues have been exposed.

The first and most important as far as the manufacturer is concerned is that significant labor shortages remain. The lack of skilled and qualified workers ranks as the prime concern for many companies — and not just in manufacturing. Close to 4 million jobs are available but are going unfilled because the applicants lack the ability to take them. Rather than worry about "creating" jobs, the focus ought to be on filling the ones that already exist.

Of course, that is far easier said than done as this solution involves creating training opportunities and convincing people to take advantage of them. Much of the reported wage gain in the last report was due to the fact that business has to pay more to get the people they need, and they also have to pay more to keep the people they have. The rise in wages is good news for some and a challenge for others.

The data also made it clear that deep divisions exist as far as what people earn. The highest rates of unemployment are for those who lack a high school education, and the lowest levels of joblessness are for those with a college education. There is no surprise here, but the gap is shocking as the unemployment rate is three times higher for the less educated. There are also major differences between the rates for black workers and white workers — the black male is four times less likely to have a job than a white woman.

For manufacturing companies, the issue is that demands have changed as far as their workforce is concerned. They need a more highly educated and skilled worker than before, and they are no longer in a position to hire on the hope they can train them fast enough. The majority of companies are more likely to poach somebody else’s worker than to hire and train their own, and that drives wages up fast.
