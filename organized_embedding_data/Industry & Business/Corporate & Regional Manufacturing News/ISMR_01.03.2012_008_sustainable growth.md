# SUSTAINABLE GROWTH

[TARİH: 01.03.2012 ISMR]

CECIMO's President and Director General discuss European machine tool consumption, markets and trends

Founded in 1951, CECIMO is the European Association representing the common interests of the machine tool industries globally and at EU level. It brings together 15 national associations of machine tool builders, which represent approximately 1500 industrial enterprises in Europe (EU, EFTA, and Turkey), over 80% of which are SMEs.

CECIMO assumes a key role in determining the strategic direction of the European machine tool industry and promotes the development of the sector in economy, technology and science. CECIMO offers member associations the opportunity to make their voice heard on the European policy-making scene thanks to the influential role it plays in Brussels. They are offered the chance to sit at CECIMO General Assemblies and meetings where strategic decisions on the future of the sector are taken and common actions are agreed upon. They also become part of a broad European network with global connections and benefit from CECIMO services such as market intelligence and statistics.

CECIMO’s new President, Martin Kapp, and Director General, Filip Geerts, discussed European machine tools trends, markets and consumption with ISMR.

ISMR: What are your thoughts on current European markets for machine tool manufacturers, metal formers and fabricators, in the wake of the global economic downturn? How do you see European markets developing in 2012? Will there be any specific regional pockets of growth?

MK/FG: The current climate in the machine tool market is fairly positive. The European machine tool industry recorded 30% growth in production (y-o-y) in 2011, reaching more than 21 billion euros. Following a sharp decline in orders (by 50%) and in production (30%) in 2009 compared to 2008, the European machine tool industry has rapidly climbed out of the doldrums throughout 2010 and 2011 thanks to a strong order intake mainly driven by big Asian markets such as China. We are still below the pre-crisis level of approximately 24 billion euros recorded in 2008; however, finally the industry is back on the growth track.

In 2012, we expect a slowdown in growth. This is partly due to prospects which indicate a modest growth in the world economy and partly because the sector is coming back into the normal business cycle. Capacity utilisation is currently at its highest levels and the order books of companies are, to a large extent, full for the first half of 2012. Manufacturing output is currently growing faster than the global economy. Nevertheless, we should note that increasing concerns over the Eurozone debt crisis and fluctuations in financial markets might have negative effects on the future performance of the sector.

As for sources of growth, we do not expect to see any drastic changes. The Chinese market will remain the main driver of consumption together with other Asian countries including India, Taiwan and Japan. With a market size of nearly 30 billion euros, China is the world’s number one consumer of machine tools and accounts for more than 40% of the world’s machine tool consumption.

## FACE TO FACE

Mature economies have also shown an increase in machine tool consumption. After the post-crisis stagnation, last year Europe showed nearly 30% market growth against 2010 while the US recorded a spectacular consumption growth rate of more than 50%.

ISMR: Please outline current metal forming and fabricating trends, technologies and developments in the sector?

MK/FG: There is an increasing demand for high precision, high performance and flexible production systems to meet the needs of high-tech user sectors such as automotive, aircraft manufacturing and medical technology for the production of highly sophisticated parts and components. Moreover, customer industries are keen to equip their manufacturing facilities with highly productive machines to tap into the market opportunities proffered by the recovery and tackle increasing global competition. Therefore, innovation is becoming an increasingly important driver of competitiveness.

We also see the industry increasing efforts geared at developing resource- and energyefficient solutions. These technologies, which help customers save on material usage, slash energy consumption and reduce overall life cycle costs, are developed in close cooperation between machine tool builders, component vendors and customer sectors.

ISMR: What are your main objectives and targets over the next few years? How do you intend to achieve them?

MK/FG: In the face of constantly increasing consumption and transformation in global markets, our main goal is to help the European machine tool industry to prepare for the challenges of globalisation. This includes increasing competitiveness in exports, expanding to new markets in third countries, developing innovation capabilities as well as upgrading the skills of the existing workforce and training the next generation of engineers and technicians. The recently published ”CECIMO Study on the Competitiveness of the European Machine Tool Industry” unveils the challenges we need to tackle, but it also puts forward the guidelines we will make use of in this process.

Another important target for CECIMO will be to encourage and help facilitate the shift of companies to more sustainable production patterns. This requires a comprehensive set of actions including awareness-raising and communication campaigns, as well as support for research and innovation. CECIMO will play a strategic role in guiding the sector towards setting standards in sustainable production processes and products and improving their eco-competitiveness.

Last but not least, we will continue to promote the machine tool industry as a key enabling sector and communicate the contributions it makes to the competitiveness of the European economy and to the quality of life in Europe. CECIMO advocates maintaining a strong machine tool industry in Europe, which is a strategic asset for Europe.

ISMR: Please outline CECIMO’s main successes and achievements over the last two years, as well as any current or ongoing initiatives that are particularly noteworthy.

MK/FG: Over the last two years, CECIMO has been focused on guiding the industry towards opportunities which occurred during the recovery period after the global economic downturn. CECIMO streamlined the market intelligence and economic forecasts it provides to member associations and European companies.

Our forecasts and statistics are not only a major reference point for companies in making their strategic decisions, but they also provide banks and financial institutions with a sound global overview of the sector.

Sector-specific data provided to banks enables them to take their decisions more easily when companies apply for credit. Thus, CECIMO contributes to easing the access of machine tool companies to finance. CECIMO is also a major facilitator of European companies to world markets, especially through the organisation of EMO exhibitions. EMO 2011 was an overwhelming success with a record number of exhibitors and visitors. EMO provides our companies with a springboard to expand their business to international markets. This is significant especially in view of the strong shift of markets towards Asia.

On the policy and regulatory level, CECIMO has worked hard to establish a strong and positive image of the machine tool industry in the eyes of European policymakers and European society in general. Machine tools within the broader framework of advanced manufacturing systems are now recognized as a key enabling industry by the European Commission’s Communication on key enabling technologies (COM(2009)512). CECIMO actively contributed to the work of the High Level Group on Key Enabling Technologies (KETs) and to drawing up recommendations for European decision-makers to help facilitate the deployment of these technologies in Europe.

Another noteworthy achievement is the progress recorded by CECIMO in the preparation of a self-regulatory initiative as a support to the EU’s Eco-design Directive establishing a framework for the setting of eco-design requirements for energy-related products (Directive 2009/125/EC). CECIMO’s intensive research related to energy consumption of machine tools has led to comprehensive results. Its findings provided an important input into the study undertaken by the European Commission, which provides the technical, economic and environmental analysis of the machine tool industry.

Market surveillance was another point of focus for CECIMO in 2011. We have launched an initiative in which we will publish guidelines to help customs and market surveillance authorities identify machine tools which do not comply with EU regulations. These guidelines are published on a common online industry platform co-funded by CECIMO and other machinery industries.

ISMR: Do you see environmental and green issues becoming more important in manufacturing? How are you and your members embracing sustainability issues?

MK/FG: Yes, there is an increasing interest in sustainability-related issues. Companies are taking action by stepping up their innovation efforts and changing their corporate practices to embrace sustainable practices. The main drivers of this process are climate change pressure, soaring energy and raw material prices, and national regulatory frameworks. These dynamics compel the sector to generate solutions, which improve process efficiency and reduce the environmental footprint and the lifecycle costs of their products.

Sustainability-related action undertaken by CECIMO is based on several pillars. CECIMO makes efforts to direct the EU research agenda toward increasing the funds allocated to research in sustainable manufacturing processes and systems. This is done mainly through the active involvement of CECIMO in the Factories of the Future public-private research initiative. At the regulatory level, CECIMO is conducting a self-regulatory initiative leading to the development and implementation of the best methodology and framework conditions to help boost the ecological performance of the sector.

Finally, under the Blue Competence Machine Tools initiative, CECIMO, in cooperation with some member associations, aims to set sustainability standards for the sector, increase corporate awareness on ecological issues and trigger process and product innovation in resource- and energy-efficiency.

ISMR: What are the main concerns of your members at the moment? How are you helping to alleviate those concerns?

MK/FG: European machine tool companies are mainly concerned about the challenges of globalisation such as internationalising their operations and accessing third* markets, as well as facing global competition heated up by state interventionism in some competitor countries. CECIMO is assuming a pro-active role in explaining the needs of the sector to European decision-makers and influencing the European policy agenda. Specifically, CECIMO closely follows the free trade agreement negotiations of the European Commission with third countries and watches over the interests of the machine tool industry in these processes.

As a member of the EU’s market access platforms, we inform the European Commission about market access barriers faced by our companies in third* countries and about protectionist policy practices. Another important concern is related to the ambiguity surrounding the application of EU’s Eco-design Directive when it comes to the machine tool industry. Under its self-regulatory initiative, CECIMO strives to develop a sound methodology and criteria for the quantification methodology for energy consumption of machine tools. Meanwhile, we closely follow the EU study for the assessment of energy-efficiency of machine tools and contribute to it. We also watch over the compatibility of EU findings with an ‘industrial’ eye.

ISMR: Please let us know the latest news on CECIMO’s events and exhibitions. What is the news on the next EMO exhibition?

MK/FG: CECIMO is the representative and promoter of the European machine tool industry in global markets and at the European policy-making level. It will organise two roundtable discussion in Brussels with members of the European Parliament and the European Commission in the coming months; one focusing on the competitiveness of the European machine tool industry (based on the recent CECIMO report) which will take place at the European Parliament on 21 March 2012. The other one, based on sustainability and energy efficiency, will take place in May.

The next EMO exhibition will take place on 16-21 September 2013. The CECIMO Fall 2011 General Assembly in Zurich appointed Carl Welcker as General Commissioner for Hannover 2013. Promotional activity will start shortly. CECIMO will organise, as at previous shows, meetings between international machine tool associations to discuss issues of common interests. Blue Competence Machine Tools, operated by CECIMO and some member associations, will be present at the show with a booth presenting sustainable manufacturing solutions and technologies.

We are also considering organising an innovation booth (or area) at the next show where results from European research projects funded under the EU’s Factories of the Future programme will be presented to the public. CECIMO will continue attracting European decisionmakers to the show by organising meetings focused on policy and regulatory issues. ■
