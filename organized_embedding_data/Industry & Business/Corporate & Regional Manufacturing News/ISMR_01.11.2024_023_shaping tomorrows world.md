The CIDAN Machinery Group offers a comprehensive machine portfolio from manual segment folding machines and modular machine solutions to systems for fully automated sheet metal profile production. The Group was founded in 1907 and has around 350 employees, three factories and four well-known brands: CIDAN (metal folders and metal shears) from Sweden, Forstner (coil lines), nuIT (software) from Austria and Thalmann (long folding machines) from Switzerland.

With the power and synergies of a flexible manufacturing group to support its customers, and over 116 years of combined history, CIDAN offers a wide range of solutions and products. At the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition in Hanover this October, it showcased its entire range of sustainable sheet metal processing equipment over an area of around 360m². This included modular machine solutions, systems for fully automatic sheet metal profile production and its self-developed, user-friendly software applications.

ISMR went to CIDAN Machinery's stand at EuroBLECH to meet its CEO, Petter Hjelmqvist, and its Head of Industrial Sales, Joachim Schäfer. They shared the Group's philosophy and strategy underpinning its innovation, sales and service initiatives with ISMR.

## A focus on technology

At EuroBLECH (22-25 October 2024) in Hanover, Germany, the CIDAN Machinery Group presented a range of new and upgraded sheet metal technologies.

The Forma Z32 up-down folding machine, with a material-friendly brush table, was shown for the first time in Hanover. It was equipped with the nuLINK control system, which can now also be used to produce boxes. The newly developed nuLINK control system is simple, rapid and visual, guiding the operator to the desired result. The user-friendly interface, accompanied by interactive graphic instructions, guides the operator through each step of the process (from positioning, rotating and flipping to finishing the folded part).

The latest version of CIDAN's nuEVOLUTION sheet metal profile production software was also presented at the trade fair. This intelligent planning tool is designed to fundamentally optimise and digitise the entire sheet metal profile production process.

Two slitting and cut-to-length lines from Forstner were also on CIDAN's stand at EuroBLECH 2024: the modular nuSLIT and an approximately 14m-long multicoil line. The nuSLIT slitting and cut-to-length line is a compact, fully automated and programmable slitting and cut-to-length line. Options on the line are a six-roller straightening unit, guillotine shear, sectional shear and labelling. The MULTIPLE DECOILING LINE slitting and cut-to-length line offers benefits such as scrap minimisation, fully automatic material change and parallel loading of the coil stations during production.

The TD double folder from Thalmann demonstrated almost fully automatic, efficient sheet metal profile production. The sheet metal blanks are automatically drawn in from the side, automatically flipped and the finished folded sheet metal profiles are automatically removed.

The FX32 folder on CIDAN's stand featured a rotating combi beam and nuLINK control system with newly developed software for folding cassettes. The CIDAN FX Combi Series power folder is equipped with welded side frames for strong stability, twin-drive folding and upper beams, and eccentric clamping.

"With a wide variety of customisation options, it has been designed for flexibility and faster job turn-over," CIDAN told ISMR.

Also on display on the stand at Hanover was CIDAN's compact MFB manual segment folding machine. The segmented quick-change tool offers flexibility, and its small size and lightweight design means that it can be easily transported.

## TD Double Folder

The TD Double Folder utilises 50mm synchronised torsion drive shafts on the folding and upper beam movement. Because of the five torsion shafts, all movements in the bending process are precisely controlled to create complete parallelism along the entire length of the machine.

“This double folder is great at forming radius parts and offers strong longevity. Its accuracy derives from the rotary encoder application directly on the torsion shaft. The synchronised distribution of force to the machine completely eradicates the ‘twisting' that generally occurs over time. This means that the TD Double Folder will produce an accuracy of +/- 0.5° from its first fold to its last," confirmed CIDAN Machinery to ISMR.

## Trends, opportunities and challenges

Both men saw automation as a key trend in sheet metal markets, driven by the shortage of skilled workers worldwide.

“Since skilled labour is a huge issue, automation has really come to the fore. I also see an increased focus on ergonomics, as a result. Machine manufacturers must design machines that make an operator's work easier. We focus heavily on this area, making our machines user-friendly and easy to operate with semi-automatic processes for folding. This also increases the quality of the metal folding per shift," outlined Joachim Schäfer.

He also highlighted waves of material usage in different regions of the world.

"Our customers work with a variety of materials i.e. aluminium, stainless steel, copper etc. but there are regional differences.

For example, in southern Europe, we see more aluminium being processed whilst in mid-Europe, pure zinc is more popular. I see more use of stainless steel in folding nowadays, rather than pre-coated steel, to reduce the need for powder coating (so no more oily sheets). I also see more copper being used more in historical buildings (we supply architectural markets). You see waves of material usage in different markets," he added.

Both men pointed to clear opportunities in markets for coil processing lines, highlighting large material savings with reduced scrap, the ability to process different material thicknesses and faster changeover times. They also see further scope for growth for their 'connected machinery' strategy, including control of other branded machines in a production line.

“European markets have experienced a downturn over the past year. I expect Europe to recover within the next two years. In the meantime, we will continue to grow in North America and South America. We have our own company in the U.S. market and enjoy a strong presence there. We took orders on our stand recently at the FABTECH exhibition in Chicago and expect continuously good growth in that market," commented Petter Hjelmqvist.

Schäfer also highlighted the continuing success of the architectural market for CIDAN Machinery.

“Sheet metal is becoming more popular as a material of choice for the architectural industry (i.e. for facades in commercial buildings etc.). I think that will continue, and underlying market growth will help the industry," he added.

Both men also see potential growth from the advent of new technologies, such as artificial intelligence (AI).

“We are working with AI and assessing its benefits for our technologies and our customers. In some areas, it may offer enormous benefits. In other areas, it may not. This is a cost-benefit analysis, and it is about choosing the right applications for the technology. We are focused on how AI technology can make the life of machine operators easier. Where this makes sense, we will choose this route for AI adoption, confirmed Petter Hjelmqvist.

## A sustainable view

CIDAN Machinery has a clear vision of how sustainability fits into its founding principles and strategy going forward.

"'Shaping Tomorrow's World' is our company slogan, and we would like to be in the market for another 100 years, at least. So, to us, sustainability means taking care of customers, taking care of employees, taking care of the environment and taking care of each other. We adopt this approach every day with our software and efficiency initiatives to reduce scrap/waste and encourage recycling. For example, we buy back machines that are old (when we sell a new one), refurbish/upgrade them and sell them on," Petter Hjelmqvist told ISMR.

The company has also developed hybrid drives on its long folding machines, for further energy savings, and showcased numerous efficiency benefits across its machinery and software portfolio.

“We aim to develop and produce machines with minimal environmental impact. We moved from oils and hydraulics to electricity and pneumatic systems. This means that machines do not leak harmful residue or by-products and that they are quiet, energy-efficient and only consume energy when they are in operation. We have spent a lot of time and resource into investigating different sustainability solutions. Saving energy and saving natural resources go hand-in-hand with your wallet; it makes financial sense to make these changes for better returns," concluded Joachim Schäfer.

We highlight trends, technological developments and innovations in global HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) markets for manufacturers.

HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) systems are designed to create and maintain a comfortable and healthy indoor environment, no matter the weather outside. They play a crucial role in residential homes, commercial buildings and industrial facilities by regulating temperature, ensuring adequate ventilation and improving air quality. Analyst BCC Research projects the global HVAC market to reach US$ 346.7 billion by 2028, growing at a 5.6%CAGR.

HVAC systems are installed in two main ways: new construction and retrofitting or replacement. New construction refers to installing HVAC systems in newly built structures (such as homes, offices or commercial buildings). Retrofitting and replacement involves upgrading or replacing existing HVAC systems in older buildings to improve efficiency, performance or to meet updated regulations or standards.

Energy efficiency is a major driver for the HVAC market, influenced by stringent regulations and standards aimed at reducing carbon footprints

“Technological developments in HVAC systems have made them more efficient, smarter and better for the environment. New systems use less energy, saving money and reducing emissions. Smart controls allow users to adjust settings remotely with smartphones, while artificial intelligence (AI) and IoT integration helps systems to learn preferences, predict maintenance needs and optimise performance. Improved filters and ventilation also enhance indoor air quality, making spaces healthier and more comfortable," outlined BCC Research.

"Upgrading to advanced HVAC systems can cut energy use by 50%, and integrating AI and IoT can save an additional 20-25% on electricity, it added.

## HVAC in focus

Heating: This refers to systems and equipment used to generate warmth inside buildings. Common heating systems include furnaces, boilers and heat pumps. They help to maintain a comfortable temperature during cold weather.

Ventilation: Ventilation is about ensuring the flow of fresh air into indoor spaces and removing stale air. This is crucial for maintaining indoor air quality, controlling humidity and preventing the build-up of pollutants. It can be achieved naturally through windows and vents or mechanically by using fans and ventilation systems.

Air conditioning: This component involves cooling and dehumidifying the air to make indoor environments comfortable during hot weather. Air conditioners and heat pumps are common devices used for this purpose. They work by removing heat from the indoor air and releasing it outside.

## Smart HVAC equipment upgrades

Building operations and their hardware components are undergoing a wave of digitalisation by implementing smart technologies that enable convenience, comfort control, energy efficiency, and operational cost savings. Pressures to comply with sustainability regulations and demand for energy efficiency encourage building owners and engineers to develop optimised heating, ventilation and air-conditioning (HVAC) strategies to reduce operational costs.

According to global technology intelligence firm ABI Research, the current commercial HVAC hardware shipments' market is valued at US$ 161 billion and will grow to US$ 354 billion in 2030, at an advancing CAGR of 11.9%. This includes packaged systems and rooftop units, conventional systems, Variable Refrigerant Flow Systems (VRF) and multi-split system types.

Traditional HVAC systems have a binary function of 'On & Off'. Smart HVAC systems have different modes which condition the air based upon multiple factors such as ambient temperature, occupancy, equipment scheduling and energy consumption.

"Smart HVAC systems go beyond basic cooling and heating functions by bridging the connected and unconnected through intelligent climate control (temperature, air quality and humidity tracking), remote management, proactive, predictive maintenance and energy optimisation. Currently, over 75% of the HVAC systems are hardwired. As awareness grows over the forecast period by 2030, adoption of wireless connected Smart HVAC systems will reach over 55%," explained Rithika Thomas, Senior Analyst for Sustainable Technologies, ABI Research.

Building codes and energy utilisation regulations globally stress the need for efficient use of HVAC equipment, low-impact/sustainable refrigerant and optimal set points to reduce energy costs. Energy Performance of Buildings Regulations (EPBD); F-Gas Regulations; the Health and Safety at Work Act 1974; the Control of Legionella Bacteria Regulations 2014; and Indoor Air Quality (IAQ) Regulations are a few edicts shaping the HVAC landscape with a focus on refrigerant type and use, maintenance, environmental impact and a healthy workplace.

“Bosch Group's recent acquisition of Johnson Controls Residential and Light Commercial HVAC business has cemented its market share in the HVAC market. Established brands such as Haier Electronics; Honeywell International; LG Electronics; Mitsubishi Electric Corporation; Panasonic; Midea Group and Samsung Electronics are strengthening their offerings powered by Artificial Intelligence (AI), to cater to high energy-efficiency HVAC systems with low-frequency noise, efficient water use, smart control features and enhanced cooling capabilities," continued ABI Research.

SMEs digitising building operations and Building Automation System (BAS) providers (such as ALE, CIM, SensMax and Sensgreen) are partnering with established market players and commercial developers to prototype, manufacture, test and deploy smart HVAC solutions in commercial buildings.

HVACs contribute between 38-70% of a conventional commercial building's energy consumption.

"Maximizing energy efficiency in HVAC systems for commercial buildings is crucial to creating energy-efficient, sustainable and comfortable built environments. Smart HVAC empowers occupants to control the temperature of a space on a granular level from dedicated devices, such as a digital dashboard or phone, to achieve thermal comfort. Upskilling of building operators, technicians and installers on new digital and smart HVAC capabilities is critical to the success and implementation of connected HVAC systems. The smarter era of HVAC will drive connected living to new heights through comfort, convenience, efficiency and savings," Thomas concluded.

## Global technology trends in HVAC

Technology trends in the HVAC market include the use of connectivity and data to improve control; eco-friendly systems; software automation to improve business functions; 'smart' HVAC technologies designed for automation and ‘ductless’ HVAC systems.

Smart sensor technologies can, for example, manage interior temperature and humidity levels, along with lighting, and track outdoor conditions (such as temperature, humidity, brightness and position of the sun).
