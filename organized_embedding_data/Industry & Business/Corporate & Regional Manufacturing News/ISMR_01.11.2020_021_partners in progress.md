# PARTNERS IN PROGRESS

[TARİH: 01.11.2020 ISMR]

French toolmaker and stamping specialist, SOPIL, recently commissioned a new coil processing line from Dimeco for electric connector production.

ISMR SAYS:

“SOPIL and Dimeco have forged a strong and enduring partnership over the years.”

SOPIL, a family firm founded in 1961 in Pirey (near Besançon, in the Doubs region of France), started as a toolmaker and now also stamps small parts in ferrous metals and various alloys used in connectors. It supplies a variety of industry sectors, including the automotive and electrical industries, telecommunications, rail and aerospace industries as well as medical and household appliances sectors. Curiosity, innovation and ‘taking calculated risks’ are key elements in SOPIL’s philosophy, it told ISMR.

On premises covering almost 12,000m², with a workforce of 116, SOPIL produces more than 1,600 different items/parts in batches that can vary from 10,000 to several million units, using a range of 800 different raw materials. It works with materials such as copper beryllium (0.08mm parts) for the aerospace industry (50-60 million parts per year). It also produces 4mm copper parts for electric power transmission.

“We want to stay a family-owned business and keep our core competence in the toolmaking and metalworking industry. We also wish to increase our added value to customers without necessarily having to drastically increase the number of personnel. Technical expertise is important, and we give our customers a great deal of technical advice and support for tooling and stampings,” Thomas Lebaut, Export Business Development Manager (and future General Manager), SOPIL told ISMR.

## Toolmaking and stamping

The production of blanking tools has always been SOPIL’s core business and it has never stopped investing in people and machinery to maintain its expertise and keep production of its tools in-house.

“The sheer variety of tools designed and maintained at SOPIL means that our toolmakers are multi-skilled and adaptable. When it comes to fitting a new tool or maintaining one already in production, the same care is taken to ensure the reliability and longevity of the tool in question,” Thomas Lebaut, Export Business Development Manager (and future General Manager), SOPIL told ISMR.

“We design and produce tools for a variety of customers. We make around 30 tools per year, with 20 people working in our tooling workshop and 15 more working in tool maintenance. Many of our stampings are for electric vehicle part production and we see an increasing need for parts that serve this very important industry,” he added.

Typical stamped products from SOPIL include electronic and telephone components on carrier strips and complex stamped circuit boards. A wide variety of products are made by blanking or stamping from a large range of materials, in different grades and thicknesses (ranging from 0.08 to 6mm). From mass production of loose products through to components on carrier strips, including assembling parts by welding, riveting etc., SOPIL is making its mark.

SOPIL was recently selected by TOTAL in France to receive an award as a sub-supplier award to TOTAL subsidiary, SAFT in Florida and France, which makes batteries.

## FACE TO FACE

## Investing for success

“We have three workshops equipped with 35 blanking presses (Bruderer, Zame, Yamada, etc.), ranging from 25 to 300 tons, to enable us to meet our customers’ many different needs. New investments enable us to integrate the latest technologies to meet our customers’ requirements. Integrating new blanking machines and new production management and supervision systems, as well as implementing continuous improvement, also allows us to enhance our skill levels overall,” added Thomas Lebaut, whose grandfather founded SOPIL.

We have three workshops equipped with 35 blanking presses (Bruderer, Zame, Yamada, etc.), ranging from 25 to 300 tons.

“Although we have a lot of European customers, one of our largest customers is a Chinese automotive and industrial supplier which takes 10% of our turnover. We make a lot of high-volume parts for this customer,” he added.

SOPIL has developed a close working relationship with French coil processing specialist, Dimeco, since the 1960s – both companies were founded within years of each other, are family-owned businesses (now in their third generation) and are based very close to each other in Besançon. Both companies have also embraced the stamping sector, with SOPIL becoming a stamper and Dimeco moving into stamping equipment supply. The most recent investment of Dimeco equipment at SOPIL includes press feed equipment (decoiler, straightener and NC roll feeder) as well as a die cart, to load and unload dies from the press to the storage.

Dual decoiler, installed at SOPIL, with powered pressure arm for narrow strip processing.

“Due to the small size of the parts which SOPIL produces, we supplied low-duty machinery (light coil weights, narrow strip etc.) in May this year, during the first pandemic lockdown in France. The line incorporated a three-ton dual decoiler, with motorised straightener, and processes steel and copper (material thicknesses of 0.8 to 6mm and widths of 30mm-240mm). We expect the line to stamp parts made of thicker 4mm material,” explained Sylvain Touboul, who heads the U.S. business for Dimeco.

“This line, as well as others that we have supplied, is dedicated to SOPIL’s core business i.e. the production of small parts for electric connectors,” he added.

SOPIL will supply parts from this line to European companies making components for electric vehicle power systems for different European automotive suppliers.

“Working with heavier 4mm material is harder to feed into the machine, so we decided to invest in dedicated machinery to process this material,” added Thomas Lebaut. “The parts are stamped on a 300-ton Zame press, connected to the Dimeco equipment. We are delighted with the way that the line is running and have already showcased this latest investment to all our employees. We asked Dimeco’s advice on customising this line to our requirements, particularly as it is producing thick copper parts, and have developed a strong partnership with them over the years.”

## Dimeco in focus

Established in 1957, Dimeco is a French family-owned company specialising in coil-fed flexible fabricating lines, as well as coil handling and press feeding lines, with users all over the world. It manufactures press feed equipment for coil-fed lines; cut-to-length lines as well as “Flexilines” (LINAPUNCH flexible coil punching and LINACUT coil-fed fibre laser) equipment for press control and safety; quick die and coil change equipment.

It opened a new factory in Oiartzun (near San Sebastian, Spain) to manufacture flexible punching and rollforming equipment. Its most recent Joint Venture, DKMFS in Pune (India), manufactures press feed equipment and cut-to-length lines for the Indian market.

Dimeco reacted quickly to the postponement of the EUROBLECH exhibition by organising an open house week to welcome customers and partners in Pirey, France, from 12-16 October 2020, almost the same week that EUROBLECH 2020 was originally planned. It introduced new technologies and new solutions such as the LINACUT (coil-fed laser cutting line) and the LINAPUNCH (coil-fed punching line) on which parts will be produced, as well as some heavy duty decoilers (up to 35 tons) and high-capacity straighteners. Also on show were some 6-ton capacity ROTOBLOC STACKMATIK quick tool-change trucks, fitted with push-pull devices and other ROTOBLOC quick die-change and quick coil-change systems, the core of its SMED (Single-Minute Exchange of Dies) methodology.

The most recent investment of Dimeco equipment at SOPIL includes press feed equipment (decoiler, straightener and NC roll feeder) as well as a die cart.

“Dimeco celebrated its 63rd anniversary this year. Since the start, we have specialised in coil processing equipment, mainly pressing lines, which chimes with SOPIL’s activity. We have also developed our own coil-fed punching and other fabrication equipment for coil lines. Today, more progressive dies are being used for step-by-step operations in developed countries. We followed this evolution of the tool trend and now also supply equipment for large press lines. In our workshop today, you will see 2 x 35-ton decoilers, for example. We supply a huge range of machinery for small to large coil processing,” explained Sylvain Touboul.

Dimeco also offers die handling and coil handling equipment to reduce downtime as much as possible in the production line. It recently incorporated a new office in the U.S., where Sylvain Touboul is based.

“One of our major achievements over the last couple of years is our 2-6kW Linacut coil-fed fibre laser. We are still developing the technology – there are major benefits in terms of material savings, downtime, better markets for this technology to help us adapt our offering to the market. Fibre lasers are much cheaper to operate and maintain than CO2 lasers. You can also combine this technology with punching, for a combination process,” added Touboul.

Dimeco has also developed a new app which calculates coil/material/ production parameters to optimise coil-fed production.

## Meeting the COVID challenge

“COVID-19 has, of course, affected our operations, sales and marketing activity. Before the first lockdown, we had already taken measures to protect our employees. We made extensive use of the internet and teleconferencing to contact our customers. Most of our machines are connected to the internet so remote diagnostics was possible. Of course, travel was difficult, many exhibitions were cancelled and we had to reorganise our sales and marketing strategy as a result,” explained Sylvain Touboul.

Thomas Lebaut experienced similar challenges, as SOPIL stayed in operation during the pandemic.

“The lockdowns were difficult in France. 40% of our turnover is in the export business so we had to keep working during the lockdown. As a minimum, we kept one press working because our Chinese customer needed parts and had already got over the virus. Some of our customers gained market share because of what their competitors chose to do over lockdown. We saw opportunities to pick up market share ourselves, but we also lost business. One of our customers was at 120% of his production capacity during lockdown as he had to keep his customers going. However, we took precautions so that our employees could work safely. At the beginning of July, we recovered 100% of our capacity (everyone had returned to work). The second wave has been worse, as more people have become affected, but has so far not affected our production like the first wave,” explained Thomas Lebaut.

Copper coil processing on Dimeco equipment.

He believed that by focusing on its core business, SOPIL will stay a strong technical partner for its customers. He has been surprised at how quickly the concept of home working caught on in France and, from SOPIL’s perspective, will keep the practice going into the future for staff.

“Within days, 10% of our workforce were working from home at the start of the lockdown. We had never implemented home working before, so this was a major change for us. However, technology and teleconferencing made this possible. Now, I think we will continue with this going forward for a percentage of our staff as it has proved popular,” he told ISMR.

He also believes that COVID-19 has driven more manufacturers to want to know the origin of their tooling, to ensure certainty of supply.

Protective equipment around the coil line.

“Since the start of the pandemic, some industries collapsed and some boomed. Many manufacturers held off on investment during this period. When the economy collapses, it will inevitably rise as confidence returns and people will start to re-invest. We will continue to explore key market sector strengths so we are ready for when the market inevitably recovers,” Sylvain Touboul told ISMR.

## Strategies and targets

Thomas Lebaut is determined to drive greater market share for SOPIL in specialist industry rail and medical sectors.

“We run an ERP system and are currently implementing an MES system to reduce levels of paperwork. We use tablets to control the machines on the shop floor and for quality checks. We can also view machine data directly on the tablet. In the future, I believe that technologies such as Artificial Intelligence will have a greater role to play for our industry in terms of data management, machine maintenance and bottleneck predictions etc. I am also keeping an eye on new technologies, such as metal 3D printing,” he told ISMR.

Sylvain Touboul is similarly determined to grow Dimeco’s business, particularly in the U.S.

“Our main concern is to improve the levels of automation around our machines, such as the line we supplied to SOPIL. The aim is to increase operator safety and reduce downtime as much as possible. Changing coils manually takes time whereas automatic positioning, automatic settings of the straightener, automatic head adjustments etc. take just seconds. These are significant time savings that translate into higher production levels,” he explained.

“Automation has become so much more important for overall productivity. Dimeco machines are all Industry 4.0-ready, and can interface with customer ERP systems,” he concluded.
