# THE REVOLUTION OF EFFICIENCY

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

A book written by Andrea Dallan (CEO, DALLAN SpA) enjoyably illustrates the rewards of being open to innovation as a manufacturer – reducing waste, increasing productivity, increasing quality and saving time, material and cost. Leveraging the four fundamental concepts of efficiency, productivity, flexibility and automation, and offering a wealth of practical tips and advice borne of experience, Andrea Dallan outlines several key principles of industrial success.

ISMR caught up with Andrea Dallan to find out more, and to chronicle his company’s response to the COVID-19 pandemic.

ISMR: Congratulations on your new book! Please summarise its central points. What sparked the idea of writing this book?

AD: DALLAN does not just provide coil processing and roll forming machines to customers – we also give them advice and help them to generate profit by making efficiencies. When we, or one of our customers, had a production-related problem, 80 per cent of the time it was down to one of the following reasons:

■ Efficiency – linked to waste (such as raw materials, energy consumption, and others)

■ Productivity – starting production of a new product or increasing product volumes on a line.

■ Flexibility – due to the variability of demand and the decreasing size of batches.

■ Automation and simplification - linked to shortages of specialised technical staff and the amount of time spent handling products, which produces no added value.

I decided to write about how we have helped our customers overcome challenges relating to these four fundamental issues and illustrate certain principles of Lean Production, applied to the world of thin sheet metal processing. The second part of the book shows how these concepts led to the development of extraordinary machinery, such as the coil-to-punch/laser, coil-to-window, coil-to-pack and coil-to-box systems. The third part of the book contains some of the tools I use to, for example, calculate system payback and total production costs etc.
