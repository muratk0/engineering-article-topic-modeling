# Integrated evolution in action

[TARİH: 01.01.2026 The Fabricator]

How Epta and Steelcomp demonstrate the strength of closely integrated storage and metal processing

By

Robert Colman

"Evolve by integration" is Prima Power’s slogan for its strategic commitment to enabling its clients to evolve and excel through the power of integration. There’s no better way to demonstrate the point than by speaking to two very different customers who have achieved significant efficiencies through such technological integration.

COOL ASSETS: EPTA’S AUTOMATION BLUEPRINT

Epta Group is a privately held, Italy-based company that specializes in commercial refrigeration for the retail; food and beverage; and hotel, restaurant, and catering sectors. It has 11 manufacturing facilities around the world, with three based in Italy.

The company has grown through the steady acquisition of existing brands that serve the same market space. Its Casale Monferrato facility, which recently hosted a press tour, was acquired with the purchase of IARP in 2013. "This facility is dedicated about 80% to the food and beverage market," noted Davide Corallino, industrialization manager at the facility.

Corallino noted that at 75,000 sq. m. and 600 people, the Casale Monferrato site is the second largest for the company. (The largest is its Limana facility, which employs 1,200.) It produces such appliances as vending machines, scooped ice cream fridges, and vertical and semivertical cabinets with and without doors, of varying heights and with varying features.

"Throughout the facility, which has four production lines assembling a variety of products, the metal shop is the most vertically integrated," said Corallino.

Prima Power has a long history working with Epta. "In the 1990s, Epta had several stand-alone machines and had a one-piece flow at its Limana factory," said Riccardo Pellegrini, sales manager, Italy, for Prima Power. "The owner of the company wanted to add automation and in 1999 engaged with us to introduce our automation solution. In this way, we grew together because the customer asked us to help integrate software and came to us to determine howto process certain parts. What we both learned helped both in Epta’s facility but also our other customers."

That partnership also became a blueprint for other Epta facilities around the world.

In 2019, the Epta Casale Monferrato location installed its Night Train material storage andretrieval system with 400 cassettes for either raw material or cut parts, paired with a PSBB (punch, shear, buffering, and bending) line, which includes a punch machine with a right-angle shear; a buffering station with a picking and stacking robot where parts can be either reoriented for the panel bender, returned to the storage system, or put aside for processing elsewhere in the shop; and a panel bender.

The storage system generally holds about 70% raw materials and 30% semifinished materials waiting to be formed as needed on the production line.

"The goal for the customer was to have a very productive, unmanned, flexible automatedline," said Pellegrini. "The design of the system means that you don’t have torun the same parts or components for hours. The line can be adjusted for rush jobs or prototypes as necessary. The use of the right-angle shear means that material can be saved using common cut lines between parts."

A short two years later, the company added a stand-alone SBe6 Shear Brilliance punching and shearing combination machine to the other side of the Night Train, as well as another stand-alone panel bender. The SBe6 allows for a larger selection of tools, which means the only time the company has to switch tools out is for maintenance.

Perhaps most interesting from an integration standpoint is that at the end of the PSBB line, Epta has integrated a third-party press brake operated by a robotic arm.

"We understand that our customers work with other vendors," said Pellegrini."What is important for them is that they evolve the right way for their needs. Being able to integrate other companies’ machines with our system is important for that reason."

The press brake can retrieve materials from the panel bender or from the Night Train directly. It is contained in its own safety area so that if something needs to be fixed in its operations, the PSBB line can continue to run. If something has to be adjusted in the PSBB line, the roboticized press brake can continue to run.

For this whole system, there are two operators and three programmers. The machine can run seven days a week, three shifts as necessary.

CONTRACT FLEXIBILITY: STEELCOMP’S LEAN PROCESSING

Large automated systems seem a natural fit for an OEM like Epta. However varied production might be, the end result is an in-house-designed appliance.

Finland-based Steelcomp is a different proposition. The company is a subcontractor manufacturing everything from sheet metal parts to welded steel structures and product assemblies.

Automated material handling moves a blank into position on a panel bender at Epta Group’s Casale Monferrato facility.

The company’s Kauhava, Finland, location focuses on punching, forming, joining, and bending assemblies in sheet thicknesses between 0.5 and 6 mm. The factory has 22 people on the shop floor and seven in the office. Its current roster of jobs includes everything from long-run parts for refrigeration and electrical distribution to short-run and prototype work.

The Kauhava factory moved to its current location in 2022. The layout of the new building allowed the company to introduce a 600-cassette-capacity Night Train storage and retrieval system. At the same time, the company introduced a new Combi Genius laser/punch machine and a Shear Brilliance machine to the shop floor. These run on one side of the Night Train, while the company’s two older Prima punch/shear machines (purchased when the company was founded in 2006) run on the other side of the storage system.

"The older machines were very easy to integrate with the new storage system," said company representative Daniel Tot-Egete. "That’s something we really appreciate about the Prima Power machines—the software is nicely developed and helps to integrate all the machines together. It’s also easier to educate our people because whether you are working on the punch/laser or the punch/shear, the software is streamlined, a similar environment."

The company has seen a number of benefits from adding the storage system and new machines.

"One of the main differences is that we don’t have a lot of forklifts moving around the shop," said Tot-Egete. "That automatically makes the space around the machines safer. There is also less opportunity to damage raw materials. We have two people who run the machines during a shift, and that includes loading materials into the storage system as necessary. We use about 500 of the 600 available cassettes in the system. The storage system is always working to position materials closer to the machines on which they will be processed, soeven in the storage system, time savings are being found. For this, I credit our team. They are not just content that the system works; they look for opportunities to make it a little bit more optimized."

The Combi Genius has been a game changer as well.

"If anything, we overused this machine when we first introduced it," said Tot-Egete. "There was a bit of a learning curve because it was the first laser we’d introduced to our operations. But it is so easy to program and so fast that we wanted to use it all the time. Now that the excitement has cooled down a bit, we try to be smarter about how we divide up the work. The important point is that it’s a flexible technology that we can use as necessary. Everything that is geometrically simple goes to the punches; anything with complicated shapes goes to the punch/laser. That’s our general rule now."

The Shear Brilliance machine, meanwhile, has a larger tool carriage, which means the shop doesn’t have to change out tools as often.

Six people have been trained to run this side of the facility, alongside four programmers. In a shop with 22 people on the shop floor, it’s a remarkable testament to efficient production.

"What we really need is more automation in our press brake department now to keep up with production on this side of the building," said Tot-Egete.

Both companies have gained greater efficiencies with this investment. In addition, it is straightforward to pull production statistics from the storage system and newer machines to helpbetter understand material usage and flow.
