# Investment, robustness and an international focus. Building on this foundation, Ficep continues to develop and consolidate its activities in Italy and overseas.

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

Since its foundation in 1930, the synergy between expertise and entrepreneurial vision at Ficep S.p.A. has enabled the company to achieve and consolidate a leading position in the manufacturing of innovative CNC machines and systems for the steel construction and forging industries. Continuous growth has been possible through continuous and sizeable investments in the company, even throughout the most testing years of economic downturn and recession.

Ficep’s turnover for 2013, in global terms, reached 120 million euros.

“These investment decisions,” explained Barbara Colombo, Deputy Financial Chairman, Ficep S.p.A., “have allowed us to establish a level of capital strength that has made our company solid, from a financial and capital perspective, and established our reputation as a trustworthy partner.”

Ficep’s turnover for 2013, in global terms, reached 120 million euros. Approximately 300 employees work at its main site in Gazzada Schianno (VA, Italy) and 500 employees in total work across the Group.

## An international focus

“We are able to operate on a global scale, aided by all-inclusive insurance policies that reassure our customers and enable us to participate in global tenders,” added Barbara Colombo.

First and foremost for the company is its international presence. Ficep has always believed in de-localisation and strong technical after-sales service in its customers’ countries.

As Barbara Colombo explained, “Qualified, reliable overseas personnel, with whom we have worked for many years, enable us to export more than 85 per cent of our production, 74 per cent of which is outside the European Union.”

An extensive and diversified presence includes twelve sales offices offering technical support and spare parts in France; Spain; the United Kingdom; Germany; Ireland; the United States of America; the Middle East; Hong Kong; Russia; India and South America as well as more than 100 agents in other countries.

Barbara Colombo, Deputy Financial Chairman, Ficep S.p.A. at Gazzada Schianno (VA), Italy

“The strategy that we have employed is to invest in our subsidiaries in terms of qualified staff and facilities equipped with offices and showrooms, repeating the model established at our main facility in Gazzada Schianno for years. Every subsidiary can invite the endcustomer to its own offices and demonstrate a working machine model for each family of steel fabrication products: machines to process beams, sheet metal plates and angle bars,” Barbara Colombo outlined to ISMR.

In addition to the services offered, training plays a key role in the company’s growth. The ultimate objective is to design and build reliable and productive machinery for the industry. But staff training, expertise and knowledge are also important to the point where, as Ficep says, ‘it sometimes becomes the discriminating factor and the difference between a job well done and a bad choice’. Ficep has embraced this philosophy for many years, constantly increasing its efforts on this front. 》

## The “turnkey” factory

In accordance with its training strategy, the company recently modernised its Gazzada Schianno main facility with the construction of a new 2,400 sq. m. dynamic showroom and building which houses the Ficep Academy.

“The machines on show are fully operational and can perform all the main operations required by customers in this sector, starting from real and sometimes complex tests of designed workpieces to be produced on the demo machines,” added Barbara Colombo.

Around ten operational medium-large scale models are in the showroom, all of which feature loading and unloading systems to satisfy diverse technical performance criteria.

## The Ficep Academy

It is known as the Ficep Academy and is the final piece of Ficep’s training philosophy. Training courses are held in the six training rooms and two conference rooms at its Gazzada Schianno (VA) facility, split over two levels and covering a total area of 1,500 sq. m. The aim is to constantly provide up-to-date information, so that the company’s technicians and sales staff are always informed of the latest products available, while customers can benefit from a training package with their machine and subsequent ad hoc courses on other machines, explaining any new functions introduced etc.

Ficep Academy also offers other facilities, such as conference rooms (where technical seminars and specialist fora are organised). Guests are selected based upon their respective production types and demand as well as the geographical market to which they belong. Speakers, who are specialists in their field, may from time to time tackle a specific sector and examine the most suitable technological and productive solutions based upon market demands, from product design through to construction, with the appropriate machines.

It is possible to test-run punching, drilling, marking, scribing, milling and sandblasting processes, as well as thermal cutting (or plasma cutting and oxyfuel cutting) and shearing. All of the typical machined pieces common in modern steel fabrication, such as angular profiles, plates, sheet metal plates, beams and dimensionally geometric tubes, demonstrate what the machines can do in practice.

“However, demand for ever-increasing productivity is growing, with the option of embracing the output of an entire ‘turnkey’ production system, rather than just a single machine,” Ficep pointed out.

As Barbara Colombo explained: “It is not unusual for our clients to be confronted with the need to design and build entire facilities. Rather than just individual machines, designing the entire layout of a facility to achieve required production levels is becoming more and more necessary.”

Errors with the facility logistics or allocating too much space unnecessarily for certain sections could lead to a waste of resources which would otherwise be saved, used or optimised in other, more profitable ways. That is why Ficep now supplies “turnkey” production facilities to fully cater to the demands of those customers who decide to start (or re-start) from scratch with new business capacities and branding.

As Barbara Colombo noted: “Some of the best technicians in our company work in the new engineering office. These are qualified technicians who, over the course of their careers, have accumulated and developed an invaluable wealth of expertise and experience.”

Starting from the target market in which the customer is working, through to his annual production capacity and finally to key success factors (which could include quality; process efficiency and affordability; speed and optimisation of order processing etc.), Ficep manages down to the smallest detail to achieve an effective result for the customer, without compromising on the ultimate overall quality of the process.

“We use software,” confirmed Barbara Colombo, “that can simulate the entire factory, enabling a probable result to be obtained and shared with the customer before starting actual construction.”

Thanks to a number of close partnerships with leading companies in the sector, Ficep can supply machinery and services to the customer which it does not itself directly produce. These services include contractual matters, the design and construction of industrial buildings and production facilities, or even the supply of specialist devices.

“The advantage,” as Barbara Colombo explained, “is simple: the customer only needs to speak with one supplier, who then takes care of every single detail for the desired result, without detracting from his own human resources.”

## Continuous innovation culture

Technological innovation, growth and positive market results (and share) should become an integral part of a more complex operation with one single objective, Ficep told ISMR. Aspects most intimately linked to Research and Development must not be overlooked, it explained, as they are essential to a company’s ability to look to the future and engage (or even anticipate) the market and its requirements…

As Barbara Colombo concluded, “At Ficep, the Research and Development team is made up of around fifty people, including engineers, specialist technicians and CAD/CAM operators, who can count on the direct support of the manufacturing department for in-field testing and the creation of prototypes. This is a department that represents the beating heart of the continuous innovation activity that has always defined our company, which currently is allocated more than 4 per cent of our annual turnover.” ■
