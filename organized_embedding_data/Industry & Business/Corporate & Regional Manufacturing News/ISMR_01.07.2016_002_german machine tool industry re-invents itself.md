# German machine tool industry re-invents itself

[TARİH: 01.08.2016 ISMR]

"FROM A POSITION of strength, German machine tool manufacturers have to utilise changes in the markets, among customers, in technology and in the products themselves, to generate new opportunities for enhanced competitiveness," commented Dr. Heinz-Jürgen Prokop, Chairman of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders Association), speaking at a recent anniversary press conference in Frankfurt am Main. 400 invited guests from the business and academic communities, media and trade associations celebrated with a ceremony and a summer gala evening themed around "125 Years of the VDW"

One of the major tasks for the near future, said the VDW, is to open up new high-growth markets. At present, the sector is confronted with substantial shifts in the export structure, according to the VDW Chairman's analysis.

"China, since 2003 the biggest export market for German manufacturers (accounting at times for up to one-third of the total) is becoming less significant. Hopes for the re-industrialisation of the USA, with high capital investment in production technology, have so far remained broadly unrealised. Russia, too, for many years the third-largest market, will in the foreseeable future no longer be assuming this role. Europe is accordingly attracting increased attention from German companies, because European customers are confronted with stringent quality requirements in the global markets and are responding with the appropriate investments," said the VDW.

"There is definite promise, however, in the ASEAN region and India. They represent huge potential for the machine tool industry. The task here is to gain customers by offering better products, and to purposefully wrest market share away from Japan, the leading competitor there. This means more involvement, more presence, more capital investment and, where appropriate, more alliances in the event that a mid-tier company cannot put all the requisites in place from its own resources."

Dr. Prokop, Chairman, VDW

The automotive industry, too, the largest customer grouping for machine tools, is facing a paradigm shift and a transformation of its business model, with substantial effects on production equipment vendors. Driving forces include regulatory requirements from politicians and the increasing urbanisation of society as a whole. Paramount issues cover the technological transition from the internal combustion engine towards alternative drive forms, plus networked, automated driving. New competitors from outside the sector, such as Tesla or Google, are entering the market. And there are new services for customers, such as car-sharing models. Digitisation and networking make it possible. "We have to keep an eye on this development,' warned Dr. Prokop, Chairman, VDW.

"The task is to generate new customer benefits through networking. As in our private lives, this enables many activities to be simplified or even automated," commented Dr. Prokop. Thinking in terms of networking solutions, however, is still new for many companies and requires an altered perspective.

"The focus must no longer be solely on the machine itself. On the contrary, it has to be optimally embedded in a company's intralogistics. This gives rise to questions such as how, for example, will the workpieces be optimally fed into the machine? How will the machines give the workpieces an identity, or further enrich this identity? How will workpieces be assigned to orders, tracked and located at all times? The answers will lead to products and services that create new customer benefits. They could also lead to entirely new machinery concepts, to new assistance systems or to solutions for the material flow and for part tracking," continued Dr. Prokop.

He regards an excellence initiative for vocational training as a necessity. Above all, he believes it is imperative to assure adequate financing for vocational colleges to cover equipment and advanced training, so that young people can be trained in the latest technologies. He urged the sector itself to take on board the necessity of enthusing "digital natives" for the machine tool industry, to make optimum use of the potential offered by networking.

What might the sector look like in 2041? To explore this question, the VDW is launching a future-driven dialogue. On the weblink www.industryarena.com/125-jahre-vdw, theses can be found on the future development of topics such as markets, machines and people.
