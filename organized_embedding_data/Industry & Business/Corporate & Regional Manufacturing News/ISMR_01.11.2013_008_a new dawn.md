# A NEW DAWN

[TARİH: 01.11.2013 ISMR]

What does the future hold for industrial robotics? ISMR speaks to Henrik A. Schunk, Chairman of EUnited Robotics, European Robotics Association, and Managing Partner of SCHUNK GmbH & Co. KG

ISMR SAYS:

“Robots are at the dawn of a new era: safer collaboration with humans, seamless cooperation with other machines and sensors, as well as increased flexibility and ease of use will transform them into ubiquitous helpers able to improve our quality of life by providing efficient services in our homes, offices, factories and public spaces”

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes/achievements /awards and the reasons for this?

HS: Since its foundation in 2004, EUnited Robotics has established itself as the collective voice of the European robotics industry and has built up important contacts in the European Commission to shape future EU policies and activities.

In FP6 and FP7, EUnited Robotics, together with its members, put great efforts into establishing EUROP (European Robotics Technology Platform), coordinating activities for the benefit of the European robotics community and strengthening the ties between academia and industry. This has led to the (shortly expected) establishment of a Public-Private Partnership for Robotics in Europe under the soon to be established Horizon 2020 (EU Framework Programme for Research and Innovation) – the biggest civil funding package for robotics worldwide!

As a result of our efforts, the robotics industry has gained more influence on decisions concerning call topics and funding priorities. Only an industry-driven strategy and close cooperation of key stakeholders will move European robotics forward thus ensuring its competitiveness in global markets.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for robotics for industrial applications in the metal industry?

HS: According to the International Federation of Robotics, global robot sales will increase by about 2% to 162,000 units in 2013. The IFR Statistical Department expects that between 2014 and 2016 worldwide robot sales will increase by about 6% on average per year. In 2016, the annual supply of industrial robots will reach more than 190,000 units. So, we are definitely on the right track!

Robots can be found in many industries. In 2012, about 13,700 robots were sold to the metal industry. That means that almost 9% of total robot sales globally end up in the metal industry. Industrial robots are used in foundries, metal processing or metal production e.g. for metal casting and stamping, forging and bending as well as dispensing or spraying. These applications have increased substantially since 2009.

ISMR: Please give us a brief background on your association and explain your mission and objectives?

HS: EUnited Robotics, the European Robotics Association, was founded in 2004 by major European robot manufacturers as an independent voice for the European Robotics industry. EUnited Robotics is a platform for robot manufacturers, key component suppliers and system integrators in Europe, creating a network of industry leaders.

EUnited Robotics is also the key contact to the European Commission representing the industry’s view on relevant issues and challenges. The members are in the driver’s seat, deciding which activities are needed and which issues should be tackled.

Cost-efficiency and quality requirements in production will drive higher levels of automation globally

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your members at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

HS: The service robotics sector in Europe has been growing steadily over the last years. More and more companies have been founded and more and more professional service robots have evolved from prototypes to commercially viable products. Therefore, supporting companies active in service robotics as well as having a working group which concentrates only on establishing and promoting the service robotics section at the forthcoming AUTOMATICA 2014 (the leading international trade fair for robotics, automation and mechatronics), are currently on top of the EUnited Robotics’ agenda.

Besides focusing on business-to-business aspects and supporting companies to bring products to market, our goal is also to create public awareness and visibility for a realistic image of robotics as opposed to what is usually portrayed by the media, which is often far from reality.

EUnited Robotics is in close cooperation with euRobotics AISBL, the newly established organisation serving as the private partner of the soon to be established Public-Private Partnership for Robotics under Horizon 2020, and supports their activities.

Last but not least, it is important that EUnited Robotics follows and monitors the activities of the European Commission and the European Parliament which are relevant for robot manufacturers, such as trade and industrial policy, funding for research and innovation and European Directives on Energy Performance.

Robots underpin competitiveness in manufacturing

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

HS: EUnited Robotics is known to be a reliable partner and a network of industry leaders. Together with its members, EUnited Robotics will work on a favourable industrial environment for robotics in Europe. Robotics is needed to optimise manufacturing and production in Europe by producing more cost efficient, user friendly and easy-to-operate robots.

Serving and supporting service robotics companies, as I mentioned before, is of great importance to us as well. We believe that, by 2020:

■ Both industrial and service robots will be seen as essential for economic progress, high quality production, cost effectiveness, higher quality of life and the creation of better jobs.

The above-mentioned facts will be better accepted and acknowledged by the public and media.

Service robotics will be further established as a major market segment.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in industrial robotics and automation markets?

HS: Robots underpin competitiveness in manufacturing. They are known to save costs, to improve the quality of production and working conditions and minimise waste in production. The use of robots in production optimises efficiency as well as quality and helps to limit the workers’ exposure to hazardous environments.

Robots are at the dawn of a new era: safer collaboration with humans, seamless cooperation with other machines and sensors, as well as increased flexibility and ease of use will transform them into ubiquitous helpers able to improve our quality of life by providing efficient services in our homes, offices, factories and public spaces.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities for your members?

HS: There are many opportunities for the European robotics industry. Cost-efficiency and quality requirements in production will drive higher levels of automation globally. Furthermore, growing consumer markets can only be satisfied by an expansion of production capacities for which robotics is a solution provider.

Robot and robot component manufacturers have to deal with energy-efficiency requirements, decreasing life-cycles of products and an increasing variety of products. Hence, more flexible automation is required.

Technical improvements for industrial robots – e.g. easy-to-program, easy-to-use, human-robot interaction – will further increase the use of robotics in general industry as well as in small- and medium-sized companies.

With regard to the basic technologies, technical challenges for industrial robots and service robots are converging. These are in fields such as system engineering tools; humanmachine interface; safety; power management; actuation components; navigation; knowledge systems; learning; modelling; sensing; perception and cognition, to name just a few. Improvements in one of these technologies will have an impact on both service robotics and industrial robotics.

ISMR: What are the benefits of membership in the EUnited Robotics? Can you list these?

HS: EUnited Robotics provides its members with opportunities for networking, dialogue and cooperation. Furthermore, it streamlines all industry relevant information serving its members with market and statistical data, current EU strategies and policies, EC funding, future challenges as well as important and relevant news and events e.g. through regular newsletters, website, organised meetings and events.

EUnited Robotics represents the European robotics industry to the European Commission, politicians and other organisations in their surroundings. EUnited Robotics is the collective voice for the industry with regard to issues of regulation and policy, and is known as a reliable partner for policy makers.

ISMR: Do you liaise or network with any other international organisations? Please elaborate.

HS: We have strong ties to the IFR (International Federation of Robotics) and other national robotics associations worldwide, such as VDMA Robotics + Automation in Germany. Of course, EUnited has also strong links to the newly established association “euRobotics” which will serve as private partner in the (shortly expected) Public Private Partnership for robotics, as well as other PPPs such as Factories of the Future, SPIRE and Photonics.

ISMR: Do you hold events for members over the course of the year? Can you give any examples?

HS: The EUnited Robotics members – key players in the European robotics industry – meet on a regular basis to discuss various issues and address challenges in the European robotics industry, as well as any other current concerns that they might have.

Lately, a Working Group on Service Robotics has been set up to serve the interests of European service robot manufacturers. Companies meet for networking, discussing the latest market developments and tackling topics of common interest together such as standardisation, press and public relations and building a joint trade fair for service robotics.

At the AUTOMATICA trade fair, EUnited Robotics usually organises a high-level dinner and guided tour for key industry decision-makers and the European Commission.

Every year in November, the EUnited Competitiveness Review Dinner Debate is organised by the EUnited head office, to which members of all EUnited sectors (Robotics, Cleaning, Metallurgy, Municipal Equipment, Valves and Vehicle Wash) as well as representatives from the European Parliament and European Commission are invited.

Competitiveness Review Dinner Debate is a moderated event involving a panel of experts. It is a networking dinner followed by debate on an industry-relevant topic including a question-and-answer period.

ISMR: Is there anything else you wish to add?

HS: I wish you a successful Blechexpo exhibition and would like to invite you and your readers to the AUTOMATICA 2014 Trade Fair, the international leading trade fair for robotics, automation and mechatronics to learn about the latest innovations in robotics! ■
