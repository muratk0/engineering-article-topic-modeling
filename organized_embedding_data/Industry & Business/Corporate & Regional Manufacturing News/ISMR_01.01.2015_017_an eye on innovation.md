Sheet metal processing technology is used at the plant to process terminal device housings and mechanical parts.

Oki has continuously refined its processes to ensure that accuracy and batch size for sheet metal processed parts outstrips those of its stamped parts. ## A strong history

Kibataro Oki was far ahead of his time when, in 1881, convinced that Japan was about to enter the age of communications, he founded Meikosha Ltd, the origins of today's Oki Electric, and developed the first Japanese telephone. His progressive spirit has lived on through Oki Electric's contributions to the development of the information and communications industries. Oki Electric is one of the world's largest manufacturers of ATMs and LEDs (Light Emitting Diode), and pioneers in leading edge technologies such as iris recognition.

With net sales of 455.8 billion yen and 17,459 employees in Japan (86 subsidiaries), the wider Oki Electric group provides products and technologies for information/telephone systems and printers. It spun-off the semiconductor business in 2008. Oki products include printers, ATMs, cash dispensers, government systems, ticketing terminals at stations, IP network systems and telephone carrier systems. It also makes production control/ERP systems.

Oki was also the world's first company to develop cash recycling ATMs in 1982. It has 27 years of experience in the technology and has developed and manufactured seven generations of cash recycling ATMs for financial markets and four models of cash recycling ATMs for retail markets (through its Mechatronic Systems Plant in Tomioka). The ATM-Recycler G7 is Oki's seventh generation cash recycling ATM for the global market. Oki Electric is currently one of the world’s leading ATM manufacturers (50,000 units shipped).

## Approach to sheet metal manufacturing

Oki’s Tomioka facility is dedicated to sheet metal processing, assembly, inspection and staging for a mass production process. It also assembles parts from Oki’s Shenzhen plant in China for domestic ATM production. The factory ships systems such as branch terminals, ticket terminals and passbook printers. The Tomioka plant was the first in Japan to introduce punch/laser combination machines for hole punching, cutting and tapping with a single clamp as well as certain forming and tapping processes. The plant also installed several bending robots soon after they were launched.

Oki has continuously refined its processes to ensure that accuracy and batch size for sheet metal processed parts outstrips those of its stamped parts

“Since the company installed its first laser machines in 1978, we have used punch/laser combination machines for sheet metal processing. We now use several Amada machines. It has been 25 years since we started using combination machines. What we required from the machines was accuracy and productivity. In our display booth here, there are samples comparing productivity when identical parts were processed using combination machines installed in 1986, 1996, 1997, 1998, 2006 and 2011. Whereas only 7.5 products per hour were processed in 1986, with the Amada ACIES installed in July 2011, productivity is almost three times that, at 23 products per hour,” explained Yoshinobu Yamashina, General Manager of the Production Department at the Mechatronic Systems Plant.

The plant uses mainly (90%) coated mild steel from 1/1.6mm to 2.9mm material. It is currently looking for a fibre laser punching machine with material loading/unloading and full material throughput capability. Six new press brakes are due to be installed by Amada in July 2015 and an extra welding machine and press brake robot was also due for installation.

The 62,500m2 Tomioka plant has a 38400m2 production area with approximately 190 personnel. It produces 500 sheet metal items per day in batches of 50 pieces per batch.

Oki produces sheet metal components with a high-mix, low volume production approach. Its small batch production involves handling 200,000+ components (500 units per day, average batch of 50). For example, it manufactures 3000 plus plates for its cash handling machines, in average batches of 20. Its high value-added production from plate/sheet to surface treatment involves a leading edge integrated production process from plate to surface treating with accurate and efficient universal processing technology and an integrated management system for workers and production facilities.

## FACE TO FACE

Components are produced around the clock at Tomioka using a mix of universal and single-purpose processing production. ‘Universal processing’ refers to steel processing and is a manufacturing method for processing with turret punch presses, laser processing machines, press brakes etc. ‘Single-purpose processing’ refers to production with power (stamping) presses. The manufacturing method it uses for press processing is based on creating a metallic mould for each part/product.

The machine tools used for universal processing are a mix of punch lasers and punching machines. For bending, Oki employs six Amada ASTRO100 systems and an EG-AR extended press brake as well as a range of Amada FBD and SPH bending systems. Oki also uses a range of resistance and TIG/MIG welding machines at its Tomioka plant, and employs two coating and two painting lines for its product surface processing.

For single-purpose processing, Tomioka employs a number of stamping presses (approximately 22) and mould-making facilities.

“We find it is increasingly cheaper to manufacture overseas than in Japan. Universal processing work for high accuracy is done in Japan. Some single processing work is done in Japan – most is done overseas. Volume production is done overseas,” explained Yoshinobu Yamashina.

Oki’s target has always been to support manufacturing in Japan by exceeding areas within press processing (cost and accuracy) in sheet metal processing. It aims to increase speeds and automation of universal processing to reduce cost levels as well as improve the quality and accuracy of components.

The factory policy is to be the world’s No.1 mechatronics factory in high-mix, low-volume production. For sheet metal processing, this means ever greater speeds, more automation and simplified processes as well as a focus on employee upskilling, with training and certification for employees.

## Greater speeds, automation and simplified processes

To increase production speeds, Oki introduced the first punch/laser combined machine in Japan (in 1980). Back then, with the TCI80K (5 sets), it could make 7.5 pieces per hour. Now, with the Amada ACIES 2512T (one set), it can produce 23 pieces/hour. It can also do forming/tapping at the same time. Combined machines now operate 24 hours/day for 365 days/year. The machine running ratio is 90%.

The factory has also acquired technologies to enable it to customize and create NC data and has created its own in-house maintenance system to maintain the long-term operation of facilities.

It first started automating bending procedures in the 1990s.

Since 2004, 6 sets of Amada’s ASTRO100 have been installed for automated bending. It has also installed Amada’s EG-AR small bending robot.

Tomioka has also set up an integrated management system for workers and manufacturing facilities (the T-CAM or Tomioka Total Computer Aided Manufacturing System). It is designed for:-

■ Quantifying processing expertise (expansion from former CAM).

■ Clarifying work direction / test specification.

■ Visualization of process management and manufacturing activities.

The system coordinates with the production management system for a fully integrated approach. ISMR was given a full factory tour which started with the production management board (visualisation of progress, manufactured volume, facility load etc.). The operating rate is strictly managed and everyone from plant manager to on-site employee can check the plant operating rate using the internally developed operation management system.

Sheet metal processing technology is the core technology at the Mechatronics Systems Plant. Working in close partnership with Amada has helped it to achieve its sheet metal targets and productivity/ efficiency goals as it continuously refines its processes, procedures and machine tools. ■
