# FOCUSONITALY

[TARİH: 01.11.2010 ISMR]

Fourth in the world as a manufacturer and third in the world as an exporter, the Italian machine tool, robot and automation industry has certain features that distinguish it from the industries of other countries.

“The differences become apparent if we consider the structure of the industry, which is much more fragmented than in other countries,” explained UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, the Italian machine tools, robots and automation manufacturers’ association. “Indeed, most of the 500-odd companies in Italy are small or medium-sized. In a typical company, management is firmly in the hands of the owner and normally no more than 70 people are employed, compared with the 200 that are the average in Germany and Japan, where, in addition, management has a highly vertical structure.

“Italian companies exploit to the full the limited size of their companies, successfully penetrating markets worldwide with their flexibility and quick response. It is no coincidence that one of the strengths of the Italian machine tool industry lies in supplying custom-made machines and systems - in other words, machines and systems produced to meet the specific requirements of individual users. As to the quality of products, it is significant that almost 70% of the machine tools produced in Italy are electronically controlled, and 20% of these form part of integrated systems (cells and systems made up of more than three machines).

“Italian companies are also ‘special’ because of the marketing policy they have adopted, based on the conviction that technology has to be shared. Consequently, they present themselves on world markets with an approach marked by readiness for dialogue and co-operation,” concluded the association.

## MARKET STRUCTURE

The structural characteristics of the Italian machine tool, robot and automation systems manufacturing industry are similar to those of the national production system – smaller companies, a strong export tendency and high quality offerings.

The geographical distribution of the machine tool sector proves that manufacturers must be close to their customers and suppliers - most production centres in this sector are located in Lombardy (48.2%), Triveneto (17%), Emilia Romagna (14.2%) and Piedmont (13.5%). The region with the highest density of robot producers is Lombardy, with 42.9% of companies. However, Piedmont posted the best percentage turnover in 2008.

“Despite the global financial meltdown, Italy held onto fourth place world ranking as a producer and third place world ranking as an exporter”

The main user of machine tools is the metal products industry (35.9%), with production and first processing of metals, subcontracting machine operations, production of building elements and products other than machinery, followed by the automotive sector (25.1%).

## 2009 ITALIAN MARKET DATA

In 2009, the Italian machine tool, robot and automation industry had to operate in the wake of the global financial meltdown, that exploded towards the end of 2008 and had a massive impact upon the economy. World production and consumption of machine tools fell by almost 30%. Despite this, Italy held onto a fourth place world ranking as a producer and third place world ranking as an exporter.

According to UCIMU, Italian production of machine tools, robots and automation in 2009 came to 4,093 million euros, down by 30% compared to 2008. Consumption fell by 44.2% to 2,333 million following the major downturn in domestic deliveries (-37.6%, 1,566 million) and imports (-54.1%, 767 million). Foreign machines held a 32.9% market share.

Exports also turned in a negative performance, down by 24.2% to 2,527 million: the export-production ratio increased from 57% in 2008 to 61.7%. The balance of trade grew by 5.7% to 1,760 million. The sharp downturn for Italian industry in the sector was reflected in the use of production capacity, where the annual average fell from 82.3% in 2008 to 61%.

## EXPORTS

According to UCIMU figures, Italian exports of machine tools fell by 25.2% to 2,399 million euros in 2009. This downturn saw foreign sales at their lowest value since 2005. The quarterly trend provides a snapshot of the freefall in exports – down by 16.8% in the first quarter, 24.2% in the second quarter and 30% in the third. In relation to these figures, the -27.9% result in the fourth quarter may be viewed as the sign of a slight turnaround.

International sales of machine tools based on non-conventional technologies (laser, electro-erosion) came to 123 million (-40%), metal cutting machines came to 1,231 million (-16.9%) and metal forming machines came to 1,045 million (-31.2%). The main international outlets for Italian production are Germany (14.1% of total), China (14%), USA (8.7%), France (6.3%) and Russia (4%).

Exports to the European Union, that covered 41.8% of total sales abroad, fell by 35.2% to 1,004 million: -27.5% in Germany (337 million), -24.2% in France (151 million), -55% in Spain (72.3 million), -38.6% in Poland (72.2 million) and -46.3% in Austria (44.1 million).

Sales to non-EU Europe fell by 37.3% to 253 million: -42.1% in Russia (95.8 million), -20.2% in Switzerland (54.1 million) and -29.8% in Turkey (49.3 million). Exports to Africa fell by 22.4% to 85.4 million, where 70% of demand is generated by Mediterranean countries.

Exports to NAFTA countries fell by 18.7% to 248 million: -10.2% in the USA (210 million), -54.8% in Mexico (23.6 million) and -23.5% in Canada (14.4 million). There was a negative trend for exports to Central-South America (125.7 million), driven down by Brazil (80.5 million, -28.5%).

The only region in 2009 where sales of Italian machine tools grew was Asia (658 million, +3.6%), thanks to an increase in exports to Eastern Asia (406 million, +23.7%), +27.9% in China (335 million) and +23.1% in South Korea (44.4 million). In this region, only sales to Japan fell.

In the Middle East, Italian exports fell by 3.8% to 137 million – the main outlets were Iran (38.1 million, +215%), the UAE (33.6 million, -25.4%) and Saudi Arabia (19.1 million, -52.1%). Exports to India also fell back as well as those to South East Asia.

Italian production in 2009, confirmed UCIMU, saw 36% for domestic deliveries, 21% sales to euro countries and 43% exports to other countries.

## OUTLOOK FOR 2010

“The short-medium term situation for Italian industry is positive, despite concern over possible new setbacks,” Giancarlo Losma, president of UCIMU, confirmed during a recent briefing at the BI-MU exhibition. He has called for more government incentives to support the Italian economic system and spark new impetus.

“I refer here to de-taxation of profits reinvested to purchase new machinery as well as the creation of a system of incentives to trade-in obsolete machinery and, in the wake of what is envisaged in the US, the free amortisation system,” he explained.

UCIMU expects international demand for Italian products to recover in the second half of 2010. Over the first six months of 2010, Italian domestic deliveries grew by 47.5%.

“Recovery in demand is also demonstrated by the increased use of plant production capacity in the sector. After the sharp drop culminating in the negative peak in third quarter 2009 with 54.6%, the indicator began to improve, posting 68% in the second quarter of 2010.”
