B&J Industries rebrands as Future Form Manufacturing, expands in Nevada

B&J Industries, a manufacturer of precision-engineered products for the data center, medical, defense, energy, and transportation industries, has announced its rebrand to Future Form Manufacturing. The new name accompanies a facility expansion in Sparks, Nev., that will triple daily production capacity.

The company has leased an additional 30,000-sq.-ft. facility in Sparks near its existing headquarters, bringing its total production and warehouse space to nearly 100,000 sq. ft., and an additional 90,000-plus square feet is currently being built.

Future Form Manufacturing’s capabilities include large-scale CNC machining, sheet metal fabrication, tube laser cutting, welding, powder coating, assembly, and engineering.

Tide Rock announces acquisition of two companies

Tide Rock, an unlevered buyout firm with a portfolio of B2B businesses, has acquired Precision Waterjet & Laser (now Precision Advanced Machining) in Anaheim, Calif., and Made in America Manufacturing in Buda, Texas. These acquisitions establish two complementary precision machining platforms serving the aerospace, space systems, and advanced industrial markets.

Precision Waterjet & Laser specializes in waterjet cutting, laser cutting, CNC machining, and full-service metal fabrication with capabilities including forming, welding, painting, and plating. Made in America Manufacturing is a precision machining company offering tight-tolerance, complex cylindrical and milled components, operating advanced milling and lathe machines.

The two platforms will operate independently as fully equipped manufacturing bases.

GrayMatter Robotics unveils 100,000-sq.-ft. AI robotics center

GrayMatter Robotics has opened its new 100,000-sq.-ft. headquarters and innovation center in Carson, Calif. This expansion includes the establishment of an interactive robotics experience center for AI-powered manufacturing automation.

More than 25 robotic cells actively perform real manufacturing operations on parts from current and prospective customers and partners. The interactive experience center allows visitors to see how AI-powered robots autonomously sand, grind, polish, buff, spray, blast, and inspect parts across diverse materials and geometries.Using physics-informed AI, the robots don’t require manual programming. Instead, they incorporate established physics models—understanding forces, materials, geometries, and tool behaviors—to ensure safe, accurate operation even with parts they’ve never encountered, the company states.

The project has created more than 100 skilled jobs in advanced AI, robotics, multidisciplinary engineering, manufacturing operations, and technical support.

Survey reveals critical gaps hindering skilled trades entry

For years, the narrative around the skilled labor shortage has been that young people simply aren’t interested in the trades. According to Airgas, its new research based on comprehensive surveys of nearly 100 students and 90 educators shows this assumption to be wrong and reveals where the real problem lies.

The company’s surveys found that while 76% of skilled trades students are interested in pursuing a career in the skilled trades, 72% of those same students feel they lack the basic information needed to start.

The central problem is the gap between high student interest and the low exposure students have to the field. Students are eager but lack access to the handson training, internships, and in-person mentorship that transforms curiosity into commitment. They desire real-world exposure to trades careers through facility tours and direct conversations with professionals; transparent information about pay, benefits, and growth potential in trades careers; and online access to information about different types of skilled trade opportunities.

Teachers report their top needs are more funding for modern equipment and industry professionals to be physically present in their classrooms to act as role models and demonstrate that the trades offer high-tech, viable careers.

To help bridge the talent gap in skilled trades, Airgas encourages companies to focus on three key areas: providing resources to educational institutions through equipment donations or sponsorships, offering hands-on experience via internships and workplace tours to build a future talent pipeline, and expanding perceptions of skilled trades careers by hosting educators and counselors at their facilities.

Alro Steel Pittsburgh completes expansion

Masaba expands in South Dakota

Masaba has expanded its existing 211,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility in Vermillion, S.D., with 147,500 sq. ft. of additional metal fabrication and office space. The $15 million investment includes new equipment.

The former steel processing area has been repurposed for welding and assembly. Production at the new plant began in May 2025.

The company manufactures conveyors and material handling equipment for the mining, aggregate, and construction industries.

FabArc Steel Supply completes largest project in company history

FabArc Steel Supply Inc., Oxford, Ala., a steel subcontractor and AISC-certified fabrication facility, has announced the successful steel structure completion of its $200 million-plus contract for Amplify, a manufacturing facility located in Byhalia, Miss. This is the largest contract in FabArc Steel Supply’s history.

The project required more than 28,000 tons of structural steel, featuring W14×500 columns and W40×655 beams, some of the heaviest standard shapes produced in North America. The company also fabricated and delivered massive roof trusses more than 8 ft. long, 9 ft. deep, and weighing up to 40,000 lbs. each.

To meet the heavy welding demands, the company used more than 100,000 lbs. of weld wire between February and July 2025, producing an estimated 2,300 miles of shop welds in six months.
