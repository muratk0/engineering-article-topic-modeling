# Call for UK apprenticeship levy review

[TARİH: 01.09.2023 ISMR]

Manufacturing growth in the UK is “being hamstrung by an out-of-date Apprenticeship Levy scheme that is failing to address key labour shortages in the workforce", according to the UK Metals Council.

Rachel Eade MBE, Chair of the UK Metals Council, is calling on the UK Government to review the current system, tear up unnecessary bureaucracy and ensure that entry-level apprenticeships benefit from the same level of funding and importance as higher-level qualifications.

“Rachel believes that more than 10,000 jobs are currently available across foundation industries in the UK and there is little sign that these positions will be filled as a complicated training landscape is putting off training providers, companies and young people looking to enter industry for the first time," said the UK Metals Council.

The manufacturing specialist, who has held senior positions at supply chain initiative 'Accelerate' and the Manufacturing Advisory Service, points to the fact that significant sums raised under the Levy last year were unspent and have gone back to the UK Treasury. A letter on behalf of the 12 trade associations represented by the UK Metals Council has been sent to Ministers and MPs in a bid to highlight the issue.

“We're calling on the Government to urgently review the current system and look at four important improvements. Reduce the amount of bureaucracy involved, widen the focus of standards so they cover all engineering and manufacturing disciplines and ensure realistic funding levels for Level 2/Level 3 apprentices and the foundation skills that employers need. Finally, we must rebuild the engineering training provider capability in the UK; giving them the funding level that they require to deliver the skills that our sector needs (now and in the future) is vital," continued Rachel Eade.

The UK Metals Council features twelve industrial trade associations including the Aluminium Federation, the Confederation of British Metalforming and the Cast Metals Federation. Together, they represent over 11,000 companies, employing nearly one million people (directly and indirectly).
