# Innovation award and showcase for KUKA

[TARİH: 01.05.2024 ISMR]

Innovative ideas from young research teams, an AI (artificial intelligence) showcase for simpler robot programming and the "Follow the Twin" challenge from the Open Industry 4.0 Alliance were highlighted by automation specialist, KUKA, at Hannover Messe in Germany this April.

"For us, this year's Hannover Messe was all about innovation as well as clever, young research talents," said Wilfried Eberhardt, Chief Marketing Officer, KUKA.

The KUKA Innovation Award encompassed skilled trades and SMEs. The teams developed creative robotics solutions for bakeries, bending processes or clothing companies. At Hanover, they presented their innovations to an international audience on around 200 square metres in Hall 5, stand D12, from 22-26 April 2024. The winner of the €20,000 Innovation Award was chosen by the jury on the KUKA trade fair stand.

On the Microsoft stand, KUKA demonstrated how AI could simplify programming in the future. Its aim is to make it as easy as possible for its customers to access automation. A team from the Augsburg-based technology company is working on how AI can support the creation of programming code. Microsoft Copilot chatbox is used for this purpose.

"In the long term, AI is intended to supplement the classic input that is currently made via the KUKA smartPAD. It would then be possible for end users to give so-called prompts i.e. text input or voice instructions, directly to the AI. The AI then creates the code to program the robot for the respective task," KUKA told ISMR.

As a founding member of the Open Industry 4.0 Alliance (OI4), KUKA also took part in the "Follow the Twin" challenge, with its digital subsidiary Device Insight, and was represented at the OI4 trade fair booth. The OI4 Alliance demonstrated to visitors that the introduction and handling of digital twins is less complex than some industrial companies assume.

The "Follow the Twin" competition led visitors across the trade fair to the stands of Alliance members. On the stands, QR codes marked the digital twin of the devices and exhibits on display.
