# One current trend in machine tool technology is to bring more intelligence and automation into the machine tool process

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

ISMR: Please give us some background history on CECIMO. Who are its members, how many do you have and how do you help them? Please also outline the benefits of membership.

FG: CECIMO was established in 1950 and has become the voice of the European machine tool industry worldwide. We have 15 member countries in Europe (representing around 1600 companies) in total. We represent around 97% of total machine tool production in Europe. We also represent more than 40% of worldwide machine tool production.

Now that there is a European single market and a range of regulatory powers from the EU, CECIMO has become an important player in European policy-making. We are an interface between the European machine tool industry and the EU decision-makers (European Commission, European Council etc.). We monitor European legislation and policies, report it to members, evaluate the potential impact on our business and create common position papers.

There needs to be a continuous dialogue between industry and the European Union. We lobby and communicate European machine tool industry needs to the EU to try to create a level playing field for our members within the EU and worldwide. We also try to improve the image of the machine tool sector, make the EU aware what the machine tool industry represents and the degree of modern equipment/technology in the sector.

We provide our members with statistics, market intelligence and economic awareness. We also give our members updates on European policy and legislation. We promote research and innovation, which is important for competitiveness, and are involved in different initiatives (public-private partnerships) and projects. We also offer a range of networking opportunities to members, such as workshops, the General Assembly etc.

EMO was created in 1975 and has become an important international exhibition for machine tools. It takes place every two years and is owned by CECIMO.

ISMR: What are your main objectives and targets over the next few years? How do you intend to achieve them?

FG: We are emerging from a very tough period of economic recession. There are some positive signs of recovery this year and now we have to focus our attention on boosting the competitiveness of our industry again. The environment has become more competitive for strong, traditional manufacturer countries such as USA and Japan, because non-European manufacturers (in countries such as China, Brazil and India) have emerged stronger from the recession because they still enjoy growth and high consumption in their domestic markets.

There are also great challenges on social and demographic change, increased security, environmental considerations etc. so we require new manufacturing solutions for these new market needs. CECIMO itself needs to help prepare its manufacturers to meet these challenges by supplying more market intelligence instruments and economic forecasts for the most profitable sectors and markets (eg energy, medical etc.). We have launched a CECIMO self-regulatory initiative for members so we are an active promoter of energy efficiency in the sector. It is a marketing tool for members. We will also continue to promote the excellence and innovation of the European machine tool industry at EMO.

ISMR: Please outline CECIMO’s main successes and achievements over the last two years.

FG: The last two years have been very difficult in our sector and we have tried to help ward of the destructive impact of the economic meltdown on the European machine tool business. We closely observed the evolution of the economic crisis by supplying facts and figures to our members and then putting in place a set of measures to alleviate the impact of the crisis on our industry. We also supplied EU policy members with critical updates on the situation so that they could formally drive policy response.

On the industrial side, we have a CECIMO market intelligence strategy and a forecasting tool (which can be found on our website) to help guide members to potential areas of opportunity. We have identified trends in seven different industrial sectors (automotive, energy, medical etc.) in this forecasting tool – we also look at what is happening in our customers’ markets. Our lobby activities have changed too – they are now more centred around the issues and concerns of other players in our value chain like our major customers (such as those in the automotive sector).

ISMR: Please let us know the latest news on CECIMO’s events and exhibitions. What is the news on the next EMO event?

FG: We are continuing to make EMO more international. The last EMO attracted 41% of non-European attendance and we would like to increase this figure, particularly as roughly one third of the machine tool market is in China. We would like to see more Chinese visitors. By the time EMO takes place, the market will look much more promising and we are aiming for the same level of exhibitors and visitors as we had at the last EMO exhibition in Hanover. We invite members of the European Parliament and the EU Commission to tour the exhibition and view the machine tools.

We invited members of the European Union to an event recently to discuss trade for the machine tool sector and get our message across. We will also run a high level Open Day on 15 November in Brussels (organised by the European Commission) on key enabling technologies. The European Commission has launched an initiative on key enabling technologies which identified micro and nano technologies, photonics, electronics, new materials and, most importantly for us, advanced manufacturing systems. The EU report on this should be released next year and will be integrated in the new EU Industrial policy announced in the EU 2020 Strategy.

Our General Assembly will take place in November this year for CECIMO members. We also run a number of events to disseminate information to members on the EU’s Research and Development project funds for machine tools. We promote the results of these R&D projects at major international exhibitions.

ISMR: Do you see environmental and green issues becoming more important in manufacturing? How are you embracing these issues?

FG: Until now, the EU has promoted energy efficiency for consumer products. But now, for the first time, energy efficiency has to be reduced for business-to-business products. One of the first formal focuses on this for the EU is on the machine tool sector. We are working with the EU on this and are promoting a modular approach to machine tool construction. For this to work, the machine tool builder has to work with the customer to determine the kind of machine as well as how it will be used and this will dictate the machine components. Everything depends on the application of the machine and this is a very new concept in EU regulatory approach.

We have been working on our self-regulatory energy efficiency initiative for one year now – what we did not want was a direct comparison of one machine against another for energy efficiency because this makes no sense in terms of the machine’s application. Energy efficiency needs to be properly quantified and proper monitoring needs to take place.

ISMR: What are the main concerns of your members at the moment? How are you helping to alleviate those concerns?

FG: In the short term, financing is the issue. In the long term, it is trade. On the financing side, there has been a liquidity issue in European markets and this has had an impact on our investors who have not had the money to buy new machines. We have tried to convince the European Commission of the importance of having the right financial means available for our customers. Our own sector also needs to invest in R&D and, for this, it needs the appropriate financial support. We are accordingly investing in this Public-Private Partnership initiative and other new projects to unlock more finances.

What has become more important is the initiative to put people on temporary leave and not lay them off, which governments are supporting. There is a skills shortage issue in the machine tool sector and it is important to keep key skills in companies. The recovery will happen more quickly if confidence stays in the market and financing can be unlocked for new machine tool purchases.

In the long term, we see a shift in the consumption of machine tools towards Asia. We want to see fair trade in these regions and not see protectionist measures taken. This means, for us, collecting information on any barriers to trade in certain regions to address this issue and ensure a level playing field for CECIMO members. We are in favour of Multilateral and Bilateral Trade Agreements. Free trade agreements are important for our members for access to key markets.
