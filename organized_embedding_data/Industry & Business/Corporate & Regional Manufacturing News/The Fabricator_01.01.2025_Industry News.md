WILA USA ramps up domestic production

Tooling supplier WILA USA has moved its headquarters to Florence, Ky., where it also opened a new 52,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing plant to serve the North American market.

Within the past year, WILA USA has significantly ramped up its North American presence. It moved its headquarters from Baltimore to Louisville, Ky., where it also started production of its press brake punches and dies out of its new 52,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing facility.

"We are excited to relocate [WILA USA’s] corporate headquarters to Louisville and establish our North American manufacturing operations," said Hans Willemsen, WILA CEO, in a company statement. "This strategic move will allow us to strengthen our market presence, improve customer service, and further collaborate with our valued partners in North America."

"The company’s two main plants are in The Netherlands. In recent years, we evaluated our global manufacturing strategy and identified the United States as the ideal location to expand production capabilities and better serve our North American customers."

That was Caitlin DeVore, operations process manager for WILA USA, adding that automated machining centers started arriving at the Louisville facility in early 2024. "We’ve been cutting chips since August, and we just completed production of our first tooling pieces in early October."

One automated machining line is already running, and the company plans to add up to three production lines that could incorporate as many as six robotic machining cells. "Our first cell [which launched in the fall] can run 24/7 without supervision. The production technology here mirrors exactly what we use in The Netherlands.

"We’re focusing on our most in-demand tools to start," DeVore continued, "and we plan to expand our library as our domestic production capacity grows."

An important part of this capacity will be accelerated response for custom tooling. "The move will allow us to quickly respond to someone who’s looking for a custom or semicustom tool, without waiting for something to be shipped from across the Atlantic," said Jeff Sloan, president of WILA USA. "We’re also centrally located here, so it will allow us to respond quickly to all of our North American customers."

"Louisville is centrally located and close to transit," DeVore added. "We have hubs for both UPS and Fed-Ex here. Relocating and ramping up production here just makes sense. Our ultimate goal is to make nearly all WILA tooling destined for the North American market in our Louisville plant."

FMA asks for industry support in identifying unsafe fabricating technology

The Fabricators and Manufacturers Association (FMA) has announced a closer working relationship with the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) as it looks to protect the metal fabricating workforce.

"A safe and healthy workforce is necessary for a vibrant metal fabricating industry. That’s why FMA is working with FDA to ensure that fabricating equipment being sold in the U.S. meets all the safety requirements necessary to minimize the risk of injury to shop floor personnel," said Ed Youdell, FMA president/CEO. "For example, a worker should never have to worry that the safety glass on a laser cutting machine isn’t actually rated to contain the laser beam."

In support of this commitment to worker safety, FMA is cooperating more closely with FDA to share information related to equipment and manufacturers that they have identified as being unsafe. FDA’s most recent safety update, Import Alert 95-05, has a "red list" that contains firms that have been found to have one or more of the following objectionable conditions:

• Are subject to a Center for Devices and Radiological Health Quality Control and Testing Program disapproval in accordance with section 534(h) of the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic (FD&C) Act

• Do not appear to comply with the applicable performance standards prescribed pursuant to section 534 of the FD&C Act

• Do not have the certification required by section 534(h) of the FD&C Act and as specified in 21 CFR 1010.2. These conditions might include products without a permanently affixed label or tag, products for which evidence of a valid test and quality control program is lacking, or products for which the certification label is invalid because reports are deficient, and the firm has failed to provide requested information as required in 537(b).

Hickey Metal Fabrication acquires Ohio Laser

Hickey Metal Fabrication, Salem, Ohio, has acquired Ohio Laser, Plain City, Ohio. The company plans to maintain Ohio Laser’s current management and operational team.

"This is an exciting moment for both Ohio Laser and Hickey Metal," said Leo Hickey, president, Hickey Metal Fabrication. "We have long admired Ohio Laser’s commitment to quality and their exceptional reputation in the industry. Together, we are stronger and ready to meet the needs of our clients with enhanced resources and a commitment to the values that define us."

"Finding the right partner in Hickey Metal Fabrication was not just about operational growth, but about honoring Ohio Laser’s legacy and future. Hickey’s dedication to integrity, respect, and innovation aligns perfectly with our values, and we are thrilled to enter this new chapter together," said Ohio Laser’s Gregg Simpson.

Hypertherm Associates and BLM GROUP form strategic partnership agreement

Hypertherm Associates and BLM GROUP have announced a strategic partnership agreement aimed at expanding the market reach of both companies’ products. It includes a minority stake investment in Italy-based BLM GROUP by Hypertherm Associates and a commercial agreement to expand the demand generation for BLM GROUP’s tube laser cutting and bending systems and pipe and plate cutting systems, as well as 3D robotics systems, in North America and Europe.

Hypertherm Associates is a 100% employee-owned company with plasma manufacturing facilities in New Hampshire, waterjet manufacturing in Minnesota and Washington, and laser consumables manufacturing in Sasso Marconi, Italy.

The strategic partnership allows each company to maintain its current leadership structure and operations.

Hascall Steel opens Michigan facility

Hascall Steel Co., a flat-rolled steel distributor and service center founded in Grand Rapids, Mich., has opened a new facility in Canton, Mich. The 250,000-sq.-ft. plant expands the company’s capabilities and helps strengthen its role in the supply chain.

The plant features four slitters that can process 60,000-lb. coils from 0.015 to 0.400 by 72 in. A stretcher leveler will be installed in spring 2025. This new location will streamline logistics, improving delivery efficiency for regional clients. The company maintains a large inventory of unallocated steel and offers advanced processing capabilities, including slitting, blanking, shearing, and stretcher leveling.

Solv Metals Division acquires WickFab Inc.

Solv Metals LLC has announced the acquisition of WickFab Inc., a metal fabrication company based in Avilla, Ind.

Founded in 1993 by John Wicker, WickFab specializes in metal processing services, including metal forming, laser cutting, waterjet cutting, and welding. Employees Joe and Cheryl Cochran, president and operations manager, respectively, purchased the business in 2016.

"We are honored that Joe and Cheryl Cochran have chosen Solv to help steward WickFab’s legacy," said Mark Webb, CEO of Solv Holdings. "This strategic acquisition embodies our mission to grow and innovate in the manufacturing sector."
