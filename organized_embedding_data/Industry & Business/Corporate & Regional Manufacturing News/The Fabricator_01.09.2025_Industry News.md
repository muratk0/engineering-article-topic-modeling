MC Machinery launches Finishing Division through strategic partnership with EMC MC

Machinery Systems Inc., Elk Grove Village, Ill., has launched its new Finishing Division, formed through a strategic partnership with Italy-based EMC Srl, a provider of metal surface finishing technology. The new division is led by Mitch Foley, product/sales manager—finishing.

As the exclusive U.S. distributor of EMC’s deburring, grinding, and polishing machines, MC Machinery is expanding its equipment portfolio to offer end-to-end fabrication capabilities—from laser cutting and bending to surface finishing.

Great River Industries rebrands to TankWorx + Construction Services

HBH Holdings Inc., the parent company of Great River Industries, has announced that GRI is changing its brand name and identity to TankWorx + Construction Services.

Based in Geismar, La., the company provides manufacturing, installation, maintenance, and repair of field tanks, as well as small capital construction, across a variety of industrial market segments.

The rebrand takes effect immediately and will be supported by a targeted trade advertising and social outreach campaign in fall 2025.

Generac opens manufacturing facility in Wisconsin

Waukesha, Wis.-based Generac has opened a new 345,000-sq.-ft. manufacturing and assembly facility in Beaver Dam, Wis., for the production of commercial and industrial generators.

"Some companies talk about bringing manufacturing home. We’re focused on what comes next, because America’s critical infrastructure depends on it," said Aaron Jagdfeld, chairman and president/CEO. "With the rapid growth of data centers, clean energy projects, and advanced industrial networks, the demand for resilient domestic supply chains has never been greater. Our products are central to that shift."

About 130 people work at the site, and the company expects that number to increase to 320 by the end of 2025.

L&T Precision expands Tijuana facility, earns ISO 9001 certification

L&T Precision, a TPC company and manufacturer of precision sheet metal and machined components, has completed an expansion of the finishing capabilities at its Tijuana, Mexico, facility. In addition, the plant has received ISO 9001 certification. The finishing equipment and facility updates include a washer station, a masking room, two liquid paint booths, a powder booth, two ovens, a silkscreen room, and an integration room for part assembly. The facility enhancement also includes integration of technology across the manufacturing lines to capture operational data in real time for a nearly paperless operation.

Baltimore Fabrication to expand manufacturing operation in Pennsylvania

Baltimore Fabrication has announced plans for a $6.2 million expansion of its manufacturing operations in York, Pa. The project will create at least 75 full-time jobs over the next three years while also retaining 47 current positions. The Baltimore-based company, which opened an initial manufacturing location in York in 2024, now is expanding to a second nearby location through the acquisition of a 35,000-sq.-ft. building.

The company fabricates and erects steel frames for buildings, including beams, columns, trusses, decking, bracing, and other structural members. It also handles miscellaneous metal fabrications, such as staircases, railings, ornamental and decorative work, and other custom metal components.

Hill Companies acquires QMI

The Hill Companies, a group of precision manufacturing businesses based in the south central U.S. and led by Hill Manufacturing & Fabrication, has acquired QMI, an ISO-certified CNC machine shop located in Pryor and Tahlequah, Okla.

The addition of QMI expands Hill’s capabilities in tight-tolerance machining; diversifies its customer base; and strengthens its position in the energy, aerospace, and industrial sectors.

Following this transaction, the Hill Companies totals 72 employees, 63 CNC spindles, and more than 13,000 sq. ft. of dedicated welding and fabrication capacity across the portfolio.

The facility now operates as QMI – A Hill Company.

Bystronic partners with SSAB to enhance cutting and bending processes

(From left) Bystronic CEO Domenico Iacovelli and SSAB President/CEO Johnny Sjöström Bystronic

Bystronic and SSAB have announced a nonexclusive partnership to advance laser cutting and bending technologies using new materials for the sheet metal industry including alloying concepts, recycled steel, and fossil-free steel.

The agreement facilitates comprehensive data exchange on the impact of various materials on Bystronic’s production processes. Bystronic will work with newly developed steels and specially designed sheet metal for testing and will provide feedback on process quality.

This cooperation aims to improve both companies’ products and strengthen their leadership in sustainable practices.

Prima Power completes expansion of Cologna Veneta plant

Prima Power has announced the inauguration of the new expansion of its Cologna Veneta facility, a center for sheet metal bending technology in Italy.

With nearly 27,000 sq. ft. of additional space, production capacity has increased by 50%. The building is equipped with a 150-kW photovoltaic system to reduce its energy footprint. In addition, a new 10,763-sq.-ft. service building houses an Academy Hall for training, additional offices, a cafeteria, and new changing rooms.

The plant manufactures the entire range of the company’s bending systems, from stand-alone press brakes to robotic bending cells and fully automated panel benders, all of which can be integrated into flexible production lines. All machines are based on servo-electric technology.

This project responds to the growing market demand for advanced bending systems while also reflecting the company’s recent acquisition of robotics specialist Sistec AM, which has strengthened the group’s expertise in robotics integration, a strategic area for bending.

Howard Industries investing $236 million in Mississippi facilities

Electrical distribution transformer and technology products manufacturer Howard Industries is expanding operations across its Mississippi facilities in Clarke, Jones, and Simpson counties. The $236.95 million project will create a total of 450 jobs.

The expansions will enable the company to meet the U.S. Dept. of Energy’s new requirements for electrical transformers while addressing the growing demand for power from sectors such as data centers, electric vehicles, transportation, and industry.

The company plans to complete the project and fill the new jobs within five years.

Accurate Metal Products installs AI-enhanced grinding robots

Steel processor and fabricator Accurate Metal Products has installed two AI-powered Teqram EasyGrinder robotic systems at its facilities in Rockford, Ill., and Milwaukee.

The systems automate the cleaning and finishing of flame- and plasma-cut parts. Using AI and 3D vision and requiring no programming, they identify parts and pick them up with magnetic grippers. Processing parts autonomously using the best-suited tools from their automatic tool changers, the robotic systems can clean both sides of a part by flipping it with the integrated EasyFlipper.

The systems can remove slag and lead-ins, grind and round edges, and clean interior diameters.

GE Appliances to invest $490 million for clothes washer production in Kentucky

GE Appliances, a Haier company, has announced a $490 million investment at its Louisville, Ky., global headquarters and largest manufacturing site to create its most advanced manufacturing plant for production of clothes washers.

The project will move production of more than 15 models of front-load washers to Building 2 at Appliance Park in Louisville, bringing the total area of clothes care production to the equivalent of 33 football fields and creating 800 full-time jobs.

"We are bringing laundry production to our global headquarters in Louisville because manufacturing in the U.S. is fundamental to our ‘zero-distance’ business strategy to make appliances as close as possible to our customers and consumers," said Kevin Nolan, president/CEO, GE Appliances. "This decision is our most recent product reshoring and aligns with the current economic and policy environment."

Building 2 will be redesigned with new automation, robotics, and material handling technologies. The plant is vertically integrated and will include in-house manufacturing of critical parts such as stainless steel baskets and cabinets, high-precision metal stamping and forming, and injection molding and production equipment.

The new manufacturing lines will open in 2027.

Rhoads Industries to expand operations in Philadelphia

Rhoads Industries plans to invest nearly $100 million to expand its manufacturing operations at the Philadelphia Navy Yard. This project will create at least 450 jobs and retain 541 existing full-time positions.

Weiss-Aug consolidates divisions, announces rebranding

Weiss-Aug, East Hanover, N.J., has announced a rebranding initiative aimed at streamlining its organizational structure and unifying its market presence. The company will transition from operating as Weiss-Aug Group and multiple divisions—previously named after acquired entities—into two newly defined business units, each dedicated to serving distinct industry segments.

Weiss-Aug will serve the industrial and commercial sectors, specializing in high-volume metal stamping and value-added assembly for the aerospace and defense, automotive, interconnect, telecommunications, and consumer goods industries.

Weiss-Aug MedPharma will offer precision machining and laser processing, primarily focused on developing and manufacturing precision medical components, assemblies, and finished devices for the medical and pharmaceutical industries. Lupine Research will remain its R&D center for artificial intelligence, machine learning, and materials science, supporting both units.

Patriot Aluminum to relocate to Delaware for expansion

Patriot Aluminum, a veteran-owned fencing manufacturer and distributor, has announced plans to relocate its operations from Denton, Md., to unincorporated Seaford, Del.

The company purchased a 45,000-sq.-ft. warehouse there and is investing $4.8 million in equipment and fit-out at the new building, which is being upgraded for production, assembly, and distribution of metal fencing products.

Twenty existing jobs came with the company from Maryland, and 19 additional full-time jobs will be created at the Delaware site over the next three years. The company will bring in metal fencing components and will fabricate its own products to meet customer specifications.

Patriot Aluminum was acquired in 2022 by current owner John Forrest, who already operated two other fencing-related companies in Felton, Del. Moving the company to Seaford brings it closer to Forrest’s operations in Felton and allows it to expand into other areas of production.

Kent Corp.

, a manufacturer of tube mill equipment in North Royalton, Ohio, has announced the death of company founder

Tony Costello

. He earned a mechanical engineering degree from Baldwin Wallace University, followed by a master’s degree from Columbia University. He went on to work at AEF, Yoder, and Loopco before founding Kent Corp. in 1971.
