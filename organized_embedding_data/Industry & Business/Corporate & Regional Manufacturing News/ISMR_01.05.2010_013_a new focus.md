# A NEW FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.05.2010 ISMR]

Bystronic's CEO, Ferdi Toengi, discusses new strategies, sheet metal market trends and a new investment focus with ISMR

Bystronic is a worldwide active supplier, based in Switzerland, of high quality solutions for the economical processing of sheet metal and other sheet materials.

It develops application-oriented systems and services for laser and waterjet cutting as well as bending and automation.

Bystronic currently has around 10,000 customers around the world - 8% in US, 15% in Asia and 77% in Europe. Around three quarters of its customers are job shops, working across a range of industries.

ISMR spoke to Bystronic's Chief Executive Officer, Ferdi Toengi, to discuss Bystronic's strategy and future development in sheet metal markets, following one of the worst economic downturns in recent times.

## BATTLING THE DOWNTURN

“Our turnover was 500 million euros in 2008 but, in the wake of the global economic downturn, it dropped to 250 million euros in 2009 - a decline of 50%," he told ISMR. "We saw the highest decline in US and eastern European markets, but less of a decline in markets such as the UK and central Europe. Surprisingly, we found last year that the market recovered in China – the real decline in the Chinese market, for us, was in 2008. However, we found that the market in China in 2009 was excellent. Unfortunately, that was not reflected in other Asian markets."

Toengi believes that Bystronic can improve on its 2009 turnover this year by 10-15% but points out that forecasting is difficult in today's volatile market conditions.

"We believe that markets have now stabilised sales at quite a low level and Asia and the new/emerging markets are already recovering (markets such as Brazil, India, Russia, Turkey and Vietnam). In older European markets (such as UK, France, Germany etc.), it seems that our customers have more workload on their machines and we are still waiting for these markets to recover. I see no signs of recovery in these markets at the moment. However, I am optimistic that we will see an eventual upturn in most markets by the end of 2010 – starting with Asian markets and followed by new/emerging markets."

Despite the challenging market conditions, and the restructuring that the company made in response last year, Bystronic has made considerable investments in new infrastructure. ISMR attended the opening of a new Bystronic sales and service office in the UK (Coventry) in March. Bystronic also opened a new sales and service office in Russia in 2009. The existing sales and service companies in Germany and Korea also moved into new facilities with their own infrastructure. Last but not least, a new assembly hall with an adjoining service and administration wing was opened in Gotha (Germany), where Hämmerle and Beyeler pressbrakes are developed and manufactured.

“I am also happy to confirm that, despite the difficult market conditions and the restructuring we went through, we have not lost any capacity, sales or R&D staff," Ferdi Toengi told ISMR. "We also launched two new products, the BySprint Pro (which we believe is the fastest laser cutting machine on thin material) and the ByJet Classic waterjet cutting system for the mid-range market."

## SETTING NEW STRATEGIES

Toengi has set a range of new strategies for Bystronic to follow over the next five years.

"For us, the Chinese market (the fastest growing market) will take priority over the next four-five years," Toengi told ISMR.
