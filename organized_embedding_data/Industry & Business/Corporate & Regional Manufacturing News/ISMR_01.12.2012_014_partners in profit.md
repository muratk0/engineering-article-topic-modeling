# PARTNERS IN PROFIT

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

ISMR spent the day with KMF Sheet Metal to gauge how the company has invested in its people and facilities and discuss its strategic focus for the future

Above: Gareth Higgins, MD, KMF Sheet Metal

KMF is the largest subcontract manufacturer of precision sheet metal components in the UK, supplying a diverse customer base from sectors such as aerospace, medical, renewable energy and telecommunications. Its 100,000 sq. ft. Newscastle-under-Lyme facility and head office has long held ISO9001 (Quality Management System), ISO14001 (Environmental Management System) standards and OHSAS 18001 (Occupational Health and Safety Management System). It recently opened a satellite factory in Slovakia to support customers in mainland Europe and to generate new business from customers in eastern Europe.

Recent new machinery investments at KMF include a stainless steel processing cell which went into full production this year with the installation of a Kuhlmeyer ZBS belt grinding machine. The cell, which includes folding, spot welding and CNC stud welding, was set up to speed up the processing of stainless steel sheet metal assemblies and increase overall capacity. Two new TRUMPF laser processing cells, linked to an automated TRUMPF TruStore 3030 storage tower system, were also installed this year. KMF spent a further £1.2 million on a 17,000 sq. ft. extension that also houses a dedicated programming department as well as a covered goods-in and material loading area.

More recently, KMF has had great success with its self-funded apprenticeship scheme in a bid to attract more young people into the sheet metal industry. ISMR spent the day at KMF with its managing director, Gareth Higgins, to gauge how the company has invested in its people and facilities as well as discuss its strategic focus for the future.

## Customers and business climate

Business challenges this year for KMF include a tough competitive climate and the continuing march of offshore production to lower cost bases overseas such as Asia and Eastern Europe. Quality, as Gareth Higgins points out, can often take a hit when this happens as customers may not always factor in the total cost of quality (e.g. engineering and customer support, quality itself etc.). However, KMF has devised strategies to cope with these challenges.

“Selecting and targeting the right customers and then trying to form a partnership with them so that expectations are met is key,” Gareth Higgins, managing director of KMF Sheet Metal, told ISMR. “90 per cent of our customers are in the UK, with the rest spread over groups in US, Romania, Southern Ireland, Germany etc. The main reason behind us setting up a factory in Slovakia was to try and negate some of the risk to customers so we said to them, ’OK, we realise you have to outsource 25 per cent of your turnover offshore but we will do that for you and take the risk away’. This is working very well for us at the moment and we are now starting to see more onshoring activity with products coming back from China. The tide is beginning to turn now.

“We have always taken a very strong view on quality and are willing to make the machinery and time investments needed to make sure that a part can be properly manufactured to the right quality and delivered on time to the customer. Investing £200K for a business contract that is worth, for example, £2 million is just common sense. The sheet metal business is basically about common sense, after all.”

## FACE TO FACE

Above: Detailed planning ensures a smooth workflow Left: TRUMPF machinery on the KMF shop floor

Last year, KMF’s turnover was just over £30 million and it has targeted a turnover of £33 million for 2012. In the last two years, during the recession in the UK, it managed to attract new business and now that some of its older customers are bringing business back to previous levels, it is reaping the reward.

“It is critical that we continue to invest in our facilities and our people to achieve business growth. Our vision is to be the best subcontract sheet metal manufacturer in Europe. We want to be the preferred choice of supplier for our existing customer base whilst striving to be the supplier of choice for new customers,” Gareth Higgins explained.

KMF’s headline vision is ‘to develop KMF into a £40 million business whilst achieving targeted profitability by 2015’. To achieve this, the company strategy includes continued investment in people and training, investment in its new Slovakian facility (which has a targeted sales turnover of £4 million by end of 2014) and continued investment in new technology.

It has also actively targeted growth in the aerospace, defence and security sectors. An investment of £1 million in 2010-2011 was the platform to grow its business levels in this sector. KMF’s new Advanced Solutions facility, which gives it an extra 10,000 sq. feet of manufacturing capacity, has a targeted aerospace business value of £3 million by the end of 2015. ISMR toured the new facility during the visit. KMF is in the final accreditation stage of AS9100 for aerospace and was recently awarded silver level at SC21. Its commitment to continuous improvement has helped it to win business, remain competitive, create capacity and secure employment.

## Sheet metal market trends

Gareth Higgins pinpointed a selection of trends that he sees developing in sheet metal markets and highlighted areas of opportunity for the company.

“Design for manufacturers has become very important with customers starting to understand the capabilities of their suppliers and then designing systems/products around those capabilities. We have installed new equipment such as our Salvagnini panel bending machine and a new CNC spot welding machine. As technology changes, design for manufacture will change. Years ago, for example, we didn’t want to put radius bends on parts as it was hard and difficult to control – the Salvagnini machine solved that. Similarly, in the past, we didn’t want to put lots of stud welds on a part as we didn’t have the automation – the CNC stud welder has now solved that. It’s very important to let customers know the capabilities you have and then sell around those capabilities. As technology changes, you need to keep up with it.”

He is seeing the use of alternative materials for KMF’s aerospace work i.e. aluminium etc. Its other activities mainly utilise mild steel (80 per cent) and 20 per cent stainless steel plus aluminium, copper and brass. KMF specialises in sheet metal components of under 3mm in thickness and Gareth Higgins does not see this changing in the future.

“We installed two new lasers this year (a new fiber laser and the 3030 laser from TRUMPF). The cuts are very clean and consistent and the first part comes off right. I’m not sure that the actual quality of the cut has changed significantly but the speed definitely has which means we can do a lot more nesting of components so our material utilisation is better. We are now looking for longer runs on the laser with the automation we have i.e. ten sheets of lots of different products, rather than sheet by sheet. Batch sizes have come down hugely. Average batch size is less than 50 now and average lead time is between three and ten days.

“We are seeing a lot more assembly and integration of products to give customers a higher level of assembly. Other customers may just want metalwork – each requirement is different. We have over 300 active customers each year. Some, we just deal with annually and some send us orders every three days so we probably deal with around 75 customers per month. Our strategy is to have lots of capacity by investing before capacity becomes an issue – we never get to the point where we have to wait three months for a machine before we can start delivering on time. When we see there is 80 per cent capacity utilisation, we start investing,” he told ISMR.

KMF also works closely with customers to develop new products. For example, it is working on a video conferencing product with a newly established business that needed a prototype and design partner. It is currently refining the product with them and will move forward with them in the future as a partner and reap the business reward. It may do twenty deals like this over the course of a year. It also runs design open days with customers to let customers know how much technology has changed and progressed.

Sheet metal products and components made by the company are highly diverse including, for example, high quality spectrometry products for laboratories and food manufacturing, radiator covers for prisons, sunbeds, door furniture, elevator parts, desks for schools, fruit machine components etc. This year, one of its customers includes a well-known international coffee chain manufacturer ‘Costa Express’ for which KMF produces up to 50 coffee machines per week.

## An eye on success

KMF’s much-lauded apprenticeship scheme is self-funded and has been very successful, with former apprentices now taking on highly skilled roles in the manufacturing facility and others taking on management roles. It also launched an awards scheme to attract young people into manufacturing – the KMF Young Engineer of the Year Awards (supported by the UK’s National Apprenticeship Scheme) has been rolled out across Staffordshire with over 20 schools to date signed up. Winners are given the opportunity of an apprenticeship and career at KMF, as well as taking away an iPad.

This year, the company offered 12 apprenticeship positions. Each apprentice is challenged to develop the skills required to be successful in a modern engineering manufacturing environment through a combination of practical and classroom studies.

“Our greatest success over the last few months has been the training and development awards we have won, particularly locally, with the apprentice training school,” said Gareth Higgins. “The UK’s Business Secretary, Dr Vince Cable, came here recently, operated a press brake under the guidance of one of our apprentices and sang our praises when he left and we are proud to have attracted someone of his calibre to our facility,” he added.

“I am particularly proud of our apprenticeship scheme as we have worked so hard on it and have seen an excellent calibre of apprentice come through the programme. We think the structure is right. I’m also proud of our employee productivity share scheme where every employee is rewarded for the improvement activities they carry out. Employee involvement is really important – it’s an extension of the same partnership practice we have with customers. We have a fantastic culture here which takes a lot of hard work, effort and communication to move forward.”

ISMR saw this for itself as it toured KMF’s facility and viewed its extensive facilities for apprentices (including a dedicated practical area for apprentices to work plus separate facilities for classroom training with teachers and trainers).

“Ensuring that we have a constant flow of skilled people coming through to replace those that are retiring is justification in itself for the time and effort that we put into apprentice training at KMF,” concluded Gareth Higgins. “However, unlike many of our contemporaries, we do not see this as a cost to the business, but as a genuine investment and the results that we celebrated at our apprentice awards evening recently are testament to the hard work that everyone has contributed.” ■
