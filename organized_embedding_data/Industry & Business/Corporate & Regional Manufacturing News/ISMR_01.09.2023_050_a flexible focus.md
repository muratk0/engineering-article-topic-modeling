# A FLEXIBLE FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.09.2023 ISMR]

Gregory Wilfred, CEO of YSM International, outlines how the company has embraced a modular philosophy, inline production and new technologies to simplify manufacturing processes.

“YSM has invested significantly in a new line of radial and linear machines (a combination of both processes) as well as forming technology, to significantly reduce the time and effort required for manufacturing certain types of parts."

ISMR SAYS:

Ihshen Machinery Co., LTD. (YSM) in Taiwan was set up in 1979 to manufacture machines for cutting, coiling and forming metal springs. Today, it is a global specialist in forming technology, mainly stamping and bending lines.

YSM's headquarters are in Taichung (Taiwan), with various support facilities based in India, Indonesia, Turkey and other parts of the world. It boasts a global workforce of over 100 people and has delivered various projects in Asia and Europe, designed to remove its customers' dependence on manual processes/operations by automating equipment.

Gregory Wilfred, CEO of YSM International, outlined to ISMR how the company has embraced a modular machine-building philosophy, new technologies, inline production and the simplification of manufacturing processes.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to your company.

GW: Established in 1979 with over 40 years of machine experience, YSM has evolved into one of the top three global leaders in the supply of stamping and bending manufacturing solutions. We deliver fully tooled-up manufacturing processes that are pre-tested and production-ready. These are the latest “high tech" and "low investment" manufacturing solutions, made in Taiwan.

Our greatest asset is not just an effective workforce, but our well-trained and competent support team of over 100 employees worldwide to ensure the consistent uptime of our customers' machines.

Left: Gregory Wilfred, CEO, YSM International.

We have set up local Technology Centres in Asia and Europe so that we can support our customers with faster response times from locations closer to where they are based. Our European partners also support our customers locally and offer trials and local testing of machinery to end-users.

Our machines are all Industry 4.0-compliant and IoT-enabled. We have developed high-tech systems to ensure the maximum up-time of our machinery.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for metalforming professionals?

GW: The key driver of growth in the metalforming industry is the increasing global demand for manufactured goods. As the global economy continues to grow, especially in southeast-Asia (namely places like China and India), there is a growing demand for manufactured goods. This demand is being driven by several factors such as the increasing population, the rising middle class in developing countries and the growing demand for consumer goods. Because of these factors, the metal fabrication and sheet-metal fabrication industries are expected to continue to grow in 2024, driven by the increasing demand for durable and lightweight materials, rising infrastructure investments and advances in technology.

Industry trends (such as automation and robotics, sustainable manufacturing, additive manufacturing and Industry 4.0) are expected to shape the future of these industries.

Companies that adopt these trends and embrace innovation are likely to thrive in the years ahead.

The main challenge that I see is the shortage of skilled workers, particularly welders and CNC machinists. The industry needs to invest in training and education programmes to attract and retain skilled technicians.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus/vision over the next few years?

GW: "Speed to market" has been the most critical issue for us when we take on a project for our customers. The direct result of achieving this is a win-win for both the customer and us. How do we do this? Over the last couple of years, we have developed the machinery listed below which we supply to our customers very quickly.

Standard machines: We have a range of standard machines that we make in batches of ten or so at any given point. We have these machines in stock and they only need electrification so, for customers who need our standard machinery, we can deliver as quickly as within 4-6 weeks.

Machine module standardisation: For customers that have special machinery requests (e.g. they need a larger press or a combined linear and radial configuration), we can deliver machines to them within 12-14 weeks. This is because all our modules are standard and we can put them together as per the customer's requirements.

ISMR: Do you project any growth in turnover for this year?

GW: We see very positive growth in the market and with our customers. We have booked in several large orders, which are special projects, which will result in positive turnover growth for us this year.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes or achievements and the reasons for this?

GW: As a company, we have been continuously focused on improving the manufacturing technology that we sell to our customers. Some areas that we have been focused on developing, and in which we have invested heavily in R&D over the last few years, are listed below.

Inline production: The idea is for all the different steps or processes that are required for the manufacturing of any components are re-engineered so that all processes can be done on one single machine. The reason for doing this is to simplify manufacturing processes which result in material savings for customers as well as higher production speeds.

Linear and radial benefits: Most stamping and bending machines have been designed to operate in the radial manner. We have seen, over the years, the need to re-align these slides in a linear configuration to simplify the process of manufacturing parts. Our new line of radial and linear machines is a combination of both processes, which significantly reduces the time and effort required for manufacturing certain types of parts. We are also able to offer customers the option of cam- or servocontrolled systems.

ISMR:Which trends do you see developing in global metalforming markets?

GW: The metalforming domain has seen transformation since COVID-19 and has undergone drastic transformation due to various issues (such as skilled manpower shortages and increasing costs for energy, raw material, steels etc). To manage this situation, we are seeing new trends that companies are adopting.

Industry 4.0 and automation: The adoption of Industry-4.0 principles is transforming metalforming processes. Automation, data analytics, artificial intelligence and the Internet of Things (IoT) are being integrated to enhance production efficiency, quality control, predictive maintenance and overall process optimisation.

Energy efficiency and sustainability: Concerns over environmental impact are driving initiatives to make metalforming processes more energy-efficient and sustainable. To manage ever-increasing energy costs, researchers and companies are being pushed towards exploring techniques to reduce energy consumption and material waste.
