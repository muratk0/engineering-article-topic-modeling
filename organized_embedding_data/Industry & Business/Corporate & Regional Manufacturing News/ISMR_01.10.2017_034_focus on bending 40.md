# FOCUS ON BENDING 4.0

[TARİH: 01.10.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“Jean Perrot has focused on a middle, modular bending solution for single and multi-batch production, so that customers can keep, and improve, business.”

## Jean Perrot is launching a new and exciting Bending 4.0 initiative with key partners, called E COBOT BEND.

A firm focus on producing metalworking equipment – in 2003, it became part of the Pinette Emidecau Group based at Chalon sur Saône in France. It has since developed expertise across several disciplines, including mechanical and hydraulic bending systems, automation and robotic systems; tandem press lines; turnkey systems/lines; software and tooling and has also pursued key partnerships with suppliers and customers to further its development.

In September 2017, Jean Perrot Industries became Jean Perrot, with a new logo, new colour scheme and a new motto 'Innovation Adaptée à Vos Besoins' ('Innovation Adapted to Your Needs'). It is supported by its Group's worldwide network in the U.S. (Michigan, U.S.), Germany (near Hamburg) and Japan. It maintains its key technical focus - of its 120 employees across these three countries, 90 of them are engineers and technicians.

This month, Jean Perrot launches a new and exciting initiative Bending 4.0 initiative with key partners (Radan, Mate Tooling, CLEIA and Abraservice France) – its E COBOT BEND — and a new approach to Global Bending.

## Finding 'a middle way'

"The market is changing. It is becoming more and more difficult to find skilled workers. The sheet metal industry is also becoming more competitive so productivity needs to be improved. Worker safety also needs to improve. We therefore decided to focus our innovation efforts on methods of operation, safety and productivity," Erick Rousseau, Business Unit Manager, Jean Perrot and Project Department Manager, Pinette Group, told ISMR.

"2017 is a special year for us because we have changed, updated the design of our machines, launched a new website and are launching new bending innovations. We are organising a special 4.0 Bending Day, in partnership with Radan, Mate Tooling, Abraservice France and CLEIA, at our facility at Chalon sur Saône on 10 October 2017 where we will present a new bending innovation, the E COBOT BEND."

During his time with Jean Perrot, Erick Rousseau noticed, on various factory visits, that metal bending only takes 10-15% of the overall bending operation. In other words, he told ISMR, it is quite an unproductive process and needs improvement. Tool loading/unloading and job set-up takes a large percentage of this time, for example. He also visited companies with robotised bending cells and saw a lot of safety fences around the machines, which operators could not enter. On speaking to customers, he realised that largescale production can be an expensive, lengthy process if you are a small manufacturer using traditional press brakes - it effectively 'priced many small manufacturers out of the market'.

"I immediately saw a gap in the market between high-end, robotised bending cells for high-batch production and operators using traditional press brakes for smaller-batch production. We decided to focus on a premium middle, modular bending solution for larger and smaller customers, aimed at single and multi-batch production, so that our customers can keep, and improve, business. The result of our collaboration, with our partners, is a very flexible solution to meet different customer requirements, with an eye on overall productivity. I believe that this is really the first solution of this type for the bending industry," explained Rousseau.

## Innovation adapted to your need

There are three modes to the E COBOT BEND solution. In the first mode, the robot will work with the human operator. The robot will load the sheet metal, the worker will bend the part and the robot will unload the bent part. The operator will therefore only spend his time bending the part.

In the second mode, the robot will load long parts onto the press brake. Generally, a second worker usually helps the operator to load and bend long sheet metal parts. So, instead of using a second worker, the operator will work with a collaborative robot instead. There will also be a guidance aid on the system. This is an option where the operator can decide the path/location of the robot- he can move the robot to any position he wishes.

The third mode will be a fully automated option. The operator only works during the day but, at night, the bending operation is done by robot. This option offers lights-out machining, even at lower speed, for high- or low-batch production

All three options are designed to avoid injury to the human operator and increase overall productivity.

"Augmented Reality (AR) is also part of the E COBOT BEND project, especially for the collaborative robot on the press brake, We will use AR for two things; the first is for predictive maintenance. We selected one make of glasses in the market and developed some software to use with the glasses. The customer will be able to use the glasses to call our maintenance department, for troubleshooting and repair of the machine to try to decrease machine downtime as much as possible. The maintenance crew here will be able to see exactly what the operator sees on the machine (such as the tooling, stats/ data) etc. So, the press brake operator will be the eyes and we will be the head... The second avenue for AR will be for training press brake operators who, when wearing the glasses, could be trained by the specialists based at our offices in France," explained Rousseau.

Jean Perrot will announce its preventive and predictive maintenance initiative for the press brake at the Tolexpo exhibition next year, in its quest to develop new innovations and new technology around the press brake. Erick Rousseau talks about Pliage 4.0 (Bending 4.0) as the virtualisation of this new technology. with AR as the first step. The second step will be the collaborative robot on the press brake, which is a relatively new step for the sheet metal industry and a potentially important contribution to safety for press brake operators.

## Drawing on experience

Jean Perrot has a history of developing award-winning products - its Maneo Premium LCS press brake/bending system, with its laser control system, won the TolExpo sheet metal award in November 2015. Press brake technology has now developed to such a degree that, Erick Rousseau told ISMR, 'you can now compare a press brake to a milling machine in terms of accuracy'. The company has also developed robotic bending cells and tandem press brake lines for customers in Europe and successfully launched its Toolbox one year ago.

"The Toolbox is a manual toolbox which enables the tool line to be changed in less than ten minutes (instead of 40 minutes). Tool-change can be a heavy and demanding job for press brake operators. To avoid this, the Toolbox is installed on the side of the press brake so that tools/dies can slide onto the guide and new tools/dies can be installed. Sliding, rather than lifting, protects both the operator and the tool - key for health and safety. It is a manual, rather than automatic, process as this is more affordable for companies and means that there is no downtime on the press brake if the robot is out of order. It also means that, in one year, there is full Return on Investment based on two tool changes per day," said Rousseau.

It has also developed new innovations for its shearing machine, with a belt to move cut parts from the front towards the back of the machine and onto a trolley for safety and productivity reasons. The operator does not need to move - he/she can stay at the front of the machine for greater productive time.

Interestingly, in the development of its new website www.jeanperrot.eu, Jean Perrot has drawn on the system used by automotive OEMs allowing customers to choose options and configure the cars that interest them on the OEM website. Similarly, Jean Perrot offers similar options to customers who are interested in configuring their own options for bending systems on Jean Perrot's website

"This speeds up our quotation process to customers so that we do not lose time sending out the quotations - a win-win for us and for our customers,"said Rousseau. "In the last two years, we have significantly multiplied our turnover due to innovation and the work of our R&D teams. Innovation must have a tangible purpose and benefit, otherwise it is not worth doing."

## Developing new export markets

Erick Rousseau is also focused on developing new overseas export markets for Jean Perrot, as the majority of its customers are in France. He has accordingly hired new sales staff and agents, and is actively looking to engage new agents for service and support functions in other global markets, including China.

"At the end of October, I will be at the Metalex exhibition in Ho Chi Minh City and I intend to be at EuroBLECH next October in Hanover, Germany. I am working to find an agent in the U.S. to provide service/support to customers in that market. We are focusing on innovation and Industry 4.0 and I need new partners in new, global markets to underpin that. However, I do not want to 'run before I can walk' - supporting the customer is key," he told ISMR.

"There are lots of competitors in the pressbrake market. You must therefore bring something new to the customer to stand out from your competitors – new technology, new innovation and new services."
