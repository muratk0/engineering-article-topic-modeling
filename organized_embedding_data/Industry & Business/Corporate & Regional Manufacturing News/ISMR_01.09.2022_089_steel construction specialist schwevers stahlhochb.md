# Steel construction specialist, Schwevers Stahlhochbau, has made considerable project and production time savings with a new intelligent welding robot system.

[TARİH: 01.09.2022 ISMR]

Schwevers Stahlhochbau GmbH & Co. KG based in Kalkar, Germany, is a well-established partner for steel and steel building construction solutions. It constructs modern production halls; warehouses; sales halls; office/commercial buildings; leisure halls and sports halls using steel that is processed at its factory. The manufacturer takes on the full range of services from conception to consultancy and planning, including steel processing in its factory to turnkey construction, and builds functional, flexible and creative construction projects using steel.

Founded in 1937, today it has around 63 employees, most of whom have been with the company for ten years or more. They include civil engineers; architects; technicians; site managers; welding engineers and steel fitters. On its 21,000m² property, there is a 5,900m² workshop and 1,600m² of storage space. More than 60% of its business partners and clients are existing customers.

Steel quality is vital for Schwevers and it uses various systems to process steel at Kalkar including drilling and sawing; blasting and burning systems. A recent addition to its inventory is an intelligent welding robot system, manufactured by CLOOS: the QIROX RoboScan.

## Stepping into automation

The new welding robot needs minimal programming and enables workpieces, even in batch sizes of one, to be automatically welded.

"I was looking forward to using the system," explained Maximilian Vogt, an equipment operator at Schwevers who had no previous experience with robot systems. "The system was so easy to understand that I was able to operate it after a basic robot course. It's also really fun to use."

Up until the end of January 2021, he had just manual welding experience and welcomed the company's decision to simplify everyday operations with an automated production solution.

In November 2020, the CLOOS robot system was installed to create programs for automated welding, using QIROX RoboScan, in the shortest possible time. It has been in continuous operation since January 2021.

As the first robot system purchased by Schwevers, it was doubly important that the fully automatic system met the highest safety requirements. It was also important to the company that its system operators did not need detailed knowledge or experience of the QIROX RoboScan robot to be able to work with it.

"With this investment, the hall builder was reacting to digitalisation trends in the industry and wanted to use new technological opportunities, at an early stage, to gain experience in automation," explained German welding automation specialist, CLOOS.

## Robot welding in focus

The new robot system expands the inventory of the existing 16 CLOOS welding power sources at Schwevers, such as the QINEO Pulse.

"We have been welding and relying on CLOOS technology for decades," explained Heinz Schwevers, managing director of the company. "CLOOS welding machines were at the company long before I was," he smiled.

The company's decision to automate was made at the beginning of 2020. Because programming time for the automated welding of small batch sizes is often disproportionately high, especially in steel and metal construction, the QIROX RoboScan was chosen. One of Schwevers' selection criteria was ease of use of the system. Every welder should be able to work with it. Because the company's employees were involved in the project from the beginning, the introduction was smooth. Even initially sceptical employees were quickly convinced by the new technology.

“We wanted to support our manual welders with the possibilities offered by automation. In this way, they can acquire new skills in everyday life, learning by doing..." continued Heinz Schwevers.

Today, the company has eight manual welders and the QIROX RoboScan.

"Programming is much easier with QIROX RoboScan," confirmed Marcel Bergkämper, an equipment operator who has worked with many CLOOS robots. "What I previously had to program in detail is now automatically done by the system. That's a great relief for me."

## QIROX in action

The hall builder offers its customers everything from a single source, from steel construction to turnkey halls. A car hall manufactured for a single owner, has different requirements and challenges than an order from a large construction company. The robot system therefore had to offer a lot of flexibility.

Large steel beams with head and foot plates, for example, are processed in one-station operation. To do this, the operator first positions the workpiece on the system's workstation and the scanner, mounted on a longitudinal chassis, scans the work surface of the component and saves the result. The system detects welds such as throat and corner seams, as well as butt and flap seams, and converts the scanner's stored data into a 3D model, which appears on a screen.

“By comparing the 3D model with the component geometry stored in QIROX RoboScan, the welding program can be automatically generated. The operator can check the result and, for example, make corrections to the weld seam length or change weld direction. QIROX RoboScan then transmits the fully generated program, including all welding data, to the robot controller and the welding process can be started via a button on the monitor," CLOOS told ISMR.

“The six-axis QIROX QRC-350 articulated robot, the heart of the system, can then be used. It is mounted on a C-shaped stand,

directly on a ground track, and is moved horizontally by a carriage. Since the welding program with all welding parameters (such as speed, wire feed and gas) has already been specified, the robot can start welding immediately. Overhead positioning of the robot ensures optimum accessibility, as well as better welding positions. Additional movement devices and extensions are also easy to implement. The MAG pulse arc enables it to achieve optimum weld seam quality at high weld speed," added CLOOS.

An arc sensor is used for all welds. For welding the riser seams of the workpieces, a separate pendulum figure is defined in which the measuring points of the sensor are programmed. A tactile sensor with welding wire is used as a “stylus.” Since clean nozzles are a prerequisite for exact adherence to welding parameters and good quality of the weld seam, a fully automatic welding torch cleaning is also part of the robot system.

## Flexible and versatile

In addition to the large workpieces, various other sheet metal parts (including ribs and connector plates) are manufactured by construction specialist, Schwevers. It can use the robot system flexibly in either one- or two-station operation.

"This not only allows us to weld steel beams automatically, but also to process smaller parts. That is real added value for us. We are happy to have such a competent partner at our side, who solves tasks openly and appreciatively," announced Heinz Schwevers.

In two-station operation, the system can be equipped by the operator on one side while welding is done on the other side. This leads to time savings in the production process and shorter project times for the hall construction specialist. This flexibility also requires safety adjustments. Technical measures, such as fixed guards (e.g. protective fences) or non-contact ones such as light barriers, can be adapted according to requirements to protect the operators, for example, from robot movements.

Markus Rompf, a longstanding application engineer from CLOOS in charge of the installation, explained: "Everything slotted in well together. During this project with Schwevers, we continuously improved the software and overall product recognition so that a wider range of components could be processed by the QIROX RoboScan. The partnership between CLOOS and Schwevers was simply great. I think we all benefited from the project!"
