Aerospace component manufacturer increases machine productivity 23% with monitoring system

Situation

Employing 335 people at its site in Leicester, U.K., SPS Technologies operates as part of PCC Fasteners, a manufacturer of critical fastening systems, assemblies, and precision components for aerospace and defense applications, as well as the power generation, wind turbine, marine, and performance motorsport markets.

With increasing inflationary cost pressures, skilled worker shortages, and longer lead times for machinery and equipment, the company challenged itself to improve the overall productivity of its manufacturing operations. The goal was a 50% increase in overall output by the middle of 2024.

Company management realized they needed to invest in more machinery and people while also ensuring they were getting the most they could out of their current asset base. Initially, the team set an objective to increase machine utilization by 2%, which would reduce overall labor costs by around £10,000 per month.

"Increasing productivity is a key lever to help offset inflationary increases and keep pace with demand," said Richard Baker, continuous improvement manager at SPS Technologies. "Finding a solution that could help us increase throughput before looking to purchase new equipment, which itself is a time-consuming and costly exercise, to maximize the output of our current machines was vital.

"To increase productivity, we needed to understand how productive machines were and what opportunity for improvement was available," Baker continued. "Real-time data would be the key to removing time-consuming, labor-intensive processes, freeing up team leaders and managers. By identifying inefficiencies, we could proactively make process changes to increase productivity."

Resolution

After reviewing multiple system providers, SPS Technologies chose an IoT hardware and cloud-based machine monitoring system from Four-Jaw Manufacturing Analytics. Starting with a trial of 10 machines over six months, the system now is monitoring 50 machines.

"Our technology monitors each machine, regardless of its age, brand, or function, to reveal when and why it is not being used to its full capacity," said Chris Parkin, head of customer success at FourJaw. "This information is made available in real time so that machine operators and managers can work together to identify issues, understand bottlenecks and inefficiencies, and resolve them much more quickly to get production back on schedule."

At SPS, the machine monitoring system has removed inefficiencies in procedures, improved communication between the machine operators and the inspection lab, and reduced downtime spent waiting for results of critical inspection processes. More efficient work and scheduling has increased shift capacity, and cross-department cooperation has improved, helping to ensure problems are identified immediately and machines remain operational.

"All of these activities have led to an increase in machine productivity of 23% which equates to 273 additional productive hours per week on the machines that have the platform running, which we see as a significant result for the business operationally and commercially," Baker said.

"The project was led by the continuous improvement function with SPS; however, importantly, a cross-functional team across all operations from shop floor operators to the general manager were part of the project team, and all interested in delivering positive results for the business. Getting this cross-functional stakeholder buy-in as early as possible resulted in high levels of engagement which has resulted in getting an immediate benefit from the technology."

Automated deburring system improves safety, reduces labor requirements for tower crane manufacturer

Situation

With more than seven decades of experience, Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH is a specialist in lifting technology for all types of structural engineering tasks. A workforce of around 1,630 employees at the Biberach plant produces tower cranes for construction sites worldwide, including fast-erecting, top-slewing, luffing jib, and special-purpose cranes, as well as mobile construction cranes.

Until recently, workers manually performed edge rounding on plasma-, oxyfuel-, and laser-cut metal sheet for its tower crane production. Despite high safety standards in place at the plant, there was still a certain risk of accidents with workers using manual angle grinders.

In addition to these safety concerns, Liebherr was finding it increasingly difficult to recruit qualified employees for the finishing area. The company realized a need for a deburring line that would improve safety while also reducing labor requirements.

Resolution

Liebherr brought together three partners in the field of metal processing and robot technology—ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH, Rösler Oberflächentechnik GmbH, and Teqram B.V.—to develop a fully automated deburring process for its cut parts.

The system removes slag and performs descaling, deburring, and edge rounding to prepare metal sheet weighing up to 441 lbs. for subsequent production steps. Robots equipped with camera technology position the sheets, which are up to 49 in. wide and 3.93 in. thick, and remove them again after processing.

"Automating processes means we can free up employees from monotonous and physically demanding work," said Robert Piesche, production technology engineer at Liebherr-Werk Biberach. "They are also exposed to less dirt and noise as a result, and we have been able to significantly increase occupational safety."

"This setup, as it exists at our plant, is the first to bring together components from this trio of companies," explained Dominic Kreutle, head of production technology at Liebherr-Werk Biberach GmbH.

The robot systems with various grippers were supplied by Teqram B.V. Equipped with intelligent cameras, the EasyBots can recognize parts in their work area regardless of their position on the pallet. This eliminates the need for time-consuming teaching or programming of each robot.

The robots also have an option for automated processing of parts that are too large for the line. With access to various tools such as chisels and angle grinders, they can finish parts with precision.

Rösler Oberflächentechnik GmbH supplied the wire belt blasting system for descaling, paint stripping, derusting, roughening, and cleaning the sheet metal. For slag removal, deburring, and edge rounding of the sheet metal, ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH contributed the EdgeBreaker 9000, which performs these processes in a single run. Compared to single-sided machines through which sheets have to pass several times, this system cuts time by 50% while increasing productivity.
