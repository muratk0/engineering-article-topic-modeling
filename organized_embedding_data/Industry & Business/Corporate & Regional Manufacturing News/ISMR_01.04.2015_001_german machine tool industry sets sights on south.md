# German machine tool industry sets sights on South Korea

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

The VDW Technology Symposium ended with a VDW delegation visit to Doosan Infracore in Seoul Incheon, where construction machinery and engines are produced. (Source: VDW)

FOR DECADES NOW, South Korea, as an up-and-coming industrialised nation, has ranked among the most important markets of the future, not least in the high-tech sector. This has long been realised by German manufacturers of high-precision, high-performance machine tools, who aim to further intensify their business with South Korea in the future.

Following a successful first event in 2012, 23 German manufacturers of machine tools showcased their products, solutions and services at this year’s Technology Symposium hosted by the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association) in Seoul on 17 and 18 March 2015. 270 representatives from South Korean companies responded to the VDW’s invitation. They came predominantly from the automotive industry, the general mechanical engineering sector and the metalworking industry. The symposium was opened by Carl Martin Welcker, the VDW’s Vice Chairman and Managing Partner of Alfred H. Schütte GmbH & Co. KG, Cologne.

2013 and 2014 were boom years for South Korea. Last year, for example, machine tool consumption rose by 5% to reach 3.6 billion euros. For 2015 and 2016, the VDW’s British forecasting partner, Oxford Economics, expects significantly more vigorous dynamics, with growth rates of almost 10%.

The most important foreign supplier is Japan, which accounts for up to half of South Korea’s imports. The German sector is the second-biggest supplier and, following a record year in 2013, accounted in the following year for 17% of total imports. For German vendors, the task now is to keep on tapping this huge potential. South Korea has, in recent years, evolved into a significant growth market for the German machine tool industry. ■
