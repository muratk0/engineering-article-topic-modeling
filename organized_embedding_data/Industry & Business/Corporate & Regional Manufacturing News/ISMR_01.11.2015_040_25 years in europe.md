# 25 YEARS IN EUROPE

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

As Nidec Minster celebrates its 25th year anniversary in Europe, its President/COO and VP, Machinery and Automation discusses the group’s latest acquisition with ISMR

Rick Schwartz, VP, Machinery and Automation, Nidec Minster  
ISMR SAYS:  
Earlier this year, press specialist Nidec Minster announced the acquisition of Spanish press-builder, Arisa, located in Logroño. Additional shared service, manufacturing and sales support between Minster and Arisa will play an important role in further market penetration for the press-maker in the European material forming industry.

The acquisition allows Nidec Minster, which has been operating in Europe for twenty-five years, the opportunity to expand significantly with a new European production site, add new mechanical and servo press ranges to its product portfolio with immediate effect and enjoy new growth opportunities in Europe. Arisa's target audience and market are primarily European Tier 1 and Tier 2 automotive suppliers.

“We have expanded and experienced steady growth in Europe during the last 25 years,” Nidec Minster told ISMR at Blechexpo. “We are optimistic that the customer service provided by Minster Germany will continue to increase and improve in the years ahead.”

## The road to expansion

Based in Minster, Ohio Nidec Corporation operates Minster service Centers in Ningbo (China), Halblech (Germany) and Querétaro (Mexico) and sales offices in Japan, Belgium, Mexico, Spain, China, India, Germany and Brazil. ISMR caught up with its President and COO, as well as its Vice President for Machinery and Automation, at this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

“At this show, we are focusing on the continued development of Minster servo technology and the benefits of the recent ARISA acquisition,” Steve Gruber, President and COO, told ISMR. “This Spanish acquisition opens us up to new technologies as well as to new markets and geographies. It also significantly adds to our service and supply capability. Our servo technology has traditionally been supplied in the range from 100-600 tonnes – we are now working on a new planetary gear box with higher speeds and will have a 100-tonne press, with that gearbox on display, next week at the FABTECH exhibition in Chicago.”

On its stand at Blechexpo, Nidec Minster showcased a high speed Kyori press for the connector market.

“This will challenge some of the existing competitors in this space. It has a long bed for longer tools, adjustable stroke and a Siemens control,” highlighted Gruber.

“As well as adding greater capability to our press portfolio range, we continue to invest in new machines for our factories,” added Rick Schwartz, VP, Machinery and Automation. “We have invested over one million dollars this year in a new employee education centre, with labs and two full-time instructors, on the Minster (Ohio) site. We make our own materials/cast iron and have our own foundry at the Ohio factory. We also do all our welding and are very vertically integrated.

“We have sold many presses into Europe and are strong there in markets such as packaging. The acquisition of ARISA will give us a greater foothold in the European market and more of an automotive focus. Within the Nidec Minster group now, the combined group size has grown to over 1000 employees. In addition, we are celebrating 25 years of operation in Germany this year and our company’s 120th anniversary next year.”

## 25 years in Germany

Celebrating 25 years in Germany, press specialist Nidec Minster Corporation continues to expand its global customer service and sales support network.

Established in October 1990, Minster Germany began as a small one-man sales office with two field servicemen to provide faster response times. Before that, most of the service support for Europe, the Middle East and Africa was dispatched from Minster U.S.A. Since those humble beginnings, Minster Germany has expanded and networks with Nidec Minster’s service centres around the world to offer a full suite of service-related solutions.

“Our 25th anniversary is a significant achievement and a demonstration of our continuous commitment to supporting our machinery globally,” said Minster Germany’s managing director, Donald Legg.

By 1997, Minster Germany was expanding with additional field service personnel and technical support operators available in the Central European Time Zone, and inventoried repair parts.

“Beverage and food container manufacturers are 24/7/365 industries, and meeting their around-the-clock needs was a major factor in setting up a hotline and rapid response capabilities,” Legg said.

As business expanded in Europe, the repair parts inventory located at the German facility increased tenfold to enable Nidec Minster to ship required parts faster by reducing freight times and customs delays. In 2003, Minster Germany relocated to Halblech in southern German, sharing a major manufacturing facility with strategic partner, Otto Bihler Maschinenfabrik. This co-location provided Nidec Minster with component and press rebuild capabilities as well as improving the reaction time for emergency repairs.

Today, Minster Germany dispatches field service personnel from six different strategic locations in Europe to customers as far afield as Mumbai, Lisbon, Cape Town and Siberia.

## A market overview

“The automotive market in North America been strong this year – we see very good opportunities in Mexico as well,” explained Steve Gruber. “Our market in containers/can production is very strong – we are exporting over 70% of our presses into that market. We enjoy the world’s largest market share for two-piece cans in the metal packaging industry. The electric motor lamination market this year has been very steady for us (Nidec motors can be found in most smartphones).”

The press specialist has also placed keener emphasis on servo press technology in recent years.

“Servo technology is hot – it needs to process stronger, lighter-weight materials. Minster servo presses are more accurate and better at energysaving. Minster can also offer the higher speed levels required by customers,” explained Schwartz. “Materials are getting thinner all the time and presses must consequently be more accurate.

“Our customers demand from Minster provision of the same equipment and same levels of service, around the world, with 100% uptime. That means rapid provision of service and equipment as well as an eye on accuracy, reliability and productivity.”

TRUMPF released a number of new innovations at the recent Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart, including two significant moves to meet the challenges of Industry 4.0 and the Smart Factory.

## Technology for networked manufacturing

Declining batch sizes and the increasing number of variations in items are constantly adding complexity to manufacturing processes in the sheet metal processing industry. Keeping an overview of activities and keeping costs under control have become a challenge. To meet this, TRUMPF has released TruConnect to help achieve the individual optimization of business processes as a whole and to reduce the number of interface points. With individualized solutions, the company aims to guide customers along the road, step by step, to achieving a fully networked Smart Factory and ultimately towards meeting Industry 4.0.

“Our experts analyse the processes used on a customer’s shop floor and identify the potential for improvement right along the value chain. They then consult with the customer to determine which approaches to a solution would make sense for the company. The customer’s criteria are decisive. He determines the direction to be followed to achieve improvement - greater transparency; improved profitability; faster throughput; speedy response to changes or a mix of several of those criteria. The recommendation for implementation shows the customer how he can achieve the desired goal,” TRUMPF explained.

The machinery and products made by TRUMPF are a strong basis for TruConnect solutions but it is also possible to include other machines and workplaces. Where needed, functions created by partners can augment the concept. Depending upon requirements, an individualized Smart Factory can be created.

Included in the implementation recommendation might be software packages for production control, warehouse logistics or automatic generation of bids. TruConnect also offers monitoring tools, solutions for marking and tracing parts and more.

The name is that of an IT services provider for companies in manufacturing, founded by TRUMPF. As an open business platform, AXOOM supports the entire value chain. The functions offered by the platform help to simplify steps in the manufacturing process, to synchronize them and to boost overall productivity to an entirely new level. With its modular solutions, manufacturing companies can use AXOOM to make the transition to Industry 4.0 at their own speed, step by step.

“With AXOOM we are thinking outside the box and are advocating a new, digital and open business platform that covers the entire value chain, regardless of the manufacturer involved. ## Fibre laser in focus

TRUMPF’s new 2D laser cutting system, TruLaser 2030 fiber, combines the advantages of a compact unit and the performance and power of a higher series of machines. This machine, powered by the TruDisk solid-state laser, very rapidly cuts thin sheet metal, in particular. The machine’s enhanced dynamics and the motion unit with gantry drives also contribute to high productivity. Machines and systems from a variety of providers working together intelligently – this is the way, says TRUMPF, to achieve shorter non-productive periods.

Standard equipment for the TruLaser 2030 fiber is the TruDisk 3001 with three kilowatt laser power - the four-kilowatt version, TruDisk 4001, is optional. The machine therefore offers the advantages of solid-state laser cutting. Its strength is in sheet metal thicknesses of up to 12mm where it is highly productive. It also offers the flexibility to process thicker materials. At three kilowatts of power it can cut mild steel of up to 20mm, stainless steel of up to 16mm and aluminium of up to 12mm thick. At four kilowatts, the boundary is raised to 20mm of stainless steel and mild steel as well as 16mm in aluminium. Non-ferrous metals are also handled by this line.

Depending upon the laser power selected, the system can cut copper and brass sheet at up to 6 or 8mm. All materials and thicknesses can be separated with a single cutting unit. That saves set-up time, since it eliminates the need to change lenses or cutting heads. The collision protection built into the cutting head, and an automatic nozzle changer with space for as many as eight nozzles, reduce non-productive time even further.

Featuring lengths of up to 8m and bending depths of up to 500mm, these machines are for machining larger parts and long profiles. The immense press power can also be concentrated onto smaller parts with short bending sides.
