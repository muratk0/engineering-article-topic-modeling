# MAKING ITS MARK

[TARİH: 01.04.2018 ISMR]

A. E. Oscroft has now bolstered its expertise in copper and aluminium pressings with the installation of a new servo press, which has also opened up new markets.

ISMR SAYS:

"Innovation is at the heart of A. E. Oscroft's operations"

A .E. Oscroft & Sons Ltd. (AEO), a specialist in presswork since 1947, is a family-run UK business which spans three generations. Originally founded by Arthur Edgar Oscroft, the company's early development owes much to the Midlands emerging post-war motor industry and its local demand for quality metal pressings.

Today, the company provides a more bespoke service in the design and manufacture of pressings, welding, value-added assemblies and prototypes in a diverse range of materials. As a dedicated presswork manufacturer and supplier with over 70 years of experience, it offers a complete service from laser cut prototypes to full production, combining technology with quality to develop its customers' designs into durable and adaptable products. Additional operations include automated welding, precision machining processes, surface preparation and plating/coating.

It has now bolstered its expertise in copper and aluminium pressings with the installation of a new servo press, which has also enabled the company to enter new automotive (electric vehicles) and rail markets. ISMR spoke to its managing director, Chris Oscroft Jr., on the company's trajectory – past, present and future.

## Innovation in focus

"This is a family business, established in 1947, in the automotive sector. Originally starting out as a one-man band, it has now grown into a much more structured business successfully breaking into other sectors. We are currently working on our next three-year business plan. Our tier of senior managers head the strategic areas of the business which is crucial for our supply into various sectors such as rail, automotive, construction and bespoke productbased industries (i.e. steam and gas). We are constantly exploring new opportunities, applying our knowledge and expertise into new sectors," Chris Oscroft Jr. told ISMR

One of the longest lead times for a sheet metal part is tooling manufacture

Innovation is at the heart of A. E. Oscroft's operation. With around 125 people working for the company operation plus apprentices in the tool room and quality department as well as graduates in the design area and sales department, it aims to transfer knowledge to the next generations.

The company has a fully functional tool room. It also uses external toolmakers and has built up capacity to manage tooling through a specialised projects department. It takes at least fifteen years to build up competence as a toolmaker, Chris Oscroft explained, so apprenticeships and partnerships with other toolmakers are necessary to keep this key UK specialism alive.

"Our decision to set up our own tool room was not only to maintain our own tooling but also for the more technically difficult and shorter lead time work. One of the longest lead times for a sheet metal part is tooling manufacture. Part of our business model going forwards is to attract more product-based companies, and also to develop our own products," added Chris Oscroft Jr.

The company owns Weller Wheels Ltd. in Stratford upon Avon (producing low volume bespoke and own design steel wheels) and Ladbroke Ltd. (which develops a soil blocking gardening product).

"We currently produce our own products, through these companies, and bring a diversified revenue stream to the Group. With the wheels business, we are welding different types of high tensile steels, putting more strength into our products, and we can therefore feed that expertise back into our customers' products. We are very active but always looking for new opportunities," Chris Oscroft told ISMR.

## Stamp of approval

A.E. Oscroft's wide variety of progression, welded and drawn pressings has now been augmented with the addition of a new 630-tonne servo press. The AIDA 630-tonne DSF MCX progression servo press was installed in 2016 and went into full operation over 2017. It joins another fifteen AIDA presses in operation at the company.

"The new servo press is being used for high-tolerance progression work for Mitsui Cable (these are specialised pressings). High-tensile steel sub-frame components are also being run on the press. We are currently stamping an innovative aluminium seat panel component (for the rail industry on it) as well as copper components for electric vehicles (a relatively new market for us). The press is also used in general operations as it is quite cost-effective and also relieves some of the work from our 250-tonne press. With its servo capabilities, it can eliminate the non-valueadded part of the stroke. We are also looking to use the press with more niche servo-type work, because it has such greater capabilities," explained Chris Oscroft Jr.

"We have built up our expertise in forming aluminium with the new servo press, as more CNC control is given over the stroke. The speed of the stroke can be adjusted to double-hit a component within the cycle. It also deals effectively with the spring back on the aluminium. With high-strength steels, we can slow the cycle right down on the press and reduce the velocity to zero as it hits and then accelerates through the material. This helps with tool life and improves part quality; simulated fine blanking is possible in many cases.”

As the automotive industry increasingly looks to 'lightweight' automotive parts, meaning higher-strength alloys and materials, he sees a stronger future for the servo press 'to engineer out any problems'.

"My aim is to develop deep collaborative partnerships with customers and to understand what they would like to achieve, so that we can show them a better way of solving issues or producing parts. We want to generate cost or time savings by perhaps pressing or fine blanking components which have previously not been stamped, if this meets the customer's requirements. We are offering a service, rather than just a manufactured product," he told ISMR.

"We are working with a lot of companies on pressing battery components, on the servo press, as the automotive industry moves towards more electric cars, especially in light of new legislation in this area. We are becoming experts through our work with Formula E and the previous CX75 project on battery components. We have become the 'go to' place for pressing copper and aluminium parts for battery components on the new press, which has brought us an increase in custom. The purchase of the servo press has enabled us to make more products on the progression route than was previously feasible. We are also using our prototyping capabilities and our serial manufacturing knowledge to help our customers design for manufacture at the earliest stages, thus reducing waste. This is another new revenue stream for us," he added.
