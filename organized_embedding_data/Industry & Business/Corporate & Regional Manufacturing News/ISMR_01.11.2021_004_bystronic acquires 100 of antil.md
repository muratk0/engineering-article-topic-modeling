# Bystronic acquires 100% of Antil

[TARİH: 01.11.2021 ISMR]

Swiss sheet metal processing specialist, Bystronic, is increasing its existing 70% stake in Antil S.p.A. and acquiring the remaining 30% minority stake. It is therefore strengthening its portfolio in automation, in accordance with its Strategy 2025.

"Flexible manufacturing aligned with automated material handling and data flow is key to the productivity of our customers in the sheet metal processing industry. In the summer of 2018, we acquired 70% of the family-owned company Antil S.p.A. in San Giuliano Milanese (Province of Milan) in northern Italy. Since then, we have integrated Antil's operations, doubled sales to approximately CHF 40 million at good profitability and increased the number of employees from 110 to 200," it explained.

"Since the beginning of the year, the demand for automation solutions for laser cutting systems has grown well above average. As of the end of November, we are acquiring the remaining minority stake of 30% for around CHF 13 million to further increase customer proximity and expand the market," it added.

Antil's portfolio includes loading and unloading laser cutting and punching systems; automated sheet metal storage systems and robot-assisted bending solutions for the gold segment. In Italy, Bystronic also operates its Group-wide competence centre for automation solutions.

Alex Waser, CEO of Bystronic, emphasised: "Automation is one of the core drivers of growth for both our customers and our own business. With this acquisition, we are strengthening our portfolio in this crucial field and will be able to more effectively address our customers' needs. Based on our growth strategy 2025, we will offer our systems in all market segments and, over the coming months, will leverage Antil's expertise to develop additional automation solutions for the silver and entry-level segment in China." Bystronic headquarters are in Niederönz (Switzerland). Additional development and production locations are in Sulgen (Switzerland), Gotha (Germany), Cazzago San Martino and San Giuliano Milanese (Italy), Tianjin and Shenzhen (China) and Hoffman Estates (USA).■
