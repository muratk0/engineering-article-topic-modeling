# YSM International is embracing automation, Industry 4.0 and Big Data monitoring capability for its forming machinery in an increasingly connected world.

[TARİH: 01.10.2022 ISMR]

Yih Shen Machinery Co. Ltd in Taiwan was set up in 1979 to manufacture machines for cutting, coiling and forming metal springs. Since then, YSM International (as it is now known) has become a global specialist in stamping and bending manufacturing solutions. This includes stamping and bending solutions integrated with operations needing a secondary process such as thread forming; welding; sub-part assembly; riveting etc. All these solutions are delivered on a single production line able to produce parts with speeds of up to 250 parts per minute.

YSM's headquarters are in Taichung (Taiwan), with various support facilities based in India, Indonesia, Turkey and other parts of the world. It boasts a global workforce of over 100 people and has delivered various projects in Asia and Europe, designed to remove its customers' dependence on manual processes/operations by automating equipment.

The Taiwanese manufacturer aims to deliver faster set-up times for part production and has designed its stamping and forming machines to be modular, depending upon the complexity of parts or applications. Machines can be set up and each operation can run independently. This allows users the flexibility to integrate several processes into one machine with minimal adjustments. Machine modules can perform a variety of functions such as stamping; bending; thread forming; welding; screw insertion etc.

## A flexible focus

“Annually, we produce over 200 machines that are sent out to customers across the world. Over the last three years, we have spent time creating individual modules of machinery that can be easily integrated together to form a complete manufacturing line that requires multiple operations to be processed for a single part. These modules reduce the need for secondary operations to be done on a part, making production processes simpler," the CEO of YSM International in India, Gregory Wilfred, told ISMR.

YSM's individual modular manufacturing systems offer its customers greater levels of flexibility to accommodate different part types on a single line. The modules are designed to reduce the need for special machinery manufacture by reducing the overall cost of the machinery.

"For example, we integrated a 1600kN/ton stamping line with our standard YSM 39T bending line. This gives customers the flexibility of bending sheet metal of up to 3mm x 100mm, increasing their range capacity and giving them a competitive edge in part production," explained Gregory Wilfred.

This move towards standardising its machinery has also helped YSM's customers to reduce their capital costs on projects. Reducing capital costs is crucial, explained Wilfred, as cost, revenue and profit are the three most important factors in determining the success of any business.

"Our modular approach means that, despite the complexity of the part or the application, our machines can be set up to run each operation independently of each other (thereby simplifying the process and reducing tool complexities and costs). For example, the slides can be adjusted to align in either radial or linear operation, so that users have the flexibility to integrate several processes into one machine with minimal adjustments," outlined Wilfred.

"With this process optimisation, we can deliver faster set-up times for part production; small batch production that is no longer costly and time-consuming as well as reduced capital investment costs for customers. That means a corresponding increase in profits for them. Our customers have also been able to adapt their existing tooling onto our machines, with some modifications," he added.

## Opportunities for growth

Gregory Wilfred sees numerous areas for growth and increased opportunities for YSM in various sectors and regions across the world.

“We are seeing a major increase in the production of vehicles worldwide as well as an expansion in the construction industry. These are major driving factors for growth in the sheet-metal industry. This growth has been restricted due to the manufacturing stoppages over 2020/21, but we can expect to see a resurgence of this kind of growth in coming years or until there is a reduction in the levels of demand versus supply," said Wilfred.

He expects the Asia Pacific automotive sector, in particular, to register significant growth as well as infrastructure and industrial construction projects in various developing countries. These are likely to propel the demand for sheet metal.

“Europe is more focused on driving new and advanced technologies, especially those relating to decreasing the environmental footprint. These factors are likely to influence the demand for lightweight sheet metal in the region," Wilfred clarified.

“Process automation and process integration are other trends that I see in sheet-metal markets. Companies that are heavily dependent on manual processes, requiring many people, have been deeply affected by the pandemic. They now realise the importance of reducing their dependency on manual processes and have begun to automate and integrate this process," he added.

Feeding into these trends, YSM's standard modules offer customers greater flexibility to integrate several operations into a single platform, he told ISMR.

"Our system is flexible—we can integrate modules that are manufactured by us as well as modules that are manufactured by other partner companies. This means tailor-made manufacturing solutions for customers," he explained.

## Lessons from the pandemic

The COVID-19 pandemic affected YSM's operations and internal processes, as it did for other manufacturers. The company took numerous steps in response to the crisis and, as a result, strengthened its operations, processes and strategic focus.

“The COVID-19 pandemic taught manufacturers the need to strengthen their supply chains by either building up support from more local suppliers or enhancing their existing manufacturing capabilities in-house. We were able to support our customers faster just by being closer to them through our local Technology Centres," Wilfred pointed out.

Building local capabilities for business continuity was a key objective for YSM. Its regional Technology Centres offer sales; service; technology support; tooling trials; maintenance; training and various other services to YSM's customers.

"Previously, our support teams were all based in Taiwan. Today we have a new Technology Centre in Pune, India, for the southeast Asian Market and the Middle East. This centre also looks after our customers in Europe. Our Technology Centre for Europe is scheduled for launch by the end of 2023. The three strategic locations of Taiwan, India and Europe will ensure that we are able to serve all our customers globally. This means that our customers will have our support as and when they need it," he explained.

## Leveraging new technologies

In the new industrial revolution, where the Internet of Things (IoT) and the resulting Big Data flow converge with artificial intelligence (AI) and machine learning, connected machines can help users to make predictions as well as avoid errors and bottlenecks in production.

In response to these 'smart factory' and 'connected machinery' trends, YSM has been incorporating Industry 4.0 and Big Data monitoring capability into its equipment.

"Using various machines and sensors, and with different types of monitoring methods, we can now capture all the necessary data from our machines and use this data to produce automatic calculations and reports. This helps our customers to lower their tool maintenance costs by introducing predictive maintenance methodology. Using this information, he/she can take corrective action by replacing tooling/ worn machine parts before they break down," Wilfred highlighted.

Predictive maintenance has contributed significantly to cost-reduction efforts by minimising pre-emptive measures for maintenance; reducing downtime; improving the quality and consistency of production; minimizing human error/intervention and sorting good/bad parts.

## Linear and radial operations

In 2020, the first few years of the e-mobility wave, the Taiwanese manufacturer launched the YSM 39T-CNC, a combination line with a set of radial (mechanical) and linear (servo/ NC-driven) slides. Although successful, the line had a limited reach due to the higher price of NC drive linear slides at the time.

“However, this year we launched a similar model with 100% mechanically driven linear and radial slides. This model is very popular in the e-mobility industry where customers need to perform several complex operations on a single machine. It gives them the flexibility of operating both the linear and radial slides together, reducing the need for them to make large, complex stamping tools as all these operations can be easily performed in the linear sliding section," outlined Gregory Wilfred.

Easily configurable slides enabling either linear or radial operations, new safety features and Industry 4.0-compliant/IOT-enabled software are just some of the innovations that YSM has introduced over the last year. The future looks promising for the forming specialist.

"We will be showcasing our new lines and forming technology at several key exhibitions over the next year. This includes EuroBLECH in Germany; Auto Expo Components and ELECRAMA in India; TIMTOS in Taiwan and Blechexpo in Germany," Wilfred concluded.
