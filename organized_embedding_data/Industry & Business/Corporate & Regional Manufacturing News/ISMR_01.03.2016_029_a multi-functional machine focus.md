# A MULTI-FUNCTIONAL MACHINE FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.03.2016 ISMR]

Mark Jones, Ficep UK’s managing director, shares his outlook on 2016 with ISMR

Ficep S.p.A. (Varese, Italy) has become a leading supplier of structural steel and plate fabrication equipment over the past 85 years. According to the company, it currently controls approximately 50 per cent of the world market for structural steel and plate processing systems.

Established in 1930 and located at the foot of the Alps in northern Italy, 16km from the border with Switzerland, it focuses on the engineering and manufacturing of machine tools for the metalworking industry. Its portfolio of machinery includes steel section and plate fabrication equipment, automated handling and forging systems.

In February 2000, Ficep S.p.A. established Ficep UK to address the sales and service requirements of the UK market after previously working with agents for many years. Since then, Ficep UK has sold hundreds of CNC lines to the UK structural industry. ISMR caught up with Mark Jones, Ficep UK’s managing director, for his outlook on 2016.

## An eye on the UK

“Last year, we delivered a multi-million pound order for a large steel processing centre to AJN Steel Stock at Kentford in Suffolk for its new facility in the south-west of the UK, worth over £3 million,” Mark Jones told ISMR.

“Another customer, Parker Steel, the second largest privately-owned steel stockholder in the UK, purchased a large fully automatic system three years ago for a new facility on the south coast which will process i-beams, channels, angles and structural sections in a 24-hour facility seven days per week. It increased its capacity with two new Endeavour 1103 DDB machines to enhance the flexibility of processing for its customer base. We also delivered a complete range of machines for processing plates, flat bar, i-beams and channels, including multiple new Excalibur 12 machines, to small and mediumsized fabricators making their first step into automatic machinery.”

Jones has increased the number of Ficep personnel in the UK by 30 per cent at its facility near Leeds. He is optimistic about the UK economy, explaining that construction activity is now buoyant and is expected to continue for many months to come. He has also doubled Ficep UK’s turnover in the last two years.

“We reached our highest turnover in 16 years last year in the UK and aim to replicate this in 2016. We are currently focused on improving our offering on the service side. We have installed key people on hotline technical support for customers and intend to recruit further multi-skilled service engineers to support our UK customers. We expect to see continued growth in the UK over the next two years – Ireland, especially, is returning to growth,” he told ISMR.

## A global view

Ficep sold its first automatic system to Sweden (for sawing round pipes, i-beams and structural box sections) in August last year.

“The global business climate is very positive at the moment – the US is picking up with more orders. Russia is a bit subdued but is picking up. Volatility in financial and global markets does affect us but, when an economy is rebuilding, we are generally the first to know.

Ficep generally exports 85 per cent of its equipment outside Italy. Our strongest market now is in the Northern US. China is still a positive market for us, Asia is picking up and we supply a lot of machinery to South America. We are certainly optimistic for this year and next year,” Jones told ISMR.

Globalisation is spreading but customers still need an immediate response to their demands for service, spares etc. Ficep has over 80 engineers based in Italy that can support global customers. Every subsidiary locally, such as the UK, has local engineers. Within the group, all subsidiaries and our head office, near Milan, hold spares for immediate delivery anywhere in the world.”

## New product innovations

Ficep is still a family business after 85 years of operation, spending 5 per cent of its annual turnover on R&D.

“Our objectives are to develop new products and innovations. We have lots of ideas coming through and will be launching new equipment for manufacturing transmission towers (angle punching and shearing). Our new RAPID 25 and 35 range, launched over the last year, is highspeed drilling, milling and sawing equipment for high spec. work such as transmission towers, bridges and applications where quality and speed is a prerequisite. We are also constantly developing new ideas and patents. Our latest major development, the Ficep Endeavour, turns the standard drilling machine into a multi-process machining centre,” Jones told ISMR.

“Ten orders were placed over the last year for this machine in the UK alone. We will be exhibiting and demonstrating the capability of this new machine at this year’s MACH exhibition in April for the first time.”

At the MACH show, Ficep’s demonstrations on the Endeavour will showcase its new auxiliary axis movement which allows multiple holes to be processed without moving the section in the machine. The drill spindles move in a 250mm stroke. This enables multiple holes, scribing and machining to be made very quickly.

“We can put fabrication lines and marks on components without moving the section and machine apertures in steel work (up to 250mm diameter) as well as notching at the end of sections without moving the profile. The capability to make slots and apertures of any shape reduces the time needed to produce a component. Material can be up to 50mm thick or more,” explained Mark Jones.

Ficep’s RAPID is a new CNC high speed drilling line for angles and flats. The machine’s powerful spindles with their high rotational and feed speeds, together with the new generation high performance tools, enables the use of Indexable Carbide Drilling tools to further enhance productivity.

The RAPID CNC drilling lines are also modular and can incorporate scribing, hard stamp marking, single or double shearing or, alternatively, a high speed circular carbide saw. Depending upon the RAPID model, fast cutting is provided by either a single cut hydraulic shearing unit or a high speed circular saw with hardened carbide inserts.

Ficep’s Excalibur 12 is a small footprint, automatic CNC beam line for drilling shaped installation of a multiple spindle drilling line. It offers fast carbide drilling, scribing, slotting, tapping, countersinking of beams and requires 50 per cent of the space of a conventional beam drilling line, according to Ficep.

The Italian manufacturer was one of the first companies to manufacture the monospindle gantry drilling line to process beams using a cantilever system. The beam to be processed is firmly clamped in position and scanned by electro-mechanical sensor. The drilling head will then automatically move along the loading table to complete the working cycle.

It also has the ability to load multiple short pieces along the loading table which allows the operator to continually rotate pieces while the system is drilling. The wireless remote control feature of the Excalibur eliminates the need for the operator to stand by the CNC control of the machine. The operator is therefore able to respond quickly to such events as a drill bit starting to fail.

Paul ERNST deburring machinery is also represented by Ficep in the UK. Ernst is a German manufacturer of edge deburring and grading machines covering all materials and thicknesses from 0.2mm up to 100mm thick. It has wet solutions for edge-rounding sheet metal in aluminium and stainless steel. The company produces deburring technology for laser cut, punched, plasma cut and oxy-fuel processed components offering customers clean edge solutions with deburring, edge breaking, oxide removal and surface finishing.

Mark Jones sees a key opportunity for Ficep with automation – many of his enquiries to date are based on this.

The new FICEP Gemini 36 HD gantry style plate processing centre can machine, drill, tap, bevel, mark and thermal cut with oxy-fuel and 400-amp plasma components from plate in one operation

“We will continue to offer multi-functional equipment. Multi-functionality is key to eliminating the need for multiple operations as jobshops continue to streamline operations,” concluded Mark Jones. “Most customers are looking to reduce their reliance on labour due to the skills shortage and our machines provide fully automatic solutions to improve their overall profitability.”
