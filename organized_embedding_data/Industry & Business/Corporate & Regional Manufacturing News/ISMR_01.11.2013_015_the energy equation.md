# THE ENERGY EQUATION

[TARİH: 01.11.2013 ISMR]

We highlight emerging global trends and forecasts for the heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) market ISMR SAYS:

“There is a need for more energy-efficient HVAC equipment”

In line with the need to reduce energy emissions that has taken centre stage worldwide in recent years, policy-makers are also trying to reduce carbon emission levels from commercial and residential buildings. In a building, the thermal layer, windows, doors and insulation are identified as key areas for carbon emission and energy loss and there has been a renewed drive for more energy-efficient heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) equipment.

The building sector is one of the primary consumers of energy in Europe, accounting for 40 per cent of energy demand and 30 per cent of greenhouse gas emissions. This has underlined the need for improved energy efficiency in both existing and new buildings. Initiatives supporting energy efficiency, reinforced by EU legislation, are providing a fillip to the building automation systems (BAS) market.

Another trend is the increasing integration of heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC), lighting and blinding in the building automation systems market to optimise energy consumption by buildings. As a result, companies that offer complete systems and solutions with integration capabilities, rather than individual products, are gaining the competitive edge. We analyse some of the latest HVAC market trends, by region, below.

USA

With space heating and cooling accounting for close to 40 per cent of all the energy consumed in the building sector in the United States, there is a pressing need for energy-efficient heating, ventilation and air conditioning (HVAC) equipment. Due to the economic downturn, building owners and facility managers deferred equipment replacements but, with markets bouncing back, they are gradually un-freezing their budgets. Additionally, replacement HVAC purchases will benefit from ageing commercial, institutional and industrial buildings.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan’s “Analysis of the North American HVAC Equipment Market” research finds that the market earned revenues of US$ 13.05 billion in 2012 and estimates this to reach US$ 14.78 billion in 2017.

“Acknowledging the need to reduce energy consumption and achieve significant energy savings, end users are opting for HVAC equipment with the latest technologies,” said a Frost & Sullivan energy and environment industry analyst.
