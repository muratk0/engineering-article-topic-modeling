GE Vernova to invest $50 million in onshore wind business

GE Vernova has announced plans to invest $50 million at its Schenectady, N.Y., facility and hire about 200 full-time employees to establish a new manufacturing assembly line for its onshore wind business. The site will assemble three key components for the company’s 6.1-mW turbine, designed for low to medium wind speeds and equipped with a carbon two-piece blade design.

The line will be installed in the building where the company continues to manufacture steam turbines and generators. Completion is scheduled for summer 2023, with the first set of components produced by early fall. Parts to be manufactured include the machine head, hub, and drive train.

Altec Industries to expand in Virginia

Altec Industries will invest $1.4 million in its construction equipment manufacturing facility in Daleville, Va. The project is expected to create 150 jobs.

The company produces aerial devices, digger derricks, boom truck cranes, and many other truck body tools for customers in more than 100 countries.

Halton begins $7.4 million expansion at Kentucky manufacturing facility

A $7.4 million expansion has begun at Halton’s manufacturing facility in Scottsville, Ky., dedicated to the commercial air-handling segment. The project, scheduled for completion during the first half of 2024, will create 60 full-time jobs.

The manufacturing operation will be expanded by 50,000 sq. ft., bringing operational space to 91,000 sq. ft. When combined with its sister facility, the company’s total operational area will be 180,000 sq. ft.

The added space will allow room for an R&D lab specifically for the air-moving product line. The company also will add manufacturing space to allow for new product lines. The 60 full-time jobs created from the project will bring the company’s total Kentucky employment to 240.

Georgia Tech partners with Stilfold on origami engineering program

Georgia Tech’s School of Civil and Environmental Engineering has partnered with Stockholm-based startup Stilfold to teach students new approaches to sustainable manufacturing inspired by the Japanese art of origami.

Stilfold’s industrial origami technology uses robots to fold flat sheet metal over curves to create strong, sustainable structures. The company will share its insights into curve folding and its industrial origami software and hardware with students at Georgia Tech to teach them how this approach to manufacturing can streamline production processes, reduce costs, and minimize engineers’ environmental impact.

PPG invests $44 million to boost global powder production

PPG has announced it is investing $44 million to upgrade five powder coating manufacturing facilities in the U.S. and Latin America. The projects are part of the company’s strategic efforts to expand its powder coatings offerings and increase global production.

StrataTech Education Group awards three scholarships to welding competition winners

Manhattan Mechanical becomes first merit shop to receive TRMA Platinum Award

Manhattan Mechanical Services, Manhattan, Ill., has been named as the first merit shop contractor to receive the Three Rivers Manufacturers’ Association (TRMA) Platinum Award.

The association recognizes and awards industrial contractor companies that exhibit high-level safety practices at TRMA manufacturing plants. To qualify for the Platinum Award, a contractor must have held a three-year average OSHA-recordable rate of 0.6 or less and a three-year lost workday rate of 0.2 or less in 2022.

In addition, the contractor must have held an OSHA-recordable rate of 0 in 2022 for work completed at TRMA facilities, with a minimum of 250,000 total labor hours worked in the past three years. Manhattan Mechanical Services’ total recordable incident rate is 0.00, with more than 2.5 million labor hours.

The contractor also received the Gold Award, which acknowledges companies that decreased their OSHA-recordable incident rate by at least 25% in 2022, and the Meritorious Award for high-risk work.
