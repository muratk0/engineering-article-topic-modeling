# AN E-VOLUTION IN SHEET METAL

[TARİH: 01.07.2010 ISMR]

Dutch manufacturer, Safan, focuses exclusively on the continuous development of electric, hydraulic and robotic press brakes as well as shearing systems for sheet metal applications. It has developed a philosophy of keeping close to its customers for continuous product development, balancing its own ideas for product development with the requirements of its customers. This includes an emphasis on rapid cycle times and machines that are easy to learn and operate.

In September 2009, the company appointed a new two-man Board (comprising Wim van Kranen and Stefan Kok), who have maintained and directed Safan’s innovative vision and core focus. The following month, the company opened a new part of its factory (2500 square metres) in Lochem (Netherlands) and a new production transfer line for its flagship electric press brake – the E-Brake. It also added a new painting line and installed a new machining centre for its large frame machine tools. The value of the total investment was almost 5 million euros.

Electronic technology, such as that applied in the ground-breaking servo-electronic E-Brake, lies at the core of Safan’s innovative product development. The company’s distinctive vision can be summarized by the theme: ‘The E-volution in sheet-metal working’.

## AN INNOVATIVE FOCUS

“Despite the market conditions in 2009, this was a milestone investment for us and the new transfer line for the E-brake was one of the best decisions we made last year. The E-Brake is now made via eleven stations that gives us great flexibility and considerably shortens lead times,” Wim van Kranen, Safan’s Board Member, told ISMR.

“2009 was a tough year for nearly all machine tool manufacturers in the world – we saw a drop generally of more than 40% in sales. In general, right now, sheet metal companies are trying to reduce their costs – they want to be sure that they are buying the right equipment that will last. Two years ago, we introduced an E-Brake line (E-Brake B) for companies who wanted the technology but did not quite have the budget for it. This economic product line fits these customers (the machine has a standard spec, but still meets customer requirements). Demand for that machine was strong last year - it was a good fit for the market in stringent economic times.”

E-Brake benefits for customers, says Wim, include higher productivity (when compared with a hydraulic machine, he explains, operators can be 30% more productive with an E-brake). Operational costs are lower (‘less machine maintenance and up to 50% energy consumption savings as well as lower noise levels and no use of oil’). Crowning is not necessary on the E-brake and the machine’s green credentials fit well into the new environmental ethos that is rapidly gaining importance, as manufacturers get to grips with reduced CO2/energy emissions.

Safan’s latest development, a 150-200 tonne fully electric E-brake machine, has also been a great success with its customers. There are currently over 3000 customers for the E-brake, supplied via a network of subsidiaries in Germany and the UK as well as distributors in more than 30 countries.

Interestingly, Safan has also invested heavily in the upgrading of its shearing products – a technology that can be neglected by some manufacturers. It has turned its shears into green products – currently, the only ones on the market with hybrid drives. When the shear is not used, it does not expend energy and uses just 20 litres of oil. Safan has also transformed shears into small cutting cells by adding features such as ‘Return to Sender’ and scrap sorting systems for more easy cutting operations, controlling the complete upper beam so that cutting can be done on both the left and right-hand sides to extend the blade lifetime.

## MARKET STRATEGIES

Safan also has new products under development, which will be launched at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover. It has developed an E-Brake with a new drive system – Dual Drive – to ensure higher press forces for higher tonnage machines.

“It will be a 300-tonne 4m fully electric machine, unique in the world,” says Wim. “We have equipped the machine with a double (Dual) drive which gives us 40% more force per metre on the working length compared with the single drive system.”

Another product launch at EuroBLECH will be the new Safan e-control. It is a new PC-based HMI (Human Machine Interface), featuring 3D graphics, for all Safan controls on a new generation platform developed in-house by Safan. The key, says Wim, is that is ‘very simple to operate and learn’. Safan will also be focusing on the ergonomics of its small 35-tonne press brake at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition. This machine is developed to work with operators in a sitting position.

## BENDING AND SHEARING TRENDS

So, which trends have Safan identified in global bending and shearing markets? For a company that stays close to its customers, it has a lot to say about developments in these markets.

“The bending trend now is for more off-line software as batches become smaller,” Wim points out. “Customers want the beams of their press brakes going up and down as much as possible. On a lot of press brakes, customers only have the beams moving 30%- 40% of the time (the rest of the time is spent on machine set-up and programming). That needs to improve.

“The trend is also moving towards more sophisticated machines which feature quick and easy tool change. Manufacturers are investing more in off-line programming systems which improves productivity on the press brake itself. I also see a trend towards increasing requests for more accurate products – the tolerances are getting much higher now on certain products.”

He also sees much more interest in robotic systems. As manufacturers slowly recover from the downturn, and evaluate how to proceed for the future, some are considering the productivity benefits of robotic press brakes and the consequent savings on labour costs.

“In the past, robotised production has mainly been for large series work because machine set-up time was quite long,” he explained. “However, over the last couple of years, the introduction of off-line software has considerably shortened set-up times so that shorter batches (150 pieces or so) are now possible on robotised lines. The whole process is now much more flexible and we have added extra features to our robotised systems to make it more productive i.e. a patented vision system on the R-brake which saves 18 seconds within the cycle of a product.”

At EuroBLECH 2008, Safan successfully launched the R-Brake 100-3100/50 automated bending cell. It is based on E-Brake technology which has a 100-tonne press force with a full working length of 3100mm. The E-Brake technology is fully integrated into a very rigid frame structure with a gantry that has an operating range of 6 metres (or optionally up to 10 metres). Safan uses a robot that has a payload of 50kg. The first fully integrated system developed in 2008 by Safan was the I-Brake concept model, an intelligent press brake with two robotic arms.

“The more flexible a system is, the more affordable it is and the more hours it can run on different products,” he adds. “One of the issues with sheet metal is that it is a voluminous product – it needs handling, storage etc. A flexible system gives more freedom and space and allows a greater variety of products to be manufactured with automatic programming and gripper changes. Robotised systems will grow – we see a great future for them and they already represent more than 15% of our business at the moment.”

## KEEPING CONTROL

Now that Safan is under new management, what is van Kranen’s vision for the company’s future development?

“Safan now has a new Board – myself and Stefan Kok,” he told ISMR. “We believe that the company’s development goals over the last couple of years have been the right ones and we intend to follow these. Our target is to stay innovative in our product development. We will focus on being a main player in the market and establishing our distribution network worldwide.

“In the near future, we will bring customised products to fit more closely the demands of the markets we supply. We want to get closer to our markets - that could mean opening up more offices, and finding new partners. The timescale for this will be over the next two-three years so we are moving forward quickly with this strategy and aim to be a major player in the global marketplace.

“In the final analysis, Safan can offer a unique and innovative product line that makes our customers, in their markets, more competitive. We manufacture products that fit our customer’s philosophies in terms of environmental requirements and customised equipment. Our core focus remains on bending and cutting operations and that is where we are centring our development.”
