# PRECISION PROCESSES

[TARİH: 01.10.2021 ISMR]

Leading automotive manufacturer ŠKODA AUTO has been part of the Volkswagen Group for 30 years. During this time, it has developed into a strong and internationally successful company offering ten different vehicle model lines across worldwide markets. With the emerging trend of clean mobility, it plans to further expand its portfolio and not only with electric cars.

ŠKODA AUTO operates at three sites in the Czech Republic. Its main factory is at Mladá Boleslav, with the other two in Kvasiny and Vrchlabí. Vehicles bearing the winged arrow logo are also manufactured in China, Russia, Slovakia and India, primarily through Group partnerships, as well as in Ukraine with a local partner.

Hexagon and ŠKODA AUTO have been measurement partners for decades. Hexagon is a global specialist in sensor, software and autonomous solutions. This cooperation moved to an even higher level at the end of 2018, when the companies concluded an agreement to support ŠKODA AUTO in reconfiguring their inspection processes for 3D optical systems instead of tactile measurement.

This programme has resulted in a significant increase in measurement capacity and quality as well as the digitalisation of outputs from measuring devices. This was done through the modernisation of several installed coordinate measuring machines (CMMs) but, above all, through the installation of two fully automated smart measuring cells for the ŠKODA AUTO measuring centre in the ŠKODA AUTO factory at Mladá Boleslav.

## Offline programming of robotic measurement

The measuring cell is equipped with Hexagon's metrology platform, PC-DMIS - the same software solution in use throughout all ŠKODA AUTO's factories in the Czech Republic. The use of a single software platform across the factories has several practical advantages, such as the uniform integration of VW Group measuring principles directly into PC-DMIS. It has also added technical value with the possibility for offline preparation of measuring programs.

"A significant shift in the development of PC-DMIS in recent years has made it possible to take full advantage of offline programming. Operators of the new measurement cells in Mladá Boleslav have been able to make the most of this capacity, which allows them to start measuring very quickly when new parts production starts," Hexagon told /SMR.

Up to now, ŠKODA AUTO has used offline programming in a limited capacity, typically for tactile measurement on traditional CMMs. But with its new HxGN Robotic Automation software, launched by Hexagon in 2021, this functionality within PC-DMIS can be more fully exploited for optical robotic measurement.

A significant shift in the development of PC-DMIS in recent years has made it possible to take full advantage of offline programming.

"The HxGN Robotic Automation software significantly reduces the time required for the offline programming of robotic measurement and the debugging of measurement programs," explained Martin Jehlička, manager of the measuring centre in Mladá Boleslav, responsible for measuring technologies in car production.

"The comparative measurement results confirmed the effectiveness of the new solution and are a good prerequisite for the implementation of this software for other robotic measuring machine tasks. Hexagon's fully robotic measuring cells are the first embodiment of our cooperation in optical measurement, but it is already clear that this is just the beginning. We would like to continue other projects as quickly as possible and use the HxGN Robotic Automation Software for in-line and at-line measurement."

## Tailor-made and smart

The measuring cell itself is based on robotic absolute measurement powered by the large-volume measurement capabilities of Hexagon's advanced laser tracker and 3D laser scanner technology.

"These systems are the real key to productive and highly accurate robotic measurement," added Jaroslav Vesely, who leads Hexagon's manufacturing intelligence division in the Czech Republic. "The Leica Absolute Tracker AT960 is capable of six degrees of freedom (6DoF) measurement as standard, allowing the use of a 3D laser scanner or probe from up to 60 metres away.

"Ensuring fully automated control of the dimensions, shapes and positions of measured elements is then just a matter of programming using a unique combination of integrated Hexagon accessories within the cell - from interconnected metrology and programming software, selected rotary tables and flexible fixtures to the new cell control system architecture. The system, as configured, ensures rapid data exchange between all peripherals and therefore enables the automatic creation of measurement programs based on CAD data of parts and jigs, as well as measurement simulation, the precise positioning of clamping jigs according to reference points and, last but not least, the rapid evaluation of large volumes of scanned data into graphically presented measurement results," he added.

The robotic measuring cell concept now in place in Mladá Boleslav is the result of close cooperation between ŠKODA AUTO and Hexagon, and the solution installed within a few months is a prototype tailored specifically to the needs of the measurement centre in this location. It was designed to deliver the versatility needed, with an installed tool changer unit for direct automated switching between high-speed scanning and accurate tactile measurement.

"This variability means that the system will always be able to measure difficult-to-access areas, such as openings, in a way that would simply not be possible for a less bespoke solution," added Hexagon.

The smart cells also contain two workstations with rotary tables that use the flexible magnetic FIVE U-nique fixturing system, which allows accurate fixtures to be rapidly built for an unlimited number of part configurations to provide support and reference to the workpiece.

"The FIVE U-nique system provides considerable flexibility to automated measurement processes due to its reduced installation time, allowing for fast changes between configurations. The reference position of each part-holding fixture is automatically determined from the CAD data of the part. The robot enters this position with a special tool from the tool changer and determines the final position and height of the fixture for the location. Thanks to this, rapid readjustment of the jigs for all measured parts is possible, which significantly reduces the costs of acquiring and storing individual fixed configuration jigs for every part," said Hexagon.

The accuracy of the jig placement is defined by the accuracy of the measuring system, in this case a high-accuracy laser tracker and scanner. This system measures the position of the robot as it positions the part-holding fixtures, and subsequent iteration ensures zero deviation from the reference position.

The established FIVE U-nique system has previously been used to position parts exclusively in coordinate measuring machines but this new concept, developed especially for ŠKODA AUTO, demonstrates how this solution can still play a role in the world of smart manufacturing, said Hexagon.

## Service and support

Installing a tailormade automated control measurement solution using laser trackers is already a challenging proposition. This installation was made more complicated by the COVID-19 pandemic. However, the project was successfully implemented within an acceptable timeframe.

Following this implementation, ŠKODA AUTO continues to credit its strong partnership with Hexagon to the continuous support of Hexagon's service technicians and application engineers, supported by an accessible and user-friendly training system.

The CEO of YSM International in India, Gregory Wilfred, outlined its latest new initiatives, as well as his views on industry trends and developments, to ISMR.

Established in 1979, YSM International (Yih Shen Machinery Co. Ltd) in Taiwan has become a global specialist in stamping and bending manufacturing solutions. Its goal is to deliver fully tooled-up manufacturing processes that are pre-tested and production-ready. This includes stamping and bending solutions integrated with operations needing a secondary process such as thread forming; welding; sub-part assembly; riveting etc. All these solutions are delivered on a single production line able to produce parts with speeds of up to 250 parts per minute.

YSM's headquarters are in Taichung (Taiwan) with various support facilities based in India, Indonesia, Turkey and other parts of the world. It has delivered various projects in Asia and Europe which have removed dependence on manual processes/operations by automating them.

The manufacturer has designed its stamping and forming machines to be modular, depending upon the complexity of parts or applications. Machines can be set up and each operation can run independently. This allows users the flexibility to integrate several processes into one machine with minimal adjustments. Machine modules can perform a variety of functions such as stamping; bending; thread forming; welding; screw insertion etc. YSM International aims to deliver faster set-up times for part production.

Gregory Wilfred, CEO, YSM International (India)

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals? What about the climate in 2022?

GW: 2020 has been a year like no other in recent history and the manufacturing industry has felt the impact. With declines in production, forced shutdowns in the early days of the pandemic caused a significant dip in manufacturing output levels. In the first half of the year, the climate for 2021 did not look to be any different from what we saw happening during the end of 2020 as the industry hit "pause mode".

This quarter, however, we have seen demand returning to normal and detect a similar trend globally. There appears to be a rebound in the manufacturing industry and many organisations have not only gone back to pre-pandemic levels but also met the targets that were set or are en route to meet their targets for 2021. This is a very encouraging sign that we see now from the global manufacturing industry.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers right now and how are you addressing these issues for them?

GW: Process automation/process integration; this term has gained new relevance in the current global pandemic. Companies that are heavily dependent on manual processes requiring the heavy involvement of people, have been affected the most by this pandemic. They have now realised the importance of reducing their dependency on manual processes and are beginning to automate and integrate these processes.

We, as a company, have delivered several projects that have removed this dependency on manual processes/operations and automated them. This has benefited customer by reducing their dependency on people while ensuring the higher productivity, consistency and reliability of processes. We are all familiar with the earlier misconception that automation would replace human workers. This is not entirely true, but automation is now able to save and even create better quality jobs after re-skilling people.

ISMR: What, in your view, are important industry trends, challenges and opportunities? How are you responding to these?

GW: Reducing capital costs is crucial. Cost, revenue and profit are the three most important factors in determining the success of any business. With the business situation today, due to this pandemic, most businesses are either stagnant in growth or are growing at a very small rate. Therefore, reducing capital costs in existing processes or on new investments is of prime importance.

We have been able to help our customers with cost reductions on new investments (capital costs) with projects such as machine standardisation and making our stamping and bending machines 'modular' in structure. We designed our machines to be modular so that, no matter the complexity of the part or application, our machines can be set up and each operation can run independently of each other. This greatly simplifies the process and reduces tool complexities and costs. The slides, for example, can be adjusted to align with radial or linear operation. This gives users the flexibility to integrate several processes into one machine, with minimal adjustments.

With this process optimisation, we deliver faster production set-up times and reduced capital investment costs (with subsequent increases in profits for customers). Small batch production is no longer costly and time-consuming. Our customers have also adapted their existing tooling onto our machines with some modifications. This enables customers to move from complex processes to simple optimised processes.

ISMR: Are there any products, technologies or services that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

GW: Our upgraded CAMLESS servo-driven stamping and bending machine has been in the market for many years now. We have now upgraded its hardware and software systems. Significant improvements include higher speeds, simplified user interfaces and improved safety systems. We are now able to run these servo engines consistently at speeds of around 300 strokes/minute. Other features that we have improved include:

■Easily configurable slides to operate either linear or radial operations. 
■New safety features with SLS systems.
■Initiation of pre-emptive measures for maintenance.
■Reducing down-time.
■Industry 4.0-compliant - IOT enabled software.
■Two-point stamping presses with longer bed lengths.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus for the company in 2022?

GW: Our focus is on building local capabilities for business continuity. This pandemic has taught us the need for companies to strengthen their supply chains by either building more local support or by enhancing their existing manufacturing capabilities inhouse. They have had to reduce dependency on local suppliers and overseas imports.

We have started setting up local Technology Centres in Asia and Europe so that we can support our customers with faster response times from locations closer to where they are based. The newly launched Technology Centre for YSM India in Pune has been successfully running for over a year now. Its goal is primary support of the Asian and Middle Eastern markets. It is also currently servicing our customers in Europe.

For markets in Europe, we have established relationships with local partners who are able to support our customers locally. Currently, through our partners, we can supply machines that are CE-approved in Europe and offer trials and local testing to end-users.

We had intended to launch a YSM International Technology Centre in 2020/21 for Europe. However, because of the current COVID-19 situation, we have had to defer this to the end of 2022, working alongside our local partners.

From our three locations (YSM Taiwan - Taichung, Taiwan; the India Technology Centre - Pune, India and the Technology Centre - Europe), we will be able to offer sales; service; support tooling trials; maintenance; training etc.

ISMR: How are you leveraging technologies such as artificial intelligence, Big Data, VR, robotics etc. for your business. How have you positioned YSM International with respect to Industry 4.0?

GW: Smart manufacturing is the future. Industry 4.0 may seem like a buzzword and, in a sense, it's the mother of all buzzwords. However, it represents the new industrial revolution where the Internet of Things (IoT) collection of data is the first step, but there are still doubts about what to do with this data and how to put it to best use. The entire process doesn't need to be fully automated but, taking simple steps, we have made remarkable progress and impacted areas such as lower costs in tool maintenance by introducing predictive maintenance and the initiation of pre-emptive measures for replacing tool parts/wear parts before they break down. Predictive maintenance has contributed significantly to cost-reduction efforts by minimising the following:

- Improvement in the quality and consistency of production.
- Minimizing human errors and intervention.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.

Tower storage improves manufacturing efficiency for Swiss specialist, Mechtop.

Founded in 1995 as a machinery maintenance firm, Mechtop now has over 50 employees who design and build conveyor systems, tanks, pipework and custom steel structures (such as railings and staircases).

Mechtop has traditionally served a wide range of sectors including the paper, automotive, chemical, nuclear, environmental and pharmaceutical industries. Mechtop AG's customers include medium-sized and large companies from the food, paper, pharmaceutical and chemical industries. Leading companies in environmental and nuclear technology also rely on Mechtop's expertise.

Based in Wangen bei Olten in northern Switzerland, the multi-faceted company needs a high degree of flexibility in its day-to-day manufacturing operations. It has therefore upgraded its materials logistics by installing two tower storage systems from KASTO, as well as a KASTO bandsaw.

## Conveyor solutions

Mechtop's acquisition of a conveyor company in 2016 led to a concentration on this technology, with eight project managers currently creating conveyor solutions mainly for food and beverage industries.

Dominic Felice, a director of Mechtop, commented, "We mainly manufacture small and very small batches down to one-offs. This is often the case when spare parts are needed urgently, for instance. To meet these demands, we need systems that are versatile enough to deal with a range of processes, including our internal logistics. We try to make delivery of raw materials through to shipment of our products as seamless as possible."

Demands placed on Mechtop by these sectors are particularly stringent, with strict hygiene standards, fierce international competition and time and cost pressures to match.

Suppliers are expected to deliver high-quality products reliably every time, otherwise confidence in a brand is quickly eroded. Consequently, the machines and systems used in equipment manufacturing must meet rigorous standards.

Well planned and implemented conveyor technology is vital for efficient and trouble free flow in production and distribution.

The company processes sheet metal as well as long stock including bar, tube and profile. In the past, both of these raw material types were stored in manually-operated cantilever storage facilities.

"This was extremely time-consuming and laborious," said Felice. "Employees first had to search for the required items and then painstakingly remove them by hand or forklift."

Mechtop therefore decided to modernise and automate its storage infrastructure. It opted for a compact KASTOecostore and a UNITOWER tower storage system, both manufactured by German company KASTO (based in Achern).

Both storage facilities are space-saving tower systems with a loading height specifically designed to meet Mechtop's requirements. The UNITOWER is equipped with 108 storage cassettes for holding long stock of up to six metres in length. The cassettes are lined with aluminium to avoid damage to the stainless steel materials during storage. The KASTOecostore has 26 pallet storage shelves able to accommodate sheet material of up to 3 x 1.5 metres. It offers high storage density over small areas.

"The two tower storage systems are positioned on the outer wall of Mechtop's factory. The UNITOWER was installed in a pit three metres deep, enabling optimal space utilisation despite the limited building height. The bar stock storage system has an external goods delivery station that can be accessed directly by delivery lorries," explained KASTO.

Mechtop also visualises and optimises the material flow of production systems. Using its Plant Simulation software, it simulates and visualises equipment processes and shows possible scenarios. These results help to validate equipment requirements (such as capacity, throughput etc.) early in the project, optimise control strategies and plan ahead. This is how Mechtop AG envisages Industry 4.0; innovation and added value for customers.

"Well-planned and implemented conveyor technology is vital for efficient and trouble-free flow in production and distribution. It often determines whether a process is economical or not. That is why we thoroughly examine individual needs in close dialogue with our customers," Mechtop explained.

"When it comes to special machinery and plant construction, we take a pioneering approach. Our project managers accompany the customer on the project through the entire development and manufacturing process up to commissioning. Motivated design engineers, fitters and our production facility support the process. The result is ground-breaking solutions in plastic, steel or chrome steel - clearly planned and leanly implemented. This approach not only saves time, energy and material, but also cost," it added.

## Handling and storage

The most commonly used materials are stainless steels, since the finished components and systems must be suitable for use in hygiene-sensitive food applications.

## Keeping control

Sheets are brought into the factory through a roller door and transferred to the internal storage and retrieval stations of the KASTOecostore. An internal handling device automatically takes the pallets and cassettes to the allocated storage location, making the material available on a goods-to-man principle.

"The automatic storage systems offer us a range of advantages," explained Felice. "We have gained a lot of valuable space, a better overview of our inventory and more control over our stocks so we can work more productively and efficiently. Inventory management is via our SAP but the two storage facilities are not connected to it. KASTO could have enabled this but, given the diversity of our orders and the small batch sizes, it didn't seem necessary."

The two KASTO storage systems have been in operation at Mechtop since the beginning of 2020. At the same time, the company invested in a KASTOmicut A 2.6 automatic bandsaw. It is designed to cut-to-length and mitre sections of tubes, profiles and bar.

"With a cutting range of up to 260mm diameter and infinitely adjustable mitre angles from -45 to +60 degrees, the pivot-bow bandsaw offers enough scope to cut a wide range of different components. The heavy cast iron saw frame ensures high-quality processing, even of materials that are difficult to cut. Accuracy is to within ± 0.1mm per 100mm of stock length," added KASTO.

Intelligent additional equipment enables largely unmanned operation for processing parts in batches of up to about 20 pieces. The automatic material infeed is particularly beneficial, as the saw can process a series of batch orders without manual intervention. Sawn sections are transported via a chute to a bin where they are collected by the operators. Blade tension on the KASTOmicut is electrically monitored and can be continuously adjusted by using a manual ratchet. Hydraulically-actuated, horizontally-acting vices optimise clamping of the stock and make it possible to separate short rest pieces (30mm for single cuts and 40mm in automatic operation), maximising material usage. An integrated sensor automatically detects the end of the workpiece, so that no manual presets are necessary.
