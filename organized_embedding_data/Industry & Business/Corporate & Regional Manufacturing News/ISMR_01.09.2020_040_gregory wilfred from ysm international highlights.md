# Gregory Wilfred from YSM International highlights strategic and technical routes to help get manufacturers through the COVID-19 pandemic.

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

Gregory Wilfred, YSM International’s head of marketing and business development.

Established in 1979, YSM International (Yih Shen Machinery Co. Ltd) delivers ‘high-tech/low investment’ stamping and bending solutions, with a support team of 75 employees worldwide. Its goal is to deliver ‘fully tooled-up manufacturing processes that are pre-tested and production ready’.

ISMR spoke with YSM International’s head of marketing and business development, Gregory Wilfred, to discover how it has been supporting and protecting its employees and customers during the COVID-19 pandemic. He also outlined his view of the business climate and highlighted strategic and technical routes to help get companies through the recovery.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements.

GW: In two words, ‘process automation’. This term has gained a new relevance due to the current global pandemic; companies that are heavily dependent on manual processes, requiring the high involvement of people, have been affected most by this pandemic.

They have realised the importance of reducing their dependence on manual processes and have now begun to automate these processes.

We, as a company, have delivered several projects in Asia and Europe which have removed dependence on manual processes or operations and automated them. This has not only benefited the customers by reducing their dependence on people but also ensured that production schedules are not affected. We are all familiar with the earlier misconception that automation would replace human workers. Not only is this not true, but automation is now able to save and even create jobs, especially when it makes a vital difference to helping companies to reopen. ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal stamping professionals? What about the climate in 2021?

GW: The COVID-19 pandemic and its disruption to industrial production has begun to impact metal stamping production worldwide. This comes on the heels of a difficult period for the industry in 2019, when it struggled with early signs of a demand slowdown.

Today industries are on day-to-day monitoring mode, trying to forecast demand for products from end-users, including the automotive sector and others such as white goods (large home appliances) manufacturers. As this crisis plays out, metal stamping professionals will need to improve forecasting on the potential for slackening demand, especially in industries that may be classified as ‘non-essential’ (e.g. the automotive industry) and focus their efforts and resources onto industries which are ramping up production (i.e. for the medical devices industry). The climate for 2021 does not look to be any different from what we see happening today, the industry has hit a ‘pause’ button where demand is there, but nobody’s buying…..

ISMR: How has the COVID-19 crisis affected your company’s operations and which steps have you taken in response?

GW: We have followed the guidelines laid out by local authorities. Some of the steps we have taken are below:

Work from Home: All our non-essential staff were advised to work from home and only report to work if absolutely necessary.

Safety First: We have made our internal regulations, to maintain the safety of our employees, more effective. Introducing additional hygiene measures throughout our facilities and separating employee groups allowed us to effectively isolate a limited number of employees in case of infection.

One of the hardest-hit departments is customer service because our employees are required to travel extensively to customer locations to support their needs. We have been able to manage this problem quite effectively by supporting customers remotely using IoT - through this, we are able to connect to our machines, at the customer’s location, and help troubleshoot machines online thereby reducing downtime on customer machines.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus for the company right now?

GW: We designed our stamping and forming machines to be modular, depending upon the complexity of parts or applications. Our machines can be set up and each operation can run independently of each other. This allows users the flexibility to integrate several processes into one machine with minimal adjustments. Machine modules can perform a variety of functions such as stamping; bending; thread forming; welding; screw insertion etc.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers and how are you addressing these issues for them?

GW: Reducing cost is key for customers. Cost, revenue and profit are the three most important factors in determining the success of any business. With the business situation today, due to this pandemic, most businesses are either stagnant in growth or are growing at very small rates. Therefore, reducing costs in existing processes or for new investments is of prime importance.

We have been able to help our customers with cost reductions, especially for new investments in projects, with the standardisation of our machines. All our machines are built as standard stamping and bending units, thereby allowing the customer to easily add units to perform any secondary operations such as thread forming; welding, pick & place operations etc. without any modifications to the main structure of the machine.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

GW: I would say that the greatest challenge and opportunity is in building local capabilities for business continuity – this pandemic has taught us that companies need to strengthen their supply chains either by building up more local suppliers or by enhancing their existing manufacturing capabilities in-house. We can support customers with this through the set-up of our local Technology Centres where we can support customers with faster response times from locations closer to where they are based.

In the past, our support teams were all based in Taiwan. However, today we have a new Technology Centre in Pune, India, for the South East Asian market and the Middle East which also currently services our customers in Europe. This year, we were scheduled to launch our local Technology Centre for Europe. However, due to the current COVID-19 situation, we have deferred this to the first quarter of 2021. These three centres will ensure that our customers have the support they need from us, when they need it.

ISMR: Are there any products/ technologies that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

GW: Our camless servo-driven stamping and bending machines have been in the market for many years and we make improvements to them every year. The machines that we were scheduled to exhibit at the EUROBLECH exhibition this year come with a new set of features. The key upgrade is speed of operation – the new version of the machine can operate at over 300 strokes per minute.

The slides are easily configurable to operate for either Linear or Radial operations. Most importantly, we have been able to integrate these servo modules to our standard mechanical stamping and bending machines. This gives the customer the flexibility of all three operations (stamping Linear bending and Radial bending) in one single machine.
