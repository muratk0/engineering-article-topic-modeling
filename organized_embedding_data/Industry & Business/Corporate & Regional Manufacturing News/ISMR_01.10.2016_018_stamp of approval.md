ISMR visited Sertec's new £16m press shop in Birmingham and discussed its latest investments, expansion plans and acquisitions with managing director, Dave Steggles

The Sertec Group is the UK's largest independent manufacturer of stampings and assembled components and a key supplier to automotive OEMs such as Jaguar Land Rover (JLR). Until recently, the company has been producing over three million sheet metal parts per week from aluminium and steel across its five stamping and assembly sites in Birmingham (UK). But a recent acquisition has seen its business diversify and expand into other areas, both in manufacturing terms and its geographical footprint.

The Group invested heavily last year across all its UK operating centres in new robotics, presses and site upgrades. In particular, transferring manual and welded assembly work out of its main Coleshill factory into a new dedicated assembly hall at Sertec's head office has enabled expansion plans and the development of a high-tech, stand-alone stampings facility. This major investment will facilitate Sertec's continued expansion to support JLR's development of new vehicles, such as last year's launch of the Discovery Sport and Jaguar XE and this year's arrival of Jaguar's new crossover F-Pace Sports

## Stamping in focus

The Sertec Group, a world-class manufacturer of stampings and manual/welded assemblies, has tapped into the 'machinery' expertise of Italian manufacturer, Zani, to identify and purchase 12 machines to help it meet growing volume demands from many of the world's largest carmakers. The deal was brokered through Bruderer UK (Zani's UK agent).

The Tier 1 supplier committed more than £16m to the expansion in bricks and mortar and reorganising the shop floor, with £10m of that dedicated to the three-year modernisation programme that has seen it install a host of Zani Motion Master presses this year (with ram velocity slow-down kinematics), ranging from 250 to 800 tonnes. The new presses will give it the speed, quality and capacity to develop structural stampings – in both steel and aluminium – for all of the new Jaguar Land Rover models.

"We have invested heavily in new plant and equipment with Zani providing the presses, Dreher fitting the transfer technology and Servopresse providing feed systems from coil. We are currently completing the twelfth Zani press which should be ready to stamp metal this month. We have worked very closely with Zani, with very positive results and new press capacity/new press technology which gives us a competitive edge. We look forward to continuing to develop the relationship further – I'm certain that we will also be looking at new automatic press technology for our Gorsey Lane plant in the very near future," Dave Steggles, Sertec's managing director, told ISMR.

"We grew dramatically from a position of 350 employees, turning over £40m in 2008, to employing 1500 people with a turnover of £197m by the end of the financial year in April this year. We expect to grow our turnover significantly in 2016 and the key to continuing this momentum is to ensure that we are investing in our technology and our capability. This project is unprecedented in terms of size and has quickly escalated from six presses to 12. It's a learning curve, but one that we'll complete and that will deliver greater quality, manufacturing performance and capacity to our customers.

"What we get from our automated presses, rather than manual ones, is greater productivity (generating more sales per employee). To really compete in the UK market, and with lower cost markets in Eastern Europe, we have to take that path. We plan to unveil the completed press shop and all the new installed presses at the end of this financial year (April 2017). We are adding new material stores to the building and upgrading/modernising the building, with extensive site restructuring. We are re-insulating the building and adding other cosmetic touches. It will be a thoroughly modern press shop that will help to deliver new opportunities for us with UK and European automotive OEMs."

## Zani in focus

The Zani presses in Sertec's new press shop will be processing mild steel and aluminium parts (of between 1-4mm in average thickness). The automated presses (from 400-800 tonnes) will be replacing older, manual presses. Italian press specialist, Zani SpA, has spent over fifty years delivering over 5000 machines to five continents. It generally works on around fifteen planned projects over the course of the year, with three press types in its arsenal – the Power Master eccentric press, the Motion Master (double knucklejoint press) and the Servo Master (eccentric or knuckle joint servo-driven press). Zani is able to provide a wide range of presses with rated power starting from 1000kN to 32000kN and bolsters of up to 7 metres.

Zani is the contraction of the firm's family name, Zaffaroni. Formed in 1960, its activity moved from the production of machines for woodworking to the construction of machines for the manufacture of tools, and finally to the production of mechanical presses. It is based in Como, Italy, and the third generation of the Zaffaroni family is now driving the business. With 5000 presses manufactured since 1960, and over 700 working presses currently in operation, around 90% of its production is sent to customers outside Italy.

## Sertec press shop

"We produce over a million pressed parts per week at this factory alone," explained Adrian Muston, process engineer, Sertec, during ISMR's tour of Sertec's new press plant in Birmingham. "We have also built an extension to the existing factory, in what used to be the pallet yard, which now houses four new presses (3 x 500 tons and 1 x 800 tons presses). All the presses will be eventually be integrated with our MRP system, augmented by the Shop Floor Connect system, for even greater efficiencies. With manual and robotic spot welding and manual assembly now taking place at our Wincaster House facility, just up the road, our new tool room at this press shop should be completed by the end of the year."

ISMR also saw a new 800-tonne Zani progression/transfer press, featuring a Dreher transfer system. Eleven new Zani presses and two existing Zani presses were on-site at the new press shop, running material from coil. It was apparent when viewing the site that the Zani Motion Master noise levels were much reduced, thanks to greater control over the stroke motion. Other advantages include a remote diagnostic capability offered to Sertec by Zani.

"We run three shifts here per day," added Adrian Muston. "We have also significantly reduced any secondary operations by sourcing five new PROFIL fastener machines which insert fasteners into any part during the actual press operation. The modular structure of JLR's car parts reduces costs and we are also employing a rolling bolster to further reduce time (and cost) during pressing operations. Strokes on the new presses average out at 10-20 strokes per minute and our target is a 20-minute change-over. The motto here is 'Work smarter, not harder!'."

Material processing, and the move to lightweight materials for automotive OEMs, has also been a key issue for new press purchases, as Dave Steggles explained.

"We bought our first Zani press (250 tons) in 1999. We still have that 17 year-old press running two shifts per day. But the greatest change for us has been the transition from steel to aluminium. We have embraced this for a number of years (e.g. the Range Rover and its Sport derivative moving more to aluminium in structure). We are also deeply engaged with the automotive academia in the UK to ensure that we are at the forefront of new material technology. We have been in collaboration with

## A new foothold in Europe

The Sertec Group recently announced its largest ever acquisition with the 100% acquisition of Midlands-headquartered WILD automotive (AWC Industries Ltd), creating a £250m turnover Group (increasing from the £197m for the year to April 2016), with a total of ten manufacturing plants now across the UK, Germany and Hungary.

"This will support our venture into Europe manufacture and sales and increase our capacities in the UK. It is the single largest investment that we have made. Inheriting plants in Hungary and Germany, as well as two in the Midlands, means that we have an extensive project ahead to find new efficiencies and streamline across a further four factories within the Group," said Dave Steggles. "We now have to negotiate the installation of new presses for the Hungary plant. We will also look to refurbish the 600-ton Zani press that is already at this factory. This acquisition represents a transformational opportunity for us through enhancing our product capabilities, customer portfolio and providing an international platform at the heart of the European automotive hub."

The WILD group of businesses specialises in the manufacture of technical components and assemblies for Automotive OEMs and Tier 1 suppliers. WILD's expertise is in precision components; fine blanked components; wire forming; wire seat frames; spring parts and complex stamped assemblies, with the business enjoying a leading market share in the supply of wire seat frames to the UK automotive industry.

Whilst Sertec currently manufactures wire products, this is not its core capability and the acquisition of WILD will significantly enhance the company's offering to existing and potential customers.

"With WILD's manufacturing plants now at our disposal, this is the perfect fit for us to fulfil our European expansion plans," explained Dave Steggles. Combined with Sertec's primary experience in steel and aluminium body structural assemblies, WILD's own strengths present an exciting opportunity to diversify into the manufacture of other components, in areas of a vehicle not yet explored by Sertec.

The acquisition is precisely the kind of accelerate growth provided for by the £20m funding invested in Sertec by the Business Growth Fund (BGF) and Lloyds Banking Group in February this year. Both have invested further to support this latest transaction, alongside Santander Bank, which makes its first syndicated contribution to the growth plans of Sertec. In addition, K&H provided local asset finance in Hungary.

"Not only is this the largest investment ever made by Sertec, but it is probably the most important acquisition in Sertec's history, not only because of the size of the business but because of the new opportunities it presents. The expertise and experience of the WILD Management Team will be invaluable in our future growth plans," continued Steggles.

"WILD's skill set complements ours and together we can develop an even broader engineering expertise to offer our combined customer base. Whilst we can now provide our own customers even greater capability on wire and spring products, we are already working with the new customers WILD has brought to the Group, and the opportunity to introduce our expertise to their businesses. Additionally, our continuing development and knowledge in lightweight technologies is enhanced by the engineering resource now available to us at WILD's facility in Germany.

"The Hungarian plant is ideally located for certain automotive OEMs, such as the new JLR operation in Slovakia. Slovakia enjoys the largest number of cars per inhabitant than any other country in Europe. We are trying to create, in Eastern Europe, a facsimile of what we have here in the UK. Having a footprint in Europe also enables us to meet the strategy requirements for other UK OEMs as we look to expand our operations."

Sertec CEO, Grant Adams, added: "The additional support of our funding partners has enabled us to implement this, the next stage of our growth plans, which is essential not only to our own vision but to that of our major customers."

## An eye on growth

"The challenges for our customers are security of supply and quality of supply. Our latest investment here guarantees those core values, delivering an efficient, competitive process. The pace of change for our new investments will be challenging but we will deliver on this," explained Dave Steggles.

As part of the Sertec's Group's ongoing expansion plans, it also completed an agreement to lease a new facility at Ham's Hall Estate earlier this year. Just one mile from its Wincaster House head office at Coleshill, the 145,000-square foot warehouse has become the Group's distribution centre for finished parts.

"By centralising The Group's logistics operation, we have freed up essential manufacturing space across all our sites," explained Steggles.

In addition to the new press shop and assembly hall at Birmingham's Coleshill sites, Sertec's Tyesley plant in Birmingham has presswork capability of up to 1000 tonnes but has become particularly proficient at the weld assembly of aluminium components. Parts for Range Rover, Range Rover Sport and Jaguar XE are produced there. A new 800-ton Zani press is being installed at the factory. The Group's Presslite facility, in Aston (Birmingham), manufactures small pressings from steel and aluminium, supplying parts, to the iconic Land Rover Defender for many years until it was recently discontinued, now allowing increased capacity to concentrate on components for other vehicles such as the Jaguar F-Type.

Always looking for leaner processes, Dave Steggles told of plans for continued improvement. "We are introducing a new MRP system to be installed over the next two years to connect automated presses and welding cells, driving us down the efficiency and competitive edge route. We will also be investing further in our China office to support our press tooling engineering team. This means better communication systems and investment in people. It is a critical part of our business. This will enable us to support the anticipated increase in order volumes from existing and new customers with our new capacity and technologies.

"We are a very ambitious business – the ownership structure changed in February 2016 when we received the BGF and Bank funding to help accelerate our growth. Our new management structure is very lean, so we can make decisions quickly."

And as for Brexit? Dave Steggles, along with many other UK business leaders, is not sure at this stage how UK manufacturing will be affected.

"We will need to get on with it now and make the best of what we have, in traditional British style, regardless of whether we agreed with the referendum vote. There has to be a structure of tariffs which is supportive of trade to Europe. Our main market is Europe – we are a European supplier, not a British supplier any more – so sensible trade and tariff relations with Europe will be critical for us. That means a mutually agreeable position for the UK and Europe to encourage trade levels between us all and no costs, on either side, to make us uncompetitive," he told ISMR.

## Addressing the skills gap

Alongside its investment in machinery and plant, Sertec is also investing in people with the launch, in June this year, of the Sertec Learning Academy. Training has always been an integral part of building an efficient and effective workforce at its five factories in Birmingham but it can now take new, unskilled people, offer them an induction and train them before a single day's work is done.

Based at Sertec's Wincaster House headquarters at Coleshill, the Learning Academy will cover all aspects of manufacturing processes involved in the supply of automotive components not only for new starters but also for existing employees. It was opened earlier this year by Craig Tracey, MP for North Warwickshire.

Sertec Group Chief Executive, Grant Adams, explained: "This is only just the beginning and the possibilities are endless in our efforts to upskill our workforce. The Academy is an essential development in the future of our business."

"We have run a very successful apprenticeship programme over the last six years," concluded Dave Steggles. "We have taken people from all different walks of life e.g. ex military personnel, with different skill sets. This has worked very well for us and ensured that we have the relevant skills in our organisation to move forward with our expansion. We have also taken this approach with our press technology acquisitions. As each new press was being ordered, we would move more recruits into our training programme so that they were ready to efficiently operate the new presses when they were installed. You need to invest in your people at the same time as you invest in new equipment.

"We are a very ambitious business and want to reach £300m in a very short period (over the next five years). We now employ 2300 people and will be pushing that up to 2500 people quite quickly. With the infrastructure, people and systems that we have, the business is driving itself. The service we provide generates the opportunities for our business...."
