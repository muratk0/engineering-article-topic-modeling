AP&T's Per Josefsson discusses trends in sheet metal pressing, new technologies and key markets with ISMR

ISMR SAYS:
“Hot forming, press hardening, heat exchanger and hydroforming technologies take the spotlight at AP&T”

Swedish press specialist AP&T designs, manufactures and markets automation, presses, tools and complete production lines for the sheet metal and tube forming industry.

“We are no longer just a machine builder – we are now a complete system supplier. We have moved from supplying individual machines to supplying complete system solutions and expertise. This includes special tooling knowledge and helping our customers to develop and integrate new systems so that they can operate more efficiently and productively. Nowadays, it is more of a partnership relationship that is developed with a customer, we call this our ‘One Responsible Partner®’ motto,” Per Josefsson, AP&T’s global sales and service manager, told ISMR.

AP&T’s service portfolio includes field service, contract service, rebuilds, spare parts, technical support and training. One important aspect of the company’s offering involves machinery rebuilds – and not just machinery manufactured by AP&T.

“We can upgrade and rebuild most presses and production lines so that they satisfy the current high demands on productivity and reliability,” said Josefsson.

The AP&T Group intends to show new developments in press hardening and hot forming in its booth at the forthcoming EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover, where visitors will be able to interactively view 3D simulations of various press processes.

## Business and market trends

Despite challenging business conditions in some Eurozone countries, Per Josefsson is optimistic about the future.

“Right now, the business is still very positive. Of course, Europe has been slowing down but our North American and Asian business has been good over the last couple of months and I particularly see more growth potential in Asia. We opened a new business in China three years ago and it has been very successful – we are picking up good orders from companies that we were not initially familiar with and we are adding more people to our staff,” he explained.

“Geographical markets tend to be cyclical so we take a 3-5 year overview of our business in different markets. Over the last three years, our business split has been primarily in Europe and Asia. The US market has been slow but it is picking up now so I forecast that our future business will be more equally split between the three regions. We have increased our turnover since 2010 – this year, I expect to see another turnover increase,” he added.

He also highlights the gradual resurgence of AP&T’s hydroforming business.

“One particular business that has been successful for us over the last two/three years is hydroforming and we have sold hydroforming machines into Asia and Europe. We see that hydroforming is finding its way back again into the automotive business. These machines are much more sophisticated than they were ten years ago in terms of capacity and productivity,” he explained.

“Another strong business niche for us is the heat exchanger plate business – inside an air conditioning system is a heat exchanger (made from stainless steel metal plates). We have supplied systems to well known customers in this industry (for example, in Sweden) and it has been a constantly active sector for us. With such a large focus on energy savings nowadays, we believe this will remain a key sector in the future for us.”

## Hot gas forming

Tougher new fuel economy regulations in Europe and the USA are driving automotive OEMs to develop lighter weight parts and increase their cost savings and efficiencies, all of which open the door to innovative manufacturing processes such as hot forming.

“We have been particularly successful over the last couple of years in developing a complete hot forming system for our customers. We have been involved in developing the technology over the last ten years but it is really only in the last couple of years, as the automotive industry has moved more closely towards hot forming, that this has really taken off for us. We see a great deal of demand for hot forming technology over the next few years,” Per Josefsson told ISMR. ”We are currently delivering this hot forming solution to the major automotive OEMs in Europe and their sub-suppliers.”

Just over a year ago, SAPA’s research and development centre in Finspång, Sweden announced that a method was being developed for aluminium hot forming using equipment from AP&T. The information was cloaked in secrecy and no details were revealed. Now, however, the door to hot gas forming has been slightly opened.

At room temperature, extruded aluminium is slightly harder to form compared to steel in sheet metal forming, but the opposite applies in hot conditions. An aluminium section that is heated up to the right temperature and pressurised from the inside using gas can basically be formed into anything. That means not only a high degree of flexibility, but also a quicker and more effective process with fewer processing steps. This is what hot gas forming (HGF) is all about. Thanks to the new method, many pressed or cast steel parts and plastic parts could be replaced by gas-formed aluminium parts in the future.

Hot gas forming (HGF) can be used for parts for furniture, bicycle frames, furnishings and cars, where weight is very important. The production costs for HGF are significantly lower than for hydroforming says AP&T and, compared to casting, the method produces thinner material with a pore-free structure. Aluminium is much easier to weld than castings.

One of SAPA’s customers is currently test manufacturing parts. So far, the trial has proceeded according to plan, and series production can hopefully commence on a limited scale this winter.

## Press hardening in focus

AP&T is increasing its current production of large presses. The reason underlying the decision to expand is primarily a strong global demand for press hardening lines from the automotive industry, it told ISMR.

The press hardening business has developed positively over the past few years along with the need for large and more advanced presses, it added. To meet growing demand while cutting down on delivery times, AP&T has heavily increased its production capacity in Tranemo, Sweden. The new machinery room was inaugurated less than four years ago, and has now been expanded to twice its original size.

Volvo Cars is drafting plans for its next car model, and press hardening technology will play a central role. AP&T has been entrusted to supply a turnkey press hardening plant for car body parts to Volvo’s factory in Olofström in southeast Sweden.

“This is a strategically important investment decision for Volvo Cars and a large and prestigious order for us,” said Per Josefsson.

Press hardening technology enables car body parts to be manufactured that combine low weight with high strength and rigidity. Lighter weight and more fuel-efficient cars can be produced while maintaining the level of safety. The product advantages are on target with Volvo’s ambition to manufacture cars with world-class safety and environmental qualities.

In modern Volvo cars, press-hardened parts constitute between seven and seventeen per cent of car body weight. There will be twice as many press-hardened items in the next generation of body parts being built on Volvo’s new platform. This primarily applies to parts that form the car’s safety cage, which is a rigid frame meant to prevent injury. It surrounds the passenger compartment and protects the driver and passengers in the event of a collision.

As a means of ensuring access to press-hardened parts that have the right quality at the right price, Volvo has decided to manufacture some of its own press-hardened parts to supplement the parts it buys from external suppliers.

The procurement process was completed in October 2011, and intense collaboration between the companies is ongoing for design, testing and analysis. Collaboration will result in two complete and turnkey press hardening lines that can be operated around the clock. In addition to the technical equipment needed to transform a blank into a finished car body part, training, service and support are included in the delivery which should be completed in 2015.
