# KEMPPI HELPS BUILD WORLD’S LARGEST CRUISE LINERS

[TARİH: 01.07.2010 ISMR]

Kemppi OY, Finland and its UK subsidiary Kemppi (UK) Ltd, supply welding equipment to the world’s shipyards including UK yards with welding technology and process interchange between Kemppi and the yards playing a major role in the design and development of both equipment and software. This interchange was very apparent in the Kemppi equipment that helped STX Europe’s Shipyard in Turku, Finland build Royal Caribbean International’s ‘Oasis of the Seas’.

“At 225,000 gross tonnes, she is the world’s largest and most innovative cruise liner with 16 decks rising 72m above sea level. She is 360m long with a 66m beam and can carry 5,400 passengers. Her sister ship ‘Allure of the Seas’ is due for delivery towards the end of 2010,” Kemppi told ISMR.

Each vessel is built using over 2,400 kilometres of weld seams and requires over one million kilogrammes of filler material in the process. Around 2,000 X-rays using Ultra Sound technology are used to inspect over 8.5 kilometres of butt welds, whilst penetrant solution and magnetic particle testing was also carried out on these welds.

Using modular construction, STX can build on a very large scale, starting with block fabrication of the hull, where welding plays a major role. Blocks are welded together to form larger sections weighing from 400 - 600 tonnes with some measuring 22m long x 32m wide and 13m high. The ship is made up of 181 sections, which are hoisted into the dry dock and welded together with their respective tubing, bulkheads and decks being connected.

Each section comprises different materials, material thicknesses and a number of welding processes including MIG/MAG, Submerged Arc, (TIG and MMA) welding as well as laser welding and cutting on plate and pipe work fabricated from C-Mn steels, aluminium, stainless steels and duplex. In the early stages of production, welding is mainly automated, but as work progresses it becomes increasingly more manual.

“Fitweld 300, used in the welding of both vessels, is an example of this co-operation. It is a lightweight, compact, robust MIG/MAG inverter welder specifically designed for shipbuilding and heavy fabrication to replace traditional MMA tack welding techniques, with its slow welding speeds, high labour costs and large use of energy. MIG/MAG tack welding is much faster, more efficient, produces higher quality welds, requires less input power and the power source is lighter,” said Kemppi.
