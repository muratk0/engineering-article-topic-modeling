ISMR: How has the COVID-19 crisis affected your company's operations and which steps have you taken in response?

FM: The crisis generated by COVID-19 has negatively affected the entire manufacturing sector and has heavily hit Produtech's activities. The severe limits imposed on mobility have strongly influenced our relationships with customers and potential buyers. This is because we offer innovative and little-known systems that need to be seen in operation and require the presence of potential buyers in exhibition and demonstration areas, which the pandemic has prevented.

Sold systems need to be installed by specialised technicians, but our technicians (like many others) were subject to travel restrictions and therefore it was often impossible to install and operate systems delivered to customers. ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus for the company? Please elaborate.

FM: Produtech's core business is the production of a wide range of machines and systems for sheet metal cutting and processing, but with a particular feature: direct feed from coil. This production method (which includes punching systems, laser cutting systems and systems with EffiCOIL "combined" machines) avoids the preliminary processing of raw material cutting to obtain the "sheets" and therefore the handling needed to load sheet into metalworking machines for the required processes.

Coil feeding of machine tools therefore results in significant savings of raw material; less occupied production space; labour reductions; energy savings and less environmental impact, with waste reduction and lower CO2 emissions. In fact, production with direct raw material feed from coil enables optimisation of workpiece cutting with nesting programs working on long coil lengths, compared to sheet metal blanks with limited lengths. It also avoids generating the head and tail scrap which is typically found in sheet metal blank production.

Direct coil feeding also reduces the frequency of labour interventions because it avoids all operations that relate to the loading of raw material in sheet form. Produtech machines evacuate any metal scrap generated, during the production of useful workpieces, through a trap-door and can unload the useful workpieces produced, minus any metal scrap, as part of a completely automatic cycle. This production process avoids the complex activities of separating useful workpieces from scrap and their manual or automated unloading processes.

ISMR: What prompted the development of your EffiCOIL coil-fed punch-laser system, and which benefits/advantages does it bring to sheet metal manufacturers?

FM: The long experience we have gained over the years in the design and construction of coil-fed punching systems, coil-fed laser production systems and the production needs of the metal components market, led us to design and build EffiCOIL. This system combines the two most important technologies for metal component production: punching/forming with mechanical tools and fibre optic laser cutting. These two different technologies interact on a single machine and operate over a single work area, but with another additional feature that makes this machine unique on the market: direct coil feeding.

The result of this technological innovation is the EffiCOIL, a coil-fed "combined" machine. This technology offers significant advantages in processing high production volumes, thanks to a high degree of production cycle automation from the continuous feeding of raw material from coil. This technology is used to produce structures for household appliances; steel furniture; shelving and work tools drawer units; electrical equipment and lighting components as well as panels and structures for building construction.

An EffiCOIL system offers important benefits for heterogeneous orders with small and diversified quantities obtained in real-time thanks to high operational flexibility, wide availability and programming simplicity. The ability to optimise the raw material obtained from direct coil feeding makes it possible to create nests of different shapes on a large sheet metal surface with no head and tail waste (common with sheet-fed production). All this allows us to produce in "Just in Time" mode with enormous benefits in terms of response to a market that increasingly requires speed and flexibility.

ISMR: What, in your view, are important industry trends, challenges and opportunities? How are you responding to these?

FM: The current standard technologies for sheet metal processing are based on the use of sheet obtained from coil and involve a series of different operations performed on raw material (coil). Initial operations in the standard process are performed with cutting lines that include sheet metal straightening and cutting units. The metal sheets obtained must be picked up and palletised to be relocated to loading points on the processing machines (punching or laser cutting), where the main forming and cutting process takes place.

During this working mode, the processed sheets must be moved from one operating machine to another to perform all required operations. Furthermore, the final product obtained from these forming operations must be manually discharged from the machines that produce it, separating it from the sheet metal scrap generated from forming the useful workpieces. Various human interventions are required, mainly to handle the sheet metal during the various work phases and during the picking up and unloading of finished parts and scrap. Alternatively, complex, expensive and bulky automated systems can replace manual labour.

All of this results in long processing times, high energy consumption, the use of considerable production space and expensive logistic interventions with numerous machines used for the different processes and consequently a strong financial commitment to purchase various machines and equipment. These are important industry challenges that we are confronting with new production methods that are designed to eliminate these hurdles.

By evaluating the factors related to the production process with sheet feeding, we have been designing and manufacturing machines for sheet metal processing (punching and laser cutting) that feed directly from coil for a long time, proposing a new production method that eliminates all activities relating to the preparation of sheet which are ancillary to the production of metal components. Our EffiCOIL coil-fed combined machine (punching machine and laser) is Produtech's response to these challenges, which enables significant savings in labour, space, time, raw materials and harmful emissions.

ISMR: Are there any products/ technologies/services that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

FM: Our technical team is working on a new project that will give even greater added value to current production. Aware of the numerous and important benefits of producing with coil-fed systems, we decided to analyse the main problem that hinders this production method, namely the difficulty of changing the material being processed by replacing a coil with another made of a different material. It is therefore a question of studying and designing systems that make coil changing fast, easy and automated, with the insertion of the material head directly in the production machines, all through an automatic CNC cycle.

We are examining not just low-thickness, but also high-thickness, coils to enable processing across the entire range of thicknesses normally used by this production sector and the ability to interchange them with automated cycles. In this way, we can overcome the current limit to producing components made of different materials, without complicated operations and without supervision. The goal is to produce the same conditions as sheet feeding in the material change to be processed, while maintaining all the advantages of coil. This project will make it advantageous, in every respect, to use Produtech systems that produce metal components directly from coil, with material thicknesses of up to 8mm, while maintaining the cost/energy savings and better environmental impact offered by coil-fed systems.

ISMR: How have you positioned Produtech with respect to Industry 4.0?

FM: Produtech S.r.l. has chosen to pursue a growth path as part of its Industry 4.0 plan by applying digital technologies to the design, production and distribution of its products to bring the goal of total automation and interconnection closer. Through analysis of our current production, we have defined and developed a project that has modelled (in an advanced way) the management process of all the activities which make up the Produtech system.

This includes the ability to process the analysis of procedures adopted; data collection and parameters to be shared; information on the processes in place to monitor them; functional programming for the activities to be carried out and the generation of data that determine the objectives to be achieved in defined times and costs. It brings together the centralised and continuously monitored management of our production process to ensure the absence of duplication, interference, energy waste, resources and time.

We have therefore completed the digitisation of our business processes through a reorganization and innovation project examining the management processes and IT systems used in Produtech. These are the objectives achieved and introduced into our company organisation, in line with the aims of Industry 4.0.
