# A lifting experience

[TARİH: 01.02.2020 ISMR]

AIRPES, A MANUFACTURER of lifting, weighing and below-the-hook equipment, will deliver its expertise to two high-profile industry meetings in the first half of 2020. At both events, Tad Dunville, general manager, Airpes Americas, will be joined by his father and industry stalwart, Larry Dunville, principal of Overhead Crane Consulting.

The pair contributed to discussions surrounding the crane-related B30 Standards Committee, when the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) sat in New Orleans on 26-30 January 2020, before they address the Association for Iron & Steel Technology’s (AIST) 27th Crane Symposium, in Pittsburgh, on 7-9 June 2020.

"The two events are very different," said Tad Dunville. "The ASME group was mostly engineering professionals from crane companies, while the AIST delegation will be employees of [steel] mill crane owners. Furthermore, our role at ASME was to contribute to roundtable discussions relating to iteration to the rules in B30.2, while at AIST we will prepare a presentation and address an audience."

Airpes has three principal lines of business, namely pre-engineered weighing and monitoring kits for overhead cranes, including load cells, overload protection, readouts and data loggers; engineered lifting devices for wind turbine erection and engineered below-the-hook devices, such as coil grabs, rotating blocks and magnets.

The company has been in the U.S. market for over ten years but Dunville, who was chief financial officer at his father’s old company, Dearborn Crane & Engineering, stepped into his current leadership role last year.

Dunville has co-authored a presentation written specifically for owners of mill cranes. He said: "When discussing crane safety, inspection and maintenance, the audience is very different between five-ton capacity package hoist owners and those who own process cranes or mill cranes. If a process crane goes down, it could result in hundreds of thousands of dollars in lost productivity or even death. The focus is not on 'how much does it cost to repair?'; the focus is on 'how much does it cost to be down?'. A US$ 1,200 part might be a deal-breaker on a 'stick crane' repair, but neglecting a part of similar value on a mill crane is a good way to get fired or kill someone."

Beyond ASME and AIST, Airpes will also have a presence at Material Handling Industry's (MHI) MODEX show in Atlanta, on 9-12 March 2020, and the same organiser's spring meetings in Charlotte, on 26-29 April 2020. The Offshore Technology Conference (OTC) follows in Houston, on 4-7 May 2020.
