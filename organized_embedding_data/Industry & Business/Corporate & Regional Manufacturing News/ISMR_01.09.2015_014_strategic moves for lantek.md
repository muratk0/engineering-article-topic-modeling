# Strategic moves for Lantek

[TARİH: 01.09.2015 ISMR]

LANTEK’S GLOBAL agreement with Air Liquide Welding will lead to the marketing of its Lantek Expert sheet metal CADCAM software in conjunction with Air Liquide Welding’s plasma cutting and oxy-cutting machines. The Spanish company recently announced a global strategic partnership with Air Liquide Welding through the company’s distribution channels which cover 90 countries around the world.

16,000 companies rely on the technology developed at Lantek’s headquarters in Miñano. This year its business is expected to grow by 15 per cent, in line with the trend that the multinational has seen in recent years, despite the global economic crisis. The international market represents 88 per cent of Lantek’s results.

## BENDING THE FUTURE.

Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association), outlines his views to ISMR on market trends, challenges and the global economy

As a leading industrial association in the metalworking sector, VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association) offers its members a range of services. These include exhibitions, events, international and domestic market information and forecasts, technical studies and training. Together with the Sector Association Machine Tools and Manufacturing Systems within the VDMA (German Engineering Federation), it represent its industry’s interests to legislators, authorities, client industries and the public.

Dr. Wilfried Schäfer, Executive Director of the VDW (German Machine Tool Builders’ Association), outlined his views to ISMR on market trends, challenges and the global economy.

ISMR: Please outline any recent VDW successes and achievements with the reasons for this?

WS: The VDW operates successfully in many different fields. I will cite just a few examples… As a trade fair organiser, we have created a new concept for our own trade fair, the METAV 19th International Exhibition for Metalworking Technologies. It will not be possible to measure its overall success until next year, as the exhibition in Düsseldorf will be held from 23 to 27 February 2016. Feedback from the market, however, is already very encouraging.

When it comes to recruiting young people for metalworking careers, the VDW plays a proactive role with its Youth Training Foundation. Since it was created in 2009, we have launched a multitude of projects and brought about lasting improvements in the training provided for metalworking careers. Recently, in fact, we set up the Vocational Training Excellence Initiative. It is joined by companies who want to support the work of the VDW’s Youth Training Foundation. So far, ten companies have already come on board.

The VDW is also a shareholder in the IndustryArena B2B platform. With more than 415,000 registered customers, it is the biggest online portal in this category. Very recently, by purchasing a portal in the USA and by finalising an alliance with a Spanish language publisher, crucial steps have been successfully taken for internationalising IndustryArena and extending it to the USA and South America. And, last but not least, the VDW, in conjunction with the VDW Research Institute, organises joint research for the German machine tool industry.

Next year, we shall be celebrating our 125th anniversary

ISMR: What are your views on the current European/German business climate for the sheet metal and machine tool industry?

WS: Developments during the first half of 2015 were not as dynamic as expected. Demand in Germany, in particular, was weaker than originally predicted.

For this year’s second half, we are anticipating increased momentum. Overall, we see moderate growth in total demand for 2015. Besides domestic orders, bookings from Asia, particularly, are predicted to continue a stable single-figure growth trend.

ISMR: Please give our readers a brief background of the VDW, and explain your mission and objectives?

WS: The VDW is the mouthpiece for the German machine tool industry. Next year, we shall be celebrating our 125th anniversary. We currently have almost 300 members, who account for approximately 90 per cent of the sector’s total turnover.

As a trade association, we represent the members when interfacing with the public, politicians, business associates and the academic community on a national and international basis. In dialogue with these partners, we aim to address issues that are important for our sector, thus positioning ourselves as a significant provider of relevant input when our industry is involved. Subjects include sales markets; business cycle observation and market data acquisition; technical and legal questions; standardisation or youth recruitment.

In addition, as a service provider, we inform and advise our members on all these issues and support them as needed, on an individual basis wherever appropriate. We want to be their dependable partner.

For over 90 years, the VDW has been organising trade fairs. Today, these include EMO Hannover – a global trade fair for the metalworking sector – and METAV in Düsseldorf, the International Exhibition for Metalworking Technologies. They enable the sector to showcase its capabilities in the global market. »

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your members at the moment, and how are you addressing these issues for them?

WS: For our members, the primary questions are always the state of the global economy and developments in international demand for machine tools. Their business depends upon this. The VDW, with its forecasting partner Oxford Economics, keeps a very close eye on developments and informs its members four times per year on how they are progressing.

Great efforts are made to improve the forecasting model still further, to perfect the database and, above all, to take sufficient account of short-term external influencing factors in the model.

The VDW is also a shareholder in the IndustryArena B2B platform

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

WS: We shall, of course, be very closely observing technical developments, both in machine tool manufacturing itself and for the component suppliers and customers, to distil what is important for our companies, or may become so.

Issues that customers are currently raising with us include electro-mobility, lightweight construction and new materials. We are also doing intensive work ourselves on energy efficiency in machine tools, the integration of additive processes and Industry 4.0.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal and machine tool markets?

WS: Business in forming technology is dominated by long-running, large-scale projects. The biggest customer grouping is the automotive industry, whose purchasing behaviour in this segment is driven substantially by strategic considerations.

Forming technology contributes between 27 and 30 per cent to total production output in the German machine tool industry. In 2014, production output in the forming technology sector was running at around 2.9 billion euros, 11 per cent down on the preceding year’s figure. This is primarily attributable to a very good performance in 2013, which was characterised by particular dynamism in the dominant press technology category.

In 2015, business performance is very much more subdued. In the year’s first half, bookings fell by 3 per cent, after still increasing over 2014 as a whole. Capacity utilisation in July, at 81.7 per cent, was running at almost 8 per cent below the figure for 2014, while the order backlog, at five months, was half a month down on the preceding year’s figure.

For the ongoing year, we are predicting an increase in production output for the machine tool industry as a whole, although our prognosis of +3 per cent is an ambitious one. Although in the forecast we do not distinguish between the various technologies involved, on the basis of the data cited it looks as if forming technology will this time be able to make only an under-proportional contribution. »

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities for your members?

WS: Our members are always faced with the challenge of upgrading their technical lead over their international competitors, since this is a key factor in their success. This means that research and development has to be driven forward, which also includes offering the right solutions for the disparate markets involved. High-tech is, in this context, not always fit for purpose.

In addition, they have to meet the requirements of globalisation, meaning an international presence in terms of sales, service support and even production. To this end, German mid-tier companies may in future have to cooperate even more closely. After all, it’s imperative to gain the best recruits for the sector. The VDW’s Youth Training Foundation offers meaningful assistance in this regard – together with the companies involved, it provides information on attractive career opportunities in the mechanical engineering sector and for mid-tier employers.

ISMR: What are the benefits of membership in the VDW? Can you list these?

WS: Members of the VDW first of all have to be a member of the VDMA (German Engineering Federation). As such, they can, of course, make use of the entire, highly differentiated spectrum of services offered by the VDMA. Membership of the VDW, in contrast, is an add-on, one that facilitates access to additional capabilities specific to the machine tool industry.

I can give you four examples below to illustrate the range of services provided by the VDW:

- Services: We offer a patent service, for example, which updates our members on the worldwide patent situation in production technology.

n Support: At many events, for example, we explain the effects of the EU’s new Machinery Directive on machine tool manufacturers. Or we prepare all globally available market data on the machine tool industry and its customers, to support our members’ market research.

n Marketing: We assist our members in cultivating the markets of their international customers. The VDW itself organises two important trade fairs for the sector, EMO Hannover and METAV in Düsseldorf, accompanies its members to key international machine tool trade fairs and, for more than ten years now, has been organising symposia abroad, which are reserved primarily for members.

n Innovation: Finally, the VDW’s Research Institute progresses innovations in the machine tool industry, by helping to define projects within the context of joint research work, and itself conducts major studies on technical questions from a specifically machine tool-related viewpoint. Examples include the development of electromobility, aircraft manufacturing or 3D printing. The results are available solely to members of the VDW.

ISMR: Do you liaise or network with any other international organisations?

WS: We cooperate with practically all the international machine tool trade associations in the countries that possess a significant industrial sector in our field. These include the big markets in the Triad, plus, of course, European partners, with whom we collaborate through CECIMO, the European Association of Machine Tool Industries.

Issues that customers are currently raising with us include electro-mobility, lightweight construction and new materials

We also maintain intensive contact with national and international trade associations of our most important customer groupings, chief among them the mechanical engineering sector and the automotive industry, plus of course with the German Chambers of Commerce abroad.

ISMR: Please can you update our readers on any special events that you will be holding in 2015/16?

WS: This year, there won’t be any more trade fairs organised by the VDW. However, EMO will be held in Milan during October 2015 – it is the world’s premier trade fair for the metalworking sector and a major event for the industry, with German manufacturers providing the largest number of exhibitors from abroad. The companies concerned are supported by us.

November will see the Blechexpo event in Stuttgart. It is an important event in forming technology, which is why the VDW is one of its conceptual sponsors.

Also in November, the VDW will be organising a technology symposium in Jakarta, Indonesia. It’s our first symposium in this market, which once started off so promisingly and then was very severely affected by the Asian crisis in the late 1990s. Currently, the market is stabilising again. With a population of almost 250 million, and the country’s important role in the planned ASEAN Economic Community (AEC), Indonesia has huge potential. At any rate, we are determined not to leave the market to our Japanese and other Asian competitors without putting up a fight.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
