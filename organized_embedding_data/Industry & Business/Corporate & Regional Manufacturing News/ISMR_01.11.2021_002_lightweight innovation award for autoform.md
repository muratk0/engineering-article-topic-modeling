Software specialist AutoForm has won this year's 'Leading Lightweight Enterprise Innovation Award' for enabling lightweighting of car bodies. Its digital process model and 'robust springback compensation methodology' helped to achieve a reduction of 14kg in weight and 42k tons of CO2 for one hood component alone in a recent project in China.

China has committed to reach carbon peak by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2060 and set ambitious targets for the development of electric vehicles (EVs) and fuel-efficient vehicles. The 13th Five-Year Plan promotes automotive lightweighting as one core technology under the electric mobility and intelligent manufacturing pillars of the "Made in China 2025" programme. A 10% reduction in vehicle weight provides a 6-8% improvement in fuel economy for gasoline vehicles and a 14% range extension for electric vehicles. Lightweight materials are expected to decrease vehicle weight by 20% in 2025 and by 35% in 2030.

"Car body engineering and manufacturing requires process excellence to deliver lightweight and high crash safety at low costs within evershorter development cycles. Aluminium offers 20-40% lightweight potential compared to mild steel and is therefore applied most widely. However, aluminium's limited formability, stronger springback behaviour and softer consistency make it difficult to reach robust production within geometric tolerance and high surface quality. Today, most Chinese automakers and tool suppliers require over ten tryout and quality loops for aluminium skin panels before reaching robust production. This wastes several months of ramp-up time and makes aluminium prohibitively expensive for wider application," explained AutoForm. AutoForm's robust springback compensation methodology delivers a robust manufacturing process for high geometric and surface quality. First, engineers build a digital process model in the AutoForm software to predict and solve issues in the virtual phase. A stochastic analysis considers variables, to identify and validate a robust process window. Second, AutoForm software provides guidance to the shop floor when manufacturing the physical tools and setting up the real-world production process according to its digital process twin.

"This enables us to reach an engineered robust process in the most systematic way to deliver quality parts 'right first time'. This methodology is one of the crucial building blocks to achieve industry 4.0 in stamping and body-in-white," it told ISMR.

The awarded application project was for PSA, which applied aluminium instead of mild steel for the inner and outer hood in its premium model DS X83, produced in China. This reduces the weight of this component by 14.21kg and generates an estimated 42k tons of CO2 savings over the lifecycle of this car. To capture this potential, tool supplier Push Mould from Chengdu applied AutoForm's methodology for these aluminium hood panels. Based on the digital process model in the AutoForm software, the engineering team could predict strong springback of 3-4mm and managed to compensate for it in the virtual phase. "As a result, this project delivered quality panels right in the first physical tryout loops that were inside geometric tolerance and with flawless surface quality," said AutoForm.

Twelve winners were selected in seven categories out of over 2,000 applications for this year's 7th IALTA China Green Auto Lightweight Technology Innovation Awards. The awards were handed over during the Global New Energy Vehicle Development and Lightweight Innovation Summit as part of the 13th International New Energy Automotive Technology and Lightweight Materials & Intelligent Equipment Exhibition 2021 in Shanghai in June 2021.

SAIC Volkswagen introduced its lightweight strategy for the latest EV models from the ID series at this summit. The ID EV series uses aluminium for the battery case and SAIC Volkswagen uses AutoForm software to ensure feasibility and quality of these lightweight components.
