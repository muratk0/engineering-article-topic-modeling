# TRUMPF focuses on electromobility and Industry 4.0

[TARİH: 01.08.2018 ISMR]

GERMAN SHEET METAL specialist, TRUMPF, sees major opportunities for its business and for German industry as a whole in the two megatrends of Industry 4.0 and e-mobility.

"The mechanical and systems engineering industry in Germany can reap big benefits from connectivity in industry and e-mobility, said Mathias Kammüller, Chief Digital Officer, TRUMPF, at the company's in-house trade show, INTECH, which hosted some 2,200 visitors from all over the world. "Two key areas are connected manufacturing, as the next stage of technology on the shop floor, and future mobility. These two factors will help safeguard jobs in development, manufacturing and application engineering – and even create new ones," he said.

Kammüller also noted that digitally connected lasers will play an essential role in the transition from vehicles powered by internal combustion engines to all-electric vehicles. Digitally connected lasers will also allow manufacturers to alternate, at the touch of a button, between the two types of vehicle on production lines.

TRUMPF has witnessed a surge in demand for manufacturing technologies in e-mobility. Battery manufacturing already makes up 10 percent of TRUMPF's turnover in the automotive sector, a figure that is continuing to climb. The company has already sold more than 500 lasers worldwide for use in battery manufacturing.

"Lasers really are the only option when it comes to mass-producing batteries, high-power electronic systems and electric drives," said Christian Schmitz, Chief Executive Officer Laser Technology, TRUMPF. Finding an affordable means of producing electric vehicles by the millions requires robust manufacturing methods that can be scaled up quickly from today's relatively low volumes to full-scale mass production

At the INTECH trade show, TRUMPF demonstrated a series of laser techniques that can weld all the seams on an electric motor in one minute. The hairpin method involves using a compressed-air pistol to fire a rectangular copper wire, similar to a hairpin, into a slot on the side of the motor. The protruding parts of the wire are then twisted together and welded using a laser

TRUMPF technology also plays a role in manufacturing batteries for electric vehicles. The batteries consist of multiple layers of wafer-thin copper and aluminium foils that are cut to size using lasers. After adding liquid electrolyte, the battery is welded shut with a cap. It is essential that the welds seal the cell completely, because battery failure during operation could pose a risk of fire and injury. From the battery cells and battery modules to the battery pack, all the welding is carried out using a laser.

## How laser technology is making electro mobility possible

"Our lasers for the automotive sector are a good example of how important digital transformation is to TRUMPF. To ensure our own production processes remain fit for the future, we are constantly expanding our in-house digitalisation efforts." said Mathias Kammüller. TRUMPF already has over 500 employees working on more than 30 digital transformation projects. "That's helping us to improve efficiency, cut costs and boost our competitiveness. Digitalisation is a crucially important factor particularly for our locations in Germany with their high labour costs," Kammüller noted.

During this fiscal year, the company opened a smart factory in Chicago as well as a connected logistics centre in Ditzingen near Stuttgart, Germany. The company's TruConnect solutions not only monitor laser systems, but also offer a means of boosting the productivity of sheet metal processing.

"We are basically digitalising the entire production process: from initial customer queries to issuing invoices, and from ordering raw materials to dispatching finished products. The activities carried out before and after manufacturing itself make up almost 80 percent of the process. Our solutions can help make drastic reductions to time and money spent," said Heinz-Jürgen Prokop, Chief Executive Officer Machine Tools.

The INTECH fair showcased the TruLaser Weld 5000, a new robotic welding cell that can process parts up to four metres long. The company also unveiled two new laser tube cutting machines: the TruLaser 5000 and the TruLaser Tube 7000 fiber, the latter of which can handle tubes with diameters up to 254mm. Cutting speeds are high, with its solid-state laser and RapidCut feature. The show also marked the world premiere of the new TruLaser Cell 5030 with a new disk laser.
