# Locker manufacturer, Locit, has significantly boosted its production efficiency after investing in digital design technology and hiring a new chief designer.

[TARİH: 01.10.2024 ISMR]

Locit, a UK designer and manufacturer of steel lockers and storage solutions, has doubled capacity and cut waste by 60% after investing in new digital design technology and skills. The company, based in Deeside (North Wales), has been able to achieve this by integrating a suite of software including Radan, SolidWorks and 3D AutoCAD to its advanced manufacturing processes.

To lead this transformation, Locit hired Radoslav Murgas as its chief designer. With a rich architectural design background and extensive sheet metal industry experience, he has played a pivotal role in integrating the latest design and manufacturing technologies.

Locit produces over 500 locker designs, including bespoke designs under the Pure Lockers brand, sold across the UK and Ireland. Its 64,000 square-foot digital manufacturing facility sits within Deeside Enterprise Zone, home to advanced manufacturing companies in the aerospace, automotive, food and pharmaceutical sectors. The company is a member of the 'Made in Britain' initiative, which supports and promotes British manufacturing and enables buyers and consumers to identify British-made products.

## Enhanced productivity

Adopting 3D design technology at Locit has dramatically improved production efficiency and reduced material waste. Transitioning from 2D to 3D drawings has brought greater clarity to the manufacturing processes and assembly, significantly cutting sheet metal waste.

"Nest efficiency refers to how well the material is used, and this has improved. Material usage rose to 80% per sheet thanks to better planning, adequate sheet sizes and exploration of machine limits. Optimised tool paths and part geometries have also halved the production time for large batches of smaller components, further boosting operational efficiency," Locit confirmed to ISMR.

These gains have reinforced Locit's commitment to lowering its carbon footprint. The company already holds PAS 2060 certification and is reportedly on course to reduce emissions by a further 50% this year.

## Meeting new challenges

Reflecting on his new role, Radoslav Murgas remarked: "Joining Locit has been an exciting journey. We have moved from basic 2D flat patterns to detailed 3D models and drawings using SolidWorks and AutoCAD. This shift has significantly improved the visualisation of our designs and streamlined operations on the shop floor, leading to enhanced overall efficiency.

"Our biggest challenge was ensuring that everything was correctly aligned and followed in the production process. We aim to streamline processes, eliminate bottlenecks and optimise resource utilisation. We can increase productivity and output by introducing clear, detailed drawings and easy-to-follow instructions without increasing resources or capital investment," he continued.

Locit's substantial investment in technology has positioned it as one of the UK's most advanced steel locker manufacturers. Implementing and integrating the software for creating part drawings and generating NC codes, combined with using AMADA punch machines for sheet metal fabrication, has revolutionised its production process.

## Innovation in focus

The improved tool paths and part designs have also made the cutting process faster. For example, if it used to take 45 minutes to produce a batch of 100 items, Locit confirmed that it now takes just 22 minutes (cutting production time by half).

Richard Williams, CEO of Locit, emphasised the importance of technology and design in driving the company's mission. “By integrating advanced software and design practices, we are creating a robust production system that enhances efficiency and aligns with our broader goals for sustainability and innovation in steel locker manufacturing," he said.

Locit's comprehensive approach enhances production efficiency through 3D models, detailed drawings and close collaboration with operators, reducing errors and reliance on memory.

Advanced digital manufacturing methods have enabled the company to be agile and fleet-footed in responding to demands for new, innovative products. One such innovative product under the Pure Lockers brand, is Phone Safe. It is a wall-mounted locker that features individual compartments for students' mobile phones. The solution, said Locit, has resonated with educators and administrators.

## A sustainable approach

Locit deploys a sustainable approach through its entire process, from supply chain and design to manufacture and delivery. It starts by using recycled steel sourced from UK-based suppliers only. 3D CAD/CAM software solutions enable the design of products with minimum waste materials.

Advanced manufacturing techniques are used to reduce energy consumption. This includes advanced sheet metal machinery for fully automated production runs, bespoke short-volume orders and R&D, and powder coating techniques that recycle waste and reuse heat between processes. Digital manufacturing means that production is integrated with cloud-based Material Requirements Planning (MRP) software to optimise stock and delivery management. Finished products are delivered to customers and suppliers using recycled packaging.

In 2023, Locit received PAS 2060 certification which deploys a holistic approach to reduce the environmental impact covering energy, supply chain, design, manufacture and packaging. Its PAS 2060 certification was independently audited by the Centre for Assessment (CfA). PAS 2060 is the internationally recognised specification for carbon neutrality published by the British Standards Institution (BSI). It demonstrates that carbon neutrality claims are both credible and verified to increase accountability and consumer confidence.

This was a significant accomplishment for the SME manufacturer, which booked almost £5m of sales in its first year's trading to the end of September and created 40 new jobs.

## Looking to the future

Moving forward, the company plans to integrate new design concepts and software to cut wastage further and improve efficiency. It will also establish a library of approved designs for consistent and reliable documentation.

The carbon-neutral steel locker manufacturer was on course to book more than £6.5m in sales in its second year of trading, ending September 2024, an increase of 40% over the previous year. This follows investment by Arete Capital Partners, a Liverpool- and Manchester-based private capital investor, in 2022.

"This accelerated sales performance has been achieved through a robust order pipeline which includes orders from various industries including schools; gyms; police; leisure and entertainment venues," confirmed the company.
