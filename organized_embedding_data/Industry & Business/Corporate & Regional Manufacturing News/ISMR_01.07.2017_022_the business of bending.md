As the family-owned business looked to continue its growth strategy, managing director Ross Taylor had two options; more of the same or diversify and expand the tube bending side of the business. Taking the second option has proved highly successful for the company, with turnover increasing by more than 55 per cent in the last financial year. Much of this growth has been attributed to the addition of two BLM ELECT all-electric tube benders.

"We are well known across our key industry sectors of automotive, rail, agricultural equipment and shopfitting for our tube laser cutting capabilities and we could have opted to expand further on this but, as laser-cut tube accounted for 70 per cent of our business, we wanted to opt for a different strategy. Tube bending, particularly larger diameter tube bending, provided an opportunity for us to become a single source option for a lot of our customers, providing laser and bending services under one roof," explained Ross Taylor.

## About Duright

Established in 1978, Duright Engineering is an accredited ISO9001:2008 company specialising in close tolerance tubular cutting. It can process a wide range of tube ranging from 3.5m up to 8.5m in length. It has developed a broad automotive customer base. Precision tube laser and saw cutting to +/-0.1mm is its speciality and a CMM measuring facility allows it to check close tolerances. See www.duright.co.uk

## FOCUS ON TUBE

## Focus on tube

Duright already operates four BLM tube laser machines 24 hours, six days per week. The latest of these is the ADIGE LT FIBER tube laser system. This combined laser capability allows Duright to process round tube of up to 154mm and square rectangular tube of up to 120mm section in lengths of up to 8.6 metres.

Duright was one of the first in the UK to adopt a fibre optic laser cutting system. As Ross Taylor told ISMR, it is very unusual for the tube sector to be leading the flat sheet sector but when it came to fibre laser, the tube industry was 'way ahead of the curve'.

"We had our first fibre laser from BLM four years ago and we cannot see any downside with fibre, especially on the mix of materials that we are cutting," he added.

With the success of the company's tube laser business it would have been an 'easy option' to further expand this and bring in more of the same, but the greater potential for business growth came from tube bending. Here again, Duright could have simply expanded on what it already had but, on reviewing the market, it spotted the chance to develop opportunities in larger diameter tube bending. The decision, therefore, was taken to invest in two ELECT, all-electric, multi-stack tube benders from BLM.

The first to be installed was an ELECT 102 machine with the capacity to bend tube of up to a diameter of 102mm with a wall thickness of up to 2.5mm. This was quickly followed by an ELECT 130 which can bend material of up to 130mm diameter and 3mm wall thickness. There are many advantages to an all-electric system, such as the elimination of hydraulics, lower power requirement, improved machine setting and component quality.

"Competition is tough in the small diameter tube bending sector, and chasing that volume work wasn't of interest to us," said Ross Taylor. "However, there are few companies that have invested in larger capacity tube bending and we could see the benefits of going after what may be smaller volume work that would provide better margins. We have reduced set-up times by between 50 and 70 per cent compared to those with our old hydraulic machines, but the biggest advantage for us is in getting the first-off part right. This is a straightforward process, compared to previous ones, as the set-up process is almost totally automated."

The efficiency of the ELECT machine is highlighted on smaller batch sizes

This automation of the set-up process is particularly important as Duright recognised the variables that operators can bring to the process. Previously, around 10-20 parts may have had to be produced before consistent production was achieved. Now, said the company, the second part off the machine will be exactly to drawing specifications. The savings in material and time are significant. This speed and consistency of set-up fits well with Duright's 'just-in-time' production philosophy and, with batch sizes varying from 20-off to 1000-off, efficiency in machine set-up is also a valuable benefit.

A further benefit is that there is no need for hydraulic systems in the bending process.

This takes away the risk of oil leaks and the need for a pump to be constantly running, which in turn leads to a better and safer working environment. Electric operation also gives greater control over the bending process, delivering the right torque for different material types. The result, said Duright, is consistent quality of bends.

Another significant factor is the machine control and software system from BLM, which provides seamless crossover between programs for the laser tube cutting machines and the ELECT tube benders. The control also provides detailed feedback to Duright, which the company finds extremely valuable as the information can be used to further develop bending strategies and create a library of information to enhance productivity and performance on any future jobs, requiring similar bending parameters. The control software also plays its part in winning business for Duright.

"Speed is key when it comes to quoting, but those quotations need to be accurate. To achieve speed and accuracy, we invested in a multi-user license for the BLM software and we gave all of our salesmen training in its application. So now, when a customer sends us a CAD 3D model (stp or igs), our salesmen can upload it to the software, which then analyses every option to bend that tube whether its multi-stack, one centreline radius, multiple centreline radii or variable radius bending," explained the company.

"The software confirms if the bend is possible and outputs a reliable cycle time, which leads to the rapid production of an accurate quotation. Using the system in this way eliminates the time that we used to need to get the right people together to discuss the part as well as removing any guesswork from the quoting process," concluded the company.

## Surveying its customers

"Before the development of our Strategy 2020, we interviewed our customers to assess demand. The establishment of our own materials laboratory, where we can characterise almost any material (including composites and bonded materials), is the logical result of this," explained Anne-Claire Höppner, Head of the Test & Validation department and Director of the Centre of Competence (CoC) Material Testing & Simulation at ARRK Engineering.

By covering a multitude of project environments over the past few decades – primarily in automotive material development – the company has succeeded in building up substantial technical expertise in materials. ARRK Engineering specialists can access a comprehensive body of expertise in the use of machines and a broad-based knowledge of the material characteristics of elastomers, thermoplastics, fibre-reinforced composites, adhesives, surfaces and metals. The ability to conduct its own tests in an in-house laboratory means that it can now intensify cooperation with a wide range of customers.

## High-speed testing machine

To determine the characteristics of both metals and plastics, the company's existing machine pool has now been extended. The services offered include – along with sample production and standard-compliant preparation – the performance of mechanical material tests including quasi-static and dynamic characterisation.

"We record parameters such as anisotropic stresses, compressive and tensile strength, maximum shear strain or notched impact strength," Höppner reported. "To extend the dynamic characterisation process to composite materials, we will have access to a Zwick HTM 16020 high-speed testing machine from the beginning of 2017."

The thermochemical tests allow developers to determine the degree of cure and fibre volume or filler content. In addition, ARRK Engineering offers environmental simulations and analysis of separable and inseparable compounds, with a special focus on adhesives, which have undergone little research to date. This enables parameters such as heat expansion, viscosity and gel point to be described, along with glass transition and crystallisation temperatures.

## Predictive material cards for structural and crash calculations

"Our service portfolio covers all tests which are of particular relevance to the automotive industry," summarised Roman Sternagel, leader of the Materials Testing team. "The next step is to process the information we have gathered for use in simulations so that we can offer our customers an all-round solution."

To this end, material characteristics are incorporated into computer models using an optimised process to generate predictive material cards. These are required during component development e.g. for structural or crash calculations.

"Translating the information into such a card is complex, because you first have to establish which software and which material model will give you a realistic picture of the material's behaviour," Sternagel added.

The long-term aim is to create a database in which the various materials are characterised as fully as possible. Selected materials are already being tested to provide a basis for this.
