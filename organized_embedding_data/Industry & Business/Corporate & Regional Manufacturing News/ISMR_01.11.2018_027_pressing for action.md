# PRESSING FOR ACTION

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

Simpac burst into the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover this October with two new press series, the MX and SX servo presses. They are the result of the new German-Korean venture, launched in 2017 with the set-up of Simpac Europe GmbH. At its Bodnegg site, in southern Germany, the team (headed by managing director, Bernd Moritz) mainly deals with the development, construction, sales and marketing of presses for the metalworking industry in Europe. It has optimised and developed Korean press technology for international markets, in addition to providing direct support for European customers.

Founded in 1973 in Seoul, press manufacturer Simpac Inc. is now part of the Simpac Group which, under the umbrella of Simpac Holding, unites companies for the manufacturing of ferroalloys, industrial machinery and foundries. Today, the Press Division holds more than 50% market share in the South Korean domestic market. Since 2004, the company has pursued a consistent internationalisation strategy and has already established eight branches in Asia, Europe, USA and México.

ISMR met Bernd Moritz, who is also CTO of Simpac in Korea, and Andreas Kinzyk, Executive Director, Overseas Sales Division, Simpac Europe GmbH, at EuroBLECH to discover how Simpac is gearing its activity towards European markets.

## A focus on innovation

"Simpac is a South Korean company, founded in 1973. It not only makes mechanical and hydraulic presses but is also involved in steel manufacturing and the production of metal alloys. So, we take care of the entire press production process (from raw materials to cutting and welding for the press frames, the drive trains and gear cutting etc.). Simpac also has a separate business for industrial machinery. The entire group has around 650 employees and a 480 million-euro turnover. It is the number-one press manufacturer in South Korea," Andreas Kinzyk told ISMR.

"Over the last ten years, it has migrated to markets outside Korea – firstly in Asian markets (such as Thailand, Indonesia, China, India) and then into Turkey, partly Brazil, South Africa and lately North America including Mexico and East Europe. The set-up of SIMPAC Europe, one year ago, means that we can now serve the European market (including Germany). R&D on next-generation presses is headed by Mr Moritz and we are busy building up our sales and service network in Europe. Our number one goal is to be competitive in Europe, so EuroBLECH is a great platform for us to promote ourselves and launch our new press lines."

Simpac Europe's core task is not only direct market support, but also the development and expansion of a comprehensive service network for Simpac customers from various branches in the metalforming industry. This includes car manufacturers and their suppliers, manufacturers of household appliances and household goods, companies in the furniture and electronics industry as well as manufacturers in the aerospace industry.

"Our new MX and SX servo presses, which are completely manufactured in the Group's South Korean plants, benefit not only from high added value within the Group, but also from continuous improvements through insights from global markets," explained Bernd Moritz. "They are designed for highly dynamic processes. The very sturdy, stress-relieved annealed press bodies are FEM-calculated throughout and are additionally optimised by individual hot-spot analyses in the areas subject to higher loads. The entire drive concept has been revised in terms of efficiency and effectiveness. Motor, brake, lubrication and power electronics have been replaced by German brands. These offer the highest dynamic properties and a long service life."

With a press force of 3,000 to 10,000kN, the automatic punching and forming presses in the MX series are particularly suitable for processing small and medium-sized components. The standardised press concept enables application as a stand-alone press in progressive or transfer operation as well as installation as an interlinked line. The SX series covers all larger components with a press force of 8,000 to 30,000kN and features motors in three different performance classes, which are individually adapted to the customer's requirements.

"The user-programmability of the slide movement, something which is characteristic of servo presses, has been combined with a dynamic drive concept leading to high output. The integrated curve generator makes it easy to create a slide movement curve that is adapted to the individual requirements of the die. In addition, three different slide movement curves have been pre-programmed and are available to the operator, either in full stroke or pendulum stroke, and cover all common forming operations. An integrated simulation program also enables the output performance to be precisely predicted for a specific component, which increases planning reliability in production and when dealing with end-users," added Bernd Moritz.

Simpac's main R&D focus has been on servo technology. It has developed its own software and simulation competencies to calculate the speed and efficiency of servomotors and to optimise the systems.

"We didn't just want to simply add a standard Siemens drive package to our products, without our own expertise. We worked on the complete mechatronic system (simulation tool, mechanics, gear train, drive train, servo-motor, electronics etc.) to optimise our presses with regard to highest efficiency. Therefore, we created an effective simulation tool and have developed our new MX and SX press lines based on this," concluded Bernd Moritz.

## Adapting to markets

"We have sold presses in eastern Europe (e.g. the Czech market) and the Turkish market was very strong for us this year. We are trying for orders in Germany too. We have to find a healthy balance between the traditional Korean OEM customer base and new suppliers/customers in Europe. We have also picked up some interesting project work for our new higher tonnage SX series presses. We are doing one now with a Tier One customer," commented Andreas Kinzyk.

Most of the automation on its presses originated from long-established Korean automation suppliers but Simpac Europe GmbH is keeping its automation options open for local European suppliers. Alongside the development of the new press lines, there has also been a focus on standardisation. It is looking for suppliers and automation partners on project works/lines to standardise production and controls. This is a departure from Simpac's traditional role of making customised products. It is also keeping a close eye on Industry 4.0 developments, from a market requirements' perspective.

Mr. Bernd Moritz, Chief Technology Officer, Simpac Inc. and Managing Director, Simpac Europe

"We believe that Industry 4.0 will be driven primarily by the larger OEMs with the larger suppliers. You must look at what is practical for the customers, especially for smaller customers. Our objective is always to ask customers what they need and that drives our development goals. Customer feedback tells us that the picture varies. For example an OEM will tell us that it needs high-level everything with global connectivity. Tier 1 and Tier 2 suppliers have the same requirements. However, Tier 3 companies have different priorities. They are interested in Industry 4.0 but they are also more focused on stable production to establish high OEE values. They need reliable feedback production data but they don't necessarily need global connectivity," Bernd Moritz told ISMR.

## Setting new targets

"Europe and Germany have been strong markets since 2010. There will be an inevitable cool-down phase - the question is when, and to what extent will it be driven by geopolitics and movements in global economies. The same will happen in North America and we have high hopes that the Asian market will pick up again," commented Andreas Kinzyk.

Bernd Moritz agrees, pointing out the different factors and trends that can influence market developments.

In 2017, Simpac Europe GmbH was founded in Germany, focusing on sales, marketing and research and development.

"The diesel factor has had some impact on certain customers in the automotive sector. Market factors, including politics, also has influence. In the automotive sector, which is key for us, there are also trends in material usage i.e. lightweight materials such as safety structural parts for press hardening applications. A third generation of advanced high-strength steels are being launched and presses must be equipped and designed to deal with them. For the older presses, there will be more chance for replacement. There are, of course, always cycles of innovations and OEE continues to be important for customers," he told ISMR.

SIMPAC Europe has employed a realistic set of targets and goals - as a small agile team, it needs to choose its projects carefully. As well as launching new press models, it is also streamlining existing products against new ones. This avoids the need to do continuous development work on products that may no longer be required by the market.

"Developing the new line of MX/SX presses was a major initiative for us in 2018. However, there is also a need to upgrade other products too, such as tandem lines. We have built a state-of-the-art control based on the new Siemens PLC system for our new press series and the next step in 2019 is to implement this across our entire press range (as our product range includes mechanical presses and hydraulic presses). In addition, we are also driven by internal goals in 2019 e.g. the implementation of EPlan P8 as the leading software for electronic drawings. We have also developed a new style of hydraulic presses, based on a servo hydraulic system, specially for press hardening applications. We are trying to sell this to Tier 1 and Tier 2 customers," Andreas Kinzyk told ISMR.

"Our ultimate goal is to combine high-level engineering with low production cost. We aim to offer high quality at an affordable cost and we definitely have all necessary preconditions to achieve this," concluded Bernd Moritz.
