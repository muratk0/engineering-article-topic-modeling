# TOMORROW'S WORLD TODAY

[TARİH: 01.10.2022 ISMR]

CIDAN Machinery Group focuses strongly on R&D, technology development and continuous innovation. Its CEO and sales director outline its latest acquisitions, key strategic objectives and market aspirations to ISMR.

ISMR SAYS:

“Automated lines that include programming, folding and slitting will be showcased on CIDAN's stand at the EuroBLECH 2022 exhibition in Germany from 25-28 October 2022."

CIDAN's headquarters are in Götene, Sweden, picturesquely situated between Europe's third and sixth largest lakes – Vänern and Vättern. Today, the CIDAN Machinery Group is a full-range supplier of sheet-metal machinery and control solutions for multiple industries.

Its acquisition in March 2022 of Swiss folding specialist, Thalmann Maschinenbau AG, has added another company to the Group that includes CIDAN Machinery Austria GmbH (formerly coil-processing line specialist, Forstner Maschinenbau GmbH) and nuIT GmbH (which manufactures control software). CIDAN itself (formerly Göteneds) specialises in sheet-metal folders, shearing systems and rollformers.

The Group has a total of 300 global employees, with around 100 of those based at its headquarters in Götene, Sweden. It operates manufacturing locations in Sweden, Switzerland and Austria, with office locations in several European countries, the United States and China. With a brand message of "Shaping Tomorrow's World", a strong focus is placed on R&D, technology development and continuous innovation.

“We offer individual and future-oriented machine and software solutions, for every size of company and every need, from a single source," it told ISMR.

## Integrating new expertise

The integration of Thalmann Maschinenbau AG in March 2022 added another strong brand to the Group's portfolio. Thalmann has been a pioneer in the development and manufacture of long-length, metal folding machines since 1960. CIDAN's strategy is to become a "one-stop partner" for sheet-metal companies of all sizes.

“Our strategy is based on delivering innovations that customers want, providing impeccable service and supplying top quality products. The acquisition of Thalmann fits perfectly into this strategy. CIDAN and Thalmann already share a great history together. Over a period of many years, both companies have established a number one position for long folders in the Americas. With Thalmann now part of the CIDAN group, I am convinced that CIDAN will make Thalmann stronger and Thalmann will make CIDAN stronger. We look forward growing CIDAN's business and the business of our customers," said Petter Hjelmqvist, CEO of CIDAN Machinery Group.

The Swiss company has a strong track record of bringing innovations to the market that are designed to offer significant efficiency improvements, cost reductions and higher precision/ flexibility of the folding process. Thalmann's base in Frauenfeld, Switzerland, will remain a hub for longfolder innovation and production. Its 50 employees have now joined CIDAN Machinery Group and remain employed at Frauenfeld.

"I am so happy about this acquisition. The onboarding process for the Thalmann team and its product range has gone very well. Everyone in the Group is so enthusiastic about having Thalmann on board. It makes the Group even more innovative. We like to be a partner to our customers, providing help across all areas (not just on folding, cutting or slitting etc). Rather than pushing one type of product, we offer the best combination of technologies to fit their needs," added Petter Hjelmqvist.

## Automation in focus

Together CIDAN, Thalmann, Forstner and nuIT will also be stronger in automation. The Group has already developed a flexible, fully automated line for sheet-metal trims where nuIT software is 'the brain' designed to simplify and rationalise metal trim production.

An automated line, incorporating a Thalmann double folder, will be showcased on CIDAN's stand at the EuroBLECH 2022 exhibition in Hanover, Germany (25-28 October 2022). The Group will showcase modern and automated sheet metal processing and sheet-metal profile production with a line-up of folders, coil processing (cut-to-length equipment) and automation software.

"The Thalmann TD double folder (fully automated) features sideloading, flipping and sheet unloading devices designed to save time and money (especially personnel costs), because the entire system can be operated by just one employee," commented Raphael Büchel, Sales Director, CIDAN Machinery Group.

"The automated line at EuroBLECH will also feature the CIDAN FORMA Z folder (with unique geometry and nuLINK bending software); new Evolution IT production management software and an automatic slitting line from Forstner (where users can switch between six different coil types). We will demonstrate the entire process from beginning to end on the line at EuroBLECH i.e. programming; cutting; slitting and folding up to the final profile," he confirmed.

CIDAN's FORMA Z Up/Down folder, for mid-range and large batchwork, now boasts a 30% improvement in speed. The design of the bending cheek enables upward and downward bending. Rotation of the upper beam between two tool sets and its geometry mean a larger workspace for operators. It also features a newly developed bending rail design with automatic crowning, in combination with automatic adjustment of the bending centre and sheet thickness. Tool locking is automatic to facilitate handling and increase safety. The stop can be automatically adjusted up and down for greater flexibility. Non-parallel products can be bent by using the bending rail as a stop.

The combination of CIDAN, Forstner, nuLINK and Thalmann in one group has enabled CIDAN to further integrate a complete sheet-metal production line. Industry 4.0 and digitalisation, combined with the in-house development of integrated software solutions, offers benefits such as the optimisation of supply chain processes and lean manufacturing, as well as opportunities for e-commerce solutions.

The one-stop-shop concept provides a complete production line and service offer from coil to folded sheet metal, including long folding. CIDAN has embraced the digitalisation process by offering customers an online 'webshop' for customers to use. On a tablet or smartphone, they can perform a range of operations such as analysing machine performance data, obtain pricing or sending out a quote to their customers.

"Our software makes all the calculations and takes care of all the order details (including price, order confirmations, emails to customers etc.)," Raphael Büchel told ISMR. "It is all geared towards reducing our customers' workloads and their need for extra labour, with less risk of error. This full integration ensures a safe and efficient way of working."

"Automation, efficiency and software development are key for us going forward. There are shortages of skilled labour in the market, so automation is crucial. Automation and digitalisation is also about utilising material in a more efficient way and saving on material costs/scrap, reducing labour/energy costs and increasing efficiencies. That's what our customers want," he added.

## Successes and innovations

A recent success for CIDAN, shortly after the Thalmann acquisition, was the award of a 1.2-million-euro order from a customer in Iceland for "every type of branded machinery across the Group's portfolio". This included double-folder technology, IT software and coilprocessing capability.

“It is very similar to the line that we will display at EuroBLECH," confirmed Büchel. "The customer wanted a streamlined production flow, with all our latest machines connected via nuLINK software."

Despite the ravages of the COVID-19 pandemic, growth has been strong across the Group over the past five years.

“Our turnover last year was over 80 million euros. We expect a further 20% growth this year across Europe and the Americas. Around 60% of our business is in the Americas, with 35% in Europe and the rest split between other regions. The European market is more affected by the war in Ukraine, the energy crisis and material shortages than the U.S. market. We have a strong presence in Europe and are even stronger in the United States," Petter Hjelmqvist told ISMR.

"We launched ten major new product developments this year. We are investing hugely in product development. Over 12% of our workforce is working on this. More than half of the 37 people in our product development teams are dedicated to software development. NULINK will be a platform for further software development and future digitalisation initiatives in the group. We aim to launch new patented technology in the next few weeks for bending centre adjustments on folders (automated adjustment of the bending beam)," he continued.

He is also hiring more people in aftermarket sales and service which he sees as an important area for long-term success. An ambitious turnover target of one billion euros has been set for 2023.

“We have 55 people in aftermarket service around the globe and are looking for a further ten people. Having good service technicians on board is crucial for good service. Training and product development are also key pillars for us in terms of strategic objectives. We are very much focused on helping our customers to grow and be successful," explained Hjelmqvist.

The COVID-19 pandemic challenged the Group to find new ways to communicate internally and with its customers, as well as to be more efficient. The pandemic's effect on worldwide supply chains has also thrown up challenges such as material shortages, increased prices and limited availability.

"The diversity of companies, experience, expertise and cultures in our Group has cushioned us over this period. We support each other well and have all grown and learned new skills throughout the COVID-19 crisis. It is a tough time right now, but this combination of different companies and brands has helped us to stay successful over the last couple of years. Our customers are still ordering from us and the construction industry in Austria is still strong, with lots of ongoing projects. The sheet-metal business is still healthy while automation/process optimisation offers new opportunities to compensate for material cost increases," added Raphael Büchel.

## An eye on opportunities

The Group opened a new branch in the UK this year. It also opened its own branch, and hired new people, in Germany to be closer to its customers there. Across Europe, it combines direct sales activities with strong local partners for sales and service.

“It is important to have local support for our products. We continue to investigate countries that fit our portfolio to decide whether direct sales or a local partnership approach with a dealer is more appropriate. We work very closely with our dealers, as true partners, to build our international presence," Büchel highlighted.

"Our aim is to be a global player, attract great people to work for us and continuously grow with good management of cash and revenues. Our main market is the architectural roofing sector (with full automated lines/ software). There is a lot of potential to further exploit this market segment in Europe and the U.S.," added Petter Hjelmqvist.

The Group has also moved its folder technology further into traditional press-brake markets and is currently exploring more industrial markets (with partners).

"We have increased sales into more industrial markets over the last few years. A large portion of our growth has been in coilprocessing markets in the U.S. and Europe. Customers now realise that using sheet-metal blanks in a line is just not efficient in terms of the amount of scrap produced. Integration with a coil line is much more efficient; scrap and downtime are drastically reduced and the logistics are much easier (coils take up less space). Return on investment for these lines is possible within a year. A fully automated 300m-long coil line also needs no operator. This segment of the sheet-metal market is growing, particularly as material costs are rising so much," confirmed Raphael Büchel.

Software underpins this planned expansion into new markets. The Group has invested heavily in making its software products simple and easy to use for operators.

“Our software is designed to simplify the workload of a programmer or operator. Whether they are using smartphones, tablets or desktops, the software must be responsive, easy to understand and easy to operate. The challenge is user friendliness so that users can take advantage of the software's many capabilities," added Büchel.

## One-stop partner principle

CIDAN Machinery Group's one-stop partner principle encompasses sheet-metal customers of all sizes, from large and automated OEMs to small job-shops and individual roofers. Flexibility and innovation drive this approach.

"Start-ups cannot always afford fully automated lines and we aim to be a partner to all our customers. This includes start-up roofers and smaller companies. Our aim is to grow with them as a one-stop partner. Even if someone just wants a refurbished machine, we are happy to supply this. This flexibility is very important and, in partnership with these industry newcomers, we can nurture our next generation of customers," concluded Raphael Büchel.

The Group has its sights firmly set on the future, embracing the digitalisation chain and unlocking new partnerships for growth.
