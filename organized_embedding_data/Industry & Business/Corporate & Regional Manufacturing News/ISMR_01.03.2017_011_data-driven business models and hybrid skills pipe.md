# Data-driven business models and hybrid skills pipeline

[TARİH: 01.03.2017 ISMR]

A RECENT SURVEY by CECIMO (the European Association of Machine Tool Industries) showed that data-driven business models are high on the agenda of European machine tool builders. Building a hybrid skills pipeline, that merges ICT and production technologies, is also seen as an important priority. Greater certainty on the right to access different types of data generated by machines would be a necessity for machine tool builders to tap into new business opportunities.

The CECIMO General Assembly, that took place in Rome on 26 November 2016, confirmed that:

- European machine tool production grew slightly to a level above 24.2 billion euros in 2016.
- Exports will remain stable in 2016 at 18.7 billion euros.
- European machine tool production's global market share is growing and exceeded 40% in 2016.

During its General Assembly Meetings, CECIMO conducted a survey with CEOs and owners of European machine tool companies. For most of the companies that participated in the survey, data-driven business models will be the most prioritised over the next ten years (other business models include services-based on in-house knowledge, product-as-a-service and platform-based).

Half of the machine tool builders questioned said that launching new education programmes in Europe, that merge ICT and production technologies, is the most important policy pillar to underpin new business models, followed by future-proof and technology neutral regulations. The majority of the machine tool builders find that understanding customers' digital needs and cooperating with them are essential to developing new business models.

"Machine tool builders are increasingly facing difficulty in finding the workforce with the knowledge and skills needed to apply digital solutions in advanced manufacturing. To tackle this pressing challenge, public authorities at national and regional levels, as well as education providers together with the industry, should invest more in the design and delivery of a new education approach merging various disciplines including software programming and production technologies," stated Filip Geerts, CECIMO Director General.

"Secondly, we see that Member States are launching and implementing ambitious investment programmes for the modernisation of manufacturing and service-based business models. Nevertheless, the EU has an important role to play in the coordination and acceleration of such investment programmes. We can only cope with the competitive pressures arising from across the world by pooling our resources in Europe and coordinating our actions," he added.

According to CECIMO, in spite of the global economy's weakening growth and high levels of uncertainty in international trade relations, the European machine tool industry shows clear signs of strength. Machine tool production in Europe grew its global market share to exceed 40% in 2016.

"Investment in modern manufacturing equipment remains relatively low, but the recovery of the economic activity is visible and the business confidence in Europe may support additional growth in investment. All economic indicators from the destination markets point to a stable growth momentum," said CECIMO.

"Global machine tool production will decrease slightly (2%-3%) from 61.5 billion euros in 2015 to 60 billion euros in 2016. Brazil and China are reporting negative MT consumption and production with negative double digit figures. We estimate that the European machine tool industry will defeat this trend and confirm the good results of 2015 by again exporting an amount worth 18.7 billion euros. Exports to Asia are declining in line with local economic developments. Exports to Russia are heavily impacted (-30% y-o-y) by the EU trade sanctions. Exports to the Americas are increasing due to investment into new or upgraded automotive plants in the U.S. and Mexico. Domestic European consumption is supported by a resilient investment climate in Europe with ongoing investments into more efficient production equipment."
