# MEETING MARKET CHALLENGES

[TARİH: 01.10.2024 ISMR]

ISMR sat down with Voortman's Head of Commerce, Rutger Voortman, to discover how Voortman's philosophy, investments and scope of operations have evolved to fit changing market and customer requirements.

Voortman Machinery has been a leading manufacturer of CNC steel processing machinery and software solutions. It creates high-end steel processing machines with integrated business software and local service that automates and optimises production processes. With a wide range of products for beam, plate and pipe processing as well as automated welding, Voortman has installed numerous systems worldwide through a network of international subsidiaries.

Left: Rutger Voortman, Head of Commerce, Voortman.

Machine operations include drilling; welding; sawing; cutting; marking and milling.

The company has grown significantly in recent years to around 700 employees worldwide and invested in its business, establishing new bases in Germany and France. To offer employees an inspiring working environment and receive visitors properly, the Dutch machine builder recently created an ultra-modern campus in Rijssen, The Netherlands. In addition to the long-established Voortman Experience Centre, a new Voortman Engineering Centre was opened last year.

ISMR sat down with Voortman's Head of Commerce, Rutger Voortman, to discover how Voortman's philosophy, investments and scope of operations have evolved to fit changing market and customer requirements.

## Innovate to accumulate

Voortman focuses on six key market sectors for its machinery, software and services. These are steel/metal fabrication; plate processing; steel distribution; machinery and equipment; offshore/energy and process equipment. Forging strong relationships with customers has always been a key focus for the company.

“We identify common customer needs and invest and develop in a targeted way to fulfil as many of those needs as possible," Rutger Voortman told ISMR. "In doing so, we aim for the long term. For example, where is the market going? Are bolted structures becoming increasingly important with an eye on circularity and sustainability? We translate these findings into business cases, which we then develop."

In the steel/metal fabrication industry, finding skilled labour is increasingly challenging. Therefore, to remain competitive and ensure long-term success, it is crucial to prioritise automation, maximize output and maintain flexibility. Rutger Voortman highlighted automation as one of the common needs in European markets and "the need to be independent of people's knowledge and skills." "We want to minimise any operation where human interaction is required. That means high levels of automation in steel processing. We have invested very heavily in that philosophy," he explained.

“In the steel fabrication market, it is all about costs and being in control of your margin and your entire production process in an environment of labour shortages. So, we help our customers by offering a high-output, optimal production process with embedded control software that can help them to predict their costs and increase their productivity," he elaborated. In today's rapidly evolving plate processing market, positioning as a comprehensive one-stop shop for customers has become more important than ever.

The offshore and energy market has been a growing market for the company. Its customers are processing more high-strength steels, thicker materials and larger pipe sizes. For pressure vessel manufacturing and piping, manufacturers are primarily concerned about the quality of welding and cutting operations. Voortman's solutions for these industries include profile, pipe and plate processing systems as well as automated welding, supported by software.

"Our philosophy is to do as much as we can in-house i.e. R&D; design; production; software; installation and servicing. Our vision is to deliver software, machine and service solutions to customers worldwide," explained Rutger Voortman.

## Widening its scope

The Dutch machine builder is going through a transition, focusing not only on its machines but also on the development of its own software and the continuous expansion of its service activities.

"We aim to ensure that our customers' machines are performing at their maximum throughout their lifecycle and processes are automated. We know the different markets that we serve, understand our customers' needs and can respond to them in a targeted way. Our focus is on developing and building fast, sturdy and stable machines," said Rutger Voortman.

"However, that is no longer enough. We couple that with software and service to serve our customers throughout the lifecycle of the machines. For example, the facilitation of CAD/nesting software, which greatly relieves work preparation, is already linked to our machines. We have also developed EVI, our own dashboarding software, to closely track the performance of our machines," he continued.

But that is just the beginning, Rutger Voortman told ISMR. The company is working with around 75 developers in England to further expand its control software and develop process software specifically for the industries that it serves under the name 'DIGI-STEEL'.

In the last few years, Voortman has also made significant investments in customer support by setting up local service hubs. It has been hiring service technicians around the world to respond quickly to any customer queries.

"There are many facets of the services that we offer to customers. This includes, for example, supporting the customers in maintenance and repair on machines. We offer service from local service hubs so that we can reach our customers quickly and minimize downtime on machines. But our service also extends to parameter development and tooling sales, including through web shops. Service is a high priority for us," highlighted Rutger Voortman.

## Accessing new markets

Voortman's three strategic pillars are automation, output optimisation and customer service. It is important to Voortman to offer customers a complete portfolio of products.

This is one of the reasons that it acquired Müller Opladen, a German manufacturer of pipe processing machines, in early 2023. This expanded Voortman's product portfolio to include pipe cutting machines for steel pipes, pressure vessels and offshore jackets.

Voortman also acquired Müller Opladen to widen its technology portfolio and access new markets.

“The main reason for this acquisition was synergy. We already offered machines for processing plate and beam; the round profile processing operation was missing. That was because in steel construction (the market where our base lies), round profile processing is less frequent. However, things are different in other markets where we now focus and want to grow. In these markets, they are processing more and more round profiles," explained Rutger Voortman.

Voortman's V807 robotic profile processing machine already featured a round pipe processing option. The plate cutters already offered a pipe addition. A stand-alone pipe cutting system was therefore a logical next step. However, its development would take time and Voortman was not well known in that field. The decision was therefore made to acquire Müller Opladen which, at a stroke, gave Voortman three models for pipe cutting: the entry-level MO compact Series for complex pipe cutting, the MO Classic Series pipe cutting machine and the MO Heavy-Duty series for large-diameter pipe, pressure vessels and heat exchangers.

In addition, this gave Voortman access to a production facility with an engineering hub in Germany, allowing it to recruit employees in Germany and take advantage of the supply chains there. This strengthened Voortman's position in Europe.

“The integration of our two companies is going well, both technologically and in other areas. There is now one sales organisation, and the marketing and service organisations have also merged and are working according to the same principles and with the same resources. Voortman's organisation is flat, enabling quick choices and flexible operations," confirmed Rutger Voortman.

## New technology launches

At the EuroBLECH sheet metal trade show in Hanover, Germany (22-25 October 2024), Voortman will be displaying four machines as well as its software offerings.

"We will be launching our new V353 fibre laser cutting system at EuroBLECH. The new fibre laser cutting system, which will make its European debut at EuroBLECH, is suitable for steel suppliers and the plate processing industry. With a bed of up to 36m long, it processes plate of up to 3m wide and 40mm thick. The V353 operates autonomously, even overnight. It automatically changes nozzles and adjusts to each plate thickness. The 3D bevelling unit creates complete parts in one step, saving time. The low bed makes loading and unloading easy, even during processing, to speed up workflow," outlined Rutger Voortman.

"Visitors to our booth will also see our V807 robotic coping machine as well as the Voortman Fabricator for automated welding and the MO classic pipe-cutting machine," he added.

In June this year, the company launched its Voortman V623 steel drilling, milling and sawing machine designed for continuous, unmanned operation.

"The V623 can handle a very wide range of profiles, producing them efficiently with a logical output sequence that maximizes cross-transport use. The innovative gripper truck virtually eliminates clamping limitations, allowing for the automatic loading of almost any profile without the need for operator intervention or weld-ons," explained Rutger Voortman.

"Advanced VACAM control software provides full visibility of all buffers on the infeed, outfeed and short product removal, enabling seamless management of the entire process," he added, confirming that the company has already installed several of these machines in Europe. Voortman also uses the machine in its own manufacturing operations.

## Moving forward

As well as launching new machines, Voortman is continuously pushing forward on the technical and service sides.

"We want to keep growing our service and after-sales network worldwide to better serve our customers. We must always create value for our end customers through our solutions and software. We will further develop our software and automation solutions for machines like 'The Fabricator', our automated fitting/welding robot. We will also continue developing the new V353 fibre laser machine with, for example, other modules and higher power laser sources," confirmed Rutger Voortman.

The company is also working on new service products such as optimising its web shops and tool vendor systems. However, underpinning each new machine that it develops are the software and Big Data systems that support it.

"We have moved on from just selling machines now," outlined Rutger Voortman. "Industry 4.0 is about understanding and modelling a full line from A-Z, including supply chain, not just offering a single machine. This enables customers to streamline their entire production process. It's about the intelligence in and around the machine, the production flow, service, machine uptimes – the entire holistic process involved. That includes analysing and applying learnings from gathered machine data," he told ISMR.

The company will be exhibiting at FABTECH (Orlando, USA) and EuroBLECH (Hanover, Germany) this October. It will also be at MAKTEK Eurasia in Turkey, MSV BRNO in Czechia and the Tech Industry exhibition in Latvia later this year.

"We are also running an internal Open House event in the DACH region at the end of November. We already offer training, technology and educational webinars to customers through our website and will be releasing more of these webinars next year," concluded Rutger Voortman.
