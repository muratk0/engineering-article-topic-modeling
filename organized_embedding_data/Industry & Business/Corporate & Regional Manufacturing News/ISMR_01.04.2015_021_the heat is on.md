# THE HEAT IS ON

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

Project nesting software has cooked up impressive savings for a leading cooker/oven manufacturer’s punch and laser operations

The combination of Radan’s Project Nesting and Radimport software ensures that one of the UK’s leading cooker and oven manufacturers can efficiently machine 190,000 parts per week on its CNC punch presses and lasers.

It gives Glen Dimplex Home Appliances full manufacturing flexibility to produce a weekly combination of 9,000 hobs, free-standing cookers, range cookers and built-in ovens, turning over £120m last year.

“Glen Dimplex Home Appliances is the only major kitchen appliance company still committed to manufacturing in the UK. Our manufacturing base is in Merseyside near Liverpool, and the distribution centre is in Stoke-on-Trent, ideally situated to delivering UK-wide. We manufacture and market a broad range of domestic appliances under the Stoves, Belling, New World and Lec brands,” said the company.

ISMR SAYS:

“Improved productivity, better savings and reduced scrap levels have all contributed to operational flexibility at Glen Dimplex Home Appliances”

## Turning up the heat

Glen Dimplex Home Appliances parts manufacturing manager, Dave Pearce, says that one of the numerous benefits of using project nesting is that the company only needs one sheet size for all jobs, which has improved machine running times from 70 per cent to around 95 per cent.

“When we analysed downtime before starting to use Project Nesting, we found the biggest cause was waiting for the right-sized steel sheets. Project Nesting has wiped that delay out,” he explained. “It also means that we’ve negotiated an improved rate for our steel as we only purchase large quantities of one size, and we’ve reduced the amount of locations for sheet metal on-site from 24 to two.”

Improved yields from each sheet has also considerably reduced scrap levels, he told ISMR. “Before starting to run Project Nesting, we were getting around 60 per cent material utilisation on some jobs. But now it’s at least 88 per cent,” he added.

Manufacturing Engineer, Lee Harding, confirms that the figure is even higher on its TRUMPF Trumatic L2510 laser.

“Discounting the holes, which we can’t do anything about, we’re getting more than 90 per cent sheet utilisation,” he explained.

The company recently invested in an Amada EM 2510 NT punch press, using full Project Nesting from day one. The program is run on all but the oldest and slowest of its 15 CNC lasers and punches, and they will use it on all new machines in future. While it has become a vital and integral part of its manufacturing system, Harding says that the company would not be able to use it to its full capacity without Radimport.

“If we only had a couple of machines with 15 to 20 jobs, a programmer could easily input the data manually. But we use four CNC lasers and 11 punch presses to cut around 190,000 sheet metal components per week, with a variety of 500 different components. When we were just using Project Nesting on the TRUMPF, I was able to key in the daily build, but that task grew considerably when we started running it on the other machines as well.”

## Preparing for nesting

Preparing parts for nesting is one of the most time-consuming tasks for sheet metal companies – but Radimport automatically converts geometry files to tooled Radan parts.

“With just one mouse click, my selection of geometry files is converted to a nest project. Radimport simply creates a Project Nest from the parts I’ve imported, and I’ve got the option of editing the quantities and other properties of the parts before creating the nest. Or Radimport can automatically launch the nester to start nesting straight away,” explained Lee Harding.

He says that, before switching to Radimport, it took a full day to manually input the data, but now it takes around an hour to complete the program. “We simply copy and paste our Excel database. As everything is now automatic, it also eradicates human error.”

The process begins, says Harding, when he receives STEP files from the Glen Dimplex 3D CAD system. “I import them into Radan within seconds, input the gauge of the material and flatten the components out, then tool and nest them.”
