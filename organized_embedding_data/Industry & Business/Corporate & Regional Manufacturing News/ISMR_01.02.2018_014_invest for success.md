# INVEST FOR SUCCESS

[TARİH: 01.02.2018 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"KMF's objective is to reach a £50-million turnover by 2021, when it celebrates its 50th anniversary"

ISMR visited KMF in the UK to see how it has invested in new sheet metal technologies and discuss its focus for the future.

Gareth Higgins, KMF Group's MD (centre), surrounded by KMF apprentices

KMF is the one of the largest subcontract manufacturers of precision sheet metal components in the UK with a turnover of more than £35 million and a diverse customer base from sectors such as aerospace, medical, renewable energy and telecommunications. With its headquarters in Newcastle-under-Lyme and an additional production facility in Slovakia for its European customers, KMF has over 550 employees, operates 24 hours per day and has its own apprenticeship training centre. KMF Group's managing director, Gareth Higgins, recently received an MBE for services to UK apprenticeships.

The company has been steadily investing in new machinery and equipment over the past few years, including a new Salvagnini panel bender and associated sheet metal equipment. It recently ploughed over £900,000 into a new electrostatic powder painting facility.

In August 2017, it invested £500,000 into a new purpose-built manufacturing facility for non-ferrous and stainless steel fabrication. It will share metal punching and laser services with the main site but houses a dedicated bending department, welding bays and a linishing/finishing room and metal dressing service. KMF Special Products houses some of KMF's largest stainless-steel customers and offers an opportunity for KMF to improve its current offering to clients who favour separate processing of ferrous and non-ferrous materials, mainly manufacturers of food processing and washroom equipment.

ISMR spent the day with KMF at its Newcastle-under-Lyme facility to gauge how the company has invested in its people and facilities and discuss its strategic focus for the future.

## An eye on sheet metal

At its modern engineering and assembly facilities, KMF offers sheet metal fabrication processes such as CNC punching; laser cutting; welding; powder coating; 5-axis machining and CNC milling and turning, as well as value-added services such as product design, electromechanical integration and supply chain management. It supplies metal enclosures and components to a broad range of industry sectors including aerospace; medical; food processing; defence; security; energy and sanitation.

"Continuous improvement of our workforce, systems and processes is a central plank of our business objectives while the training and development of our sheet metal manufacturing employees drive our competitiveness and performance and helps us to achieve prominence above other UK and European providers," the company told ISMR.

"We have spent over £10 million in the last three years on new machinery."

"Investment in the latest sheet metal engineering technology benefits our customers through quality, cost and delivery. It is this investment that has kept KMF at the forefront of UK precision sheet metal manufacturing and processing. We are always investing in new machinery – we have spent over £10 million in the last three years on new machinery."

KMF also runs an employee Productivity Share Scheme (PSS) every year which saved the company over £530,000 last year. Half the saving goes into the KMF Group and the other half is split between the employees who come up with a cost- or time-saving innovation. The scheme was set up to encourage employees to come up with innovative ideas to improve productivity, such as using scanners to find tools in the factory or painting tooling profiles onto KMF-made tooling cabinets.

With over 225,000 square feet overall of manufacturing space, KMF's objective is to reach a £50 million Group turnover by 2021 when it celebrates its 50th anniversary (or a minimum turnover of £3 million per month).

"We believe that the new stainless steel (KMF Special Products) facility will help us access new markets (such as sanitation and food processing equipment) and contribute to this increased turnover. We are also increasing our focus on the aerospace market to make the most of this sector. The KMF Precision Engineering division, which has been part of the Group for two years, is now up and running with new equipment and new processes, and is ready to expand its customer base," explained the company.

## Bending in focus

KMF has impressive sheet metal bending capacity, including 14 TRUMPF TruBend machines and automated TruBend Cells, incorporating 110-ton press brakes (a total of 16 manual press brakes plus automated/ robotic bending cells) and two Salvagnini panel bending systems. Around 30 staff and operators are involved in bending operations on the KMF shop floor.

In July 2006, KMF invested in a P4-2516 panel bender from Salvagnini, with zero set-up times, which has provided over 78,000 hours of working time (3250 days) to KMF to date. In April 2017, it also bought a new P4lean-2516 Salvagnini panel bender (P4L), using 60% less electricity than the P4-2516 (based on a single shift operation with a cost of 10p per KW, this equates to a saving of around £8,000 per annum). The new machine also features several upgrades including CLA-SIM auxiliary blade command, MAC 2.0 to calculate suitable metal bending trajectories and a Patented Bending Formula which will automatically adjust a bend to any mechanical or thermal deformation. ISMR watched it in operation on KMF's shop floor.

"The P4-2516 required 600 litres of oil, but the new P4L machines use electronic actuators with only 13 litres of oil in total," explained Steve Williams, managing director, Salvagnini UK. "All current P4L machines use touch-screen controls running on a Windows 10 platform. The panel bending system is Industry 4.0-embedded, including the service support option of 24-hour monitoring of machine to ensure all systems are working within tolerance."

The P4lean-2516 Salvagnini panel bender also has a MAC2 option which has proved extremely beneficial to KMF and checks both material thickness and tensile strength.

"In terms of material thickness, if the system sees a variation of more than 0.05mm from the program-defined thickness, the machine automatically recompiles the program. During bending of the first flange on a panel at approx. 60 degrees on the first bend, we can measure the tensile strength of the sheet. If this is within 7% of the material parameters, then no change is applied. If it is outside this range, a change is made and applied to all other bends on the part. This works on every sheet, ensuring more consistent bending accuracy, and zero scrap," added Steve Williams.

The new panel bender also boasts option CLA_SIM (for setting sectionalised tooling lengths). CLA_SIM automatically sets the length of the CLA tools for both CLA and CLA/N tools (for positive and negative bends). Adjustments to bending length are controlled via the program commands, eliminating the need for the operator to make the changes.

The latest version of the EDIT P4 OFFLINE software works from 3D models and can automatically determine the referencing points and bend sequence required to produce the part. This can then be visualised in 3D, showing the cycle time. It can then be sent to the panel bender for production. There is no lost time as programs can now be created in the office.

A blank-holder camera allows the operator to view, on the monitor, the movements of the bending tools. This, explained Pete Krynicki (Programming and Folding Technician, KMF Precision Sheet Metal), is a great help when running the first-off part to see the machine movements, especially when performing special bending operations.

"This new panel bending machine helps us to maintain our strong customer lead times and further improve productivity levels in the Precision Sheet Metal manufacturing facility. It also ensures that we can work closely with design engineers to ensure cost-effectiveness at the design stage. It is recognised that 80% of the component cost is locked in during the design phase, so having a fast and flexible bending cell ensures that our customers benefit from a competitive product. To give you an idea of the efficiency and productivity levels on the panel bender here, it is around 80%," explained Krynicki.

The Salvagnini machine can manufacture metal panels up to a maximum of 2500mm long and form bends of up to 165mm high. A favoured feature for Pete Krynicki is its interactive graphic programming which will significantly decrease the task of KMF programmers with its intuitive programming and 3D stimulation of different bending phases. The new panel bender also offers automatic tool changing and the ability to switch from one operation to another in a few seconds. Automation is key on the panel bender at KMF.

"As a metal fabrication subcontractor, being able to complete small and large batches without manual tool changes will significantly improve speed of processing and flexibility," added Pete Krynicki. "We also appreciate the personalised service, training and support on the panel benders that we get from Salvagnini – it is a real partnership."
