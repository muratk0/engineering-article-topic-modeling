# PEOPLE, PLANET AND PROFIT

[TARİH: 01.10.2015 ISMR]

Cutting specialist Hypertherm has adopted a '3P’ philosophy - ‘People, Planet and ‘Profit’. Its regional director for EMEA, Theo Cornielje, outlines this to ISMR.

Hypertherm designs and manufactures advanced cutting products for use in a variety of industries such as shipbuilding, manufacturing and automotive repair. Its product line includes plasma, laser and waterjet cutting systems, in addition to CNC motion and height controls, CAM nesting software and consumables.

The company's reputation for cutting innovation dates back nearly 50 years to 1968, with Hypertherm's invention of water injection plasma cutting. The 100 per cent associate-owned company (since 2014) has more than 1,300 associates along with operations and partner representation worldwide. ISMR discussed market trends, new product launches and reducing the cost of cutting with Hypertherm's EMEA regional director, Theo Cornielje.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes, achievements or awards and the reasons for this?

TC: Hypertherm has a philosophy based around the triple bottom line (the three Ps) – ‘People, Planet and Profit’. We are proud to say that, in this spirit, we recently received the Communitas award for leadership in community service and corporate social responsibility. We want to be a good neighbour in the community, with the environment always in mind, and the award helps to symbolise this. We see people as our greatest asset and are delighted to have also been the recipient of an award as one of the 2015 ‘World’s Most Ethical Companies’. This is one of the core values of the company and it is great to be recognised for this.

We are 100% owned by our associates and this has found a lot of traction with our customers because there is a lot of acquisition and merger activity in welding and cutting right now. Our customers can be reassured that, because we are independent and 100% associate-owned, we are here to stay and will still be here many years from now. We can afford to think long term. This stability creates confidence especially as our customers are investing in our products and our future product development. Our profits all go back into product and technology development, not to shareholders on a quarterly basis, which is also great for our customers.

We have consistently maintained a long term commitment to place 10% of our staff in product development (we currently have over 140 R&D engineers). Most of our R&D is done at our global headquarters. The company’s mantra for its product development is ‘cutting the cost of cutting’. The products we develop must align with the needs of the end users.

On the profit side, some months ago we acquired the company Jabez Technologies, a leading CAD/CAM software developer for robotic applications. With this acquisition, and the company’s RobotMaster® brand and software for 3D applications, we can now work with robotic manufacturers and 3D profilers. We are teaming up to see which new applications we can develop so watch out for further announcements this year.

## Communitas Award

Hypertherm received a Communitas Award for leadership in community service and corporate social responsibility. The Communitas Awards recognize businesses, organisations and individuals that unselfishly give of themselves and their resources, and those that are changing how they do business to benefit their communities. Judges found that Hypertherm clearly demonstrated the spirit of communitas, a Latin word that means people coming together for the good of a community, with work spanning multiple categories. Demonstrated categories covered volunteerism, philanthropy and ethical sustainable business practices.

For volunteerism, judges considered Hypertherm’s Community Service Time programme which provides associates around the world with 24 hours of paid time-off each year to volunteer.

Philanthropic work was demonstrated by financial support through the HOPE (Hypertherm Owners’ Philanthropic Endeavours) Foundation which last year awarded nearly 90 grants to non-profit organisations. Ethical, sustainable business practices were underscored by Hypertherm’s environmental work and corresponding goals, its safety programme, continued investment in associates and culture of ethics and transparency.

Many industries, including metal cutting, are facing challenges, mainly for political and economic reasons (unrest, wars in areas where growth was expected e.g. Russia and the Middle East). Low commodity prices do not help (oil companies are shutting down operations for cost-cutting reasons) so the investment climate is not very positive at the moment.

All this means that you have to be smart about how you position yourself in the marketplace. With 24/7 availability of information and globalised demands, companies need to ask themselves how they add value. What does the Digital Factory and Internet of Things mean for them? How can they be on top of these developments and ensure that they have not invested in the wrong technologies which can significantly affect product and pricing outputs – their cost of operation might not be aligned with the output of the machine. It is difficult for companies to understand the implications and to have a neutral view on the efficiency of their production floor. We want to be closer to our users and help them to make the right decisions for their business. This might not necessarily be about buying a new machine but perhaps, for example an upgrade to a product or new software.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to Hypertherm and explain your mission and objectives?

TC: Hypertherm was formed in 1968 as a family-owned company. It is now 100% associate-owned. Our mission is to cut the cost of cutting. We adjust our tactics and offerings in line with the changing demands of the market. We have a multi-technology approach – plasma, waterjet and laser cutting as well as areas related to the cutting process e.g. motion control of cutting tables and robots. This also includes understanding the change in demands for our product users.

New elements are starting to play a role such as environmental impact – this is now a key driver for organisations. Large manufacturers such as JCB are committed to reducing their footprint through technology and software (for example, by reducing scrap levels).

This also means reducing energy in the cutting process with 300 amps now, not 500 or 600 amps, and with better output. Higher efficiency is critical which puts pressure on product design. One of our sustainability goals is for 100% of our new products to be recyclable.

## Ethical Award

Hypertherm was selected as a winner of the 2015 World’s Most Ethical Company® by the Ethisphere Institute. The World’s Most Ethical Companies designation recognizes organizations for having a material impact on the way business is conducted by fostering a culture of ethics and transparency at every level of the company.

Hypertherm is one of eleven companies new to this year’s list, and one of only three companies in the Machine Tools & Accessories sector. Other companies earning the ‘Most Ethical’ designation include Deere and Company, GE, Google and The Nature Conservancy.

“The World’s Most Ethical Companies embrace the correlation between ethical business practice and improved company performance. These companies use ethics as a means to further define their industry leadership and understand that creating an ethical culture and earning the World’s Most Ethical Companies recognition involves more than just an outward facing message or a handful of senior executives saying the right thing,” said Ethisphere’s chief executive officer, Timothy Erblich.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

TC: We want to get close enough to our users to understand their current and future needs and are committing more to service after the sale now than ever before i.e. the cradle to cradle approach. We aim to ensure that customers get the best out of our products with the best possible service and support, operator training and upgrade opportunities.

Remote diagnostics is a great example. Our experts in the US and in the Netherlands recently solved an issue remotely for a customer based in Russia, meaning we are now able to troubleshoot a system from far away. Preventive maintenance schedules are another example. Operator training is also of key importance to reduce downtime and optimise productivity. We are currently developing new technologies and should be able to announce major breakthroughs in the next six months, supporting customer needs not just with providing access to experts but also by having tools available that provide insight and transparency of their cutting operations.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

TC: Our long term vision is to enhance products and services across multiple cutting technologies. We want to develop meaningful products for the industry that are focused outside in and not inside out. This is also in line with increasing user demands. In the perfect world, there would be fully automated operations from the moment that the metal sheet arrives to the moment the finished product leaves the facility. Doing that in a cost-effective and meaningful, staged approach is very complicated and often entails organisations adopting new philosophies such as lean principles.

We aim to play a trusted advisor role here to our customers which, eventually, means that we will have to develop deeper and wider relationships with other industry suppliers in the chain (such as fume extractor manufacturers).

We do have aggressive growth goals in various industries and parts of the world. Markets, however, are highly unpredictable. For example, the BRIC countries are no longer key growth drivers. Political events have hit the Russian market, India is a promising but very complex marketplace and China is highly dependent upon government investment and its reduced growth has impacted other countries. So backing the BRICs is not so good any more. The challenge is to find the next pockets of growth, with an eye on how they might be affected if, for example, commodity prices change.

## Top and bottom deburring machine

Machine for edge rounding and finishing of parts in a single pass.

The oscillating system is equipped with 12 brushes. The 6 top and 6 bottom, left and right rotating grinding flap brushes process the parts on both sides simultaneously.

## Multi rotating brush machine

Machine for uniformly deburring, rounding edges and finishing of parts. For laser, punched and machined parts, or parts with pockets/protrusions in all directions. The rotating carrousel, equipped with 8 left and right rotating abrasive flap brushes, processes the parts in all directions, no matter how fine the edges of the work pieces are.

Width: 200 – 1500 mm Thickness: 0,5 – 10 mm Composed by straightener, 2 feeders, lubricating system and cutting device.
