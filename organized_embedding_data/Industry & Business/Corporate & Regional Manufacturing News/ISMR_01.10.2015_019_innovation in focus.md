Co. KG celebrates its 171st birthday, making it one of the oldest family-owned businesses in Europe. It has become a global specialist in metal sawing machines, semiautomatic and automatic bar stock and sheet metal storage systems, as well as for automatic handling systems for metal bars, tubes, sheet and parts cut to size. This market position is underpinned by more than 160 patents as well as over 140,000 sawing machines which it has shipped to every part of the world and an installed base of more than 1,700 automatic storage systems.

In addition to a satellite factory at Schalkau, Thuringia, KASTO has subsidiaries in England, France and the USA. In 2014 the company’s 700 employees achieved revenues of about 120 million euros. ISMR flew to Germany to visit the company’s headquarters at Achern-Gamshurst (Baden-Württemberg), near Stuttgart, and meet its chairman, Armin Stolzer, who has been at the helm of the company for over 25 years.

## Sawing and storage of metal products

“KASTO is the only manufacturer of metal sawing machines to offer customers all three sawing variants (bow, band and circular sawing machines) and can deliver a wide range of products for every segment. Our portfolio extends from small manual machines for cutting jobs in the workshop through to high performance band- and circular sawing machines for all non-ferrous to high alloy materials,” Armin Stolzer told ISMR.

“KASTO has also developed and produced a wide range of semi-automatic and fully automatic bar stock and sheet metal storage systems. Products range from compact, standalone storage towers through to fully automatic high-rack honeycomb systems with thousands of storage cassettes for bar stock, sheet and residual materials which can also be returned to storage after machining.”

Combining these two lines of business has resulted in the development of “sawing centres” or “storage-saw cell systems” in

which all the storage, handling, sawing, marking, palletising and bundling processes, including material identification, are performed automatically in multi-shift operation – from the entry of the raw material through to the commissioning of the cut parts.

## Adding value with innovation

“We differentiate ourselves by adding value that brings real benefits for customers and staff alike. Thus, for example, the claim ‘More Profile’ is backed up by corporate values such as a focus on customer satisfaction and success, reliability and a commitment to progress. ‘More Responsibility’ describes the durability and resilience of our products as well as of our customer relations and decisions. To achieve this, we actively embrace values such as loyalty to Germany for our production, ‘Made in Germany’ quality and long-term, sustainable further development,” explained Armin Stolzer.

In 2010, for example, KASTO enlarged its company headquarters in Achern-Gamshurst, creating (amongst other things) 4,800 square metres of additional assembly space and a demonstration centre. The company also invests approx. 1.2 million euros every year in the professional and advanced training of its employees, placing a keen focus on expertise, quality and technical proficiency.

KASTO’s overseas sales have been constantly increasing for a number of years. While in 2013, overseas business accounted for 44.6 per cent of total sales, this figure grew to 54.3 per cent in 2014 (an approximate 45/55 split between domestic and export orders). The company registered turnover of 117 million euros in 2014. Stolzer confirmed that its 2015 target was 112 million euros, acknowledging the slowdown in European manufacturing markets. He confirmed a recent order to Benteler for a tube storage system which will shortly be in operation, the ‘largest of its kind in Europe’.

Stolzer is also reacting quickly to the growing significance of its overseas markets, in particular in South-East Asia. On 26 October, the company launched its new subsidiary, KASTO Asia Pacific Pte Ltd., in the German Centre in Singapore. KASTO is also building a site in Shanghai. The Swiss market will also shortly boast its own KASTO sales/service facility and KASTO Inc. activities are being refocused upon expanded sales and distribution activities.

“Health and safety is a key factor now for us,” Stolzer told ISMR. “Our focus is on reducing costs for our customers, examining the ‘cost per pick’ and payback periods. We still have many automotive projects in the pipeline – the sector is still very buoyant despite the slowdown in the German market. R&D is also a core focus for us – we spend approximately 5% of our turnover on this annually – and we work with various universities and institutes to keep us at the forefront of development and innovation.”

KASTO’s core skills lie as much in its creative technology and product development as its robust mechanical engineering competence and project management capabilities. KASTO’s machines and storage have garnered technical design awards such as an award from the Design Center Stuttgart and the well known Red Dot Award. KASTO’s management team has also, on several occasions, been named as a finalist competing for the Ernst & Young “Entrepreneur of the Year” business award. Claiming first place in the “Best Factory Award”, organised by the Reutlingen Export Academy and the journal “Markt und Mittelstand”, was another highlight in the company’s history.

To give particularly talented graduates from the well-tried dual system of occupational training in the technical professions of the metal industry the opportunity to gain experience, this year KASTO is also donating the new ‘KASTO Advancement Award – Sawing and Storage’. The advancement award of 2500 euros will be presented on an annual basis to a particularly talented graduate from the Ortenau area in a recognised technical training profession as a mechatronics engineer, starting in 2015.

## New product launches

2014 was an important year for KASTO. In May, the company not only celebrated its 170th birthday but also unveiled its new KASTOwin saw range at an official ceremony. Since then, it has already sold more than 400 KASTOwin machines inside and outside Germany.

KASTO is now extending its product portfolio and ISMR witnessed the introduction of the new KASTOwin F 10.6 fully automatic band sawing machine which is now available with a moveable material support table. This makes it possible to feed in and position heavy, bulky stock and large workpieces such as ingots, large tools or offset shafts easily. The bandsaw has a cutting range of 1,000 x 1,060mm and is suitable for steel manufacturers, steel traders and large-scale toolmakers. Using the table, it is possible to feed, for example, ingots, permanent moulds or materials with pre-forged geometries simply in a way that meets process requirements.

“The support length of the table is 2,600mm, while the table has a travel distance of 3,700mm and a bearing capacity of up to 18 tonnes. It can travel at a speed of up to three metres per minute with positional accuracy. The table is moved in the longitudinal direction by two linear guides, each equipped with four lubricated linear roller bearings. Precision feed is achieved by means of a zero-play ball screw spindle, while a hydraulic horizontal vice mounted on the outfeed side of the saw band holds the workpiece in place. Like all the models in the KASTOwin range, the saw excels thanks to its robust steel construction, a cutting accuracy of ±0.1 millimetres per 100 millimetres of cutting length, as well as cutting speeds of up to 150 metres per minute,” explained Sönke Krebber, managing partner, KASTO.

To respond to special requirements, an extensive range of accessories is available for the KASTOwin F 10.6. This includes a laser to project the cutting trajectory onto the stock, a travelling hydraulic horizontal vice at the end of the table, support surfaces with wearing plates and infrared remote control. A wide range of individual stop, alignment and clamping devices is also available for the saw.

ISMR took a factory tour to see the newly launched KASTOwin Tube A5.0, with its 500mm cutting capacity. Interestingly, the tool points upwards so that sawing motion takes place from the bottom upwards, rather than cutting in a downwards motion. This is a patent-pending process and is designed to limit the amount of chips that gather at the bottom of the tube/profile during a sawing operation (thus improving surface finish), extend blade and tool life and save significant cost. Once the cut has been made, the machine opens the gap for the blade (with no pressure on the tool from the side) which means that carbide-tipped steel blades can be used – an interesting development.

## An eye on automation

Various KASTO systems have been designed with 24-hour, lights-out operation in mind and the company employs various lean and Kanban systems in its daily manufacturing operations. It has developed its own ERP system to control its production processes but has developed its machines to interface with various customer ERP/MRP systems. It also produces its own software to run its machines, which are constructed using a modular principle.

KASTO recently built and delivered a bespoke, 3,332-location KASTOunicompact 4.0 automated warehouse to Pipe Centre in the UK, supplied through KASTO Ltd.

“The KASTO cassette-type tube storage system has reduced the distance that warehouse operatives have to move when picking, speeding the consolidation of orders. The warehouse design also increases the height used for storage of Pipe Center’s SKUs (Stock-Keeping Units). The consequently reduced footprint represented significant cost savings,” explained Ernst Wagner, Managing Director, KASTO Ltd.

With Industry 4.0 firmly in its sights, the company is positioning itself for the future but focusing on reducing costs for customers, increasing innovation and ensuring aftersales support.
