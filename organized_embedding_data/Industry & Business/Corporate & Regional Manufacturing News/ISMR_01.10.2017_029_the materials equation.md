The group in the global steel processing sector, with a yearly output of 5.6- million tons (2016). It is guided by key values such as synergies (raw material purchasing, processing, logistics, market), economies of scale, production flexibility and diversification of products and markets.

All these values are translated into flat steel production (both carbon and stainless steel coil, sheet and narrow strip), also including colour coated steel production and heavy (quarto) plates. The full range will be on show at Blechexpo 2017 in Stuttgart, Germany (7-10 November 2017) at the company's booth in Hall 4, Stand 4109. ISMR caught up with Lorenzo Biagi, sales and marketing director of Marcegaglia's processed Flat division, for the latest news on the Group.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes and achievements and the reasons behind this?

LB: Marcegaglia significantly reorganised its service centre activities in 2015, merging the sales teams of slit coil, specialty strip and sheet. Our aim was to improve team competencies and the product portfolio that we offer to customers. We created a single team which manages sales of three production units, making Marcegaglia one of the largest independent service centres in terms of both volume delivered and product range.

While respecting the role of distribution and continuing to sell commodity products, we wanted to enlarge our clientele to big, mill-direct end-users. Using cross training, better product knowledge and our capability to offer new solutions, we have been able to significantly grow our presence in premium markets such as automotive or yellow goods. This has created added value for Marcegaglia and more efficient business models, designed to reduce the total cost of ownership, for our customers.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals?

LB: The global steel industry is facing a process of de-globalisation, protectionism and consolidation. There is now a record number of anti-dumping investigations. Two significant mergers involving four of the major European steel mills are going through and we expect more to come.

We believe that the market will be much more structured than in the past, with few imports and a more dominant presence from a few domestic players. In this context, Marcegaglia can play an important role as one of the largest, but still lean, independent alternatives to the newly formed conglomerates.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to the Marcegaglia Group and explain your mission and objectives?

LB: Marcegaglia is the largest private steel processing company in Europe. In a little over fifty years, we have now reached a total output of over five million tons per year (mainly in carbon flat rolled, tube and stainless). Our carbon steel manufacturing process starts from black coil. We also have cold rolling, galvanizing and painting capabilities as well as service centres and tube mills.

Our main flat rolled materials plant in Ravenna, Italy, is directly on the sea and benefits from a sophisticated inbound and outbound logistics network. We have positioned ourselves uniquely in the market to try to combine economies of scale of our mill with the flexibility and responsiveness of a service centre, so that our customers receive the material and solutions that they need.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

LB: Managing stock and working capital is becoming more and more important, at all levels, for customers. We are developing several hubs in different countries to create advanced stocking facilities. We are also looking to improve service levels, thanks to proximity and various VMI solutions which enable our customers to reduce their working capital.

ISMR: Please give an example of your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

LB: Steel grades are continuously evolving in premium markets, such as automotive, to increase material strength but keep material workability. AHSS material is a perfect example of this. This will enable engineers to use lighter-weight materials as well as new material configurations.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

LB: The consolidation and regionalisation processes, which I mentioned previously, can lead to a very protected, almost oligopolistic market dominated by a few fully integrated players who can enjoy the benefits of a comfortable, short-term market. The midterm risk is that this jeopardises European manufacturing export competitiveness, which has provided growth and recovery to Europe after the 2008 financial downturn.

In this type of environment, mid-sized and more responsive players (such as Marcegaglia) can represent a valid alternative to fill some of the voids created by this new market configuration.

ISMR: Are there any products/ technologies that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

LB: We are currently launching our new product line of structural sheet for laser applications, which provides improved surface cleanliness, more homogeneous mechanical properties and guarantees flatness of 3mm/mt (before and after laser cutting). It is designed to improve cutting precision and speed, as well as our customer's efficiency, and can be processed on fully automated lines.
