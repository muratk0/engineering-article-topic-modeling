# A FOCUS ON INNOVATION

[TARİH: 01.02.2013 ISMR]

SafanDarley has launched a number of innovative sheet metal products, including the Eye, for press brake operators. Wim van Kranen explains the company’s philosophy and future initiatives

SafanDarley is a leading global specialist in developing and constructing press brakes and shears for thin sheet metal. Its head office and factory in Lochem, the Netherlands, is a versatile organisation that covers all the disciplines, from R&D and engineering to manufacture and assembly, from marketing and sales to technology and training centres, and from helpdesk to service department.

Safan B.V. was established in 1960 when five Dutch factories producing metal working machines and tools joined forces. The name Safan is an acronym for SAmenwerkende FAbrieken Nederland (Cooperating Factories Netherlands). The most important of these factories and the driving force behind the development of Safan was Wila in Lochem, which has been active since 1932 as an engineering works for metalworking machinery and tools. Darley was established in 1934 and has nearly as much experience. Both companies have introduced important and patented innovations to the sheet metal market. Darley was acquired in 2011 by the Nivora Group, Safan’s mother company.

In October 2012, at the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition, the Safan and Darley brands were united under a new global brand, SafanDarley. From 1 January 2013, the group was restructured and the new sales company, SafanDarley BV, was founded to centralise all the sales for the two factories, Safan and Darley.

Customers have responded very positively to the launch as well as to the new products we introduced at EuroBLECH, including a new hydraulic press brake. These products combine the best of Safan and Darley, presented under the new brand name,” said Wim van Kranen, sales and marketing director of SafanDarley.

“We launched the SafanDarley Eye at the Techni-Show in the Netherlands last year, won an innovation award for it and then showcased it at EuroBLECH where we also received another award. A lot of customers want to try the glasses in their production. We are formally launching the product into the market place in the first quarter of this year.

“We also launched the H-brake, a new line of hydraulic press brakes, at EuroBLECH and extended the servo-electric E-brake range with a new machine, the E-Brake 130T 4100, for applications such as cladding where thin longer sheets need to be bent with a strong requirement for straightness. I am also pleased to say that we are taking on the Mitsubishi laser product business for sales in Benelux and large parts of Germany.”

## Augmented reality

To increase the efficiency of the operator, SafanDarley introduced Augmented Reality (the SafanDarley Eye) at EuroBLECH, a special tool for press brake operators.

“With the SafanDarley Eye, the press brake operator can look through machine parts. He can also see exactly how he or his colleagues have previously made a particular product. Using the Eye, the ideal press brake operator has no trouble with smaller batch sizes and sheet metal parts that are becoming increasingly complex. With Augmented Reality, motivated personnel can make huge leaps towards becoming ideal press brake operators,” explained Wim van Kranen.

On the glasses that the operator wears, additional information is displayed that only the operator can perceive. For example, the operator can see where the back gauge stop is located whilst, with the naked eye, only the tools are visible. Other relevant data, such as the position where the tools should be placed, are projected in the glasses.

Data projections into the operator’s glasses will enable him to better his performance. Projected in the glasses is information such as tool set-up, bending sequence, working instructions of different kind and, in the future, says van Kranen, it will also be possible to activate virtual buttons. SafanDarley Eye is designed for better process control.

At the Technishow in the Netherlands, where the Eye won an innovation award, visitors to the Safan stand were given the opportunity to experience for themselves Augmented Reality and were invited to participate in the Safan Augmented Reality game. The participants had to discover the correct code on the back of a virtual cube by viewing through the Safan AR glasses. The first prize was Mobile Theatre Video Glasses, which makes it possible to view movies and DVDs full-screen without screens but projected into these innovative video glasses.

## FACE TO FACE

## A bending portfolio

SafanDarley offers a complete range of electronic press brakes. As the original inventor of the servo-electric E-Brake, it has developed the machine concept further into a number of ranges: with a pressure force of 20 to 300 tons and working lengths of 850 to 4100mm. Customers can choose from 19 different machine models.

“The servo-electronic drive based on the pulley principle ensures a very even distribution of forces so that, in 95 per cent of all cases, no crowning table is needed. Cycle times are shortened by up to 30 per cent compared to traditional hydraulic press brakes,” van Kranen told ISMR. “The absence of hydraulics makes each SafanDarley E-Brake press brake as environmentally friendly as possible. There is no risk of polluting oil and the lower energy consumption means lower CO2 emissions.”

The new generation of SafanDarley E-Control touch-screen controls include 2D and 3D graphic displays, 2D bending simulation and 3D graphic programming.

With its new H-Brake, SafanDarley has focused on energy savings and, for that reason, the machine will, as standard, be equipped with a start-stop system. The range of machines available will be from 175T up to 1250T and there will be two levels of machines – one for the mid-level market and one for the high-end market. Another aspect is machine safety. SafanDarley has created a solution which integrates the light guard into the SafanDarley Touch Screen CNC for optimum cycle times.

“The defining element of the E-Brake is the pulley drive which Safan adapted for the first time in 1995 in a press brake. The power of the servo motors is equally divided over the upper beam by the 50mm wide belt,” continued van Kranen. “A new innovation for the E-Brake 130T 4100 is the specially developed lower beam which compensates for the minimal distortion during bending. So, even for a 130-ton 4-metre press brake, crowning is not necessary. The precision and stability of the machine is further guaranteed through the rigid O-frame construction which keeps the tooling under higher pressures in line.

“The lack of oil, unlike conventional hydraulic press brakes, the fact that no heating-up of the machine is needed, there is no environmental pollution and maintenance costs are low due to the lack of hydraulic valves means that extremely rapid cycle times are possible and they are, on average, 30 per cent lower compared to conventional hydraulic press brakes,” van Kranen told ISMR. The servo-electronic drive system uses up to 50 per cent less energy. The machine only uses energy when the pedal is pushed.

In total, SafanDarley displayed ten machines at EuroBLECH. These included the R-Brake flexible bending cell in combination with ROBOWAVE off-line programming software, the M-Shear with ‘return to sender’, the GS-Shear and four more servoelectric E-Brakes including the E-Brake 300T 4100 Dual Drive.

SafanDarley's new E-App, now available in the Apple store, provides information for cutting and bending sheet metal and calculates the tonnage for bending plate on a press brake as well as calculating the maximum sheet thickness to be cut in a guillotine shear.

## Looking to the future

As an innovative company, focusing on just a few products (press brakes, shears etc.), it is important that SafanDarley develops new and innovative products every year. Approximately ten per cent of its employees are involved in R&D engineering. Work continually progresses on its press brakes, shears and machine controls and it brings out new software developments every month for its customers.

“We are currently installing a very special automated cutting line, one of the largest projects we have ever done, in China (a 4.2 million-euro project). As a taster, we are also working on a new e-brake generation which we will launch at the end of this year. I cannot say too much but it is very exciting and will represent a new technology breakthrough,” van Kranen concluded.
