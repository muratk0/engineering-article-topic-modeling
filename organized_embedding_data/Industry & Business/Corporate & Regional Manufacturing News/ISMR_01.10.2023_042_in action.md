# IN ACTION

[TARİH: 01.10.2023 ISMR]

AMADA's newly opened Global Innovation Centre (AGIC) shares its future manufacturing vision and latest technical innovations, inviting global collaboration with its customers

ISMR SAYS: “AMADA intends AGIC to play a central role for its solution centres and technical centres in Japan and overseas.”

"Co-creating the future of metalworking", Japanese sheet-metalworking specialist, AMADA, set up its new AMADA Global Innovation Centre (AGIC) this year. Opened by AMADA President & CEO, Tsutomu Isobe, on 3 February 2023 at the company's head office in Isehara City, Kanagawa Prefecture (Japan), the AGIC facility has earned Science-Based Targets Initiative (SBTi) certification to support research and verification.

However, to further the concept of future-oriented manufacturing, AMADA is also using the AGIC to introduce new technologies, software and machine tools to its customers. The AGIC showcases AMADA's history and future manufacturing vision, as well as its latest technical innovations, inviting global collaboration with its customers.

The AGIC concept also represents a step change in the way that AMADA is positioning itself for the future. It intends AGIC to play a central role for its solution centres and technical centres in Japan and overseas. ISMR therefore decided to explore how the philosophy, culture and ambition behind AGIC will help AMADA to forge closer links with its customers, staff and partners around the world.

## Sustainability in focus

Based on the concept of being a "place for co-creating the future of metalworking" the AGIC was established to solve numerous issues faced by AMADA's customers around the world including their demands for "support for new materials and processing technologies", "improvement of automation and productivity" and production innovation, AMADA told ISMR. The existing "Solution Centre" was fully transformed to create a facility with a total floor space of approximately 30,000m² and the latest functionality.

AGIC was also designed as an environmentally friendly building. Through collaboration with major general contractors and various manufacturers in Japan, the latest air conditioning equipment and lighting was installed, energy consumption has been reduced and renewable energy is utilised. This includes the large-scale installation of solar power generation; on the roof of the AGIC buildings are solar panels which can generate 241MW of power every year.

As a result, CO₂ emissions have been halved compared to that generated by the previous Solution Centre. Idling functions on chillers and compressors in the facility also help to decrease CO₂ emissions. In total, CO₂ emissions at the AGIC are expected to be reduced by 700 tons per year.

"As a machine manufacturer, AMADA is promoting environmental considerations in its own manufacturing. The AGIC was designed to replicate a customer's prototype and manufacturing site, and the facility was also equipped with panels that display everything from lighting to solar and overall power usage. This was designed with the environment in mind, not only for machine operation but also across the building's operational functions. We believe that providing highly efficient systems is a solution to environmental issues and global labour shortages. This also [strikes a chord with our customers]," Mr Yamamoto, Senior Executive Officer, AMADA, told ISMR.

In Site 01 at AGIC are Eco-Board screens that depict the current status of the AGIC facility from a power consumption point of view etc. compared to the previous facility. They show real-time CO₂ emissions and users can drill down into the data. All over the facility are monitors and cameras e.g. the system is programmed to pick up on whoever wears silver AGIC jackets (engineers, AGIC staff). Artificial intelligence (AI) is used to adjust the heating and lighting depending upon who is in the facility at the time.

The second board displays machine data specific to the site (Site 01 for blanking/blanking automation/welding machines and Site 02 for bending/bending automation technologies). Customers can choose machines on the screen and check their status remotely (including camera views inside the machine). AMADA encourages its customers to engage more interactively with all its machines and technologies at AGIC.

"The AMADA Group has always made the environment a top priority. Producing eco-friendly machines with low power consumption and high energy efficiency in an environmentally friendly facility is part of that ethos," AMADA told ISMR.

## Social responsibility

"The new AGIC philosophy illustrates our desire not just to be more innovative and advanced with our technology developments, but also to offer greater benefits and responsibility to society as a whole. This means not just reducing CO₂ emissions but also tackling societal challenges such as the ageing population, lack of skilled workers etc.

So, there is a real focus on the environment and technologies such as automation, voice control and artificial intelligence in AGIC," explained Matt Wood, Manager, EU Product Promotion, AMADA.

"The other AGIC innovation is that we have fundamentally changed the way that we explain things to customers. In the past, we explained what the machines can do. We have now changed our philosophy from the 'what' to the 'why'. We explain to customers why we are developing systems and why the technologies function in certain ways. That is quite a step change. Eventually, we will roll out this AGIC philosophy globally i.e. as our international solution and technical centres carry out minor renovations, we will embed the AGIC philosophy. That is quite a challenge, moving forward," he continued.

AMADA also showcases its new control, AMNC 4ie, at AGIC; this incorporates significant features to help it meet its social responsibility targets (such as compensating for the lack of skilled workers etc). An integrated camera with facial recognition authorises users to operate a machine (for operator safety reasons, to set access levels and to log operator use). It can give users a CO₂ report for an individual machine and features 15 different languages, applying native language settings to recognised individual users. Facial recognition can be turned on and off.

"There is a range of maintenance and set-up tutorials built into the control. If an operator is informed by the machine that the lens needs cleaning on a laser, the tutorial will highlight the steps needed to do that. They can scan a QR code on their iPhone and it will transfer that video to their phone if they need to move around the shop floor. This new fourthgeneration control meets our '4e' target: easy to use; efficient/faster to use; environmentally sustainable and an evolutionary step for customers," highlighted Matt Wood.

## A personalised experience

AGIC has also been designed to offer a personalised customer experience. This starts in the reception area, with videos illustrating how customers' feedback is vital to AMADA's ongoing technology development cycles and new product launches. Customers are given a lanyard with their own QR code. This is scanned to log their interest in any machines, systems or technologies displayed at AGIC so that further information can be sent to them later (depending upon their preferences).

"The 'Engineering Field' in AGIC examines the history of a machine and discusses the evolution of the control side, software and the move into IoT. Once a customer's QR code is scanned, it immediately begins displaying a list of all the machines/technology that the customer has bought from AMADA. It really personalises the experience for our customers," outlined Matt Wood.

Customer QR codes can also be scanned at the Sheet Metal Innovation Fair at AGIC. For the last 35 years, AMADA has shown and shared best practice for sheet-metal part production or showcased customers who make unusual or complex products.

"This year, there is a female suit of armour on show (along with food hoppers, mini car models etc.). On the walls are previous winners of the competition. We received roughly 300 submissions for this year's government-recognised engineering competition (from AMADA customers)," confirmed Matt Wood.

AMADA also invites its customers to share its vision of the future and join it on its journey. ISMR watched an impressive, immersive video experience which also plays in the 'Future Vision' room at AGIC.

"This video explains to customers how we have adapted over the last 15-20 years and how much things have changed (i.e. networking, smaller batch sizes etc.). It also invites them to think about where we are going to be in 30 years' time. This is quite hard to do, bearing in mind how much the sheetmetal market has changed just in the last 10-12 years," Matt Wood explained.

“The Future Vision room tries to give an idea of how the future might look. It shows customers that we are looking long term: it is not just about what the market needs today. We all need to be at the forefront of developments. What do we need to start doing now to make sure that we are ready for these huge changes that may be coming? How should we position ourselves?" he continued.

## The challenges of future processing

AGIC is equipped with a diverse range of environments and functions. This includes the Innovation LABO, which is a laboratory space specifically for customers, and the Innovation SITE, where AMADA customers can experience and interact with over 90 models of its latest machine and technology products. This location also includes the 'Engineering Field', showcasing AMADA initiatives with the latest IoT systems, and the 'Area for Creating the Future of Manufacturing' with modern digital imaging equipment and information devices.

In nine customer-specific spaces equipped with modern solutions and advanced inspection equipment, its customers can address various issues by working with AMADA technical staff to verify their processing and quality requirements.

"We created the Innovation Labo as a free laboratory area where customers can explore the future of manufacturing and use the facility as a place to create innovation. They can come with difficult challenges with which they need help and to solve manufacturing problems. They may bring parts, materials or 3D data etc. It is also a way for us to engage more with other stakeholders such as material suppliers and academia to create future technologies and programming systems. We feed strands from this work into our machine development cycles. It is all linked to our Future Vision strategy to help us come up with new ways of doing things," explained Matt Wood.

"In this room, there are nine private individual rooms (controlled by QR codes). We respect NDAs with our customers and respect their privacy on what happens in this room. Each room has different machines and specialists i.e. welding equipment; laser processing equipment; robotic bending; bending equipment and combination machines. Software is also included," he continued.

Matt Wood demonstrated laser cutting quality on the REGIUS 3015 AJe fibre laser at the EuroBLECH 2022 exhibition in Hanover, Germany.

Opposite the laser room is an inspection room with over one million euros' worth of equipment (20 measurement machines) installed. It includes X ray machines to check for impurities; an electron microscope to check material structures; CMM equipment; tensilestrength testers etc. Customers can check parts processed on machines in this inspection room. The Labo area also has meeting and presentation rooms; common rooms; locker rooms etc. The demonstration and verification areas are separated to accommodate customer needs.

Innovation Site 01: blanking technologies

AMADA's "Innovation SITE" has product areas for sheet metal, cutting and structural, as well as presses, but it is not simply a conventional display of the machines. With the concept of moving from "what is possible" to "why it is possible", the facility enables visitors to experience AMADA's latest technologies such as for mechanisms and structures. The aim is to deepen the understanding and satisfaction of customers by providing them with an experience centred on explaining the technical processes and benefits. Innovation Site 01 features blanking and welding machines, technologies and software.

Demonstrations on the 12kW REGIUS 3015 AJe fibre laser cutting machine at Innovation Site 01 included AMADA's new cutting innovations such as the fibre silky cut improvements on aluminium and stainless steel. The technique involves a mixture of optical path (beam) adjustments, higher power fibre engines and gas flow dynamics and is an automatic function of the machine once the operator selects the cut data.

AMADA's Locus Beam Control (LBC) technology, unique to the VENTIS-AJ Series, manipulates the laser beam motion, managing beam patterns during the cutting process.

New AMADA innovations, such as the patent-pending Soft Joint, are also showcased at AGIC. Operators can specify soft joints through the offline software and the laser will cut lines where soft joints are needed on straight or curved-edge sheet metal parts. The laser will then cut the outline of the part itself using the thermal stress of the material, through the soft joint, to clamp the part in the sheet. There is no physical joint between the two pieces of metal; only the physical pressure holds the part in the sheet skeleton.

"The soft joint can be used in up to 12mm stainless steel, 10mm aluminium and 9mm mild steel. It is a simple but particularly useful innovation to help reduce or even eliminate tipped parts and cutting head collisions. It will save operators a lot of time lifting parts out of sheet skeletons and help reduce overall reworking, such as grinding of microjoints," explained Matt Wood.

Other technologies on display include a dual gas system on a 9kW laser machine that uses compressed air as a shield to focus the oxygen through the cut. It can reportedly save up to 60% oxygen usage when cutting thick mild steel, illustrating AGIC's eco philosophy.

New AMADA LBC Flash Cut technology is also demonstrated on a 6kW VENTIS 3015 AJe laser cutting system. AMADA has developed a way of cutting holes without moving the cutting head but by manipulating the beam; a huge time-saver for manufacturers who do a lot of perforated part cutting or cut thousands of small holes in sheet metal.

AMADA's Locus Beam Control (LBC) technology, unique to the VENTIS-AJ Series, manipulates the laser beam motion, managing beam patterns during the cutting process. The geometry of this pattern is variable for each thickness and material to be processed. The power of the beam no longer needs to be diffused to cut any materials which, AMADA told ISMR, means very high processing speeds and cutting quality.

In a laser technology technical corner, customers can interactively engage with beam shape and laser power to uncover the effects on sheet-metal cutting.

## Innovation Site 01: Welding and combi technologies

"There is more of a focus on welding now at AMADA. In the past, we had our 3kW FLW ENSIS robotic welding system. At AGIC, we have now expanded that line-up to three different robotic welding systems: the 6kW FLW ENSISe; the 3kW FLW Le (with smaller welding head) and the FLW 1500 MT (a handy welding system with a cobot). This spans the high-end, middle and lower entry segments of the market," outlined Matt Wood.

The 6kW FLW ENSISe at AGIC features a TAS (teaching assistant system) to ensure that the weld position is correct for the cutting head. This previously manual fine-tuning job used to take around 15 minutes at the start of a new job; the new AI-TAS can do this in just under two minutes. Developed in partnership with Tokyo University, AI-TAS uses artificial intelligence and over 100,000 comparison images to determine whether part positioning is correct and make the appropriate adjustments. At AGIC, this system is displayed with the new AMADA machine control.

"The FLW Le (3000) mid-level system has a smaller cutting head so it can get into smaller boxes to do internal welds. Our demonstration at AGIC shows a tube being welded onto a box with no rotating table needed. FLW 1500 MT; the entry-level handy welder system linked to a cobot, is already sold in Japan but is not available in Europe yet," Matt Wood told ISMR.

A tooling technical corner showcases a part-removal assistance tool for combination machines that makes a small punch to force material up from the skeleton and aid part removal. Like the soft joint, punch tools make a small hole next to the sheet-metal part to lock it securely in the sheet skeleton. A new bridgeforming tool also locks the part into the sheet.

## Innovation Site 02: bending and automation

Innovation Site 02 at AGIC focuses on bending and bending automation. Four fully electric drive (EGB) press brake systems are on display here (two were showcased at the EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover last year). A single servo motor on each side of the pressbrake handles both the high-speed approach and high-tonnage bending. The EGB pressbrake series goes up to 130 tons.

"In the future, all low-to-mid-tonnage press brakes will be electric drive. All our punch press machines are currently electric and we use fibre laser engines on our laser cutting systems. We currently use a mixture of hydraulic and hydraulic hybrid (HRB) drives on the press brakes and will introduce more electric models over the coming years," confirmed Matt Wood.

"The move to electric press brakes is a significant change. There is an active crowning system on the new electric press brake consisting of two servo motors in the lower beam that can adjust the beam, depending upon where an operator is bending on the machine. A fully interactive demonstration of this is at AGIC, with digital dial gauges to portray the crowning process," he added.

The heart of the EGB press brakes is a patented motor gearbox design which is effectively a multi-purpose servo drive. The innovative arrangement of gears and clutch uses fewer resources (one motor instead of two) and is a new development that was first shown at EuroBLECH 2022 in Germany.

At AGIC, visitors can see an EGB 6020 pressbrake with auto tool-changer and a tablet HMI to warn operators of safety issues and portray an Augmented Reality view via a back-gauge monitor camera which can superimpose the outline of a part onto three back-gauge fingers. Voice control enables the machine to start bending or to make any corrections. Visitors can also put on a headset and speak directly (in a variety of languages) with the tablet HMI and voice control system in a separate demonstration area.

A small 60-ton, 1.3m robotic press brake, with an expanded automatic tool changing system and conveyors, has three trolleys at the front for external job set-up to reduce machine downtime. Visitors can see it bend a tiny demonstration part at AGIC.

"A larger 130-ton robotic press brake incorporates a tool changer and AMADA smart loading device. The camera examines a pallet of parts to determine the correct orientation of the parts, which is then handled by a gantry robot. This also checks each part and drops any doubled-up parts into a separate drawer to keep the system running and prevent any downtime. In the bending technology centre, there are also various interactive demonstration systems such as the inline angle-measurement system and AMADAdesigned loading carts for vertical and flat loading," confirmed Matt Wood.

## In conclusion

AMADA offers a fully comprehensive range of solutions for sheet-metal processing. To meet the changing needs of the market and its customers, the Group has set up a network of production sites in strategic areas: Japan, Europe, North America and China.

"We always try to keep an eye on new technology trends in the world and consider introducing new technologies that are useful for our products. For example, we have incorporated thrust monitors, face recognition, voice control and digital imaging technology showing machining procedures in CG for prototypes and small batches for AMNC control. We have also developed a 3D virtual machining feasibility study for CAD/ CAM applications and are strengthening our development in areas such as AI, AR and VR with control and software," Mr. Yamanashi, Representative Director, President, AMADA, told ISMR.

Its new AGIC collaborative philosophy will certainly help the company to move closer to its customers, partners and staff as it positions itself for the future.
