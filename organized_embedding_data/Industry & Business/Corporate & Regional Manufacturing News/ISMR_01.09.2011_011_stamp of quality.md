# STAMP OF QUALITY

[TARİH: 01.09.2011 ISMR]

ISMR visits Stadco which, after extensive restructuring, has emerged as a stronger, automotive-focused business.

ISMR recently visited Stadco, the largest independent UK-based international supplier of automotive Body-in-White products and services. Its core capabilities are aluminium and steel stampings, assemblies, painting and product and facility design. Solutions from niche to high volume are provided.

“If it’s metallic and cold formed for a vehicle, we can produce it,” Stadco management told ISMR. Its global sites are based in the UK (Shrewsbury, Castle Bromwich, Telford, Powys and Coventry) and Germany (Saarlouis, next to the Ford plant) and it has a design facility in Chennai (India).

With 920 employees in the UK and India, it registered a turnover of £92 million in 2010 (UK and India). Over 90 per cent of its UK production is supplied to UK-based OEMs. Key products are product engineering services, stamping and assembly of automotive body structure modules. Stadco is due to celebrate its 200-year anniversary in 2012.

“Our key customers/markets include Jaguar Land Rover, GM, BMW, Ford, Nissan, Magna, Benteler and AGCO,” Mark Hemming, Operations Director, told ISMR.

## STAMPING EXPERTISE

For stamping parts, Stadco can turn out the smallest bracket through to the largest body panel. In assembly, the simplest sub-assembly and the most complex modules and complete body structures, in steel or aluminium, can also be accommodated.

Stadco has particular expertise in the stamping of aluminium, including skin panels, with more than 20 years of experience dating back to the first generation Land Rover Discovery vehicle. Whereas most manufacturers will limit themselves to supplying one volume range, Stadco will supply niche orders of a few 100 parts per year through to high volume supply of around 400,000 vehicle sets per year and anything in between. Such a range is a central differentiator for Stadco.

Today, Stadco supplies over 70 million such parts annually to the world’s leading automotive manufacturers from five plants in the UK and one in Germany. It also provides engineering services through its facility in Chennai, India. Aftermarket is a significant part of Stadco’s business. Its Powys plant re-engineers high volumes of equipment for automotive OEMs 15 years after cessation of main automotive model production.

## NEW FACILITY AND STAMPING LINES

Stadco recently celebrated the opening of a major automotive manufacturing 24,000m2 facility acquired earlier in the year in Telford, Shropshire (UK) for high volume pressings. Two fully automated mechanical press lines (4.5m bed with 2000 tonnes lead and 3.5m bed with 1600 tonnes lead) and a fully equipped tool room, the largest in the UK belonging to a Tier 1 supplier, are included. The stamping lines can produce the largest panels on a vehicle, including the exterior ‘A Class’ skin panels in both steel and aluminium.

The new plant further strengthens Stadco’s platform for growth, with the company set to capitalise on the increase in manufacturing activity in the UK automotive sector. The acquisition will result in an additional 50 permanent jobs being created at the site with a further 30 required during the start-up phase.

Like several automotive businesses in the UK, Stadco faced many challenges throughout the economic downturn in the industry in 2008 – 09. After extensive restructuring, Stadco has emerged as a stronger, automotive-focused business, which led to the company winning several key pieces of new business with major car manufacturers. Within the last year, new business won saw 183 jobs created across the group.

The acquisition of the Telford site provides Stadco with additional capacity to meet the increase in vehicle production in the UK. Improved confidence and a more optimistic outlook for the UK automotive sector means that there is now greater demand on Stadco’s output, for example from Jaguar Land Rover, which has experienced strong sales and has an exciting new vehicle model development plan.

Stadco is already a key supplier in the UK for JLR, producing parts across the range of vehicles, including the Jaguar XF and XK, and Land Rover Defender, Freelander, Discovery, Range Rover and Range Rover Sport vehicles.

On show at the opening event was an impressive display of vehicles for which Stadco supplies parts including: Jaguar XF, Jaguar XJ, Jaguar XK, Land Rover Freelander, Land Rover Defender, Land Rover Discovery, Land Rover Range Rover, Land Rover Range Rover Sport, MINI Hatch, Ford Focus, Ford C-MAX and Ford Transit.

## COMPANY HERITAGE

Stadco can trace its roots in the production of metallic forgings back to 1812. In 1825, the company’s headquarters were established in Shrewsbury, UK, where it still resides today.

The focus of operations moved decisively toward the automotive sector during the vibrant years of the 1960s when the company became known as the Shrewsbury Tool and Die Company. This name evolved into the acronym STADCO, although today the derivative is no longer used and the marque ‘Stadco’ is independent of any further meaning.

Initial forays into automotive supply saw Stadco invest in tool making capabilities. In the 1980s, however, the company developed expertise in body-in-white stampings and assemblies. Stadco still prides itself on a particularly diverse range of offerings in this area. It regularly consumes 50,000 tonnes of steel and 11,000 tonnes of aluminium annually for its metal stamping operations.

## CRISIS MANAGEMENT

Stadco was caught directly in the crossfire of the recession as the global economic slump in 2008/9 threatened to bring the automotive sector to its knees. When the credit crisis turned into a severe downturn, Stadco acted swiftly. It laid off around 40 per cent of its 800-plus workforce and imposed four per cent pay cuts on the rest. It removed a layer of management, closed volume manufacturing at its Coventry plant and changed its emphasis to large stampings and complex assemblies. And, leaving no economic stone unturned, management and staff at the Shrewsbury headquarters agreed to clean their own offices, saving a £60,000 annual outlay on contract cleaners.

The austerity measures paid off and the company survived the recession to win a string of new contracts with Jaguar Land Rover including parts supplies for the Range Rover and Range Rover Sport. It has built up a small niche of strong and stable suppliers to support it into the future, as have most automotive OEMs.

## FACE TO FACE

The firm has now built a £650,000 extension at its Castle Bromwich factory to accommodate the extra work (2300sqm) and has increased its UK workforce. Its employee levels are back up to pre-recession levels.

## PRODUCT AND PROCESS INNOVATION

The restructuring has allowed the company to focus on flexible manufacturing systems, on lightweight technology using aluminium (a process which it is pioneering) and flexible assembly, giving a lower investment cost to manufacturers so they can produce a variety of different vehicles from the same platform. It believes in the concept of collaborative research and development, working closely with suppliers such as TATA Steel and Arcelor Mittal.

It predicts that its use of aluminium will rise sharply (up to 80% of production eventually) as European automotive demands increase for lighter weight materials with increased performance and trends move more towards electric and hybrid vehicle technology. It is collaborating on a process for R&D Warm Forming Aluminium Forming Technology (WAFT) which requires fewer stampings and is more flexible in approach. In 2012, it will set up a pilot facility to make trial parts using this process. Stadco currently produces parts for the third generation Ford Focus at its Saarlouis plant, using a high degree of automation.

“If the cells are flexible and can be used to make lots of different products, that is more cost effective,” explained programme director, Chris Fisher, who masterminded the Telford expansion and refurbishment.

The company has also investigated various ways to save on energy costs, using techniques such as efficient joining methods. Another area of interest is mechanical servo press technology which may attract future investment from Stadco. Smart tool design, to take the weight out of a tool, and a hot stamped boron steel process is also under investigation.

Welding has also hit the spotlight at Stadco. The company is examining an R&D Remote Fibre Laser Welding (RFLW) project – the process is six times faster than spot welding and ten times faster than fastening processes, it told ISMR.

Stadco also showcased an innovative new low volume chassis manufacturing process at Goodwood Festival of Speed, in conjunction with UK sports car manufacturer Zolfe, CENEX (the centre of excellence for low carbon technologies) and the Niche Vehicle Network. ## AN EYE ON THE FUTURE

Looking to the future, Stadco has streamlined and focused its business model. Core strengths in the production of aluminium and steel stamping and assemblies will drive future business while product and process development will in turn be driven by demand for lighter weight architectures to support fuel efficiency.

The company intends to become a centre of excellence in the use of lightweight materials. In the third quarter of 2010, Stadco initiated Project Dover to strengthen its leading position in the UK. The focus has been on continuous improvement with employee development playing a large part. 300 employees are being trained through adult apprenticeships to feed into a Business Improvement programme 2011 – 2013. Stadco is also taking on new apprentices this year.

“We forecast a turnover of around 250 million euros in 2011. Stadco Automotive group is in prime position to take advantage of increases in production as economic recovery continues, with the establishment of an entirely automotive-focused parent company, new banking facilities secured until mid-2012 and transfer of liability for the group’s pension scheme, which together will return the UK business to acceptable profit levels during 2011,” the company told ISMR. Its management team is bullish about the future.

“We are very excited and optimistic about Stadco’s future,” commented Managing Director, Dermot Sterne. “After having had to react to the impact of the industry downturn, then consolidate and restructure our business, we are now well placed to take advantage of the increase in economic activity. The new facility will provide the company with a further platform needed to meet the future requirements of our customers for years to come.” ■
