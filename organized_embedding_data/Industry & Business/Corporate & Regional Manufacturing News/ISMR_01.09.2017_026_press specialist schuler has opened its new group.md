# Press specialist, Schuler, has opened its new Group headquarters and delivered some innovative stamping technologies to customers.

[TARİH: 01.09.2017 ISMR]

Stamping specialist Schuler officially inaugurated its new Group headquarters, the Schuler Innovation Tower (S. I. T.), on 18 August 2017. The twelve-story engineering and technology centre will provide 750 workspaces with the latest technology, communications and a company restaurant for Schuler employees. It is the largest investment in the company's history.

Schuler offers presses, automation solutions, tools, process expertise and service for the metalworking industry and lightweight automobile construction. Customers include automotive manufacturers and suppliers as well as companies from the forging, household appliance, packaging, energy and electronics industries. Schuler is also a specialist in coinminting presses and implements system solutions for the aerospace, rail transport and large-dimension pipe manufacturing sectors.

Schuler's CEO, Stefan Klebert, explained that, from its base in the Schuler Innovation Tower, the company "will continue to drive the future topics of plant and machine engineering, the digitisation of products and company processes, and the field of electromobility."

Work on the interior fittings of Göppingen's new landmark building, as well as calibration and test runs for its supply systems and amenities (such as the air conditioning system), will be completed in late Summer and Autumn. After that, staff can begin moving into the Group's new headquarters. The dismantling of Schuler's old Engineering Building and the creation of new outdoor facilities is due to be completed in 2018.

## Borgward orders servo press line

The traditional German car brand, Borgward, has now been successfully launched into the Chinese market with its innovative BX7 model. To increase production capacity, Borgward Automotive ordered an XL servo press line from Schuler at the end of last year.

From August 2018 onwards, the fivestation servo press line based in Beijing-Miyun, will produce body panels for the BX7, BX5 and BX6 models. The line with a press force of 67,000 kilonewtons comprises five presses and is fully supplied by Schuler, including corresponding automation.

The line features ServoDirect technology and energy efficiency. The latest energy management for the main drives is just as important a requirement when it comes to designing the hydraulic drawing cushion in the first press with triple action cylinders.

Up to 16 large parts per minute are produced by the Servoline 16 while double and quadruple parts can be manufactured at a higher batch quantity. The simulation program, which is also included within the scope of the order, ensures that operators are provided with the right alignment of all movements of single presses and automation, including de-stackers, Crossbar Feeders for part transport and an exit conveyor. Rapid, fully automated replacement of dies and tooling ensures the flexibility needed for fast product changes, Schuler told ISMR.

Except for a few special components, Schuler manufactures and assembles the modules for the line – beds, uprights, slides and crowns – entirely in the Chinese production facility in Dalian. Borgward's total vehicle production capacity will increase even further, because of its cooperation with Schuler. As such, the overall operative strength of intelligent Factory 4.0 has been considerably increased and supports the production system by offering consumers more intelligent automobiles.

The Borgward Automotive Group, with its extensive product range, was founded in 1919 by Carl Borgward in Bremen, Germany.

"With an export share of more than 60 per cent, Borgward was the third largest automotive manufacturer in Germany. Numerous record-breaking and significant vehicles have been manufactured by Borgward. The German Borgward Automotive Group is based in Stuttgart. Its new 'Resource coupling, Internet release' business model calls for the globalisation of the Borgward structure. In the future, Borgward will be strongly committed to using renewable energy resources and networked cars, while offering consumers intelligent electric vehicles," Schuler told ISMR.

## First spiral pipe produced on Schuler equipment

Experts predict that in North America alone, over 10,000 kilometres of new pipeline will be laid per year by 2020. And most of that will be spiral pipes. Tuberías Procarsa, one of the leading manufacturers of steel pipe in Latin America, is equipped for this trend; the company with headquarters in Monclova (Mexico) has put a spiral pipe plant from Schuler into operation and used it to produce large-diameter pipes.

The offline spiral pipe plant can produce large-diameter pipes with a diameter of 508 to 2,235mm (20 to 88 inches) and a length of 12 to 24.4m. The raw material of up to 25.4mm thick sheet metal band made of high-quality steel (up to X100) comes on a Hot Rolled Coil (HRC). The spiral mill forms the diameter and welds the spiral pipe within the first step by tack welding. In a further process, the final pipes will be welded by several final welding stands with a submerged arc welding process.

"One innovation is the integrated coil preparation stand. It is designed for short changeover times, high occupational safety and an exact 90-degree cut of the beginning of the band. A crane transfer is no longer necessary. All relevant work steps on the spiral mill are automated. Settings are no longer made manually; instead, setting is done directly on the control panel and implemented by servo motors. This results in energy savings of up to 30 per cent and short changeover times," Schuler told ISMR.

With a length of 285m, the pipe plant also includes various equipment for inspecting large-diameter pipes in accordance with the API 5L standard. Ultrasonics, X-rays and water pressure stands are used here. The production capacity of Tuberías Procarsa will be increased by 200,000 tons per year using the Schuler system.

## An eye on forming

At the MF Tokyo exhibition from 12-15 July 2017, Schuler placed an accent on enhanced process reliability and economic efficiency in sheet metal forming technology. The company is strengthening its presence in the Japanese market. At MF Tokyo 2017, it presented solutions to enhance process reliability and economic efficiency in forming technology. In hot stamping, Schuler cooperates with industrial solutions provider, Ritech Japan K.K..

Car manufacturers use more and more hot stamped parts because they offer high strength and low weight.

With its "Smart Press Shop", Schuler demonstrated how networking solutions in forming technology can increase not only process reliability, but also cost-effectiveness in production. Part of the concept is the Smart Assist, an electronic assistant which supports production start-up on transfer presses and increases output. The Service App enables customers to communicate via smartphone with Schuler or assistance at the machine.

Schuler's TwinServo technology enables greater force distribution and reduced deflection. The new drive concept features two decentralised servo motors in the press bed, making it possible to combine load with the same press force, 30 per cent less deflection and active slide parallelism. The new transfer press is much lower than previous models, its footprint is also smaller and the moving bolsters can be placed closer to the machine for die changes. Compared to a conventional servo press, the total space requirement has been reduced by around 30 per cent.

Schuler's PCHflex technology, another one of the company's developments, allows faster cooling of hot stamped parts by distributing the contact pressure more evenly. This can increase output performance significantly and is designed to ensure a more robust process with high component quality. Its EHF ("Efficient Hydraulic Forming") technology, for example, offers energy savings of up to 60 per cent.

Car manufacturers use more and more hot stamped parts because they offer high strength and low weight. Today, hot stamped A- and B-columns, as well as roof frames or connecting plates, can be found in almost every new car. According to experts, the demand will double to 600 million pieces by 2020.

Schuler has also developed laser blanking lines which can be changed instantly to another cutting style at the touch of a button. Programming of the contours is done offline and can be simulated for production optimisation. By dispensing with dependence on heavy dies, laser blanking lines offer users significantly more flexibility, said Schuler. Lines also do not need expensive foundations or a basement.
