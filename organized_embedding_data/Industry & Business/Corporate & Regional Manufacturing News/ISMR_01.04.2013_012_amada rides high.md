# Amada rides high

[TARİH: 01.04.2013 ISMR]

FOLLOWING record-breaking success in 2012, fabrication machinery specialist Amada UK is predicting even greater things for 2013, a year that will see its investment of £5 million in the complete refurbishment of Amada’s UK customer facilities at Kidderminster.

In 2012, the company saw consolidated turnover top £51 million for the first time. The EuroBLECH exhibition in 2012 in Hanover was the launch platform for eleven of its global products. Amongst the new product introductions were the FLW series of robotic fibre laser welding systems; the FLC3015 AJ linear drive fibre laser cutting system and the latest addition to Amada’s punch/laser combination machine range, the ACIES 2515T.

Amada also launched the latest expansion of its press brake range, the high speed EG series with dual AC servoelectric drive ram system, touch-screen control and software systems. The first UK installations of these solutions will take place during the second quarter of 2013.

Investing in future skills was another area of focus at Amada UK last year – the apprenticeship scheme now has 12 engineers and company apprentice, Tom Preen, won the Worcestershire Apprentice of the Year Award 2012 whilst Amada UK became Worcestershire Employer of the Year.

Beyond UK shores, the company also reports impressive levels of success in Spain, a market for which Amada UK took responsibility in 2010. Since the merger, there has been a 30 per cent expansion of the Spanish organisation and turnover has tripled.

This year, however, will see the redevelopment of Amada’s UK customer facilities. Groundbreaking commenced in December 2012 and, when complete, the project will see the existing machine tool plaza updated to become a fully interactive technical centre. Temporarily, the showroom has been relocated to an adjacent building, although Amada still offers 1,200 sq. m. of machinery space for daily demonstrations. This, alongside the company’s network of technical centres in Europe, means it will be business as usual throughout 2013.

“The aim of the refurbishment is to provide an even better experience for Amada customers and visitors, with an expanded solutions showroom, enhanced meeting areas and interactive facilities for software and tooling. Visitors will soon be able to enjoy the very latest in sheet metal technology within a welcoming and comfortable environment that is conducive to knowledge sharing. The renovated facility will be extremely eco-friendly, boasting minimal carbon footprint due to the installation of the latest sustainable technology in lighting, heating and insulation,”

## SIA changes to MEIA

THE Sheet Metal Industry Association of Australia (SIA) has a new look, a new direction and new ideas for how employers, educators, traders, suppliers and regulators can work together to promote the manufacturing and engineering industries within Australia, for the benefit of all stakeholders. It is in the process of changing its name to MEIA (Manufacturing & Engineering Industry Association).

The association promotes innovation in the sheet metal industry. For the past 25 years, it has lobbied educators to stress the importance of keeping up to date with emerging technologies in the sheet metal industry. ■

Thalmann has become a pioneer in long-length folding machine construction

Thalmann Maschinenbau AG is a well-known specialist manufacturer of folders that can bend sheet metal of up to 20m. It started in 1948 as a manufacturer of very long folders and has developed a reputation for reliable, high-end bending solutions, selling to customers such as Mercedes-Benz.

Based in Frauenfeld, Switzerland, Thalmann Maschinenbau AG specialises in sheet metal folding. Introduced to the market in 1960, the first hand-operated machines were followed by hydraulically-driven and combined machines for cutting and bending sheet metal.

Today, the company offers a comprehensive range of standard and special machines for forming sheet metal up to 18.2m long and with a bending capacity for sheet steel of up to 3mm in thickness (400 N/mm²).

Thalmann has become a pioneer in long-length folding machine construction, known worldwide for the quality and longevity of its machine tools with awards such as the Bavarian State Gold Medal for workmanship. The company continues to develop new products as well as refine its existing products with the aim of simplifying working practices in sheet metalworking plants and industries.

## Meeting market needs

“We have a unique construction principle comparable to a four-wheel drive on a car. The machine stands are connected by a torsion shaft, like a car is connected by a driveshaft, to synchronise movements for better performance,” explained Marco Cappello, Thalmann’s general manager.

“In terms of sustainable processes, our torsion shaft principle is the basis for a very green system. One hydraulic pump system drives the shafts that drive all the machine stands. This torsion shaft technology combines the power of all the machine stands on the clamp beam, folding beam and crank resulting

in unmatched parallelism over the whole machine length. Our bending machines consequently consume very low power and can run for 30 years or so with no problems – a machine we built in 1960 is still running with a customer."

This drive shaft technology, which is a tried and tested concept in the aircraft industry, contributes to the overall quality and longevity of the bending machines. The uniform distribution of force along the entire bearing length ensures a balanced load of all axes. Excessive wearing of individual axes is therefore prevented and the overall durability of the machine is increased.

Cappello is optimistic on sheet metal construction in key markets such as construction, where Thalmann bends metal for solar panels, flashing and trims, facades, roofing and general construction. The company also serves industrial customers e.g. truck freight and panel makers for motorway noise reduction (6m parts with one operator on a double folder). Customers, he says, look for speed, accuracy, high productivity and user-friendly operation at lower cost from their bending machines.

“Although markets have slowed down in parts of Europe, particularly South Europe, fortunately, we have countries like Austria, Germany and Switzerland who still have good domestic economies. We have had to look at markets outside Europe so we are now looking at countries such as Russia and India. There are a lot of companies in India that are setting up their infrastructure and machinery based on sustainable investment and that need high-level technologies so adopt that philosophy. We see India as a strong market for us far beyond 2013.

“We also have a good business in the US that currently takes up half of our production and we believe that will remain strong throughout 2013. We are also interested in entering the Chinese market, possibly through a cooperative partner,” added Cappello.

Amongst its clientele, Thalmann serves small craft businesses as well as large industrial operations.

“For example, an industrial sheet metal processor uses our double folding machine to produce generator cladding panels, and sound insulation panels for ICE train routes. But a small sheet metal-working company buys the same machine, because they make custom facade panels and need the functionality of the double folder,” added Cappello, pointing out that the Thalmann brand of quality, highperforming machines has meant that price is not so much an issue.

“People may say that our machines are expensive,” he added. “However, to me, expensive means that you spend more than you get back. Our machines are not expensive in that sense – we offer strong return on investment. Yes, our machines cost more than our competitors but they are not expensive, because the productivity returns they offer are far greater. You have to assess any investment based on clear figures.”

## The folding principle

There are fundamental differences between bending sheet metal on a press brake and folding it on a folder. In a press brake, metal is pressed down upon a V-shaped die to make the bend. Folders, on the other hand, do not need tooling or dies as the folding beams perform the bends. There is consequently no time lost on set-up or tooling changes, which translate into significant speed/cost benefits for users.

The process itself is also beneficial – there is no need to handle the material. This is one of the key differences between press brakes and folders. On press brakes, material needs to be handled and sometimes physically turned around either by an operator or a robot. Double folders do not require material handling due to the flexibility of the process and, in general, can be very easy to operate – double folders (up and down folders) are automatic.

“One of the major milestones in the sheet metal folding machine market was the double folder for up and down folding – it virtually cut out the handling and the handling could add a lot of time to the overall process," Cappello explained to ISMR. “At EuroBLECH, we introduced dynamic crowning. “We always aim to further develop our technologies based on customer feedback – the story never ends – in terms of machine capabilities, software, user friendliness and, crucially, safety. Safety is extremely important for us – we design safety into all our equipment. That is our first priority. We are continually refining and developing our products.”

With the development of Thalmann’s first manually-operated, long length bending machine, in 1960, swing-beam bending technology became Thalmann Maschinenbau AG’s area of expertise. Simple manually–operated machines were later followed by hydraulically-driven and combined machines for cutting and bending sheet metal.

In addition to a wide range of standard types of bending machines up to 18.2m in length and with a bending capacity of up to 3mm thick mild steel, it also develops and manufactures special machines, tailor made for customer sheet metal working requirements. It also makes its own controllers and will rebuild and overhaul old machines as well as offering a range of contract work such as robot welding, design, industrial paintwork, machine production etc.

The Swiss company offers a complete series of bending machines such as the THAKO, its flagship folder, which was primarily designed as a long-length folder for facade and roof constructors. Individual selection of beam geometry, gripper systems for material handling, roll-former units as well as an insertion depth of up to 2000mm and machine lengths of up to 18.2 metres are some of the features offered by this model range.

Thalmann’s ZR panel-beating-machine is a newly constructed, sturdy and fully hydraulic swing-folding machine with standard lengths of 3.2m up to 12m; it was developed specifically for sheet metal workshops. This model can be adapted to requirements with a variety of accessories. On the ZR model, the clamp beam and the bending flange are driven over separate camshafts to achieve exact parallel courses of motion.

## Double bending benefits

With the double folding process for sheetmetal forming, the swing-folding process is used to produce edge bends on flat sheet metal and to produce profiles. It is a gentle processing technique in which the bending beam rolls upwards about its pivot point and on the material, without damaging the surface of the sheet. A bending machine usually consists of a bottom beam on which the sheet lies, a top beam that clamps the sheet on the bending edge against the lower beam and a folding beam that is moved upwards and ultimately folds the sheet metal to the required angle.

Thalmann’s type TC and TD double folders are equipped with two folding beams; the sheet can be bent upwards and downwards without needing to be turned or swivelled during the bending process. This speeds up the bending process and increases the machine’s throughput considerably. A total of nine axes must be controlled in the double bender.

Specific features of the double bender facilitate manufacturing of profiles, which is not possible with other machines – the folding beams tilted to 15° directly increase clearance around the bent component and thus provide more flexibility for portable bending solutions. The 10mm folding beams with 10mm removable extra rails now enable narrow Z bends. The automatic gripper system can securely hold all types of sheet from the very narrow 24mm width sheet to the commercial 1250mm width coil.

Sophisticated profiles in façade and roof construction often contain Z-bends. On conventional folding machines, these profiles must be repeatedly rotated and turned during the bending operation. With the double folder, manual handling is a thing of the past.

The sheet metal is held by grippers, which move the profile to its next position. Depending upon requirements, the sheet metal is bent upwards or downwards according to the pre-set angle.

For the TD series, the concept of safety was handled simply so that there could be no manipulation of the safety appliances. A safety light barrier and a laser barrier for the clamping beam and the cutter or roll-forming unit are part of the safety concept. The rule “Safety while Controlling” is given as much importance as the specifications of the prescribed safety equipment from the latest EU machinery directive.

The TC-300 is available in lengths of 1.5 to 12 metres and inlaid depth up to 2000mm and its modular concept enables the construction of even longer machines. This model has a bending capacity of 3mm for sheet steel (400 N/mm2) and 4mm for aluminium (220 N/mm2) respectively.
