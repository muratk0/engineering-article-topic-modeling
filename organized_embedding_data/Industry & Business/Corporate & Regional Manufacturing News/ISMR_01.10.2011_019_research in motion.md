## ARCELORMITTAL

ArcelorMittal’s investments in energy-saving and environmental projects in 2010 increased to US $347 million, from US$ 224 million the previous year. Its commitment to research and development, spending US$ 322 million in 2010, is helping it to reach its goal of reducing emissions by 8 per cent or 170kg per tonne of steel produced by 2020, against a 2007 baseline.

In June this year, ArcelorMittal received the Best Process Innovation award in American Metal Market’s (AMM) 2011 Awards for Steel Excellence held in conjunction with the Steel Success Strategies Conference. The innovation award recognises ArcelorMittal’s ‘S-in Motion’ concept and its efforts in advancing and improving the steel industry through new, sustainable processes.

On 5 November 2010, ArcelorMittal unveiled the results of its ‘S-in Motion’ automotive research programme. Developed through direct technical collaboration with leading partners of automotive manufacturers, the ‘S-in Motion’ portfolio comprises a range of over 60 innovative Press-Hardened Steel (PHS) and Advanced High-Strength Steel (AHSS) solutions that can be implemented in vehicles today, delivering direct benefits in terms of weight, safety and efficiency.

This portfolio includes solutions covering 43 parts of the typical C-segment vehicle. Manufacturers that implement the lightest solution for each component can save up to 73kg or 19 per cent of the typical vehicle’s Body In White (BIW) weight, compared with a baseline C-segment production car.

According to a Life Cycle Analysis (LCA) of the S-in Motion conducted throughout the process, these weight savings have the potential to deliver a 13.5 per cent reduction in CO equivalent emissions during the vehicle's use-phase, without compromising safety, durability or corrosion-resistance. A further 15 per cent reduction in CO equivalent emissions can be obtained during production.

The main objective behind ‘S-in Motion’ is to show that up to 14 per cent of additional weight can be removed from a modern 5-door C-segment vehicle body-in-white using steel grades and manufacturing processes that are available today, resulting in a reduction in CO emissions – all without compromising the strength or safety of the vehicle.

## BAOSTEEL

Recently, GM (Shanghai) issued material approval certificate to Baosteel, that “Baosteel B409M, B439M, B436L stainless steel meets the GM specifications for exhaust system stainless steel, is recognised by General Motors (Shanghai), Pan Asia Technical Automotive Center and is allowed application on GM’s global product.” This is the first time that GM has issued such certificates to the steel supplier on a worldwide level.

As ferritic stainless steel has low thermal expansion coefficient, high corrosion resistance and more economical costs, it is used more widely in automotive exhaust system components. At present, almost all European and Japanese car manufacturers use ultra-purity ferritic stainless steel for exhaust system component manufacturing so as to meet requirements for lightweight vehicles and stringent exhaust emission.

Recently, co-sponsored by Baosteel, the first domestic prefabricated steel civil construction industry technology innovation alliance was founded in Shanghai, with 23 well-known domestic enterprises including Baosteel, universities and research institutions. The Alliance will focus on the priority theme of “construction energy efficiency and green building,” and, with “prefabricated equipment, energy saving” as the guiding ideology, will manufacture prefabricated steel structure civil construction products.

## NIPPON STEEL

Nippon Steel’s environmentally friendly Eco Products are gaining success at various stages. Its high tensile-strength steel sheets (HSS) contribute to the weight saving of vehicles and the reduction of emission gas such as CO2. Its ultra-thin tinplates support the weight saving of cans, and high efficiency magnetic steel sheets are used for home electronics using plates and for hybrid vehicles, contributing to energy saving.

Its SuperDyma coated steel sheet, laminated sheets for can and coated sheets for home electronics (ZINKOTE™ black, etc) have high anti-corrosion performance. Nippon Steel is also engaged in the full-scale spread of steel houses built using the “Nittetsu Super Frame” construction method with light-gauge steel frames. SuperDyma is its new type of highly corrosion-resistant coated steel sheet in which the coating layer is composed of mostly zinc, approx. 11 per cent aluminium, approx. 3 per cent magnesium and a fine amount of silicone.

Nippon Steel’s first Sn-added ferritic stainless steel grade, the “NSSC FW 1”, was awarded the “Nikkei Business Daily Awards for Excellence” of “2010 Nikkei Superior Products and Services Awards”. NSSC FW1 (14%Cr-0.1%Sn) is the first Sn-added high-purity ferritic stainless steel, achieved through revolutionary technology that can dramatically improve the corrosion resistance of ferritic (chromium system) stainless steel (compared to that of TP430 [18%Cr]) through the addition of trace amounts of Sn (tin).
