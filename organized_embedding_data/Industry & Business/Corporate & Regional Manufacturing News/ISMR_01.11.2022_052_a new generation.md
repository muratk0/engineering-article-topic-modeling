# A NEW GENERATION

Boschert celebrated its 75th anniversary last year. Founded in 1946 as a small workshop for the textile industry, today it builds a wide range of sheet-metalworking equipment. This includes notching; punching; small and mid-sized press-brake bending systems; plasma; laser; combined punching/plasma/fibre laser and copper processing machines. It also builds special machines to meet specific customer requirements. The company made a 26-million-euro turnover in 2021.

Fifteen years ago, Boschert established a joint venture company in a new factory in Greece with Gizelis SA. Together, both companies offered a new production range of press brakes and shears to the market. GIZELIS SA, founded in 1968, manufactures machines and solutions for the sheet-metal industry. Last year, it launched two of its latest innovations; a fully automated robotic bending cell, incorporating a flexible and productive ATC (Automatic Tool Change) system, and a versatile stacking system for shears.

Boschert is based in Lörrach (near Freiburg, Germany), employs 120 members of staff and has a production and assembly area of some 8,000 square metres. There are a further 700 square metres of office space, as well as a presentation and demonstration centre. It offers in-house training to all staff. Every year, two to three apprentices complete their industrial training with the company.

## New laser generations

Two of Boschert's latest innovations were on display this October at the EuroBLECH sheet-metal exhibition in Hanover, Germany: the Boschert CombiLaser generation and the Boschert FiberLaser generation. 'Compatibility & Connectivity' with Industry 4.0 protocols were also highlighted on its booth.

The FiberLaser solution is designed for users who want to have immediate access to the cut part and not wait for an entire sheet to be nested and cut. Some customers need that level of flexibility for their own production.

It offers up to 4kW power and is supplied in two sizes: 3015 with a working range of 1,500 x 3,000mm and 4020 with a working range of 2,000 x 4,000mm. The machines can be optionally fitted with a one-, two-, three- or four-kilowatt fibre laser. This flexibility enables users to machine sheets made from structural steel (up to six and ten millimetres), from stainless steel (up to four, six or ten millimetres) and from aluminium in various thicknesses (up to three, four or ten millimetres).

Philipp Schöchlin, Boschert's export manager, on its booth at EuroBLECH 2022.

The trend in manufacturing is to make components that are lighter in weight, use less material, conserve resources and integrate functionality (i.e. thinner sheet structures). Boschert has expanded its "Combi" series of machines to include fibre laser. The CombiLaser series is available with working ranges from 1000 × 2000mm to 1500 x 3000mm. Now, users can produce complex parts on a single machine without repositioning. The Boschert CombiLaser combines the advantages of laser cutting with the unique features of CNC punching.

In the past, complex contours could only be cut on a flat sheet laser. Operations such as forming, beading, trimming and threading required a separate CNC punch. The new CombiLaser combines the best qualities of both operations. Boschert's fibre laser system can be mounted to any of Boschert's standard line of CNC punching machines, which enables users to purchase the machine that best suits their requirements.

## Improved functionalities

ISMR caught up with Philipp Schöchlin, Boschert's export manager, at the EuroBLECH sheet-metal exhibition in Hanover, to discuss the company's latest projects, new technologies and future plans.

"On our stand here at EuroBLECH, apart from the new FiberLaser and CombiLaser, we are also promoting some of our latest technologies. This includes our CuProfi punching machine that now boasts a new Schneider control. We are also showcasing a new real-time automatic angle measurement and correction system on our Gizelis GBend Plus 3140 press brake. You can see systems here with conveyor belt technology, as well as our standard laser and punching machines, along with combination laser/punching technology," Philipp Schöchlin told ISMR.

The company developed a 4020 punching machine last year which can process sheets sizes of up to 4 x 2m. It offers precision punching, nibbling and forming in one heavy-duty-machine. Sheet panels can measure 2000 x 4000/6000mm (4mm stainless steel, 6mm mild steel or 8mm aluminium).

"One of our proudest achievements over the last few years was the development of the 4020. It is the only punching machine in the world able to punch 4 x 2m without repositioning. This helps customers who want to punch large sheets. Many customers would like to process all their metal sheets in one working cycle so the machine features a new tooling system with 12 stations for TRUMPF tooling. With stepless indexable tools, it offers a total of max. 96 tools which is more than enough for most sheet-metalworking operations. It is possible to pre-adjust tooling for different sheets or for partial sheets. This reduces non-productive downtime to a minimum and enables longer machine useful life," explained Schöchlin.

“We are continuing a lot of new and interesting projects this year. It is important that we can offer a whole range of machines to our customers. For example, notching/ punching/laser cutting technology from Boschert; bigger bending /shearing machines from Gizelis but also technology from allied companies such as Picot (rollforming) and Stierli (horizontal bending solutions). We want to grow with our customers on their journey, whatever size of machine they need. No customer is too small for us," he added.

Improving functionality on its machines, in the form of additional options such as welding, milling or threading, smaller/larger tables etc., is a key area for Boschert. It also works on special projects with customers who have specific requirements. Combining forming processes with laser functionality in its combi machines has been a strong growth area for the company, as well as increasing productivity and flexibility for its customers.

## The future in focus

"We sell worldwide but our core market is mid Europe i.e. Austria, Germany, Switzerland and France. The U.S. market is also very important for us. We have 34 dealers around the world and there are always opportunities. I think northern Europe (Norway, Sweden, Denmark and Finland) will become a more important market for us. We see growth potential there. We have invested a lot of time and training in a new dealer network in Scandinavia," explained Philipp Schöchlin.

After FABTECH this year, he confirmed Boschert's presence at some in-house dealer events in Italy and Greece before the company gears up for new product launches at the Hannover fair next year (where it will mainly showcase its CuProfi punching machines and a new copper bar punch system). An in-house event at Boschert's headquarters, possibly at the end of February next year, is still in the planning stages. It will use that event to announce its new machines for next year.

"Looking to the future, we are building the first 4020 combined with a laser. It should be ready at the end of the first quarter of next year. We are also moving forward with our copper bar machines and are producing a new model with different tooling solutions and features which should be ready by the middle of next year," Schöchlin confirmed to ISMR.

“The electrical G-Electrobend press-brake line (also for 3m machines) are more energyefficient than hydraulic machines. We have already developed electrical systems for press brakes and shears. However, we will stay with hydraulic systems for our punching machines because they need to be able to form as well as punch. Punches do not consume huge amounts of energy," he added.

## An eye on automation

Automation is a key trend and target objective for Boschert. The company has set itself a target to provide more automation solutions for its customers, working with partners in various areas. It is also working with partners on the development of Industry-4.0 software for wider machine networking connectivity (for example, to other branded machinery on a customer's site).

"We see a huge trend towards automation e.g. automated loading and unloading systems. We will be taking steps in this area over the next few years and need to speed up development. Our first projects have already started and we are working really hard behind the scenes to stay on track and develop the right solutions," explained Schöchlin.

Labour and skills shortages have brought automation into greater focus across the manufacturing industry.

"People often think that in eastern Europe and Asia, automation is not needed because labour costs are low. However, this is not always the case because even those countries are having problems finding skilled labour for machinery operation so they also need automated systems. Europe has a massive labour shortage issue. We see it too as we are also looking for employees in our workshops. We try to promote apprenticeships to help with this. We generally have between ten and fifteen people on three-year apprenticeships at Boschert," Schöchlin told ISMR.

At the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart (October 2021), Boschert and Gizelis launched two innovations: a fully automated robotic bending cell, incorporating a flexible and productive ATC (Automatic Tool Change) system, and a versatile stacking system for shears.

The Gizelis ROBOBEND 2 fully automated robotic bending cell incorporates Automatic Tool Change (ATC) with bending and palletising procedures as a turnkey solution.

“The system has a smart design and can be operated in three different modes. Individual safety systems are activated in each working mode in full compliance with EU directives. There is large capacity for tooling (punches and dies) which can be safely organised in a robust, smart tooling warehouse, able to store more than 20m of punches and dies (including tooling in very small segments for bending small/complex details)," explained Gizelis.

## Challenges and opportunities

“We have some challenging years ahead of us to increase Boschert's market share as well as the capabilities of our machines. Having navigated the worst years of the COVID-19 pandemic, we are now also facing supply-chain disruption from the war in Ukraine. This is now really affecting economies in producing countries, especially re the availability of materials. We are talking about 6-10 months for some deliveries. The industry has also been hit with price increases for raw materials (steel, copper and aluminium) and also rising energy costs," outlined Philipp Schöchlin.
