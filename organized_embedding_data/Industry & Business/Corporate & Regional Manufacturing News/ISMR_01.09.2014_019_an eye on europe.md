# An eye on Europe

[TARİH: 01.09.2014 ISMR]

Despite tough market conditions in Europe, Amada has made considerable investments in its European subsidiaries. This year, it unveiled its new interactive 9,500m² Northern European Technical Centre, as part of a complete €6 million refurbishment of its customer facilities based in Kidderminster, near Birmingham (UK). ISMR toured the facility and viewed its latest sheet metal machine tool technologies.

Today, Amada Group has more than 20 organisations in Europe for R&D, manufacturing, sales and service. However, its first European subsidiary was Amada UK which opened in 1972. Amada UK employs over 200 people and has evolved from a machine sales and service company to an engineering solutions provider for the UK, Spain and Scandinavia.

The Amada Innovation Fair 2013 Europe, Amada’s largest private event in Europe, was held for five days in September last year at the Technical Centre of Amada S.A. in the suburbs of Paris, France. Approximately 1,400 visitors and 700 companies attended from countries such as Europe, Russia, Turkey etc. ISMR also flew over to see the latest technologies and processes from the Japanese sheet metal machine tool specialist. At the event, Mr Okamoto stated that the aim was to reinforce the Amada brand and double its market share in Europe.

“Fujinomiya Works in Japan is the main factory for whole Amada group of companies. Our French factory is the second largest in the Amada group. We are thinking about growing business in Europe so we expect Amada Europe SA in France to eventually provide over 90 per cent of the Amada machine tools needed for the European market. Last September, at the Amada Innovation Fair in Paris, we announced a new 50 million euro investment in our French factory and two French government ministers visited our show,” he told ISMR.

Mr Okamoto also opened a Technical Centre in Landshut, in the suburbs of the southern German city of Munich, on 7 June 2013, which will serve as a new hub for the company. It will serve as a core base from which Amada can carry out its activities, not only in southern Germany but also in the neighbouring countries of eastern Europe. Going forward, the company intends to have major satellite hubs not just in Southern Germany, but also in Austria, Switzerland and the Czech Republic. It recently opened a new hub in Poland which will also serve countries in eastern Europe.

“We have a policy for overseas manufacturing/production – our motto is ‘Made by locals to Japanese quality’. We have two big factories in France and try to implement this policy in our northern France factory. Currently, we are producing more than 100 types of machine tools but we never produce this same line-up in overseas countries. About 80 per cent of our European sales are from this factory in France. Around 17 per cent of our business and 10 per cent of our market share is in Europe. Competition is very tough in Europe but we have a long history there – mainly through our factories in the UK, Germany, France and Italy,” Mr Okamoto outlined.

“In eastern Europe, Russia and Scandinavia we mainly use distributors or agents but we would eventually like to turn these into direct sales service offices. We have already done this in Poland, for example. One reason for establishing an Amada office in Poland was to use it as a base for the surrounding area, including other eastern European countries. We needed to be able to display machines there, send engineers over and invest in the country.”

Ultimately, Mr Okamoto explained, Amada will continue to reinvent itself to meet the challenges of a changing global market and continue to support, and grow with, its customers across the world. ■

## AMADA CORPORATE MILESTONES

1948 – Amada Manufactures, a limited partnership, is founded

1955 – Completion of first contour saw (vertical band saw) and becomes leading band saw manufacturer

1964 – Company name change to Amada Co., Ltd

1969 – Headquarters re-location

1971 – Established US Amada Ltd in Seattle, WA. Developed NC Turret Punch Press

1972 – Established Amada UK Ltd in the UK and Amada GmbH in Germany

1978 – Formed a business tie-up with WASINO-MACHINE CO. Ltd and delegated mechanical press operations

1982 – Amada Co. Ltd achieved net sales of 100 billion yen

1983 – Established Amada METRECS Co., Ltd service company for Amada products

1986 – Established Amada SA (France) and Amada Austria GmbH and began local production in Europe

1987 – Created the Amada Foundation for Metal Work Technology

1992 – Completed construction of Soft Studio House, the new Amada Machine Tool Plaza

1993 – A sales service subsidiary was set up in Beijing

1996 – A sales service subsidiary was set up in Shanghai. Production of turret punch presses and bending machines started in Shanghai

1998 – 20 companies in the Amada Group obtain ISO 14001 certification

2001 – A sales service subsidiary was set up in Shenzhen, China

2007 – Established the Development Centre and the laser-dedicated factory at Fujinomiya Works

2011 – Kansai Technical Centre and Toki Works opened

2012 – Set-up of Amada (China), the regional headquarters company

2013 – Opening of the new European headquarters in France, Shanghai facility in China and Technical Centre in Austria.

2014 – Unveiling of new interactive 9,500m² Northern European Technical Centre in the UK and opening of Amada Poland. Acquisition of MIYACHI TEKNOS to create Amada MIYACHI
