# PARTNERS IN PROGRESS

[TARİH: 01.11.2018 ISMR]

## Thalmann, CIDAN and Forstner have developed a successful partnership that is delivering value for the companies and their customers.

Thalmann Maschinenbau AG can trace its history back to the one-man business founded by Otto Thalmann in 1948. As a pioneer in long-length bending machine construction, the Swiss innovator has been awarded with the Bavarian State Gold Medal for excellent workmanship. It is constantly innovating with new products and technologies aimed at simplifying working practices in sheet metalworking plants and industries.

CIDAN Machinery Sweden AB, a 111 year-old sheet metal machinery provider of folders, cut-to-length lines and shears, has partnered very effectively with Thalmann over the years to offer a completely integrated sheet metal bending portfolio. It acquired 100% of the shares in Austrian Forstner Maschinenbau GmbH in December 2017. Forstner produces a broad portfolio of coil processing machines for the industrial roofer and architectural sheet metal market.

ISMR SAYS:

“Thalmann's and Cidan's CEOs explain how their partnership has successfully evolved and where they are positioning themselves for future growth”

ISMR sat down with Thalmann's and Cidan's CEOs at this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover to find out how their partnership has successfully evolved and where they are positioning themselves for future growth.

## An eye on folding

"Thalmann long folders offer innovative technology that set new standards in performance and productivity. Clear competitive advantages from significant cost and time savings are provided through maximum processing speed, a wide range of application possibilities, optimised ergonomics and high folding precision," Marco Cappello, Thalmann's CEO, told ISMR.

"We are showcasing a fully equipped folder here on our booth. The new geometry allows the customer to bend parts with a ratio of height to depth of 2 x 1 – this is unique. It also features a new hydraulic system and a new drive system which allow him to run all kinds of part scenarios (two parts tapered or three parts at the same time – it is extremely flexible)."

CIDAN Machinery Group exhibited its FORMA Z, a newly developed Up/down folder. FORMA Z is developed and manufactured equipped with two different high divided tools simultaneously. The AGS back gauge can be equipped with height adjustment and vacuum suction cups for semiautomated operation. It also showcased a newly developed industrial slitting line from Forstner in Austria, now a member of the CIDAN Machinery Group.

"The Cidan booth is showcasing two new folders, the new K25 model (we produced over 3000 units of the previous version) and the new Up Down machine. For the Forstner lines, we are displaying new programmable slitters and a new shear, which we produce ourselves. You will notice more and more automation on the booth. In the programmable slitter, you can more or less operate 'automated' from coil to the finished piece," explained Peter Tafazoli, CEO of the CIDAN Machinery Group.

## Strong partnerships

"Our acquisition of Forstner has been a real success. We have been collaborating with Forstner for the past seven years. It fits us perfectly, with its complementary products, and we completed the acquisition in December 2017. It also fits well with Thalmann's folding portfolio – we have sold different combinations of all three brands very successfully. For example, we sold eight Thalmann folders to one customer over three years and are following it up this year with eight Forstner lines to the same customer," continued Peter Tafazoli.

"We have registered 40% growth levels, including Forstner, over the last three quarters. 2018 has been a very successful year for us, with the acquisition. We also have a great partnership with Thalmann and work extremely well together. We have made almost five times our turnover since 2011, organically and with one acquisition plus sales of Thalmann products. All three brands are collaborating on software and IT that will network the systems. The new software system, that interlinks and networks all the systems, is called NU IT. It is up and running here on our booth."

Marco Cappello agrees that such close collaboration has benefited all three companies, explaining to ISMR how it has also added major value for customers downstream.

CIDAN Machinery Group exhibited its FORMA Z, a newly developed Up/down folder.

Peter Tafazoli (left) and Marco Cappello at EuroBLECH standing in front of a Thalmann Double Folder.

"We can show the synergy of our business units. I think we have made small steps together which, added together, lead to major value for the customer i.e. on tool geometry and improvements on the back gauge and handling systems – we have also further improved the speed of our machines. We have also been working on making them safer and more efficient, with an eye on energy efficiency and improved software. Packaged up, this gives the customer real value and a better return on investment. We look at the product philosophy with Cidan - as a team, we really cover the entire production chain from coil to final product. That synergy effect is very strong, and it is linked by overarching, networked software. Much of our success has come from joining forces as partners in progress," he told ISMR.

Marco Cappello confirmed that Thalmann's turnover has significantly increased over the year before - a consistent increase over the past few years. This is mainly due to increased demand for its double folder units.

"We have been able to sell machinery to new regions and new customers (in New Zealand, Greece, South Korea, for example). The folder we sold to our Greek steel construction customer, to bend static parts, was one of the largest that we had ever built. We were really proud of it. The customer has been able to triple his output with this machine, compared to his former press brake output (folding 3mm high-tensile steel)," he explained.

## Networked production

Industry 4.0 plays a key part in this process chain, feeding data and analytics back to customers on every part of the process. The three brands can be networked together in a production environment, which was the subject of a networked customer project recently.

All three brands were recently linked in a project for a customer in Austria - his automated production line featured a Forstner coil coupled with two CIDAN solutions that linked directly to a Thalmann folder (via NU IT software) for a ready product (trim parts). This live production area relies heavily on Industry 4.0 principles.

"The challenge is to get all components into production together. It's not so much on a manufacturing level but in the preparation, planning and the data. From the time that a customer order is processed to production of the final product, we see major potential to save time and money on all sorts of levels and enhance the quality of the data throughout the process. It is all about Industry 4.0 and, more particularly, data analytics plus shorter processing times. There is now more understanding of what Industry 4.0 means for products," explained Peter Tafazoli.

## Future focus

Thalmann is currently working on integrating more automation. Peripheral devices, that can feed material to the machine or manipulate/handle parts, are becoming more popular. It aims to demonstrate this capability in two years. It also wants to continue combining its folders with other equipment in a production line (i.e. CIDAN and Forstner). IT is also a spotlight for the company.

"Service is a key focus for us now as the machines have become more complex. Customers want someone to consult if something happens. We also want to satisfy high market demand on volume. It is a risk to lose orders through delivery times, but we must also operate in a sustainable way. We need to be flexible, especially if there is a slowdown. We also want to keep our focus on solutions and innovations. There are still skills shortages in the market. Project solutions are also important - the value perception is different because a project is more sustainable," Cappello told ISMR.

Peter Tafazoli agreed with this assessment. "That is the same for us," he added. "Strategically, we want to strengthen our sales networks with our own staff and dealers. Customers want solutions now, not just products. We are in the process of merging the internal Forstner and CIDAN sales team into one team – that is a big strategic focus for us. We are also getting more into selling projects, integrated with software, with open interfaces from our machines to others. We are selling as much as we produce now, but we do expect a natural slowdown in growth in 12-24 months."
