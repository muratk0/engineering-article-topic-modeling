# THE KEY TO INNOVATION

[TARİH: 01.12.2010 ISMR]

The European Research Association for Sheet Metal Working (EFB) is a non-profit industry organisation with around 200 member companies and research institutes from 10 European countries. The purpose of the Research Society is to promote applied research in the field of metal processing and support rapid implementation of research results in the industry.

EFB was founded in 1949 as a Research Association for Sheet Metal Working in Germany. Its main tasks are to apply for funding through the Federation of Industrial Research Associations (AiF) and other public funders, manage the allocated funds, set up Working Groups to support ongoing projects, initiate new research based on frequently developed technology roadmaps and publish research results. It also runs seminars and internships to support the rapid implementation of research results in companies.

What does this mean for sheet metal markets in Europe? ISMR spoke to its managing director, Dr-Ing. Norbert Wellmann.

ISMR: What are your thoughts on current European markets for sheet metal machine tool manufacturers, metal formers and fabricators in the wake of the global economic downturn? How do you see European markets developing over the next six months? Will there be any specific regional pockets of growth?

NW: Europe has come through quite a difficult economic time. Unfortunately, economic markets are influenced by financial and psychological factors and the last few months have been characterised by severe financial problems in some European countries. The full impact from this is not yet known but, in the near future, we see industry sector markets needing more investment.

On a global, worldwide base, there are very stable market developments i.e. in BRIC countries and specifically in China. In Europe, and Germany in particular, a large portion of production volume is exported to these rapidly growing emerging markets. We see a continuous growth path, with much more growth potential, in these emerging markets. However, financial and psychological factors will continue to affect the market for the next few years.

I do see a constant path and moderate growth for European sheet metal markets. As far as the next six months are concerned, I do not see this changing for highly industrialised countries in Europe in terms of production. Germany, France and Italy seem to be on a stable path in Europe. Markets in Britain, Spain, Portugal, Poland, Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia will be affected by that. Over the next 12 months, in terms of real production output, I do not see a marked impact or slowdown as delivery times are already up to two years.

ISMR: What are the main concerns of your members at the moment? How are you helping to alleviate those concerns?

NW: The major concerns of our members at the moment are technology drivers – they have superb lead times and are highly innovative. They are concerned as to how they can best meet their customers’ needs.

ISMR: Please outline current metal forming and fabricating trends, technologies and research developments in the sheet metal sector.

NW: A common misconception in the sheet metal industry is the image of an old man bashing away at a piece of metal. However, the industry is highly technical, research-oriented and much younger than its image.

Driving this is the automotive sector, which has a staggering market and production volume as well as the money available to fund investment into R&D and new production processes and techniques. One of the keywords for the future is resource efficiency, which you hear every day now. This has been sparked by the limitations of raw materials and energy due to the sharp increase in goods production in new, emerging economies. The world population is growing and more countries are trying to produce quality products and goods.

There are also many new opportunities. Electrification is one of the new trends– environmentally friendly cars for the future. For the sheet metal industry, that means new products for car bodies, batteries and fuel cells. Crucially, it also means lightweight components to reduce CO emissions and fuel consumption. There are many initiatives in process, by automotive manufacturers such as Jaguar and Audi, to lighten material loads or use alternative materials. We have already reached the limits of 'lightweight' steel and aluminium – the next step must be to look at other materials.

Plastic is really the material of the future. In 20/30 years, plastic will have a dominating impact as a proportion of all materials used in a vehicle. It will not just be in the body shell, but also the in the structure of the body, more parts in the engine drive train and new integrated solutions for the wiring etc. Material design will be dominated by plastic. The car of the future will be a very different animal.

ISMR: Please give us some background history on EFB. Who are its members, how many do you have and how do you help them? Please also outline the benefits of membership.

NW: EFB started in 1949 – we were founded just after World War 2 to start initiatives on rebuilding Germany. People did not only want to continue with existing old production processes – they wanted better, more innovative and capable processes in the sheet metal forming sector. They believed that an association run by the industry that could drive technology research and innovation in Germany would have a great impact on improving processes.

In 1980, we moved from Dusseldorf to Hanover, close to the university which specialised in sheet metal forming. In 1992, we became known as the European (rather than German) Research Association as more European companies had joined our activities and become partners.

We currently have over 200 members – innovative companies and research institutes. On average every year, we cooperate with around 250 companies and 50 institutes on research projects. The institutes receive funding through us for sheet metal research work. More than 10% of our members are from outside Germany. All the project work is done and research is published in German although you will find English summaries on our website.

The advantage of membership is that companies have automatic insight and participation in all Working Groups and research topics in the sheet metal technology chain. They can participate in projects, network and receive subsidies for research activities. Discounts are given for event participation and on EFB literature. The main advantage for long-term members is that they are involved in our roadmap activities and trend workshops, identifying technology and industry drivers. Kernel groups, such as these, gain valuable insight into the strategic plans of other partners if they are working together on product development for real products and processes.
