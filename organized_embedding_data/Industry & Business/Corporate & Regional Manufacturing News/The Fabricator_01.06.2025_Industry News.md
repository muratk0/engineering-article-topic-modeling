AMADA AMERICA pledges support to sustain the SparkForce mission

The Fabricators and Manufacturers Association and SparkForce, The FMA Foundation, have announced that AMADA AMERICA INC., Buena Park, Calif., has pledged $50,000 to kickstart the foundation’s Spark the Movement fundraising campaign. This groundbreaking gift will be directed to the foundation’s operating endowment to ensure that SparkForce’s programs will continue to expand and help fulfill its mission to attract, develop, and engage a robust manufacturing workforce for generations to come.

"Establishing the next generation of metalworking professionals is essential to our industry. AMADA is proud to assist SparkForce in its objective to ensure that this happens. We consider our support a crucial investment in the future of sheet metal fabrication. Thank you, SparkForce, for the work that you do," said Mike Guerin, CEO of AMADA AMERICA INC.

"We are immensely grateful to AMADA for their incredible support and trust in FMA and the SparkForce Foundation," said FMA President/CEO Ed Youdell. "As the initial pledge to launch our campaign, AMADA continues to raise the charitable giving bar for companies serving the metal fabrication industry. This pledge helps us secure the long-term sustainability of SparkForce as we are committed to growing our scholarship and manufacturing camp programs to engage thousands more young people."

In addition to its campaign pledge, AMADA, a manufacturer of sheet metal fabricating and automation systems, also is the diamond presenting sponsor of the SparkForce Awards Gala to be held Oct. 23, 2025, in Mount Prospect, Ill. AMADA has been the presenting sponsor of the foundation’s annual gala since the first event in 2019. To learn more, visit

Baileigh Metal Shaping Fest draws nationwide crowd

Instructor Mike Wagner (right) of Cornfield Customs, Milford, Ohio, watches as student Brad Anderson, Willmar, Minn., works a piece of sheet metal on a power hammer.

If you want to gauge the general interest level in a classroom, just watch the students in the back. If they’re locked in, something good is happening—like at this year’s Baileigh Metal Shaping Fest.

Drawing students from as far away as Idaho and Florida to Baileigh’s plant in Manitowoc, Wis., the three-day festival gave attendees the chance to learn (and practice) stretching and shrinking metal on the company’s bead rollers, power hammers, planishing hammers, and English wheels, among other machines. Today, instructors included industry veteran Chris Rusch, inventor of many of the machines shown at the festival; Mike Wagner, longtime metal former and owner of Milford, Ohio-based Cornfield Customs; and Nick Cologero, custom motorcycle and metal forming expert of Catskill Mountain Customs in Olivebridge, N.Y.

Shane Henderson, who helped start the classes that became Metal Shaping Fest around 2012, said the company once offered a day of training with Rusch to anyone who bought a new MH-19, the popular Baileigh power hammer that Rusch invented. The event spun up from there.

"So, people started buying power hammers and they would fly into Wisconsin," Henderson recalled. "Chris would spend a day with them. Then another guy would buy a power hammer and spend a day with Chris. After four or five of these, Chris was like, ‘Whoa, whoa, whoa. I’m way too busy to spend an entire day with somebody every time you sell a power hammer. Why don’t we just sell a bunch of power hammers and create a list?’

"These light bulbs started going off and it was like, ‘Why don’t we just create our own network of metal shapers? Why don’t we just do our own thing and have metal shaping classes?’ So, I put together the first Baileigh Metal Shaping Class at Baileigh’s manufacturing facility in Two Rivers, Wis. People flew in and drove in from all over the country."

That first event drew about 30 students and was offered for free. Today, some of the 31 attendees (who paid $1,000 apiece to be there) were hobbyists interested in learning more about the trade. Others, like Joe Baune, were professionals looking to sharpen skills that they can use in their shops right away.

"I learned some stuff on the bead roller and the planishing hammer," said Baune, a fabricator from Viola, Idaho, who works at a shop that makes custom chassis for hot rods. "It’s all very interesting … to learn how to use the English wheel and stuff like that to get a nice big tub [chassis structure] to put in a car."

English wheels—those simple, ubiquitous machines that stretch sheet metal through manual pushing and pulling between a fixed upper roll and pressurized lower roll—have given countless metal junkies their first taste of metal shaping. One of them is Cologero, who started his company in 2005 and today was teaching students how to form parts on an MH-19. After meeting Wagner many years ago and using his MH-19, Cologero was hooked on what the power hammer could do. Then, through a dealer, he actually found and bought one of Rusch’s original MH-19 prototypes for himself.

SMX Industrial Solutions acquires Preston Eastin

SMX Industrial Solutions, a family of companies providing metal fabrication and welding systems, has announced the acquisition of Preston Eastin, a manufacturer of robotic and welding positioning systems.

The addition of Preston Eastin’s positioning systems to SMX Industrial Solutions’ existing offerings through Steelmax Tools and Scotchman Industries will provide customers with an expanded line of products to meet diverse industrial needs.

Preston Eastin’s operations will continue to be led by Gary Gamino at its current Tulsa, Okla., location.

Fluid Cooling Systems to expand in Michigan

Fluid Cooling Systems plans to expand and update its facility in Burton, Mich., including purchasing new machinery, overhead cranes, and welding equipment. The investment of more than $2.5 million is expected to create 82 jobs.

The company engineers and manufactures industrial, commercial, and HVAC products, including heat transfer, fluid handling, and process cooling. It serves such industries as automotive, data centers, crypto miners, pharmaceutical, oil and gas, and power.

Guardian Bikes to open bike frame manufacturing plant in Indiana

Guardian Bikes, a direct-to-consumer kids’ bike brand, has announced plans to build a largescale bicycle frame manufacturing plant in its advanced manufacturing hub in Seymour, Ind. According to the company, the $19 million facility is a first step in reshoring the industry, which has been nearly entirely offshored for decades.

Using robotic welding, laser cutting, and vertically integrated logistics, the new plant will produce bicycle frames at scale using domestic steel and aluminum.

The manufacturer reports that the goal of this expansion is to catalyze a full-scale domestic bicycle supply chain, from handlebars to hubs, anchored in the Midwest. The company has distribution centers in California, Pennsylvania, and Indiana, with additional locations planned for Texas and Florida.

New-Form Tools expands with NFT Saws Mexico

New-Form Tools, Stratford, Ontario, has opened NFT Saws Mexico in Querétaro. The new company provides direct access to carbide-tipped circular saw blades, as well as on-site saw blade technical support and training.

Local inventory and stocking agreements will help reduce downtime and prevent supply shortages for customers in Mexico, the company reports. Support includes assistance with blade selection, troubleshooting, and cutting process optimization.

The blades are designed for use with many saw brands, including Rattunde, RSA, Sinico, Bewo, AMADA, Oto Mills, MTM, Olimpia, and Adda Fer.

Fayat Group plans $13.7 million expansion in South Carolina

Fayat Group will expand its Road Equipment Division operations in Ridgeway, S.C. The $13.7 million investment will create 75 jobs.

The new 100,000-sq.-ft. parts distribution facility will help improve the company’s aftersales support for customers throughout North America. The expansion also will allow the manufacturer to increase its localization of production for road machinery at an existing facility in Fairfield County. Opsun Corp. to build first U.S. manufacturing facility

Opsun Corp., a Quebec City, Quebec, manufacturer of structures for solar panels, will invest $9.3 million to build its first U.S. production facility in High Point, N.C. The project will create 20 jobs.

As the company’s third manufacturing site, the new facility will help increase efficiency, add more warehouse space, and triple the footprint of the current operations.

Lantek, Zinser partner to help optimize bevel cutting

Sheet metal software provider Lantek and Zinser, a manufacturer of sheet metal and pipe cutting systems, have collaborated on a system for bevel cutting precut parts.

Zinser’s dedicated bevel cutting machines use optical cameras, contact sensors, and multipoint detection to identify the geometry and orientation of parts transferred from a high-production cutting machine to help optimize bevel cutting.

Through a postprocessor custom developed for the bevel cutting machines, Lantek’s software uses sensory data to calculate each part’s position and orientation, automatically adjusting the cutting parameters.

The software helps users manage bevel cutting more efficiently, especially for parts with complex geometries. Separating this process from the initial cutting helps smooth production flow and reduce downtime, the company reports.
