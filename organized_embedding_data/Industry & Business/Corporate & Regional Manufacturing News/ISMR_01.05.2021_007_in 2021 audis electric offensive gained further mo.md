# In 2021, Audi's electric offensive gained further momentum with the recent launch of the Audi Q4 e-tron. Its toolmaking team translated the designers' visions into reality.

[TARİH: 01.05.2021 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS: “Toolmaking translates the emotional design of the Audi Q4 e-tron into sheet metal."

Catching quattro blisters - with its Q4 e-tron, Audi is taking the next step in the evolution of its electric models' design language. The compact SUV brings the lines that the Audi Q4 concept demo car introduced at the beginning of 2019 to a production model. The world premiere of the Audi Q4 e-tron took place on 14 April 2021.

"In 2021, the electric offensive is gaining further momentum at Audi. For the first time, more than half of all models newly launched in one year are electric. In this way, we are more than doubling the number of our all-electric models, from three to seven," explained Markus Duesmann, CEO of AUDI AG, at the official launch of the Audi Q4 e-tron. "Sustainability is of key importance for us, beyond the power source. That is why we wish to compensate for total CO2 emissions resulting from the manufacture of the Q4 e-tron models. This means that all vehicles are to be delivered to our customers with a neutral carbon balance sheet."

The Q4 e-tron combines a 'sporty and progressive character' with SUV design. The spacious interior provides the option of a large augmented-reality, head-up display for an "innovative driving experience'. Audi's toolmaking team translates the designers' ideas into production and their emotion into sheet metal with precision. Precision down to a thousandth of a millimetre when designing and building the tools is why toolmaking plays such a crucial role in Audi's high standards of quality.

The right tooling

"Whether viewed from the front, side or rear, the Q4 e-tron captivates onlookers from every angle thanks to its expressive SUV design. The side panel of the coupe derivative, which brings the lines of the July 2020 demo car into a production model, is a prime example of just how complex the vehicle is to manufacture," said Audi.

Major challenges come together in a small area at the rear - the D-pillar merges seamlessly with the side edge at the top, while the rear spoiler extends in the opposite direction and, further down, the blister (as Audi refers to the edge above the wheel) extends over the taillight with its tight radii and deep undercut. All of these curves and lines are implemented with strong surface finish and precision.

Audi's toolmaking method planners sit at the table in the early stages of development and review proposals with respect to feasibility. Coordination and development are carried out digitally via computer, in most cases. For the Q4 e-tron, toolmakers from Audi's two German sites divided up the work - the specialists from Ingolstadt were responsible for the side panel, doors and roof, while their colleagues in Neckarsulm were in charge of the engine hood and tailgate.

"Toolmaking is where highly complex functionality meets uncompromising precision and heavy metal. Each of the pressing tools used to produce the side panels is a cast-iron colossus - approximately 4.50 metres long, 2.40 metres wide and 1.35 metres high, weighing up to 47 tons. They are the product of tens of thousands of hours of manual work and contain up to 500 individual parts, from the filling and processing slides weighing several tons to the blank holders and forming jaws to the cutters and punches. A single set of large tools takes several months to assemble," confirmed Audi.

The finished tools are first installed in single-stage tryout presses and then in large transfer press lines — most of them, initially, at the Audi factory in Ingolstadt before being transported to Zwickau, where the Q4 e-tron is manufactured. Numerous optimisation phases lie in between, often involving only a few thousandths of a millimetre.

Does the curvature of the sheet metal part exhibit a tiny sink mark or an unacceptable bulge? Do the parts exhibit any barely discernible thinning or minute wrinkles anywhere after each stage of the production process? Modern optical measuring systems provide exact data - but they still cannot replace the sharp eye of a human being, their sensitive fingertips and their sense of how the sheet metal behaves in the press, commented Audi.

## Mass production

When mass production is up and running, the Zwickau press lines need a total of six production steps to form the side panel, each using a specific tool.

Grippers transfer the component from one tool to the next. The first one draws the sheet into the desired shape and the subsequent tools are used to trim the sheet e.g. in the area surrounding the side windows, to create the fine edges and lines, the assembly holes and the complicated undercuts (folds and shapes on the inside of the sheet metal parts that are necessary for the subsequent joining processes in body construction).

Because the sheet tends to spring back slightly, it is specifically reshaped in each work step. What enters the forming process as a flat blank emerges at the end as a three-dimensional component with a high degree of precision and an 'emotional' design.

Audi's electric offensive continues to pick up steam, with the Q4 e-tron following in the footsteps of the large Audi e-tron and e-tron Sportback SUV models, as well as the high-performance e-tron GT. In Zwickau in Saxony, production of the Audi Q4 e-tron has finally started. The fully electric compact model is the first electric SUV to be produced by Audi in Germany. The Q4 e-tron features a Volkswagen-made modular electric drive matrix (MEB), a highly versatile and variable platform that is suitable for numerous fully electric models and offers considerable scope for leveraging synergies.

At the multi-brand plant in Zwickau, the Audi Q4 e-tron will be rolling off the same production line as the Volkswagen ID.4 and, in the future, another Volkswagen model. Here, 1,400 assembly line employees of Volkswagen Saxony are also producing the compact electric SUV. Working alongside their colleagues from Volkswagen, more than one hundred Audi employees are prepared for the start of production - from production planning and technical development through to quality assurance. The press tools for producing the body parts all come from Audi's own toolmaking shop.

The Audi Q4 e-tron will be launched in European markets in the summer of 2021. As a result, the Audi brand's electric offensive continues to pick up steam. By 2025, Audi plans to launch more than 20 fully electric models and expand its PHEV offering. The two main Audi plants in Ingolstadt and Neckarsulm are also set to manufacture fully electric vehicles.

"The Q4 e-tron plays a central role in the brand's electrification strategy, launching in a particularly attractive and fast-growing market segment, the compact SUV class. For our customers, it offers a competitively priced entry into the world of premium electric mobility," concluded Audi.

INDUSTRY REPORT

## On Strong Foundations

We analyse growth forecasts and key trends in global construction markets.

According to analyst, The Business Research Company, the global construction market reached a value of nearly US$ 12,744.4 billion in 2019, having increased at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 6.1% since 2015.

"The market is expected to decline from US$ 12,744.4 billion in 2019 to US$ 12,633.9 billion in 2020 at a growth rate of -0.9%. The growth decline is mainly due to lockdown and social distancing norms imposed by various countries and economic slowdown across countries owing to the COVID-19 outbreak and the measures to contain it. The market is then expected to recover and grow at a CAGR of 7.5% from 2021 and reach US$ 15,482.0 billion in 2023," it told SMR.

Growth in the historic period resulted from strong economic growth in emerging markets, low interest rates in most developed countries, increasing demand for 'elderly-friendly' infrastructure and technological development. Factors that negatively affected growth were geo-political tensions and rising material costs. Going forward, said the analyst, increasing investments in renewable power generation capacities and urbanisation will drive growth. Factors that could hinder the growth of the construction market in the future include the outbreak of coronavirus disease (COVID-19) and health and safety measures, it added.

## Construction market segmentation

The construction market can be segmented by type into building construction; heavy and civil engineering construction; specialty trade contractors and land planning/development.

In the report, the buildings construction market was the largest segment of the construction market segmented by type, accounting for 51.7% of the total in 2019. Going forward, the land planning and development segment is expected by the analyst to be the fastest growing segment in the construction market, at a CAGR of 8.6% from 2019-2023.

## Going forward, the fastest-growing regions in the construction market will be the Middle East and Asia Pacific

The residential building construction market was the largest segment of the building construction market segmented by type, accounting for 58.2% of the total in 2019. Going forward, this segment is expected to be the fastest growing segment in the building construction market at a CAGR of 6.0% during 2019-2023, said the analyst.

The utility system construction market was the largest segment of the heavy and civil engineering construction market segmented by type, accounting for 43.9% of the total in 2019.

"Going forward, the highway, street and bridge construction segment is expected to be the fastest growing segment in the heavy and civil engineering construction market, at a CAGR of 4.9% during 2019-2023," added the analyst.

The building equipment contractor market was the largest segment of the specialty trade contractors market segmented by type, accounting for 44.3% of the total in 2019. This segment is expected to be the fastest growing segment in the specialty trade contractor market, at a CAGR of 4.8% during 2019-2023.

The private end-user market was the largest segment of the end-user sector segmented by type, accounting for 54.4% of the total in 2019. Going forward, the public end-user segment is expected to be the fastest growing segment in the end-user sector market, at a CAGR of 5.7% during 2019-2023.

"Asia Pacific was the largest region in the global construction market, accounting for 41.0% of the total in 2019. It was followed by North America, Western Europe and then the other regions. Going forward, the fastest-growing regions in the construction market will be the Middle East and Asia Pacific," confirmed the report.

## COVID-19 impact

"The COVID-19 pandemic turned out to be a wake-up call for the construction industry as it highlighted why Industry 4.0 technologies are essential for sustenance in the global market," it said.

Analyst Grand View Research also acknowledged the impact of COVID-19 on the global construction market.

According to Allied Market Research, the prolonged lockdown has severely affected the supply and distribution of raw materials for the construction of buildings and commercial sites. However, the adoption of technology platforms and solutions to tackle the pandemic is expected to aid the construction & manufacturing industry post-pandemic.

"The global construction industry, once booming due to residential and commercial construction in China and the U.S., has been affected by the suspension of construction activities across the impacted economies. The construction industry in the U.S. is expected to take a major hit due to labour shortages and the lockdown imposed by the government during COVID-19, which is expected to be aggravated by resulting supply chain issues and financing pressures due to nonadherence to completion times. COVID-19 is a key market contributor," it said.

## Modular construction

Modular construction involves constructing the modules of a building off-site, then transporting them to the destination for assembly. This method often cuts costs and shortens the construction timeline - it can account for 30-50% time savings over its traditional counterpart. The market is expected to witness a renewed interest from the building & construction industry because of the growing emphasis on productivity, efficiency and workplace safety, along with reducing the time and cost associated with building activities.

"The U.K. is one of the leading markets for modular construction, owing to its high rate of adoption. The chronic shortage of skilled labour, massive ongoing investment in residential housing development and a renewed focus on upgrading healthcare infrastructure are expected to drive the market," commented analyst, Grand View Research.

"In countries such as Sweden, Canada and Finland, extremely cold weather and shorter daytime inhibit traditional building methods. Consequently, modular construction has emerged as a logical alternative to conventional methods. As of 2018, around 85% of new houses in Sweden are built using some form of modular construction," it added.

Irrespective of the type of material used, the modular option makes it possible to take advantage of economies of scale, faster speed and improved quality control, making it an increasingly attractive option. Modular construction also exhibits a substantial decrease in the waste generation associated with traditional building methods.

"Modular construction minimises the extent of disruption and closure in the existing structure. Capital-intensive sectors, such as healthcare, are increasingly using modular construction as a way of reducing the cost and shortening the construction schedules without compromising on the quality of building materials or design. The growing e-commerce industry, coupled with rising industrialisation, has resulted in an increased demand for warehouses and inventory storage buildings. As a result, the demand for modular construction in industrial applications is expected grow," added the analyst.

"The market is characterised by the presence of small- and largescale players, resulting in a moderate level of competition. Companies are focusing on R&D initiatives relating to the use of raw material, supply chain management, transportation and adoption of innovative methods to cope with rising demand from residential, commercial and industrial building applications," it concluded.

Requests from construction clients often include sustainability specifications, especially if customers want to receive eco-friendly designations or tax credits. The sustainable trend in construction shows no signs of slowing.

There is also potential for collaborative robots, or cobots, in construction. These mobile robots could help construction projects to stay on schedule and finish under budget, if deployed on time-consuming or labour-intensive tasks.

"Although a wide variety of issues and inefficiencies can make projects cost too much, industry experts have weighed in to say that technology could reduce those budgetary excesses. For example, drones can capture high-resolution photos of a site faster than other methods, and virtual reality can let clients see what new additions would look like before construction professionals start working on them. Construction firms will continue to choose technology to support their labour needs, save time and improve processes. However, while looking for options that accomplish those goals, many will also want assurance that the investments will have positive effects on project costs," added Linchpin.

## Key market trends

Apart from modular construction, other trends in the construction market include increased interest in 3D printing technology, sustainability innovations and robotics.

"In Mexico, construction is underway on fifty 3D-printed homes. A specialised 3D printer can create each abode in about a day. In 2018, a four-person team associated with the Marines used 3D printing to make a concrete barracks building in just 40 hours. Traditional methods usually require ten people working for five days to build the structure out of wood. Due to examples like these and others, 3D printing should continue to revamp the construction sector in 2021 and beyond," explained Linchpin.

Emerging trends which have a direct impact on the dynamics of the construction industry include increased demand for green construction to reduce the carbon footprint; bridge lock-up device systems to enhance the life of structures; building information systems for efficient building management and the use of fibre-reinforced polymer composites for the rehabilitation of ageing structures, according to analyst Lucintel. However, while advances in technology such as Building Information Modelling (BIM) and modular construction can help, efficiency improvements of the scale required will only be achieved if the industry evolves and develops leaner, more collaborative ways of working across the supply chain.
