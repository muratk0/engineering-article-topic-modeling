# MEETING NEW CHALLENGES

[TARİH: 01.04.2023 ISMR]

Don't miss the third edition of ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE from 30 May-2 June 2023 in Poznań, Poland.

ISMR SAYS:

"This year's event will include practical information on artificial intelligence, robotisation, digital transformation and ways to ensure production continuity in unpredictable times."

This year's edition of ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE will be held from 30 May-2 June 2023 at MTP Poznań Expo in Poland. As well as stands showcasing the latest manufacturing technologies, it will feature various demonstrations, conferences, roundtables and workshops aimed at resolving industrial challenges. Last year, the event hosted 581 exhibitors and welcomed 11,687 visitors.

Visitors will also be able to visit three other exhibitions alongside the show: the Modernlog Trade Fair of Logistics, Transport and Storage, the Subcontracting Industrial Cooperation fair and the Work Safety in Industry exhibition.

The trade fair traditionally combines industry, business and science. Exhibitors present innovative machines and solutions for industries such as metalworking; metallurgy; welding; surface treatment; varnishing; automotive; transport and railways. Overseas companies traditionally account for nearly 50% of its exhibitors from countries which include Austria; Belgium; China; Czech Republic; Denmark; France; Netherlands; India; Germany; Switzerland; Sweden; Taiwan; Turkey; Ukraine; Hungary; the UK and Italy.

## Investment in modern technologies

"Increasingly, the magnet that attracts all Industry-4.0 enthusiasts to Poznań is the programme of accompanying events that complement the innovative exhibition. This year, it will include a lot of practical knowledge on artificial intelligence, robotisation, digital transformation and ways to ensure production continuity in unpredictable times. Experts will present not only trends and optimal directions of change, but also advise on how to implement them effectively," the show organiser, Grupa MPT, told ISMR.

"A modern trade fair is not only an exhibition in halls, but an event combining the rich offer of leading companies in the industry with the energy of meetings, exchange of ideas and knowledge from numerous debates, conferences and shows held in the same space. We try to encourage our partners and industry leaders to co-create the fair through various activities. In this edition, as in the previous year, we plan to launch two stages where invited experts will share their knowledge. We focus on important issues that have a direct impact on the development of innovative technologies in industry. We will strongly highlight the themes of artificial intelligence, robotisation and digital transformation," added Anna Lemańska-Kramer, Director of the ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE trade fair.

This year's programme of events will involve both exhibitors and industry patrons. This includes the Mechanics' Day, organised by the Association of Polish Mechanical Engineers and Technicians, and the Engineering Forum whose agenda is supervised by the Chief Technical Organisation.

The ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE team also plans to continue the Industry Next Congress, lasting for three days.

“The Polish research and development market is one of the fastest growing in Europe. According to the data from the Central Statistical Office, in 2020 domestic expenditure on research and development (R&D) activities amounted to PLN 32.4 billion. This is seven per cent more than the year before. However, Poland is below the EU average in terms of spending. In 2020, 1.4 per cent of GDP was allocated to research and development. What is encouraging—almost half of this expenditure was spent on research and development in the engineering and technical sciences," explained the show organiser.

## Robotisation, automation and digitisation

One of the industry patrons of this year's edition of ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE is the Employers' Association of the Polish Automation and Robotics Forum (FAiRP), a non-profit organisation founded in 2021. It brings together Polish entrepreneurs, in particular employers of the largest Polish enterprises related to the automation and robotics market—integrators, providers of solutions and services, and machine manufacturers.

During the trade fair, the association will moderate an expert panel entitled: 'Robotisation in tandem with automation - close to you, or even closer? Nearshoring in the Polish version.'

“The event is addressed at those interested in investing in modern and smart robotisation, automation and digitisation. This panel will particularly address those looking for proven companies that will accompany them in this process of change," added the show organiser. "In this era of broken supply chains and uncertainty, a trusted and proven implementation partner that is not only close but also has competence in international projects is particularly important."

The expert panel with representatives of investors and implementation companies, as well as technology providers, will try to answer the following questions: Is the quality and value of a business partner proven by the fact that it is on the other side of the world, and why is it better than the one that is in my immediate area? What happens when my business partner, for reasons beyond its control, will not be able to perform the services or deliveries of goods ordered from it? What are the risks in the context of reaching for solutions from outside Poland? Is it realistic to launch a technological line with people from the United States, Brazil and even France or Italy? How and where to look for local suppliers? Is financing or the lack of it an obstacle to investing in technology? Where to look for funds and how to become independent from programmes, subsidies, grants?

"We have reached the point where there is only one answer to the question of whether to invest or not to invest in modern technologies in Polish industry," said Tomasz.

Haiduk, President of the Polish Automation and Robotics Forum. "It's time to make up for lost time and start catching up with Europe with the help of local companies with global experience that know how to transform manufacturing companies towards Industry 4.0.

“This is of particular importance in the current highly uncertain times, where broken international cooperative relations are one of the greatest obstacles to the implementation of investments. Hence, more and more often investors choose partners from the nearest area, so-called nearshoring. This allows for partial independence from the unpredictability of the global supply chain and ensures the company's operation based on local resources," he added.

## Digital transformation

"Is it a crisis yet? What's next? Saving or investing? Training or firing?" These questions will be answered by invited experts during the conference organised by the DBR77 company at the fair.

“For a long time, dark clouds have been hanging over the global economy in the form of high food and energy prices, interest rate increases, the war in Ukraine and the COVID-19 pandemic. Although the Polish economy does not feel it so strongly yet, there are signs of a crisis spreading in ever-widening circles e.g. in industry. We will be circling this topic on the first day of the conference during the ITM INDUSTRY EUROPE fair in Poznań," explained Dr. Piotr Wiśniewski, CEO of the DBR77 Platform.

On the second day of the seminar, the leading issue will be digital transformation.

"Digital transformation seems to be the best and only solution where Polish manufacturing companies can gain a competitive advantage in the market and acquire new orders. There are new opportunities for companies that should be used," argued Dr. Wiśniewski.

During the event, there will also be practical workshops on the topics discussed during the conference. The organisers plan to take a closer look at EU projects, calculating CO₂ emissions, analysing the profitability of investments in robotisation and building a digital development road (enterprise e-strategy).

Getting there

Poznań is situated in the centre of Europe, just a 2.5-hour drive from Berlin and three hours from Warsaw. It has Lawica international airport and a wide range of national and international rail connections. Ławica Airport is 6km from the showgrounds.

MTP Poznań EXPO is on Głogowska Street, in the city centre. Several large hotels are in the immediate vicinity of the showgrounds. Show visitors can reach the railway station on foot in five minutes.

Poznań International Fair and the main railway station are served by the “Bałtyk" bus stop. There is a bus connection to the airport via the “L" rapid bus line; bus line no. 59; bus line no. 48 and night bus line no. 242 running between the main railway station and Ławica airport.
