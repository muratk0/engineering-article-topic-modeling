ISMR was present at KMF’s Young Engineer of the Year initiative to find tomorrow’s sheet metal talent

Above: The runners-up in the under-16s section of the KMF Young Engineer of the Year Awards, pictured with Jason Bradbury  
Left: The runners up in the under-14s section of the KMF Young Engineer of the Year Awards, pictured with Jason Bradbury

KMF Sheet Metal has always worked closely with schools to try and promote the exciting opportunities available to young people if they enter a career in the engineering industry. In 2012, it decided to take this a step further by launching KMF's Young Engineer Of The Year initiative. The concept behind the initiative was to highlight to young people the importance of considering engineering and manufacturing as a career choice.

The awards ceremony for KMF's Young Engineer Of The Year initiative, with ISMR present, was held at Stoke City’s Britannia stadium in the UK in March, during National Apprenticeship Week, where the lucky winners were announced and received their prizes. The event was hosted by The Gadget Show's presenter, Jason Bradbury.

ISMR SAYS:

“KMF’s design competition aims to attract more young people into manufacturing and engineering”

KMF managing director, Gareth Higgins, explained: "It is important that young people consider a future in engineering and manufacturing. I am hopeful that this competition will help to engage pupils and parents when they consider the subjects they wish to study in the future. We want the competition to be challenging and fun, and hopefully we will find our future apprentices."

Watched by an audience that included teachers, manufacturers, skills sector specialists and press (including ISMR), the following winners accepted their awards:

Under 14s Winner (Packaging): Regan Latham Under 16s Winner (Packaging): James Heath Under 14s Winner (Clock): Ben Walker Under 16s Winner (Clock): Cameron Forrest

Eighteen schools throughout Staffordshire in the UK took part with a total of over 1,700 students. As part of the initiative, each student was tasked with designing a mechanical clock. Just as they would within industry, the students were given a design brief and the mechanisms to carry out the project – 604 clock designs were entered, 100 designs were shortlisted and 14 finalists were selected.

The entries were judged by a panel of leading members of the business community on factors such as creative concept and high street marketability with the winners receiving various prizes as well as the opportunity to have their clock made and manufactured by KMF. The ten runners-up each received an i-Pod, with two receiving an i-Pad and other prizes from the TV presenter. The judges will also support the winning students in setting up their own enterprise if they wish to do so.

“We struggle to get the right calibre of people as applicants,” Higgins told ISMR. “Maths and sciences skills must be good. The design competition was aimed at attracting these young people.

We need young and well-educated children to choose design and technology subjects at school options level.

“After the competition, we will meet with teachers to see how we can further support schools in design and technology subjects. We need young and well-educated children to choose design and technology subjects at school options level. Technology will play an important role in the future of precision sheet metal but if we can’t get the right people with the right skills to operate these machines, it counts for nothing.”

## FOCUS ON SKILLS

## KMF duo targets precious metal

The quality of the apprentices at KMF (Precision Sheet Metal) has once again been recognised with two of its rising stars going through the selection process for WorldSkills Leipzig 2013. Having completed a series of stringent skills tests, that included developing and fabricating a patio heater, the independent judges selected KMF’s Matt Page to be the UK’s representative, with his colleague Jake Repsys also travelling to Leipzig as first reserve. This is the first time in over 20 years that the UK has been represented in sheet metal at WorldSkills — the usual 18-month build-up to the skills test was reduced to six months.

“Sheet metal at Worldskills was abandoned many years ago, but it made a reappearance in 2009 with totally different criteria where competitors need more all-round skills, rather than just craft technique,” said WorldSkills mentor, Dave Vaughan, “The new contest includes CAD, CNC laser, CNC press brake as well as the traditional manual skills of developing a fabrication. Not many companies train their apprentices with all of these skills, which are vital to success at WorldSkills’ level.”
