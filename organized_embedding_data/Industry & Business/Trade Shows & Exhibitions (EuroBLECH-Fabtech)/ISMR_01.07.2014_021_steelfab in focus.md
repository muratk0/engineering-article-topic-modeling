# STEELFAB IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.08.2014 ISMR]

ISMR previews SteelFab 2015 and highlights Middle Eastern market trends and new infrastructure projects

Meet ISMR on its booth at next year’s SteelFab exhibition in Sharjah, UAE – the regional steel fabrication and metal working industry’s well-established trade event. The 2015 show, from 26-29 January 2015 at Expo Centre Sharjah in the United Arab Emirates, will feature greater participation from steel fabricators, steel contractors and manufacturers. Exhibitors in this segment can expect to meet visitors such as main contractors, construction companies, oil & gas industry, shipbuilding industry and heavy manufacturing industries.

“The core sector is flourishing in the region owing to high oil prices and a growing economy. Multi-billion dollar mega projects that are under way in the country and the region will ensure that representatives from the core sectors like construction, infrastructure, oil & gas, railways and heavy industries will be visiting the show,” the show organiser told ISMR.

SteelFab 2014 attracted added 50 per cent more exhibition space to accommodate a 25 per cent rise in the number of exhibitors. The increase in visitor turnout represented a significant surge of 25 per cent, an unprecedented rise in the 10-year history of SteelFab. Pavilions and supporting associations included UCIMU SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Association of Italian Manufacturers of Machine Tools, Robots, Automation Systems and ancillary products); VDMA & AUMA (Official German Pavilion); TAIWAN – Taiwan Pavilion and the UAE Contractors Association. TAMI – The Taiwan Association of Machinery Industry – brought 20 companies to SteelFab 2014 and has already doubled its space booking for SteelFab 2015.

## Regional market trends

The construction sector in the UAE is gradually returning to near-full capacity, with the government announcing it is reactivating a number of major development projects. Infrastructure and tourism has proved to be the primary key to speed up the uptrend, especially the upgrades to airports, ports and roadways. In the UAE, construction contracts worth US$ 24,792 million were awarded in 2012. These are expected to increase to US$ 50,591 million by 2015, with the bulk of the projects likely to be awarded over 2014 and 2015.

In the GCC, construction projects across all the building sectors worth US$ 69.91 billion were completed in 2013 and projects totalling US$ 83.41 billion are expected to be completed in 2014 and US$ 82.2 billion to be awarded across the year. GCC economies are increasingly moving away from being oil-led and are focusing on strengthening fundamental growth sectors such as manufacturing, among others. Total industrial investments in the GCC jumped from US$ 81 billion in 1998 to US$ 338 billion in 2012. The sector is fuelled by a reliable supply of gas and liquid feedstock besides competitive advantages in location and logistics for exports to Europe, Asia and Africa, apart from rising domestic demand. The number of companies in the GCC jumped from 7,089 in 1998 to 15,165 in 2012.

Sitting on an oil income of close to US$ 800 billion (2012), the highest income from oil sales in the GCC’s history and more than double its oil revenue of nearly US$ 305 billion to further expand its oil & gas industry. The UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar are spearheading this expansionary wave, where energy and infrastructure projects on a massive scale continue to forge ahead. The GCC offers huge potential for the pipeline industry, primarily due to the thriving oil and gas sector and the power and water desalination sector. The pipeline industry in the Gulf is set to add more than 21,000km to its current pipe network, representing 14 per cent of the globally extended planned pipeline network of 161,000 km.

The GCC countries will continue to invest heavily in infrastructure and diversify their economies, with spending on large-scale projects set to drive growth across the region in the near future. Project spending in 2014 is estimated to be US$ 83.41 billion in UAE, US$ 30 billion in Qatar and US$ 25 billion in Kuwait. The Saudi government is spending over US$ 50 billion just on infrastructure projects.

GCC is set to produce 35-40% of world’s steel by 2015.

A total of US$ 4.3 trillion will be invested in construction projects across the MENA region by 2020.

GCC will be investing US$ 50 billion in petrochemical projects. The region’s oil and gas industry is planning to invest US$ 666 billion to boost its capacities, as oil prices remain bullish.

GCC countries are planning to invest US$ 968 billion in major infrastructure projects over the next 10 years.

New features for SteelFab next year include the Steel-Tech conference, running alongside SteelFab, which will tap into rising demand by bringing together primary steel manufacturing and secondary processing technology for the regional steel industry. It will feature suppliers of turnkey steel plants, furnaces, rolling mills, coil handling machinery, galvanising/annealing lines etc. It is expected to attract visitors from the region‘s steel producers and steel mills, primary steel processors, tube and pipe manufacturers and steel wire manufacturers.

The installed capacity of the steel market in the UAE witnessed a steady growth in 2012 at 2.1 million metric tonnes and was expected to reach 3.6 million metric tons by the end of 2013. The steel industry attributes this expected growth in the steel market to the growing trade and real estate sectors in the UAE. With huge projects lined up in neighbouring countries such as Saudi Arabia and even Qatar, which has an overspill of requirements, this gives an indication of the mature and flexible steel industry that is now developing in the Middle East.

The demand for steel in the UAE is estimated at three million metric tonnes per year and the installed capacity of the markets is three million metric tonnes.
