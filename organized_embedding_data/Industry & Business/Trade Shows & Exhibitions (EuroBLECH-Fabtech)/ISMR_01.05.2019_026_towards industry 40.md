# TOWARDS INDUSTRY 4.0

[TARİH: 01.05.2019 ISMR]

The Mach-Tool exhibition will take place over four days from 5 - 8 June 2019 in Poznań, Poland, as part of the ITM Poland event (which attracted around 1000 exhibitors and companies from 28

countries in 2018). Throughout the four days, the ITM Poland exhibition was visited by a total of 17,090 professional visitors from the industrial sector. The Poznań International Fair, one of the largest conferences and exhibitions in Central and Eastern Europe, will once again host the ITM Poland industrial technology exhibition.

Under the ITM Poland trade fair banner, visitors will find seven themed shows: Mach-Tool (machines and tools); Surfex (surface treatment); Hape (hydraulics, pneumatics, drives); Metalforum (metallurgy, metal industry); Welding; Research for Industry (scientific achievements of research institutes) and the Work Safety in Industry exhibition. These shows will display the latest developments and a wide range of solutions in key industry sectors that are developing in Poland.

"ITM Poland's aim is to demonstrate and promote industrial achievements on a global scale. It gives entrepreneurs the opportunity to take a step into the future and is an example of an excellent combination of industry, business and science. Almost 1,000 exhibitors will present innovative machines and solutions for such industries as transport; automotive; surface treatment; railways; welding; metallurgy; metalworking; varnishing and occupational health and safety. Poznań is also a venue for the presentation of achievements of engineers, design teams and scientific bodies," said the show organiser, Poznań International Fair (MTP).

## Focus on Industry 4.0

"According to Eurostat data published in 2017, Poland is a highly industrialised country. The share of this sector in the Polish economy accounts for as much as 26.3%, which gives Poland a strong position in the world league and ranks it seventh among the EU countries," continued the show organiser.

'Towards Industry 4.0' is the motto of ITM Poland, a key exhibition in Central and Eastern Europe where products and services in line with the idea of Industry 4.0, and the fourth industrial revolution, will be presented.

"Industry 4.0 is a concept that resonates both in Poland and abroad. It is no longer just about manufacturing automation and robotisation. Nowadays, it is a change in business philosophy that requires new tools, including machines and software, new employee skill sets, as well as a transformation of customer approach. Demographic problems and disturbances observed in the labour market in Poland at the moment may lead to increased investment in technologies that will replace people and thus increase productivity. To be successful internationally, Polish companies have to face the changes and move towards new business management practices, in terms of both manufacturing and the use of workforce resources," commented ITM Poland director, Joanna Kucharska.

"Industry 4.0 is already becoming a reality where people, machines and processes are strongly integrated with one another and where production is more flexible and efficient. ITM Poland is a key venue to observe good practice as well as the latest trends and directions in the industrial sector. Please come and see for yourself!"

This event, organised in the heart of Wielkopolska region, is the meeting point for those focused on raising productivity, improving manufacturing processes and creating advanced scientific and technological environments. The trade fair also features special events, training and demonstrations, allowing participants to compare, analyse and explore new technologies.

An important aspect of the ITM Poland trade fair includes business meetings, demonstration spaces, workshops and scientific conferences organised with professional partners and specialists.

## An eye on Poland

"The Polish economy experienced a strong upswing in growth during the past two years. Three coincident cycles – a rebound in euro-area activity, a substantial increase in EU transfers and new large social benefit programmes – boosted GDP growth from 3.1 per cent in 2016 to more than five per cent during 2018. The resulting gains in employment were achieved by reducing unemployment to a record low and attracting inflows of foreign workers," commented the International Monetary Fund (IMF) in February 2019, on the release of its 'Poland Country Report'.

"Recent data suggest that the economic cycle has now begun to moderate, alongside slowing external demand. For 2019, growth is projected to be a still-strong 3½ per cent, underpinned by buoyant private consumption and continued absorption of EU funds. Over the medium term, the economy is projected to expand by around 2% percent per year, reflecting the dampening effects of a shrinking working-age population, subdued private investment and tepid productivity gains. While headline inflation has been persistently below the midpoint of the target, core inflation is expected to edge up gradually from the current very-low level. The current account balance has shifted from a surplus in 2017 to a small deficit in 2018 and is expected to widen gradually over the medium term, while remaining broadly in line with medium-term fundamentals."

Looking ahead, it added, the Polish economy faces important challenges in the near term and beyond. On the external side, an escalation of global trade tensions or a disruptive Brexit could impact Poland's trade and financial flows and dent growth. Renewed turbulence in global financial markets could abruptly tighten financial conditions in Poland and lead to exchange rate depreciation that feeds into inflation. Poland could also, it said, face severe labour shortages if foreign workers were to leave in response to new opportunities elsewhere in the region. On the domestic front, a larger footprint of the state in the economy could slow productivity growth, while investors' risk appetite could be lowered in the event of slippage from prudent policies and sound governance principles, or if relations with the European Union were to deteriorate.

IMF Executive Directors commended the Polish authorities for the economy's strong and sustained expansion, low imbalances, improved social outcomes and the smooth graduation from the FCL. They noted that solid macroeconomic fundamentals have helped to insulate the economy from volatile global financial markets. Going forward, Directors expected growth to moderate gradually in 2019, but noted that ongoing global trade tensions and reduced availability of foreign workers could cloud the outlook. Directors underscored the need for prudent, market-based policies and sustained reforms to raise productivity and ensure continued robust growth.

Poland's economic growth rate is projected to slow to 4.0% in 2019, down from over 5.0% in 2018, according to the World Bank's latest Economic Update for Europe and Central Asia, released on 5 April 2019.

"Poland's growth rate, which is robust compared to other European economies, will be driven by strong domestic consumption and accelerating investments, supported by low interest rates and the availability of European Union funds. On the other hand, the growth dynamics of Poland's economy will be hindered by a slowing global economy, most importantly in the euro zone, as well as domestic labour shortages," said the report.

A proposed expansion in fiscal expenditures for social programmes may accelerate economic growth in Poland in the short run – however, it would most likely lead to a widening of the budget deficit, which is already projected to increase to 1.4% of GDP in 2019, compared to 0.5% last year, and further to 1.6% in 2020, added the report.

"The Polish economy is still growing at a rate significantly above the EU average. The low unemployment rate and strong wage growth are still driving private consumption; investments, both public and private, are growing too. In 2018, the fiscal budget was close to a balance. We do expect, however, that Poland's fiscal situation may deteriorate soon," explained Carlos Piñerúa, World Bank country manager for Poland and the Baltic States.

The World Bank estimates that Poland's GDP growth in 2020 will be 3.6% and will slow to 3.3% in 2021. Forecasts for 2019, as well as for the period 2020-21, are the same as those released earlier, in January this year.

The expected slowdown in Poland would result from a gradual economic downturn worldwide, but more importantly in the euro zone, Poland's biggest trade partner. The World Bank projects economic growth in the euro zone to slow to 1.6% in 2019, down from 1.8% last year, and in 2020-21 to only average 1.4%.

"Europe and Central Asia region faces several long-term challenges including ageing populations, declining productivity, weakening investments and climate change. The good news is there are a range of policy options available to boost growth and mitigate these challenges" said Cyril Muller, World Bank Vice-President for Europe and Central Asia. "Countries should close investment gaps, improve governance, participate more in global value chains and ensure that more people have access to financial services including bank accounts and digital payments."
