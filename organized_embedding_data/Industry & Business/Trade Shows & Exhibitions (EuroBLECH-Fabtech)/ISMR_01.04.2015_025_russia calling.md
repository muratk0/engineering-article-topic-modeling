The Metalloobrabotka International Exhibition will take place in Moscow from 25-29 May 2015

The next Metalloobrabotka International Exhibition will take place at the Expocentre Fairgrounds in Moscow, Russia from 25-29 May 2015. Promoting the latest technological solutions for the Russian machine-building industry, the Metalloobrabotka exhibition will highlight intelligent machine tools; a new generation of high-tech equipment; innovative metal-cutting tools, components and fittings; progressive automation systems for the engineering industry and the latest software solutions.

The event programme includes the 5th International Scientific and Technical Forum on “Modern Trends in Technologies and Design of Metalworking Equipment” on 26 May 2015, organised by the Stankoinstrument Association. There will also be an award ceremony for the winners in the Open Competition “CAD Design in Machine Building” run by the Pavlov Polytechnic College No.8. As well as various industrial seminars, a conference on key trends in metalworking specialist training, as well as a subcontracting exchange, will also take place on 27 May 2015.

The Third All-Russian Forum “Metalloobrabotka: Strategy 2015-2017” will also be held on 27 May 2015 during the show, highlighting the development strategy for Russia’s metal industry ‘during the crisis and the difficult international situation’. Over 150 participants from 40 cities in Russia, as well as the CIS countries, are expected to attend, with strategic information provided by the heads of well-known groups, leading economists and research companies.

## 2014 in focus

One of the largest trade shows in the machine tool industry – the 15th International Exhibition for Equipment, Instruments and Tools for the Metalworking Industry Metalloobrabotka 2014 – ran successfully from 16-20 June 2014 at Expocentre Fairgrounds in Moscow.

The show was held by Expocentre with the Stankoinstrument Russian Association of Machine Tool Manufacturers. It was officially supported by the Council of the Federation of the Federal Assembly of the Russian Federation, the Ministry of Industry and Trade of the Russian Federation and the Russian Union of Machine Builders. The show ran under the auspices of the Chamber of Commerce and Industry of the Russian Federation and the Moscow City Government.

Last year was the 30th anniversary of the exhibition and it attracted 1053 companies from 35 countries, including 462 Russian companies, who showcased their new equipment and developments over a total exhibition space of 40,171 net square metres. Around 23,891 people visited the exhibition.

The Third All-Russian Forum “Metalloobrabotka: Strategy 2015-2017” will also be held on 27 May 2015.

Companies from Belarus, China, the Czech Republic, France, Germany, Italy, Slovakia, Spain and Switzerland arranged their exhibition areas supported by national industry associations. Taiwan’s collective stand was organised there and a Friendship Day for Russian and Taiwanese manufacturers was held as part of the Metalloobrabotka 2014 exhibition.

Danish manufacturer Valmont SM uses EWM’s forceArc® welding process when joining brake discs for wind turbine generators

EWM’s Taurus 451 Synergic S welding machines are the only machines that are used to weld the brake discs. Valmont has been able to cut the production time for a brake disc by up to 30 per cent. (Photo: EWM AG)

Using energy-saving welding machines and emission-reduced welding processes makes the construction of renewable energy plants both environmentally friendly and economical – a classic win-win situation. Wind power is an important future market. It is already the number one renewable energy resource in terms of power generation, with a share of over one third of the market. There are almost 25,000 wind energy plants in Germany alone.

Although the wind turbines might not look very large from a distance, their size is striking. Anyone who has seen a rotor blade being transported on the motorway will have gained a realistic impression of their remarkable dimensions. The turbine’s brakes therefore also need to be very large in size as they are one of its most important components and must comply with very specific requirements in unmanned plants. The rotor brake protects the wind turbine from turning too fast and acts as both a holding brake and an emergency stop brake. The brake discs alone have a diameter of four metres.

One of the largest manufacturers of these safety-related components is Valmont SM of Denmark, part of US company Valmont Industries – one of the world’s leading suppliers of technical solutions and services for infrastructure. Based in Roedekro, southern Denmark, the company produces parts for power plants including wind energy plants, for which it supplies constructional steelwork for the towers and manufactures the rotary housing, complete with integrated brake discs. In the past year alone, the plant’s 650 employees have processed 100,000 tonnes of steel over 1.2 million working hours. Approximately half of them are involved in welding tasks.

Material consumption was reduced by up to 50 per cent

## Brake disc welding

Joining technology is of paramount importance to Valmont SM who occupies a unique position in the market with the manufacture of these components. Its focus is on welding in virtually all areas. Around 50 welders are involved in the construction of the rotor housing, with six of them working exclusively on joining the brake discs.

“We manufacture them by cutting four ring segments out of a large steel plate to keep wastage to a minimum,” explained Frede Holdt Jensen, welding engineer and project manager at Valmont SM. The quarter arcs are mounted on a clamping table so they can then be connected to form one circular piece.

“For the past two years, we’ve been relying on EWM’s forceArc welding process to carry out this work,” said Carl A. Petersen, technical welding specialist (EWT/European Welding Technologist) and welding supervisor for offshore projects and rotor housing. He read about it in a trade journal almost three years ago. After that, everything happened very quickly. The welding tests carried out on the brake discs were satisfactory and the subsequent procedure test yielded a positive result. Valmont initially ordered four Taurus 451 Synergic S welding machines from EWM, quickly followed by another two.

## Cutting costs by 30 per cent

The power sources are used in joining the four pieces to make one brake disc, which involves welding S355 structural steel in thicknesses of 50mm and 60mm. To do this, Valmont uses a flux cored wire electrode (with the classification T466MM1H5) and an M21 shielding gas mixture. With the new welding machines from EWM, the Danish company uses the forceArc process. Previously, MIG/MAG welding with flux cored wire only allowed an included angle of 60 degrees, but the welding experts at Valmont were able to reduce this angle to 34 degrees using EWM welding processes. According to the welding procedure test, it would even have been possible to work with an angle of 30 degrees but the current solution is reported to be very profitable.

“This allows us to make a cost saving of 30 per cent overall,” confirmed Jensen, the welding engineer responsible for these operations. “With forceArc, we were able to shorten the weld preparation and the entire production time for the welding of a brake disc by up to 30 per cent, which means a considerable saving in a high-wage country like Denmark. In addition to power consumption, material consumption was reduced by up to 50 per cent, as fewer passes are now required due to the smaller included angle.”

The energy-reduced, heat-minimised joining process also results in less distortion. Previously, welders had to turn the workpieces three times, but they now only need to do it twice with the new EWM welding process. This means a greater reduction in downtime.
