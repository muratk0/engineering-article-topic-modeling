# EMO IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 ISMR]

After a gap of six years, EMO is now back in Italy. Promoted by CECIMO, the European Association for Machine Tool Industries, the trade show will take place from 5 – 10 October 2015 at the prestigious exhibition centre of Fieramilano (Milan).

Alternately hosted by Italy and Germany, EMO is one of the main exhibitions in the trade show calendar for international manufacturers. The biennial exhibition has chosen Milan as one of its locations, alternating with Hanover in Germany. Five months before the opening of EMO MILANO 2015, about 115,000 m2 of net exhibition space has been already booked and distributed over twelve exhibition halls. The total of 95,000 m2 of exhibition space taken by the last EMO MILANO exhibition (in 2009) has already been exceeded. Should the current trend be confirmed, the organisers assume that the exhibition will occupy 120,000 m2.

More than 1,400 companies have already registered at EMO MILANO 2015. The tally is led by Italy, closely followed by Germany, Taiwan (ranking third for the number of applications to the trade show) followed by Chinese and Swiss exhibitors. The Italian industry today ranks fourth worldwide for production and third for export.

## A full house

“The halls of the exhibition centre of Fieramilano Rho-Pero will host the biggest factory in the world, where machine tools, robots, automation, additive manufacturing, mechatronic solutions and auxiliary technologies will be exhibited, expressing the sector’s international production, representing an industry that accounts for 64 billion euros,” the organiser told ISMR.

“EMO has always been considered the most important event for trade operators from the world's manufacturing industry to keep up-to-date on the new production technologies,” added Pier Luigi Streparava, appointed by CECIMO as Commissioner General EMO MILANO 2015. "It's exactly for this reason that we decided to include additive manufacturing into the technological index of products of the sector’s leading international exhibition. This is one of the technologies that will be increasingly developed in the future and to which the trade show will dedicate a special in-depth focus”.

150,000 visitors from approximately 100 countries are the figures expected for EMO MILANO 2015 which will take place concurrently with EXPO 2015. For this, Milan will benefit from a newly renovated ‘look’ and improved traffic flow, thanks to a revised and updated public transport service. Directly connected with the exhibition centre at Fieramilano Rho-Pero, Expo 2015 will offer a programme of fringe events.

“As organisers of this exhibition,’ continued Pier Luigi Streparava, “we are committed to transforming the convergence of the two trade shows into real opportunities for our operators, by planning ‘connection/networking activities and by activating numerous special services. An example is the hospitality programme which lists a large range of accommodation options reserved for trade operators attending EMO MILANO 2015.”

More than 125,000 visitors attended EMO MILANO 2009 with 51,500 overseas visitors from 99 countries: Europe (60%), Asia (26.5%), America (9.5%) Africa (3.1%) and Oceania (1%).

## Global machine tool statistics

“In the first quarter of 2015,” said Alfredo Mariotti, Director of EMO MILANO, “the index, produced by the Studies Dept. of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian association of machine tool, robot and automation manufacturers) regarding the quantity of machine tool orders received by Italian manufacturers, registered an increase of 2.2% compared to the same period in 2014, making this six positive quarters in a row. The increase in the internal market (+15.4%) has been instrumental to this positive trend.

“This information confirms a new period of development and increase for the use of machine tools in Italy. This will materialise with stronger and stronger increases: +5.3% in 2015, +5% in 2016, +7.4% in 2017 (Oxford of Economics figures) to the benefit of EMO MILANO 2015. This data, together with the positive forecast for the global use of machine tools that will reach 60 billion in 2015, 63 billion in 2016 (+5.2%) and 66 billion in 2017 (+4.8%), are good indications for a successful exhibition.”

According to figures processed by the international Institute Oxford Economics, after a period of general recession starting from the present year, the global consumption of machine tools will again increase at a constant rate to achieve 71 billion euros in 2018 (+18% versus 2015).

“Asia is and will prove to be the most important area with regard to consumption of production systems. Even the increase expected for 2015-2018 will be higher in comparison with that reached in other areas of the world. In particular, Asian consumption will grow from 34.8 billion euros in 2015 to 42.3 billion euros in 2018 (almost double the value versus 2008), recording a 21.6% upturn. The Asian consumption rate over total global consumption amounts to 58.1% in 2015 and, in 2018, will almost reach 60%,” said the EMO organisers.

Europe, the second largest consumption area, will report a growth in demand for machine tools from 14.8 billion euros in 2015 to 17.2 billion euros in 2018 (+16.2%). The European consumption rate is slightly decreasing on the worldwide total: 24.8% in 2015, 24.3% in 2018. In the Americas, consumption will rise from 10.2 billion euros in 2015 to 11.4 billion euros in 2018 (+11.8%). In comparison to Europe, the reduction of the consumption rate in the Americas on the worldwide total is more evident – it will decrease from 17.1% in 2015 to 16.1% in 2018. In 2018, China alone will take up 45% of the world consumption of machine tools, said Mariotti.

“In the European arena, Italy proves to have greater sprint levels compared to the whole area and to Germany (with which it shares the title of leading country not only in the sector but also across the entire manufacturing activity). In particular, consumption of just machine tools will increase from 1.6 billion euros to over 2 billion euros (+23%). On the other hand, internationally, Italian industry in the sector ranks fourth among manufacturers and third among exporters,” said EMO.

Confirmation of these encouraging prospects was supplied by recent data processed by Centro Studi & Cultura di Impresa (Studies Dept. & Business Culture) of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian association of machine tool, robot and automation manufacturers). It shows that Italy is investing in machine tools again.

“In 2014, the index of orders of machine tools collated by Italian manufacturers registered a 14.7% average increase versus 2013. The outcome was determined by the positive trend of performances in overseas markets (+10.1%), as well as by excellent results in the domestic market (+37.2%). These figures confirm the beginning of a new period of development that will materialize in stronger and stronger increases,” concluded EMO’s organisers. ■
