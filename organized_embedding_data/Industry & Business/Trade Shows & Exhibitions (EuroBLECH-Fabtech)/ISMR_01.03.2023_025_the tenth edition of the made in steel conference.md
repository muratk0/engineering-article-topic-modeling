# The tenth edition of the Made in Steel conference and exhibition in Milan from 9-11 May 2023 is now sold out.

[TARİH: 01.03.2023 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

Made in Steel has closed its exhibitor registrations and is sold out, with hundreds of exhibitors signed up. The tenth edition of the international conference and exhibition dedicated to the steel supply chain will be held from 9-11 May 2023 at fieramilano Rho in Milan, Italy. The event will cover more than 14,500 square metres in Halls 22 and 24 at the fieramilano Rho exhibition grounds. Made in Steel's 2021 event attracted 13,505 visitors from 68 countries and 184 exhibitors.

"This is a record for the event, marking a 45% growth in exhibition area compared to 2021 and a 13% growth compared to the previous record edition in 2019. Exhibitors are up 28% compared to 2021; 25% of them are from overseas. Almost all major global and domestic steel groups will be present," the exhibition organiser, siderweb, told ISMR.

“Made in Steel 2023 will be a pivotal moment for international steel: three days of business and conferences, to reflect together on the future of our world at a critical moment like the one we are facing," claimed Paolo Morandi, managing director of Made in Steel.

The theme of the event this year is "GENERATIONS, re-imagining our world." Made in Steel will host a raft of meetings, fora and roundtables, promoting the sort of in-depth sector knowledge that is key to company competitiveness. The conferences will focus on the short- and long-term outlook for the steel industry. The aim is to give operators the tools to intercept market trends and "better interpret and understand the dynamics of the global steel chessboard."

## An international approach

"Dozens of buyers from all over the world will visit Made in Steel, thanks to our collaboration with ICE-The Agency for overseas promotion and the globalisation of Italian companies. A matchmaking service and the option for companies to organize meetings at 'speaker's corners' will also support business," added siderweb.

For the fifth consecutive edition, Made in Steel has renewed its partnership with the ITA-Italian Trade Agency, the governmental agency which supports the business development of Italian companies abroad and promotes overseas investment in Italy.

Starting in 2015 for the sixth edition of Made in Steel, the partnership has been consolidated over many years and has seen an increased number of international players hosted at the event, with numerous contacts and business relationships established with exhibitors.

For 2023, ITA involved 18 target countries: Germany; France; Spain; Austria; Poland; Switzerland; Czech Republic; Turkey; Russia; Ukraine; Bosnia; Croatia; Serbia; Egypt; Morocco; Tunisia; Algeria and India.

The buyers invited are mainly EPCs

—engineering procurement contractors, commodity purchasing managers of leading industrial players and large steel consumers (i.e. machine-tool manufacturers, transport equipment manufacturers, metal fabricators etc.) and those with interests in the steel industry.

Made in Steel will also be held in partial conjunction with the LAMIERA sheet-metal exhibition (10-13 May 2023) at the exhibition grounds in Milan. The international exhibition for sheet-metal processing and sheet-metalforming machines and related technologies, promoted by UCIMU-Sistemi Per Produrre, will be held in the halls next to Made in Steel. This synergy will help to increase overall visitor numbers and networking/business opportunities for both events.

## The outlook for steel

"In Italy (latest available data, source: Bilanci d’Acciaio 2022), the steel supply chain generated a turnover of 79.18 billion euros in 2021. The profit as a percentage of turnover increased from 0.63 per cent in 2020 to 4.5 per cent in 2021. 2022 was still a good year for domestic steel companies, despite exponential increases in the cost of energy, supply difficulties due to the war and geopolitical crises, and a slowdown in demand and production, particularly in the second half of the year," commented siderweb.

2023 will be a rather difficult year for steel, according to the outlook from siderweb's Research Department, with declining or stagnating demand for steel and still high energy and raw material prices, albeit on a gradually downward trend.

"The simultaneous reduction in the prices of energy sources and raw materials," explained Gianfranco Tosini of siderweb's Research Department, "will favour a decrease in steel production costs of about 7% for EAF and more than 13% for the blast furnace, paving the way for a drop in product selling prices.

“It is plausible to assume that the turnover of steelmaking companies will decrease by about 13% in 2023 compared to 2022," Tosini continued. "Consequently, the profitability of industrial operations in 2023 will fall by more than one percentage point, dropping to 2.5% from 3.5% [in 2022], practically halving from 5% in 2021," he added.

Made in Steel will be held alongside the LAMIERA sheet-metal exhibition in Milan.
