# An eye on Poland

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 ISMR]

THIS YEAR'S ITM Poland Fair in Poznań took place over 21,000 square metres with nearly 1,000 exhibitors from 27 countries, 16,955 visitors, hundreds of machines in motion, conferences, lectures and demonstrations. ISMR was an event partner and its June issue was distributed throughout the exhibition. The next show is scheduled for 7 – 10 June 2016.

"The metal industry is one of the most dynamically developing sectors of the Polish economy. At the same time, it is susceptible to numerous threats, such as the fragmentation of the market (the share of small and medium enterprises is very large), excess capacity of up to 30-40% and dependence on other industries, for example construction, railway, foundry or automotive industries.

"Despite these dangers, and in many cases thanks to their effective transformation into opportunities, the expansion of the metal industry (as well as the associated engineering industry) continues. Its export potential, the development of related industries (the suppliers of which are metal and machinery sectors) and the increase in demand for steel have all resulted in the flourishing of the sector," the show organiser told ISMR.

Visitors were able to discuss market trends and see new metalworking innovations in the nine exhibition halls. The event's cooperative Subcontracting ITM meetings provided visitors with an opportunity to find customers for any unused surplus of production capacity.

Polish exhibitor, Eckert, won this year's Acanthus Aureus prize for the best designed exhibition booth during the event (see page 45).

## SCHUNK grips new horizons with facility in China

AT A formal ceremony, SCHUNK, a specialist in clamping technology and gripping systems, opened its new Chinese headquarters in Shanghai. The company wants to push ahead with its investment programme and expand its local presence in China.

Since 2003, SCHUNK has been represented in China with its own subsidiary that has witnessed consistent growth since its market introduction. SCHUNK Managing Partner, Henrik A. Schunk, called the new headquarters an important milestone in the 70-year history of the family-owned company.

“The new location offers three decisive advantages. It enables SCHUNK to have more room for the growing number of Chinese staff. It also enables us to build relationships with customers and create a knowledge transfer philosophy through the new SCHUNK Tech Centre. With the help of proper functioning robotic cells, visitors can see live demos of what can be achieved using SCHUNK components and automation solutions. Workshops and practical training will be regularly offered by experienced technical experts,” the company told ISMR.

“One of our most important objectives in China is to be on the spot when the customer needs us," explained Henrik A. Schunk. A long-term company goal is to strengthen and expand its market position for clamping technology and gripping systems worldwide. In this, China will be a third pillar alongside Europe and USA, said Mr. Schunk.

## Unison receives Queen's Award

Directors of the tube bending machine manufacturer, Unison – (from the left) Peter Wilkinson, Alan Pickering and Julian Kidger – receiving the Queen's Award for Enterprise from The Lord Lieutenant of North Yorkshire, Barry Dodd.

DIRECTORS OF the UK tube bending machine manufacturer, Unison – Peter Wilkinson, Alan Pickering and Julian Kidger – received the Queen's Award for Enterprise recently from The Lord Lieutenant of North Yorkshire, Barry Dodd.

Unison exports around 70% of its machinery to manufacturers around the world, located in Europe, North America, Africa and Australasia. Barry Dodd commended the sustained three-year record of export growth that led to Unison winning the award, and stressed how important companies like Unison are to the UK economy.

An award scroll and crystal bowl to mark the achievement were handed over during Unison's annual open house event. Colin Sirett, head of research and technology at Airbus UK, a guest of honour at the ceremony, noted that the advanced machinery provided by companies like Unison underpins the work of major manufacturers such as Airbus. He also applauded Unison for the leading role it is playing in the current establishment of a University Technical College in Scarborough (UK).
