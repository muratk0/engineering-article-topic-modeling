Mexico is a country of extremes when it comes to manufacturing.

Summer Mfg. Camps

FABTECH trade show

If we concentrate just on the larger of the big multinationals, we see emerging power players — efficient, innovative, and productive. The other side of Mexican manufacturing is responsible for 80 percent of the jobs in the industrial sector, but they struggle to grow as they are severely hampered by the regulatory system and the taxes paid by the smaller operations. There is a new sense of urgency in Mexico as these companies are the backbone of the economy, or at least they should be.

The country has seen its economic growth slow in the last few years — down to an average of 0.6 percent per year and only 0.4 percent in 2013 as far as income growth is concerned. This has largely been attributed to a reduction in labor productivity — from $18.30 per worker per hour to $17.90 (in purchasing power parity terms). The Mexican worker is still better trained than almost any in Latin America, and the motivation is there. The problem is that companies have no ability to innovate through additional capital investment and little opportunity to train workers for the new jobs and skills required.

The government is trying to address these issues with a task force that includes a variety of participants, but the smaller manufacturers are afraid they will be lost in the crowd that includes government officials from all levels, academics, union leaders, and the biggest companies. The message from the bulk of the manufacturing community in Mexico is a need for tax relief and regulations that are at least consistent and predictable.

The reforms that Mexico has undertaken thus far have been far-reaching and will go a long way toward opening up new industry in the country, but for the country to see the kind of income growth and productivity it desires, the issues of the broader manufacturing community have to be addressed sooner rather than later. There are many examples of rapid growth when Mexican companies are not spending 50 percent of their revenue and profits on taxes and compliance.
