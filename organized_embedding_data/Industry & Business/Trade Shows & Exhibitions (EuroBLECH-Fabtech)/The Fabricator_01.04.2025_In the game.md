Sightline’s railing fabrications can be seen in stadiums and arenas everywhere

RAFAEL GUERRERO

S

ports activity in North America is practically year-round nowadays, but there remains something unique about what transpires in the spring.

In one corner, NHL and NBA arenas are preparing for playoff games and NCAA basketball tournaments. In another corner, soccer stadiums and ballparks are welcoming spectators for the start of the MLS and MLB seasons. And while larger football stadiums tend to lie vacant in the spring, a few of them are used for big events like the Final Four.

Perhaps you will be at one of these venues this spring. If that’s the case, there’s a chance that you’ll interact with the work of Minneapolis- based Sightline Commercial Solutions. The fabricator has made its presence known by providing the handrailing, guardrails, platforms, expanded and VIP seating, and ornamental work spectators may take for granted in sports arenas and stadiums.

Adding to the Aesthetic

Two of the Los Angeles area’s newest venues provide good examples of Sightline’s capabilities. At SoFi Stadium, the company custom designed and fabricated roughly 73,000 ft. — equivalent to roughly 14 miles—of railing for the 70,000-plus-seat stadium. This included nine different styles of architectural railing in 125 different configurations, according to a Sightline case study.

Stadium railing projects are complex because they likely feature multiple different railing styles, said Jon Chase, Sightline’s chief engineering officer.

The SoFi project is particularly interesting because "a lot of outdoor space was incorporated into that facility," Chase said. "Rather than just interior guardrails, there’s a lot of exterior railings. A lot of it was positioned to create this open-air feeling to it."

One mile south of SoFi Stadium is the 18,000-seat Intuit Dome, which has earned high marks from spectators and others since it opened in 2024. Sightline fabricated more than 17,000 ft. of custom railing for the venue.

Sightline Commercial Solutions’ footprint in architectural railing can be found in arenas and stadiums everywhere, including the new Intuit Dome in the Los Angeles area.

The fan experience at sports venues has changed over the years, with many places now providing unique seating.

Chase said the metal railing is fairly unique at Intuit, such as the stainless steel web-like railing for the arena stairwells. Sightline’s other contributions include glass railing, powder-coated aluminum handrails, and stainless-steel LED handrails.

While the fabricator does plenty of stainless steel and glass architectural railing, the majority of its work is historically in aluminum, which is green, recyclable, lightweight, and corrosion resistant.

"Its strength-to-weight ratio allows, from an installation standpoint, to get railings around a facility with much more ease, without needing to find some cranes or dollies to move material around," said Chase. "Two laborers can move something around by hand, up through the seating bowl, up through stairs, up through concourses with ease. With steel railings, you’re finding ways to find forklifts or cranes to move the railings around."

Long History

Sightline’s major sports venue projects date back to the early 2000s when the company, known then as Staging Concepts, was renowned primarily for its work in staging, providing portable platform systems and associated accessories.

In 2003, the business bid and won a major architectural railing project at Busch Stadium in St. Louis that called for six miles of railing, which was Sightline’s largest undertaking at the time.

Chase said the company had already provided ornate metal railing for some venues, but this project was a significant step up.

"From that point on, we developed a good relationship with that architect, that contractor, and followed them to a few other projects," he said. "From there, we were off to the races."

Sightline has adapted as venues have advanced and catered to modern-day spectators, who don’t just go to a venue, sit down, watch the game, and leave. Instead, they may experience the game, from different vantage points—sometimes from custom-built drink rails, other times from VIP or luxury seating.

Projects from the last few years demonstrate the fabricator’s approach to changing fan expectations, such as the modular VIP and ground-level seating at the soccer-specific Shell Energy Stadium in Houston and Inter&Co Stadium in Orlando, Fla.

Chase estimated that Sightline has provided railing for approximately 80% of the venues built over the last decade. The majority of its business is architectural railing and decorative metals/canopies, with the next largest segment being portable platforms.

The fabricator showcases its platform roots every spring during the NCAA college basketball Final Four. The NCAA owns several Sightline platforms, which are used by tournament organizers to add thousands of temporary seats at Final Four venues, which are typically large football stadiums, in an effort to convert them into basketball arenas. Sightline also provides the platforms needed to raise the basketball court.

"It’s essentially a stage, but it’s comprised of our portable platforms and aluminum support structures," Chase said of the raised floor. "It’s reinforced so there’s no vibration on the court; the hoops run through the flooring down and [are] anchored into the concrete floor below—they’re not anchored to the stage and equipment."

Sightline is involved in three of the largest stadium projects in the U.S., said Chase. It is fabricating the railings for the new Buffalo Bills stadium in the Buffalo, N.Y., area; the railing, aluminum framework, and bleachers for the new Ryan Field in Evanston, Ill.; and the railing and glass for the new Tennessee Titans stadium in Nashville.

"This will be a lot of stainless and glass," Chase said of the Titans project.
