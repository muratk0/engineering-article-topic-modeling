# METAV is an international showcase for manufacturing

[TARİH: 01.02.2012 ISMR]

The next METAV exhibition will be held from 28 February to 3 March 2012 in Düsseldorf, Germany. It has firmly established itself as an important technology shop window for manufacturing technology and automation in Germany. METAV will display the entire spectrum of manufacturing and metalworking technology, focusing on machine tools, production systems, high-precision tools, automated material flows, computer technology, industrial electronics and accessories.

METAV’s visitor target group includes all major industrial sectors, particularly plant and machinery manufacturers, the automotive industry and its component suppliers, aerospace, the electrical engineering industry, the metalworking sector, medical technology and the craft sector. At the last METAV in 2010, around 700 exhibitors showcased their products, solutions and services over more than 32,000 m2 of net exhibition area. They attracted approximately 45,000 trade visitors from 26 countries.

## Technology showcase

GFE (the Society for Production Technology and Development in Schmalkalden) will be hosting the METAV Technology Forum on “Sustainable Cutting Processes” on 29 February and 1 March 2012. Leading manufacturers of machines and tools will be reporting on their latest innovations.

The partners cooperating in the Technology Forum are METAV’s organiser VDW (the German Machine Tool Builders’ Association) and the Specialised Section Precision Tools within the VDMA, conceptual sponsor of METAV 2012.

## A forum for grinding

The sheer diversity of the jobs involved is enormous, ranging from forming, sharpening tool edges all the way through to fine-cutting processes for microscopic examinations. Grinding will be playing an important role at the METAV 2012 exhibition. Grinding is crucial to a component’s quality. Markus Weiss, who heads the Grinding Technology Group at the Faculty of Production Process Technology at the Machine Tool Laboratory (WZL) of RWTH Aachen University, says: “We are seeing more and more enquiries about grinding high-strength nickel-based materials, plus titanium and carbon-fibre-reinforced plastics.” He also sees a lot of potential for tools with a defined edge or for milling cutters, inserts or drills. Ultra-stringent requirements are encountered not only in the field of medical technology but also in sectors like energy engineering.

Processes for high-performance grinding are particularly sought after. The Machine Tool Laboratory, for example, has done crucial work in helping to design-enhance speedstroke grinding (table feed rates up to 200 m/min.; maximum table acceleration 50 m/s). This flat-grinding process is suitable for jobs like machining turbine blades. On the scientific side, the laboratory staff in Aachen have most recently been studying vibratory grinding, a technique for surface finishing. To quote Markus Weiss: “The process is suitable for deburring and improving surface quality.”

Energy-economy is exerting an ever-greater influence on the experts’ work: the staff in Aachen are currently improving what have up to now been very high consumption levels of cooling lubricant. Markus Weiss has obtained good results with ester-based cooling lubricants, which “in terms of performance capabilities mostly do rather better than conventional lubricants based on mineral oil”. His group is also engaged in a special research project, shared by the Universities of Applied Science in Dresden and Chemnitz, to study the thermal behaviour of machine tools. “We’re taking a long hard look at how the heat produced during the grinding process affects the machine’s structure and the component’s quality,” he explains.

The scientist will be looking for input across the entire gamut of all processes at METAV 2012. “We shall definitely be going to the fair with our group, to take a good look at the grinding technology on show there,” he added. “What’s more, METAV is a very good venue for entering into cross-technology conversations.”

Around 40 manufacturers of grinding, honing and finishing machines will be among the exhibitors in Düsseldorf - but manufacturers such as Monforts will also be showcasing new, competing processes at the exhibition. www.metav.de
