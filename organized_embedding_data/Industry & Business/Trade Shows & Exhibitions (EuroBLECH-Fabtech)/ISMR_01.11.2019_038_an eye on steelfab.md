Attend the 16th edition of SteelFab from 13-16 January 2020 at Expo Centre Sharjah in the UAE. With the countdown beginning for mega events such as World Expo 2020, regional fabricators are expecting the best for the show. Around 85% of attendees were from the MENA region.

"Strong resurgence in the GCC's economic growth prospects is fuelling growth of the region's construction sector (which remains the biggest sector by project value) and infrastructure industries; these are core sectors that are demand drivers for the metalworking industry in the region. Continuing infrastructure spending, stimulus packages, reforms and growth in the non-oil sector are also providing impetus to the industry," the show organiser told ISMR.

The 16th SteelFab is expected to showcase over 250 exhibitors, displaying close to 800 brands from categories such as Welding & Cutting, Machine Tools, Pipe & Tube Machinery, Grinding & Cutting, Surface Preparation & Finishing plus Tools & Ancillaries. The fifth edition of a dedicated vertical for the fastener and fixing industry - Fasteners World Middle East – will also be held alongside SteelFab 2020. ## EVENT PREVIEW

## Technology and trends

SteelFab is a key event connecting the regional metal working, metal manufacturing and steel fabrication industry with leading manufacturers, distributors and industry experts from across the world. The presence of over 700 brands, special focus areas, live displays, technical seminars and round-table discussions make the show a comprehensive event for the industry.

National pavilions will showcase leading manufacturers and distributors under the umbrella of their respective countries or industry associations. They include a UK Pavilion; UCIMU Sistemi Per Produrre (Association of Italian Manufacturers); Taiwan Pavilion and International Tube Association (ITA) Pavilion.

Special focus areas will continue to be an integral part of SteelFab and aim to spotlight segments within SteelFab to manufacturers and visitors. Pipe and tube machinery is seen as an interesting growth segment. Technical seminars will also be held at the show on a variety of topics.

"We have seen a trend of increasing interest towards automation and integration by the regional audience and this is reflected in the topics presented at the show. The last edition of SteelFab addressed Industry-4.0, Internet usage for remote operation and Autonomous Machines amongst others. It has been sixteen years since the region's steel fabrication and metal working industry changed the way it did business, and since SteelFab made its debut. A lot has changed since then, and the event continues to steer the industry through the ups and downs of the economy and the market," explained Saif Al Midfa, CEO of Expo Centre Sharjah.

The 4th Fasteners World Middle East will be held alongside SteelFab 2020. It will showcase a wide range of industrial fasteners and fixings, assembly and installation systems and fastener manufacturing technology as well as storage and logistics services.

## Fabrication in focus

Preparations for World Expo 2020 in the GCC have picked up pace and are generating strong opportunities for the fabrication sector. Expo 2020 has awarded two major contracts worth US$ 182 million to Laing O'Rourke to build two pavilions and an access road for Expo-2020. The organisers awarded construction contracts worth nearly US$ 3 billion in 2017 while US$ 111.5 million was allocated for nonconstruction contracts. In total, 2,745 contracts have been awarded to date.

A number of contracts are also generating growth in the region, according to the show organiser, as highlighted below:

US$ 1.8 tn is the value of projects in the construction and oil and gas industries, which account for about 58% of all projects planned or underway in the GCC.

US$ 80 bn worth of water, wastewater projects under way across the GCC.

US$ 864 bn is the value of construction projects in Saudi Arabia, the largest construction market in the region. The UAE comes second with US$ 575.9 bn.

US$ 664 bn worth of oil, gas and petrochemicals projects planned or underway across the MENA region, with an estimated US$ 396 bn worth of projects planned or unawarded.

US$30 bn major power project contracts awarded every year across the GCC.

■ US$200 bn railway network will link all GCC countries and is on course to meet its 2021 target.

"The UAE fabrication industry requires high-end machinery and equipment to provide on-time deliveries, and ensure quality and craftsmanship on every part. The industry includes well-established, major players as well as industrial houses (who have invested in fabrication facilities over time) and smallmid-size fabricators who handle smaller projects and sub-contract on major projects. They are equipped with the latest technology from top global manufacturers and prefer to grow organically," added the show organiser.

Continuing infrastructure spending, stimulus packages, reforms and growth in the non-oil sector are also providing impetus to the industry.

## Robotics and automation

"SteelFab was the first event in the region to exclusively cater to the complete welding and cutting requirements of the steel fabrication industry in the region. The formidable manufacturing and industrial sectors of the region were the main reason behind rising demand for welding and cutting equipment in the UAE and the rest of Arabia. Heavy fabrications and a focus on renewable energy usage also provided impetus for growth in welding markets. Over the years, SteelFab has grown with the industry and, along the way, expanded its geographical footprint," said H.E. Saif Mohammed Al Midfa, CEO, Expo Centre Sharjah.

"Robotics was one of the prominent themes at SteelFab in 2019. With robotics fast becoming an integral part of the metal working industry across the Middle East, visitors showed interest in the most modern technology that could help them to improve productivity, efficiency and reliability. Given the interest, a roundtable discussion on robotics was held during the show, which threw light on how robotics has changed the way that industries are functioning today and how, in the near future, robots will work alongside human beings," he added.

The participants at the roundtable revealed that robotics has become a soughtafter application in the Middle East. Now, companies are looking at the prospects of robots working alongside human beings, which 'could create a better and more reliable working environment'. Robotics can even generate more jobs and assist in the evolution of skill sets, they said.

Metal cutting machine tools have become a key segment of SteelFab. Steel fabricators are also making the change from labour-intensive material handling processes to automated material handling processes. The Middle East fabricator faces a gamut of difficulties involving labour-intensive processes. Whilst the cost of labour is going up, associated costs such as government fees, visa costs, accommodation costs and retention costs are on the rise. Added to that are the costs of training and replacement.

Over time, the steel fabrication and overall manufacturing industry has seen a trend towards automating its material handling processes whether through cranes, magnetic lifting, positioners or robotic solutions.

"SteelFab has reflected this change in the industry here," added Saif Al Midfa, CEO, Expo Centre Sharjah. "We have noticed this change on our show floor, where the exhibits have evolved over the years. We now have more representation in material handling robots, overhead cranes, magnetic lifting and vacuum lifting."
