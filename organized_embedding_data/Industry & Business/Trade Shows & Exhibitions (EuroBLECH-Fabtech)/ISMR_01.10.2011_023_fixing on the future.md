# Fixing on the Future

[TARİH: 01.10.2011 ISMR]

Brazil is a growing and well established market that offers great opportunities for suppliers in the fastener and fixing technologies industries. Fastener Fair Brazil 2011 is a highly focused exhibition exclusively dedicated to fastener and fixing technologies. Held by Mack Brooks exhibitions, organisers of the Fastener Fair Stuttgart exhibition, Fastener Fair Brazil will take place from 8 – 9 November 2011 at the Expo Centre Norte in São Paulo.

Fastener Fair Brazil is a dedicated business platform for the fastener and fixing industry in Brazil and its neighbouring countries. 135 exhibitors from all over the world have already booked their stand space. For trade visitors, entrance to the show is free of charge.

Fastener Fair Stuttgart, the flagship event of a series of targeted trade exhibitions for the fastener and fixing industry, is Europe’s leading international trade fair for this industry sector. The recent edition in February 2011 is the biggest show ever, featuring 670 exhibitors from 40 countries in three spacious exhibitions halls.

## A GROWING ECONOMY

Brazil’s economy is expanding rapidly due to rapid development and large scale investments in infrastructure as well as a marked increase in domestic demand (see our Latin American report on pages 22 – 23). The automotive, construction, domestic appliances, electronics, telecoms, petrochemical and energy sectors are expanding as quickly as the domestic market.

As the host for the World Cup in 2014 and the Olympic Games in 2016, Brazil is making massive infrastructure investments in preparation for these two worldwide events. The Brazilian government has started several construction projects related to these events.

“Brazil’s GDP growth in 2010 was 7.6 per cent,” the event organiser, Mack Brooks Exhibitions, told ISMR. “The country is a leading Latin American aircraft exporter and the fourth largest market worldwide for car consumers and sixth largest for car production. Industrial construction is set to increase by 16 per cent in 2011.”
