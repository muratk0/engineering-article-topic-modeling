# INNOVATION IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

German rollforming specialist, Dreistern, is ramping up innovation and driving down costs

Rollforming is a highly economical process – good products produced at a reasonable cost. We see an increasing emphasis on productivity from the market. Our aim is to keep finding new rollforming applications and we are constantly developing new technologies and searching for customised projects,” explained Heinrich Weber, sales director of German rollforming specialist, Dreistern.

The company was exhibiting at this year’s Blechexpo in Stuttgart, focusing its booth presence on automotive, building, logistics industry and custom rollformers.

“The show has been very busy for us and reflects our present business in Germany and central Europe (the majority of our customers are there now). Asia is a very strong market for us (China, Korea and India). The North American market is surprising – it has slowed down a little although we have supplied lines to North America and Mexico in the past.

Previously, we did not supply rollforming tools to other rollforming manufacturers but we are now doing this in Europe so we will mirror this in North America and promote this at FABTECH,” Weber told ISMR during Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

## The year in view

“It has been a successful year for us, in terms of sales volume, but the international character of our business caused high pressure on prices and we consequently reduced our prices for standard machines. We are running a lean management project at Dreistern to make our production more efficient and to make more cost reductions internally. This also means lower prices for customers. This has certainly been a better year for us than last year,” Weber explained.

“There are good market drivers for our business – e-commerce (which requires lot of distribution centres and therefore racking systems which boosts our logistics’ market business); internet technology (needing larger server farms and sheet metal storage systems for servers/electrical enclosures) and the automotive industry.”

Another focus of future growth for Dreistern is aluminium rollforming, which is becoming more popular for the automotive industry (high strength alloys).

“We have traditionally processed aluminium for very thin parts but now want to focus on building rollforming machines to process thicker aluminium. We are developing a machine that can provide a high A-class surface finish (from 1-2mm). That machine is still a research project but we hope to supply the machine next year,” added Weber.

“We will also ship a number of machines to a large Russian manufacturer of racking/shelving systems in January 2016 (with an emphasis on short product changeover times).”

## Multi-functional rollforming concept

“We just supplied new rollforming and punching technology to Bao Steel in Shanghai (a Chinese steelmaker) – a machine that does different jobs such as making tubes for hydroforming, which then become automotive parts for car chassis, and making profiles for truck trailers. These are highly complex parts. The machine features flexible CNC punching technology for high speed, flexible punching with classical punching tools and lower speeds because of the individual hole punching involved.

“We call it a hybrid tube mill and it is based on our multi-functional rollforming principle. Bao Steel wants to be become the biggest rollforming company in China. We have also sold the same technology to a German company for installation next year,” Weber told ISMR.

Multi-functional rollforming technology is based upon standardised processing modules for punching, bending or welding that can be quickly and easily integrated at any point on the rollforming machine. As a result, it is as quick and straightforward to change such processing modules as to change quick-change rollforming tools. Thanks to universal compatibility and the flexible control unit, individual processing modules can also be used on different machines. The pre-requisites for operation with a cost-effective product mix are therefore met.

“Multifunctional rollforming is a constant evolution, where we find new applications every day. Profiles for racking and shelf systems, for example. Currently, we are targeting low-end markets for smaller batch series production,” Weber explained.

## Opportunities and challenges

Weber highlighted various challenges and opportunities in the rollforming sector to ISMR.

“The main challenges are on the cost side – we need to become more cost-effective and pass these savings onto our customers. The opportunities include new applications in the automotive industry (for weight reduction) as well as in the logistics industry and for electrical enclosures. High strength aluminium alloys present new challenges and opportunities for future business,” he said.

Another focus of future growth for Dreistern is aluminium rollforming.

“Material thicknesses are also changing in two ways. The trend towards using thinner materials is to save material cost and to save weight, especially in the tube business (for example, stainless steel tubing for heat exchangers to reduce nitrogen emissions from diesel and petrol engines). Conversely, there is also a trend towards processing higher thicknesses for special applications.”

The most important innovations from Dreistern customers are aimed at new profile ideas. Checking their feasibility, preparing suggestions for improvements in relation to production, material and investment costs, and finally developing rollforming tools are part of the daily tasks of Dreistern tool designers. However, new profile ideas can often require complete process development.

In a special machine laboratory with 10 try-out machines, Dreistern can test new processes under production conditions and manufacture initial prototypes so that its customers can carry out realistic functional tests and even test market response to new products at an early stage.

“The event provides customers with innovative solutions for rollforming; welding; pickling; straightening; testing; sawing; bending; measuring; transporting; warehouse logistics and cooling lubricants. The group of partners includes Dreistern; TRUMPF; KVK-KOERNER; Sema Systemtechnik; FOERSTER; RSA; WAFIOS; AICON; Fehr; Scheffer and Kluthe, offering German-made technologies available today,” Weber added.

Kjellberg Finsterwalde launched two new power sources and a torch for InFocus welding at this year’s Blechexpo

At this year’s Blechexpo, Kjellberg presented its latest developments in welding, plasma and laser cutting as well as mechanical engineering. Kjellberg offers products and technologies for thermal cutting, joining and processing. As a well-established manufacturer of plasma cutting systems, it offers plasma and laser cutting equipment for a wide range of tasks in automated, mechanised or manual cutting – all made in Germany.

Kjellberg’s focus was on the presentation of two new power sources. Its Smart Focus series for plasma cutting has been extended with a new model, which also uses patented Contour Cut technology for very high cut quality. A new power source for plasma arc welding was also shown for the first time - its potential for joint welding and hardfacing was demonstrated with a user-friendly process control. Kjellberg also showcased its TIG technology InFocus welding torch to ISMR as well as its HiFocus plasma cutting series and XFocus fibre laser on its booth at Blechexpo.

On 1 April 2015, Kjellberg Finsterwalde opened its own sales and service company in Mumbai, India to support customers there. Its main markets in Asia are China and India although the bulk of its sales are still made in Europe. This year, it has sold 68 plasma and welding machines.

## Smart Focus extends cutting range

One year after the successful launch of its plasma systems, Smart Focus 130, 200 and 300, Kjellberg has added a new model to this series. With a maximum cutting current of 400 A for material thicknesses of up to 100mm, the Smart Focus 400 expands the application areas of this plasma series. The results, said the company, are sharp cut edges and smooth cut surfaces, fine markings and low angular deviations. The new power source was premiered at the Blechexpo trade show in Stuttgart.

“Using the Smart Focus 400, users benefit from precise cuts and high cutting speeds while, at the same time, the costs per cutting metre are low. Even under challenging conditions, the Smart Focus 400 achieves its best results on just a few settings. For cutting fine contours and small holes in mild steel, the new system uses Contour Cut technology,” Carsten Trentau, MD Sales, Kjellberg told ISMR.

Contour Cut (CC) is a technology for cutting small contours, webs and especially small holes in mild steel. When using Contour Cut technology for cutting holes with a diameter to thickness ratio of 1:1, high quality levels can be achieved, the company explained. Smooth cut surfaces and sharp cut edges reduce timeconsuming after-treatment so that productivity can be increased.

“With Contour Cut Speed, contours can be cut at similar quality up to 50% faster. Because of the shorter processing time, the costs per cutting metre can be reduced,” explained Carsten Trentau.

## Welding and laser cutting

“We are also launching our new PSI400 automated plasma welding machine at this show,” added Dr. Michael Schnick, MD R&D. “There are two powder feeders, calibrated by a special software tool. A new innovation on this product is the control panel which features three different user levels. An integrated database can be used for import and export jobs using USB sticks. A separate database has also been set up for gases. We have kept a firm eye on Industry 4.0 requirements in the development of this product.”

The power source is particularly suitable for plasma transferred-arc welding and plasma joint welding. With a maximum welding current of 400 A at a duty cycle of 100%, the PSI 400 has high productivity levels. Flexibility is enhanced due to the offered operating modes of plasma, pilot or TIG. Up to two types of flux or wires can be combined with each other. The flux types are calibrated and managed in a database. During interval welding, the power source is able to establish the pilot arc when the main arc is turned off, thus enabling quick re-ignition of the main arc. This prevents repeated HF ignition pulses and increases the lifetime of consumables.

Sometimes it is necessary to weld the inside of pipes or areas which are difficult to access. As an addition to its TIG technology InFocus range, Kjellberg now offers the InFocus T500 IN torch with a torch head that is bent by 90°. This makes it possible to weld inside pipes with an inner diameter ranging from 40mm, even when an even weld surface is required inside the pipe.

Kjellberg already offers torches with a capacity of 500 A and 1000 A as straight versions. The compact design of the InFocus 500 torch is used for welding with limited access to the weld seam. The new InFocus inpipe welding torch for orbital welding can double the speed of the welding process, pointed out Dr. Michael Schnick, MD R&D. It will further enhance this range. The defined position of the cathode tip ensures a constant TCP (Tool Centre Point) with a tolerance of less than 0.1mm which is important for automation. Kjellberg TIG technology for welding and soldering can easily be automated and considerably expands the application areas of TIG welding. InFocus welding was demonstrated for the first time in combination with plasma cutting on a robot with toolchanging system at the Blechexpo exhibition.

Kjellberg also showcased its TIG technology InFocus welding torch to ISMR

“The new XFocus 4000 fibre laser model completes the new product portfolio showcased here at Blechexpo. It can be set up in one day and makes it simple for users to take their first steps into laser cutting markets,” explained Dr. Michael Schnick.

## Innovation in focus

“We have high hopes for our new products,” Carsten Trentau told ISMR. “We are planning for growth this year, despite the political and economic climate in markets such as Russia and China. The euro exchange rate has helped us with exports and the Asian market has been strong this year for us. We also sell into the US market.”

More stable processes in welding markets are always needed, along with high speeds, high quality and higher productivity levels. Kjellberg has consistently aligned its R&D to meet these market needs.

“We invest a lot into R&D and put a lot of effort into making our products more accurate and more precise. We still see further opportunities to optimise the plasma cutting process, taking more cost out of the process and increasing its efficiency. Environmental issues (reduced noise and energy levels) also play a part in this,” explained Dr. Michael Schnick, MD R&D.

“The mechanisation of welding processes has also become more important. The forthcoming lack of skilled welders is another reason why customers wish to increase their automation levels. Machines must be easy to operate and userfriendly, making sophisticated processes easy to understand and handle."

Its patent for FineFocus plasma cutting, developed in close cooperation with Manfred von Ardenne's research institute and filed in 1962, makes Kjellberg one of the oldest plasma machine manufacturers in the market.

The product range includes more than 70 standard electrodes and numerous special electrodes.
