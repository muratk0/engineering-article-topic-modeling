# 21ST CENTURY PRODUCTION

[TARİH: 01.02.2017 ISMR]

From 4-7 April 2017, INDUSTRIE Lyon, a leading exhibition in France for production technologies, will showcase a wide selection of equipment, products and related services aimed at creating the right conditions for ensuring productivity and competitiveness for factories. Packed with machinery and equipment demonstrations, the event is expected to attract approximately 900 exhibitors and 22,000 contractors. Visitors will be able to see machines, robots and equipment in operation as well as discover the latest technologies.

"INDUSTRIE Lyon features a number of events (combining demonstrations, testimonials and feedback) which are centred on a few key themes: Innovation, Training and the Factory of the Future," the show organiser, GL Events and Exhibitions, told ISMR.

"Exhibitors can generate business through showcasing their equipment and products. We also aim to support visitors in their search for new processes to increase and improve their production."

## Industry awards

In existence for over 15 years, the Industrie awards at INDUSTRIE Lyon recognise innovators, products, solutions and services. Four new award categories will be added this year in Lyon: eco-efficiency, digital tooling, industrial performance and new technologies.

The identity of the winners will not be revealed until the evening of the Innovation Awards (Tuesday 4 April 2017), attended by around 900 people. The evening will continue with an after-dinner show which includes illusionists, acrobats and a comedian.

The Industrial Manager of the Year award created two years ago at INDUSTRIE Lyon 2015, rewards a manager who has contributed to improving the performance of his/her company by putting people at the heart of a project.

This year's award will focus on the capacity for innovation by industrial managers and their teams. A jury of industrial production professionals will review all projects and choose the Industrial Manager of the Year. This award will be presented during the Innovation Awards on Tuesday 4 April 2017 at the exhibition. Applications for the Industrial Manager of the Year award can be sent to the exhibition organiser before the deadline of 17 February 2017.

## The future live and Fab Lab

Bringing together sixteen companies in a joint project (some of whom are competitors) and setting up an identical assembly line to one already on-site, in as short a time as possible, is no mean feat. This would not be possible without the involvement of industrialists who under the auspices of the UITS trade union, invested their time, equipment and expertise into this project.

At INDUSTRIE Lyon, ADI will repeat this by bringing together a dozen companies and adding a laser cutting system to the assembly line. Workpieces will be cut and painted on the painting line live!

Throughout the exhibition, participating companies will be represented by one of their technicians so that visitors can learn more about their expertise and the performance of their technology. This activity will show the public how surface treatment and painting professionals have adapted to technical and environmental changes by evolving their processes.

For the first time at Lyon, INDUSTRIE will set up a Fab Lab, an area of collaborative and open innovation that promotes creativity. This shared space features a complete machine tool area (3D printing, laser cutting, software, CAD etc.) where visitors can benefit from the advice of experts.

"Over the four days of the exhibition, and thanks to equipment provided by exhibitors, the Fab Lab will bring together small and medium-sized companies and SMEs who are developing flagship products. Companies will be able to use the machines tools in the Fab Lab which have been donated by exhibitors," explained the event organiser.

A new feature this year are the technical pitches, led by the three partners, which will allow visitors to receive feedback from industry professionals on key topics.

Switchgear, throughput of bent sheet metal components is reportedly just as high using a manually-operated, CNC folding machine as it was when the company was using a fully automated panel bender. For over 25 years, Proteus Switchgear has specialised in the design, development and production of electrical switchgear for factories, universities, hospitals, offices, shops and houses.

The manual FLEXIbend 73.30, an all-electric model built in Germany by RAS Reinhardt and supplied through sole UK sales and service agent Press & Shear, Tamworth, took over from the automatic, hydraulically-actuated machine from another supplier in January 2016.

"The new folding centre is providing not only equivalent productivity but also greater flexibility for bending small to medium batches of switchgear cabinet components. Typical runs are from 10- to 20-off and occasionally up to 50-off," said the company.

## An eye on economy

The folder also delivers improved bending precision, helped by its rigid construction and a patented, intelligent crowning system whose sensors measure and compensate automatically for folding beam deflection. Positional accuracy is within ±0.1mm.

"There is no error at all in the bend angles, as they are checked manually after the first operation using a set square or template, allowing offset values to be entered into the control," said Proteus Switchgear.

As its pump no longer runs continuously throughout the day, energy consumption has been reduced substantially. The FLEXIbend, being all-electric, draws power only when it is in operation, apart from a small amount needed for the control screen.

Andy Roberts, the company's CNC programmer, commented, "Our previous, automated folding machine was nearing the end of its useful life and to replace it would have cost €500,000. In contrast, the FLEXIbend was one-third of the price.

"We were concerned that, being a manual machine, it would not be able to keep up with production so we retained the automated machine for a while to work alongside it. However, we only used it two or three times in the first four months after delivery of the FLEXIbend, so we had it taken out. Automated machines are space-hungry, so it has freed a lot of factory space for other activities, such as material storage and assembly of our switchgear cabinets."

The new machine has the added advantage of larger capacity, being able to bend material of up to 3,200mm compared with 2,100mm on the automated machine. Proteus Switchgear can now produce 2.5 metre-long frame sections and cover panels for its cabinets without resorting to a standard press brake, which is inconvenient when handling such large components.

The new folder provides more flexibility for the production of smaller parts. To hold down sheet during automated bending, a minimum area of 200mm x 150mm was previously required for the clamping tool. There is no such restriction with the FLEXIbend. Proteus Switchgear produces a lot of end plates that are 130mm wide, which previously 'had to be bent less efficiently on press brakes'.

## Modular, quick-change tooling

Mr. Roberts contrasted the use of the FLEXIbend with that of a press brake. He said the latter requires special tooling to produce, for example, a hemming bend or a radius bend. Such full-width tools are expensive to buy and are heavy, and exchanging them can be time-consuming which, in turn, affects productivity and profitability.

"The manual folding machine, on the other hand, needs only two sets of standard segmented tools in the upper beam to produce all of our parts. Even a radius bend can be produced by repeated hits with a sharp tool, a sequence that is worked out automatically by the RAS control software," he explained.

One set of tools, comprising mainly goatsfoot and sharp segments, is reserved for folding mild steel of up to 2.5mm thick. It remains on the machine for most of the time, as the majority of material at the Telford factory is between 0.9mm and 1.5mm thick. The other tool set is for producing components of between 2.5mm and 3mm, which is the maximum thickness that the machine can process. Swapping over the tools takes less than five minutes, as they are clamped automatically in the beam.

"It is an easy matter to adjust the positions of the tool segments, each of which is up to 200mm long, to create a number of sections along the length of the machine. The technique allows material of various widths to be folded at the different stations, without the part ever leaving the operator's hands, avoiding unnecessary handling," added RAS Reinhardt.

## Machine and software

The design of the machine promotes ergonomic operation, as the operator does not have to push heavy sheets through from the front. Instead, they can be loaded from the rear using the integrated sheet support table and back gauge, which incorporates pop-up fingers that position the workpiece before each bend. Proteus Switchgear chose a J-shaped table configuration to improve access to the tooling and allow single-handed manipulation of heavy sheets.

After a program starts, a graphic on the control screen shows the operator which foot pedal needs to be pressed. The lower beam and the folding beam, with their sets of segmented tooling, automatically adjust within ten seconds to suit the material thickness, whilst the back gauge positions the blank for the first bend. The folding beam moves at 90 degrees per second.

Programming the machine entails drawing the part on the control's touch screen, after which the software works out the bending sequence and simulates it, flagging up any potential collisions. Built-in technology tables take into account material spring back. A 3D image of the finished part is displayed for verification before production starts.

On-screen instructions are provided for tooling adjustment, as well as details of which tooling segments are required to achieve the tool length the operator has programmed. The information is stored with the folding program, ready for re-use.

Proteus Switchgear also uses RAS software for off-line, PC-based programming. With an identical graphical user interface, it is especially useful when producing prototypes and special components required in ones and twos.
