The E&D Zone at MACH 2018 will be supported by Sandvik Coromant

THE UK'S WELL-KNOWN manufacturing technologies exhibition, MACH 2018, is set to return to the NEC in Birmingham, England, from 9-13 April 2018 with a new look and a new location. The biennial exhibition will take place on the atrium side of the NEC, in Halls 17, 18, 19, 20, 6 and 7. This is the show's first move since relocating to the NEC in 1976 and reflects the changing technologies covered by the show.

"The new halls mean that the show will take place in one single uninterrupted space on one level, making the show easier to navigate for visitors and providing a unique opportunity to showcase the full range of manufacturing technologies in the UK under one roof," said the Manufacturing Technologies Association (MTA), which organises MACH 2018 on behalf of the industry.

The 2016 edition was sold out with a 10% increase in visitors compared to the event in 2014 and over £150 million worth of business attributed to the show. So far, 90% of exhibitor space has been allocated for the 2018 show. The overriding theme running through the show is interconnected technology and the future of manufacturing.

MACH showcases live working machinery and brings together manufacturers across a range of technologies including: metrology, additive manufacturing, tooling and Industry 4.0 technologies. A seminar programme and live demonstrations are also offered. The MMMA has also confirmed that there will be over 550 square metres in Hall 20 in its Metalworking Village (for over 20 member companies) at MACH 2018.

MACH 2018 will also play host to an Education & Development (E&D) Zone specifically designed for students aged 11-19 years, supported by Sandvik Coromant. The E&D Zone will be manned by a team of apprentices and graduates from exhibiting companies at MACH. They will be giving guided tours to the students around the entire exhibition. There will be a range of hands-on, interactive activities and educational seminars throughout the week in the E&D Seminar Theatre.

For more information, see www.mta.org.uk and www.mmma.org.uk
