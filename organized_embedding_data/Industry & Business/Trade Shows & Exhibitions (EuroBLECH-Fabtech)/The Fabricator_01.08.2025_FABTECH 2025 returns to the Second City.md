W

hether it’s your first time or it’s your umpteenth time, FABTECH always impresses. Especially when it descends upon Chicago.

Chicago is known for many things—its lakefront; its skyscraping architecture; its food; and its important place in American manufacturing’s past, present, and future. It is often called "The Windy City." Some call it the "City of Big Shoulders," while others may know it as "The Second City." But during FABTECH week, Chicago is second to no one.

Few places can hold North America’s largest metal forming, fabricating, welding, and finishing event. Not only is Chicago’s McCormick Place, North America’s largest metal convention center, up to the challenge, the venue does the event justice each time. The 2025 iteration will be no different.

The four-day show will host approximately 50,000 attendees and more than 1,700 exhibitors from around the world, and it will feature the unveiling of between 600 and 700 new products.

The Fabricators and Manufacturers Association, SME, American Welding Society, Precision Metalforming Association, and the Chemical Coaters Association Intl. co-organized the show.

"We will fill up Chicago for a whole week with FABTECH. … It would be a mistake to not be there," said Mark Hoper, FMA senior vice president of expositions and media.

Only at FABTECH can one condense the arduous process of adding new equipment to the shop floor—investigating what’s out there, inquiring with suppliers, bringing them into the shop, making those final decisions—from several months to a matter of days.

"It’s a one-stop shop that saves manufacturers untold time in evaluating and getting educated on the technology they’ll be implementing in their shops," Hoper said.

Attendees will have the chance to view the latest technology at the show’s Automation; Forming and Fabricating; Tube and Pipe; Stamping, Tool & Die, and Press Automation; Welding; Finishing; and Job Shop pavilions.

The Automation pavilion continues to grow, with organizers predicting an unprecedented amount of automation to be on display. Meanwhile, new this year is the Job Shop pavilion, and the former Metalform pavilion has been rebranded as Stamping, Tool & Die, and Press Automation.

Attendees also will be able to connect with and learn from others through numerous events and hangouts, such as FABTECH Industry Night, the FABTECH Biergarten, the Women of FABTECH program, the Emerging Leaders program, networking events and functions, panel discussions, and keynote presentations.

This year’s keynote lineup includes NFL Hall of Famer Emmitt Smith, performance coach Alan Stein Jr., NASCAR/motorsports innovator Andy Papathanassiou, performance consultant/leadership coach Amber Selking, and reshoring advocate Harry Moser.

For those hoping to leave the event with more technical knowledge, dozens of conference educational sessions will be available throughout the week in 13 technology tracks—ranging from automation/robotics to workforce and talent development. As in years past, attendees can earn credit toward professional credentials and certifications by attending many of these sessions, seminars, and workshops.

Panel Discussion: Finding and Keeping the Next Generation Workforce

The current workforce is changing as baby boomers retire and younger employees enter the job market. Differences in the perspectives of the old and new workforce can create challenges for companies. This panel will address these changes and challenges and explore new ideas, strategies, and alternatives to help meet current and future employment needs.

Attendees will learn what approaches other companies are taking—what is working, what isn’t working, and perspectives from those currently educating the next workforce. Prepare to leave with new ideas and outlooks on attracting, training, and keeping employees that you can take back and implement in your search for the new workforce.

This panel will be moderated by Lisa Wertzbaugher, owner of Wertzbaugher Consulting. Presenters will include Kevin Dierking, president/CEO, Voestalpine Roll Forming Corp.; Antigone Sharris, engineering technology faculty lead, Triton College; and Jim Mayer, founder, The Manufacturing Connector.

THURSDAY, SEPT. 11

10 a.m.-11 a.m.

Keynote: How OEMs and Job Shops Can Benefit from Reshoring

After decades of offshoring that cost the U.S. 5 million manufacturing jobs and tens of thousands of factories, reshoring is making a strong comeback, driven by the current administration’s policy, economic strategy, and growing geopolitical risk. Reshoring advocate Harry Moser will share insights from a national survey of 500 manufacturers, revealing how shorter domestic supply chains reduce risk and boost profits for OEMs across 20% to 30% of imported products. Learn how OEMs and job shops can capitalize on this trend—identifying reshoring opportunities, justifying automation investments, and building stronger workforce pipelines. This session also includes the 2025 National Metalworking Reshoring Award and provides actionable steps to move reshoring from talk to reality.
