# Stainless steel centenary

[TARİH: 01.03.2013 ISMR]

2013 marks 100 years since stainless steel was discovered by Harry Brearley, in Sheffield, UK. This development not only revolutionised the modern world, but shaped Sheffield into the city it has become today. As a result, Sheffield is to host a year-long celebratory events programme to ensure that this historic landmark is commemorated in style and reaffirm the legacy of innovation which still exists in the city today.

The BSSA and the Sheffield Metallurgical and Engineering Association are hosting a major conference and exhibition in Sheffield this June, which will focus on the future for stainless steel. There are 6 sessions over two days which will highlight the major achievements that stainless steel has made so far and look forward to what the future might hold. The conference programme also includes a celebrity lecture on the evening of 11 June by Mika Seitovirta, CEO Outokumpu Group.

A Centenary Dinner will be held on 12 June, hosted by the Master Cutler at The Cutlers Hall, Sheffield, with speakers including the UK Deputy Prime Minister, The Rt Hon Nick Clegg MP, and John Cridland, Director-General of the Confederation of British Industry.

It is only within the past 150 years that metallurgists have learnt how to make steels, control their properties and mass-produce them with a consistent level of quality. During the 19th century, a number of scientists discovered the excellent corrosion resistance of chromium alloyed steels. But it was not until the early decades of the 1900s that the first stainless steel was patented and manufactured.

Stainless steel was invented early in the 20th century when it was discovered that a certain amount of the metal chromium (usually a minimum of 11 per cent) added to ordinary steel gave it a bright shiny gloss and made it highly resistant to tarnishing and rusting. This rust-resisting property which we call "corrosion resistance" is what sets stainless steel apart from most other forms of steel.

Stainless steel is used in a number of applications such as improving fire safety in tunnels. The first surgical implants in stainless steel were performed as early as 1926. The first stainless steel train was built in the USA in the 1930s. 11 million washing machines using stainless steel drums were produced in China in 2010.
