# STEELFAB IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2012 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“SteelFab 2013 will be held from 14-17 January 2013 at Expo Centre Sharjah in the United Arab Emirates”

For those seeking easy access to the fast-growing Arab economies as well as existing and emerging markets in the Middle East and Africa region, the Indian sub-continent and Central Asia, SteelFab is a gateway that facilitates seamless trade.

SteelFab has become a well-attended and highly anticipated trade fair that caters to the regional metal working, metal manufacturing and steel fabrication industry in these regions. Exhibitors from over 22 countries showcase their latest technologies and systems during SteelFab. The next edition of SteelFab will be held at Expo Centre Sharjah in the United Arab Emirates from 14-17 January 2013. It will feature a special focus area on welding and cutting.

An emirate of the seven-emirate federation, Sharjah overlooks the Arabian Gulf from the West and Gulf of Oman from the East. One part of the emirate is surrounded by the emirates of Dubai and Ajman, while the other extends east to the Gulf of Oman. The emirate, however, borders all the other emirates of the UAE. Sharjah is located in the centre of the UAE, with Dubai a 15-minute drive away (i.e. 15km approx.) by one of the most advanced road networks in the country. Abu Dhabi, a 90-minute drive away, is about 200km from Sharjah and 185km from Dubai.

## Middle Eastern trends

Steel demand in the UAE has soared in the past few years against a backdrop of continuing infrastructure projects, cheap and reliable gas and energy supply, growing investments in the construction industry and other core sectors. The apparent consumption of finished steel products is expected to reach more than eight million metric tonnes by the end of 2014.

The growth in demand for welding and cutting equipment in the UAE and essentially the rest of Arabia is driven by the rapidlyexpanding industrial sector, which has made this segment an essential component of the regional steel fabrication, metal working and metal manufacturing industry. According to latest figures, the industrial sector in the UAE grew by 11 per cent in 2011. From US$ 35 billion in 2010, the manufacturing sector’s contribution to the UAE’s overall GDP swelled to an all-time high of US$ 39 billion in 2011.

In Qatar, one of the fastest growing economies in the world, the industrial sector is expect to post 18 per cent yearly growth between 2011 and 2012, with more than US$ 125 billion expected to be spent on projects between 2011 and 2016. The manufacturing sector was Saudi Arabia’s fastest growing industry in the first half of 2011, rising by 22.5 per cent, driven by a 38 per cent increase in petroleum refining. Non-refining manufacturing expanded by 14.8 per cent.

This exponential growth in the Arabian industrial sector is fuelling demand for a wide variety of welding and cutting equipment, highlighting the importance of the premier regional trade fair SteelFab which showcases not just welding machines but a complete range of welding and cutting equipment and devices. Every country has its own industrial characteristics. In Arabia, the welding and cutting segment essentially involves not just welding shops but fabrication yards and engineering workshops, fuelling demand not just welding machines but for a diverse and complete range of equipment and a substantial variety of brands.

The UAE spent 32.8 per cent of its gross domestic product in 2010, the highest level since the global financial crisis, which helped the world’s third-largest oil exporter spur economic growth and further diversify away from oil. The UAE has also recorded one of its best economic and financial eras over the past decade, with its nominal GDP, revenue and trade nearly quadrupling to emerge as one of the fastest-growing economies, a semi-official study has shown.

## Growing support

The four-day SteelFab show in 2012, which featured 216 direct exhibitors and more than 600 brands from 26 countries, was opened by Shaikh Salim Abdul Rahman al Qassimi, Director of the Ruler’s office. It featured three special focus areas: metal cutting machine tools, pipe and tube machinery and material handling. The show also attracted strong support from overseas partners such as VDMA (the Germany Engineering Federation) that organised a German pavilion, UCIMU SISYEMI PER PRODURRE (the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robotics and automation systems) and TAMI (the Taiwan Machinery Association).

In a survey at the 2012 event, exhibitors believed that demand for steel in the region would come from the expected buoyancy in infrastructure, oil and gas plus construction sectors. A total of 6990 trade visitors attend the exhibition – an increase of 22% over the previous show visitor figures. Visitor numbers from the Gulf Cooperation Council states rose by 18% compared to the 2011 event. The rise was significant for visitors from Saudi Arabia and Qatar, said the organiser.

SteelFab 2011, held in January, exhibited more than 600 brands and recorded a 20 per cent increase in the number of visitors compared to the previous year. The seventh edition of SteelFab featured 206 direct exhibitors, against 183 in the previous year, registering an increase of nearly 13 per cent. It was held over a total exhibition area of 16,000 square metres, featuring the Middle East’s largest display of machinery, machine tools, welding equipment, supplies, consumables and ancillary equipment used in the pre- and post-treatment of steel.

The steel fabrication sector has also evolved with the UAE economy, which has successfully diversified away from a nation dependent solely on oil income. This change was also reflected in SteelFab’s new special focus areas, which now include material handling, machine tools, pipe & tube machinery, live displays and technical seminars and product presentations, apart from welding & cutting. ■
