## Record floor space

"The trend towards digitised manufacturing is boosting innovation and business activity in the sheet metal working industry and its leading industry exhibition," said Nicola Hamann, managing director of the event organiser, Mack Brooks Exhibitions.

"With an increase of 2% in visitor numbers compared to the previous show, this year's EuroBLECH clearly surpassed the 60,000 visitor mark. Not even the strike by two German airlines on the third exhibition day had an impact on the buoyant mood of the participants and the positive development in visitor numbers. The results of this year's EuroBLECH, a barometer of the industry sector, show a record floor space of some 88,000 square metres net and a sustainable increase in visitor numbers; this demonstrates a healthy economic outlook for the industry sector with its global business activity."

For the first time since 2008, this year's EuroBLECH not only reached but slightly surpassed its record floor size of some 88,000 square metres net. The vast majority of visitors came from the industry (80%), followed by visitors from workshops, trade and services. The most important sectors that the visitors belonged to were engineering, sheet metal & products, steel and aluminium construction as well as the automotive industry and its suppliers.

At 99%, the percentage of trade visitors was, once again, very high. Visitors to EuroBLECH included design engineers; production managers; quality managers; buyers; manufacturers; technical directors and experts from associations and R&D. Besides the high rate of international visitors, the exhibition also registered a high percentage of visitors from top management with decision-making and buying capacity. The percentage of visitors involved in decision-making has increased by 3% to a total of 84%.

This year's EuroBLECH also sported its own app for iPhone, Android and Windows. The App not only offers the option to register and buy tickets but also contains information on all exhibitors, their products and events. It also includes interactive floor plans showing the location of exhibitors' stands.

"The preliminary results of the exhibition survey show that both exhibitors and visitors at this year's EuroBLECH were highly satisfied. The visitors praised the completeness and international range of the products on display. The exhibitors praised the qualified and international audience with its high percentage of decisionmakers. Exhibitors also stated that they had met a large number of new business contacts. Three quarters of all exhibitors stated on-site that they intended to exhibit again at the next EuroBLECH, to be held from 23-27 October 2018 in Hanover, Germany," added the organiser.

## The drive towards digitisation

The focus of this year's EuroBLECH was the digitisation of production processes and increasing automation. Flexible manufacturing systems have become even more important to meet the demand for individualised products. Simulation and innovative process control systems reduce waste and increase cost efficiency. The automotive industry, with its focus on lightweight construction, remains the major driver for innovations in sheet metal working. The latest solutions for the fabrication and processing of sheet metal and hybrid structures are, therefore, a central point of interest.

'The New Generation of Sheet Metal Working' was the overall theme of EuroBLECH 2016. Mack Brooks Exhibitions chose this motto to reflect the prevailing change in industry production initiated by the internet of things or Industry 4.0. But the motto is also directed at the younger generation as the sheet metal working industry, a high-tech sector, seeks to attract qualified junior employees. In the Social Media Lounge (Hall 16, stand J52), exhibitors at EuroBLECH announced a large number of job vacancies within the industry sector.

EuroBLECH, the industry sector's leading trade exhibition, presented the entire sheet metal working technology chain, ranging from high-tech systems to conventional machinery: sheet metal; semi-finished and finished products; handling; separation; forming; flexible sheet metal working; joining; welding; additive manufacturing; tube/section processing; surface treatment; processing of hybrid structures; tools; machine elements; quality control; CAD/CAM/CIM systems; factory equipment and R&D.

## Heavy-duty bending

A newly launched press brake on the LVD booth was the Synchro-Form, its next leap in adaptive bending technology. Synchro-Form automatically maintains angular consistency and the required geometric profile when handling, positioning and bending large parts with multiple bends.

The system overcomes the problems of cumulative error and trial-and-error bending when forming large profiles. As a result, Synchro-Form is designed to ensure precise, efficient bending, eliminating manual operations and increasing throughput. The system made its debut at EuroBLECH.

"Synchro-Form evolves our adaptive bending technology, making it easy to produce accurate bends in large workpieces. The system is a unique design. It uses a laser scanner and synchro modules (X, R, A-axes magnets) to manipulate, position and measure each bend, relaying the digital information to the TOUCH-B control, which makes adjustments to part and ram position to achieve the correct profile. Variations are not accumulated but, instead, compensated with each bend step. Even after multiple, consecutive bends, the profile will be perfectly formed," explained Fowles.

The Synchro-Form is an integrated feature of Synchro-Form Series in models ranging from 320 tons x 4 metres up to 3000 tons x 14 metres and is also available in tandem, tridem and quadem configurations. These configure-to-order machines are typically used in the crane boom; yellow goods; lighting pole; construction; transport; agricultural; offshore; oil/gas and windpower industries.

"The Synchro modules position the part, the machine makes the bend, the module scans the part and compares it with the theory versus the reality. So rather than using trial and error, where you bend the part, re-bend it and then get to the point where you cannot retrieve the part because it has been bent too far, Synchro-Form technology scans the part after each bend and then compensates after each bend so that you don't get cumulative error. There are various benefits - depending upon the complexity of the part, you can see up to a 50% reduction in production time as well as safety and part accuracy benefits," Fowles told ISMR.

Other bending systems on the LVD booth included the ToolCell 135/30 automated tool changing press brake designed to maximize bending productivity through an integrated storage of tooling, housed within the machine. Tools are automatically placed in and out of the bending zone, eliminating manual tooling set-up and adjustments. While the operator is preparing parts for the next job, the machine automatically unloads the previous tool setup and loads the next.

LVD's Easy-Form® press brake on the booth was equipped with LVD's patented Easy-Form®Laser (EFL) in-process angle monitoring and correction technology. A range of Dyna-Press high-speed electric press brakes, including a new 40-ton machine, were also demonstrated. Able to bend at speeds of 25mm per second, Dyna-Press 12- and 24-ton models are compact and portable, using a standard forklift.

## Soyer won a EuroBLECH sheet metal award in October for its innovative stud welding technology

Heinz Soyer Bolzenschweisstechnik GmbH has specialised in the development, manufacturing, sales and servicing of stud welding equipment and machines, together with the accompanying welding studs. It has won numerous national and international awards and prizes and is comprehensively certified. All its stud welding machines have been awarded the GS mark and its production sites are under the supervision of the Rhineland Technical Inspection Authority (TUV Rheinland).

Soyer's equipment portfolio encompasses everything from the latest portable miniature stud welding equipment to fully automated high-tech CNC controlled machining centres it told ISMR. Different types of welding studs are also manufactured at its Wörthsee production site. Since 1989, the family business has been awarded the German Federal Innovation Prize twelve times.

The company highlighted two new stud welding innovations at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover and proudly displayed its new EuroBLECH sheet metal award on its booth.

## EuroBLECH success

As a specialist in fastening and joining technology, Soyer showcased its new SRM® stud welding inverter BMK-8i with single-phase 230 V connection at EuroBLECH 2016. This pioneering technological development weighs just 9.5kg, does not need high-voltage current and was developed specifically for mobile use.

"Thanks to its patented SRM Technology®, the lightweight stud welder is designed for energy-efficient, safe and clean welding of studs made of steel and stainless steel up to 8mm in diameter (M10 Soyer HZ-1R). The BMK-8i uses a welding current of 300 Amps and is an inexpensive entry-level model for the SRM technique," Oliver Pohlus, Export Sales Director, Heinz Soyer Bolzenschweisstechnik GmbH, told ISMR.

"Our new PH-9 SRM12+G welding gun with integrated ground contact perfectly complements the BMK-8i stud welder. Equipped with a new generation of SRM coil, gas consumption can now be reduced by 90%. The use of separate earth clamps can be dispensed with when welding as mass transmission now takes place via a contact ring that is spring-mounted on the support tube."

This welding gun was specifically developed for M12 weld studs and won the 2016 Federal Innovation Prize in Germany this March.

"In conjunction with the mobile and miniature BMK-12i stud-welding inverter, the newly developed PH-9 SRM 12 welding gun welds studs made of steel and stainless steel of up to M16, onto thick and thin metallic workpieces, without using ceramic rings. It therefore offers first-class welding results with no weld bead or weld splatter. In addition, by using an innovative gas shroud on the PH-9 SRM12 welding gun, shielding gas consumption has been reduced by up to 90%. More than 10,000 welds (Soyer HZ-1 8mm) can now be performed with 2-litre mini gas bottles and large, heavy and classic gas bottles are therefore 'a thing of the past'," explained Oliver Pohlus.

"Since the BMK-12i welding inverter has also been reduced to a minimum in size and weight (7.8kg), and the total system drastically reduces energy consumption (up to 70%) there are hardly any limits any more to mobility, flexibility and possible applications of all types. These include mechanical engineering, oil and gas, container construction, vehicle construction, energy (e.g. wind towers) shipbuilding and apparatus manufacturing."

This welding system also received the "EuroBLECH 2016 Award" innovation award in the "Joining Technology" category at the EuroBLECH exhibition.

"This system is small (handbag-sized), highly portable and simple to use – an operator can start welding straight away with tremendously reduced gas consumption. It offers excellent weld quality of up to M16, on plates thicknesses of 1mm upwards, and features the latest inverter technology. The BKM-12i, with integrated SRM technology, has a high clock frequency of 100kHz. In safety terms, it also features a self-protecting device in case of excess temperature, excess/low voltage or phase failure." added Oliver Pohlus.

## Reproducible welds

Soyer designs and develops all its technology in-house, in line with its customers' needs and requirements. It told ISMR that a substantial number of orders have been registered for its SRM stud welding technology, since its launch five years ago.

"We have refined and developed the technology since then, and it has opened up different areas of application. It is particularly good for difficult material surfaces because it has a very low heat impact on the material (it is gentle and does not mark the material). One of its main advantages is its reproducibility (the quality of the weld). We have devised a monitoring system so that customers can evaluate, even after ten years, the quality of these welds. We can even link automated SRM technology to our monitoring system We have, for example, systems running in Asia and the US in the automotive industry. Other industries include construction, shipbuilding, farm equipment, general sheet metal engineering and aerospace (demanding welds for satellite applications) - it's a very wide field with great potential," added Oliver Pohlus.
