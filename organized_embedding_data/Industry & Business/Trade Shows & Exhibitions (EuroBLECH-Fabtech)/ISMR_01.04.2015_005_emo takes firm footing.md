# EMO takes firm footing

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

EMO MILANO 2015, the world exhibition of machine tools, robots and automation, will take place in Milan, Italy from 5 to 10 October 2015, in conjunction with the great Universal Exposition EXPO. Around 1,600 exhibitors, 150,000 visitors (representing over 100 countries) and over 120,000m2 net of exhibition surface are the figures expected by Pier Luigi Streparava, General Commissioner of EMO MILANO 2015. There will be a wide range of products on display: machine tools, robots, automation, additive manufacturing, mechatronic solutions and auxiliary technologies.

To date, over 110,000m2 net of exhibition surface have already been booked. More than 1,300 companies have already sent their applications to exhibit at EMO MILANO 2015. At the top of the ranking are the Italians, closely followed by the Germans – the Taiwanese rank third with regard to the number of participations, followed by the Chinese and the Swiss.

“According to figures processed by the Institute Oxford Economics, after a period of general recession starting from the present year, world consumption of machine tools will grow again constantly to achieve 71 billion euros in 2018 (+18% against 2015). Asia is the most important area with regard to consumption of production systems. Asian consumption will grow from 34.8 billion euros in 2015, to 42.3 billion euros in 2018 (almost double the value versus 2008), recording a 21.6% upturn,” explained EMO.

“The Asian consumption rate on total world consumption amounts to 58.1% in 2015 and, in 2018, will almost reach 60%. Europe, the second largest consumption area, will report a growth in demand for machine tools from 14.8 billion euros in 2015 to 17.2 billion euros in 2018 (+16.2%). The European consumption rate on the global total is slightly decreasing: 24.8% in 2015 and 24.3% in 2018. In the Americas, consumption will rise from 10.2 billion euros in 2015 to 11.4 billion euros in 2018 (+11.8%). In 2018, China alone will take up 45% of the world consumption of machine tools.“

In particular, Italian consumption of machine tools will increase from 1.6 billion euros to over 2 billion euros (+23%). Confirmation of better Italian prospects was also in the recent data processed by the Studies Dept. & Business Culture division of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the Italian machine tool and robotics association), recording that Italy is investing in machine tools again, taking the route to recovery.

In 2014, the machine tool order index collated by Italian manufacturers recorded a 14.7% average increase against 2013. ■
