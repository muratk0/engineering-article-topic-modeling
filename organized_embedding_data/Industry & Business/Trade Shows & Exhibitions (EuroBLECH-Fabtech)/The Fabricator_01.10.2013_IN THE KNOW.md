Inaugural GOLF4MFG Fundraiser — a Smashing Success

FMA’s CEO Ed Youdell presents trophy to the winning foursome: Joe Campise, Ryan Delahanty, John Campos, Erron Nielsen (not pictured)

The Boulder Ridge Country Club in Lake in the Hills, Ill.

The first annual

NBT GOLF4MFG

event at the Boulder Ridge Country Club in Lake in the Hills, Ill., was a huge success! Sixty-eight golfers came out to play, eat, drink, win prizes, and enjoy the day. The presentation of generous donation checks from Kelly Services, the CNA Foundation, and Production Tube Cutting Inc. kicked off the day during the lunch reception. Thanks to the event’s corporate sponsors: Mazak Optonics Corporation, TRUMPF Inc., Mitsubishi Laser, Feralloy Corporation, and CNA. All proceeds benefit Nuts, Bolts & Thingamajigs summer camps and scholarships.

As the temperature was climbing, so was the anticipation of the winning foursome for the event. A group from Gardner Business Media earned the bragging rights on the traveling trophy housed at the FMA headquarters. Special congratulations go to the contest winners: Kourtney Mulcahy (Hinshaw and Culbertson LLP) hit the longest drive; Erron Nielsen (SHL) got closest to the pin; Rebecca Citron (Mazak) made the longest putt; and Rick Dudek (Wells Far-go) toed the line with the straightest drive. To wrap things up, golfers enjoyed dinner and cold beverages. n

Politics and Business Just Don’t Mesh

For the past few years it has become abundantly obvious that politics plays a dramatic role in the conduct of business and equally obvious that businesspeople and politicians do not much understand each other’s worlds. This October will be a trying time for the businesspeople who need to plan more than a few hours in advance.

It is the month that we watch a fight over the federal budget and another fight over the debt ceiling. The stage is set for plenty of drama and last-minute dealmaking before a resolution is reached that will likely please nobody very much. The budget process is mired in wave after wave of continuing resolutions that will likely leave the whole sequester mess in place, and the debt ceiling histrionics do little except upset the rating agencies. Why does this dance occur every year? And why do political leaders seem so oblivious to the business community? It comes down to timing.

Business leaders are rewarded for thinking as far ahead as they possibly can. They buy needed material in advance to protect against shortages and price hikes, and they launch sales cycles 12 to 18 months in advance. They see potential political issues on the horizon and they react right away — usually with slowdowns and caution.

The political calendar is far different. There is no advantage to making a decision early as that just gives the opposition more time to attack. It also means there is less time to broker a deal if the requested action is going to be awkward with the constituents. The truth is that businesspeople have been reacting to what they expect to see in October during the summer months, while the political decision-makers have been of the opinion they had plenty of time to make a decision.

This is the uncertainty that many in the fabricating and manufacturing world have complained about for the last several years. It is not that business is unaccustomed to uncertainty — that has always existed due to competition and the vagaries of supply and demand. It is the certainty of political uncertainty that paralyzes business decision-making. n
