# APRIL IN PARIS

[TARİH: 01.02.2016 ISMR]

INDUSTRIE Paris, from 4-8 April 2016, is expected to attract 1,000 exhibitors and 25,000 visitors

INDUSTRIE Paris strives to offer a wide selection of solutions for SMEs, mid-cap companies and multinationals. From equipment to products and related services, it freely shares its resources and expertise with visitors as part of its mission to generate business for exhibitors through showcasing their equipment and products and to support visitors in their search for new processes to increase their production,” the show organiser, GL Events, told ISMR.

“Meetings at the show are encouraged around strong themes: Innovation, Training and the Factory of the Future. INDUSTRIE goes even further by offering a full schedule of events at the show. The exhibition, held in Paris on even years and Lyon on odd years, brings together major companies in the manufacturing field and draws approximately 1,000 exhibitors and 25,000 visitors. INDUSTRIE Paris presents a global range of equipment, products and consumables for the manufacturing of the future.”

The event will be held at the Parc des Expositions at Paris Nord Villepinte (Hall 5) in Paris from 4-8 April 2016. In one 60,000 m² hall, the exhibition has reinvented itself and offers a location without sector boundaries, including technological clusters to facilitate visitor orientation, for a holistic view of the overall manufacturing process. With nine technological sectors represented in a single hall, INDUSTRIE Paris is a key industrial exhibition in France and ranked in the European Top Five.

## Themed villages and awards

A number of themed villages will be held during the event as a focus for specific specialties, industry sectors and technological expertise.

The Village Impression 3D (Additive Manufacturing Village) will be held at INDUSTRIE Paris for the first time this April.

“Innovative and indispensable, additive manufacturing is naturally integrated into this major event. This process of making parts by successive accumulation of layers of materials will be showcased through the following technologies: 3D design; software; processes; media; printers; materials; additive manufacturing services; rapid prototyping; fab labs etc. The goal is to explore the remarkable potential of additive manufacturing,” explained the show organiser.

The Additive Manufacturing Village will showcase multiple applications that are relevant for all sectors such as customisation (customised manufacturing or restoration of parts), the creation of complex parts (e.g. in the medical field or for aerospace applications), material range (glass, plastics, metals or ceramics) and productivity gains (through shortened processes etc.).

The Strategy and Business Development Village at INDUSTRIE Paris offers advice and tips on various themes such as manufacturing organisation; marketing strategy; standardisation; human resources; industrial design; commercial activity etc.

The Start-Up Village at INDUSTRIE Paris is a cluster of several young companies chosen for their strong potential and innovative projects. The Factory of the Future is already at work at INDUSTRIE Paris.

For the first time at INDUSTRIE Paris, manufacturers can also compete for the Trophée du Manager Industriel de l’Année (Manufacturing Manager of the Year award). This award (initiated at the INDUSTRIE Lyon show in 2015) is given to a manager who has conducted an original initiative to optimize a factory’s operation.

The Trophées de l’Innovation (Innovation Awards) will also include four new categories to better meet the expectations of exhibitors: Eco-Efficiency; New Technologies; Industrial Performance and Digital Tools.

INDUSTRIE Paris is also developing a list of conferences on topical issues for the forthcoming event.

## New Laser Genius 2D

THE NEW LASER GENIUS from Prima Power combines linear drives, a carbon fibre carriage and a synthetic granite frame to produce dramatic speed and axis acceleration improvements from a dynamically stable structure.

“Power options of up to 6kW are available of the high brilliance laser source which requires no laser gases and delivers low operating costs in line with our Green Means® philosophy for sustainable manufacturing,” the manufacturer told ISMR.

“SMART Cut technology, built into the Laser Genius, minimises head travel by rapidly switching the laser beam for cutting grid shapes, resulting in cycle time savings of up to 30%. For thicker materials, MAX Cut technology provides rapid high quality piercing which can reduce cycle times by up to 40%. For high intensity production, the Laser Genius offers NIGHT Cut advanced process monitoring. This suite provides LPM piercing and cutting monitoring with the LPM process sensor, automatic re-start in the event of an error and operator warnings by email where action is required," it added.

The Laser Genius also includes Prima Power SIPS (Safety Impact Protection) to prevent damage to the laser head in the event of a collision and OPC (Optical Position Control) quick lens centring.

## Duplex stainless steel innovations

AGAINST A BACKDROP of increasing popularity for duplex grades in China, the 5th China International Duplex Stainless Steel Congress took place in Beijing from 26-27 November 2015. It allowed duplex producers and industry experts to meet with designers, manufacturers and end-users to exchange news and ideas. The congress was hosted by Outokumpu, the Stainless Steel Council of China Special Steel Enterprises’ Association and Taiyuan Iron and Steel Group.

Outokumpu showcased its recent R&D advances and experience with diverse applications. One of the highlights was the Forta FDX grades, Outokumpu’s innovative new formable duplex stainless steel. Poul-Erik Arnvig, Vice President Business Development for Outokumpu, opened the congress by reflecting on the continued increasing usage of duplex.

“Duplex has, for the last couple of decades, shown the highest growth rate amongst stainless steels and is predicted to continue the growth at a faster rate than the stainless steel demand in general on a global level,” he noted.

Erik Schedin, from Outokumpu’s Avesta Research Centre, presented data on the latest formable Forta FDX grades.

“Outokumpu Forta FDX grades provide a clever solution to the limited formability of existing duplex grades,” he said. “Combining the high durability, corrosion resistance and price-stable alloying of duplex grades, FDX further offers the fluent formability of standard austenitic grades.”

Schedin also elaborated on enhanced structural efficiency with duplex stainless steel in pressure vessels, which may be thinner and lighter, and have a longer life cycle. Duplex has also provided an alternative solution for civil engineering projects with long life cycles. ■
