## EVENT PREVIEW

## Innovations on show

Tube 2012, organised by Messe Düsseldorf GmbH, attracted 1,178 exhibitors from 48 countries as well as 34,659 visitors (55% from outside Germany). A special exhibition area on plastic tubing in Hall 7.1 will be a feature of this year’s TUBE exhibition.

At TUBE 2014, Dreistern will offer visitors the opportunity to study a tube mill equipped with multi-functional roll forming. The concept of multi-functional roll forming entails the integration of additional processes such as piercing, notching, embossing, fixing nuts or bolts and many other processes into tube mills. Any of these operations can be integrated highly flexibly at any position in the machine by exchanging work modules (approximately the same size as a Dreistern roll forming tool cassette). For even higher levels of flexibility, work modules can also be exchanged between different tube mills or roll forming machines.

“Multi-functional machines can be configured precisely and rapidly according to the actual requirements of a new product. The footprint of such a machine can be reduced by 30% to 50% compared to conventional technology. No redundant line component increases the machine-hour rate. This means more flexibility, but less cost and less space required,” explained the manufacturer.

Meta Vision Systems, a leading UK manufacturer of laser guidance systems for automating arc welding, will be celebrating thirty years of Welding with Vision at the TUBE 2014 exhibition. Meta was a spin-out from Oxford University in 1984, following a successful research project to develop a laser vision system for real-time control of robot welding. Meta will take its largest stand ever at the exhibition, 6B39, and will stage several live demonstrations of new products being launched.

In addition to remaining active in robot welding, the company has developed considerable expertise in many different tube and pipe applications over the past thirty years and is one of the leading suppliers of vision and control systems for the tube and pipe industry worldwide. Within an overall theme of applied innovation, Meta’s stand will show a completely new pipe measurement system.

Spiral pipe mill with Meta laser sensor

“With increasing demands for traceable measurement data from pipe buyers, we have harnessed our experience in pipe bevel measurement and pipe inspection to develop a suite of new pipe measurement products. These represent a major expansion our activity in the sector and will be on show for the first time in Düsseldorf,” the company told ISMR.

Meta’s recent developments in longitudinal pipe mills will be highlighted in partnership with Hyundai RB, which has built a new longitudinal pipe mill in Ulsan, Korea, using Meta systems at every stage of the pipe welding process. They are used to control tack welding, ID welding, OD weld joint milling and OD welding.

In the spiral pipe sector, Meta will be premiering a new video reviewing Meta’s new spiral mill control system. This includes mill control, welding control, laser vision and video monitoring, all within a single integrated and networked control system. ■

## ASIA IN VIEW

We examine opportunities in Asian automotive markets for sheet metal manufacturers and suppliers

The ASEAN region offers a significant opportunity to global automakers in the near-to-medium term, according to analyst consultancy Frost & Sullivan. Vijay Rao, Research Director, Asia Pacific at Frost & Sullivan confirmed the importance of ASEAN as a cluster – the region is likely to be the fifth largest automotive market in the world by 2019.

New analysis from Frost & Sullivan, ‘Strategic Analysis of ASEAN Automotive Outlook’, finds that the market is likely to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 5.8 per cent (2012-2019) to reach 4.71 million vehicles in 2019, mainly driven by rapid market expansions in Indonesia and Thailand.

Demand for machine tools and cutting tools is also expected to grow in emerging Asian economies, such as China and India, due to increased investments in the automotive, aerospace and defence industries that incorporate these tools into their production processes. With growth in the automotive and white goods segments, along with entry of multinational companies (MNCs), the steel sector in India, has also grown exponentially. The SSC (Steel Service Centre) market in India is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 18 per cent during FY 2017-18.

## Growing automotive markets

Mr Rao added that the low level of motorisation in ASEAN offers strong growth potential for the automotive market. He believes that passenger vehicle segments are likely to dominate the market. “Thailand, the key pick-up market in the region, is shifting to passenger vehicles with increased consumer preference for compact, environmentally friendly eco-cars,” he explained.

Mr Rao predicts Indonesia to emerge as the largest automotive market in ASEAN by 2019, accounting for 2.3 million vehicles, driven by sustained economic growth in the country, a growing middle classes with larger disposable incomes, increased investments in the automotive sector and the introduction of automotive regulations supporting market growth. Vehicle sales in Indonesia grew more than 12 per cent in the first half of 2013 due to a growing economy, rising wages and low interest rates. Vehicle production in Indonesia mostly caters to local sales driven mainly by the expected expansion in automotive demand and foreign investment inflow for production expansion.

Rao commented that automotive demand in Thailand is also expected to grow driven by an improved economy, more disposable incomes, capacity expansions by automakers and the launch of several new vehicle models.

“The Malaysian market is expected to grow supported by foreign model proliferation at competitive price points and by price reduction as a result of market liberalisation,” he said, adding that total vehicle production in ASEAN is expected to grow at a CAGR (2012-2019) of 8 per cent to hit 7.05 million units in 2019.

“Thailand is likely to continue its dominance as a major production hub in ASEAN due to expected significant capacity expansions, increased export and domestic demand and the availability of a skilled labour force with a well-developed automotive component industry,” he said.

Rao also noted that European and Chinese OEMs are looking at Malaysia as an assembly and manufacturing hub to set up production plants. Frost & Sullivan is predicting vehicle sales in Malaysia to grow by 4.2 per cent year-on-year to reach 675,000 units in 2014. It expects Malaysia’s GDP to grow at about 5.4 per cent in 2014, fuelled by strong domestic consumption and an improvement in exports.

## REGIONAL REPORT

To ensure that the measures outlined in Malaysia’s National Automotive Policy 2014 are effectively implemented, several roadmaps for the development of its domestic automotive industry have been drawn up. This is expected to be the catalyst to take local industry forward through a two-pronged approach and transform Malaysia into a production hub for energy-efficient vehicles (EEVs) by 2020.

## Investment and competition

The implementation of the ASEAN Economic Community (AEC) in 2015 is likely to reduce large Japanese automakers’ dominance in the region which will become a more level playing field for smaller players, F&S believes. Dushyant Sinha, Principal Consultant, Automotive & Transportation Practice Asia Pacific at Frost & Sullivan noted that Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia are the key automotive markets in ASEAN, accounting for 89 per cent of the passenger car market.

Indonesia, Thailand and Malaysia are the key automotive markets in ASEAN

However, he commented that the BRIC countries are much larger than the key ASEAN markets, in terms of industry volume, and compete aggressively for investment in the automotive sector. He added that, as one market, ASEAN is currently at the cusp of a high-growth phase, along with the BRIC markets. The BRIC markets, led by China and India, are the largest competitors for ASEAN in terms of market and investment. Their attractiveness stems from their larger market size and larger economies. As an integrated market, ASEAN will be more competitive and a better match to the BRIC economies, he added.

He believes that automotive OEMs and suppliers should focus on increasing value and reducing the cost of safety systems. Under the full implementation of the AEC, a structural change is likely in the automotive sector. “Local players, both OEMs and suppliers, stand to lose the most. Overall, market concentration is set to decrease,” he explained.

Myanmar’s automotive market is likely to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 7.8 per cent (2012-2019), driven by a growing economy, infrastructure development and increasing income. Dushyant Sinha believes that real growth is only likely to start after 2014.

“However, factors such as unpredictable regulatory changes, high car prices, an under-developed auto service market and inadequate road infrastructure might hinder potential growth,” he added, noting that, after decades of military control and closed door policies, Myanmar has gradually opened its economy to foreign trade and investment.

Dushyant said that a young labour force with a high two-wheeler ownership promises a potential car-buying group in the long term. Myanmar is highly dependent on two-wheelers, accounting for more than 80 per cent of the market, while passenger cars represent 11 per cent. Trucks and buses only make up 3 per cent and 1 per cent respectively. He expected Japanese brands to continue dominating Myanmar’s passenger vehicle market and Chinese and Korean brands to see growth.

Forthcoming industry events (such as BLECH China, 18th ASEAN Int. Sheet Metal Exhibition, EMTE-EASTPO Expo and MTA Vietnam) will also showcase the latest automotive developments in selected Asian markets. ■
