he 34th edition of the International Manufacturing Technology Show (IMTS) returns to Chicago’s McCormick Place Sept. 9-14. Conference highlights will include a new halfday Spanish-language conference, events for women in manufacturing, three job shop workshops, an automation conference for small to medium-sized machining operations, a supply chain forum, and an investor forum.

New machine tool technologies to be featured

Mazak will display a variety of machines from its Ez series, expanded with the addition of the 50-taper VC-Ez 32 with its 32-in. Y axis and the new 5-axis VC-Ez 20X, which will be processing parts.

The VC-Ez 32/50 vertical machining center is configurable with a range of options for many production needs. It features the Mazatrol SmoothEz CNC that offers both conversational and G-code programming capabilities.

Also highlighted will be the Quick Turn 250MSY CNC turning center, which provides milling and Y-axis capability, a direct-drive turret, a rotary tool milling spindle, and a second turning spindle. The Integrex i-100H ST multitasking center also will be displayed with a gantry loader.

Grinding products feature grains that help deliver long life

Saint-Gobain Abrasives will highlight its abrasive grinding products and technology, including new Norton RazorStar belts, quick-change discs, and fiber discs. Engineered-shaped sharp grains in an upright position and a supersized grinding aid help to reduce heat generation for cooler cuts and longer life on materials such as carbon steel, aluminum, stainless steel, and nickel alloys. In addition to impact grinding and off-hand applications, the belts are suitable for robotic applications.

Norton Quantum Prime grinding wheels, which feature proprietary nanocrystalline ceramic, also will be highlighted. According to the manufacturer, the grain delivers high grinding efficiency and part quality, as well as long wheel life. Wheels are available in a variety of types, including OD, ID, gear grinding, toolroom, disc grinding, mounted wheels, surface grinding, creepfeed, and flute grinding.

Tooling performs automated cross-hold deburring

Heule Precision Tool will feature COFA tooling for automated cross-hole deburring applications. This technology removes burrs from the front and back of a drilled through-hole without stopping or reversing the spindle.

Suitable for sloped, even, or uneven surfaces, the tooling provides an even, consistent edge break, the company states. It automates removal of hard-to-reach cross burrs that formerly were deburred manually, including elliptical holes and radiused edge breaks on flat parts.

Available in sizes 0.79 to 1.19 in., the tooling is suitable for applications including forks, yokes, common rails, castings, tubes with cross-bores, and other workpieces with cross-bores in their main bores.

Heule Precision Tool

Multisensor vision system designed for repetitive measurements

The L.S. Starrett Co. will be demonstrating its quality control inspection technology that supports Industry 4.0, IoT, and the digital factory.

The AVR400 multisensor vision system will be highlighted. It features a choice of optics, as well as touch probe options. It is suitable for repetitive measurements and automatic comparison to CAD files. A horizontal digital video comparator combining vision and optical comparator technology will also be demonstrated, featuring a patented interchangeable lens mounting system coupled to a high-resolution digital video camera. Digital overlays in the system are developed directly from DXF CAD files.

Also on display will be the new W4900 wireless digital indicator with touchscreen technology and the HD400 benchtop comparator with a MetLogix Mx200 digital readout.

The L.S. Starrett Co.

www.starrett.com

Conveyor helps keep coolant tanks clean

Jorgensen Conveyor and Filtration Solutions will feature a PermaClean system equipped with the new EcoFilter80 self-cleaning conveyor. The nondrum-style conveyor, which filters fine chips to 80 μm nominal, uses nondisposable filter boxes that help prevent chips and particulate from migrating to a machine tool coolant tank.

The PermaClean system, which adds options for secondary tank filtration and eliminates sludge buildup by adding agitation to the coolant collection tank, also will be equipped with a Will-Fill total coolant health system that monitors metalworking emulsion. Combining automatic measuring, filling, and conditioning, it alerts operators via a flashing color LED, buzzer, or email when attention is required.

Jorgensen Conveyor and Filtration Solutions

www.jorgensenconveyors.com

Two-press, two-robot cell performs unattended pressure vessel production

Greenerd Press & Machine Co. will discuss an automated two-press, two-robot production cell that enables unattended production of large aluminum pressure vessels.

Using sensors to locate pallets of blanks that have been placed in a staging area, the first robot picks a blank, detects and rejects any double blanks, and transfers the blank to an integrated and automated blank lube station. The robot then picks a previously lubricated blank and loads it into a deep-draw press with a 76-in. stroke. A second robot then catches and transfers the formed parts to a second press for a punching operation.

The two-press production cell uses a centralized control station that sets the operating parameters of both presses and also controls the action sequences of both robots. All equipment in the cell is updated automatically with new process parameters when changing between products.

System integrates software, machine tool controls to optimize production

Siemens will introduce Machinum to the North American market. The system brings together machine tool controls, digitalization software, and machine shop services to help manufacturers optimize their production processes, adapt to changing customer requirements and calculated uptime needs, and maximize productivity of the shop or production department.

Adaptive feed rate control helps reduce cycle times while maintaining a stable machining process. This results from instantaneous and continuous analysis of the current cutting conditions on a machine tool in real time, which automatically optimizes the feed rate.

Through strategic digitalization and software that integrates the machine tool CNCs and a shop’s internal communications, the system can help shops or production departments accommodate changes in design, quantity output, and machining characteristics.

Combination ERP/MES/QMS system links every job shop department

ProShop ERP will exhibit its digital ecosystem, a combination ERP/MES/QMS system for job shops. Integrating seamlessly with each other and nearly every other manufacturing software, this paperless digital operating platform links every job shop department from estimating to shipping.

Engineered for small to medium-sized manufacturing companies, the system can replace up to five software programs, the company states. Customized ERP modules help get jobs off to a cohesive start. The integrated MES system then tracks time, tooling, fixtures, workcells, personnel, and part programming information in real time. The QMS function features numerous modules that grab data from the ERP/MES systems, create reports reflecting preventive and corrective actions, and generate audits.

More than 25 dashboards provide shops with a big picture view of the entire operation, identifying waste and ineffective procedures in one digital ecosystem. According to the manufacturer, setup can be reduced up to 50% with total labor savings of 25% or more.

Robots bring automation topress brake bending

KUKA Robotics will demonstrate the robotic bending cells from Bystronic and the Gladwin Automation Division, both featuring the company’s robots.

The Bystronic bending cell provides flexible, on-demand automation in confined spaces for producing small and medium-sized parts. Comprising a KR Agilus robot and either the ByBend Star 40 or ByBend Star 80 press brake, the miniature-format cell delivers bending speeds of up to 30 mm/sec.

Equipped with a press brake, robot, end-of-arm tooling, offline programming software, and material handling conveyors, the Gladwin Automation cell handles a variety of bending jobs with little operator input. It offers 24/7 operation, eliminates the need for heavy lifting by operators, and helps reduce the risk of scrapped parts.
