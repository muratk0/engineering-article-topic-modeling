# THE WORLD OF METALWORKING

[TARİH: 01.08.2019 ISMR]

16-21 September 2019 in Hanover. Once again, the international trade fair for metalworking machines, solutions and services is being held in Germany, the world's third largest machine tool market.

"All major manufacturers of production technology will be presenting their products and services in Hanover. Nobody can afford to miss it," Christoph Miller, Director Exhibitions, VDW (German Machine Tool Builders' Association) told ISMR.

As of the middle of April, over 2,000 exhibitors from 47 countries had already applied to participate. Companies include Mazak; Siemens; TRUMPF; KUKA and others. The event will showcase the entire spectrum of metalworking technology – from machine tools, high-precision tools and automation components through to customised systems.

## Smart technologies in focus

In 2017, just under half of the roughly 130,000 visitors travelled to Hanover from outside Germany, with around 160 countries represented. The proportion rose to 64 per cent for exhibitors. EMO Hannover 2017 attracted almost 2,230 exhibitors from 44 countries. EMO is a registered trademark of the European Association of the Machine Tool Industries (CECIMO).

This year, EMO Hannover is addressing one of the greatest challenges facing the industry with its theme of 'Smart technologies driving tomorrow's production!'.

ISMR SAYS:

"The theme for EMO Hannover 2019 is 'Smart technologies driving tomorrow's production!"

"Smart technologies are the key to greater productivity, better quality and more flexibility. These are the classic goals for new and further developments in production technology. Digitalisation and intelligent factory networking also facilitate new functions such as process monitoring or predictive maintenance, which go one step further with self-learning machines based on smart data and data analysis. These create the basis for new business models like the platform economy, allowing customers and providers to reach the next level of efficiency," added the show organiser.

New Industry 4.0 concepts are emerging in all areas: for machines, components, controls, measurement systems and material flows.

"EMO Hannover 2019 will be a hotspot for new offerings and solutions for the smart factory," concluded Miller.

## Industry 4.0 and IoT in production

IoT will be bundled in various formats at EMO Hannover to highlight all facets of Industry 4.0. There will be a dedicated exhibition area for IoT in production. This section is reserved for individual exhibitors in control technology, software and automation technology. Production planners today not only invest in machines, they also want to see integrated solutions.

"National stands will also be featured in this area. We have received applications from China, Taiwan and Switzerland. We are also in discussion with France and Japan. They all have their own national Industry 4.0 or IoT initiatives and are cordially invited to present them at EMO Hannover 2019," added VDW.

In addition, the VDW has joined forces with the software association from the VDMA (German Engineering Federation, the second-largest customer sector for machine tools). Together, they are organising a forum on the latest Industry 4.0 and machine learning trends, including presentations and panel discussions which will run throughout EMO. The future of work will be a further focus.

There will be a dedicated exhibition area for IoT in production

"Umati – a common language for Industry 4.0, will also come under the spotlight at EMO Hannover 2019. The interface standard for machine tools based on OPC UA, with all its functionalities, will be presented at the show. This provides neutral and open connection of machines to higher-level IT systems. It is a prerequisite for the success of Industry 4.0. Only then can data from different machine manufacturers with different control systems be transferred to ERP and MES systems or into the cloud. There they are further processed or evaluated and form the basis of new services and functions," explained EMO Hannover.

The VDW, with eight well-known German machine tool manufacturers and all major control suppliers, launched the umati interface more than a year ago. Umati stands for universal machine tool interface. The international machine tool community is now able to participate in revising and disseminating the standard. The goal is to present a complete implementation of the specification at EMO Hannover 2019 – to the extent that it is available by then – together with a larger number of partners.

## The future of industrial production

EMO Hannover 2019 will also be addressing production-related challenges, from machine technology and IT solutions through to the changing world of work. With many supporting events, it will show how networked production is increasingly spreading to all areas. Order processing, production preparation and planning, manufacturing and logistics will all change. New functions and services are being created. Those working in industry need to have the skills to perform these tasks.

EMO Hannover will showcase the latest machines, plus efficient technical solutions, product-supportive services, sustainability in the production process and more. Its principal focus is on metal cutting and forming machine tools; production systems; high-precision tools; automated material flows; computer technology; industrial electronics and accessories.

Trade visitors to EMO come from all major sectors of industry, such as machinery and plant manufacturers; the automotive industry and its component suppliers; the aerospace sector; precision mechanics and optics; shipbuilding; medical technology; tool and die manufacture; steel and lightweight construction.

## 3D printing and additive manufacturing

Additive Manufacturing is the third trend currently being talked about in industry, alongside Industry 4.0 and Automation.

"A lot is happening here, there's huge interest and a great need for information," said Miller. Disadvantages such as the time required to manufacture a workpiece, the lack of materials suitable for a particular application, automation potential or the need for post-processing are gradually diminishing. Additive manufacturing is increasingly being integrated into industrial production as a result.

Powerful and demanding customer sectors such as the automotive and aircraft industries or medical technology are increasingly requesting 3D printing and are themselves developing more and more parts with geometries that can only be made using additive processes. Examples include lightweight components made from complex materials or spare parts in the automotive industry, the hydraulic control block for the Airbus 320 or the T-pieces for the fuel system in the Airbus 400 M, as well as bionic structures in medical technology, implants, dental crowns, hearing aids and surgical instruments.

According to a recent VDMA survey, almost half the companies in the mechanical engineering sector, for example, are already using additive components. About half of those surveyed also use their own 3Dprinting systems to produce prototypes and samples. However, 44 percent also print spare parts, serial parts and tools. About a quarter of the printed parts are made of metal. Around one fifth of EMO visitors stated in the 2017 visitor survey that they were interested in 3D printing.

"That is why we are organising in 2019 the additive manufacturing circle, together with the Konradin Media Group," says Miller. The special show complements the offerings of the major manufacturers of 3D printing equipment, such as Stratasys and TRUMPF, who already have their own stands at EMO Hannover. The additive manufacturing circle is aimed at companies from the entire additive manufacturing process chain who are participating in EMO Hannover and can exploit the enormous interest shown by visitors. These include smaller system providers and companies in the software, 3D scanning, materials and production service sectors. For many customers, service providers are the first entry point to the technology," explained the show organiser.

"We are convinced that the additive manufacturing circle will become a magnet for visitors," added Miller. "The process is developing at a rapid pace and there is still a great deal of untapped potential. The attraction of EMO Hannover is that the entire AM process chain, including post-processing, can be compared and contrasted to conventional machining processes."

## Recruitment and skills

Industry is currently facing challenges arising from the shortage of skilled employees. Specific conditions may differ, but the need for action is apparent worldwide. In addition, the demands placed on employees in industry are currently undergoing fundamental changes as a result of networking and automation.

"Many industrialised countries are struggling with demographic change, which basically means that there are fewer skilled workers who could migrate to other sectors," explained Miller of the VDW. "Emerging countries, on the other hand, have sufficient numbers of young people, but in many cases no efficient training system to produce the skilled workers they need."

VDI-Nachrichten, the magazine from Germany's biggest engineering federation, will therefore organise a two-day event at EMO Hannover on the 17-18 September 2019. The target group includes EMO exhibitors and customers presenting themselves as attractive employees. The fair will target academics and industry specialists. It will be accompanied by a forum on successful personnel marketing and job application tips.

In Germany, the Youth Education and Development Foundation for Mechanical Engineering, a subsidiary of VDW and VDMA, has worked intensively over the past ten years to improve and update training in the metalworking professions in cooperation with vocational colleges, authorities and other institutions. It has also launched training projects for digitalisation.

All of this will be presented at EMO Hannover 2019 as part of the special youth show, aimed at fostering highly skilled young talent. The special show will be accompanied by a forum on modern strategies for personnel development, recruitment and on tips for making successful applications.
