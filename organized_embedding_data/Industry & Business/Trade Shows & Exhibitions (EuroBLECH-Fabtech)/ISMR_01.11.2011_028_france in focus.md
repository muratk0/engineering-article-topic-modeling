# FRANCE IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2011 ISMR]

ISMR previews MIDEST 2011 in Paris, run in conjunction with TOLEXPO and Maintenance Expo, which is expected to attract almost 40,000 trade visitors.

The 41st MIDEST, a leading industrial subcontracting show, will run from 15 – 18 November 2011 at the Paris Nord Villepinte Exhibition Centre in France. Currently, MIDEST’s figures are tracking those of 2010 both in terms of area and exhibitor numbers and around 1,750 exhibitors are expected to be present when the show opens its doors on 15 November.

MIDEST 2011 will be held in conjunction with Maintenance Expo, the show for the full range of industrial and service sector maintenance solutions, and TOLEXPO, the international exhibition of equipment for the production and working of metal sheets, coils, tubes and sections. Last year, the show recorded a 3 per cent increase in space and a 1.5 per cent increase in exhibitor numbers. There were almost 1,750 exhibitors, 40 per cent of which were from outside France and were drawn from some forty countries.

“In order to thrive, companies have to continue to innovate and to help its exhibitors and visitors the forthcoming MIDEST is setting an example,” the show organiser told ISMR. “Numerous new initiatives have been launched: the creation of an Environment & Services Village, the Partnership / Alliance Award, the choice of Portugal as country of the year, the launch of the b2fair – Business to Fair® business meetings, a clearer sector structure, a downloadable show app for IPhones, Androids and Blackberries to make visiting even easier etc…”

At 60 per cent, French subcontracting continues to account for the bulk of the exhibitors and 17 French regions bringing together some 600 companies are taking a total area of more than 6,500 m² in the regional and national pavilions sector. One of them, Brittany, is making a high profile return. On the international front, over 650 companies from more than 30 countries are participating and 20 of these countries will have oficial national pavilions.

“The trend in 2011 is for a fall in Asian exhibitors and a steep rise in those from Eastern Europe and countries such as Bosnia-Herzegovina, Serbia, the Czech Republic, Hungary, Romania, Bulgaria and Poland. As far as Western Europe is concerned, Germany has moved ahead strongly and Portugal, country of the year, is a real stand-out posting record participation. A whole host of special events will be linked to its presence. There will be a pavilion at the show’s south entrance divided into an area where the country’s institutions will present its industry and subcontracting and a part hosting individual Portuguese subcontractors and manufacturers. There will be also be a dedicated conference programme and a series of round tables,” continued the organiser.

## NEW INITIATIVES

In terms of sector representation, MIDEST 2011, a general industrial subcontracting show, has strengthened its position and is offering a higher profile for all its sectors which, in many cases, benefit from special projects run in conjunction with industry partners. A number of sectors whose structure was not suficiently clear have undergone considerable development. These include electronics and forging and the new joint ventures/villages such as European fastenings, plastics or special machines, coupled with the addition of a new one: wood processing.

## ENVIRONMENTAL VILLAGE

The Environmental Solutions Village will deal with eight major themes: Treatment of Water, Industrial Waste and Air; Recycling and the Exploitation of Industrial Waste; Soil Remediation; Energy Production and Saving; Eco-Solutions for Transport; Analysis, Measurement and Monitoring; Certification and Standardisation; Services and Advice.

Another area where MIDEST is innovating is in helping visitors to find the subcontracting expertise that they are looking for more quickly and more easily. To make it easier to identify the principal activity of each exhibitor when walking along the aisles, each stand will have a colour-coded sector sign used throughout the show and in particular for the exhibitors in the regional French and international pavilions.

Visitors who have an IPhone, an Android or a Blackberry will also be able to download a free app by using a flash code. Amongst other things, this app will guide them around the hall by displaying the site of the stand they are looking for. It is a development that carries on from the major efforts made over the last few months on the Internet front via the www.midest.com site, as well as an increased presence on social networks such as Twitter and Viadeo, a special hub that allows professionals to discuss industry news.

Finally, several events that have already proved their worth will be making a return. Some sixty free lectures, both comprehensive and short format, will cover the latest technical, strategic or economic advances in subcontracting. The Technology Centres manned by the CETIM (mechanical engineering technical centre), the CTIF (the development centre for the materials shaping industries – foundry) and the LRCCP (the research and monitoring laboratory for rubber and plastics) will also be offering their advice and expertise to visitors and exhibitors.

Nearly 40,000 trade visitors from all sectors of activity are expected to visit to meet these exhibitors and some 15 per cent of them will be international customers drawn from over 70 countries. The initial pre-registration figures are encouraging.

For the first time, MIDEST is offering a programme of business meetings set up by b2fair – Business to Fairs. In partnership with the forty or so organisations in the Enterprise Europe Network, its aim is to organise qualified meetings between potential partners (subcontractors and/or customers) who are looking to forge real international contacts in a specific sector.

In another development, the MIDEST Awards have added a new category – Partnership / Alliance – alongside the existing five: Innovation, Model Implementations, International, Organisation and Design offices.

MIDEST is also highlighting the environment through the launch of an Environment Solutions Village, the organisation of lectures on environment topics and the creation of the IFETS pavilion (French Institute of the Environment, Technologies and Services), which will offer information about developments on regulations, on technical subjects for the prevention and treatment of pollution or those that relate to standardisation / certification. ■
