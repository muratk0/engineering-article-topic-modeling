# Don’t miss EuroBLECH 2014

[TARİH: 01.04.2014 ISMR]

THE 23rd EuroBLECH International Sheet Metal Working Technology Exhibition will open its doors at the exhibition grounds in Hanover from 21-25 October 2014. Already, more than 1,300 exhibiting companies from 40 countries have booked their stand space at the world’s leading exhibition for the sheet metal working industry. They will occupy a net exhibition space of more than 87,000m² across eight exhibition halls.

“The high demand for stand space goes hand in hand with the positive economic situation in this industry sector. Renowned for its international trade audience that is ready to invest, EuroBLECH is the leading business platform for the industry’s key players as well as small and medium-sized enterprises,” said show organiser, Mack Brooks Exhibitions.

Exhibitors will present live demonstrations of an impressive range of machines, tools and systems. Visitors will discover an extensive product range, from conventional systems through to advanced high-tech processes, as well as gain an overview of what is available worldwide.

EuroBLECH showcases the entire sheet metal working technology chain: sheet metal; semi-finished and finished products; handling; separation; forming; flexible sheet metal working; joining; welding and surface treatment; processing of hybrid structures; tools; quality control; CAD/CAM systems and R&D. For further details, see www.euroblech.com ■

ISMR visited sheet metal specialist LVD in Belgium to view its latest technology innovations and gauge its view on global markets

LVD Company nv is marking its 60th anniversary as an innovator in the design and manufacture of sheet metalworking equipment for fabrication. The company began producing hydraulic presses in 1952. Today, the LVD Group is a leading manufacturer of a comprehensive range of sheet metal/plate working machine tools and software solutions, including laser cutting systems; punch presses; press brakes; guillotine shears and mid-level automation systems, integrated and supported by its CADMAN® PC-based Windows® compatible software. It currently employs over 1000 people in over 45 countries.

LVD maintains production units, sales and customer service operations in more than 45 countries. In addition to its headquarters in Gullegem, Belgium, the company operates production facilities in France, Central Europe, Asia and the United States. LVD also maintains technical centres in Belgium, the US, the UK, China, Thailand, Korea, Malaysia, the United Arab Emirates and, most recently, India. Its products serve a variety of industries such as subcontract/job shop; fabricated metal products; transportation; construction; agricultural; and electrical and electronic equipment.

ISMR visited its headquarters and production facility in Gullegem, Belgium to view its latest sheet metal innovations and gauge its view of global markets.

## World class manufacturing initiative

“As a manufacturer, we have had to constantly evolve our own production processes through lean production (over 15 years now) and green initiatives to save energy, such as solar panels on our roof. We are also now implementing WCM (World Class Manufacturing) processes here in Gullegem, in our factory in the US and at our factory in Slovakia. WCM is a process-driven approach that improves throughput, reduces waste and ensures product quality. We’ve embarked on this continuous process improvement to create an even leaner organisation and to remain flexible and responsive to our customers,” explained Matthew Fowles.

The solar panel installation will enable LVD to generate more than 30% of its yearly electricity from a renewable energy source, as well as to reduce carbon dioxide emissions. The installation also feeds renewable source power back to Belgium’s national grid during weekends and holidays.

Under the guidance of its new Director of World Class Manufacturing, Peter Maes, the company is evolving its manufacturing practices and efficiency following the first adoption of lean manufacturing principles by the company in 1995.

WCM is primarily focused on continuous improvement in quality, cost, lead time, flexibility and customer service. As part of its WCM initiative, LVD’s production assembly departments are participating in an extensive programme of process improvements following the principles of the ‘house of lean’, seven mudas, continuous improvements, 5S etc. aimed at optimising each individual step of the assembly operation to increase throughput, reduce waste and ensure the highest finished product quality. Sequential methods of performing work are being replaced with concurrent methods to compress time, and functional and hierarchical divisions of duties are being expanded by team-driven activities.

## From Industry 4.0 to Art to Part 2.0

LVD is constantly developing enterprise solutions for customers to help them with the entire process flow of their parts. Sheet metal manufacturers face common challenges such as shorter lead times, increased part complexity and the need to work to smaller batches. LVD has therefore created holistic solutions to help manufacturers cope with the entire production process and track a part through each step of a production process.

“The original LVD Art to Part principle, several years ago, was to make the part once and make it right. This concept has now evolved. Now, it is about the actual throughput and not just the right part. Approximately 65% of customers right now are subcontractors, all using 3D CAD systems and all trying to make one-piece parts with less welding. They are looking to see how they can take the more complex parts, bring them through the CAD system and schedule them for production in smaller batches etc.,” explained Matthew Fowles.

New OSM (Open Sheet Metal) technology – a new LVD file format for 3D files – can seamlessly import files from SolidWorks with no feature loss. LVD’s CADMAN Job software can then schedule it to control the flow. CADMAN-Job organises and schedules the fabrication shop workload, classifying and grouping jobs in the most efficient manner to increase throughput, minimize set-up time and streamline work flow. It manages the day-to-day relationship between a user’s ERP system, CAM and workshop to improve machine production time and fabrication resources. The software creates or imports work orders from an ERP system, generates jobs for bending or laser cutting, minimizing tool set-up and lens/nozzle change. It also tracks the progress of each work order, provides estimated and real production time information, as well as generating lists to launch any re-work necessary.

“One of the biggest opportunities that we have today is that more and more fabricating companies are becoming more focused on the big fabricating picture in controlling the whole process, part flow, part level management etc. It helps them with challenges like shorter lead times, smaller batches, increased part complexity etc. We understand how to make things in sheet metal – and this is our greatest advantage,” concluded Carl Dewulf.
