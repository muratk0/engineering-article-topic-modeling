Show Preview

What to expect at FABTECH 2024

By

Rafael Guerrero

FABTECH arrives in Orlando, Fla., for three days this fall, attracting companies, manufacturers, and metal fabricators from all over.

FABTECH

T

his October, the newest attraction in the Orlando area won’t be a new ride at one of its iconic theme parks. But just like a new theme park ride, it will attract thousands of people.

This attraction will be a familiar name to readers: FABTECH.

For the first time ever, North America’s largest event for all things metal forming, fabricating, welding, and finishing will set up shop in the city of Orlando, in the heart of Florida and its fast-growing manufacturing scene.

The show, co-organized by the Fabricators and Manufacturers Association (FMA), SME, American Welding Society (AWS), Precision Metalforming Association (PMA), and the Chemical Coaters Association Intl. (CCAI), is expected to draw more than 35,000 attendees, more than 1,500 exhibitors from around the world, and showcase more than 500 new products at the Orange County Convention Center on Oct. 15-17.

The show remains a unique and fun experience for all involved. Attendees can purchase the newest laser equipment and inquire about powder coating, all under one roof. It puts buyers and sellers together in an instant and provides free and paid educational and networking opportunities.

"It would take months to do what they can do in just a few days at FABTECH," said Mark Hoper, FMA senior vice president of expositions and media.

"If you’ve got a need for equipment, there’s a good chance you’re going to go to FABTECH because it’s a one-stop shop where you can see all of your manufacturing needs—from forming and fabricating to finishing to welding," he added.

As one of the most-visited cities in the U.S., Orlando is well prepared to handle tourists and convention/conference attendees alike, with a large international airport, various hotel options, and convention facilities at its disposal.

Speaking of convention centers, the Orange County Convention Center is the second-largest convention facility in the U.S. At FABTECH, visit the North/South Building to check out the Forming & Fabricating, 3D/Additive Manufacturing, METALFORM, and Tube & Pipe exhibitor pavilions. Visit the West Building for the Welding and Finishing pavilions as well as the new Automation pavilion.

New to FABTECH in 2024 is the Emerging Leaders Program, a three-day initiative for young professionals under the age of 35 that includes a keynote luncheon, interactive workshop, guided show floor tours, and exclusive networking events. Those interested in applying to the program can visit the FABTECH website for more details. If accepted, the registration fee is $125.

As in years past, attendees can earn credit toward professional certifications from SME, FMA, and AWS at FABTECH conference sessions. These sessions are broken down into 15 technology tracks—including cutting, finishing, robotics, welding, and workforce development—and designed to help attendees gather information they can take back to their shops, facilities, and offices.

And don’t miss out on this year’s Special Events, which range from the Women of FABTECH Breakfast to the FABTECH 2024 State of the Industry panel discussion to keynote presentations from astronaut Peggy Whitson and Titans of CNC CEO Titan Gilroy.

Don’t miss out as this one-stop-shop event visits the Sunshine State!

FABTECH 2024 State of the Industry

This panel discussion will feature leaders who will share their invaluable insights on current trends, challenges, and innovations shaping the metal fabrication industry. This session will delve into topics such as the impact of global economic dynamics and current business challenges and outlooks. The impact processing technology and AI are having on the industry and how these major companies are navigating those challenges will also be discussed. Attendees can expect to hear how these companies and leaders approach these challenges and how they are preparing for the year ahead.

The presenters include Tim Heston, senior editor, The Fabricator; David Harrold, vice president—corporate purchasing, Alro Steel Corp.; Greg Frye, CEO and president, Anchor Fabrication; and Eric Miller, president, Miller Fabrication Solutions.
