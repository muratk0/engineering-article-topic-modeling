AMADA WELD TECH celebrates grand opening of its East Coast Technical Center

AMADA WELD TECH Inc., a manufacturer of welding, marking, cutting, sealing, and bonding technology equipment and systems in Monrovia, Calif., celebrated the grand opening of its East Coast Technical Center in High Point, N.C., on Oct. 13, 2022. At the event, senior leaders from both AMADA WELD TECH and AMADA AMERICA came together to welcome more than 30 regional customers to its new lab.

The event provided an opportunity for customers to get a first-hand look at the new facility, which provides a venue for application development, hands-on training, and sample evaluation.

Attendees toured the entire 252,000-sq.-ft. space, where they were able to view the manufacturing area, technical center, and showroom. The event concluded with live demos and a Q&A session.

Kohler Power Systems expands generator plant in Wisconsin

Kohler Power Systems has completed an expansion to its North American generator manufacturing facility in Mosel, Wis. The project, which began in March 2021, includes a 155,000-sq.-ft. production, testing, and warehousing space, as well as a 10,000-sq.-ft. customer experience center.

The factory builds large diesel industrial generators and integrated power systems for such markets as data centers and health care. The expansion addresses current and future capacity requirements to provide a safer, more efficient, and seamless flow through assembly, testing, and enclosing of large generator sets from 250 to 4,000 kW.

The new customer experience center allows guests to engage in hands-on training and collaborate with product engineers.

American Friction Welding expands capacity with new 50-ton friction welding center

American Friction Welding has installed a new Thompson 50-ton rotary friction welding center at its facility in Waukesha, Wis., to expand capacity and provide manufacturing redundancy and scheduling flexibility. The center features enhanced technology with rate control and forge collapse capability to help ensure weld repeatability and maintain length tolerances.

The new equipment, the company’s 17th high-precision friction welding center, will primarily serve the automotive and electric vehicle market.

AFW provides bi-metallic friction welding of dissimilar metals and near-net-shape parts using direct-drive rotary friction welding.

Hexagon, Raytheon Technologies developing 3D printing simulation tool

Hexagon and Raytheon Technologies have partnered to develop a simulation tool that will enable evaluation and optimization of metal additive manufacturing processes through the prediction of thermal history and defects at the laser path and powder layer scale.

Combining technologies from Hexagon’s Manufacturing Intelligence division and Raytheon Technologies’ Pratt and Whitney business, the new software will predict defects before 3D printing begins. This analysis will help designers and engineers evaluate the print outcome and test combinations of materials, geometries, and print processes.

"The ability to evaluate full laser powder bed fusion parts on the mesoscale will reduce the effort to achieve part certification and thereby support industrialization of metal 3D printing," said Jeff Robertson, Hexagon’s director of global business development.

Friedman Industries begins shipping product from new Texas facility

Friedman Industries, a manufacturer and processor of steel products based in Longview, Texas, has begun shipping material to customers from its newly constructed, 70,000-sq.-ft. coil processing facility in Sinton, Texas.

Material is processed on a new stretcher leveler cut-to-length line that handles steel coils up to 1 in. thick and 96 in. wide, with yields exceeding 100,000 PSI. The facility currently is staffed to operate a single shift, but the company is hiring and training additional personnel so a second shift can be operated as production ramps up.

Canoo to acquire vehicle manufacturing facility in Oklahoma

Canoo has entered into an agreement to acquire a vehicle manufacturing facility in Oklahoma City for producing its LDV and LV vehicles. Featuring a dedicated training center, the facility will employ more than 500 people and be equipped to produce 20,000 electric vehicles per year by the end of 2023.

The existing commercial site offers more than 630,000 sq. ft. of space and significant room for further expansion. It will be adapted to accommodate a full vehicle assembly line with robotics, a paint shop, and upfitting center and to be powered with clean energy.

Momentum Manufacturing Group acquisitions expand capabilities into semiconductor market

Metal manufacturer Momentum Manufacturing Group (MMG) has announced its entry into the semiconductor market with the acquisition of two companies that support semiconductor manufacturing.

Little Enterprises in Ipswich, Mass., and Evans Industries in Topsfield, Mass., have joined MMG, adding 86,000 sq. ft. of manufacturing capacity and nearly 160 skilled team members to the Georgetown, Mass.-based enterprise. Both companies provide precision metal machining and manufacture components that support wafer fabrication equipment. The acquisitions will enhance MMG’s existing manufacturing operation, which includes 10 facilities across New England.

"The acquisitions add high-end precision machining and additional robotic technologies to our existing portfolio and give us a stronger presence in the semiconductor and defense industries," said Jim Moroney, CEO of MMG.

Novarc Technologies partners with Lincoln Electric

Novarc Technologies Inc., Vancouver, B.C., a provider of cobots and AI-based machine vision systems for autonomous welding, has announced a partnership with arc welding equipment manufacturer Lincoln Electric.

As part of the partnership, Novarc will integrate Lincoln Electric’s welding technologies, including its patented Surface Tension Transfer root pass technology and HyperFill twin-wire GMAW systems, into Novarc’s automated cobot pipe welding systems. Additionally, Lincoln Electric will promote Novarc’s Spool Welding Robot for automated cobot process pipe welding.

The agreement will allow Novarc to further collaborate on more products, including incorporating AI and machine learning.

Weaton Companies acquires Steffensmeier Welding and Manufacturing

Iowa-based manufacturer Weaton Companies has acquired a strategic interest in Steffensmeier Welding and Manufacturing, a privately held, woman-owned small business.

Located in Pilot Grove, Iowa, SWM offers custom welding, CNC machining, laser cutting, and metal fabrication for end users in the agriculture, refrigeration, government, and industrial products industries.

Ryerson acquires Excelsior Inc.

Industrial metals processor and distributor Ryerson Holding Corp., Chicago, has acquired Excelsior Inc. The Fresno, Calif.-based fabricator offers machining, laser and waterjet cutting, welding, and complex assemblies.

Ryerson has about 100 operations in the U.S., Canada, Mexico, and China.

"Since 1996, Excelsior has served customers with the equipment and expertise to exceed their expectations," added Ray Roush, Excelsior’s CEO and founder. "We are pleased to continue that legacy as the newest member of the Ryerson family of companies."

Wall Colmonoy to modernize New Mexico plant

Wall Colmonoy has announced plans for a $2.5 million modernization of its Alloy Products plant in Los Lunas, New Mexico. The 52,000-sq.-ft. facility manufactures nickel-based hardfacing and brazing products, as well as the Fusewelder torch, Spraywelder system and NicroSpray systems.

Upgrades are being made to plant infrastructure and environment, alloy furnace equipment, and the R&D laboratory. New equipment in the lab includes a wet/dry sieve vibrator, hardness tester, robotic paste dispenser, vacuum mixer, digital microscope, ICP analyzer, carbon/sulfur analyzer, and moisture analyzer.

The melting deck has been reinforced to improve and secure structural integrity, and a central vacuum system was installed to improve dust collection.

Hypertherm Associates to close its legal entity in Russia

Hypertherm Associates, a manufacturer of industrial cutting products and software in Hanover, N.H., today announced plans to formally close its legal entity in Russia. This announcement follows a decision in March of this year to suspend business because of Russia’s military invasion of Ukraine.
