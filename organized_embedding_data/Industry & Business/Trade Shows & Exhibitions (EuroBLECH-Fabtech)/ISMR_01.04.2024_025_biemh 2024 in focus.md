A historic anniversary

BIEMH is 63 years-old this year. The first International Machine Tool Biennial took place 63 years ago, on 3 March 1961, at the historic Bilbao Trade Fair.

Featuring 226 company exhibitors, and attended by thousands of Spanish and overseas visitors, this first Fair laid the foundations for today's global event.

## BIEMH 2022 in the spotlight

In 2022, BIEMH welcomed 35,145 visitors from 52 countries as well as more than 1,400 exhibiting companies from 28 countries. Over 3,400 machines, products, services and innovations were showcased at the event and 300 major buyers were invited.

The sectors that attracted the greatest interest were process and manufacturing automation; machine tool accessories; tooling; machine tool components; additive manufacturing and 3D printing; machine tool by chip removal; parts and tool handling and digitalisation. The profile of professionals who attended were those who held the positions of director or manager, owner, head of department and technician.

A total of 300 major buyers visited BIEMH to see the latest advanced manufacturing. Overseas delegates arrived at BEC from countries such as Argentina; Canada; Colombia; Ecuador; Egypt; Finland; Guatemala; India; Mexico; Nigeria; Turkey; Czech Republic; Canada; Slovakia; Portugal and the United States.

The event also featured awards, such as the Advanced Manufacturing and Digital Innovation Awards 2022 organised by AFM Cluster, with the support of BEC and Beaz. A new feature was the prize for the most innovative start-up.■

## The smart home

Manufacturers are using new digital technologies to design products that are intuitive to use and enable consumers to control and manage intelligent appliances. The HCA 1.0 interface specification has established an industry standard for Cloud-to-Cloud (C2C) interoperability across long-life appliances, HVAC systems and TVs within the connected home ecosystem. This aims to ensure that longlife appliances, HVACs and TVs are seamlessly interoperable with one another, regardless of manufacturer.

Any collection of gadgets, systems or appliances that are connected to a single network and may operate independently and from a distance are referred to as smart home automation. A connected home is a more general term for a home's technology when it functions as one system. As an illustration, the home's thermostat; lighting; audio speakers; security cameras; TVs; locks; appliances and more are all interconnected into a single system that can be managed from a smartphone or via a mobile touchscreen device.

## Global home appliance market

According to analyst, Mordor Intelligence, the home appliances' market size is estimated at US$ 537.28 billion in 2024. It is expected to reach US$ 664.53 billion by 2029, growing at a CAGR of 4.34% during the forecast period (2024-2029).

“Technological advances have led to the development of smart appliances and are expected to drive market growth over the forecast period. Smart appliances offer advanced features and are more energyefficient. The rise in consumer disposable income, high living standards and the need for comfort encourage consumers to upgrade their existing appliances to smarter versions and is further expected to impact market demand. Product innovation, new product development, product differentiation and the integration of numerous value-added features are further expected to catapult market demand over the next few years," explained the analyst.

Whirlpool, for example, has introduced new smart appliance features that enable consumers to get more out of their smart kitchen appliances with connected updates such as new oven cycles and connectivity with popular kitchen gadgets. Since 2018, it has been creating smart home appliances that make providing care in the kitchen and laundry room easier for its consumers, whether that is being able to preheat the oven from the soccer field or receiving an alert on their phone when it's time to switch the laundry to the dryer.

On 8 January 2024, Panasonic announced an expanded partnership with Fresco, a smart kitchen platform for the appliance brands. The companies will collaborate to deliver a cooking assistant for Panasonic kitchen appliances, starting with the Panasonic HomeCHEF 4-in-1 multioven. Panasonic showcased the new smart appliance and connected cooking experience at the Consumer Electronics Show (CES) in Las Vegas (USA) in January 2024.

Panasonic is embracing connectivity by incorporating Fresco's AI and smart cooking technology into its multi-oven products. Within the Panasonic app, Fresco's AI cooking assistant will allow home cooks to customise recipes to fit dietary preferences, make ingredient substitutions based on what's in the pantry, and seamlessly adjust serving size and cooking settings.

"Consumers are increasingly opting for wireless and technologically advanced products, mostly for the convenience they offer. Furthermore, a rise in replacement sales in developed regions is also expected to drive market demand. This may be attributed to a rise in per capita income, coupled with rapid urbanisation," explained Mordor Intelligence.
