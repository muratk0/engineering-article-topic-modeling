ix people from the U.S. and the United Kingdom, including one grand prize winner, were named as the winners of Toronto-based Metal Supermarkets’ 10th annual Metal My Way 2024 contest.

For the past decade, Metal Supermarkets, the contest’s creator and presenting sponsor, has showcased and awarded outstanding metal projects from rising and veteran fabricators, metalworkers, sculptors, and welders.

More than 830 photo and video submissions from the U.S., Canada, and the U.K. were received for the 2024 contest, with entries ranging from small-engine models, car restorations, and commissioned art to large-scale renovations and construction projects.

Eyes on the Prize

Cindy Chinn, an artist from Chester, Neb., was named the 2024 grand prize winner for her sculpture, "The Peacock."

Chinn said while the 6.5-ft.-tall peacock sculpture took about a year to make, it actually was four years in the making. COVID-related delays impacted the project’s timeline, she said.

On her website, Chinn wrote how her sculpture required brass, copper, stainless steel, scrap metal, wood, enamel, and "a ton of patina!"

She worked with copper sheets over a framework of rebar and pipe covered with a copper mesh to create the animal’s body. The legs and head were made from mild steel rod and scrap metal, and the frames for the wings were made from copper rods and tubing.

Chinn made the wing feathers from copper, brass, and Padauk wood, and the tail feathers are stainless steel. The peacock’s train feathers were made from stainless steel rods and copper wire and also includes the animal’s iconic eye feathers. To make these "eyes," Chinn used the cloisonné technique— a copper sheet layered with enameling glass and copper wire.

Chinn said the sculpture’s color is natural rust and patina—no paint.

"It was just one of those projects you want to do, but you know you’ll never get to because it’s so big. I’ve wanted to do this for years," she said, adding that originally, she wanted to make two birds.

As part of the grand prize, Chinn received an Evolution chop saw that she said she was excited to have in her shop, which is housed in a converted school building.

"You look at past winners and go, ‘I don’t know if I can compete with that.’ When I got the notification, I went, ‘Wow, look at me!’ It was pretty exciting," she said.

Metal Supermarkets’ Metal My Way contest named Cindy Chinn’s "The Peacock" as its grand prize winner for 2024.

Artist Nancy Bole, Wichita, Kan., won the Best Artistic and Design Project category for her piece, "The Indian." Some of Bole’s work is inspired by her Cherokee and Creek roots.

Bole spent about six months finishing the bust, using a plasma table to cut the metal before welding and sculpting the crown, feathers, and head.

The bust required a lot of cutting, welding, and carving, she said. The face is solid steel, parts of the feathers are stainless steel, and the headdress is raw steel. Bole currently is working on a bronze version of the bust.

"That’s sort of my new, exciting adventure," Bole said about her metal sculptures. "I have a lot of different art mediums I do, but this one I am really pursuing because it’s fun and exciting."

Bole admitted that she almost missed the contest submission deadline. She found out about the contest on the last day entries could be submitted, so she sent what she could to meet the deadline. As part of the recognition, Bole won a Lincoln Electric welding machine.

"I was surprised and honored and excited all at the same time. I really didn’t think I was going to win," Bole said. "I was amazed that I did."

Four others were recognized as category winners. In the Best Vehicle Project category, James Frankland of the U.K. won for "Homemade Submarine." According to the press release, Frankland fulfilled a lifelong dream and engineering challenge by taking on this project, which required welding, cutting, machining, and general fabrication over six years. His submarine was rated at a diving depth of 100 m.

In the Best Furniture and Home Project category, Deborah Mitchell, Easley, S.C., was recognized for "Security Doors," which required more than 100 hours of work and featured brushed copper sheeting, steel, steel rods, and copper wire. To make the commission for an art collector client, she used a tube bender, angle grinder, Dremel tool, hammer, and welding tools.

Keith Broomfield from the U.K. won the Best Industrial or Shop Project category for "4 Cylinder Hot Air Engine." According to the announcement, Broomfield purchased metal from a Metal Supermarkets store in London and spent almost five years creating the four-cylinder engine, using mild steel, stainless steel, silver steel, brass, and phosphor bronze. Broomfield welded and soldered his own design using parts he created.

And in the Best Knife Project category, Isaiah Chevalier purchased metal from the Metal Supermarkets store in Anchorage, Alaska, to create his "Snake Head Dagger." Chevalier took about 40 hours to create 80 layers of 15N20 carbon blade steel and 80CrV2 high-carbon tool steel for his project.

Amazing Metalwork

The Metal My Way winners were selected based on complexity, detail, and intricacy; scale; uniqueness and creativity; craftmanship; quantity of metal used; and ingenuity. A committee consisting of Metal Supermarkets corporate staff selected the winners.

"With the ongoing need of skilled trade works across North America, it’s always encouraging to see all the people who have the skills to create amazing metalwork across Canada, the U.S., and the U.K. Congratulations to Cindy and all of this year’s winners," Metal Supermarkets President/CEO Stephen Schober said in the press release.
