# New ideas at AsiaBLECH

[TARİH: 01.08.2016 ISMR]

ASIABLECH 2016, Asia's international trade show for the sheet metal working industry, took place from 11-13 May 2016 at Suzhou International Expo Center, China. The three-day event attracted over 3,000 visitors from 30 application sectors who saw a variety of innovative technologies and solutions presented by nearly 100 sheet metal working industry leaders from 13 countries/regions.

"Today's production is undergoing major changes. With product variety increasing and batch sizes decreasing, manufacturing processes in sheet metal working are becoming ever more complex and require highly efficient and flexible solutions. Companies in the industry sector are, therefore, currently facing an environment of change that requires vital decisions for the long-term adaptation of their production processes. With this in mind, exhibitors at AsiaBLECH 2016 managed to engage visitors in a more interactive and creative way. The three exhibition days saw the signature of numerous purchase orders and contracts on-site," the organiser, Mack Brooks Exhibitions (Shanghai) Ltd, explained.

"Statistics show that 98% of trade visitors are willing to return to the next show, with 49% wishing to find the right suppliers and business partners. All visitors came from sectors closely associated with sheet metal working. Visitors were most interested in such traditional exhibition areas as sheet metal forming and working technologies, automation and robotics, flexible sheet metal working and surface treatment, which took up 65% of visitor attention."

Nearly 30 sessions of the "New Technology Seminar and Meet the Buyer Programme" enabled intensive, face-to-face interaction between exhibitors and visitors. The Technology Seminar invited Keynote Speaker, Mr. Werner Stelzer, an automotive and aluminium consultant, to share his insights. The "Big Five" Buyer Meeting (automotive; elevator; engineering machinery; white goods and agricultural machinery) proposed system solutions for these industries and presented a new market outlook.

With over 7000 developed profiles, and 100 international patents, Dallan operates worldwide.

ISMR recently flew to its 15,000 sq. m. headquarters in Castelfranco Veneto in Italy, 45km from Venice, to see its factory as well as the separate Dalcos punching and laser cutting facility. It also took the opportunity to visit Dallan's Pordenone-based customer, ELLECI, which took delivery of the new Dalcos ELXN combination servo-electric punch/laser line in January this year.

## Rollforming in focus

"We have focused on manufacturing precision rollforming machines for thin walled sheet metal profiles (up to 2mm) since 1978," commented Michela Dallan, of the sales department. "We have therefore acquired a great deal of experience in the production of precision profiles and in processing pre-painted and finished materials, with more than 3000 systems installed and 7300 profiles created in the last 38 years. Our high degree of specialisation means that we can offer customers not only the forming machines and lines (with all the equipment, tools and machinery to obtain the finished product) and the complete integrated production system with automatic packaging but also the original design of the product as well as expertise on sourcing the right raw material suppliers."

For example, to make a roller shutter, you need the rollformer that makes the profile, the machine that assembles the curtain and applies the end caps, the machine that makes the tube (axle) on which the curtain is wound and the machine for making the shutter box that houses the shutter. Dallan can offer this plus contacts for finding raw material suppliers.

"When we study a rolling shutter profile, we rely on precisely defined design parameters which ensure optimum closing and winding, silent operation, light control and extreme rigidity. Customers can choose between two possible automation levels - Coil to Package and Coil to Window technology," explained Daniele Fratin, Dallan's marketing manager.

"Our engineers continuously develop the software for the configuration and production of the rolling shutter profiles. The software can deliver pre-set configurations or be customised to each customer's requirements. The process tables and job lists can be received from existing ERP software via Ethernet. Our advanced software makes it easy for the operator to intervene in every single part of the machine."

Dallan rollforming lines combine several machines for carrying out different processes on sheet metal. The lines consist of various types of machines and machining, including coil punching with flexible DALCOS EXN and PXN systems; trimming of the coil; rollforming and bending; cutting and forming of the profile; ink-jet marking; labelling; application of plastic or metal accessories as well as handling and packaging systems for the finished products - specifically for rolling shutters, metal ceilings, plasterboard profiles and Venetian blinds. Dallan also engineers and produces the rollers and tools for its machines internally from concept to design, manufacturing and assembly along with software and a range of line accessories.

"Customers come to us with a product, profile, sample or idea that we develop together. For example, with the rolling shutter, we supply the slat plus the complete system – coil to window. The line output is ready to be sold as the final product. We can offer rollforming, punching, laser, tooling and highperformance production systems," added Daniele Fratin.

## An eye on coil

"We made a 28 million euro turnover last year – 20% of this was devoted to our Dalcos punching and laser machines and the rest to other equipment such as our rollforming machines, tooling, rollers and peripherals/accessories. Approximately 60% of our production is sold in Europe (15% of that figure is in Italy) and 40% is sold outside Europe to regions such as the USA, China and South America," explained Michela Dallan.

The Dalcos division of Dallan specialises in coil punching machines and fibre laser cutting systems.

"Our focus now is on the new Dalcos ELXN servo-electric combination punch/laser system, which spent four years in development before its launch. It is the only one in the world to offer independent punching and laser cutting modules from coil. The punch module is used for machining processes, which are quicker or indispensable (such as multiple holes and forming) while the laser cutter is preferred for large openings, notching or cutting irregular shapes," explained Michela Dallan.

"Servo-electric punching technology is very energy-efficient as well as being fast, reliable and accurate – while simultaneous laser cutting, with its high flexibility, optimises the efficiency and speed of the entire production system. The market trend now is to improve system integration. We have a lean production philosophy to engineer our systems so we try to pool efficiency to reduce waste, focus on JIT production and eliminate non-productive time."

Presented at the last EuroBLECH exhibition as the 'world's first combo coil-fed punch-laser', the ELXN consists of an electric punching machine (EXN 1000) in line with a laser cutting zone, for making complex notchings, special perforations and variable forms. The system features a decoiler, loading 6000kg. The 8-roller straightener eliminates the curve due to winding. At the head of the ELXN system is the patented Vega gripper feeder.

The first part of the process is carried out by the electric coil-fed punching machine that uses standard thick turret tools (A, B, C and D). This part of the machine can also be equipped with larger diameter punching tools for perforating and cluster operations, up to 6 inches in diameter, as well as with rotating DALCOS G-Tools. The punching zone uses a moving servo-electric hammer that generates 20 tons of force, inserted into a heavy duty closed-frame structure that allows the processing of thin as well as thick materials of up to 4mm. Once the material is punched and completed with embossing and deformations, it is delivered to the laser cutting zone for completion of the notching or special cuts.

The laser cutting head is powered with a 2kW fibre laser source that can be upgraded to 4kW. The working principle of this laser cutting head is simple: the laser cutting head is free to move in the X and Y direction, while the material is held still by the patented support bed that uses brushes to keep the material in axis, while the discharge zone is movable and always positioned under the laser cutting head. After completing the cut, the blades are activated and deliver the piece to the discharge zone, where the operator receives it automatically. The system can process material of between 200mm and 1000mm in width.

"Fitted with our Dalcos Select electric actuator, the punching zone can reach up to 200 strokes per minute when nibbling and maintains a force of 20 tons on all the tools for high performance, low noise levels and minimal electrical demand. The line can process strip from 200mm up to 1000mm wide - one great advantage of coil-fed production is that the material is optimised up to 100% by using materials of the same width as the end product, basically eliminating the skeleton as well as the need to separate the pieces," Michela Dallan explained.

The first installation took place in 2015 and Dallan, in early 2016, installed the most complete line to its customer, ELLECI. /SMR visited its facility near Pordenone in Italy.

"We installed the ELXN line in January 2016 at ELLECI, which has been operating a Dalcos hydraulic punching line for 12 years. It is making parts for electrical cabinets on the punch/laser. Productivity increases (which include optimised raw material usage) of up to 200% have been registered with the new line," explained Michela Dallan.

"We manufacture air conditioning units for electrical cabinets on the combined punch/laser machine – we are able to produce parts of different lengths e.g. 1000mm and up to 4000mm on the machine. The in-line punching operation is done first, followed by laser cutting with a 2kW fibre laser. An integrated vision system then checks the parts," ELLECI told /SMR.

The line can handle 4mm max. thickness (0.3mm/min.) using coil or sheet. It can also process a range of materials such as stainless steel, Aluminium, mild steel and prepainted material.

Along with the complete solution for punching and laser cutting, Dalcos is investing strongly in the development of the Dalcos LXN coil- and sheet-fed laser system, a production concept specifically for OEMs, designed to be versatile, efficient and easy to use. It can process coil and sheet of 6-, 8- and 14 metres in length, using 1- up to 4kW IPG fibre laser sources. LXN uses a patented vision system to establish the exact position of the strip edge and machined holes to ensure maximum cutting precision. The laser systems can pre-cut panels in sizes of up to 14m in length.

"LXN offers better performance than a traditional laser cutting system because the process is continuous, and loading and unloading takes place simultaneously," Daniele Fratin told /SMR. "Processing from coil is faster because it eliminates sheet loading and workpiece unloading times. It also ensures high material efficiency levels because it can process products with the same width as the coil. There is no need to trim the workpiece, which takes a few seconds in a sheet-fed punching machine. With the vision system, the material does not need a physical guide into the machine and this makes it possible to process coils directly from the steel mill."

Applications for the Dalcos LXN and ELXN systems include road signs; metal furniture and shelving; lighting fixtures; frames for HVAC; automotive; door frames and production from coil.
