Metal supplier carves out niche with waterjet technology

Situation

Metal Supermarkets® in Villa Park, Ill., is a two-man operation that sells steel and aluminum plates several inches thick, in addition to metal barstock, tubing, structural support, and sheets in various sizes and thicknesses. The 8,700-sq.-ft. store carries a variety of inventory, including more than 8,000 metal products, shapes, and grades. Customers vary from small job shops to larger manufacturers.

The company was cutting its thinner materials using band saws and shears, and outsourced its thicker materials for flame cutting, which added to customer delivery times. According to Garrett Morrell, owner/manager, customers disliked working with flame-cut steel and used the process only because of limited options.

“They don’t like flame cutting because the process hardens the edges of the steel, which complicates their abilities to machine it afterwards,” Morrell said. “Sometimes they would put it on their CNC machine, and those hard edges would break their tooling. At the very least, it adds considerably to their machine time.”

Having to spend extra time machining hardened flame-cut edges can be especially time-consuming on the larger jobs. For instance, if an order calls for 300 pieces of steel and each piece requires an extra hour of machining, it will result in 300 extra hours of machine time.

Resolution

Morrell began to consider adding a waterjet system rather than using flame cutting.

“Waterjet machines cut cleaner and more accurately, which gets them closer to a finished product. I saw an opportunity to start selling thicker steel and aluminum plate and save my customers a tremendous amount of machine time by adding a waterjet cutting machine,” he commented. “There were only about four job shops with waterjet cutting machines in the Chicago area at the time, so the demand was certainly there.”

At the 2012 International Manufacturing Technology Show (IMTS) in Chicago, Morrell saw various abrasive waterjet cutting machines in action and interviewed several manufacturer representatives about the products. He ordered an OMAX® MAXIEM® 1530 abrasive waterjet machine with a 40-HP, 50,000-PSI direct-drive pump from Tristate Machinery Inc. of Wheeling, Ill.

The 14- by 8-ft. machine cuts simple to complex parts in stock up to 5 by 10 ft. from most materials. It also cuts parts directly from the plate with a single tool to help reduce setup time and material usage.

The waterjet uses OMAX’s Intelli-MAX® software suite to help operators generate complex geometries and produce quality parts. With its patented motion control technology, the software ensures fast, accurate waterjet cutting across a variety of materials, according to the company.

Upon delivery of the machine, Morrell and his staff received operation and maintenance training. Within a short time they were up and running.

The unit saves customers several hours of finishing work. “We will give our customers the near-net shape,” Morrell said. “Sometimes they might still have to put a bevel on it, thread some holes, or do other finishing work, but other times they might just need to finish-grind it. In any case, they save a ton of time by not having to machine the edges. This helps them to operate more efficiently and meet their own deadlines more quickly.”

The store now fills about 30 orders a day, offering same- or next-day delivery.

“We do have people who just walk in, take a piece of scrap metal, pay for it, and walk out the door,” Morrell said. “Then we have those manufacturers that need 400 pieces of a certain material. In one recent order, for instance, we cut 300 pieces of 1-in.-thick steel plate over 40 hours without a single hiccup. It was a significant size job, and one that could not have been done cost-effectively without abrasive waterjet cutting capability.”

Welding system optimization increases control cabinet manufacturing productivity by 12 percent

Situation

Rittal GmbH & Co. KG, Rittershausen, Germany, is a system provider for control cabinets, power distribution, climate control, and IT infrastructure, as well as software and service.

The plasma powder welding process that the company was using was well-suited for welding control cabinet frames because of their high surface quality requirements. However, the process was maintenance-intensive. The company needed to increase cycle time, reduce downtime, and reduce maintenance costs.

Resolution

Rittal installed six welding systems from Carl Cloos Schweisstechnik GmbH, including two fully automated production systems. Each of these comprises 15 or 13 chained robots, various manipulators, and comprehensive control technology coordinating the production runs.

The welding systems produce control cabinet frames that can differ significantly in length, width, and height. Depending on the production plan, the tools are changed automatically to the required size, which is selected from more than 1,800 control cabinet types. And the production lines run day and night, and sometimes weekends. As a result, hundreds of welded control cabinet frames leave each of the two systems per shift.

Recently a project team comprising Cloos and Rittal employees examined the complex production run and process technology. On the complex systems, they shot video and took time measurements of different processes during the production run. During the investigations, they paid particular attention to the following criteria: cycle time, weld seam quality, process safety, and rework.

Permanent analyses of the production process and the optimization measures derived from them led to a constant stability and improvement of productivity. Cycle times could be improved significantly and downtimes reduced permanently. Overall, Rittal has been able to improve production line productivity by 12 percent.

The company now uses a MAG pulse welding process. It produces a flat seam weld so that the doors of the control cabinet close properly. This has reduced maintenance expenditure compared to the plasma powder welding process used previously.

Mining equipment-maker increases productivity with automated plate processing line

Situation

The Chinese mining industry is booming and, therefore, so is the mining equipment sector. The Zhengzhou Coal Mining Machinery Group provides stationary equipment for roof and long-wall support, as well as for mobile machinery that operates in mines.

At the plant in Zhengzhou, the capital of China’s Henan province, the cutting operation for each machine took 4 to 8 hrs. The company needed to increase productivity to keep up with rising demand.

Resolution

The company commissioned a production line with plasma cutting machines, including a material flow system. The system is from MicroStep Group, which supplied a fully automated sheet and plate processing system in cooperation with Terex Material Handling. The heart of the installation is a logistics system with a Demag process crane and Demag warehouse management system (WMS). They are used to supply and remove the metal plates, which measure up to 41.34 ft. long and 1.57 in. thick, and to serve the flame cutting tables around the clock.

Every day the new installation can process up to 155 metal plates in three shifts, completing the cutting operation for each machine in 1 to 2 hrs. An intralogistics system helps ensure machinery is utilized at a consistently high level. As a result, production in the entire plant has been increased by 40 percent, for a monthly machine output of 13,228 tons.

The plasma cutting equipment and process crane had to be synchronized with each other. For this purpose, the Demag WMS and the crane control system were integrated into the customer’s IT system and linked to its host computer. While the individual orders, including the calculated processing times, are managed by the host computer, the WMS coordinates storage of the raw material, deliveries to the machinery, and further transport of the processed material with the aim of achieving uniform utilization of the individual cutting tables and minimizing downtime.

Metal plates are fed into the automatic processing zone by a rail-mounted transverse transporter. The process crane, which is equipped with a magnet spreader, picks up the metal plates at the transfer point and takes them to a vacant machine position or to one of two buffer zones that are assigned to each flame cutting table.

With an additionally integrated load sway damping system and the spreader at the highest lifting position, the crane can reach long-travel speeds of up to 393.70 FPM.

When the cutting operation has been completed, the crane picks up the entire cut material and transports it to a conveyor belt. The metal plates are then conveyed out of the automatic zone and transported to the next step in the process, where they are sorted by hand. Workers are assisted at this stage by handling equipment with magnets, which can be positioned by the Demag KBK modular light crane system.

A dedicated access concept prevents any personnel from entering the individual processing areas when they are automatically approached by the crane. When routine maintenance work is performed on one of the flame cutting systems, the automatic crane bypasses the danger zone to protect the personnel.
