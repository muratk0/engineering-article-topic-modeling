# PRECISION MANUFACTURING

[TARİH: 01.04.2016 ISMR]

Martin Stucki estimates that his company could realistically manage series production of between 80 and 100 machines.

“This is a great benefit and creates independence and flexibility, especially for time-critical small batches which often occur in the aircraft industry,” explained Christian Laube, Head of Engineering, Marenco. The company cuts a wide variety of materials ranging from soft materials to those as hard as steel, from foam through to titanium. With a pressure of up to 6,000 bar and using fine sand particles from the Australian desert as abrasive, the parts that are processed include copper conductor rails and sheet metal parts, such as switch panels or door lock components, as well as foam inlays for cases to transport the sensitive material.

CNC operator, Stephan Sauzet, who was able to gain experience with a Bystronic prototype machine, praises the performance and precision of the new acquisition. He processes work-pieces that are 0.05mm thick, which he reinforces with PVC, through to the hardest and toughest precious metals which the ByJet Flex cuts ‘like butter’. The accuracy of the cuts is 0.1mm. According to Stephan Sauzet, the real art is to take precise account of the kerf, to achieve the exact specified dimensions.

“Thanks to the new Bystronic machines, we were able to expand our competencies. And they make the company more attractive,” Christian Laube explained. It is not least due to this investment that Marenco has been able to acquire new customers in the region.

## From concept to production

Marenco’s high-tech enterprises have their roots in the Stucki cidery in Pfäffikon in the Swiss canton of Zurich which, for many years, was widely renowned for its wholesome apple juices. In 1997, Martin Stucki founded Marenco Engineering. Five years later, he first thought of producing a helicopter and started creating initial CAD drawings and a business plan. The initial idea was to build a small helicopter but, shortly afterwards, better market opportunities were perceived for a six- to eight-seater in the 2.5-ton class.

Since 2007, in addition to the engineering company, the former cidery has also been home to Marenco Swisshelicopter AG, which has an additional production location in Mollis in the Swiss canton of Glarus. Some of the helicopter components are manufactured inhouse at Marenco. Recently, two new Bystronic machines, a waterjet cutting system and a press brake, were installed in Pfäffikon and have since been in operation.

The development of the Swisshelicopter focused fully on the question: What does the pilot need? Being a professional pilot, Martin Stucki wanted to produce a machine ‘by pilots for pilots’. Hence the cockpit is simple and user-friendly, and the main screens displaying all the important flight and turbine data, warning signals and the moving-terrain display are positioned optimally. The navigational and communications instruments are positioned as close as possible to the collective lever and the cyclic stick, which are the instruments and controls that pilots use most frequently during flight.

The overhead panel is also positioned in such a manner that the pilot does not risk dislocating his neck while switching the master switch and the other system buttons. Special attention was given to providing all-round vision. The patented viewing duct between the pilot seats offers a direct downwards view which is important, especially when flying with payloads attached below the helicopter.

However, the Swisshelicopter also offers a cohesive interior, which allows fittings to be customised but also enables bulky loads to be transported inside the helicopter.

“As the first prototype quickly proved that the basic concept was solid, and no significant changes were necessary, the second prototype currently being constructed is already very similar to the series model. We already have commitments for 72 machines, which we hope to be able to start delivering to the first customers this year,” said Marenco.

What is required to develop a product from idea right through to series production? For Martin Stucki, engineering competence and the ability to perform a fundamental product definition are paramount.

In Switzerland, the manufacture of aircraft and aircraft components is a long and proud tradition. For maintenance and servicing of rotary-wing aircraft, the country with the highest density of helicopters can rely on extensive experience and well-trained experts. In the highly sensitive aerospace industry, expertise relating to certification and validation is equally important. In this sector, there can sometimes be excessive bureaucracy and perhaps a tendency towards over-regulation, according to Stucki.

“All the regulations specified by EASA, the European Aviation Safety Agency, are implemented with Swiss thoroughness. A problem that we have to solve before it kills us….” concluded Martin Stucki. ■
