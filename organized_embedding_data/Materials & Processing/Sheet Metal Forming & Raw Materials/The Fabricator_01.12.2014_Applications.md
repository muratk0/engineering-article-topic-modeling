Upender with V groove keeps steel coils in place

Situation

A world market and technology leader in steel wire transformation and coatings required a system to prevent steel coils from rolling off an upender when in operation. The company, originally founded in Europe, has customers in more than 120 countries involved in the automotive, agriculture, and consumer goods industries.

Resolution

Pentalift designed a V groove to fit the upender, allowing it to accommodate the safe tilting of steel coils. When the coils are placed vertically on the equipment by a fork truck, the upender tilts 90 degrees, allowing the coil to slip into its groove and preventing it from rolling off once in the horizontal position.

The upender was built to client specifications with a low height of 18 in., tilt of 90 degrees, and platform size of 54 in. square and 36 in. high. The upender has a self-contained, 15-HP power unit.

Upward expansion helps manufacturer increase production space in existing facility

Situation

Royal Basket Trucks, Darien, Wis., is a manufacturer of basket trucks and specialty laundry carts. Every product is made to order, a philosophy that allows the company to customize wire frame carts by making size modifications specific to a customer’s environment. Having full welding and industrial sewing departments located in its facility enables the company to produce orders on a short timeline.

The company was looking to increase its production rates to meet growing customer demand. To accomplish that goal, the firm first considered expanding its facility with a new wing. However, that option proved too costly—$500,00 for a 12,000-sq.-ft. building addition—and it would take a minimum of six months to complete.

The company decided that it needed to improve operational efficiencies within its existing facility space. Its flow operation—trying to get materials from one part of the building to another where production was located—wasn’t working well.

Resolution

The company added a work platform and Delta™ Lift vertical reciprocating conveyor (VRC) from Wildeck Inc. to a large, unused space in its existing facility. Wildeck recommended locating materials and production closer together to improve product flow.

The company converted 6,000 sq. ft. of unused space into a productive work area. The 40- by 120-ft. work platform is used for production, and materials and other supplies are relocated underneath it. The VRC moves materials back and forth between the ground and upper levels.

The company coverted 6,000 sq. ft. of unused space into a productive work area. The 40- by 120-ft. work platform is used for production, and materials and other supplies are relocated underneath it.

“We took their product transfer flow from a distance of about 75 ft. and reduced it to virtually nothing,” said Brad Lehman, outside regional sales manager with Wildeck. “The VRC … now takes raw materials up to the workers and brings finished products back down.”

The work platform not only expanded the company’s capabilities without expanding its facility, but it also improved employee safety.

“The work platform created a much safer work environment for employees because now they’re up off the floor,” Lehman said. “They’ve got forklifts and carts moving about, so the danger of someone being struck is greatly minimized.”
