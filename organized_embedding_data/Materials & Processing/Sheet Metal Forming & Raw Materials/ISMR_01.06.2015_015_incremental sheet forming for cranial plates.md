# Incremental sheet forming for cranial plates

[TARİH: 01.06.2015 ISMR]

A PRE-CLINICAL collaboration between AFRC (University of Strathclyde’s Advanced Forming Research Centre), Pascoe Engineering Ltd and the Greater Glasgow Health Board is aimed at a potential new market for the company – the innovative field of cranial implant manufacture. The UK market currently requires 2,000 implants per year and the project has helped the company to expand into this market.

“Current component manufacturing cycle times are 6-8 weeks. Incremental sheet forming reduces the cycle from scan data to surgery to 5-10 days. This leads to shorter patient waiting times and less implant adjustment during surgery, thus improving surgical outcomes,” commented Pascoe Engineering.

The AFRC has worked with Pascoe Engineering Ltd to establish Incremental Sheet Forming (ISF) techniques and developed a number of key processes that support the successful control and operation of incremental sheet forming processes. It has demonstrated the process on aerospace components.

Incremental sheet forming is a transferable process that is able to provide low cost manufacture close to the point of use and uses conventional CNC-controlled manufacturing equipment that is widely available. A sheet is formed into the final workpiece by a series of small incremental deformations. It is used for prototyping new designs, forming vintage car parts and part manufacture for defence applications in remote locations.
