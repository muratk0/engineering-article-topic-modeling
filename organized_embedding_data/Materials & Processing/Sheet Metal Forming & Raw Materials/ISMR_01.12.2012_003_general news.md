## ALUMINIUM HOT FORMING IN FOCUS

JUST OVER a year ago, SAPA’s research and development centre in Finspång, Sweden announced that a method was being developed for aluminium hot forming using equipment from AP&T. The information was cloaked in secrecy and no details were revealed. Now, however, the door to hot gas forming has now been slightly opened….

”The method we are currently developing opens up entirely new opportunities in terms of manufacturing aluminum parts for different purposes and for several different industries,” said Conny Falk, Technical Area Manager, SAPA Technology.

At room temperature, extruded aluminium is slightly harder to form compared to steel in sheet metal forming, but the opposite applies in hot conditions. An aluminium section that is heated up to the right temperature and pressurized from the inside using gas can basically be formed into anything. That means not only a high degree of flexibility, but also a quicker and more effective process with fewer processing steps. With the new method of hot gas forming (HGF), many pressed or cast steel parts and plastic parts can be replaced by gasformed aluminium parts in the future.

”Some examples of what HGF can be used for include parts for furniture, bicycle frames, furnishings and not least cars, where weight is very important,” explained Conny Falk.

“The production costs for HGF are significantly lower than for hydroforming, for example, and compared to casting the method enables thinner material with a porefree structure. Aluminium is also much easier to weld than castings,” added AP&T.

”We have always said that sheet metal forming is exciting, and that sheet metal is superior to other materials. Having the opportunity to be a part of SAPA pushing the boundaries for what we thought was possible opens up entirely new usage areas,” said Jan Jonasson, senior area sales manager at AP&T.

One of SAPA’s customers is currently test manufacturing parts. Thus far the trial has proceeded according to plan and series production can hopefully commence on a limited scale this winter.
