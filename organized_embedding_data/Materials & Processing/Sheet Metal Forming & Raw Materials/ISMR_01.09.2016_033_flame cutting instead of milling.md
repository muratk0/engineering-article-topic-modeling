# Flame cutting instead of milling

[TARİH: 01.09.2016 ISMR]

JEBENS GMBH, a specialist in large, heavy flame-cut parts, mechanical processing and complex welded components, is increasingly opting for flame-cutting instead of milling when high ablation rates are needed. With one single cut, it told /SMR, this can remove massive amounts of material, saving up to 50% on the time required for the job. And that means significantly lower costs.

It has the necessary machines and equipment for processing plates and blocks with thicknesses of up to 1,100mm and unit weights of up to 55 tons. Jebens sees a perfected flame-cutting technique as a viable competitive alternative to the complex mechanical processing of largeformat parts.

“In many cases, it enables milling times to be considerably reduced – if not dispensed with completely – and thus offers significant rationalisation potential,” it explained. One example is a casing for minting presses which Jebens produced for the Schuler Group from S355J2-grade structural steel with APZ 3.1. The job was to produce workpieces, with dimensions of 400 x 710 x 840mm, from a 30-ton steel plate. This required ablation of large segments with tight radii on several levels. These notches had to be made in a wide range of different cutting heights, widths and depths. Without resorting to milling, the thermal cutting specialists produced the complex geometries exactly to the customer's specifications using flame cutting. A Sato thermal cutting machine was used to reduce the thickness, and then a chamfering robot took over the fine finishing of the complex geometries on the different levels. Before starting, the exact course of the cut was simulated with 3D modelling to ensure that the cut really would be perfect first time around...

“Final mechanical finishing cannot always be dispensed with, but the main portion of the material to be ablated can be removed at considerably lower costs. The prospect of faster production time, reliable processes and lower part costs in the processing of large components are very attractive," concluded the manufacturer.
