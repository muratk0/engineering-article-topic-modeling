# PACKING A PUNCH

[TARİH: 01.02.2022 ISMR]

Boschert's general manager, Manuel Lang, outlined its latest plans, initiatives and technology developments to ISMR.

ISMR SAYS:

“Sheet metal innovator, Boschert, is celebrating its 75th anniversary this year.”

Established in 1946 by Ludwig Boschert as a small workshop for the textile industry, it builds notching, punching, bending, plasma, laser and copper processing machines. It established its international reputation in the sheetmetal working industry through its notching and punching machine portfolio. Today, around 20,000 of its notching machines are in use around the world.

However, it is the continuous development and integration of new ideas and pragmatic values that made Boschert the worldwide patent-holder of the folding joint. The manufacturer, based in Germany's Black Forest, has also set new standards in safety chuck techniques. Quality, accuracy and strong production values are part of its sheet metal heritage.

Fifteen years ago, Boschert established a joint venture company in a new factory in Greece with Gizelis SA. Together, both companies offered a new production range of press brakes and shears to the market. After acquiring a further holding in 2016 in French company AMB Picot, a specialist in roll bending machines based on the three-roll principle, Boschert has further extended its range of products and services.

Today, Boschert offers a complete portfolio of sheet metal working machines including manual (as well as CNC-controlled) punching machines; combined punching/plasma/fibre laser machines; copper working machines and centres; and small and midsized press brakes and notching machines. It also builds special machines to meet specific customer requirements as well as supplying retrofitting, support and service activities. It is currently celebrating its 75th anniversary in the industry.

Manuel Lang, General Manager, Boschert GmbH & Co KG.

## A strong foundation

"In the last 15 years, Boschert has developed from a manufacturer of notching and punching machines to a full-range supplier. Together with our partner Gizelis, we have been answering enquiries for press brakes and shears for a long time. In the few years, the product range has also been expanded to include flatbed fibre lasers and Punch CombiLasers. We also developed a Multipunch 4020 punching machine last year which can process sheets sizes of up to 4 x 2m," Manuel Lang, Boschert's general manager, told ISMR.

The company has agents in countries all over the world. Germany is its largest market, followed by Switzerland and the United States. Boschert's owner, Peter Kunz, puts its success down to the expertise of its staff who aim to make nearly 100% of its products in-house.

Many of them have spent most of their working lives with the company.

"We are a traditional, family-owned company that is not necessarily looking for continuous growth, but for stability. We build good machines and our customers are happy; this is our main aim. It may not be a standard approach but it works for us. We have low staff turnover levels and a more long-term view of the business, which is better for our company and our employees. I have been at Boschert for 41 years myself," explained Manuel Lang.

Boschert GmbH & Co. KG, based in Lörrach (near Basel), employs 115 members of staff and has a production and assembly area of some 8,000 square metres. There are a further 700 square metres of office space, as well as a presentation and demonstration centre. It offers in-house training to all staff. Every year, two to three apprentices complete their industrial training with the company.

Boschert supports its employees during their further education and training, for example by guaranteeing to keep their jobs open for them. More than half of its workforce obtained their qualifications with the company and therefore possess specific product expertise in each manufacturing technology.

## Outlook for 2022

"2018 was our best year ever and this year has started well, with a very strong order situation. We may even reach 2018 levels this year. COVID-19 hit our sales last year (as well as the material, delivery and labour shortages that everyone suffered). However, we made a 26-million-euro turnover in 2021. We have certainly become a more global company since we partnered with Gizelis, who also bring 50 staff to the organisation," Manuel Lang told ISMR.

"At the moment, the order situation is very good (despite COVID-19) and we are not expecting a slowdown at the moment. However, there are always risks (above all, unforeseeable global political developments) which can be difficult to assess and classify. In the face of COVID-19, we had to adjust to new circumstances in 2020 and bridged the decline in orders with the short-time work financial packages offered by the German government for five months. As with most companies, we worked from home where applicable and took the appropriate production measures to reduce the risk of infection," he added.

Boschert has made a name for itself as a provider of customised solutions, thanks to the high levels of technical expertise of its employees. Almost 50% of the orders that it receives are adapted to customer requirements. This, said Manuel Lang, offers added value to customers and he intends to continue offering customised solutions.

"We listen to our customers' requirements and provide the solutions that they need. We can be very flexible in our approach and make any changes that they request. This also feeds into our innovation and R&D initiatives. We have set ourselves a target to provide more automation solutions for our customers. We are currently looking for partners for elements of our automation planning (for example, material storage). Standard machine loading, however, is an element that we wish to provide ourselves," he explained.

Boschert is also working with partners on the development of Industry 4.0 software for wider machine networking connectivity.

"Our machines already provide customers with Industry 4.0 capability, for example, to check machine/production status. However, we cannot yet offer wider connectivity to other branded machines on the wider factory floor so our target is to develop this capability, supported by external partners. This is an ongoing activity," added Manuel Lang.

## Automated bending cell

At the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart (October 2021), Boschert and Gizelis launched two of their latest innovations; a fully automated robotic bending cell, incorporating a flexible and productive ATC (Automatic Tool Change) system, and a versatile stacking system for shears. Also on display was the Compact EVO, a new punching solution, and the new Boschert Fiber Laser generation (4kW).

The Gizelis ROBOBEND 2 fully automated robotic bending cell incorporates Automatic Tool Change (ATC) with bending and palletising procedures as a turnkey solution.

"The system offers ultimate flexibility thanks to its smart design, as it can be operated in three different modes. Individual safety systems are activated in each working mode in full compliance with EU directives. There is large capacity for tooling (punches and dies) which can be safely organised in a robust, smart tooling warehouse, able to store more than 20m of punches and dies (including tooling in very small segments for bending small/complex details)," explained Gizelis.

The three working modes for the robotic bending cell are as follows:

■Operation as a fully automated robotic bending cell (e.g. for mass production of repeated parts).

■Automatic Tool Change operation only, where the robot automatically builds the tool stations then parks itself at the side. An operator then carries out the bending.

■Operation as a standard bending machine, by an operator, where the robot stays at the parked position and the operator bends the parts (e.g. for complex parts, in small batches).

"The ROBOBEND 2 system features powerful offline software for programming and simulating the entire robotic cell. It accepts 2D/3D drawings of the part to be bent and then (while the system is working) a trained engineer can program the complete robotic cell offline to get it ready for the next job," added Gizelis.

The system is, said the manufacturer, compatible with Industry 4.0 protocols. Direct remote maintenance services are also offered.

## Fibre laser cutting

By launching a fibre laser machine, BOSCHERT GmbH & Co. KG is setting a further milestone in the expansion of its delivery programme. With experience gathered from more than 4,000 punch, punch-plasma and punch-laser-machines installed worldwide, the sheet-metal processing specialist has developed a compact solution design which offers a range of practical advantages. The blank sheet is moved in the X-direction with travelling clamping jaws on the roller table, while the "flying" laser head travels in the Y-axis.

"The trend in sheet metal working is strongly towards laser cutting. That is why we have developed our FiberLaser solution. The system is designed for customers who want to cut a part immediately and not wait for an entire sheet to be nested and cut. Some customers need that level of flexibility for their own production. We cannot offer higher than 12mm part cutting capability so it makes no sense to continuously increase the laser power capability on this machine, so we offer up to 4kW power," Manuel Lang told SMR.

Boschert currently supplies the new FiberLaser in two sizes: 3015 with a working range of 1,500 × 3,000mm and 4020 with a working range of 2,000 x 4,000mm. The machines can be optionally fitted with a one-, two-, three- or four-kilowatt fibre laser. This flexibility enables users to machine sheets made from structural steel (up to six and ten millimetres), from stainless steel (up to four, six or eight millimetres) and from aluminium in various thicknesses (up to three, four or six millimetres).

The machine table is easily accessible for loading and unloading and has a flap for the immediate removal of small cut parts. These workpieces can be up to 350mm wide and 1,500mm long. For this purpose, the small parts flap lowers by 20mm followed by a tilting movement through 40 degrees to transfer the parts to a conveyor located underneath. This eliminates the problematic "uprighting" of small, cut workpieces, increases process reliability and enables these workpieces to be accessed quickly. Slag and residual waste are transported via a second conveyor belt to a waste container located on the operator side.

“Clamping of the blank and leftover metal sheet is solved equally cleverly. The sheet metal is fixed and clamped by up to four clamping jaws guided on recirculating roller bearings. The whole unit travels and positions via a rack-and-pinion drive in the X-axis. Any number of clamping jaws can be used depending on the nature of the blank and leftover metal sheet. The jaws are automatically positioned on the blank by the controller. The laser head can be rapidly controlled in the Y-axis and also via the rack-and-pinion drive. The height is also controlled by a capacitive distance regulator to ensure that the fibre laser head is at the optimum height and correctly focused on the particular material to be processed," explained Boschert.

The precise cutting system and the practical clamping concept also enable leftover metal sheets as small as 500 x 50mm to be processed, resulting in a very high degree of material utilisation. Under the machine hood is a three-stage safety system. This allows laser processing to be carried out on the materials and thicknesses mentioned without setting up special safety areas. The maintenance opening for the laser head is at the front of the machine, reducing downtime when changing the nozzle and increasing productive run-time.

## The punching factor

Boschert's Compact EVO punching machine offers stepless 360-degree rotation of all tools up to 105mm diameter, using brushless DC motors and pneumatic indexing of Revotools. The seven-way Revotool was especially built for this model.

“In addition to six tools with a diameter of 16mm each, with a slitting tool of 5 x 30mm it is possible to punch nearly every contour, allowing complete processing of the sheet without changing tools," explained Boschert.

An L-sheet is provided with all machines for working very small pieces and pre-bent parts. Automatic tool lubrication increases tool life when nibbling stainless steel (VA) and decreases edge burrs in aluminium. Repositioning can be done up to 10 metres, as well as clamp movement, without contacting the sheet. The machine features two robust clamps for sheets up to 200kg (expandable to four clamps). Standard capacity is up to 7mm or 12.7mm when required. Programmable repositioning of the clamps is also available for faster set-up of the machine.

Other features include vacuum slug removal; nibbling up to 800 strokes/min. with integrated oil cooling; tapping and drilling attachment for threads M3 up to M12 and smaller drilled holes in thicker sheets as well as marking apparatus for marking parts in ten different fonts.

Boschert's new Multipunch machine offers precision punching, nibbling and forming in one heavy-duty-machine. Sheet panels can measure 1250 x 2500mm (4mm stainless steel, 6mm mild steel or 8mm aluminium).

"Many customers would like to process all their metal sheets in one working cycle so the Multipunch features a new tooling system with eight stations for TRUMPF tooling. With stepless indexable tools, it offers a total of 64 tools which is more than enough for most sheet-metalworking operations. It is possible to pre-adjust tooling for different sheets or for partial sheets," explained Boschert.

"The combination of simple and rapid sheet-metal part handling, as well as split-second tool change, reduces non-productive downtime to a minimum and enables longer machine useful life. Programming and handling of the machine is very easy. The Multipunch is equipped with an automatic small parts chute for sheets of up to 300 x 200mm. Tool set-up is easy via changeable cassettes. Automatic tool lubrication and vacuum slug removal means that nothing gets in the way of continuous sheet-metal part production," it added.

"Nevertheless," Manuel Lang told ISMR, “forming and embossing are still required in sheet metal processing, which is why we have developed our laser punching Combi machine.

The trend in manufacturing is to make components that are lighter in weight, use less material, conserve resources and integrate functionality. Customers are therefore working with thinner sheet structures. Boschert has expanded its successful “Combi" series of machines to include fibre laser. The CombiLaser laser punching series is available with working ranges from 1000 × 2000mm to 1500 x 3000mm. Customers can now produce complex parts on a single machine without repositioning.

## Multipunch 4020 deliveries

"Last year, we delivered Multipunch 4020 machines to customers, who process large sheet sizes, in The Netherlands, Switzerland and Germany. These customers are generally involved in the construction market for cladding/swimming pool fabrication," Manuel Lang told ISMR.

One such customer is Swiss subcontractor HMH Hungerbühler GmbH who was looking for a die-cutting machine to process sheet metal in XXL or super format quickly, easily and economically. Hungerbühler mainly processes stainless steel but also aluminium, some brass and copper. The company turns around 2,000 tons of material per year. It specialises in large parts of up to six metres.

After consulting with Boschert, it placed an order for a customised punching machine that could process super-format sheet metal (which became the Multipunch 4020). The machine can punch, nibble, shape or mark sheet metal up to six millimetres thick. The 12-fold tool changer significantly reduces non-productive times.

The MultiPunch 4020 is equipped with two punching heads. The upper punching head with a punching stroke of 55mm is equipped with HDE hydraulics that ensure a fast stroke and accurate punching. The punching head can be continuously rotated 360 degrees and has a Boschert T-Nut as a tool-holder for all standard TRUMPF tools up to size three. The machine also has an active die with an automatic straightening function for punching from below. The lower punching head, with a punching stroke of 25mm, is equipped similarly to the upper one. The maximum stroke rate of the MP 4020 is 800 strokes per minute.

The system can process sheets from 100 x 280mm up to the super format 4,000 x 2,000mm. The company uses them for many different jobs, including providing super-format stainless steel sheets for pool floors with welding bosses, which ensure that the pool is non-slip. The Multipunch 4020 is also used to produce newly developed lightweight safety components for mobile homes.

## In conclusion

The mechanical machining of copper and other materials using cutting, punching and forming tools demands special expertise. The machining of copper rails, for example for equipping electrical systems and switching cabinets, represents a considerable challenge for manufacturers and suppliers. Boschert long ago developed its CU-PROFI series of machines for the flexible handling of copper and aluminium rails.

"We are currently developing a new machine for copper bar processing. The machine prototype will not be launched until next year," confirmed Manuel Lang. “In the meantime, we will continue to focus on meeting our customers' machine requirements and Industry 4.0 expectations. We will also find time to hold a small celebration with our employees to celebrate our 75th anniversary this summer," he concluded.

INDUSTRY REPORT

DRIVING CHANGE

We analyse trends and forecasts for the global automotive industry including electrification, digitalisation and sustainability goals.

2020 represented a ‘tipping point' in the adoption of EVs

Over 6% of total EU employment is linked to the automotive sector, and the sector's turnover represents over 7% of EU Gross Domestic Product (GDP). At the same time, the automotive sector is a major private investor in research and development (R&D).

However, according to 'The Future of the EU Automotive Sector'- a recent report requested by the European Parliament's committee on Industry, Research and Energy (ITRE)– the industry finds itself at a crossroads with various trends fomenting change. These relate to the green transition (electromobility, hydrogen fuel cells etc.) and the digital transition (connectivity, autonomous driving, software etc.).

This twin transition is already an enormous challenge for the global automotive industry, which has also been battling the supply chain challenges presented by the COVID-19 pandemic as well as semiconductor chip and raw material shortages. The pandemic itself also acted as an accelerator, positively impacting consumer demand for electric vehicles (EVs) along with electrification, digitalisation and global value chain resilience measures. 2020 represented a 'tipping point' in the adoption of EVs.

## Global market analysis

Analyst IHS Markit forecasts new light vehicle sales of nearly 82.4 million globally in 2022, up 3.7%, for the global automotive sector. It projects that the global automotive industry will finish out 2021 with nearly 79.4 million light vehicles sold, and "industry demand levels will continue to be restrained in 2022 as the semiconductor supply chain remains challenged."

Tentative demand recovery will continue across most regions, it added, assuming the ongoing availability of effective vaccines and apart from any major impacts from the Omicron variant.

Electrification remains a growing dynamic—2021 has seen an “arms race” of ambition as OEMs declare electrification targets for the coming 5-15 years.

Full year 2021 sales are expected to be up just 2.9% from the levels achieved in 2020. IHS Markit remains cautious on recovery prospects, as the global auto industry grapples with this “perfect storm" of unprecedented circumstances. Depressed vehicle output levels are expected to impact vehicle lead times for some time, pressuring depleted inventories and delaying fulfilment of prevailing order levels.

“The path of the pandemic remains an important driver of the 2022 auto demand cycle, especially the 'race' between vaccine and variants. Concerns remain as winter arrives for Northern Hemisphere nations, and the emergence of the Omicron variant represents a worrying development," said Colin Couchman, Executive Director, Global Light Vehicle Forecasting, IHS Markit.

Most regions face limited recovery prospects on supply chain challenges and potential further COVID-19 flare-ups.

The European auto industry looks set for a bleak mid-winter as widening virus concerns combine with ongoing supply chain woes, with concerns for German-based production. The 2021 Western and Central European demand forecast foresees 13.9 million units, just scraping into growth territory, up 0.2% y/y. 2022 demand is set at 15.0m units (+7.8%), according to IHS Markit.

“European car consumers are expected to hunker down for a second winter of COVID-19, but the new year might struggle to deliver meaningful improvement to new car sales levels," said Couchman.

Looking at 2022, U.S. sales volumes are expected to reach nearly 15.5 million units, up an estimated 2.6% from the projected 2021 level of approximately 15.1 million units.

"For 2022, the pace of sales is expected to quicken in the second half of the year. Given current inventory conditions, it's difficult to project significant demand recovery in the first half of 2022. But we expect to exit 2022 with a pace of sales more recognisable to pre-COVID levels, setting the stage for better volume outlooks into 2023 and 2024," according to Chris Hopson, Manager, North American Light Vehicle Sales Forecast, IHS Markit.

In Mainland China for 2021, IHS Markit analysts foresee the market down by 1% y/y, to 23.4 million units, as supply chain shortages choke off market growth. Near-term risks are balanced, and 2022 is currently set at 24.2 million (+3.3% y/y), with more meaningful recovery expected for 2023—back above pre-crisis levels to 26.9 million, up by 11.3% y/y.

Global light vehicle production in 2021 is expected to finish at 75.5 million units, a paltry 1.2% improvement over 2020 levels.

For 2022, IHS Markit forecasts a rebound in light vehicle production of 9.0 per cent, to 82.3 million units. The outlook will continue to be characterised by the availability of automotive-grade chips, at least until 2023. The balance of incremental capacity gains within the semiconductor sector, heightened 'chips-per-vehicle' requirements and robust non-automotive chip demand all feature in this assessment.

"Overall, while manufacturing operations in most regions are expected to improve, capacity constraints within the semiconductor supply chain remain the single most influential feature of the forecast. As the semiconductor tide recedes, will this expose further risks to the auto recovery? Threats elsewhere within the supply chain could become more apparent as chip supplies improve, notably, logistics, worker-related issues and key raw materials shortages," said Mark Fulthorpe, executive director of light vehicle production forecasts at IHS Markit.

In Greater China, IHS Markit forecasts modest growth for 2022 of 1.6 per cent, to 24.3 million units. Europe is expected to produce 18.5 million units in 2022, up from an estimated 15.7 million in 2021. For the North American region, momentum is improving heading into 2022, although the IHS Markit outlook based on current forecasts remains at nearly 15.2 million units. This reflects growth of just over 2.2 million units year-over-year. A more normalised supply chain is expected to support a return to the pre-pandemic output levels of 2019.

Electrification remains a growing dynamic—2021 has seen an "arms race" of ambition as OEMs declare electrification targets for the coming 5-15 years.

Electric vehicles are fast evolving from a compliance side hustle into fully fledged core offerings for many OEMs. At COP26 in 2021, policymakers and regulators also shared their visions for a greener future, including the U.S., the EU and the UK. Transformational change is firmly on the agenda and making sense of this arms race of ambition represents an ongoing challenge.
