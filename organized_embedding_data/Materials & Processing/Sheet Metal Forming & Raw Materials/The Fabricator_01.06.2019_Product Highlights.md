Bimetal band saw blades cut variety of materials

The L.S. Starrett Co. offers INTENSS™ bimetal band saw blades featuring an M42 highspeed steel cutting edge welded to a fatigue-resistant alloy steel backing to help improve blade life and cutting action. The blades resist heat, abrasion, and shock for improved cutting rates over a variety of materials including carbon steel, carbon steel alloys, cast iron, copper alloys, and aluminum.

Suitable for horizontal machines and light-duty vertical machines, the blades perform contour and general-purpose cutting in toolrooms and maintenance shops with varied cutting operations.

The band saw blades include proprietary bi-metal unique® technology, which joins two strips of high-speed steel wires to the backing steel using solid-state diffusion bonding. The bonding area between the backer and the high-speed steel is increased 170 percent compared to conventional electron beam or laser welding, the company says.

CAD/CAM software made for multiaxis laser processing

Prima Power Laserdyne has released FASTTRIM™ CAD/CAM software for use with its multiaxis precision laser processing systems. The all-inone software enables users to model parts, define process paths, define feature locations, and build part fixtures. It allows posting a complete program for 2D and 3D laser welding, drilling, and cutting applications.

The software supports the LASERDYNE® 795, 430, and 606D systems for welding, percussion drilling, hole trepanning, and cutting. Features include full part simulation with collision detection and automatic programming based on the geometry of the model.

Ceramic oxide flap discs provide cooler grinding

PFERD has introduced the POLIVLIES

®

ceramic oxide Co-Cool flap disc. The abrasive material provides high grinding performance, with top-sizing for reduced loading and cooler grinding on poor heatconducting materials. The interleaved flap construction alternates between aggressive coated abrasive and nonwoven material, allowing for grinding followed by surface conditioning in a single operation.

The discs are recommended for work on large surfaces made from stainless steel but can be used on nearly all materials. Offering high flexibility, they are compatible with angle grinders.

The discs are available in 41/2- and 5-in. dia., in both plain arbor hole and threaded hub configurations with a maximum speed of 13,300 RPM. They come in coarse-100, medium-180, and fine-240 grit sizes.

Deburring blade offers material removal versatility

ATI Industrial Automation has introduced the new Compliant Deburring Blade. The pneumatically controlled, articulated deburring tool allows users to tune the contact force to accommodate specific applications by changing the air pressure.

The tool is designed for light and medium deburring, chamfering, and scraping operations on materials such as plastic, aluminum, steel, and brass. The collet accommodates a variety of blades and media used with hand deburring tools. The blades can be changed without the use of additional tools.

Its axial and radial compliance is suitable for maintaining constant force where surface irregularities are present.

GMAW system uses twin-wire design for high-deposition welding

Lincoln Electric

®

has introduced Hyper-Fill™, a patent-pending, twin-wire system for high-deposition GMAW. Developed for semiautomatic or robotic applications, the system increases the usable deposition rate.

It uses two smaller-diameter wires to produce a larger weld droplet and arc cone. This generates a large weld puddle, allowing operators to increase usable deposition rates on average up to 50 percent over traditional single-wire processes, says the company.

The system uses a single power source, feeder, gun liner, and contact tip.
