# Waterjet cutting without angle errors

[TARİH: 01.05.2017 ISMR]

"THE ANGLE ERROR, the deviation between upper and lower surfaces of the material being cut, is created in waterjet cutting from the waterjet losing power when cutting the material and thereby removing more material from the upper surface. The result is the typical V-shaped taper. For many cutting jobs, the angle error is irrelevant and sometimes even helpful. But, for highprecision jobs or when cutting material which has to match other jobs along the cut edge, preventing the cutting angle errors is essential. However, particularly where thin materials are concerned e.g. 2mm stainless steel, the otherwise insignificant angle error produces an effect of up to 7 degrees," explained STM.

To answer this issue, development engineers at STM have developed a new cutting head as an addition to the modular system. The new STM TAC/12 (Taper Angle Control) cutting head was based on the STM3D68 cutting head (68°). With a swivel range of up to 12°, the head compensates for the cutting angle error by using a swivelling movement which makes it possible, according to the manufacturer, to work with even greater precision at higher rates of feed. The STM TAC/12 cutting head automatically compensates for the angle error to below +/- 0.05mm.

"With the STM TAC/12 cutting head, perfect 90° cuts, 100% round holes and parts which have to fit together, can all be cut easily and rapidly. This can be achieved with extremely low capital expenditure and without having to use an additional software module. Because of its compact design, the cutting head can be easily installed on existing STM systems. It also has integrated height scanning and collision protection," added STM.

"STM waterjet cutting systems are adapted technically to exactly match requirements. Customers do not have to purchase any additional applications but can retain flexibility. This is because even entry-level models can be upgraded as required to adapt quickly and reliably to changing requirements. Various tuning options are available, such as vacuum monitoring in the abrasive dosing unit as well as the option of operating several cutting heads simultaneously."

STM focuses on waterjet cutting systems without bellows. The standard water-protected linear guide, it explained, is 'consequently easy to clean, exhibits a better protective effect, offers many years of use and considerably improves the appearance of the equipment. The low consumption of compressed air for equipment operation also keeps operating costs low'. See www.stm.at
