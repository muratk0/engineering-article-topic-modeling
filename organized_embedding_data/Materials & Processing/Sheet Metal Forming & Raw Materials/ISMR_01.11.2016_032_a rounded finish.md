# A ROUNDED FINISH

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

Timesavers' managing director demonstrated the company's latest innovations to ISMR at EuroBLECH 2016

"Sharp edges on material are no longer tolerated in the sheet metal industry. With the increase in powder coating painting systems, sharp edges are just not acceptable. More and more customers want sheet metal parts to be rounded off. The use of fibre lasers now for cutting material also creates sharp edges, which increases the need for deburring machines. We also see an increasing interest in edge-rounding in the market," Paul Hartendorp, Timesavers' managing director, told ISMR at this year's EuroBLECH exhibition (25-29 October 2016) in Hanover, Germany.

The last few years have been very successful for the Timesavers Group, which registered a 60 million-euro turnover last year. The diversified group, with its headquarters in North America, also manufacturers machines for stainless steel finishing and calibration of multiple materials. As a specialist in sanding and grinding technology, Timesavers also produces machines for special applications. Over 50,000 Timesavers-built, wide-belt sanding and grinding machines are in operation worldwide.

"I am very pleased with the growth we have enjoyed this year in the grinding/ deburring business. Germany has always been our biggest market in Europe. However, markets in France, the UK, Switzerland, Denmark and Austria have also been successful for us. We expect growth next year and are interested in new market regions, such as the Middle East," added Hartendorp.

## Robotic deburring

At EuroBLECH 2016, Timesavers presented, for the first time, a combination of its 42 series WRBW rotary brush machine and a robot. The workpieces, to be deburred or edge-rounded, pass the machine in a dedicated zone on.

the conveying belt; the robot identifies the parts, turns them and, for the final brushing operation, places them on the opposite zone of the conveying belt.

Paul Hartendorp explained that, although robotic systems have been generally adopted across production industries, they have not been used as much with deburring or edge-rounding processes in the sheet metal industry.

“Everyone still sees deburring as a manual process – we want to show customers here at EuroBLECH that they can, if they wish, integrate robotic technology into the deburring process. Several of our customers in Germany and France have already started doing this with our deburring technology, so we felt that we really needed to offer this option.

Our software engineers can liaise with robot manufacturers of a customer's choice," he told ISMR.

"Using this combination, rotary brushing makes enormous gains in profitability – only the loading and unloading of the 42 series WRBW needs to be done by the operator. The development also increases the level of integration into fully automatic processing lines, as expected from the forthcoming Industry 4.0 standard. Whether the parts are prepared by gas, plasma, laser or waterjet cutting processes, or whether they are punched or machined (flat or embossed), our brushing process guarantees reliable deburring and edge rounding of steel; stainless steel; copper and aluminium – galvanized, coated, laminated or even printed."

## New systems and products

The deburring specialist confirmed to ISMR, at EuroBLECH, that it had again won a prize for its 42 series Rotary Brush (RB) machines.

"We built a 42 RB series with a two-way conveyer belt and a fully integrated robot arm with vision technology ('de vision technic'). The combination of the robot arm and 'de vision technic' makes it possible to recognize different sizes, weights and types of materials. All this is done at high speed so that the robot arm can process this at the right time, in the right way," added Hartendorp.

"The other new innovation that we are showing at EuroBLECH, in our RB machine, is the combination of oxide skin removal and deburring/edge rounding processes," Hartendorp added.

"Ten years ago, oxide skin removal was a key issue. Customers wanted to transition from nitrogen lasers to oxide lasers because they were cheaper and more environmentally friendly. However, this led to a blueish tinge on the material being cut. This had to be removed because, at the painting stage, paint would not adhere to the material. We have now found a solution for this, using different type of brushes in our machines. Customers can now combine oxide removal and edge rounding processes in the same machine just by replacing a few of the eight brushes already in the machine for oxide skin removal brushes."

Other highlights on the Timesavers' booth were a 42 series WRBW 1350 rotary brush machine with eight rotary brushes, incorporated pre- and finishing belt/drums and a 42 series WRBW 1000mm rotary brush machine (a more compact version of Type 1350 with a width of 1,000mm of the conveyor belt). The manufacturer also showcased a 32 series WMDMD 1100mm wide from the group of Type 32 Series machines featuring a new design (a solution made for laser cut parts) and a Tenth Series manual grinder (with manually changeable, operational mode between abrasive disc and brush unit to save costs)

"There is strong demand for a mid-sized machine that also uses brush technology - so we are also showing the first machine with this technology on our booth here," continued Hartendorp. "As of January next year, we will manufacture an even larger range of machines, using the brush and/or in combination with drum technology that you find in larger machines, but on a smaller scale."

## Continuous innovation

One of the things that Timesavers has learned, with the level of competition that it faces, is the need to continuously innovate.

"Some of our competitors are moving from the wood industry to the sheet metal industry. However, there is a huge difference between customers in the wood industry and those in the sheet metal industry. In the sheet metal industry, users are more interested in the technological solutions and the machine itself. They want to know how the machine is built, what it means for the lifecycle of the machine, maintenance etc. In the wood business, however, customers tend to be much more interested in the end result of the wood products, rather than in the machine itself," explained Hartendorp.

"This means that your sales people have to be very technically-oriented in the sheet metal industry and you must continuously innovate with your products. In the late 1990s, we therefore decided to fully concentrate on the metal industry and not the wood industry. You need to have a clear focus to be successful."

In the future, Hartendorp sees new opportunities in areas such as calibration for Timesaver machines.

"Customers want to see a machine that automatically calibrates itself, so that users no longer need to input all the parameters – the machine will automatically take care of these adjustments. Automating the calibration process takes this task away from operators. We are looking into developing a project like this with selected technology partners. Vision, HMI (Human Machine Interface) and sensor technologies will need to be integrated into the machine. We are using these technologies in a very rudimentary way at the moment and I believe we can do more with these to make machines more customer-friendly. This, I believe, is the right direction to take..."
