# Ferdi Töngi, Bystronic’s CEO, highlights key trends and challenges in global sheet metal markets

[TARİH: 01.12.2012 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“Bystronic launched nine new products at the EuroBLECH sheet metal exhibition in Germany recently”

ISMR: Which current and future key challenges and trends in sheet metal markets can you highlight?

FT: There are a few key current and future challenges in the market that I can see. Chief amongst these is the need to focus on overall process optimisation, to reduce overall costs and lead times as well as to increase efficiency, rather than individual machine performance. Lightly manned future production means that machines must become more autonomous – power and performance will become even more important. At the same time, machines must become easier to operate because of the current lack of skilled personnel in some countries.

I see an important trend towards more flexible systems and larger formats with an emphasis on better material usage and throughput. In terms of cutting and bending at the same speed, there is a need for faster press brakes that can bend thin material. Branding has also become more important – we see this in the increasing sales of German cars in China, for example.

Fiber laser usage will completely change market behaviour in the future. Every two weeks, I see new competitors emerging. I believe, in the long term, that fiber laser will take up to 50 per cent of the laser cutting market in the future with CO2 lasers taking the rest of the market.

ISMR: You mentioned at EuroBLECH that more customers will make future purchasing decisions based on soft factors rather than hard factors. Can you elaborate on this?

FT: Yes – I have seen this happening already. Lasers and press brakes are becoming commodities. It is becoming more difficult to make differentiations based on product USPs. In the past, manufacturers have been able to differentiate products for example by laser power etc. However, the differentiation factor today is becoming smaller and smaller. The remaining issues around the machine therefore become more important and we now have to differentiate on issues such as service, support, application expertise, customer trust etc.

Just five or six years ago, customers on our booth at EuroBLECH were asking about the type of drives, chassis etc. on our machines – they no longer ask these questions now. They now ask much more about application support, process optimisation and cost reduction to improve their processes.

Overall process optimisation is key, in my opinion. The entire process is important to reduce overall costs and lead times as well as to increase efficiency. Think of a car that travels at 300km/hour but needs the right road to achieve this speed. In the sheet metal business, we are not really prepared for fast machines and this is a big step. The big European OEM customers need to move in this direction and dramatically reduce their costs. However, just making the machine faster does not reduce their costs by 30 per cent – they need to look at the overall sheet metal process for that.

ISMR: What percentage of your sales are made in Asia? What is your strategy for Asia?

FT: We achieve just over a quarter of our sales in Asia and 50 per cent in Europe. That is a tremendous shift if you compare this with the last ten years. If we work just on the size of the market, not the number of units sold, I think Asia will go up 50 per cent and Europe will lose some of this percentage. In number of units sold, China is still the biggest market for us.

Over the last few years, the Asian market has been largely dominated by China. However, we now see markets like Thailand and Indonesia growing. Indonesia and Thailand need large machines for infrastructure projects. These projects create business for job shops.

We have opened a sales and service company in Vietnam for the Asian market. We also have a factory in China and make about one quarter of our laser machines in China for the Chinese market.
