## Grinding, deburring and finishing

Dutch surface finishing specialist, Timesavers International, offers a range of budget machines for deburring, edge breaking, slag removal and finishing. Its three smallest machines are the 10 series, 12 series and 22 series.

The 10 series is a starter model known as the manual grinder. This machine has two heads that can be fitted with various types of abrasive tooling. While grinding discs can be used for deburring and finishing, there are also brushes available for edge rounding, laser oxide removal or slag removal. Users can switch to another application by turning the head or changing the type of brush. The table is 1300 × 800mm and has a vacuum part in the middle to keep smaller parts in place. Anyone, Timesavers told ISMR, can operate the 10 series.

Another of Timesavers' smaller machine solutions is the 12 series, suitable for deburring and finishing products up to a thickness of 125mm. These are used worldwide for the processing of small and larger parts of up to 600mm. The magnet in the transport table ensures that smaller objects can also be deburred.

"Our 22 series can be equipped with the hammerhead, a solution for slag removal. For this reason, it is a popular solution for companies who work with plasma cutters. The hammerhead hammers off the slag in a fast and effective way, without damaging the material surface. This deburring machine can be built with one up to three machine heads such as belts, brushes, discs and, of course, the hammerhead. These 'configurations' are available in three different working widths: 900, 1050 or 1280mm," explained Timesavers.

## Manual grinding at Tummers

Tummers Plaatbewerking in The Netherlands is a 'one-stop shop' for sheet metal and mainly processes stainless steel by laser cutting, bending and assembly. It has over 25 years of experience and a line-up of modern sheet metalworking machinery including TRUMPF laser cutting machines and press brakes.

Although the process is largely automated, the company cannot do without a manual deburring machine and uses a Timesavers 10 series manual grinder. The machine works in a line with two Timesavers 42 RB deburring machines at Tummers Plaatbewerking.

"Much of our sheet metal is for the food industry and the stainless steel must always have rounded edges. These Timesavers machines are suitable for that operation. We use the manual grinder if we have products that have a slightly lower grinding quality, so that we can process them in advance. Further processing is then done via the RB series machines; this is mainly for the smaller products. In the past, an angle grinder may have been used for this operation, but we don't use these anymore. Safety comes first," explained Ricardo Moerkens, Director, Tummers Plaatbewerking. When laser cutting virtually all types of metal, sharp edges and sometimes burrs are created which it is important to remove. The company's deburring drum and Timesavers machines remove these imperfections from steel, stainless steel and aluminium sheet metal. This not only improves the appearance of the metal, but also contributes to the functionality and safety of the end-product.

"We have been working with Timesavers for over ten years now," added Moerkens. "We choose their machines because they are rugged, offer low maintenance costs and guarantee a long tool life. The machines are easy to operate and explain. The directionless brush in the 42 RB series is suitable for our products. Our customers in the food industry expect this quality from us so, in turn, we expect that level of quality from our suppliers."

## Constant Group invests in surface finishing

The UK-based Constant Group provides a full design-to-manufacture service that encompasses its own products, including LED lighting systems for industrial and commercial applications and architectural products (such as kick plates and ventilation systems). It also provides a subcontract fabrication service, with customers across many industry sectors including pharmaceutical, food processing, enclosures and packaging. Today, it is a full-service supplier of fabricated parts and assemblies, operating from 50,000ft² premises with the latest manufacturing technology.

With batch sizes ranging from 10-off through to 1000s off, the management team at Constant Group took the decision in 2019 to make a significant investment of £1 million in fibre laser and press brakes. While these machines may have been the main focus, the arrival of a Timesavers 12 series 600mm W deburring and finishing machine has also made a significant impact on the Group's efficiency when grinding stainless steel materials. "The Timesavers machine was purchased specifically to process stainless steel, which we targeted as an area of operation where we could make the greatest efficiency," said Matthew Garlick, Joint Managing Director Constant Group. "Prior to the arrival of the 12 series machine we were hand-grinding all these parts, which could take 20 minutes each. We now achieve that in seconds, with consistent quality."

“The Timesavers machine has been running eight hours per day minimum, with no issues whatsoever, and the ability to have anyone running it is a big advantage especially during the COVID-19 pandemic (where if we had staff absent, we knew we could continue to process work because of the simplicity of the machine). Beyond this, we are also seeing significant savings in consumables compared to manual grinding, where the lack of consistency between operators meant increased spend on abrasive pads. We are now in total control of that aspect of the business," he continued.

"While we only specified the entry-level machine, our experience has certainly given us the confidence to go for a higher specification as we identify further areas of growth for the business," added Matthew Garlick.

## Bedding in with a 22 series

Manufacturing a range of standard and bespoke beds is a labour-intensive process that includes welding, fabrication, assembly, painting and coating. All of this is done in-house at the Wrought Iron and Brass Bed company, whose manufacturing facility is at Wolferton on the Sandringham Estate in Norfolk (UK). However, increased demand put pressure on production and the need to automate certain processes became inevitable. Technical director, Jack Thompson, turned to a Timesavers' distributor for a solution, introducing the 22 series automatic deburring machine.

All the Wrought Iron and Brass Bed Company's products are made to order, so production is a mix of parts with relatively low volumes, with control of the process from order to delivery of the finished product undertaken in-house. Part of that process includes deburring of individual component parts for each bed and, where required, creating a brush finish prior to coating for specific charge parts. This was a process that was previously undertaken manually; it would take an entire week to fully prepare a complete set of components by hand. The installation of a Timesavers 22 series dry finishing and deburring machine has now reduced the time required to a single day.

“The Timesavers machine has given us major time savings on what was an extremely labour-intensive process. We have freed people to work on other things," said Jack Thompson.

"As we develop the business, the machine will also be a great asset for deburring flat sheet products."

While bed manufacture remains central to the company's development, the increased efficiency and capacity created by the addition of the new machine is allowing expansion into other areas, with gazebos and garden sculptures now available.

"The plan is to have a standard range of these products, made from section material or flat sheet, but we have the flexibility and capability to design and manufacture to offer a bespoke service. The Timesavers machine will be a great advantage here as we can process flat sheet quickly, creating a brushed finish on all edges. This corner rounding is vital, both in terms of health and safety for customers but also a precise corner is essential in the coating process to ensure good adherence to the material," concluded Jack Thompson.■
