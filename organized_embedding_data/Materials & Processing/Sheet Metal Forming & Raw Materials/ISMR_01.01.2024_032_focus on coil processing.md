There are several stages to sheet metal slitting, as outlined below:

■Storage station: This is where the processed raw material and the workpiece is stored. For slitting, this is usually a big, broad roll of metal sheet.

■Transport trolley: A trolley is used to move the workpiece from the storage station into the uncoiler. The unwinder unwinds the coil as it feeds the metal sheet into another machine for slitting.

■Slitter: Once the material leaves the uncoiler, it is fed into the slitter. How this happens depends upon the type of slitting undergone by the material. The slitter is set to cut the material in several narrow sheets according to the order requirements.

Recoiler: The last stage of the process is the recoiler. It is the opposite of the uncoiler. The recoiler winds the different strips of materials neatly. In some cases, a tension device can be used to ensure that the coils are neat and firm.

Sheet metal slitting requires careful set-up of the slitting machine to avoid inconsistencies that could be caused by leaving the blades with too little clearance, too much clearance or without proper vertical clearance.

## Multi-blanking/blank cutting lines

In sheet metal processing, ever greater flexibility of blanks is required for complex applications. Even with changing sheet thicknesses, demanding materials often need to be cut precisely and quickly. Multi-blanking systems combine both cut-to-length and slitting steps in a single run to save time and resources.

“This means that even changing cut sizes can be carried out efficiently in-house, without additional logistics costs," commented manufacturer, B+S.

Using multi-blanking systems, several precise and individual sheet metal blanks can be produced in one run. In addition, the high vertical range of manufacture offers greater independence for rapidly changing market requirements.

The B+S group multi-blanking lines combine a cut-to-length line with a slitting line and are designed for sheets with a material width of up to 2,500mm and a material thickness of 0.1 to 6.0mm.

"Before the first cut, the coil is first levelled and only then cut into longitudinal strips," it explained. "These strips are then cut crosswise, resulting in up to five sheet blanks per segment in the desired length. Due to the large number of sheet blanks with small dimensions, the stacking and packaging area plays an important role. Therefore, we paid attention to different stacking techniques such as a vacuum, magnetic tape or swing arm stacker," it added.

Multi-blank cutting lines are designed to transform a parent coil into several flat packaged parts. This type of line is characterised by its high flexibility.

"The main characteristics of these lines are the same as the cut-to-length lines, with the difference that a circular shear is incorporated.

Positioned before the catenary of the line, it cuts the strip into smaller bands. Once the cut has been made, the bands must be separated from each other to carry out subsequent processes. After the band-cutting process, it should be possible to process the edges by cutting them (with shear blades) or rolling them in a coiling machine," outlined coil processing specialist, FAGOR ARRASATE.

Combined lines are also a combination of slitting lines and cut-to-length lines. Depending upon the product to be obtained (strip or sheet), the strip will be processed on different machines so that the required end product is obtained.

The type of machines used in this type of line will depend mainly upon the thickness of the material to be processed. In general, these lines feature the following working areas:

■Coil loading area. Depending upon the type of material and thickness (fine/coarse) to be processed, the configuration of the loading area can have different machines to suit its function properly. To process stainless steel or aluminium, tube extraction systems and paper winding machines can be installed.

## ■Strapping cutting area. The

Tensioning area. The function of the tensioner can be adapted according to the material to be processed, the surface quality to be obtained and the thickness to be processed. Options include belt tensioners (stainless steel); felt presses; tensioning rollers; 'S' rollers etc. The tensioner can be fixed and therefore the strips will clear the pit via a mobile clamp or a loop duplicator.

■Cross-cutting area. Depending upon the processing speed, different types of shears can be used.

■Coil-winding and extraction area. Depending upon the type of material and thickness (fine/coarse) to be processed, the configuration of the coil-winding and removal area will vary to suit each case.
