# ENSURING GOOD PRACTICE

[TARİH: 01.04.2011 ISMR]

Correct storage makes stock control and materials management easier and reduces damage and wastage. Improving your storage and handling of materials will result in reduced costs and improved management, quality and safety.

CIRIA has put together a list of good practice guidelines that will help ensure greater efficiency and protection for stored materials.

## Benefits will arise from:

■ Easier recovery of stored materials and components

■ A tidy site – which can help in achieving a safer and more efficient site

■ Reduced waste disposal costs (as fewer material and components will be wasted)

■ Avoidance of re-ordering delayed materials

■ Reducing the risk of pollution/spillages.

The associated cost savings (brought about either directly or through programme improvements) can more than offset the costs of correct management of materials and components. This will improve the ‘bottom line’ on your projects.

Aim to eliminate background factors that constrain effective management of materials and components on site, says CIRIA, such as:

■ An untidy site

■ A lack of information and training for site staff

■ A lack of incentives to improve practices

■ Sloppiness and entrenched attitudes

■ Use of inappropriate plant

■ Poor planning of site layout and inadequate weather protection.

## KEY ISSUES FOR MATERIAL STORAGE

The essential component of an overall site storage strategy is to allocate appropriate areas for central storage and worksite storage. Prepare storage areas in advance so that materials can be off-loaded straight into them – double handling increases the risk of damage and losses. Prepare a clear site layout plan showing material and component storage locations.

The following issues need to be considered for effective management of materials:

## 1. Layout of storage area

Take into account the three distinct activities taking place in the storage area: receiving materials, storage and issue of materials. Then plan to minimise the movement of materials and components. Would two entrances to the storage area be better than one? Group items of stock by how frequently they are required; place the materials and components that are used most often nearest to the access point.

## 2. Covering

A translucent lightweight cover over the storage area allows all-weather use with natural light, but it must be carefully designed to withstand wind pressures and allow mechanical handling equipment to move freely.

## 3. Stacking

The following suggestions from CIRIA can improve the efficiency of storage operations.

■ Make best use of the available height of the storage area – use shelves, racks and pallets

■ Where racking is appropriate, use sectional interlocking metal racking or designed systems rather than traditional steel scaffolding. Consider using an A-frame for storing bars, sheet materials and timber planks

■ Always make sure that any stacking or racks used are safe.

## 4. Protecting materials and components CIRIA also has the following advice for the

protection of materials and components on-site.

■ Materials or components that are valuable or attractive to thieves or vandals should be stored in a secure area, out of sight

■ Would some form of mobile storage hut or container be useful, such as steel shipping containers?

■ Does each trade have a use of a lockable storage box or lock-up?

■ Always store materials well away from waste storage containers and from areas where vehicle movements could cause accidental damage

■ Secure lightweight materials to protect them from wind damage

■ Locate material stacks away from the edge of excavation

■ Always protect materials and components that are vulnerable to frost
