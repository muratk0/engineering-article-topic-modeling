# CHINA STEEL INSTALLS MASSIVE WATER JET

[TARİH: 01.10.2011 ISMR]

CHINA STEEL, a custom fabrication and machine shop in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, recently added a massive 90KSI Jet Edge water jet cutting machine capable of cutting virtually any material up to 24’x13’. With its 100hp 90KSI Jet Edge X-Stream powered system, China Steel (pronounced “keena”) can cut parts up to 50 per cent faster than waterjet shops with standard 60KSI waterjets. It also can cut large parts twice as fast with dual abrasive-jet cutting heads with mirroring capabilities.

Established in the late 1950s, China Steel continues to be owned and operated by the China family, which traces its proud metalworking heritage to early 19th-century blacksmiths and metalworkers in Friuli, Italy. Under President Fred China and brother Vice President Carlo China’s direction, China Steel has grown to become a diversified and technologically advanced metalworking plant with sales worldwide.

Its diverse projects have included everything from decorative staircases to locomotive under-frames to dozens of parts for the US Army Stryker armoured vehicles to stainless steel dewatering wheels for steel mills in the Ukraine and India.

“In order to stay in business, you have to be diversified and keep up with technology,” Carlo noted. “If you don’t keep up with technology, you might as well put a lock on the door.”

“We were looking at lasers and plasmas, but the water jet is more versatile,” Carlo recalled. “It has higher precision and is capable of cutting thicker diverse materials, plus it’s environmentally friendly and there’s no heat distortion. You cut a part and it fits. We went with the 90,000 psi system because it gives us the cutting horsepower that we need and we went with the large table because we have a diversified shop and many of the parts we cut are of significant size.” FOR MORE INFORMATION, PLEASE SEE www.china-steel.com or www.jetedge.com

## 19% VISITOR INCREASE FOR MTA VIETNAM

HO CHI MINH City’s largest machine tools and metalworking trade exhibition ended on a high note in July after seeing a total of 9,974 attendees. The total number of trade visitors totalled 8,243 – a substantial 19 per cent increase from its previous edition in 2010.

MTAVietnam 2011 buzzed with activity over four days. TRUMPF told ISMR that it sold two machines that were on display at the show and Edward Yuen, General Manager at TRUMPF, confirmed he had met quality visitors and received many new enquiries from the local sheet metal fabrication sector.

MTAVietnam 2012 will return from 3 – 6 July 2012 at the Saigon Exhibition and Convention Center (SECC) in Ho Chi Minh City.

## NEW RESEARCH RECEIVES NIOBIUM METALLURGY AWARD

RESEARCH CONTRIBUTING to an enhanced metallurgical understanding in the processing of HTP steels to make commercial higher grade line pipe steels in modern plate and strip mill facilities won the 2011 Charles Hatchett Award. The award was won for the first time by a research group led by scientists from China.

The annual award sponsored by CBMM – Companhia Brasileira de Metalurgia e Mineração – is given to the authors who have written the best paper on the science and technology of niobium and its alloys. The winning researchers from the University of Science and Technology, Beijing, China and the McMaster University, Canada were presented with a medal at this year’s Institute of Materials, Minerals and Mining (IOM3) Annual Dinner and Awards Ceremony held in London recently.

This winning paper describes research on identifying an important temperature window in the upstream rolling of high niobium low carbon HTP steel for grain refining austenite. Identifying this temperature window gives better control of austenite processing in hot rolling, allowing the production of higher grade line pipe steels with excellent strength and low temperature toughness.

HARLOW GROUP, an innovative UK specialist in precision sheet metal components and assemblies, has installed a new Safan E-Brake to replace two existing machines. Selected after a visit to the EuroBlech 2010 exhibition, the decision to buy a Safan machine sees Harlow Group move away from its established press brake supplier.

“The arrival of the Safan E-Brake in April 2011 is the first in a programme to replace all nine of our existing press brakes over the coming years,” said company’s manufacturing director, Marc Boulden. The company’s decision to opt for an E-Brake is already paying dividends – according to Harlow Group, cycle times have been cut by 25 – 30 per cent, while set-up times have been halved.

Plant capabilities at Harlow Group include CNC punching, laser cutting, forming, welding, paint finishing and electromechanical assembly – the company even offers a ‘concept to customer’ outsourcing and logistics OEM service. Materials up to 3mm thick form core business, while typical batch sizes range between 25 and 200.

“Reducing set-up times is particularly fundamental for our business, especially moving forward,” says Mr Boulden. “I want Harlow Group customers to get what they want, when they want it, and in the quantity desired. Batches are getting smaller and lead-times are shortening to meet increasingly rapid time-to-market. It’s no longer commercially viable to simply accept that a press brake takes a while to set-up. We want to apply the Formula One tyre-change model to press brake operations – and the Safan E-Brake is the nearest currently available. It’s very easy to program and use, which is vital because sheet metal forming has been a black art for too long. With the E-Brake, no special knowledge is required.”

This progressive approach to tackling manufacturing’s established challenges has seen Harlow Group achieve 25 per cent sales growth since Mr Boulden and two fellow directors completed an MBO around six years ago. Today, the company is a 24/7 operation that employs 104 people – up 9 per cent in the past 12 months – and is now looking to take on its first two apprentices later this year. Furthermore, the company is two years into a five-year rolling programme that will see it invest around £100,000 per annum moving forward.

The press brake model acquired by Harlow Group is a Safan E-Brake 35-1250 (35 ton pressure force x 1250mm working width). The machine features a patented roller drive system in the upper beam to ensure uniform and direct distribution of forces.
