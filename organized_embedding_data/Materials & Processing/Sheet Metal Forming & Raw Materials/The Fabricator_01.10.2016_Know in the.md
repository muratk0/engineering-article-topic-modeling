GOLF4MFG FUNDRAISER RESULTS IN MORE SCHOLARSHIPS AND SUMMER CAMP GRANTS FOR MANUFACTURING STUDENTS

Eighty-eight golfers gathered to play one of their favorite games on a gorgeous day for the Nuts, Bolts & Thingamajigs

®

(NBT) fourth annual GOLF4MFG at Cantigny Golf Course in Wheaton, Ill. The generosity of the players and sponsors allows NBT to continue the mission of awarding more scholarships and summer manufacturing grants. Thank you all for the part you played to help close the skilled-labor gap.

Before the outing on Aug. 22, participants were able to tour Clipper Industries Inc. in Itasca, Ill. They saw technology in action, including laser, punch, and plasma cutting; precision forming; machining; as well as robotic welding. NBT appreciates the open door and hospitality from Clipper Industries.

New games were introduced at GOLF4MFG 2016. Contestants were invited to play SPLASH 4 CASH on the driving range. Does it seem like an easy shot to hit a baby pool at 49 yards out? It proved to be a bit tricky for 21 golfers to forget about avoiding the water hazard.

The on-site golf professional was waiting on Lakeside Hole 4 to take on the generous folks of GOLF4MFG 2016. Golfers were asked if they wanted to BEAT THE PRO on a difficult par 3. Eight players came up victorious and went home with a prize!

Cocktails, appetizers, and dinner followed, along with a short award presentation. The first-place foursome won on a scorecard playoff with nine under par. Rick Schwer, Dave Schwer, Rick Blesi, and John Fitzgerald hold this title for two of the four NBT GOLF4MFG events.

What Really Matters in this Election

By Dr. Chris Kuehl

We all remember those civics classes that discussed the division of powers, but our political races tend to obscure the facts. This is the time of year the candidates focus on economic issues but rarely stop to point out that they really have very little power over the way the economy works. The most fundamental fact is the economy is the result of tens of billions of small decisions made by consumers and the business community that is dealing with these consumers. Beyond that the majority of the decisions made regarding the economy are in the hands of the Federal Reserve (monetary policy) and Congress as they set the country’s fiscal policy.

Vote for innovation and growth.

Much of what we are hearing from Clinton and Trump is not much more than a suggestion for what the good men and women of Congress and the Fed should be doing. The votes that will matter the most are those that will bring members of the House and Senate to power. Regardless of how the standard bearers of the two parties present themselves, their parties have been far more traditional as far as policies are concerned and it therefore matters which party is in control.

In general, the GOP candidates tend to favor less regulation, lower taxes, more trade, and other pro-business positions, but that is not always the case. Many pro-business Democrats and many Republicans take an anti-business stance. It becomes the voter’s responsibility to decide which of the two most reflects the positions important to that voter.

It is already clear that more people will split their ticket than ever before. They may or may not support the party’s standard bearer and may elect to vote their usual affiliation down the ballot but not at the top. It is unlikely that either party will win both the Senate and House, and that makes the position of the individual legislator that much more important.

There are many reasons to vote for or against a politician, but for the manufacturer, it matters what a given leader feels about taxes, regulation, trade, job training, and the research and development efforts that drive innovation and growth.
