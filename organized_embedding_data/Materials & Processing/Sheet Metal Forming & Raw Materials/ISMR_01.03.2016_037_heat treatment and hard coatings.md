## Lean tool concept

BIHLER WILL present its new “lean tool concept” at the Wire exhibition in Düsseldorf (Hall 10, Stand F12). Reaction times as well as the production cost of new tools for the type “RM-NC” and “GRM-NC” servo-controlled stamping and forming machines have been reduced and new products are brought to market faster, said the company, even in very small batch sizes.

“The new concept is the product of over 60 years of Bihler tool technology experience. The cost of new tools on the two servo-controlled machines has been significantly reduced thanks to the small number of tool components and quick and easy implementation. Savings of over 50%, compared to forming tools on mechanical equipment, can be made,” said Bihler.

With the lean tool concept, many tool parts are now implemented as standard parts. This means that their manufacturing can be standardised. The number of new tool parts that need to be designed therefore decreases. This standardisation, the simple design and the universal application facilitate short reaction times from the initial request to the finished new tool. This stringency includes all areas, from planning through to design with Bihler “bNX” technology software to manufacturing of the tool components. New products can therefore be brought to market quickly and cost effectively, even in very small batch sizes.

Bihler offers a two-day design seminar for training on the new lean tool concept. In this seminar, participants will learn how to achieve a clear and structured approach for the design of their bending tools with the correct selection of standardised tools. Email info@bihler.de ■

## Flexible demagnetisation system

AT WIRE 2016 in Düsseldorf, Goudsmit Magnetic Systems (Hall 15, Stand G13) will present a newly developed mobile demagnetisation system with which magnetisation in metals can be removed or reduced to an acceptable level. This is done in situ, on location.

“The demagnetisation system is automatically configured for use by simply answering a number of questions on the display. Undesired magnetism is often caused by magnetic fields in the immediate vicinity. Common causes include clamping tables, magnetic conveyance, induction heating, tear testing and magnetic grippers. Such magnetisation can also be worsened by electric welding, mechanical vibration, grinding, sawing and cold deformation.

“This can have many different consequences. Plasma soldering is often impossible, because small metal particles and metal dust stick to the surfaces. Adhering materials also cause rapid wear of bearings, breakdowns and waiting time at automatic processing stations, problems with arc welding, unevenness and thickness differences during electrogalvanisation and chrome plating, or sharp edges during hot-dip galvanisation. Quite simply: undesired magnetism costs time and money and has a negative impact on the quality of finished products,” Goudsmit explained.

The system consists of a low-frequency generator and a specially designed cable system that is wound around the pipes to form a coil. This generates a strongly alternating magnetic field that dissipates in strength.

For example, PE-coated pipes for an oil refinery construction project in southeast India were found to contain remanent magnetism and could therefore not be welded. As a result, the entire project came to a standstill. In this case, there were around 40 pipes made of carbon steel, each 12 metres long with a diameter of Ø470 mm. Goudsmit performed the demagnetisation on site, a job that took four days. In addition to the recently developed mobile demagnetisation system, the company has developed several demagnetisation systems in tunnel and table form (optionally in combination with belt or roller conveyor) and another that is a handheld tool. See www.demagnetiseren.nl or www.goudsmitmagnets.com ■
