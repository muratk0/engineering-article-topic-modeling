# Miele chooses green steel from Salzgitter

[TARİH: 01.03.2022 ISMR]

The Miele Group, one of the world’s leading manufacturers of household appliances, has opted for Salzgitter AG's green steel in a drive to reduce its CO2 footprint. From November 2021 onwards, the domestic appliance group has been sourcing just under 24 tons of low CO2 steel per month as part of a pilot project. The steel will then find use in top covers for all stoves and ovens with a diameter of 60cm, which are produced at its Oelde plant. Further applications in other appliances are being examined. “The CO2 emissions generated by the manufacturing process are reduced by more than 66 per cent in this material through harnessing climate-compatible sources of energy and steel scrap. In taking this step, Miele is making a first contribution to lowering emissions in purchased goods and services (Scope 3.1) and taking an important step towards implementing its ambitious sustainability goals," explained Salzgitter.

Miele had already announced that, before the end of 2021, it would already be operating in a CO2-neutral manner across all its locations in terms of greenhouse gas emissions from its own combustion processes (Scope 1) and emissions from energy suppliers (Scope 2). In September 2021, the company also announced a specific target for emissions from the utilisation phase of Miele appliances that account for a major part of Scope 3. These emissions are to have been lowered by 15 percent by 2030 (compared to 2019) in terms of the overall consumption of all appliances in use.

"At the same time, we are intensively engaging in investigating options for optimising purchased goods and services," stated Dr. Stefan Breit, Miele's executive director for technology. These emissions are responsible for all CO2 emissions in connection with Miele.

The low CO2 steel grades are produced at Salzgitter's Peine's mini mill and processed further in rolling mills and galvanizing plants.

"When steel scrap is melted, and through the deployment of climate-compatible energy sources, the CO2 footprint of the galvanized material is 66 per cent lower compared to conventional production of steel via the blast furnace route. This has been verified by the TÜV SÜD (German technical inspectorate) based on data from 2018 on the various process routes for producing strip steel," Salzgitter told ISMR.

"Going forward, hydrogen and power from renewable energies can fully replace the carbon formerly required to produce steel, which will serve to lower the CO2 emissions from steel production by more than 95 per cent.“

Salzgitter AG is working on this ambitious goal through its Salzgitter Low CO2 Steelmaking transformation project (SALCOS). The key component is power from renewable sources and hydrogen that, together, will replace the coal currently used in the conventional blast furnace process.

In November 2021, Salzgitter Flachstahl won the Gold Award at the 19th MATERIALICA Design + Technology Awards in the 'Material – CO2 Efficiency' category for its low CO2 steel in serial automotive production.■
