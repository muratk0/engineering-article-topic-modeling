Custom grille manufacturer tests leveler’s versatility with short runs of different materials, sizes

SITUATION

Architectural Grille, based in Brooklyn, N.Y., manufactures custom grilles for the residential, commercial, hospitality, medical, architectural/decorative, and education markets. The 40-person shop produces custom designs in high volume—or quantities as small as one—cost-efficiently. Materials include various metals in thicknesses from 0.062 to 0.375 in., as well as nonmetals.

For years the firm used a hydraulic leveler, but blemishes caused by dirty leveling rollers were causing problems. “We had to bring in a rigger to tear down the machine so we could get at the roller section,” said Vice President and Engineering Manager Stephen Giumenta. “If a machine is difficult to maintain, it won’t get much maintenance.” With the company’s regular cleaning of leveling rollers to prevent blemishes on parts, simple maintenance was essential.

In addition, the company needed a machine that could handle parts made of aluminum, stainless steel, mild steel, brass, bronze, and copper in different shapes, sizes, and thicknesses, often heavily perforated with openings cut by laser, waterjet, and punch.

RESOLUTION

The company replaced the old hydraulic leveler with an Arku FlatMaster 55 in early 2014.

“We throw everything at the machine, and it usually takes just one pass to get the parts flat,” Giumenta explained.

Servo-hydraulic control of the leveling gap allows the machine to level the company’s variety of part sizes, shapes, and materials by adapting its performance to the nature of the part, whether the leveling rollers encounter the tip of a triangular part or the full cross section. As the part enters the leveling rollers, the machine can recognize any change in the required leveling force in a fraction of a second and apply more or less force to maintain a correct gap throughout the leveling section.

“The machine is a no-brainer to run,” said Giumenta. “You set the material thickness, put in a distorted part, and it comes out flat. We had it installed and began training on it in one day, and we were running parts the next day.”

One person can remove and clean the rollers in a few minutes, and the electrically efficient machine generates very little noise.

Architectural Grille’s part volume keeps the leveler busy about five hours a day. The excess capacity allows the company to solicit contract leveling from fabricators in the region.

Florida manufacturer solves noise problems with acoustic sound panels

SITUATION

A large manufacturer in central Florida recently was looking for a way to minimize excessive noise in its manufacturing complex and operations building.

In a manufacturing setting, a worker’s overall noise exposure may come directly from an individual machine, but noise from other machinery or processes elsewhere in the workshop may also contribute to the overall noise level. This means that not just machine operators are at risk, but also people working nearby, such as maintenance fitters, cleaners, forklift drivers, and shop floor supervisors.

Excessive noise is not only a potential liability for hearing loss and OSHA fines, but it also affects production output and creates possible safety issues when employees are distracted by it.
