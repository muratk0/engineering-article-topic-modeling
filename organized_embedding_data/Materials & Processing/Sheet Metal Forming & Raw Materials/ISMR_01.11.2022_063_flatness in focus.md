# Flatness in focus

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

ATHADER's stretch leveller eliminates visible shape defects such as edge waves; centre buckles and mandrel breaks. The Leveltek Process also can eliminate “Memory,” the distortion that can occur in cut parts after laser or plasma cutting of visibly flat sheets. The only measurable effect on the coil, it explained, is a slight reduction in material width, and a slight increase in length.

“Most visible flatness or shape defects occur because of length variations which develop during the rolling process. Edge waves occur when the strip is rolled with edges longer than the centre, for example, and centre buckle happens when a strip has a longer centre than edges,” it explained.

The stretch levelling process is simple. The coil is uncoiled, stretch-levelled and either recoiled or cut-to-length. Through a Leveltek proprietary non-marking process that grips and stretches the unrolled coil, ATHADER equipment eliminates shape defects such as edge waves, centre buckle, quarter buckle and mandrel breaks restoring coils that would otherwise end up as scrap metal. It does not mark or scratch the surface being stretched.

"One important market trend is [the strengthening of] stretch-levelling technology in the European market, after many years of being employed in North America. ATHADER, together with its partner Leveltek, has supplied three cut-to-length lines with stretch levellers in the last two years. Our customers that use this technology have substantially increased their sales of laser-cutting processors," he outlined.

"Another main trend is for fully automated coil-processing lines, without operator intervention in any phase of the process. It has been a challenge, but we are already taking steps in this direction," he told ISMR.

He confirmed that ATHADER's after-sales department personnel includes additional technicians and equipment to enable rapid remote assistance for customers, as well as the traditional on-site, face-to-face assistance. Remote operation of lines has been a key focus for the company.

“Steel prices have seen a huge increase over the past two years, with steel service centres making big profits. An important part of these profits has been invested in new coil-processing lines, filling up the order books of manufacturers. The steel price is now stable, and production costs have been adjusted after the enormous rise in energy and labour costs. The market is now looking at reducing the number of lines to reach the same production targets. The new equipment must provide better quality end products using fewer operators and at lower operational cost," he highlighted.

“Our greatest challenge is to achieve excellence in service and customer support. We intend to provide customers with added value to improve the productive running of their lines by using 'Big Data' from our software on maintenance, production levels etc. We also see an opportunity in creating new business units that deal exclusively with service: 'Production Assistance' and 'Consultancy'," he added.

## Going digital

The move to digitalisation has been a strong focus for ATHADER. It has already introduced robotic solutions designed to automate many processes in its lines that used to be done manually. These solutions, said Luengo, reduce the risks to operators, lower running costs of the line and increase overall productivity levels.

"We are currently using our proprietary software to capture data from our lines in operation. We are analysing this 'Big Data' with the aim of supplying machine learning and preventive maintenance alerts. We are also developing Virtual Commissioning tools that will flexibly train operators before they start working on the lines, reducing the time that they spend learning. Another new initiative for us is the implementation of an Integrated Platform for remote assistance, designed for our customers' benefit," concluded Harkaitz Luengo.
