# We highlight a selection of new global stamping and pressing technologies and innovations

[TARİH: 01.06.2016 ISMR]

Stamping (also known as pressing) is the process of placing flat sheet metal in either blank or coil form into a stamping press where a tool and die surface forms the metal into a net shape. Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes, such as punching, blanking, embossing, bending, flanging and coining. This could be a single-stage operation, where every stroke of the press produces the desired form on the sheet metal part, or could occur through a series of stages.

Operations include progressive die stamping; deep drawing; hot forming/press hardening (forming metal to make high strength parts through quick quenching of boron steels heated above 900 degrees) and hydroforming (a forming process with an active fluid). Progressive stamping is a metalworking method that can encompass punching, coining, bending and several other ways of modifying metal raw material, combined with an automatic feeding system. In this article, we highlight a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing innovations.

## The forming equation

THE AIDA GROUP offers a broad selection of small- to large-capacity mechanical and servo presses and related equipment. For FY 2015, the AIDA Group set a new record of ¥76.8 billion in consolidated net sales (a 10.5% year-on-year increase). In 2002, it completed development of the world's first direct-drive servo press and now manufactures all of the principal components for its presses in-house, including servo motors. The company also manufactures a complete line of metalforming presses, including gap, straightside, progressive die, transfer, high speed, cold forging and servo-driven mechanical presses. AIDA also offers complete and total turnkey solutions with feeders and automation (for further details, see our article on page 18).

AP&T delivered the world's first production line for hot forming of aluminium with a new method - HFQ®. The technology enables parts to be manufactured that have low weight, high strength, complex shapes and high dimensional stability in one single step. CIPCO, a 100% subsidiary of Impression Technologies Ltd (ITL), is the purchaser (HFQ is a registered trademark of Impression Technologies Ltd.).

AP&T built a fully automated and integrated production line for Aesculap.

AP&T recently developed a new method, with Sandvik and Cell Impact, for the cost-efficient manufacturing of fuel cell plates. At Blechexpo last November, the company focused on lightweight forming – the warm forming of aluminium (fuel cell lines that it believes will grow in the medium term). It showed a 3D simulation with a fuel cell line and a 3D simulation of warm forming of aluminium (6000 and 7000 series) on its booth. AP&T has started to offer automation equipment from NORDA through AP&T in Germany and Poland.

AgustaWestland, an Italian specialist in vertical flight innovation, has installed a Flexform™ fluid cell press from Avure Technologies at PZL-Swidnik, its helicopter factory in Swidnik, Poland. The Avure model QFC 0.7x1.8-800 press, featuring a forming area of 0.7 x 1.8m (27.5 x 72 inches), provides production capacity of several hundred thousand sheet metal parts per year. Often compared to sheet hydroforming presses, Flexform fluid cell presses employ ultra-high pressure to form a wide variety of complex shapes.

The Kale Pratt & Whitney joint venture is also using an Avure hydroform deep draw press to manufacture aircraft engine components in Turkey. Avure Technologies is a large global provider of ultra-high pressure systems utilised to process advanced materials and to form sheet metal. It has installed over 1,700 high-pressure systems worldwide.

At the FABTECH trade show in November 2015, Otto Bihler Maschinenfabrik presented its new BIMERIC servo production and assembly system. The BIMERIC is designed to be a platform for flexible, productive and cost-efficient sub-assembly production for stamping and forming as well as assembly technology.

A new £500,000 deal to supply a second high-speed precision stamping press to Harwin (a UK electronic component specialist) was the highlight of Bruderer UK's appearance at MACH 2016. The new BSTA 280-75 B2 press will feature Bruderer BSV-75 high-speed servo feeder technology and an integrated planetary gearbox. Bruderer UK unveiled the BSTA 410-110 at the MACH exhibition, the first time the high-speed press had been seen in the UK.

Just before the exhibition took place, the manufacturer also announced that The Sertec Group, a manufacturer of stampings and welded assemblies, has tapped into its 'machinery' expertise to identify and purchase 12 presses to help it meet growing volume demands from many of the world's largest car makers. The Tier One supplier has committed more than £10m to the three-year modernisation programme that has seen it start to install a host of ZANI Motion Master presses (with ram velocity slow-down kinematics), ranging from 250 to 800 tonnes.

## An eye on forming

Taiwanese manufacturer Chin Fong's new single-point servo presses (iLS/DLS series) ranging from 80 – 300 tons; its double-point SDS series from 400 up to 1000 tonnes; the compound link LF and KW series for stamping forging composite processes; the long stroke knuckle joint presses KL and warm-hot forging WF series have facilitated its slogan: "Intelligence of Forming, Vision of Forging".

Chin Fong now has offices in Taiwan, China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and the USA as well as representatives in numerous countries around the world. Its A0 tandem press line for the automotive industry includes stages such as deep drawing, following by trimming, piercing etc. The basic A0 line weighs 2400 tons with 800 tons of weight for the rest of the machines. To gain ground in production efficiency, Chin Fong replaced the conventional hydraulic press with a mechanical press featuring a link drive and long stroke.

Chin Fong's straightside double crank power press, with 400-ton capacity, has a bed area of 2.5m x 1m and can produce metal stampings from material measuring up to 400mm wide and 3.5mm thick. It has a variable speed of between 30-50 strokes per minute and a maximum shut height of 450mm.

DEES Hydraulic has successfully developed a closed loop, hydraulic, servo-controlled die spotting press. With microprecision control and energy efficiency at the top of its R&D goals, the servo system's fixed-flow control as the core technology used in the hydraulic spotting press, can further improve the precision of the conventional design or overcome the ballscrew's wear and tear to reduce overall maintenance costs.
