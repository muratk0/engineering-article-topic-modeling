# FAST AND FLEXIBLE

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

## Irish contract manufacturer, Keltech, has been on the front line of medical equipment provision during the COVID-19 pandemic, using RADAN CAD/CAM software to design badly needed products.

A WATERFORD SHEET metal specialist and contract manufacturer used RADAN CAD/ CAM software to prototype and manufacture emergency medical waste bins for the Irish health service in the fight against COVID-19. The company has now developed a hand sanitisation station capable of dispensing 6,000 shots of gel before needing to refill the unit’s four-litre tank.

Keltech has traditionally been a specialist in three main industrial sectors: reservoir, hydraulic and fuel tanks; overhead guards and cabins for equipment (such as forklift trucks) and acoustic enclosures for the power generation industry. It produces complex metal fabrications for customers such as Caterpillar; Combilift; Moffett; JCB; Liebherr etc. – taking a sheet of steel and transforming it into a finished painted product.

Based in the south east of Ireland, Keltech has 150,000 square feet of modern production facilities and serves customers in Ireland, Northern Ireland, the UK, mainland Europe and the United States. With a growing staff of 240 and sales of over €22 million, its customers range from globally recognisable OEM brands to public agencies looking for solutions.

“The continuous improvement of processes, plant and, most importantly, our people is central to achieving our business objectives. The training and development of our employees provides us with a significant competitive edge in the marketplace,” the company told ISMR.

## Medical waste bins

The idea for the medical waste bins came after one of its power generation customers needed to supply both the NHS and the Irish Health Service Executive (HSE) with emergency diesel generators at temporary patient care and COVID-19 testing sites.

“We stayed open to manufacture the acoustic enclosures to reduce the noise of the generators, but that was only taking around 30 per cent of our machining capacity,” said business development manager, Seamus Lawlor.

“We therefore approached the HSE to offer support by manufacturing medical waste bins, as we had the in-house CNC cutting tools, RADAN software to drive them and the necessary expertise to rapidly prototype a product in keeping with the spec. that it required. Its reply was almost instantaneous… saying that bins were in huge demand globally, and that it was having difficulty sourcing them.”

The initial prototype was built in seven days and the first production batch, based on the fourth version, came off the CNC machines three days later. Keltech has now gone on to manufacture around 500 bins per week from two different material specifications, depending upon the particular application. One is a stainless steel version, while the most popular is a mild steel, powder-coated unit.

Each bin comprises around a dozen individual components, with RADAN software creating nests to maximise the yield from each metal sheet and avoid wastage. Keltech’s senior projects engineer, Sean McNamara, confirmed that the company buys a considerable amount of sheet metal and need to utilise every piece.

“RADAN software is excellent at optimising each sheet. We can see exactly where we are with the nests, and how they can be improved if we need to use a larger blank to add more components, or a smaller blank if there are fewer parts,” he told ISMR.

One of Keltech’s greatest challenges was the opening and closing mechanism for the lid, but RADAN’s ability to quickly edit the toolpaths and amend the programs was crucial in speeding up the process.

“For instance, if we needed to change the position of holes we could do that quickly and accurately,” explained McNamara.

Seamus Lawlor added that the footoperated opening system meant that staff do not need to touch the bin lid.

“The lid itself, when closed, is perfectly sealed, ensuring that potentially contaminated waste is secure. It is also designed with a silent close feature so as not to disturb patients. The front opening door allows staff to easily change bags…and we use glossy powder paint which will give a good, easy-toclean finish, so we expect the bins to last for between 10 and 15 years,” he said.

“This was more of a mission to support the urgent COVID-19 requirements of the HSE, rather than looking at the product as a longterm business model, but private companies are now coming to us wanting these bins for places such as nursing homes and other care centres. We’re getting interest from the UK, mainland Europe and the United States.”

## Hand sanitiser units

With RADAN able to program both Keltech’s TRUMPF laser cutting system and punch press, the decision as to which parts are cut on which machine rests largely on profiling criteria, based on the component’s curves.

“It’s difficult to produce curves on the punch, so those parts would be lasered,” added Lawlor.

Keltech has also developing a third product to continue the long-term battle against COVID-19.

“We’ve rapidly prototyped a hand sanitising unit, which we believe is unique because of its large tank. It’s predominantly for high footfall retail areas, and we’ve already had a number of orders for it from major brands,” explained Seamus Lawlor.

Sean McNamara and his team have developed an internal tank, nested and cut using RADAN software, which can hold four litres of sanitising gel, giving the unit one of the largest capacities on the market (able to dispense around 6,000 shots before it needs to be refilled).

“This means low maintenance for retailers, even in a high-usage, high footfall area, and is a complete game-changer from traditional units on the market. Early customer feedback is extremely strong and positive – they say they can now buy sanitiser gel in bulk and the units are so easy to refill. Busy supermarkets with high footfall have only had one refill after a fortnight, and they say that the units ‘look great and are a real addition to their brand’,” concluded Seamus Lawlor.
