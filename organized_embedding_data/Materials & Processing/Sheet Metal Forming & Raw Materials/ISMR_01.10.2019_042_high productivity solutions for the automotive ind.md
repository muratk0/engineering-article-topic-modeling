## Markets and trends

Ceretti sees continuing strong promise for coil processing and metal stamping activities.

"The market for metal stamping looks very promising over the next few years since the decline in steel prices, caused by the oversupply of these metals, is pushing the utilisation of sheet metal, particularly in the automotive industry. Automotive applications, such as engines, transmissions, chassis and exterior panels (these sheet metal parts are all stamped), will drive (together with consumer electronics and industrial machinery) overall revenue share," he told ISMR.

"As far as processes are concerned, embossing will grow widely but it will be the blanking process that will be used even more in the coming years since it is ideal for mass production lines. China and India, due to their continuously growing industrialisation and infrastructure development, will be the players to watch in metal stamping…."

FACCIN believes that the metal industry will attempt to standardise as much as possible, with measures such as Industry-4.0, to maximise the production capacity of equipment, apply energy-saving measures and lobby governments for further tax reforms and incentives to stay competitive and improve workforce development. Asia-Pacific is expected to drive demand in the metalforming market, with countries such as India and China being key players. Markets in Europe, North America and Middle East/ Africa, FACCIN told ISMR, are evolving their infrastructures which is also driving the metalforming market.

When a customer specialising in series production of rolled parts for the automotive Industry tasked FACCIN to develop a special solution to produce thousands of thin and narrow parts per day, difficult to handle and roll, with maximum repeatability and minimum workmanship costs, FACCIN's engineers proposed a tailor-made solution.

"For the rolling machine, the key was to use our HCU two-roll Fast Plate Roll which, using a urethane roll, can roll parts with total pre-bending in one pass and no flat. For the handling, our engineers decided to combine the HCU Fast Plate Roll with a high-tech six axes anthropomorphic robot, a solution appreciated by the customer for its versatility and automation," FACCIN told ISMR.

"The integration was not a problem because of the latest electronic technology on the HCU machines and the result has been better than expected: high productivity, no operator needed and high production quality proven by an extremely high OEE percentage on a machine working 24/7," it concluded.

The company's consolidation initiatives include the opening of a new office in Germany (FACCIN Deutschland GmbH), with a showroom and stock for its customers. The Group has also opened new offices, a showroom and a warehouse in the United States to spearhead further growth in the North American market. Industry 4.0 has been a central pillar which FACCIN has embraced, quick to see its productivity potential for customers.

"Industry 4.0 has become more and more prevalent in the industry and is crucial for any manufacturer that wants to bring high-quality machines, advanced connectivity, analytics and people together to change day-to-day operations for powerful business outcomes. As a group, we see Industry 4.0 as a way to capitalise on the technology that we already provide. Our machines are Industry 4.0-ready, featuring smart packages that offer, for example, system diagnosis; teleservice; management control; drawing import; rolling and production batch statistics and flexible network solutions. ## FOCUS ON COIL PROCESSING

Produtech's CEO, Fabrizio Mologni, gives ISMR his view of coil processing market trends, challenges, opportunities and new technologies.

Produtech designs and manufactures innovative coil-fed systems, protected by international patents, for sheet metal processing. These include coil-fed punching/ forming systems; coil-fed fibre laser lines; automatic lines for copper bar processing and a new coil-fed combined line (high-speed fibre laser cutting and punching/forming using mechanical tools).

Produtech offers a wide range of machines to automatically produce sheetmetal parts in different materials (carbonsteel, stainless steel, aluminium, special steels) and thicknesses (from 0.3mm up to 6mm). Complete coil-fed lines which, using accessory systems, automatically load the coil and process it using various advanced technologies. These lines are designed to offer time, space and raw material savings to users, increasing users' flexibility and product quality.

ISMR spoke with Produtech's CEO, Fabrizio Mologni, to gauge his view of coilprocessing markets, trends, challenges and new technologies.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes/achievements/awards and the reasons for this?

FM: We have recently seen a strong increase in our sales volume, both in Italy and overseas (especially in Germany, USA and Australia). I put this mainly down to our portfolio of innovative coil-fed fibre laser lines. These lines are equipped with the most advanced technologies and offer a significant reduction in maintenance and machine setting operations, using linear and torque motors that reduce mechanical transmissions. They also offer substantial labour and raw material savings by automating work cycles, by production optimisation through continuous coil-sheet processing. Using coil, rather than metal sheet, also leads to substantial reductions in production space, as well as a reduction in cost of the raw material supplied in coils. Substituting metal sheet with coil sheet significantly saves space on the shop floor and reduces raw material costs (as coil is more economical).

In addition to our long-standing coil-fed fibre laser lines and punching lines, a strong contribution to our success is a new coil-fed combined line that joins fibre laser cutting with punching and forming technology, using mechanical tools. This project, protected by an international patent and called EffiCOIL, was made possible by the contribution of the European Community as part of Horizon 2020, certifying the project's excellence. It is now receiving a lot of attention and interest from component manufacturers who require complex work processes from a single coil-fed machine with no handling required or tooling changes needed.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals?

FM: The overall economic situation is a matter of some concern for the future, due to areas of economic conflict (such as the U.S.-China trade tariffs). Some European countries, Germany in particular, are also registering alarming slowdowns in their economies.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

FM: Our customers, who work in the sheetmetal processing sector, ask for flexibility (as large batches are less popular now) and 'Just in Time' production – they also want technological innovations to make their products competitive. We therefore significantly invest every year into research and development, testing new solutions to fulfil the production requirements needed by potential customers.

Our aim is to design customised machines and systems that meet the customers' specific production requirements and allow them to carry out complex production in a simple, economical and high-quality way. It's a tremendous challenge that requires constant commitment and continuous research on new solutions, but it helps us be a leading manufacturer of coilsheet processing machinery.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus/ vision over the next few years?

FM: I believe that the growth we have achieved in recent years will continue and we will morph our company structure and organisational management to handle the technological challenges that we face.

We intend to boost our share in markets where we are already present; unlock new markets where we are not yet operating, and structure and strengthen our customer service offerings to satisfy our customers' requirements. We also aim to increase our product range to cover the entire sheet processing cycle, starting from coil with no additional processes needed to cut sheet blanks from coil with required packaging, handling and loading operations on the line.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in global coil processing markets?

FM: Coil processing undoubtedly has advantages that have not been adequately exploited up to now, because of the habit of processing material in sheets which led companies to work in this way.

Technologically advanced systems, such as those that we offer, have a lot to offer.

The development of new equipment to complete an already wide range of coil-fed processing machinery can make the coil production process even more advantageous, expanding further the potential market for coilfed systems.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

FM: Investing resources into technological research and development; being present in potential geographical market areas of interest (through trade fairs and marketing initiatives); collaborating with local agents on sales and technical service support and making sure that we keep up with our competitors in the most technologically developed countries — these are key challenges that we face to reach our objectives.

ISMR: Are there any products or technologies that you have released or are releasing this year that you would like to discuss?

FM: Our flagship product, which we will also present at the Blechexpo trade fair in Stuttgart this November, is the “EffiCOIL" coil-fed combined line, that enables users to carry out fibre laser cutting with punching and forming, using mechanical tools. It is a coil-fed line which automatically produces parts that need complex work processes without the need for manual intervention by machine operators. You can find us in Hall 9 (stand 9201) at Blechexpo 2019.

This is a new line, covered by an international patent and developed with the participation of the European Community. It has evaluated the project as 'innovative' and released a certificate of excellence for Produtech's work in this project.

ISMR: Thank you for your time.
