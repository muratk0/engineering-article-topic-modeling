We highlight a selection of the latest global stamping innovations and processes

In 1769, John Pickering (a London toymaker) patented his machine for stamping articles out of sheet metal. It was a simple transition for Richard Ford, a Birmingham manufacturer, to employ shaped dies which enabled pots, pans, dishes and other articles to be manufactured from metal sheet in bulk quantities. Stamped components today are widely used in markets such as automotive; telecommunications; consumer electronics; aerospace and defence, and electronics.

The current global metal stamping market is set to grow at a CAGR of 3.02 per cent over the period 2014-2019, according to a recent report unveiled by Research & Markets. According to the report, the demand for stamped metals from the automotive industry has increased significantly over the years. Stamped metals are used in several car components, which include fenders; bonnets; deck lids; side panels; roofs; floor pans; exhaust systems; brake shoes; trim pieces; brackets; valves and hangers. The increased demand for such components has contributed significantly to the growth of the market.

Modern stamping and forming technologies offer manufacturers more sophisticated and capable options in the battle to form ever lighter, stronger and more flexible parts. In this update, we highlight a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing developments.

## Sheet metal innovations

Japanese press specialist, AIDA, is constantly investing in products, technology, manufacturing and service facilities to meet its customers’ changing production requirements. It pioneered servo press technology by independently developing and manufacturing its proprietary compact low-speed high-torque servo motors, suitable for press metalforming applications. AIDA was the first company in the world to develop and market direct-drive servo presses, delivering to date over 1000 servo presses worldwide.

The company is a specialist in the design, manufacture, sale, service and support, refurbishment and modernisation of metal stamping presses and metalforming automation equipment. It has a worldwide network of sales and service locations in 36 cities across 18 countries, and press installations in over 60 countries worldwide. AIDA Direct Drive Servo Former (DSF) presses are AIDA’s new forming systems.

AP&T designs, manufactures and markets automation, presses, tools and complete production lines for the sheet metal and tube forming industry. It specialises in forming technology and automated processes, delivering complete production systems where it assumes complete supplier responsibility – a concept which it calls One Responsible Partner.

More stringent requirements on reduced CO emissions have opened up the market for the production of body parts made from various aluminium alloys. AP&T will be at AEE, Automotive Engineering Expo, in Nürnberg (9-10 June 2015) where Dr. Christian Koroschetz, from AP&T’s research and development department, will speak on the topic of how advanced hot forming of aluminium can be done profitably and energy-efficiently in a complete production line for industrial series production.

Press hardening technology experienced its major breakthrough ten years ago. AP&T produced functioning solutions early on, and thus got a head start when the technology made a commercial breakthrough. AP&T has now established itself as a leading supplier of complete press hardening lines to both international players with production in China and Chinese producers that focus on the domestic market.

The company is also introducing new stop/start energy-saving technology into its hydraulic presses. The “Frequency Drive System” is similar to the start/stop function found in modern vehicles.

Avure Technologies, a leading Swedish manufacturer of ultra-high pressure systems, recently established an Application Centre to assist fabricators utilising advanced materials in the development of hot and cold isostatic pressing applications. The facility, next to the Avure manufacturing plant, is equipped with two Hot Isostatic Presses (HIPs) and one Cold Isostatic Press (CIP).
