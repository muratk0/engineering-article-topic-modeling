# We outline a selection of new and innovative waterjet cutting technologies for sheet metal manufacturers.

[TARİH: 01.11.2020 ISMR]

Waterjet cutting systems are extremely popular in a variety of industrial applications and can cut most types of material. They are used in industries ranging from food processing to steel production. There are two main types of cutters – standard waterjet cutters (water only) and abrasive waterjet cutters (using water with a mixed-in abrasive).

With minimal material waste, low water consumption and total flexibility, the waterjet offers an environmentally-friendly solution for profiling material. There are no chemically contaminated pieces, it generates no noxious fumes during the cutting process and requires no cooling or lubricating oils. The introduction of five-axis cutting heads has triggered massive growth in waterjet cutting systems. No longer confined to cutting out flat objects, waterjets are now able to produce continuous bevels, revolve around the workpiece and cut at angles from 0-90 degrees.

“More often than not, a waterjet machine will create a precise cut that requires no machining or additional finishing treatments to complete the job. In addition, there is no cutting dust to be cleaned or ‘burnt edges’ to polish. The technology has minimal kerfs – the amount of material along the vector line that is removed during the cutting process, which is usually 0.030” to 0.040” (0.762 to 1.016mm), resulting in less waste of product and materials. This is much more efficient as cutting guides can be closer together and more products can be produced per piece of material,” said Bradley McBain, MD, MBA Engineering.

Below is a selection of the latest new and innovative waterjet technologies for sheet metal manufacturers, in alphabetical order.

Latest technology developments

Following the recent introduction of a new corporate identity, AAG has added a five-axis cutting head to its WARDJet range of waterjet cutting systems. Key attributes of the Apex-60 include the ability to cut at angles of up to 60 degrees using a cutting force of 39N and at a maximum speed of 50.8 metres/minute, along with a Z-travel capability of 304.8mm.

The ALLFI Quantum 4550 waterjet pump is designed to be up to 60% more energy efficient than standard hydraulic boosters. This “green” waterjet pump requires up to 70% less cooling water and less electricity and oil, according to the manufacturer.

ANT’s Water Abrasive Suspension (WAS) cutting system consists of ConSus, a cutting device and a pump. Optionally, an abrasive recycling unit (ARU) and a water treatment system can be integrated.

“At less than half the pressure, the ANT suspension jet cuts twice to three times faster than conventional waterjet cutting methods. Since the abrasive particles in WAS systems are bound directly in the water without air, the suspension jet is much more stable, precise and powerful than the injection jet,” ANT told ISMR.

Biesse Group’s Intermac Primus series of waterjet cutting machines process a wide range of ferrous and non-ferrous materials, including non-metallic alloys, titanium, aluminium, plastics and composites.

BTG GmBH has showcased two highpressure pumps in its ECOTRON range – the 40.37+ and the 40.45+.

Luxembourg manufacturer CERATIZIT has produced its PREMIUM+ FOCUSING TUBES for demanding waterjet cutting applications. Optimised wear protection ensures longer tool life (which supports improved machine utilisation). Improved material quality and processing offer up to a 30% improvement in performance, according to the manufacturer.

Eckert’s Opal Waterjet Combo is a patented, rapid CNC waterjet for plasma and waterjet cutting. Equipped with the Pro-X 3D waterjet cutting head, it combines the advantages of ultra-fast plasma cutting and precision waterjet cutting. Eckert cites a reduction of production costs ‘of up to 70%’ in comparison to standard waterjet machines. It also offers a stand-alone Opal Waterjet with 2D and 3D cutting heads.
