# THE WATER MARGIN

[TARİH: 01.05.2019 ISMR]

What can waterjet cutting technology offer sheet metal manufacturers? Bradley McBain outlines the benefits of the process.

## By Bradley McBain, Managing Director, MBA Engineering

Waterjet technology is currently one of the most advanced cutting methods in the market, thanks to its ability to cut practically any material. This ranges from soft substances (such as gel, food and sponge) to some of the hardest materials to cut in the industry (stainless steel, titanium, granite, marble and ceramic at up to 200mm thicknesses) which is almost impossible for many alternative cutting technologies on the market. Such a wide range of abilities can be attractive to a client, offering them reassurance that the technology can deal with any materials and projects that may come their way.

When it comes to composite materials, manufacturers can also enjoy peace of mind. The finely focused jet of water cuts reliably and precisely in any direction with incredible detail, including through materials made of several elements such as fibreglass and carbon fibre reinforced plastic (CFRP).

To cut such materials, this technology uses surprisingly little water; a typical amount is half a gallon per minute, depending upon the size of the orifice. The water offers an instant cooling element whilst cutting so there is an insignificant heat-affected zone. Consequently, materials such as plastics can be cut accurately without warping or melting, which may not be the case with other methods that rely on a heat element to cut. Such methods can also put added stress on a product, resulting in harsh edges and, in some cases, distortion of the material – an issue a manufacturer should not encounter with a waterjet machine.

## Process benefits

On occasion, materials such as glass, stone and ceramic can splinter during the initial piercing process. Waterjet machine manufacturers, such as Kimla, incorporate a low-pressure piercing option to reduce the jet impact force. This provides a more economical process as there is no wastage from chips and splintering, while reducing the chances of potential impact damage.

More often than not, a waterjet machine will create a precise cut that requires no machining or additional finishing treatments to complete the job. In addition, there is no cutting dust to be cleaned or 'burnt' edges to polish. The technology has minimal kerfs – the amount of material along the vector line that is removed during the cutting process, which is usually 0.030" to 0.040" (0.762 to 1.016mm), resulting in less waste of product and materials. This is much more efficient as cutting guides can be closer together and more products can be produced per piece of material.

With minimal material waste, low water consumption and total flexibility, the waterjet offers an environmentally-friendly solution for profiling material. There are no chemically contaminated pieces to dispose of either and, unlike thermal processes such as plasma or laser, waterjet generates no noxious fumes during the cutting process and requires no cooling or lubricating oils.

A common misconception with these machines is the time that they take to perform. However, the accelerations and operating speeds of waterjet cutting are constantly developing. The cutting time of these machines has reduced radically, and it is now more common to find that the limit in cutting speeds is because of restrictions in control system capabilities so finding a technologically advanced company with the latest control systems is imperative.

Overall, waterjet machines offer an extremely precise, innovative solution to a manufacturer's cutting needs. The machines are easy to maintain with no need to shut down work to sharpen or clean blades – this means more time cutting and extra capacity to take on customers in the long run.
