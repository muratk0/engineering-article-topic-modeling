# Italian manufacturer STAM has designed and built “the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses up to 7mm”

[TARİH: 01.11.2013 ISMR]

“IN 1963 when we started trading, roll forming technology could only produce the profile of a given section using a set of dedicated forming rolls. Only through manual set-up, which was a rather complex operation, was it possible to modify the geometry of the profile,” Italian manufacturer, STAM, told ISMR.

At that time, in the automotive industry, roll forming was hardly used at all. Truck beams, for example (consisting of U-profiles of various sections with thicknesses varying from 4 to 12mm) were produced from enormous presses and press tooling dedicated to individual profiles. To change from one model to another, unwieldy press tooling needed to be changed.

In 1989, when Volvo Trucks built its new plant in Tuve (Gothenburg), it decided to introduce a flexible, self-configuring roll forming system for U-beam production and STAM won the tender for the flexible roll forming machine. At that time, STAM had already successfully manufactured and sold some flexible roll forming systems for U and C profiles to the construction industry.

This marked a turning point in flexible roll forming operations. By adjusting the position of the forming rolls, when the machine tool is in stop mode, it was possible to automatically modify the section of the profile (width, height and thickness) which remains constant over the total length of the piece. Over the years, all the major truck manufacturers have adopted this technology from STAM because of the benefits it offers in terms of flexibility and rapidity of change-over as well as cost reduction.

Today, STAM employs 130 people and has additional facilities in Spain and Brazil. More than 1000 machines and lines have been delivered and installed. At STAM, every stage of the machine’s production is managed in-house including mechanical, electrical and software design, building (i.e. fabricating, machining, assembling and painting) and mechanical, electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic testing.

## Facing the challenge

In 2007, STAM (along with Spanish company, Valle Perfiladoras, which belongs to the STAM Group) faced a new technological challenge from the automotive sector. For many years, the roll forming industry had been investigating the possibility of roll forming a product of variable section i.e roll forming a product that, in length, would also change its geometrical cross section For example, for a U-shaped profile, the product would change its web along the profile.

Meetings were held at a European level on this issue and several manufacturers ventured into prototype production, hoping to reach a product with the required levels of quality needed for the final application.

STAM SpA moved from theory to action by manufacturing and putting into operation the first roll forming line for variable section beams and U-profiles used in the mass production of light commercial vehicles. The line was totally innovative as each roll forming stand moves cross-wise and rotates through numerical controls, as the strip moves forward in the line, to follow the curvilinear shape of the profile which is in production.

## Making the breakthrough

In 2013, STAM reached a new technological frontier. It designed and built the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses of up to 7mm. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength. The success of the project, followed by the positive testing of the line, was the most rewarding way to celebrate 50 years of STAM activities.

After seeing the success of the machine producing U-profiles for light trucks, automotive customers began to request a machine capable of producing U or other large thickness sections (up to 7mm) for heavy vehicles. The variable section beams help manufacturers to improve the structure of the vehicle’s chassis by lowering the height of the web at one or both ends of the beam.

Most of these products are manufactured today using large presses or welding centres which bend or weld pieces together to produce the beam of the desired section. In addition to the enormous manpower and space needed for production, the work pieces obtained from these pressing and welding operations show deformations that need to be corrected in subsequent straightening operations.

“Producing these special beams, in lengths of up to 15m, using the automatic and continuous roll forming method is therefore an enormous advantage in terms of flexibility and number of personnel employed, and consequently in quality and cost of the final product,” said STAM.

“The STAM machine provides roll forming stands which can withstand not just the stresses from the bending action of the strip but also the tension and the loads generated by the displacement of the roll forming stands during roll forming, when the material is between the forming rolls.”

The system must also be able to accommodate the shape of the incoming strip and reach the required roll forming speed. The faster the speed of roll forming operations, the quicker are the transversal and rotary movements of the roll forming stands to follow the curved shape of the flanges being formed.

A further important aspect is the guidance system used for the input material into the roll forming machine and throughout the roll forming process. The traditional guide system must simply guide and maintain in position a strip of constant width from the beginning to the end of the process while, in variable section roll forming, the strip width varies continuously and the guides must carefully follow the strip shape. A machine of such mechanical complexity requires a high-level electrical and electronic control system.
