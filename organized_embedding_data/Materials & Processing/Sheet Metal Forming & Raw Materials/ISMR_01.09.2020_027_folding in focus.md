# FOLDING IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

Swiss sheet metal folding specialist, Thalmann Maschinenbau AG, believes that innovations which increase automation and reduce costs for its customers benefit the sheet metal processing industry in general. It recently launched two new developments designed to automate the long folding process and reduce usage costs. Thalmann aims to drive increased speed, reliability and safety in the bending process; increased dimensional scope of folded parts and an expansion of applications and markets for bent sheet metal.

Its managing director, Marco Cappello, outlined Thalmann’s latest new initiatives to ISMR.

## New initiatives and technologies

The company has been putting significant resources behind two new developments in long folding that aim to change the way that sheet metal processors operate their double folders. The prime benefit for sheet metal processors, it told ISMR, will be a significant reduction in manual labour and part manipulation using Thalmann TD and TC Double folders.

The first development is a sheet metal loading unit which feeds sheets into the double folder. With the integrated sheet metal feed-in device, sheet metal is fed automatically, rapidly and flexibly into the machine. This can be done as a single or multiple sheet feed, from a stack of sheets up to 100mm high, or with material from a slitting and cut-to-length line.

Depending upon the length of the machine, the loading device can load several sheets onto the machine e.g. 4 x 2m sheets on an 8m machine, and these can subsequently be folded simultaneously. This unit is an option that can be added onto Thalmann TD and TC machines.

“Customer and market feedback have been overwhelmingly positive,” Marco Cappello told ISMR. “Since its launch, in the first quarter of 2020, almost all our sold machines have incorporated this feature.”

The second development is a fully automated flipping device which turns the already folded part without any manual interference. The flipping operation is done by suction cups mounted on the upper folding beam in combination with the extendable folding table.

The diagram above is Thalmann’s visualisation of activities in the folding process. Feeding and flipping of sheet metal is fully automated and manual activity is limited to loading sheets to a feeding table and unloading folded parts from the machine.

Both developments aim to reduce the manual operation on a Thalmann TD and TC long folder, reducing cost and improving safety.

“Thalmann’s development strategy has been focused on three core benefits that our customers tell us they need - efficiency improvements or reduced costs in operation; accurate bends or increased dimensional freedoms and, finally, safe operation. These recent new developments meet the first and third need of most of our customers,” added Marco Cappello.

## Business conditions in 2020/2021

“In the second half of the year, we will launch an additional development that further advances automation of the folding operation. The development will automatically take parts from a machine and convey them to a point where they can be collected for packing and/ or for dispatch,” he explained.

Despite the challenges facing the global economy, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, Thalmann remains positive.

“The major economies will support the recovery of industries with favourable conditions for investments and subsidies. Societies and economies, he added, might have to drastically change. For example, in Europe, investments in green energy (the ‘green deal’), supported by the European Union, will open up opportunities for the use of folded sheet metal for solar panels, he believes. The construction industry will also focus more on energy-efficient solutions.

“The use of folded sheet metal for architectural applications will definitely play a part in this transition,” he explained.

Looking back at the challenging first half of 2020, Cappello believes that Thalmann has been very fortunate. Before the COVID-19 pandemic, Thalmann’s challenge was to meet shorter delivery times for machines. The factory entered the pandemic era with an order book which exceeded nine months production at full capacity. A limited number of orders were postponed and, as a result, manufacturing remained at full capacity but lead times have come down to more acceptable levels. Although the sales rate of new machines declined during the first months of the pandemic, Thalmann now sees sales picking up again. The launch of these new technology developments helped the company to develop new customers and markets.

“We are very proud that we did not have to apply for reduced work-time government support and that employment has been maintained. The management has been implementing strict rules for hygiene and social distancing in the factory and office. Employees have been very supportive and comply with these measures during worktime to contain the spread of the virus. This resulted in zero COVID infections in the factory, as well as in the families of the employees,” said Marco Cappello.

However, we expect some companies might have to further reduce costs and that their products will have to become more competitive,” Marco Cappello told ISMR.

“Given the economic uncertainty, due to COVID-19, we also believe that customers who further reduce their operational costs to increase their competitiveness will eventually win in the market. Investment in a Thalmann folder is significant but the reduction in labour costs and improved efficiencies guarantee a good return on investment and increased competitiveness for years to come. That is why we are focusing our R&D efforts on further automation of the folding operation.”

## Strategic and technical focus

Thalmann Maschinenbau AG has a clear strategy, based on three pillars:

1. Focusing on the innovations that its customers need and request e.g. automation and operational cost reductions.

2. Unlocking markets that traditionally use different technologies for folding sheet metal. It has already started initiatives to expand in selected markets in the Asian region.

3. Further optimising the supply chain and processes to manufacture machines in a way that safeguards high quality standards at an acceptable cost base.

The Swiss manufacturer’s technical focus is on automation and Industry 4.0 as well as on automation leading to cost reductions in folding operations and the reduction of manual part manipulation. The future of the manufacturing industry, it believes, is to further integrate supply chain processes. For the bending operation, this means automation of the folding process to minimize human error, improve reproducibility and reduce levels of waste production to meet the demands of process integration.

“We believe that customers want costeffective, efficient, accurate and flexible bending operations and more dimensional freedom as well as safe operation for machinery users. All our technical focus is designed to meet these demands through automation of the folding process,” explained Marco Cappello.

“Customers want to be able to develop new products and enter new markets. We are in continuous dialogue with customers all over the world to explain how our long folding technology can help them to expand their business.”

Cappello observed that a range of customers and markets which are traditionally using press brake technology could move to long folding. These customers, he told ISMR, are looking for safer operations that can be done by one (instead of two) operators and an operation that improves reproducibility. Its technical strategic focus supports the transition from press brakes to long folding.

“Depending upon the region, we see that customers are prepared to invest in folding machines that a) deliver parts safely; b) consistently deliver accurate parts with the smallest possible tolerances; c) deliver parts at the lowest operational cost and d) deliver parts using a machine that has the highest uptime and low levels of maintenance,” he explained.

## Challenges and opportunities

Thalmann sees a clear opportunity to unlock under-developed markets for folding technology. Its strategy is to be a first mover in such markets, either from a geographic or an application perspective.

“With major success in European, American, Australasian and some selected Asian markets, our greatest challenge and opportunity is now to further introduce Thalmann technology into markets where long folding has not yet been established. These markets are predominantly in Asia,” Cappello concluded.

Last year, Thalmann started to develop strong business alliances in Korea that have already resulted in new business. In Japan, its first successful steps have been taken to establish strong partnerships that will support the introduction of Thalmann technology to a market not yet accustomed to long folding.

“We expect that architects and product designers will gradually discover and better understand the possibilities offered by folding technology to bend sheet metal cost-effectively, producing a wider scope of dimensions than ever before.

Marco Cappello concluded: “With the substantial challenges and opportunities that Thalmann is addressing, we will remain committed to innovative solutions that reduce costs and improve the safety and accuracy of the folding process. Further automation will be the key focus of our development to deliver these customer benefits.”
