# INTEGRATEDOPERATIONS

[TARİH: 01.08.2015 ISMR]

Heinrich Weber, Director of Sales, Dreistern

A new generation of production technology, dubbed Rollforming 4.0, has the potential to save significant operational time and cost.

Weight and cost savings are some of the most important development targets in the automotive industry. Rollforming can efficiently support both these targets. It has demonstrated its capability to process Ultra-High-Strength Steels (UHSS) with a tensile strength up to 1,600 MPa. Such steels already contribute to the considerably reduced weight of today´s vehicles.

Automotive experts have calculated further weight reduction potential by extended use of UHSS of up to 30% [1]. While some materials, such as aluminium, magnesium or carbon fibre reinforced plastics have a substantially better strength-to-weight ratio, any weight reduction from using these materials can attract a much higher cost.

The majority of automotive body parts are produced by cold stamping on presses. The maximum tensile strength which can be processed economically using cold stamping is between 800 – 1000 MPa. Higher tensile strength of up to 1,600 MPa can be achieved by using hot stamping (or press hardening) processes [2]. Compared to rollforming, this offers almost unlimited 3D forming capability. On the other hand, the higher process cost of hot stamping results in a cost differential of approximately US$ 2 for a typically rollformed part such as a rocker.

## Specialised forming technologies for processing UHSS

Today rockers, bumpers, door beams, crash tubes and many other Ultra-High Strength Steel (UHSS) parts are manufactured economically on rollforming systems. The strength of the material itself does not represent a challenge to a rollforming machine as long as the machine has been designed to cater for the higher forming forces.

Possible wear of the rollforming tools is also not really an issue. After producing several million parts in Ultra-High Strength Steel, a rolltool does not show any significant wear. The reason is simple; there is almost no differential speed between the forming rollers and the material being formed. This is not the case with press tooling. Press tool wear is a critical issue and can require massive counteractive measures such as sophisticated surface coatings and excessive lubrication. The real challenge of Ultra-High Strength Steel for the rollforming process is the enormous range of mechanical property fluctuations and, often, very poor flatness levels.

Dreistern has developed new forming technologies to cope with the challenging properties of such steels without compromising tolerance demands. These technologies include a few important elements. One is called “controlled bending” by Dreistern tool designers. It means that the sheet metal is always guided by rollers from both sides during the entire forming process. Another can be found at defined adjustment points. Sometimes, the excessive difference in material properties between one coil and another can induce a tolerance deviation of one or more bending angles. Such an occurrence requires an adjustment of the rolltool. It is essential that tool adjustment is done quickly and efficiently at defined adjustment points.

In this way unacceptable quality deviations, caused by changing material properties, can be efficiently countermanded. In addition, the end flare, which is typical for conventional rollforming, is eliminated. Numeric simulation of the forming process and comparison with real measured results has played a key role during development. As a result, a rocker made from Arcelor Mittal martensitic steel MW 1500 can be produced, on a daily basis, with a tolerance range of ± 0.5mm in all dimensions.

## Cost-saving concepts for in-line piercing and embossing operations

In most cases, automotive components feature pierced or embossed areas. Consequently, almost every automotive rollforming installation contains pre- or postpunching equipment. Since such equipment can substantially increase investment and part cost, it is worth considering cost-saving alternatives to existing solutions.

Dreistern has developed a series of costeffective compact hydraulic punches in the 200 – 2000 kN range. They can be equipped with high speed hydraulic cylinders for up to 120 strokes per minute. If a specific part design requires a higher press force, two or more of these presses can be installed in the line one after the other. Sometimes, a punch pattern is made up of a constant and variable portion. In that case, only the tool for the variable portion would be exchanged. Tool change-over times and tool costs can therefore be reduced. This would not be possible using just one large punching die.

Automotive parts frequently have a right- and a left-hand version. While the cross-section remains the same, the punch pattern is reversed left to right and requires two different punching dies which have to be exchanged. Since rollforming lines, in most cases, work with a continuous metal strip, this means cutting off the strip and laterally removing the punching die. Consequently, scrap is produced and production is stopped which leads to increased time and costs.

Dreistern engineers found a simple solution to eliminate this scenario. Instead of exchanging the punching dies in a lateral direction, the second die is in a waiting position in front of or after the press (in a longitudinal direction). Once the change has to be made, the dies are simply exchanged by pushing them into or out of the press without the need to cut off the metal strip. Scrap can therefore be avoided and the change-over is effected more rapidly.

This is not the only interesting feature of this punching press concept. The press can control up to 21 individual punches, which can be activated at full production speed. This allows for highly flexible punch patterns. If even higher flexibility is required, the press can be transformed quickly into a CNC (plotter type) press by inserting a special CNC punching insert. This is especially useful for highly flexible, low volume production in small batches. Several lines with such presses are presently assembled on the Dreistern shop floor in Germany.

## Ultra-High Strength bumper with variable sweep radius

The integration of bending operations into rollforming lines offers two main advantages. It simplifies the process for parts with a sweep. Off-line bending operations require straight ends which have to be cut off in case the part design requires a sweep at the end. This scrap can be avoided by rollforming.

Bumpers and other automotive components, such as B-pillar covers with a constant sweep radius, have been produced on rollforming machines for many years. Today´s front module design often requires at least two different radii – a large one in the middle of 2,000 – 3,000mm and a smaller one on both ends of 400 – 600mm. Even these small radii could not be produced with the required quality levels up until now because of the wrinkles and waves produced in the compressed areas of the metal sheet.

Dreistern engineers have therefore demonstrated a newly developed in-line bending process, supporting the feasibility of producing bumpers in perfect shape without wrinkles and waves. The lower cost of this process makes it an attractive alternative to stretch bending.

## Multifunctional rollforming technology

Another technology from Dreistern – Multifunctional Rollforming Technology – is considerably enhancing the production flexibibility of rollforming. It is based on standardised work modules for punching, bending or welding that can be integrated quickly at any point on the rollforming machine. This capability is transforming the rollformer into a highly flexible sheet metal processing centre.

Each work module is provided with an independent and mobile control unit, which allows the work module to be used on different machines. This capability is especially useful if specific work modules cannot be sufficiently utilised on just one line. The exchange of one work module takes approximately 10 minutes. The rollforming line can therefore be adapted to different process requirements more rapidly and easily. Consequently, multifunctional rollforming lines are capable of economically producing completely different parts such as rockers, open and welded bumpers or crash tubes.

## New technologies for customised mass production

For many years, rollforming line design has not purely been based on just the rollforming process but also a number of auxiliary processes such as piercing, embossing, welding or even assembling and packaging operations.

In the past, such lines were dedicated to one or a small number of specific products. Nowadays, rollforming lines are increasingly producing highly variable parts on a “made-toorder” basis for various applications. For example, they are producing parts for kitchen drawers in all dimensions and ready for assembly “just-in sequence” to the assembly lines of kitchen manufacturers requiring a product change for the rollforming line several times per day.

The same applies to producers of high bay warehouses. They have to produce all the necessary components of an individual warehouse in the shortest possible time. The designs of all these parts are very different. But they all rely on rollforming’s ability to rapidly adapt to different part geometries without the need for costly and time-consuming tool changes. The control systems of these machines are linked to Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) systems to process the next production order and report present status.

It is the beginning of a new generation of production technology, which we call Rollforming 4.0. It also has the potential to significantly contribute to some of the challenging requirements laid down by the automotive industry for customized mass production. ■
