# A FLEXIBLE FRIEND

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“We outline a selection of storage and handling options for sheet metal stock”

Modern sheet metal processing calls for flexible storage, stacking and handling systems with safety in mind

A Tecnomagnete MaxX manually controlled lifter handling metal plate

Sheet metal stock should always be stored on a stable, clean, dry, flat and level surface. Stored stock can be either freestanding or supported (stock is restrained or supported by racks or other permanent or semi-permanent storage equipment – including frames or stillages).

The choice of storage system will depend upon many factors, including type and stability of stock; the handling system to be used; anticipated stock quantities; frequency of movements and the picking requirements of the user.

Stock is handled during vehicle loading, delivery and unloading as well as processing/manufacture. Handling involves both lifting and transporting operations. A wide range of mechanised lifting and handling equipment is available to transport stock. These include cranes, C-hooks, forklifts, vacuum lifting systems, clamps and magnets. Safety is a key consideration for handling, storage and stacking of sheet metal stock.

## Racking and stacking

For long, narrow products, various racking systems can be used. Cantilever racking (single- or double-sided) feature vertical struts and horizontal arms which hold the stored material. Toast racking consists of substantial uprights (supported by a frame) with bundles of material stacked between them. A-frame racks feature an A-shaped upright frame fitted with arms similar to a cantilever rack. Products are stored either horizontally across the arms or upright between the arms, when they are known as leaning racks. For horizontal storage, they can be loaded and unloaded by overhead cranes.

Ladder racks (similar to toast racks but with detachable horizontal members supported by vertical metal uprights) as well as pigeon hole racks, U-frame racks, cradles, stillages and splitting racks can also be used for storage of long, narrow products.

Coils are generally either narrow (where the diameter-to-width ratio is over 6.5:1) or broad.

The physical properties and requirements for safe storage and handling are very different for the two coil types, according to UK HSE advice.

Coils with small widths and large diameters are unstable if left free-standing with bore horizontal. Narrow coils should therefore be either banded together to form a more stable composite coil or stored in a leaning coil rack. Where the ratio is greater than 10:1, coils should be stored bore-vertical or in a fully enclosed cassette. Narrow coils should not be stored bore-horizontal unless they are adequately supported by fixed supports. If these supports are not available, coils should be stored in the bore-vertical configuration.

For floor storage, coils should be safely laid flat and formed into a stack. Pallets can be used to support such stacks. Battens should be placed between the coils to permit access for safe handling. Cages may also be used for floor storage – these portable units are fixed to the floor with openings on the side or top and are used for safe transportation of narrow coil. Cantilevered arms can also provide temporary storage for narrow coils. Leaning coil racks, horizontal supports and standing coil racks can also be used for narrow coil storage.

Broad coils can be stored single-level or stacked (with suitable chocking of the base layer to prevent movement). Single-level rows of coils always exert lower forces on the chocks than stacks, so stacking should be avoided wherever possible.

Coil wells (floor depressions), cradles and standing coil racks are also used for general coil storage.

Plates and sheets are most stable when laid flat. When stacked on edge, they should

always be adequately supported/restrained in suitable racking which prevents them from falling over and enables safe retrieval of individual sheets. Battens are used for horizontal plate support and separation, and the gaps between sheets allow access for the use of lifting equipment. Sheet and plate can also be stored on suitable pallets. Care should be taken to avoid material overhanging the pallet edge. Stock should, where possible, be strapped to the pallet to aid safe handling.

Pallet racking can be used for the safe storage of stacked pallets of sheet stock. The stability of these racks can be improved by fixing them to floors and walls. Toast racks can be used for the storage of vertical sheets or plates in compartments which prevent sideways movement and enable the removal of individual sheets.

## Automated sheet metal storage

Automated tower systems can also store flat products safely and securely, presenting a cassette to a work station at the front, rear or sides and on multiple floor levels, even through the roof of the building or installed outside in a sheet-clad framework with loading from vehicles on the outside and unloading inside, optimising and reducing space requirements. The machine design is tailored to suit the product size and operational requirements.

These systems are often connected to machine tools to feed material directly through for processing. Individual machine tool manufacturers, such as Amada, also offer their own dedicated flat sheet storage options connected to, for example, laser cutting systems to enable rapid feeding and emptying of the machines as it cuts the metal. This has a significant impact on overall operational productivity.

“Modern sheet metal processing is unthinkable without a flexible form of sheet metal storage, especially without continuous feeding and emptying of the production machines as they operate. When viewed as a whole, the largest potential for improving productivity lie not with the processing machines themselves, but automated sheet metal storage,” commented sheet metal storage specialist, Stopa.

“Today's production lines contain expensive processing machines that often make round-the-clock operation necessary to be economically viable. As sheet metal storage systems and their guaranteed continuous flow of materials continue to gain importance, the storage system itself becomes more and more a form of logistics centre.” ■
