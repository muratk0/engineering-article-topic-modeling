# Smoothing the path

[TARİH: 01.05.2012 ISMR]

One approach to minimise minor damage during handling and fabrication is to apply a plastic coating to the sheet, before delivery to the equipment manufacturer. This protective film may be removed at a later stage. To some extent, this coating inhibits cutting and welding processes, as laser or plasma profiling of the covered sheets causes edge fusion of the plastic. An extra dressing process is then required to secure a clean edge for welding.

The marginal cost of securing a bright polish on simple fabrications may not be high when bright polished sheets can be used with little forming or welding, hence a surface finish specification may be enhanced for cosmetic effect, or other reasons, at relatively little cost. Chemical treatment may be used to modify the protective passive oxide film to produce textured or coloured surfaces to enhance the decorative appeal of the surface. Certain finishing techniques, such as bobbin polishing that introduce a strong visual pattern on the surface, have the added useful function of distracting the eye from identifying minor blemishes or unevenness in the fabrication.

A specific quality of finish may be specified. The pleasing appearance not only reflects the quality of construction, but is also able to satisfy easiclean and hygienic finish, corrosion and stress resistance, noncontamination and good surface slip properties within a common specification. Specialised surface treatments, such as bonded dry-film lubricants, may be applied to improve lubrication and/or avoid seizing or galling. Other finishes offer an improved surface for soldering or to minimise bimetallic contact corrosion.

## SURFACE FINISHING

Ion implantation and PVD (physical vapour deposition) can be applied to improve surface hardness. Metal sprays are also used to deposit a hard-wearing surface, or sometimes to roughen the surface, to resist wear by preventing surface slip. Austenitic stainless steels cannot be hardened by heat treatment, but are strengthened by cold working and they retain good ductility and toughness even at high strength. Shot peening of the working face is therefore used to harden the surface.

The grinding and polishing of corner welds, to blend in with the complete absence of stressraising conditions, is an essential step to counter the aggravation of stress corrosion attack.

“Our industry is becoming very aware of the defects a layer of laser oxide causes underneath paint,”explains surface finishing specialists, MetalFinish. “Delamination of the coating is one of the unwanted results; Corrosion is another. New findings, however, do not correlate corrosion as much to the oxide layer as to the sharpness of the edge of a punched or laser cut part. Salt spray tests of cleaned parts as well as untreated parts showed almost identical significant corrosion originating from sharp edges where the coating is almost non-existent and thin after 300+ hours. Parts with edge-rounding have better coverage and therefore significantly outperform parts without rounded edges.”

Chrome-free coatings for long-term corrosion protection are under development and Akzo-Nobell, for example, has tested the corrosion performance of its new chrome-free coating technology in a High Solids coating system suitable for aerospace applications. ■
