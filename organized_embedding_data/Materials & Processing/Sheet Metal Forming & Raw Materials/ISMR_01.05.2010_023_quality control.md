Authors: Martin Stuckmann (Erichsen GmbH) and Dr Manfred Sommer (Ball Packaging Europe GmbH).

In the investigation of anisotropy i.e. directionality responsible for the ear forming tendency in sheet metal, the automatic ear scanning instrument can provide significantly more accurate information compared with manual systems i.e. compared with the procedures laid down in ISO 11531. Ball Packaging, a leading beverage can manufacturer, recognised this and has invested in Erichsen equipment.

Ear forming tendency in drawn cups is an important aspect of the deep drawing quality of sheet metal. However, this property is often only measured using primitive manual methods, which are not adequate for a clear result. The lack of clarity may hide significant quality variations, which can have serious consequences.

## Metals Analysis

The department of measurement technology in the engineering office of the company is under the leadership of Dr. Sommer and his duties include specifying measurement technologies for quality assurance. If the necessary equipment or systems are not available from the market place, the company develops what is needed, working in association with relevant specialists.

Analysis of the properties of the metals used is a basic consideration for full control of the performance of beverage cans to meet varying conditions and demands, to suit different canned substances and the requirements of subsequent packaging, transportation and storage procedures.

At its heart are the measurements of geometric, mechanical and surface properties. Only through meticulous control of all relevant parameters can the correct filling volume, mechanical stability and stacking properties be assured. In the event of changes in the initial material, resulting from general developments or in the event of changes in product design, it is particularly important to check all the elements thoroughly.

It is also essential to maintain consistency of the mechanical quality parameters of beverage cans. Using the latest measurement and analysis systems, the geometry (i.e. the wall thickness, the height of the can and neck and flange contours which are all affected by the ear forming tendency) need to be maintained accurately within a fraction of a millimetre.

## The entire complex 3D geometry as well as the formability characteristics of the material are fundamental

Only by having full control over all aspects is it possible to ensure that, when innovations or product improvements are introduced, the quality standard of the beverage cans will be unaltered.

Can production takes place at high speed and very thin material wall thicknesses are involved. The tooling consists of nine or ten individual tool stations arranged side by side, one after the other. This quite complicated process cannot cope with quality variations in the sheet metal used.

Years ago, the ear forming tendency of drawn cups was measured with dial or slide gauges. These manual methods are time consuming and the results tend to vary with the individual laboratory equipment operator. The method does not lend itself to the easy production of an accurate and full record of the ear shapes and sizes. Ball Packaging Europe therefore looked for an automatic precision method that would provide a full and reproducible result.

## Automatic ear scanning

The Ear Scanning Instrument 126 C was developed jointly with MFP. The initial aim of the project was to save material and to investigate material problems that appeared in the first forming station, and the possibility that there was a concentricity problem on the machine on which the cans were manufactured. The quality of the sheet metal (including the ear forming tendency) would play a vital part in avoiding scrap and the resulting financial loss.

The measuring instrument has a turntable, which can be positioned as desired, and a measuring head with vertically arranged, long stroke, high precision, displacement measuring sensors. The drawn cups can be prepared as described for the Earing Test ISO 11531 or any other size within the range specified for the instrument.

During one revolution, the height of the cup rim is scanned and the measurement interpreted for the earing effect, establishing the effect of the structure of the sheet metal on the deep drawing process.
