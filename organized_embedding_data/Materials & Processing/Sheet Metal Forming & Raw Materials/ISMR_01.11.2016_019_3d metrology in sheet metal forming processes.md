# 3D metrology in sheet metal forming processes

[TARİH: 01.11.2016 ISMR]

22 FEBRUARY 2017 the GOM Workshop "3D Metrology in Sheet Metal Forming Processes" will take place in Coventry, UK. The event is part of an international workshop series running between January and April 2017 at 20 different locations from Asia to America.

The global GOM network established this event series to transfer process-related and metrological knowledge to design engineers, tool makers as well as specialists from research & development, production and quality assurance around the world.

Measurement systems from GOM are used in stamping, bending, drawing, pressing and forming process chains. They are designed to provide precise material parameters by determining the Forming Limit Curve (FLC) for design and simulation. During try-out, sheet metal parts are checked for shape and dimension as well as material defects: component geometry, spring-back, trimming, hole patterns, material thickness and forming limit analysis. For the series-accompanying quality control, all measurement and inspection processes are automated.

"The free workshop is an industrial meeting platform for an exchange of knowledge and sharing experiences. In fourteen different countries, leading industrial companies will show how integrating optical measuring systems into the entire process chain helps them to shorten development times and improve production workflows. In addition, the GOM network will introduce the latest developments in 3D metrology as well as in measurement and inspection software. Sheet metal forming workshops will be held in Brazil; China; Croatia; Czech Republic; France; Germany; India; Japan; Mexico; Portugal; Russia; Spain; UK and the USA - see www.gom-workshop.com," the company told ISMR.

In September 2016, GOM moved to its new headquarters in Braunschweig, Germany In addition to production halls and logistics, the new headquarters includes research and development laboratories, administration as well as a customer centre for training and presentations within an area of 8 ha. At the new site, up to 500 employees, including engineers, computer scientists and mathematicians, will shape GOM's optical measurement technology.
