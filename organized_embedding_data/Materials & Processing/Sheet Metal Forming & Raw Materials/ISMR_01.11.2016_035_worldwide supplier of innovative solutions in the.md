As SEYI Europe increases its foothold in the European market, its managing director explains the importance of viewing the stamping process holistically

ISMR SAYS: SEYI Europe has made strong inroads into fine blanking markets

specialist, Shieh Yih Machinery Co., Ltd. (SEYI), has established a strong position in the metal forming industry. It manufactures mechanical presses, ranging in size from 25 to 2400 tons, and servo presses, from 80 to 1600 tons. SEYI products have been sold to customers in over 40 countries and received numerous quality awards from around the world.

SEYI Europe has made good headway in Europe since its inception five years ago, and ISMR met Michael Juhl, its managing director, to discuss its achievements and future strategic direction at this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover.

## Establishing credentials in Europe

"Our focus is mainly on servo presses but we also supply mechanical presses (from 25 tonnes to 2400 tonnes). Mechanical presses are still a volume product here in Europe. It is important for our customers to understand that we can do both. Servo is the future, and where we are moving ahead, but mechanical presses are still sold in high-volume numbers," Juhl told ISMR.

"For me, EuroBLECH has been a special event this year. In 2012 and 2014, we shared a booth and were not a very well-known brand in Europe. In 2016, we now have our own booth at EuroBLECH with our own logo. People now know us as a high-quality supplier in the advanced stamping processes market and are asking us to quote for projects. This is because of our hard work promoting ourselves and visiting customers over the last five years. Our focus on fine blanking has also attracted a lot of EuroBLECH visitors to our booth. EuroBLECH is our most important show this year."

SEYI has made strong sales in Sweden, not only in stamping tools but also in fineblanking, a booming technology, especially for the automotive sector. Customers in the fine blanking sector are being pushed on margins. By controlling press movement and accuracy in its servo presses, SEYI Europe has positioned itself for the fine blanking parts market and has significantly lowered the investment level for customers.

"We sold a highly interesting project, based on servo press technology, to Spain this year (SD1-200 – 200 tonnes), making specific fine blanking parts in the Barcelona area. In the same press, we showed customers that we can also deep draw parts with extreme precision. We uploaded lots of videos of this onto YouTube and EuroBLECH visitors here, from countries like India, the US and Brazil, now know us from these videos. EuroBLECH is an international show and is very important for us. The quality of visitors this year is much better and this is a good sign for the industry. The contacts here have been of very high quality," added Michael Juhl.

## A successful year

Juhl confirmed that 2016 has been a very good year for SEYI Europe - it installed large production lines in Poland, introduced its fine blanking technology and installed a new press in Spain. It has also delivered presses to the Czech Republic, Hungary and Scandinavia. A complete stamping line was also recently sent to Slovakia for a large automotive manufacturer. A new servo press will be installed in Italy at the beginning of 2017.

"Our portfolio is growing – five years of hard work is paying off now! Europe is not an easy market but it is growing fast and getting better for us. Our sales are good for 2016 and we already have a large sales order book for 2017. Our focus this year has been to ensure that customers fully understand the flexibility of our services and technologies. Selling a servopress into an automotive supplier in Slovakia is very important - it flags to other customers the quality of our presses. We are also installing a 200-tonne press in Norway next month. This year, we are heading for growth. We may even double our turnover this year," Juhl told ISMR.

"One trend I see is that everyone is trying, in some way, to optimise their processes and increase their output. For example, two years ago we sold a servo press in Norway. The customer wanted to double its output on the press. In 2016, it has now tripled many of its press outputs whilst improving the quality of the part, reducing the need for other processes such as grinding, deburring and polishing etc. This makes the price of the part much lower and has been a real success. It is wonderful feedback to hear from a customer...," added Michael Juhl.

## An eye on the future

Michael Juhl has several strategic targets and objectives for 2017, building on SEYI Europe's foothold in the European market and growing sales in the region. Next year, SEYI Europe will concentrate on sub-supplier shows in Sweden, LAMIERA in Italy and Blechexpo in Stuttgart.

"With the growth we experienced in 2016, and expect in 2017/8, we need to reassure our customers that our after-sales service organisation is ready to support them at every stage. We want to promote this even more - we want customers to know that we will be there for them 24/7. We must also maintain and increase our foothold in the European automotive industry. We have hired a key account manager with specific goals on this, " he told ISMR.

A stamping example on the SEYI booth at EuroBLECH

"Our European headquarters, with our showroom, in Germany has grown. Our Technology & Test centre in Sweden, focusing on SEYI servo technology and developing advanced tools specifically for SEYI servo processes and presses, is operational and welcoming customers at the demo centre. We therefore expect even further growth in 2017 as we move to the next level," said Juhl.

He explained that Industry 4.0 will be the next step for SEYI Europe in terms of adapting its processes and technology to Industry 4.0. Juhl sees this developing at different levels 'depending upon customer viewpoints and investment decisions'. However, he is adamant that value can always be added to processes and presses, and output increased, if a holistic view of the stamping process is taken.

"Here, in Europe you don't want to handle a product too many times in the process – each time you touch it, it costs money. We will never be the cheapest in the market, if you look at the price of the press itself as an isolated value. Our goal is the best technology at the right price. We don't just look at the press, we look at the process holistically. In the final analysis, we are always most competitive," he explained.■
