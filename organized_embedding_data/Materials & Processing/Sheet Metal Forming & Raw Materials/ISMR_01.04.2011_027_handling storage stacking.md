## 5. Central storage

The following storage suggestions from CIRIA will help to protect your stock.

■ Choose a location that minimises the risk of theft and vandalism. Protect the materials from contamination, spoiling and accidental damage

■ Cover all or part of the storage area to protect it from the elements or use a storeroom

■ Ensure that there is adequate access within the storage area to withdraw materials as needed – do not allow early deliveries to become trapped within stacks. Good access is vital if mechanical handling equipment is used

■ Surround the central storage area with a secure fence

■ Level the ground and pave it

■ Position the storekeeper’s accommodation within the compound, preferably near the entrance, to help them manage the materials and components

■ Assign responsibility for the custody of keys

■ Ensure that the area is kept clear of waste packaging, broken glass, etc.

■ Avoid exceeding shelf lives

■ Avoid damage or contamination from incorrect storage

■ Avoid loss, theft and vandalism.

## 6. Ordering

Unhealthy ordering practices can also contribute to headaches in the warehouse.

■ Avoid over-ordering

■ Avoid ordering the wrong type, quality, size or length of materials

■ Avoid ordering standard lengths rather than the lengths required

■ Avoid ordering for delivery at the wrong time.

## 7. Delivery

CIRIA recommends the following delivery guidelines.

■ Ensure that care is taken during unloading to avoid damage

■ Avoid delivery to inappropriate areas of site

■ Avoid accepting incorrect deliveries, specification or quality.

## 8. Checklist

A final checklist should help to ensure compliance.

■ Is the area kept clear of waste packaging, broken glass, etc.

Double handling increases the risk of damage and losses.

■ Is it out of the way of vehicle movements?

■ Are the materials, components protected from the elements, and from splashing by mud?

■ Is the area tidy?

## LOADING, MOVING AND HANDLING GOODS

When storing at the workplace, do not exceed the design capacity of the structure (whether it is permanent or temporary like scaffolding) and take into account of the maturity of concrete at the time of loading. Be aware that materials can be heavy.

■ If the load is particularly heavy it can be spread to beam and column lines through a timber or steel grillage

■ If the loaded slab is back-propped, check that the capacities of the props and the lower slabs are not exceeded

■ Always spread loads and allow a reasonable space between them for easy access, consistent with the methods to be adopted for handling

■ Ensure loadings are safe by dividing storage areas into painted bays and indicate the materials allowed and the stacking heights.

In all cases, temporary works and structures should be designed and checked by competent designers and their construction verified on site.

There are various methods for moving materials and components around site; options include the use of cranes, trucks, hoists, fork-lifts and even manual handling. The choice of handling method can be widened if these issues are considered when the site is set up. The choice of handling method depends on what is suitable for the material or component (particularly so that packaged loads retains their integrity), what is available and what is economical. When moving materials around site or handling at the workplace, use materials in order of delivery i.e. use the oldest materials first. Mechanical handling may save some money in reduced labour costs and reduced wastage.

## Use mechanical handling to:

■ Move materials and components that are too heavy for manual handling

■ Avoid having to split packs into lots that can be manually handled

■ Move large quantities quickly

■ Avoid damage or spillage through incorrect or repetitive handling

■ Avoid delivering the wrong material to the workplace.
