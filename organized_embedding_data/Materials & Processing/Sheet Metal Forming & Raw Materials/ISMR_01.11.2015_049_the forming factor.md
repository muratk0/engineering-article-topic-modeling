# THE FORMING FACTOR

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

AP&T is gearing up for manufacturing challenges such as lightweight material forming, Industry 4.0 and electric car production

ISMR SAYS:

“The press hardening specialist outlines current and future key initiatives to ISMR"

For AP&T, the route to future research and product development has been determined. It wishes to be a world-leading supplier of press production solutions who contributes to weight reduction, energy efficiency and safety in its customers' products.

As a supplier of production solutions for press hardening of sheet metal parts for the automotive industry, it is now setting its sights on new materials and methods. Hot forming of high tensile aluminium and the forming of composite materials are examples of production solutions where there is a great deal of interest in the industry. The company develops and manufactures production solutions, automation, presses and tools for different customer segments and product categories.

ISMR discussed its latest initiatives with Michael Hunger, AP&T’s director and head of sales and marketing, Europe on AP&T's booth at the Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart this November.

## European successes

“One really important European order for us this year is for a customer producing a new steel coating process. The line will be delivered to Germany early next year. Recently, we also sold a cold forging press and I see a good niche in that market. We started our first hot forming lines in Europe with some good customers and have seen consolidations of volume in that market,” Hunger told ISMR.

Interior door section for the Lotus, manufactured with hot formed high tensile aluminium in a complete line supplied by AP&T within the framework of LoCoLite, an EU project

“The automotive industry is good – with automation and tryout presses being delivered into that sector - but the largest volume for us is in press hardening and, perhaps, automation. At the moment, most of the European car manufacturers are stable so there is still good potential for us.

“Here, at Blechexpo, we are focusing on lightweight forming – the warm forming of aluminium (fuel cell lines that we believe will grow in the medium term). We are showing a 3D simulation with a fuel cell line and a 3D simulation of warm forming of aluminium (6000 and 7000 series) here on our booth,” Hunger explained.

In the pursuit of lower weight and desired component properties, new materials are making their way into cars. One example is hot formed aluminium, which is a competitive option for some components despite its higher material cost. AP&T already markets complete production lines for the hot forming of aluminium.

AP&T recently started to offer automation equipment from NORDA through AP&T in Germany and Poland. NORDA is part of the AP&T Group. It specialises in flexible transfer solutions and automation for press lines, complementing AP&T’s own product lines. At Blechexpo, AP&T showed 3D simulations of the complete features of automated press lines.

“Our customers include both OEMs and suppliers of parts and components,” Hunger told ISMR. “We find that 3D simulations can be very effective ways of showing customers working line principles and synchronised equipment.”

## Market movements

Hunger outlined some of the main trends and movements he has identified in European stamping markets to ISMR.

“Industry 4.0 is a trend I see more and more in our machines i.e. from the service point, in maintenance, for safety on the machines and for communication between the machines. Industry 4.0 is still a bit further into the future but it is, nevertheless, a requirement that we need to incorporate,” he emphasised.

“For the automotive industry, the main drive I see now is in lightweight material development. There is also a range of aluminium alloys for more niche car production. Certainly, electric car production is another market trend. Self-driving cars, further in the future, will mean a total change of car design.”

Thanks to its development initiatives and cooperation with research institutes and partners, AP&T was an early forerunner in supplying complete solutions for press hardening. For car manufacturers, it told ISMR, weight reduction is key in terms of both lower fuel consumption and lower emissions of carbon dioxide. At the same time, demands on impact safety are becoming increasingly stringent.

Over the past ten years, press hardening has made a breakthrough on a broad front in the automotive industry. From the beginning, this concerned selected parts such as A-pillars and B-pillars but, when Volvo presented its most recent XC90 model last autumn, the proportion of structural parts in the car was 38%. Even if that proportion is far from standard in the industry, it demonstrates press hardening's impact.

The technology has since been developed and refined. The formed part can be given customised properties such as controlled deformation in the event of a collision since different materials can be used in the same part.

## An eye on the future

There is still a great deal of potential in adding more press-hardened parts in cars of the future, but new materials and methods are being developed in parallel. For the industry, it is a matter of finding the most efficient way to achieve the desired properties in the end product and, in the long term, being able to offer products that live up to both legal requirements and customer wishes for fuel-efficient and safe cars.

AP&T is taking part in the development of efficient production methods for forming composite materials in the TripleUse project, together with the KTH and Chalmers universities, the Swerea Sicomp research institute and leading companies in the aviation and automotive industries such as GKN, SAAB and Volvo. The goal of the project is to produce quick and cost-efficient methods for the production of high-tensile composites based on carbon fibre.

"Our goal is to be at the forefront of industrialisation when it is time for our customers to develop their production. During our development work, we cooperate with leading researchers on new materials and methods, as well as keeping close track of the industry's needs and circumstances. One way we do this is with our seminars where we share new knowledge, as well as interact and discuss solutions that meet the needs of the industry," AP&T explained. ■
