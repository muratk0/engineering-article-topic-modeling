# US SHEET METAL STATISTICS

[TARİH: 01.05.2010 ISMR]

According to statistics from the US Bureau of Labor Statistics, US sheet metal workers held about 170,700 jobs in 2008. About 63 per cent of all sheet metal workers were in the construction industry, including 46 per cent who worked for plumbing, heating and airconditioning contractors; most of the rest in construction worked for roofing contractors and for building finishing contractors. Some worked for general contractors engaged in residential and commercial building and for other special trade contractors.

About 23 per cent of all sheet metal workers were in manufacturing industries, such as the fabricated metal products, machinery and aerospace products and parts industries. Some sheet metal workers work for the Federal Government. Compared with workers in most construction craft occupations, relatively few sheet metal workers are self-employed, according to the statistics.

In May 2008, median hourly wages of sheet metal workers were US$ 19.37. The middle 50 per cent earned between US$ 14.39 and US$ 27.03. The lowest 10 per cent of all sheet metal workers earned less than US$ 11.43, and the highest 10 per cent earned more than US$ 35.36. Apprentices normally start at about 40 to 50 per cent of the rate paid to experienced workers. As apprentices acquire more skills, they receive periodic pay increases, until their pay approaches that of experienced workers. Around 32 per cent of all sheet metal workers belong to a union. Union workers in some areas receive supplemental wages from the union when they are laid off or experience shortened work weeks.
