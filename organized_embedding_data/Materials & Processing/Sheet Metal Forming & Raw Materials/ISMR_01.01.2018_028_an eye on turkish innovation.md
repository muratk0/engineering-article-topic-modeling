# AN EYE ON TURKISH INNOVATION

[TARİH: 01.01.2018 ISMR]

In operation for 67 years, Baykal is a leading Turkish manufacturer and global supplier of sheet metalworking machinery. It specialises in the production of press brakes; shears; notching systems; punching machines; laser-cutting systems; plasma cutting machines; waterjet cutting systems and vertical machining centres.

Baykal has three factories, with a combined production area of 70,000 square metres, making it one of Europe's largest facilities for sheet metalworking and fabricating machinery. Baykal is accredited to ISO 9001 Certification and, since 1995, has been building machines that conform with the European CE regulations for safety, as the first Turkish machine-tool manufacturer certified eligible to bear the CE-Mark on its products.

Over the last 40 years, Baykal has become a major exporter of sheet metalworking machines to global markets. It is currently represented in over 100 countries through appointed dealers. Attending over 80 exhibitions worldwide every year, Baykal regularly launches new machine tool innovations. Tolga Ogsar (Baykal's sales and marketing manager) took ISMR through the latest company developments.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes, achievements or awards.

TO: With a history going back to the early 1950s, Baykal today is a leading manufacturer and global supplier of sheet metalworking machinery. We specialise in the production of press brakes; shears; notchers; punching machines; laser cutting systems; plasma cutting machines; waterjet cutting and vertical machining centres.

We have received many awards and clocked up several achievements over the years. We started manufacturing shears in 1962 and hydraulic press brakes in 1973, with serial production of sheet metal processing machines following in 1974. We manufactured the world’s largest press brake in 2005 (and again in 2010) and the world’s heaviest tandem press brake in 2006. In 2011, we manufactured the world’s largest tandem press brake. We purchased Weinbrenner GmbH in 2007 and started making bending centres in 2008.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

TO: We are currently focused on fibre laser cutting technology. The next step is diode laser technology, but this is, I believe, a more ‘marketing-oriented’ technology. On a fibre laser, the most important aspect is control of the laser beam, which is done by software regardless of which company has produced the resonator. Laser cutting systems manufactured by Baykal Makina are well above the global standard. A further advantage is that the software is completely owned by Baykal.

Baykal is also an R&D Centre and has been awarded the title of 23rd R&D Centre in Bursa three years ago. With its R&D Centre Certificate, Baykal Makina aims to develop innovative and original designs in the sheet metalworking machine sector, with its 61 employees and a 550 sq. m. technology base. Our colleagues are working on lots of R&D projects right now, focused on a more practical, hands-on approach to issues such as higher productivity, improved systems and a better environment for our customers etc.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal markets?

TO: Industry 4.0 is the most popular trend that we see in the sheet metal market. Manufacturers are trying to integrate machines, for better connectivity, but we have been doing this for the last ten years. We are already strong proponents of Industry-4.0 and have integrated Industry 4.0 practices into our machine tools and products. All our products are compatible with Industry-4.0 requirements.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

TO: The global machine tool sector was worth US$ 95 billion in 2015. In 2016, machines exported outside the European Union (including Turkey) amounted to 18.9 billion euros (Asia, Japan, China and Turkey).

I do not have any figures yet for the end of 2016, but I believe that there is a volume of US$ 120 billion on the market. We are targeting this market very strongly. We have a huge customer network, but 2016 was a very challenging year. Turkey’s internal problems, and the sad things we experienced, unfortunately affected people badly.

But, as I said before, I will not be able to sell a US$ 50 million machine in a US$ 100-billion marketplace. This is all about how hard you try!

ISMR: What was the focus of your booth presence at Blechexpo in Stuttgart and FABTECH in Chicago in November 2017? Did you launch any new products?

TO: We introduced our new fibre laser cutting system, BLS-1530, at the Blechexpo and FABTECH exhibitions. BLS Series fibre laser cutting systems cover a wide range of industrial applications. Manufactured with an “All-in-One Box” design structure, the BLS has a built-in resonator and a chiller with dual motorised tables. A range of fibre power sources, from 500-2500W, are available. The BLS uses a new generation, high-performance linear axis system with rack and pinion.

With fibre laser technology, there is no need to move the laser source or sheet, enabling high-speed, high-accuracy cutting and dual fullsize pallet maximum production throughput. Machines have a simple and user-friendly operator interface, as well as an extensive cutting database.
