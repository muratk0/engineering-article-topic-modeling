# A 'DIRECTIONLESS' FINISHI Directionless finishing of the metal sheet used for its packaging machines has become increasingly important for GEA Food Solutions at its factory in the Netherlands.

[TARİH: 01.06.2023 ISMR]

'he manufacturing of packaging machines is high-tech; just ask Allard van Haaften from GEA Food Solutions. The multinational's machine factory is at Weert, the Netherlands, and specialises in machines for packaging food and lollipops (for production and packaging). A directionless finish is required for these packaging machines.

GEA Group is one of the world's leading suppliers of machinery and systems for food processing technology and related industries. The company employs over 18,000 people and is represented in 62 countries. At its Technology Centre in Weert, high-speed packaging is no mean feat whether it is packaging chicken; nuts; lettuce; sweets; chips or lollipops etc.

So, how do they make the machines in Weert? The basis of the machine is partly stainless-steel sheet metal. After the sheets go through the laser cutting machines, they are then processed in a Timesavers 32 RB series compact dry wide belt grinder for deburring, edge rounding, finishing and/or laser oxide removal.

“The machines we make are largely made of stainless steel and anodised aluminium, because they have to be easy to clean. You don't want to find any residue from a machine in a food product. Parts must be perfectly flat and completely deburred," explained Allard van Haaften, who is responsible for supply chain and operations at GEA Food Solutions.

## A smooth process

GEA Food Solutions chose the Timesavers '32 RB series' compact dry wide belt grinder for deburring, edge rounding, finishing and/or laser oxide after careful consideration.

"We mainly looked for a machine that would allow us to finish as smoothly as possible," explained Van Haaften. “Products come out of the laser cutter, are tapped (when necessary) and are finished in the Timesavers machine. The finished products go through the belt onto the return table. The return table is proving to be very valuable because it allows us to ensure that the machine is used by many people working around the machine. Further down the assembly line, the machines are assembled into fully fledged food packaging devices."

A technician from GEA Food Solutions was finetuning a lollipop packaging machine and Allard van Haaften asked if he would like to give a demonstration.

"We are in the test phase and only have a fraction of the capacity," he shrugged. Nevertheless, the lollipop packaging machine shoots packed lollipops into a collection tray. The machine can also do it faster.

"We do between 800 and 1400 lollipops per minute," explained Van Haaften. "This number depends a little on the shape and size of the lollipops."

## Directionless finishing

Van Haaften stressed that directionless finishing is important.

"A machine that you don't finish 'directionless' simply does not look nice. If you finish the sheet metal 'directionless', it doesn't matter how you put the sheets in the Timesaver machine. Moreover, you do not want deep surface scratches because then the plate gets dirty faster and is harder to clean. This is a 'no-go' if you work with food products," he highlighted.

GEA Food Solutions has been operating the Timesavers 32 RB series for a year now.

"Within a week, everyone was able to operate the machine flawlessly. Together with Timesavers, we examined how we could replace the existing machine with one that could both finish directionless and process very small parts. The promise was then made that we could finish a euro in terms of size. This was proved possible without any problems. We then went to the factory with lasered parts and did tests e.g. how fast is the machine, which grain size should we choose etc.? This way, with Timesavers' help, we chose the machine that suited us best," added Van Haaften.

It was, however, in brush selection and brush pressure that Timesavers' expertise came in handy. GEA Food Solutions is a specialist in packaging machines, among other things, but not in deburring operations.

"We do a lot of single-piece operations and there is therefore a lot of variation. The question was whether we could still use the same settings as much as possible. The sheets we process are generally between 1-3mm thick. Sometimes we have 5mm workpieces. We normally only use brushes for finishing and, very occasionally, a pre-grinding belt. You only need the pre-grinding belt if you wish to remove a large burr but it is preferable not to use it as a grinding belt leaves marks [on the material]. For us, there is no debate: we want directionless," confirmed Van Haaften.

## A sheet metal focus

Timesavers International specialises in the surface finishing of sheet metal, including operations such as deburring; edge rounding; laser oxide and heavy slag removal and finishing/precision grinding.

"We registered a 70-million-euro order intake worldwide in 2022. Our 'bread and butter' business is edge-rounding and deburring machines. We have such a wealth of process and application knowledge in these technologies. We place a strong focus on continuous innovation every year. At the Blechexpo trade fair in Stuttgart this year, for example, we will be introducing more new technologies for our machines," Paul Hartendorp, managing director of Timesavers International BV, told ISMR at the EuroBLECH trade fair last October.

"We have set a new strategic path and are growing the company worldwide with acquisitions in the U.S., as well as other global acquisitions. These will see the company grow to a turnover of between 150 and 200 million euros (employing between 700-800 people)," he added.
