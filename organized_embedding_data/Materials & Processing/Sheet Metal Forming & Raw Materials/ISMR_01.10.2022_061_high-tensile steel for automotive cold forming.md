# High-tensile steel for automotive cold forming

[TARİH: 01.10.2022 ISMR]

Japan's JFE Steel Corporation, one of the world's leading integrated steel producers, and Germany's largest steelmaker, thyssenkrupp Steel Europe, have jointly launched a new 980 and 1180MPa class high-strength steel for coldforming.

“These products represent higher yield strength and higher ductility, especially excellent local ductility, compared to conventional high-tensile steels. These characteristics contribute to further weight reduction of the automobile body frame (Body in White) and better crash safety performance, as well as higher productivity and lower manufacturing costs because the complex components can be formed by conventional cold- (press/roll) forming processes without using the hot-stamping process," said the companies.

“In the current vehicle design, energy savings, weight reductions for reduced CO₂ emissions and crash safety performance are required. However, the higher strength of steel for weight reduction inevitably means inferior formability, and it has been difficult to apply higher strength steel to more complex components," they explained.

To overcome this challenge, JFE and thyssenkrupp Steel have developed these products by designing a new steel composition and microstructure, particularly focusing on the local ductility of steel and by establishing a new heat-treatment method utilising the quenching and partitioning process. These products can be supplied globally for automotive applications.

"The strength levels of these products are 980 and 1180MPa, with various types of coating or uncoated, galvannealed and galvanized. They are designed for wide applications to next-generation vehicles including battery electric vehicles. In addition, we continue to jointly develop products with even higher strength levels," added the companies.

Thyssenkrupp Steel has set itself the goal of producing three million tonnes of CO₂-neutral steel per year from 2030 onwards, and to make its steel production fully climate-neutral by 2045.
