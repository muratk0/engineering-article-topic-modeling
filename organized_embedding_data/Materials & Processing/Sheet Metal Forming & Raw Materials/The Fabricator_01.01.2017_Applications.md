Assembly Line Helps Metal Parts Manufacturer Produce Heavy Structures

Juan Diego Camino Muñoz, COO of Tecade

SITUATION

Tecade S.A., located in Sevilla, Spain, has been manufacturing heavy metal structures for 30 years. Though its main focus is on domestic markets, the manufacturer has increased its work with international companies over the past seven years.

To boost production and drive growth in the bridge and offshore industries, the company was in need of production machinery for heavy and large-diameter products. The firm began searching for a supplier that would be able to provide reliable machinery and a complete line from welding to material handling.

"Our need was a solution that could not only weld with high quality and join cans together, but also to move heavy solutions from place A to place B," explained Juan Diego Camino Muñoz, COO of Tecade. "We didn’t have a solution for big pipes beforehand, so we needed bigger equipment,"

RESOLUTION

The company collaborated with Pemamek to get a new PEMA Assembly station up and running as soon as possible. Working on a tight schedule, Pemamek delivered the assembly line on time, and it was ready for production just one week after commissioning.

The assembly line enables Tecade to speed up the manufacturing process while producing high-quality products that can weigh up to 300 tons. Efficient production of large-scale cylindrical and conical pieces requires the ability to operate in a broad area with a diameter of 4.9 to 26.25 ft. with no additional setting times needed. The station also enables precise and fast shell fit-up and tack welding. Furthermore, with an integrated column and boom, the equipment allows welding of each workpiece’s seams in the same station.

Tecade’s first project with the assembly line—which included manufacturing piles with a diameter of 8.8 ft. and thicknesses of 1.96, 2.4, and 3.94 in.—was finished in March 2016.

"The assembly line has certainly saved time," said Camino Muñoz. "It has provided more efficiency and assembling capacity, but also better prices to manufacture, which ensures us a better competitive position in our favor."

Bearing manufacturer achieves smooth, even cutting with circular cold saw

SITUATION

ThyssenKrupp Rothe Erde, located in Dortmund, Germany, is a global manufacturer of slewing bearings, seamless rolled rings, turntables, and structural elements for use in equipment such as cranes, excavators, antenna systems, telescopes, and turbines.

The company needed to replace an old sawing system, and the new saw had to be integrated in the existing production line. ThyssenKrupp wanted to achieve a high level of process reliability and a good cutting result on quenched and tempered steels (such as 42CrMo4) and construction steels.

RESOLUTION

The company chose a framag KKS 1600 cold circular saw that cuts round materials up to 2 ft. dia. and 19.7 to 32.8 ft. long and out-of-roundness up to 1.5 percent. A three-point clamping device, combined with a table height adjustment system, supports the material during cutting.

The newly developed hydraulic saw blade damping, patented by framag, helps keep the saw blade running smoothly, which helps increase blade service life and keeps the cut even, with a minimal cut gap.
