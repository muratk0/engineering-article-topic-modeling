# ALLOY DEFORMATION and fracture behaviour

[TARİH: 01.05.2013 ISMR]

LENTON, manufacturer of electric laboratory and industrial furnaces, ovens and incubators, has produced a bespoke split tube furnace for tensile and fracture testing undertaken at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Imperial College London (UK). The furnace is designed to allow researchers to observe and record the effects of high tensile stress and temperatures on metal alloy components, with the aim of understanding the deformation and fracture behaviour of high temperature alloys and to enable model validation.

As part of the work at Imperial College London, researchers will heat relatively large tensile and fracture mechanics samples to temperatures of up to 1000°C and place them under load until the structure fails. Surface strains can then be mapped in 2D or 3D to examine how they evolve over time and under different conditions.

In cases where components have been welded, for example, researchers will be able to determine the deformation gradient in neighbouring regions of the inhomogeneous material. This information may then be used to develop and verify deformation and fracture models which can be further used to predict in-service component failure in, for example, the powergenerating industry.

The furnace produced by Lenton needed a high tolerance on the temperature distribution across the chamber to guarantee uniform temperatures in large samples. The furnace also required a large viewing window to allow the formation of deformation and fracture to be observed effectively and safely. A camera mount was included alongside the viewing window to image and record the deformation in the test samples. See www.lentonfurnaces.com
