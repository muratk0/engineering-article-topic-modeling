## Quality control is important

It is imperative to ensure that metallic compositions used in additive manufacturing are of the highest quality. Failing to do so can significantly compromise the integrity of the final product, leading to 3D-printed products that break, or that fail to bind and adhere properly. However, quality assessment can be challenging due to the subtlety of the variables involved, especially when working without specialist equipment.

Key quality assurance challenges need to be addressed and resolved before additive truly go mainstream.

The alloy powders used in metal additive manufacturing require carbon, sulphur, oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen content to be carefully balanced to ensure tensile strength, impact resistance, heat tolerance and resistance to corrosion. Engineers who are unable to account for these variables will not be able to meet the quality standards expected by end-users, which may result in higher manufacturing costs due to production failures.

## Elemental analysis can enhance material quality

Elemental analysis instruments are becoming key components in the quality control process for additive manufacturing. Inductar elemental analysers make it possible to identify impurities within raw materials on an industrial scale. This not only helps to ensure that low-quality materials are filtered out, but also gives engineers more control over the physical properties of the final product, and greater insight into how 3D-printed components will behave in various circumstances.

As the process requires the sintering of different components to form an alloy, a precise understanding of how the chemical composition of the starting materials impact the finished products is needed. This is because the elements that were present at the beginning of the process can be lost or changed during the heating process. The composition of carbon, sulphur, oxygen, nitrogen and hydrogen will impact on the physical properties of the final product.

By checking the quality of the finished item after the manufacturing process is complete, comparisons between the compositions of high and low-quality products can be made to identify which characteristics are causing the failure. Given that the elemental make-up of a 3D-printed item can often change during the additive manufacturing process, this kind of post-production evaluation is essential.

Additionally, elemental analysers can be used to maintain quality even when recycling leftover powder for a subsequent production run. This process is usually performed within an inert gas atmosphere, but the extremely high surface area of the powders means that some oxidation usually takes place regardless. By carrying out an elemental analysis before reusing the powder, manufacturers will be able to ascertain whether it is still fit for purpose.

## Testing and analysis

By removing the physical limitations of products by conducting thorough quality assessments, additive manufacturing will continue to go from strength to strength. One area of development is to improve the strength of the alloys formed to match that of cast/forged metal items. When this technique is refined, it will be possible to produce 3D-printed items far more cheaply than cast/forged items, which are expensive due to the cost of the moulds that need to be created for the production of the workpiece.

For additive manufacturing to continue playing a crucial role in production, it is imperative that the materials used are properly tested and analysed so that any concerns over whether a powder is unsafe or unsuitable can be allayed.
