## Athader SL

Athader has been a leading supplier of coil processing equipment in the market since 1992 and manufactures custom-designed coil processing equipment. Its portfolio includes automatic slitting lines; cut-to-length (CTL) lines; combined lines (slitting and cut-to-length); automatic banding and packaging lines for sheet and coil plus recoiling lines. Customers include Tata Steel, Bluescope and Outokumpu.

In 2012, Athader became a member of The Bradbury Group (www.bradburygroup.com), a manufacturer of roll forming, coil processing, levelling and automated production systems. The Group has an extensive sales network, manufacturing facilities and after-sales support around the world, including manufacturing facilities in North America, Europe and Oceania.

Athader and Leveltek International (www.leveltekint.com) design and supply cut-to-length lines with stretch levellers, complete lines or upgraded existing lines with roller levellers to produce laser-cut flat sheet. Athader and Samuel Nelson Technology (www.nelsonsteell.com) signed a strategic agreement to jointly market and sell push-pull, semi-continuous and continuous pickling lines.

## Marcegaglia

Marcegaglia is a leading industrial group worldwide in the steel processing sector, with a turnover of more than 5.5 billion euros. The group has 6,600 employees, 60 sales offices and 28 steel plants. Around 6.2 million tons of steel are processed yearly, serving more than 15,000 customers.

Marcegaglia Carbon Steel manufactures flat products (coil, strip and sheet), carbon and pre-painted (PPGI) steel plus carbon steel welded tubes. In 2019, the Marcegaglia Group approved investments totalling 150 million euros for Marcegaglia Carbon Steel Flat Products. These include, at its plant in Ravenna (Italy), a new highly automated rolling mill for special steels; extension of the covered area by 20,000 square metres; a logistics automation project; a 33MW co-generation plant and a galvanizing line revamp to improve quality and productivity. At Gazoldo degli Ippoliti, investments include a second 22 MW cogeneration plant, a pickling line revamp and a slitting line increase.

## Lankhorst Engineered Products BV

Lankhorst Mouldings produces a wide range of single and multilevel coil store systems. With over 35 years of experience, the company's steel coil storage systems can be found worldwide.

Its product range includes coil pads; coil saddles; coil blocks; coil mats; coil cradles; coil wedges and the turnkey KLP® Roll Stop System.

"Whether you want to store hot rolled, cold rolled, galvanized, painted or tin-plate (slit) coils, there is always a coil storage system that will fit your needs. Our KLP® RollStop System is used by major steel companies worldwide as a turnkey storage system offering maximum flexibility and safety. Coils can be stacked up to three levels. This coil storage system consists of only four components: rail, connector, spacer and RollStop for very simple installation on-site," said the Dutch manufacturer.

Lankhorst Mouldings is a subsidiary of the Royal Lankhorst Euronete group. Its origins date back as far as the year 1803.

## As Timesavers celebrates its 25th anniversary, its managing director and sales and marketing director discuss the company's latest new initiatives and technology developments.

Operating from three facilities in the USA, the Netherlands and China, the organisation supplies customers worldwide and has placed a strong focus on innovation and R&D (particularly during the COVID-19 pandemic).

## EffiCOIL

Schuler outlines how impact extrusion can be used to efficiently manufacture prismatic battery cell cases and lids for electric vehicles.

ISMR SAYS:

"With impact extrusion, up to five times more parts can be made than with deep drawing, while the material utilisation is also much higher."

Introduced in the early 1990s, the prismatic cell satisfies the demand for thinner sizes and lower manufacturing costs. Wrapped in elegant packages resembling a box of chewing gum or a small chocolate bar, prismatic cells make optimal use of space by using the layered approach. These cells are predominantly found in mobile phones with lithium-ion. Each manufacturer designs its own.

Prismatic cells are also making critical inroads into larger formats. Packaged in welded aluminium housings, the cells deliver capacities of 20 to 30Ah and are primarily used for electric powertrains in hybrid and electric vehicles. The prismatic cell requires a slightly thicker wall size to compensate for the decreased mechanical stability from the cylindrical design, resulting in a small capacity drop. Optimising the use of space makes up this loss. Prismatic cells for portable devices range from 400mAh to 2,000mAh.

Prismatic cells are contained in a rectangular can. The electrodes are either stacked or in the form of a flattened spiral. They are usually designed to have a very thin profile for use in small electronic devices such as mobile phones.

## Prismatic battery can production

Schuler is known for supplying systems forming structural and body parts made of high-strength steel, aluminium and composites. This lightweight technology is also used for electric vehicles. However, Schuler also offers equipment for the manufacture of electric motors cores; the laminations. The battery cases at the heart of the electric vehicle can also be produced with Schuler solutions.

"Generally speaking, there are two ways to make prismatic battery cans," Schuler's Markus Röver explained at a virtual battery exhibition held by the German Mechanical Engineering Industry Association (VDMA) last year. "Either you start with a coil of sheet metal and stamp a blank out of it to form the cell case by deep drawing, or you start with a piece of aluminium called slug."

Yet, in the first case, fabricators can only exploit part of the metal strip depending upon the size of the blank and the coil width. "Here, you can achieve utilisation of 65 per cent, which means that 35 per cent of your metal goes back to scrap," explained Röver, who is head of sales e-mobility at Schuler. "You also have to complete quite a few complex production steps which will limit the output, since you also have to transfer the part from one forming station to the other."

In the second process, the aluminium slug is utilised almost in its entirety. Thanks to the impact extrusion process, the prismatic case can be produced in one shot, followed by just wall-ironing (to achieve the final geometry) and a trimming operation.

"This is a solution which is really applicable for the mass production of 20 or 30 million parts per year," Röver pointed out. While deep drawn cases can reach a production rate of 20 to 30 strokes per minute, impact extrusion can achieve an output of up to 100. "The cost-to-performance ratio is better because you need fewer operators and a smaller footprint."

## A turnkey solution

With impact extrusion, the slug is punched into a die, resulting in the aluminium flowing backwards. Schuler has already sold more than 700 presses with this technology, when the company started to apply it to the manufacture of batteries about four years ago. The thickness of the can is reduced by wall-ironing. The geometry can therefore be calibrated at the same time.

Trimming is done by cutting from the inside of the container, so that the burr is on the outside. In this way, the content is not damaged when it is later filled into the battery. Since a lubricant has been applied to the slug at the beginning of the process, the cans must be washed and dried at the end of the line. The geometry and surface of the final product is finally inspected by cameras.

"In our R&D department, we can do a simulation of new sizes, geometries and materials of the cells," Röver added. Schuler supplies the production line as a turnkey solution including tools, integrated control unit and full service. According to the company's Smart Press Shop concept, each of the machines in the line is connected to a data loft. The information on runtime and from visual inspection can be used for applications such as process monitoring and preventive maintenance, for instance.

The Feintool Group's Smartline EV 3.8 high-speed press from Schuler, at its new production site in Taicang (China), for the manufacture of interlocked electric motor cores.

Each battery case also needs a lid, which is manufactured in a stamping operation.

"The part itself is not very complicated", Röver says, "but in the downstream process, a lot of assembly work has to be done."

However, some steps can be integrated into the forming process - the burst valve, for example: "Usually, a foil is applied here. But the metal can also be made to pop up at a defined pressure by stamping a corresponding geometry into the lid." This is possible at high speeds e.g., on a Schuler 200-metric-ton stamping press.
