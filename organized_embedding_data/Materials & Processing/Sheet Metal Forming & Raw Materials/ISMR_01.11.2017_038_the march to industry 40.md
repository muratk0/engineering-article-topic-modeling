# THE MARCH TO INDUSTRY 4.0

[TARİH: 01.11.2017 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"Customers want increased automation and the ability to run parts as quickly as possible"

Marc Jorns, CEO of Swiss bending specialist, Jorns AG, discusses new folding technologies, market trends and the march to Industry 4.0.

Jorns AG is a Swiss manufacturer of folders, double folders and accessories used for processing sheet metal. Its customers mainly work in roofing or architectural sheet metal. Products range from simple NC-controlled machines to sophisticated CNC-controlled sheet metal bending systems and a J-Bend app, functional on tablets and mobile phones, to support the management and production of sheet metal profiles. In 2016, the company also launched its own CP200 touch-screen control panel.

JORNS AG double bending is an efficient method of processing sheet metal in a slitter folder machine. Its TwinMatic-Pro double bending folders operate using both upper and lower bending beams to process sheet metal profiles. The Norma-Line, Maxi-Line and Super-Line long-length slitter folders from Jorns AG boast a modular design which allows owners to configure the machine in the way that suits their own bending requirements. Optional features include variable lengths (4m, 6.4m, 8m, 10m and 12.2m), CNC controls, shearing systems, hydraulic material thickness adjustment, depth stop and top beams. Its J-Bend app, for tablets and mobile phones (Apple/Android), supports manufacturers in recording, managing and producing sheet metal profiles.

The Jorns factory is in Lotzwil, between Zurich and Bern, in Switzerland. It builds around 100 machines per year, in different lengths and sizes, and sells them worldwide. Marc Jorns, CEO of the company, took ISMR through some of its latest innovations and developments at this year's Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart.

Marc Jorns, CEO, Jorns AG, with a profile bent on the double folder

ISMR: What was the focus of your booth presence at Blechexpo this year?

MJ: We are mainly present in the roofing and cladding industry – our customers produce mainly roofing and cladding products for that market. We have therefore brought to our booth a fully automated folder for the industrial production of roofing and cladding products, the TwinMatic-Pro 150. It is a 4m long double folder, which will be sent to a German customer after the show. We are a family company, which my parents started in 1973. I took the company over in 2005, when we started producing the double folding systems.

The folder features a gripper system to hold the material and feed it automatically to the correct bending position, where it bends positive and negative bends. There is no need to handle the material at all - a key benefit when you have long, light gauge material. The material being processed on this folder is up to 1.5mm mild steel – we also have machines that can process up to 3mm mild steel. Our double folders range from 4m to 16m. The fully-automated TwinMatic-Pro and semi-automated TwinBend-Pro double folders are designed to be more adaptable and many times faster than conventional folders. In many cases, rotating or flipping of the profile during the bending process can be avoided. All you have to do is program your part, insert your blank and get out of the way. The dual bending beams bend parts both up and down, and parts are finished with accuracy and speed.

We are very proud of the new options that we have developed. The basic structure of the machine has not changed much but a lot of small things in the controller have changed. Special functions enable profiles to be run in a more automated process – this is always the goal. There are new systems for the back-gauges i.e. instead of just using a clamp, there is a vacuum system to hold the part. There are increased production speeds and different options for taper back-gauge systems. We try to use the latest technologies in machine, drive and control systems to improve accuracy, quality and speed.

ISMR: What are your views on the current business climate for sheet metal professionals?

MJ: It has been a wonderful year for us – the best in five years so far, I would say. We have made at least 15% more than last year and 2016 was a good year for us. There is strong demand for the folders right now and our order book is full until June 2018. Slowly, customers are starting to move from standard folders to fully automated equipment.

We are very positive about the business climate in 2018 – the last few exhibitions have been very busy. Even though the U.S. market is still our strongest market, we also see a lot of demand from different European countries. The market split for us is 30% USA, 25-30% Europe, 10% in Switzerland and 30% for the rest of the world.

We started in the Asian market two years ago, through agents, and we see an increase in demand from Asia (particularly in China and Indonesia). China is an interesting market – it is looking for the latest technology and automation equipment. Next Spring, we will introduce a fully automated loading and unloading device, which will be interesting for the Chinese market. It will process parts of up to 8m long.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal bending markets?

MJ: The trend is really towards fully automated systems. Five years ago, the split of machines we sold was 20% automated machines and 80% semi-automated machines. Now, the trend is almost 50/50. This year, we sold 60% semi-automated machines and 40% automated machines. Next year, we believe that it will be much more focused towards automation. Our customers generally bend painted steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

Industry 4.0 is another trend – software needs to be networked and compatible with other systems. Fully automated loading and unloading is also important. We produce our own software, with a German partner, and work with three companies in this area.

We are also seeing higher demand for longer folders right now. This is not to make longer parts, but to make multiple parts at the same time.

There is a shortage of skilled personnel in this industry - automation has to fill the gap.

Customers want automation and they want to run parts as quickly as possible. Their needs centre around the speed, flexibility and productivity of the machine. Sometimes, we find that they need to bend special architectural panels, which are challenging to bend, with tapered sections that need narrow bends on long lengths.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

MJ: Automation of the entire bending process, for better production planning, is our main goal. The control needs to eventually take over the work from the operator to support him/her in making the product but also limiting the risk of any damage to the machine. There is a shortage of skilled personnel in this industry – automation has to fill the gap.

## The TwinMatic-Pro

The TwinMatic-Pro is the latest up/down long folder from Jorns. It is equipped with the new CP200 Twin control, for automatic sequencing of a part's construction, as well as the servicefriendly TeamViewer system. This machine comes in standard lengths of 10, 13, 21, 26, 32 and 40 feet.

With an upper and lower bending beam, it can fold the sheet either upwards or downwards. This flexibility reduces the amount of handling required and accelerates the bending process. A key feature is the programmable air gap which, depending upon the operating cycle (top and bottom bending beam), adapts automatically to sheet thickness.

Our products are not used for mass production of parts – they are usually used for individual parts' production or small batch production. However, we are still trying to bring automation into this process. This helps customers to increase production and to guarantee throughput in shorter times, producing parts more quickly.

We will stay firmly focused on the folding business, on light gauge material in long lengths. We do not intend to produce any shorter folders than 3.2m right now.
