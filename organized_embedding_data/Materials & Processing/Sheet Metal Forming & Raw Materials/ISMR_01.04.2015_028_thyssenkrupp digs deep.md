# THYSSENKRUPP DIGS DEEP

[TARİH: 01.04.2015 ISMR]

Ground work is underway for a new 1850mm-wide coil slitting line at Vetchberry Steels, under a two-year £4m investment programme

A two-year £4m investment programme is taking shape at Vetchberry Steels, the coil processing division of ThyssenKrupp Materials (UK) Ltd., a subsidiary of ThyssenKrupp A.G. Following the re-location of a newly installed 1650mm-wide coil cut-to-length line, which will form the nucleus of a dedicated sheet and blank production centre for stainless steel and aluminium, the ground work is getting underway for a new 1850mm-wide coil slitting line.

Vetchberry Steels is strategically located and equipped to provide a comprehensive service in the UK and abroad for stainless steel and aluminium flat rolled products. Offering the facilities to slit, cut-to-length, traverse wind, blank, de-burr and polish stainless steel coil, and to slit, cut-to-length and blank aluminium coil.

## FOCUS ON COIL PROCESSING

## Preparing the ground

Adrian Palmer, operations manager for the business, explained to ISMR: “The new line, built by Georg in Germany and the UK, requires a looping pit as deep as a 3-storey block of flats is tall…”

Preparation involved sinking 132 concrete piles 15 metres deep to support the excavation, and around 100,000 gallons of subterranean spring water has been environmentally recycled.

“It’s a pity we hadn’t thought of installing a bottling plant at the outset,” said Palmer, “we could have introduced two new development products, instead of just aluminium!”

The work to commission the new slitting line for stainless steel and aluminium coil production will take three months, yielding significant benefits for ThyssenKrupp’s existing customers and increased capacity to develop new markets, cementing Vetchberry’s position as a premium UK service centre for stainless steel and aluminium.

## A wide portfolio

Purchased by ThyssenKrupp AG in 1999, Vetchberry is one of the largest stainless steel stockholders in the UK offering flat rolled stainless steel products as well as temper rolled stainless steel strip. It can also supply traverse wound material from 0.6mm up to 3.00mm thick and 10mm to 60mm wide, in all grades of steel.

“Traditional 'pancake' coils at narrow widths often have the disadvantage of restricted OD i.e. short coil lengths. For continuous feed applications e.g. presswork, there is a growing demand for longer running feedstock. Why stop your production six times to load traditional 'pancake coils' when you could do it once with traverse wound coils?” Vetchberry told ISMR.

Vetchberry Steels also has in-house capabilities to produce temper rolled stainless steel strip. It has been rolling steel since 1992, when the 4-high Robertson rolling mill was commissioned. A tension-levelled product can also be offered where flatness is crucial.

“We take pride in developing special stainless steels for new and challenging markets. The lean nickel austenitic grade AISI 201 is one of these specials. The 200 series was developed in the 1930s but only in recent years has the lean nickel-based, high-manganese stainless steel come into play. As a result, reductions in alloy surcharges, particularly when nickel prices are jumping, can be expected,” the company told ISMR.

“The steel was developed so that almost half the expensive nickel content has been replaced by manganese; a cheaper alternative.” ■
