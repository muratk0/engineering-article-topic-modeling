# THE TIG PRINCIPLE

[TARİH: 01.03.2016 ISMR]

Umicore Bausysteme GmbH is using a new TIG welding process for the production of downpipes

What strikes the eye when you take a tour around the production halls of Umicore Bausysteme GmbH in Gatterstaett, in the German state of Saxony-Anhalt, is the diversity of downpipes and gutters packed and ready for shipment. The company produces pipes of different diameters and also elements for roof drainage systems from copper, aluminium and titanium zinc. The main products are titanium zinc pipes and gutters which are available under the trade name VMZINC with different surfaces such as NATURAL-ZINC, QUARTZ-ZINC, ANTHRA-ZINC, PIGMENTO and AZENGAR.

A 2.2km mill-finish titanium zinc strip is processed into downpipes in a continuous process. (Source: Umicore)

Currently 2,500km of pipes with diameters ranging from 60 to 120mm and lengths of between 1 and 4m are produced yearly on three production lines, 80 per cent of which are from titanium zinc. This also means that 2,500km of seams have to be welded. The source material are strips with a length of approx. 2.2km which are unrolled from the coil, formed into endless pipes, welded edge-to-edge and finally tailored.

Welding longitudinal seams The production facilities are well utilised for multi-shift production. However, demand is growing so production manager Torsten Hanke has had to discuss, with his colleagues, how it might be possible to produce even more downpipes in less time. “The bottleneck is the welding of longitudinal seams,” he explained. “With conventional TIG welding, the welding speed is only about 5m/s.”

The company’s specialist welding distributor suggested trying a new process: the TIG-welding process, InFocus, from Kjellberg.

“It was not necessary to invest in new power sources because existing ones could be used after they had been upgraded with efficient cooling systems. The technology carrier is the InFocus welding torch. With its highly focused arc, it is a tool that makes weld seams to excellent quality similar to plasma or laser welding,” Kjellberg told ISMR.

InFocus is a high performance TIG process for welding low-alloyed and high-alloyed steels, nonferrous metals as well as galvanised sheets at high speed. Welding of thin and thick sheets (up to 10mm in one layer) is also possible. The process, says Kjellberg, is spatter-free. The weld seams are characterised by an even seam surface, a small heataffected zone and low distortion.

These properties are not only required on the construction site but the seams have to prove their quality in the factory. After welding, each downpipe is expanded at one end by up to 2mm, creating a sleeve that is used later on the construction site as a connecting element. If the seam quality is insufficient, the seam will tear during expansion and the pipes are useless.

## Identifying welding parameters

Other pipes are further processed into pipe elbows. For this, they are bent up to an angle of 85° and, after cutting to length, also equipped with a sleeve. Despite the double load on the seam – bending and stretching – there is greater danger of tearing during the expansion of the sleeve, according to Torsten Hanke. What is done in the factory with even force by a mechanical pipe expander requires a plumber’s skill when processing leftover pieces on the construction site. And the lower the temperatures, the more susceptible to cracking are the weld seams.

To prevent this from happening, Kjellberg carried out a detailed process analysis and, with Umicore, identified the optimum welding parameters for the InFocus process. The general conditions for the welding process are dictated by the three existing production lines. Regardless of the welding process, the production lines could presently produce at a maximum throughput speed of 7.5m of pipe per minute.

“While welding with InFocus, the potential of the lines can be fully exploited. In comparison to conventional TIG welding, this means a 50 per cent increase of welding speed without major modifications on the production line,” explained Kjellberg.

The weld seams are characterised by an even seam surface, a small heat-affected zone and low distortion.

The cuts shaped as pipes have a small weld gap similar to conventional TIG welding, making the use of filler materials unnecessary. The titanium zinc cuts are welded with alternating current and argon as shielding gas. Forgoing the use of expensive helium reduces the production costs. When the coil is not too oily, the process runs smoothly and stably over the entire length of the coil, Kjellberg explained. A stable process has a positive influence on the wear of the electrode. A further supporting factor is the optimised ignition process.

In addition to process-related consultancy, Kjellberg also provides support for mechanical engineering ranging from project planning for complete welding workstations to the construction of simple devices. For Umicore, Kjellberg standard support was optimised and adapted to existing techniques. For example, the exact manual adjustment of the torch above the seam. The operator can move the torch in four dimensions, adjusting it exactly and to the right distance.

“Operators rely on their experience so they quickly found the ideal position on the basis of the appearance of the arc and the seam. It was therefore not necessary to re-adjust the torch during the welding process,” Kjellberg explained.

“Since Autumn 2015, the first of three production lines has been running with InFocus. The other two lines follow in Spring this year. Further modifications on the production lines will be made to enable the customer to fully benefit from the potential of InFocus. The InFocus process is designed to weld up to 9m per minute. These modifications are worthwhile because, in the end, the quantity of produced downpipes can be further increased.”

InFocus torches boast an interesting electrode design. The optimised water cooling and minimised electrical and thermal resistances enforce the concentration of the arc at the electrode tip, thus increasing the energy density in the arc many times over. For the manufacture of filigree components or welding in pipes with a diameter of up to 40mm, Kjellberg has developed compact torch versions. ■
