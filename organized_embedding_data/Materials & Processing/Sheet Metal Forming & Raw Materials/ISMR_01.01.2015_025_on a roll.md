# ON A ROLL

[TARİH: 01.01.2015 ISMR]

Purchasing a roll former is a major investment. Here are seven questions to help you make your decision……..

Roll forming is a method of shaping sheet metal through a sequence of bending operations on a roll forming line. The metal stock is fed through a number of roller stations, or dies, that are positioned on both sides of the metal. The positioning of these roller dies will greatly influence the outcome or the shape produced; dies may be above and below, along the sides, at angles or any other positions. Each station will perform one particular stage in this complete process and may even be lubricated.

The selection of the model and roll former configuration best suited to a particular application depends upon a number of factors. Typically, section thickness, speed and part profile determine the machines series whereas production requirements determine the most appropriate configuration.

Purchasing a large piece of machinery like a roll former is a very important decision. Before you make your choice, why not ask yourself these seven questions?

ISMR SAYS:

“The selection of the model and roll former configuration best suited to a particular application depends upon a number of factors”

## Does the roll former support multiple sizes and gauges to fit my needs?

Know what you are going to build and the engineering requirements. Based upon your location and size of structure, you will most likely need more than one size or gauge of steel to build all the walls, floors and trusses. Do a comparison of all the steel that will be needed in a project and see how much will be able to be produced from your roll former.

## 2. Is the roll former built to last?

Roll formers take a lot of wear and tear from regular use and vibration. Not only does the machine need to last, but so do the moving parts and tools. Take a close look at the quality and durability of the machine and weigh the cost of tool replacement and wear and tear over the years. A less expensive machine may end up costing you more a couple years down the road….

## 3. Can the roll former change sizes and gauges quickly?

The best way to reduce the amount of steel that goes into a building is to optimize the sizes and gauges of steel but only if you have a roll former that can change to a new size or gauge quickly. Time is money and if you are spending too much time adjusting the machine, instead of rolling, you are not making money…..

## 4 How fast is the roll former?

Most roll formers can roll simple shapes quickly, but you should be asking “What is the true throughput of the machine – including all the stops for punches and tools?” Additional punches and tools slow down a machine, but speed up assembly. You need a machine that gives you all the punches and tools you need for efficient assembly and can still run at a high speed.

## 5. Does the system offer flexibility?

Some steel framing systems only use one shape. This can be an advantage by making the machine and system simple, but it is also very restricting. Single shape systems don’t give you the flexibility you need to make field adjustments.

## 6 What kind of trusses does the roll former produce?

Not all trusses are the same. Trusses designed in the weak axis use more steel than trusses designed in the strong axis and are structurally weaker. Investigate the structural requirements you will need for your trusses and see which system can handle those requirements.

## 7 . Does the roll former include design and engineering software?

A roll former without software is like a printer without a computer. You could buy a cheap roll former without software and try to find software that might interface to the roll former or manually input all the pieces, but that is a risky proposition. Make sure you look for software that not only engineers but also designs all the required components and can interface flawlessly with the machine. Some systems can be a broken compilation from different companies and not a tested and integrated solution. ■
