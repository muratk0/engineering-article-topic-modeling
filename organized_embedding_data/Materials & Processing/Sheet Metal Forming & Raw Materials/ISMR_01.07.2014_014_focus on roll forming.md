At the Tube 2014 exhibition earlier this year, software manufacturer Data M presented, amongst others, the COPRA® RF CageForming system for tube and pipe forming. The latest developments of its roll forming simulation software, COPRA® FEA RF, are focused on essential enhancements of the re-start functionality. In previous versions, it was possible to re-start a simulation from a specific position and continue the simulation with modified rolls, thus significantly speeding up the optimisation process of the roll tooling and forming strategy.

“The advanced re-start functionality in COPRA® FEA RF now allows not only a change of the tooling but also a change of the mesh at any stage during the simulation. This mesh can be defined and optimized by the user. Refinements and additional elements can be defined where they are needed and, most importantly, the necessary refinements can be introduced at a stage where needed,” the manufacturer told ISMR.

Growing demand and resulting changes have led German roll forming specialist, Dreistern, to relocate its service center to its new Arnsberg location in early April 2014. It has also developed special forming and straightening processes for components. In addition, it is possible to make specific changes to particularly critical individual component dimensions in the event of dimensional variations.

At its recent Dreistern Innovation Forum (21-23 May 2014), discussing the most recent developments and important technical trends in roll forming applications, it pointed out the advantages of multi-functional roll forming technology – in effect, changing the roll forming machine into a highly productive and flexible sheet metal production cell.

“To produce value-added, complex-function parts, roll forming is combined with many additional processes. Multi-functional roll forming is the consequent advancement of this development. In most cases today, a tube is a semi-manufactured product which has to undergo further processing until it becomes the final product. Tube manufacturing itself is only one part of a comprehensive process chain,” explained Dreistern.

“The concept of multi-functional roll forming is based on an integration of down-stream processes into the tube mill. Both tube manufacturer and client can benefit from this development. A reduction in the number of processes in a process chain always means cost reduction. The capability to supply value-added tubes opens up new market opportunities for tube manufacturers.”

Multi-functional roll forming involves integrating additional processes such as piercing, notching, embossing, fixing nuts or bolts and many other processes into tube mills. Any of these operations can be integrated flexibly at any position within the machine by exchanging work modules. For even higher flexibility, these work modules can also be exchanged between different tube mills or roll forming machines. For that reason, they are equipped with separate control units with standard interfaces which can communicate with the main machine control.

Roll formed sections also have advantages over extrusions of similar shapes

“The advantage of such a solution is obvious. Multi-functional machines can be configured precisely and rapidly according to the actual requirements of a new product. The footprint of such a machine can be reduced by 30 per cent to 50 per cent compared to conventional technology. No redundant line component can increase the machine-hour rate. The objective of the Dreistern development was more flexibility, but less cost and less required space,” the manufacturer told ISMR.

Hadley Group roll forming machines can produce almost any geometric shapes of different sizes and material thicknesses of up to 8mm in material thickness. Its roll forming lines can be set up with multiple configurations to punch and cut off parts in a continuous operation. For cutting a part to length, the lines can be set up to use a pre-cut die where a single blank runs through the roll mill, or a post-cut die where the profile is cut off after the roll forming process.

Features may be added in a hole, notch, embossment or shear form by punching in a roll forming line. These part features can be done in a pre-punch application (before roll forming starts), in a mid-line punching application (in the middle of a roll forming line/process) or a post-punching application (after roll forming is done). Some roll forming lines incorporate only one of the above punch or cut-off applications, others incorporate some or all of the applications in one line.

Incorporating multiple operations saves time and, therefore, cost. Metal forming companies have long been asking for the ability to weld a seam as the product is being formed by a mill. Samco has therefore developed an in-line, high frequency welder co-ordinated alongside its roll former which does not require a separate operation. The key features of this step beam with inline weld roll forming line include a robust design double decoiler (10,000 lbs) with coil snubbers and feed-up drive for threading and rewinding.

Samco’s Universal roll former showcases a high quality design with shaft deflection and factory laser alignment of stands and shafts. The HiCarbon and HiChrome roll tooling set provides extended life. A flood coolant system allows for quality surfaces and reduced pick-up of contaminants on the rolls. The in-line, solid state, high frequency welder with squeeze box includes a cooling trough and welding mechanicals for continuous welding. The system also features a hydraulic cut-off press system for continuous operation and higher production rates. The Samco Machinery Weather Seal roll forming system incorporates an integrated process whereby the weather stripping is automatically mechanically crimped into the profile.

Italian manufacturer STAM has designed and built, it says, the ‘first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses up to 7mm’. STAM’s new roll former is able to roll form pieces of different shapes and thicknesses. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength. STAM has delivered and installed, to date, over 1000 different roll forming machines.

The machine has more than 100 dynamic axes which are constantly changing position during the roll forming operation as well as over 40 fixed axes which are positioned according to the profile to be produced and will constantly be maintained in position during the process. Each axis is driven by servo-motors, with position control in a closed loop, with each axis moving in a different manner to the other axes depending upon its physical position along the machine.
