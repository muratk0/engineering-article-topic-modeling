# INDUSTRY 4.0 IN FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.11.2019 ISMR]

Kemal Ileri, Durma's sales and marketing director, discusses the technologies showcased on its booth at Blechexpo in Stuttgart, as well as his view on market trends, Industry 4.0 and the current business climate.

Durmazlar Makina Sanayi Tic. A. S. (trade name, Durma) is based in Bursa, Turkey, and was established in 1956. An early sheet metal machine pioneer in Turkey, the family business (part of the huge DURMAZLAR Holding Group) now manufactures bending centres (panel benders and press brakes); laser cutting systems; shears; punch presses; plasma cutters; plate roll machines; profile benders and corner notchers.

As a founder member of Turkey's Industry 4.0 platform, Durma has also started designing machines that can communicate with each other through customised cloud technology, designed in-house. It has also developed 3D laser cutting technology, with advanced features, designed to accurately process 3D metal cut-outs.

We also teach our customers how to prepare for Industry 4.0 - this is part of our digitalisation strategy.

ISMR: What are you showcasing here at Blechexpo in Stuttgart?

KI: We are showing two new technologies here on our booth – Bluebend panel bending machines and HDFL 6G laser cutting machines (10kW power source, with high acceleration for faster speeds). The laser machine's design has been completely updated. We are now using fully electric servo motors (no hydraulics) in our rapid panel benders. Feedback on both these machines has been excellent from visitors to our stand.

Our 3D laser cutting machine cuts through metal alloy parts with thicknesses of between 0.5mm-8mm. It is mainly used in the automotive industry for drilling and perimeter cutting of pre-formed materials which cannot be punched.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate for sheet metal professionals?

KI: The tariff escalation between the U.S. and China is affecting our business. Our machines represent a large investment for customers and they need to be sure that the economy is strong before making such a large investment. We are, however, growing by 5-10 per cent (compared to last year) but our expectation was for more (15 per cent) this year.

For 2020, I feel the same. However, we do have an advantage because we have a large customer network in 120 countries as well as dealers in 80 countries. If a region is experiencing difficulties, we are shielded by our business in other regions.

Our largest market is Europe, followed by North America then Russia; Italy; the Far-East; South America and Africa. We will be exhibiting at FABTECH next week as the U.S. has been a good market this year for the sheetmetal industry.

ISMR: Please explain your strategic and technical objectives?

KI: Everybody knows that the Industry 4.0 revolution is happening. I am happy to say that, over this time, we have been preparing ourselves for it. Our initial digitalisation strategy was to develop our base of DURMA software engineers – we now have 120 engineers (20 of them are pure software engineers).

DURMA was a founder member of the Industry 4.0 group in Turkey and we started developing our Industry 4.0 software and developments in concert with this group i.e. angle measurement systems; angle monitoring systems etc. Now, all of our machines use these systems and we have developed our own cloud systems to store data etc.

Communication is key and our machines can now offer this type of connectivity to our customers. We also teach our customers how to prepare for Industry 4.0 – this is part of our digitalisation strategy.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

KI: In our business, the most important things that customers need are quality, speed and accuracy. Other issues of importance for them are machine reliability; after-sales service/ support and, of course, price.

Machines need high uptimes and strong support, so that customers do not lose money. Productivity is key.

ISMR: How do you approach environmental issues when designing your machines?

KI: Green technology is firmly on the table for the future. Not only is it more environmentally friendly, but it also saves money and time for customers.

Environmental issues are extremely important. For example, latest generation cars, when they stop at a red light, automatically switch off their engines so that they are not idling and creating harmful emissions, wasting energy. We are using the same technology in the design of our machines. When press brakes are idle, for example, they are not expending energy or leaching cost for customers.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in sheet metal stamping markets?

KI: Automation is the most important trend that I see. Lights-out, automated working is becoming more common now. This chimes with our own strategy (e.g. our Industry 4.0 initiatives and our online and real-time monitoring systems for greater accuracy).

We are also investigating technologies such as Artificial Intelligence (AI) and Virtual Reality (VR). Customers can, for example, see machines modelled in VR through our apps on smartphones and tablets – this helps when showing machine status updates, for example, for maintenance or training. This is now available for all customers to access.

ISMR: Where do you see the greatest challenges and opportunities?

KI: As far as opportunities are concerned, the Internet has definitely expanded our reach. However, it is very important that information is correct. We have created a new marketing strategy where we try to share all our data with our dealer networks via extranet systems. They can easily find information about our products and systems. When they are with customers, they can show them videos of our machines from our YouTube channels or social media feeds. This helps our sales and is a new business model for us, helping us to reach every type of customer.

A key challenge for us is to reach and satisfy the demands of every customer. This includes customers who want low-cost machines in certain parts of the world. We want to be able to offer machines that meet the requirements of all our customers, wherever they are in the world.
