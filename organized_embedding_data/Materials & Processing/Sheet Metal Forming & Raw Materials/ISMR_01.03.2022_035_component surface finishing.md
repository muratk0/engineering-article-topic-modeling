# Component surface finishing

[TARİH: 01.03.2022 ISMR]

Finishing specialist Fintek (Hall 19, stand 26) is using the MACH 2022 exhibition in the UK this April to display how advances in automation have improved surface finishing capabilities for larger components. It will also demonstrate how new electro-chemical processes are being used to remove support structures and help the finishing of internal and external surfaces for additively manufactured parts.

Jonathan Dean, managing director of Fintek, commented: "Since the last [MACH] show, there have been significant advances in surface finishing for traditionally engineered parts and metal components manufactured by 3D printing. From OTEC Präzisionsfinish, we have the new SF-HP which brings fully automated high-energy stream finishing to workpieces of 650mm in diameter and 650mm long, weighing up to 200kg. From RENA Technologies, we have the Hirtisation® process that finally resolves one of additive manufacturing's major hindrances, the cost-effective and efficient removal of support structures, residues and the smoothing of hard-to-reach internal surfaces and deep cavities."

In addition to the new systems, Fintek is keen to show improvements made by OTEC across the full range of CF (disc finishing), DF (drag finishing) and SF (stream finishing) machines for deburring, edge-honing, smoothing and mirror-like polishing of metal parts, from the extremely small and thin up to aircraft landing gear. From table-top variants to fully automated systems with robotic loading and unloading for inline production, the Fintek team at MACH will be advising visitors on the best process for any metal part.

"In an exclusive deal with a UK company, we will also be introducing another new technology for finishing the internal and external surfaces of 3D-printed metal components," added Mr Dean.■
