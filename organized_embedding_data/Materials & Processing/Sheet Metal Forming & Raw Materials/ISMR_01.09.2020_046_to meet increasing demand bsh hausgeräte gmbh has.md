# To meet increasing demand, BSH Hausgeräte GmbH has replaced an ageing system in its Giengen plant with a modern multi-blanking system to produce plates for refrigerator housings.

[TARİH: 01.09.2020 ISMR]

According to analysis by Statista, Germany-wide sales in the refrigerator segment are set to increase to almost 1.625 million. However, the production of housing components for refrigerators is extremely complex because, despite their high residual stress, the materials used must be completely flat for any subsequent processing steps. This is a time-consuming process.

To meet increasing demand, BSH Hausgeräte GmbH replaced an ageing system in its Giengen plant with a modern b+s multi-blanking system. It levels and cuts in a single pass, so that changing cut sizes can be produced in-house quickly, efficiently and without additional logistics efforts. The new system enables speeds of up to 30m/min. with mating limits above 300N/mm2 and material strengths of above 500N/mm2.

Since refrigerators housings are visible parts, smooth processing of the sheet metal used is essential and the blanks must not be scratched during production. This can only be achieved if the components are flat but, because of the high internal stress, they tend to corrugate and therefore must be levelled first before being cut to size.

“Due to the sharp rise in demand, our previous system had reached its limits and led to a capacity bottleneck,” reported Dominik Schiffner, project manager in the Prefabrication Production Planning department at BSH Hausgeräte GmbH in Giengen. “We therefore decided to replace it with a modern multi-blanking facility from the b+s group. It allows several precise and individual sheet metal cuts to be made in one pass.”

Using this machinery, BSH now has a greater vertical range of production and can react more flexibly to changing market requirements.

## On the level

The new equipment at the BSH factory in Giengen has been running since June 2019. It combines a cut-to-length line with a slitting line and is designed for sheets with a material width of up to 1,650mm and a material thickness of 0.35 to 0.6mm. The SCHNUTZ leveller is particularly suitable for material strengths above 500N/mm2 and yield strengths above 30N/mm2.

“Before the first cut, the coil is levelled and only then divided into longitudinal strips,” explained Thomas Baral, Managing Director, Burghardt + Schmidt (b+s) GmbH. “These strips are then cut transversely, resulting in up to two sheet metal blanks of the desired length per segment.”

Because of the high number of sheet metal blanks with small dimensions, the stacking and packaging area plays an important role. The b+s group has therefore focused on different stacking techniques, such as a blow air stacker. Compared to the old system, the new multi-blanking system is about 70 per cent more efficient, which has increased daily throughput, b+s told ISMR. The levelling result also meets required specifications from BSH - for example, the result is extremely low-burr.

“Our employees appreciate the ergonomic advantages of the new system,” said Schiffner. “It also works at less than 75dBa, which significantly reduces noise emissions. This, of course, contributes to a much more pleasant working environment”.

Based on this positive feedback and the extensive support throughout the project, further lines from the b+s group are already planned in other BSH plants.

## Consultation and design

Initial planning for the project began in 2016, and to optimally adapt the new equipment to customer requirements, the b+s team first visited the BSH plant in Giengen.

“Every machine park poses different challenges when designing new equipment,” explained Thomas Baral. “Therefore, on-site appointments are essential for our service - this is the only way new equipment can be optimally integrated into existing production processes.”

The control and drive technology, straightening technology and production speed of the equipment played a decisive role in the conceptual design. Further requirements could be added during the design phase, thanks to efficient preliminary planning.

“For safety reasons, for example, it was important for us that the new system enabled automatic strapping with edge protection,” reported Schiffner. “Likewise, the new equipment had to do without a pit, but had to have a sample drawer so that the quality of the blanks could be checked. In addition, the lowest possible noise level was desirable to minimize the burden on our employees through unnecessary noise emissions.”

Once all requirements had been recorded, construction of the ‘individualised’ multiblanking system could begin. Using the synergy of the individual companies in the b+s group, special requests could be quickly implemented so that the equipment could be delivered ready for operation in March 2019.
