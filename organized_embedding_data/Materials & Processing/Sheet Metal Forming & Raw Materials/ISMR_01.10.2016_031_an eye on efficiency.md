# AN EYE ON EFFICIENCY

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

Sheet metal CAD/CAM software is fundamental to Groundhog's manufacturing processes

"Radan software drives laser cutting, punching and bending processes for steel at Welsh firm, Groundhog"

LASER CUTTING on a fully automated Bystronic Fiber 3kW machine, driven by Radan software, now takes precedence over punching for a leading manufacturer of sheet metal welfare units for construction site and road workers.

Welsh firm, Groundhog, produces units for workers to wash, sit, eat and change their clothes on-site. Made from high tensile CA 240 cold-rolled steel, they can be static for long-term sites, or mobile for ease of switching from one site to another. Groundhog was one of the first companies to introduce mobile units around 20 years ago, enabling contractors to avoid the cost of transporting and installing static assemblies.

Groundhog manufactures 600 mobile and 240 static units annually. The mobile units are all 2.5 metres wide, and come in four lengths (between 3.6 and 6 metres), while the static units are 2.9 metres wide and either 6.1 or 7.6 metres long. Facilities include seating areas for six, eight or 12 people, offices, showers, toilets and hand washes, noticeboards, televisions, Wi-Fi routers and 3G.

## Connected communication

Groundhog had used Radan for many years, then changed to another sheet metal CAD/ CAM system which had links with MRP software. Groundhog design manager, Matthew Stevenson, explained: "I wanted to improve communication with the shop floor by linking the sheet metal CAD/CAM software to MRP and, at the time, Radan couldn't provide that. But we had issues with the new supplier right from the start."

Eventually, after a period of having to constantly scrutinise the NC code as it was selecting the wrong tools, Stevenson decided to move back to Radan software, which he said is now fundamental to Groundhog's manufacturing process. Radan has just introduced Radmanager, enabling it to integrate more fully into Groundhog's complete IT structure.

Radmanager has direct links with Radan CAD/CAM and can communicate easily with ERP systems, creating nests from multiple customer orders, maximising efficiency and sheet utilisation, and managing customer orders through the nest production process.

"This is exactly what I wanted," said Stevenson.

## Laser in focus

Around 100 different sheet metal components go into an average unit, mainly cut on the laser now. Radan also drives Groundhog's TRUMPF 2020 punch press and four Bystronic press brakes. Laser, however, is Groundhog's preferred cutting technology as the line is equipped with a ByTrans Extended tower, giving considerably more automated throughput of sheets and parts, so that production can continue after the shop floor closes for the night.

"Our standard items are made from 1.5mm and 2mm steel, most of which can be cut on the laser at around 20 metres per minute. The only time we use the punch now is if the component has any rolling offsets or other features that specifically require punching," commented Stevenson.

While 1.5mm gauge accounts for more than half of the 100 tons of steel used by Groundhog each month, the laser cuts thicker metal just as efficiently. “For example, it has cut steel up to 15mm, along with 8mm 310 stainless and 10mm aluminium," explained Stevenson.

## Software benefits

Groundhog has four seats each of Radpunch and Radprofile, with two of Radbend. The bending software begins the process as well as creating the final folds at the end of the.

Matthew Stevenson added: "When we design the components in Autodesk Inventor, we use Radbend to make sure that they can be bent as we wish, before we even take it into Radan. It also allows us to check the tonnage and that the right tooling is available, along with flanges and clearances."

He creates projects, comprising multiple items within the bill of material, applies priorities and selects sheets.

"The auto nester does the rest, and then I see if anything needs tweaking. I can get a nest created, adjusted and onto the shop floor in around 30 minutes, all done in Project Nesting, as that enables me to see what has been nested and what hasn't," he explained.

Material utilisation is also important to Groundhog, and Stevenson added that spending additional time on a fixed nest for a long-running program is easily justifiable. He also highlighted how Radan 3D analyses the model immediately after it has been imported, showing a bill of material-style parts list in the new right-hand side panel.

"It shows whether we can progress the sheet metal parts to the next stage, by detailing whether the material is available and if the thickness has been properly calculated, considerably reducing the risk of errors," he concluded.
