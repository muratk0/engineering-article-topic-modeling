ISMR reports from the recent LAMIERA sheet metal exhibition in Bologna, Italy

The seventeenth LAMIERA sheet metal event, which took place at Bologna from 14-17 May 2014, ended on a positive note. The number of visits officially recorded at LAMIERA reached 18,620 (826 of which were from outside Italy).

370 companies exhibited at the show, 40 per cent of whom were from outside Italy, occupying an overall exhibition space of 35,000 square metres.

“Compared to the previous exhibition," said Luigi Galdabini, President of UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, “these figures confirm once again the central role of the exhibition for the operators in the sector, and show an increase in the number of visitors as well as a demonstration of the good health of LAMIERA and the sector that it represents.”

The international character of the event, promoted by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE (the association of Italian manufacturers of machine tools, robots, automation systems and auxiliary technologies) was confirmed by the strong presence of exhibitors from: Germany; UK; Spain; the USA; Switzerland; Holland; Austria; France; Turkey; Belgium; and Ireland as well as Australia; China; Denmark; Finland; Japan; Greece; Monaco; Romania and Taiwan.

## Italy in focus

The second largest consumer market in Europe, Italy can boast, with LAMIERA, of one of the most recognised exhibitions dedicated to the metal forming machine tool industry, and to the technologies developed for the processing of sheet metal.

The two-yearly event is promoted by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE. Organised by CEU-CENTRO ESPOSIZIONI UCIMU, the exhibition has always been a point of reference for the end users of the main outlet sectors: from general mechanics to metallurgy, from machine tools to automotive, from household appliances to building, from the food and packaging sectors to energy and aerospace.

The spotlight at LAMIERA was firmly on technologies such as: sheet metal forming machines; welding machines; cutting and oxy cutting machines; bar, tub, and profile processing machines; presses; shearing and punching machines; treatment and finishing; components and accessories.

LAMIERA also launched its own catalogue app, for the first time, to make it easier to find exhibitor data during visits to the stands. Available free of charge, for iOS and Android operating systems, the app proved to be a practical and rapid way to move through the halls, acquire information and take notes, with the option of adding photos and notes to users’ notebook systems in real-time.

The exhibition opened on 14 May with the inaugural meeting Russia: guest country of LAMIERA 2014. New partnership opportunities among Italian and Russian companies. The meeting hosted the round table entitled

There was also an accent on quality at LAMIERA – its ‘Blue Philosophy’. An area was dedicated to the introduction of (already developed) technologies and processes centred on eco-compatibility from companies which have been awarded the UCIMU Blue Philosophy seal and were taking part in Lamiera. The UCIMU seal is awarded to UCIMU associate member companies capable of meeting certain quality criteria, including the rational exploitation of resources and minimum use of energy, raw materials and means. Protagonists included: Adige; Blm; Colgar International; Ficep; Gasparini; Ltf; Marposs; Millutensil; Omera; Prima Industrie; Salvagnini; Tecnocut Cms and Tiesse Robot. With over 40 presentations, the area featured debates on interesting and current topics in the sector, comparing the business world with research and training sectors.

One of the companies awarded the Blue Philosophy seal, FICEP, has developed a range of technologically advanced multi-function and flexible Gemini systems for profile cutting, drilling, machining and scribing with plasma bevelling and an advanced material cutting capability. Over 150 machines have been installed worldwide and now its latest HD machine, which features a plasma bevelling head, joins the Gemini range.

“The Gemini HD is a technologically advanced, small footprint CNC machining system for the profile cutting, drilling, bevelling, machining and scribing of parts from plate, from 5mm up to 120mm thick with cutting lengths from 6 to 30 metres – in just one set-up. The new compact bevelling head is now 25 per cent lighter than similar machines and has internal kinematics, making it even faster and more flexible. The scribing system can also significantly reduce fabrication time and manpower requirements,” explained Barbara Colombo, owner of FICEP, to ISMR at the show.

Other conferences at LAMIERA included Laser, a Research Bridge Between University and Industry organised by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in collaboration with Promozione L@ser and sponsored by Alma Mater Studiorum, University of Bologna.

A press conference on Living Robotics Today was also organised by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in collaboration with SIRI, and a conference on New Regulations in the Manufacturing of Metal Carpentry Structures was also organised by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE, in collaboration with IIS.

The DESIGN-MTS project partners also ran a conference during the exhibition on 15 May 2014. The aim of the conference was to present the multi-stakeholder platform of the project DESIGN-MTS – Defining Social responsibility Interventions for Grounded Networking in Machine Tools.

“Innovative CSR and sustainability initiatives have always been widely implemented by European machine tool companies. With the DESIGN-MTS project, we will disseminate these business practices to our sector and value chain, and show our stakeholders how the European machine tool sector contributes to society, the economy and the environment,” said Filip Geerts, CECIMO Director General.

The European project ‘DESIGN-MTS’ was launched in July 2013 and is coordinated by the Università Degli Studi di Macerata. The project aims to raise awareness on CSR in the European machine tool sector, enabling enterprises from the sector to have a strategic approach to CSR and sustainability, and supports the coordinated management of a CSR platform. The DESIGN-MTS project will identify best business practices in CSR and sustainability and disseminate them on a single platform towards the value chain. Its four focus areas are employability & skills, demographic change & active aging, workplace challenges and environmental challenges. See www.designmts.eu

A new area of the exhibition was dedicated to the steel, metal alloy and non-ferrous metal manufacturing chain, in which approximately fifty companies took part. Conceived to “showcase” the two contiguous branches – the machine tool and the steel and alloy processing machine sectors – the area proved to be extremely interesting for exhibitors and visitors alike.

Building upon the success of previous demonstration areas, LAMIERA also re-ran its LAMBDA project which this year focused on sustainability. Populated by prestigious international entities of the academic, research, enterprise and start-up world, the LAMBDA SOSTENIBILITÀ area was designed to demonstrate the commitment of the sector towards eco-compatible production. It showcased the debate on the opportunities and the methods for machinery and connected technologies capable of combining social, environmental and economic sustainability needs.

The main attraction in the LAMBDA arena was the Alfa Romeo 4C, designed by the Centro Ricerche Fiat, and displayed as the representative object of these new production techniques. The conference programme, which ran side by side with LAMIERA, was attended by approximately 170 operators.

The protagonists of LAMBDA SOSTENIBILITÀ are research centres, universities, enterprises and start-ups that compare opportunities and ways of producing machinery, production systems and connected technologies capable of fulfilling social, environmental and economic sustainability requirements. These included Ata Associazione Tecnica Dell'Automobile (Technical Automobile Association); Cna Innovanet; CRF Centro Ricerche, Fiat (Fiat research centre); Fraunhofer IWU, IAM-Polo Automotive; Musp; Quadratum; Safen Fluid & Mechanical Engineering; Siri Italian Association of Robotics and Automation and Stanimuc, University of Bologna.

“Sustainability, expressed through the development of systems capable of rationally exploiting resources and minimising the use of energy, raw materials and means, is in fact nowadays a determining factor in the purchasing processes of the operators in manufacturing sectors, who are increasingly focused on investing in solutions that ensure the best machine performance to consumption ratio,” explained UCIMU.

“The number of bilateral meetings, involving about 40 Italian companies and many operators from delegations outside Italy, reached approximately 400. Invited by UCIMU-SISTEMI PER PRODURRE and ICE-Agenzia, the event was attended by delegations from: Brazil; India; the United States and Turkey. Delegations from Canada, Croatia and Serbia were also invited independently by the association. These were joined by the delegation from Russia, the appointed ‘country of honour’ of LAMIERA 2014, invited within the scope of the Machines Italia in Russia project, promoted by the Ministry for Economic Development and FEDERMACCHINE,” UCIMU added.

Among the special initiatives of the exhibition, of particular success was Cosmos: The History of Space, which identified the missions of the former Soviet Union, and today of Russia, for sending satellites into orbit.

According to the latest UCIMI statistics, Italy produced forming machine tools in 2013 to a value of 1,962 million Euros, a 7.9 per cent decrease compared with 2012. Italy now ranks third among international manufacturers, after China and Germany, but ahead of Japan, Korea and the United States. Among its most exported machines, first place goes to curving and bending system, followed by presses.

The main outlet markets for the Italian sector are the United States, with a +11 per cent increase when compared with the previous year, followed by China (+12.5 per cent).
