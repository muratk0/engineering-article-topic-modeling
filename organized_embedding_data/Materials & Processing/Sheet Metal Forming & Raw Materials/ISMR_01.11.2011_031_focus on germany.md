Maximum plant flexibility and output performance are particularly important for the South African steel manufacturer, which is a member of the Aveng Group headquartered in Roodekop. The company specialises in structural and panel blanks for car manufacturers.

The Schuler line ordered features an edge trimming device which chops up the residual strips but can also coil them up again. The edge trimming equipment can trim the coil edges by up to 130mm on each side. This enables Aveng Trident Steel to flexibly trim coils to the desired width – a major benefit for the company, as this makes it independent of country-specific supply bottlenecks when purchasing certain coil widths.

In addition to the normal coil curvature, Schuler’s straightener with bowing option can also straighten edge and centre curvature – a particular problem with inferior-quality coils. This means that the line can process an extremely wide variety of coil qualities – even cheaper materials – with the same results. A further flexibility bonus was Schuler’s first-ever press with a slide adjustment of 550mm. This allows customers to fit a wide variety of dies.

Downstream of the press, the stacker not only features the usual two stacking stations but an additional scrap stacking station, which can also stack varying blank sizes.

## INDONESIAN ORDER FOR SEVEN PRESS SYSTEMS

Outdated machines in the battery facility of ABC Battery in Jakarta coupled with strong demand for zinc-carbon batteries were the main motivating factors for the company to procure new presses. It chose Schuler’s new generation of high performance impact extrusion presses in combination with its trimming machines to more than double its output – from around 120 batteries per minute to as many as 300 batteries per minute.

The Indonesian ABC Dry Cell Battery Factory – a member of the P.T. International Chemical Industry Group – is replacing part of its facility with seven press systems to be supplied by Schuler as of mid-2012. The new press systems, each consisting of one horizontal impact extrusion press (1,500 kN of press force) and one rotation trimming machine, are to be used to produce zinc-carbon battery cans. The production of zinc-carbon batteries begins with the casting or stamping of a zinc or zinc-plate blank. The Schuler presses are used in the second step when the flat, coin-like blanks are formed into cylindrical battery cans in a backward extrusion process.

The opening of the can is then trimmed to size as it moves on rotating spindles past a fixed trimming segment – resulting in a high-quality rolling cut. The zinc cans are then further processed in other areas of the factory.

Within the press line, the straightener is responsible for smoothing the previously unrolled metal coil. The straightener prepares the sheet metal for its actual forming process in the press. As the carefully coordinated rollers are very sensitive to dirt, the machine needs to be regularly cleaned.

“Our hinged straightener can be easily opened, leading to a drastic reduction in maintenance and resulting downtime for suppliers to the automotive industry. At the same time, individual adjustment of the upper rollers ensures optimum straightening results. The straightener also boasts a particularly rigid design – of particular importance in the case of high straightening forces. With a maximum straightening speed of 40 m/minute, the AC-SH-9-53/160-1300 hinged straightener exhibited at the fair can straighten steel, stainless steel and aluminium coil stock with widths of up to 1300mm and thicknesses of 0.5 to 4mm,” Schuler explained.

All metals with an ultimate elongation of at least ten per cent and an accentuated yield strength can be straightened. An automatic cleaning device, as well as motor-driven, automatic roller adjustment, and a coil-end welding device are all optionally available to further reduce set-up times.

## ENERGY OPTIMISATION

The steady rise in energy prices is putting a significant strain on company results. This is one of the greatest challenges for industry. The Schuler Group’s new POWERSAVE measurement device creates the suitable conditions for raising energy efficiency. Efficient use of available energy can significantly reduce a company’s energy costs.

Schuler now offers its POWERSAVE services for the energy optimisation of press systems – regardless of brand. Schuler’s POWERSAVE experts can show customers exactly which areas of the existing line offer energy-saving potential. Customers can then choose either individual measures or a complete concept of tailored solutions for optimising energy. Following this, the machines are made fit for the future in terms of energy consumption – thus ensuring that press systems are optimised for energy efficiency throughout their life cycle.

“A Schuler environmental certificate documents the benefit for the environment. After all, energy savings not only have a positive effect on a company’s balance sheets, but also on its corporate image. In future, major corporations in particular will pay increasing attention to the commitment of their partners with regard to environmental protection,” Schuler told ISMR.

## NEW TWIN-ROD HIGH-SPEED BLANKING PRESS

With a performance rating of 300 strokes per minute, the new MCF twin-rod high-speed blanking press not only offers considerably more speed, but also increases productivity. The high-speed press from Beutler Nova, the Schuler Group’s Swiss subsidiary, also features a special slide guiding system which guarantees a high degree of precision during blanking.

“We’ve already designed and built numerous customised cells with similar performance specifications on request. We have now taken the decisive next step: with immediate effect, we are offering fast blanking cells with up to 300 strokes per minute and press forces of 630 kN to 2,000 kN,” explained Benedikt Schenker, Technical Director of Beutler Nova. “The new high-speed blanking presses are particularly suitable for the production of precise parts with a high cutting accuracy”.

The slide’s pre-loaded and play-free roller bearing guiding guarantees a high degree of precision during blanking. This enables the production of small parts with tight die clearances and a wide variety of parts in quick succession.

## NEW PRESS GENERATION FOR LAMINATION PRODUCTION

The need for greater energy efficiency and the search for alternative energy sources and drives are currently among the key innovation drivers in industry. European countries have created the political framework for this drive with such measures as the obligation to reduce energy consumption by 20 per cent by 2020.

As a consequence of the eco-design guideline, the EU has issued a series of decrees with binding regulations for specific product groups. As of June 2011, for example, manufacturers of motors are only allowed to offer highly efficient asynchronous AC motors in the performance range 0.75 kilowatt (kW) to 375 kW. These regulations create direct impact on the production of electric motor laminations. With its new generation of high-speed presses, Schuler now offers machines for the production of laminations which are geared to these requirements. The new Smartline presses promise maximum productivity and part quality with greatly enlarged die areas and much lower die wear.

According to Hans Dieter Jung, Divisional Manager of the Drives & Generators market segment of Schuler Presses, manufacturers currently face three major challenges.

And, finally, cost pressure will remain high as international competition becomes even fiercer. As machine manufacturers, we need to face these changes and deliver compelling solutions which help our customers compete successfully on the market.

The new line is expected to achieve 10 to 30 per cent increases in output while at the same time offering much larger die-areas with bed lengths of up to 3,300 millimetres. Only such large die-areas make it possible to use correspondingly large-scale progressive dies, which in turn enable the production of electrical motor laminations with much more complex geometries.

The service life of a die has a direct impact on profitability. A particular benefit of the new press generation is its patented depth penetration control. This regulates the penetration depth of the upper die with a precision of 1/100 millimetres.

The new feature enables the mechanical interlocking of extremely thin sheet metal, as well as being more gentle on the die. Users can also raise production speeds with the aid of an interlock control which is integrated into the press control system. This is made possible by a new drive concept for the rotation of punches and matrices. ■
