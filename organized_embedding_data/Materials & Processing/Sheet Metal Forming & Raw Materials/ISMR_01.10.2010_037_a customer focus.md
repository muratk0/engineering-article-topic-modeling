# A CUSTOMER FOCUS

[TARİH: 01.10.2010 ISMR]

Belgian specialist, Soenen NV, discusses changing coil processing customer and technology requirements with ISMR

Soenen NV, based in Belgium, builds tailor-made machines for the sheet metal industry and has a turnover of 35 million euros. The company exports 95% of its products and has placed a strong focus on emerging markets. ISMR spoke to its sales manager, Philip Quintijn.

PQ: Soenen has four large product groups - perforating presses, punching lines for truck chassis manufacturing, cutting and stacking lines for the electric industry and slitting lines for silicon steel. However, any tailormade project for the sheet metal industry could attract our attention. Two years ago, we did a special project for RFC, a US rollforming company that required a special installation for welding titanium profiles that are used as seat racks (rails) in the new A380 from Boeing.

Our biggest strength is that we are able to deliver very solid machines that feature high speed, high accuracy and high availability – for example, our perforating presses offer press forces of up to 600T with feeding accuracies of up to 0.02mm and speeds of up to 800SPM.

IMSR: How has the global economic downturn affected Soenen as well as the rollforming/sheet metal market and sales of rollforming/coil processing systems in your view? Has cutting cost become more of an issue for sheet metal users? How do you see the future?

PQ: There was a small dip just before our summer holidays but today orders are coming back in as usual. I think we were lucky that, when the crisis hit, Soenen was processing many orders from the previous year. There was an especially large demand for punching lines for truck chassis manufacturing during that period.

We are less familiar with the rollforming industry but I know that our first Quickstack customer has put a stop to further investments this year and maybe next year too. This is probably the main reason why response was not as forecasted.
