ISMR: PLEASE OUTLINE ANY RECENT SIA SUCCESSES AND ACHIEVEMENTS?

WS We have been very active in combating Australia’s skills shortages. Eleven years ago, the inaugural SIA NEW Apprentice of the Year Awards were held to recognise the achievements of our best apprentices and to demonstrate to parents and school leavers that the sheet metal trade is an attractive choice.

To expose our industry to schools and the general public, seven years ago we introduced a sculpture competition for school-aged students. The main criterion is that the sculpture be made predominantly from metal. The sculpture competition has grown rapidly in status and now attracts entries from all over NSW. In celebration of Australian manufacturing, the SIA awards and the outstanding achievements of the award finalists, we hold a formal Industry Gala Dinner. This industry night now attracts over 250 guests annually.

ISMR: WHAT ARE YOUR VIEWS ON THE CURRENT BUSINESS CLIMATE? HOW IS THE SHEET METAL BUSINESS IN AUSTRALIA?

WS The overall current business climate in Australia is mixed. It has been all over the place, and is keeping our finest economists guessing. Some market segments are very strong and others are struggling.

We are optimistic about the next two quarters – so far, we have weathered the economic storm well. We just have to keep Australian businesses and consumers buying Australian-manufactured products.

ISMR: WHICH PARTICULAR MARKETS AND SECTORS (I.E. AUTOMOTIVE, DEFENCE ETC.) DO YOU FEEL OFFER THE MOST POTENTIAL FOR GROWTH FOR SHEET METAL MANUFACTURERS AND WHY?

WS Our sheet metal Industry is involved across all market segments. Mining and commercial markets are strong for most of the industry to supply and provide services. The main reason for this is because we are in a mining boom.

The one industry the sheet metal fraternity is mostly aligned with is the technology market sector, where our services are sought for products such as switchboards, cabinets etc.

ISMR: WHICH MARKET AND TECHNOLOGICAL TRENDS DO YOU SEE DEVELOPING IN DIFFERENT MARKET SECTORS FOR SHEET METAL AND WHERE DO YOU SEE THE GREATEST OPPORTUNITIES?

WS Our industry has to be quick in reacting to different market trends and emerging markets. Most of our members are very lean businesses that can take advantage of any opportunities arising.

The sheet metal manufacturing industry has advanced rapidly over the past twenty years. With the evolution of smart technology and automation in all facets of this exciting industry, the challenges for industry participants and educators have become significant. Flexible manufacturing techniques offered by manufacturers enable them to service industries such as aerospace, telecommunications, gaming, hospitality, electronics, air conditioning, electrical, lighting and medical. Computer Numerically Controlled services include punching, laser cutting, folding and robot welding as well as high-end technologies for spot welding, design engineering, mechanical assembly, computer aided design and 3D modelling, powder coating and any type of metal finishing.

ISMR: WHAT IS YOUR STRATEGIC AND TECHNICAL FOCUS THIS YEAR?

WS The major challenge for our industry is the new Carbon tax that our Federal government is currently debating. We have been lobbying our Federal and State parliamentarians from both sides of the house - the question is, will they listen to our arguments?

The sheet metal industry in Australia, like all manufacturers, has similar challenges after the GFC “How do we maintain our service level to our clients with increasing costs?”

The association meets about ten times per year on a monthly basis. Guest speakers often address our meetings on a range of issues including the state and forecast of the economy, OH&S and emerging technologies. Site visits to various suppliers and industries have also been a feature of our meetings.

ISMR: HOW DO YOU SEE THE SIA DEVELOPING OVER THE NEXT FEW YEARS? ARE THERE ANY MARKET OR TECHNOLOGICAL TRENDS THAT WILL AFFECT THIS?

WS All of our members have to keep abreast of new technology and we will survive if we concentrate on tomorrow’s products and not try to compete with high volume, low cost manufactured products from overseas. Our focus is innovation and quality to be a step ahead of our competition.

ISMR: YOU RECENTLY HELD YOUR AWARDS EVENT – CAN YOU GIVE US SOME DETAIL ON THIS?

WS We had our annual SIA Gala dinner where we celebrated our new potential entrepreneur with our Apprentice of the Year. We also held our Annual Secondary Schools Sculpture Competition. It was a fantastic evening and a wonderful success.

The annual industry Gala dinner was held at Doltone House in Darling Harbour, Sydney on 3 June 2011. Over 250 industry members and guests were joined by the New South Wales Premier, Barry O’Farrell.

The 2011 Art of Sheet Metal Sculpture Exhibition was held at the MLC Centre in Sydney for one week prior to the sculpture launch held on Friday 3 June 2011. The sculpture launch was held at the NSW Trade and Investment Centre in Sydney and was officially opened by Pam Christie, Acting Director-General, Department of Education and Communities.

ISMR: ARE THERE ANY INTERNATIONAL REGIONS OF GROWTH FOR SHEET METAL MANUFACTURERS THAT YOU WOULD LIKE TO HIGHLIGHT?

WS This is a hard one to answer in that we see our immediate region as potential markets for our innovative products but are mindful of the competitive nature of the region with one of our major trading partners being China, which is literally on our doorstep.

So, we will keep doing what we do very well, innovate and provide a superior service to our valued clients on time and safely. Our industry in Australia is very safety focused; we do not want any LTI, MTI or first-aid injuries.

ISMR: IS THERE ANYTHING ELSE YOU WISH TO ADD?

WS Yes, I would like to remind your readers to support your local manufacturers. No matter where you are, a strong manufacturing base will give you a stronger economy and high levels of employment.

ISMR: THANK YOU VERY MUCH.
