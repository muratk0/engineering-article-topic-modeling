# THE RIGHT PROFILE

[TARİH: 01.01.2014 ISMR]

Roll forming is a continuous bending operation in which a long strip of sheet metal is passed through sets of rollers mounted on consecutive stands, each set performing only an incremental part of the bend, until the desired cross-section profile is obtained. Roll forming is ideal for producing constant-profile parts with long lengths, in large quantities.

Roll formed sections can have advantages over extrusions of a similar shape. Roll formed parts may be much lighter, with thinner walls possible than those produced in the extrusion process, and stronger, having been work-hardened in a cold state. Parts can be made with either a finish or already painted. In addition, the roll forming process is more rapid and uses less energy than extrusion.

## On a roll

German specialist ARKU offers a broad range of levelers – from precision levelers for parts and sheets to high-capacity levelers for coiled material. Its levelers are integrated into turnkey press feed systems, coil preparation lines for roll forming and cut-to-length lines.

ARKU recently showcased a newly developed plate leveller at BlechExpo 2013 in Stuttgart. The PlateMaster® has been designed to level large-surface sheet metal (widths of up to 2000mm) and thick plates (thicknesses of up to 50mm) economically. The FlatMaster® 55 precision leveler and the ServoMaster® HiSpeed high-performance feeder were also on display at its booth in Stuttgart.

Davi has, for many years, been the largest manufacturer of heavy plate rolling systems. At the BlechExpo 2013 exhibition in Stuttgart, it showed what can be built with its machines, displaying a cold formed small diameter cylinder of 175mm thick (only a very few plate rolls in the world are capable of this). Samples of large-sized sections were on display at its booth to demonstrate the capabilities of its angle roll machines.

Longitudinal members, bumpers, side impact protection, crash tubes plus many other automotive parts can now be economically manufactured from ultra-high-strength steels, with strengths of up to 1,500 MPa, on roll forming systems whilst saving weight.

German roll forming specialist Dreistern has now developed special forming and straightening processes for these components. In addition, it is possible to make specific changes to particularly critical individual component dimensions in the event of dimensional variations.

“Thanks to their high production speeds, roll forming systems are often not fully utilised with a single product. It is therefore appropriate to manufacture several different products on one system. This is not a problem if the system was designed for different products from the start. However, this is not the case if a system is to be subsequently set up for a new product. This is because, as a rule, additional processes such as perforating, punching, embossing, bending or welding must also be integrated. Often systems are not prepared for the additional requirements and making changes is expensive and time-consuming,” Dreistern told ISMR.

A new technology from Dreistern, multifunctional roll forming technology, now offers a solution to this problem. It is based on standardised processing modules for punching, bending or welding that can be quickly and easily integrated at any point on the roll forming machine. As a result, it is as quick and straightforward to change such processing modules as to change quick-change roll forming tools. Thanks to universal compatibility and the flexible control unit, individual processing modules can also be used on different machines. The pre-requisites for operation with a cost-effective product mix are therefore met.

“These new developments in forming and machine tool technology on roll forming systems open up further opportunities for saving weight on bodywork components by using extremely high-strength materials. High process reliability and optimal utilisation of the systems, thanks to operations using a product mix, make roll forming an even more attractive alternative for a series of bodywork components,” concluded Dreistern.

UK manufacturer Formit Ltd has processed orders for design and manufacture of tooling for complex roll formed profiles. Leading subcontract section manufacturers are increasingly turning to the company to design and manufacture tooling for more intricate profiles. It has supplied tooling and roll forming equipment for many industries including construction, automotive, heating and ventilation, aerospace and increasingly “green” projects in line with current government initiatives. These include solar panels, double glazing and insulated cladding etc.

Past contracts have included an innovative lock-seamed tube production line with automated inline powder infill, a suspended ceiling Tee grid section and a specialised roll former producing three products on the same machine with circling facility.

Italian specialist Gasparini recently supplied BW Industries with a new roll forming machine to significantly increase throughput for the company’s product range of up to 500mm x 6mm sections and the automatic changeover has also minimised downtime, resulting in further savings. The line can now produce plain and lipped channel sections, in sizes 100mm x 2mm thick to 500mm x 6mm thick, with flying shear and punching in one operation.

“Production analysis has shown that tooling changeover times from section to section have been reduced from 1½ – 2 hours to some 10 minutes. The production throughput has increased by 72.4 per cent because of the increased speed of changeover, production speed and online punching,” it told ISMR.

Coil specialist IRON SpA of Mareno di Piave has manufactured a new FMS flexible system, Punchpress & Bender, for stamping and bending coil. IRON’s four products include its cut-to-length lines, coil feeding lines for mechanical presses, punching and bending line from coil and laser cutting from coil line.

Roller King in Taiwan produces customised roll forming machines for different sections and shapes. The machines can make in-line hole punching, welding, forming and product length is not limited. The roll forming products have wide applications in architecture, automotive, decoration, appliance and furniture markets.

Meanwhile, Italian manufacturer STAM has designed and built the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses up to 7mm. STAM’s new roll former is able to roll form pieces of different shapes and thicknesses. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength. The success of the project, followed by the positive testing of the line, was a rewarding way to celebrate 50 years of STAM activities. STAM has delivered and installed over 1000 other machines and lines.

The new roll former has more than 100 dynamic axes which are constantly changing position during the roll forming operation as well as over 40 fixed axes which are positioned according to the profile to be produced and will constantly be maintained in position during the process. Each axis is driven by servo-motors, with position control in a closed loop, with each axis moving in a different manner to the other axes depending upon its physical position along the machine.

Roll forming technology will continue to evolve as machine tools become more capable to meet more exacting production requirements. ■
