Fabricating line allows on-site silo production

MG Srl, together with Ariostea MHS Srl, has built a line for the production of silos at the operational site. The traditional method is to build a silo in sections, transport the sections to the final location, and then assemble and weld them together. With the new system, built for a Japanese company, silos can be made and assembled on-site, eliminating preassembly, disassembly, and transport costs.

Placed on a base with dimensions suitable for transport on a standard truck, the line comprises three MG machines: a leveling machine, a forming machine, and a plate rolling machine. The material first runs through the leveler, where it is straightened. The forming machine then folds the material’s edges to create angles, which are welded in a later step. Finally, the material goes through the plate rolling machine, which creates the bending radius. An advanced radius control system from Delsy is mounted on the exit side of the plate rolling machine.

Ariostea technology then is used to weld the spiral material at every turn to obtain the desired height. The silo then is anchored to the ground, the bottom part inserted, and the upper cap assembled.

Kits of round punches, dies available for ironworkers

American Punch Co. has introduced Punch Paks, kits comprised of up to 17 pairs of round punches and dies in the most common incremental sizes, depending on the customer’s ironworker. Multiple kit sizes are available.

The kits allow fabricators to order a variety of round tooling styles and sizes at a discounted price to have extra tooling on hand, the company reports. They come in a durable utility box with a sample of the company’s Punch Lube to help extend tooling life. Two reference magnets for determining typical die clearance and required tonnage for punching mild steel also are included.

Laser automation system controls entire production process

Mitsubishi Electric Corp., an established MC Machinery automation solutions partner, has teamed up with ASTES4 to offer its laser automation system. This multifunctional, fully integrated system can control the entire production process from loading, cutting, sorting, and unloading to the storage of finished parts.

The system uses four cartesian grippers for loading, unloading, and sorting. Each gripper can use vacuum or magnetic tools in a variety of shapes and sizes to produce a more effective sorting speed. The process sorts the parts into kits, and its variety of options allows for different material thicknesses to be kitted on the same pallet. Downstream processes are taken into account to provide seamless integration into the production process.

Fiber laser delivers 12 kW of fiber power

The ByStar Fiber from Bystronic has been enhanced with a 12-kW fiber laser and a newly designed cutting head that enables the BeamShaper option to help ensure consistent cutting quality on various material qualities up to 1.125 in.

With the 12-kW laser, the system’s cutting speeds increase up to 20% on average (when laser cutting with nitrogen) compared to the previously available 10-kW laser source. This increases productivity throughout the range of sheet thicknesses from 0.125 to 1.125 in., the manufacturer reports.

A slimmer design for the new cutting head decreases the possibility of contact with cut features during the cutting process. In addition, the company has reduced the number of different components and accommodated important technical functions in the interior of the cutting head. The integrated technology is protected from contamination, such as cutting dust, which helps decrease maintenance and operating costs, states the company.
