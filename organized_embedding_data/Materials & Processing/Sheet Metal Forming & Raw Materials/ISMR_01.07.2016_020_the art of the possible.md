# THE ART OF THE POSSIBLE

[TARİH: 01.08.2016 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

"HFQ® is a hot forming and die quenching method for sheet aluminium which enables complex, deep drawn forms to be produced"

Dr. Alistair Foster, CTO of Impression Technologies, discusses the new HFQ® aluminium sheet forming process for lightweight part production.

The new patented Hot Form Quench (HFQ®) technology, developed by Impression Technologies & Imperial College, is a hot forming and die quenching method for sheet aluminium. The forming technology enables complex, deep drawn forms to be produced with excellent material properties. A post-forming heat ageing process can be used to further increase material strength. Impression Technologies started trading in January 2013. It was formed to take to market the ideas and technologies developed at Imperial College London's Engineering Faculty.

ISMR spoke with Dr. Alistair Foster, Chief Technical Officer, Impression Technologies, about the new HFQ® aluminium forming process which has ramifications for automotive OEMs wishing to further 'lightweight' their vehicles. Alistair, a chartered mechanical engineer who most recently held design analysis and work management roles in the rocket propulsion and nuclear energy sectors, worked on the original HFQ® concepts and is a named inventor on HFQ-related patents. He holds a PhD from the University of Birmingham.

ISMR: What is the HFQ process and its benefits? What does it mean for automotive OEM and Tier One manufacturers moving forward?

AF: The new method, the Solution Heat treatment, Forming and in-die Quenching process (HFQ®) process, was developed at the

Universities of Imperial College London (ICL) and Birmingham (UK) and is now being industrialised in LoCoLite, an EU FP7 project (GA 604240). The new process allows high-strength aluminium alloys to be formed into complex geometries through increased formability with deeper draw, sharper bend radii and almost total elimination of spring-back. These are significant advantages compared to traditional stamping processes. These qualities are of major interest for the automotive industry and give the technology a large potential market. We license HFQ® technology to automotive Tier One manufacturers to press their parts/components. The technology is also suitable for the rail, transport and aerospace sectors.

OEMs are challenged with moving to cost-effective lightweight vehicles but they need to maintain or even improve the safety response of their products. They are therefore torn between adding mass to improve the structural capabilities/crash performance of their vehicles and developing a lightweight structure which is efficient. Aluminium has been very successful in 'lightweighting' the next generation of vehicles but it is not an easy material to work with as it is not ductile. The sort of shapes that are feasible for manufacturing, especially from high-strength grades, are nothing like the ones achievable from steel. However, it's still a worthy material for the automotive industry (e.g. Ford and JLR are really pushing the boundaries of what aluminium can achieve at the moment).

Dr Alistair Foster, CTO, Impression Technologies

The next stage was to develop a technology which gives aluminium some of the capability that clients are used to with steel processing i.e. tighter bend radii and deep draw capability on high-strength grades for high levels of part integration. This means that, with HFQ®, parts that have to be split up into sub-assemblies can be produced as a single pressing because the process enables far more complexity, with fewer press tools and associated operations.

HFQ® is a hot forming aluminium production system and provides geometry to the components whilst simultaneously tempering the material. We use 6000 series aluminium (widely used in the automotive sector) and 7000 series aluminium.

ISMR: Please outline any recent successes/achievements/awards and the reasons for this?

AF: Recently, when Aston Martin launched the DB11, it chose our HFQ® technology for the production of key structural pressings. The Lotus Evora 400R model also uses HFQ® technology. Having UK customers that are so proud of the technology, at such an early stage, is fantastic for us.

We are also delighted to say that, at our new facility in Coventry, the world's first dedicated production line for HFQ® is now up and running (using AP&T equipment). It is producing production components and helping us to further develop the technology and introduce it to potential licensees.

ISMR: Please give a brief background to Impression Technologies and explain your mission and objectives?

AF: We started 3.5 years ago as a spin-out company from Imperial College - the whole idea was to commercialise HFQ® technology. HFQ® has been developed within Imperial for the past 10 years with initial research done at the University of Birmingham. The technology showed enormous potential for vehicle lightweighting. We are now a team of 20, based in Coventry.

Governments around the world, at the time, were realising that the automotive industry needed to significantly lower its emissions. The timing for the spin-out was excellent, with several factors converging. Automotive OEMs were looking for a disruptive technology that would cost-effectively enable lightweighting of their vehicle structures.

We worked closely with a Tier One manufacturer in Coventry to develop the commercial side of this process. UK car manufacturers have taken a very early interest in developing HFQ® through to production and we are very grateful for that.

ISMR: Which issues are of prime importance for your customers at the moment and how are you addressing these issues for them?

AF: This technology really shines at making a car lighter and less expensive. We can do this in areas where high-strength HFQ® pressings can be used to delete extra reinforcements of lower-strength aluminium pressings. The technology also provides accurate part geometry so that there is little or no part spring-back. The amount of spring-back we see is much lower because we form at high temperatures.

Also, when an aluminium part is formed using conventional cold forming, the part work hardens (as part of the forming process) so, although you can have complex part geometry, you have already used up most of the ductility of the material during the forming process. With HFQ®, because we are tempering at the same time as forming at hot temperatures, the remaining ductility of the material is far higher. This is a significant development for parts within a vehicle structure which need to effectively absorb crash energy in an impact.

HFQ® allows some of our customers to remove steel from their structures - others can make panels that are super lightweight or just have a single part, rather than a multi-piece assembly, which considerably reduces their tooling and production costs. Designers are using HFQ® to reduce costs and weight as well as reduce the number of joining operations. It has been very illuminating to work with automotive Tier One and OEM designers on this technology.

ISMR: What is your strategic and technical focus/vision over the next few years?

AF: Now that the technology has been proved for automotive markets, our goal is to work with customers in the development phases and move them up into production. We are working with different OEMs across the world on this (US, China, Europe, Japan etc.). They are all at different stages of engagement with the technology.

The challenge is having a technology that is a step change to the market - it is a disruptive technology and we have to guide people through its introduction. For example, we work with Tier Ones on the production and OEMs for the design. OEMs and Tier Ones work well together. We are licensing the technology to OEMs, but mainly Tier One manufacturers, to use this on their production lines. We have software partners in the project as well as our own developers.

We also know that there is more that can be done with the technology, with an increasing array of applications including the rail and aerospace sectors. The LoCoLite grant (European-funded) project has further developed the technology and we are working with ESI (which creates PAM STAMP) on the development of the sheet metal forming modelling capabilities and the project will be completed later this year.

As well as European LoCoLite funding, we have Innovate UK funding too. Our internal R&D is focused on the modelling of the process for OEMs as well as every aspect of production (e.g. the production line with AP&T, tooling etc.). We also work with aluminium producers, as the HFQ® process enables high- and ultra-high strength grades to be formed that cannot be formed conventionally, which opens up some very significant new markets for our aluminium manufacturing partners.
