Thermal spray process provides corrosion protection for One World Trade Center spire

Situation

The 408-ft.-high, 20-ft.-dia., 17-section spire of One World Trade Center required corrosion protection. The owner of the spire, the Port Authority of New York, planned to have the high-strength steel galvanized to protect it from corrosion.

Drytec Trans-Canada, which has worked with the Port Authority of New York in the past on other parts of the Freedom Tower, was contracted for this one-year project. After contacting many galvanizers in North America, Drytec could find none willing and able to accomplish the task. The large size of the spire pieces, their varying thicknesses, and required double-dipping eliminated galvanizing as an option.

After meeting with the Port Authority to determine their requirements and discuss options, Drytec suggested painting or metallizing, which the Port Authority rejected. It was a three-coat system comprising zinc primer, epoxy, and urethane. Since the spire was to be instrumental in the use of satellites, the Port Authority required pure zinc for conductivity and a dry film thickness (DFT) of 10 to 20 mil. This could not be achieved with paint.

Resolution

Drytec then demonstrated flame spray technology to the Port Authority. The company spent about a year testing flame spray equipment across the U.S., finally achieving success with the 16E arc wire combustion spray gun manufactured by Oerlikon Metco. The use of the gun to apply the zinc material allowed the company to meet the required blast and thermal spray specifications, SSPC SP10 and SSPC-CS23, as well as the Port Authority’s adhesion test requirements, ISO 2063, and DFT of 10 to 20 mils.

Each of the 17 sections of the spire comprised 12 pipes that interlaced, creating pockets that were hard to reach. For those areas, Oerlikon Metco’s Angular Cap XT6-18T spraying 45-degree extension poles was used. For pockets that were impossible to reach, special epoxy putties were applied and sprayed over with cold galvanizing.

There was also a very short window between the time of blasting and the application of the zinc coating. High-strength steel corrodes very quickly, so coating had to be applied within four hours, before the onset of corrosion.

KTA-Tator, a U.S. inspection firm, conducted tests and inspections before the start of each shift; testing took about two hours before application could begin. About 11 ft. of the antenna could be blasted and thermally spray coated or final coated within an eight-hour shift.

The first six sections were too big and heavy to be transported, so Drytec rented a hangar near the port to do the final metallizing. The company built each of these sections in three parts in its fabrication shop in Terrebonne, Canada, and then shipped them to the port, where they were welded together.

The application of the zinc corrosion coating required the use of three of the wire combustion spray guns at the same time. The company kept six on hand at all times to allow for gun maintenance. Each gun was certified and qualified in writing before each shift. The company used 25,000 lbs. of zinc wire to complete the application.

Laser tracker calibrates rolling mill for steel plate production

Situation

Handan Iron & Steel Group (Han Steel), Handan city, Hebei province, China, can produce up to 13 million tons of steel annually. To maintain quality control in the production process, the company must ensure proper calibration of its rolling mill machines.

After the steel ingots leave the smelt plant, they are rolled by the rolling mill machines to complete the production process. A bearing provides and controls the pressure, while two rollers roll simultaneously. After passing through the rolling mill machine, the steel ingots form into a steel plate, cool down, and become the finished product.

Because the rollers are under constant stress, the position between the rollers and the roller housing can deviate after long periods of use. Over time the surfaces of the rollers lose their flatness, leading to the production of uneven steel plates.

Resolution

Han Steel chose to use the Radian laser tracker from API, combined with software, to resolve the problem. Using the laser tracker, the company recorded multiple reference points on the roller and roller housing and used those reference points to create a standard position relationship between them.

The company then used the software to analyze the standard position relationship to figure out the value that required adjustment. This value was key in guiding Han Steel’s engineers in adjusting the roller’s position. To resolve the roller’s surface flatness problem, the laser tracker collected several reference points on the surface of the roller, which were used to make a standard cylinder in the software. The company then compared the corresponding actual value with the standard value to determine exactly what needed to be fixed or maintained.

The laser tracker withstands harsh working environments. Water poured on the hot steel during processing creates large amounts of steam inside the plant. The tracker can work in 100 percent humidity (no condensing).
