# MAKING ITS MARK

[TARİH: 01.09.2016 ISMR]

We highlight a selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing technologies and innovations

Stamping uses a moving ram to transmit force, or an amount of tonnage, to specific tooling to achieve a product's final shape, often with little or no scrap and, whenever possible, with minimal operator intervention. A wide variety of parts can be formed in different press sizes and types. Stamping (also known as pressing) is the process of placing flat sheet metal, in either blank or coil form, into a stamping press where a tool and die surface forms the metal into a net shape.

Stamping includes a variety of sheet metal forming manufacturing processes such as blanking; punching; piercing; bend/draw forming; embossing; flanging and coining. This could be a single-stage operation, where every stroke of the press produces the desired form on the sheet metal part, or could occur through a series of stages. A wide range of materials, including stainless and cold/hot-rolled steel; hardened and tempered steel; aluminium; copper; titanium and specialty alloys, can be processed.

AIDA EUROPE GmbH will have sales and engineering functions, as well as project management, purchasing and other activities. The technology enables parts to be manufactured that have low-weight, high-strength, complex shapes and high dimensional stability in one single step. CIPCO, a 100% subsidiary of Impression Technologies Ltd (ITL), is the purchaser.

AP&T is also now launching a new generation of its Multi Layer Furnace for press hardened vehicle parts. The new generation furnace features improved process stability and availability. The press manufacturer has also developed a new method, with Sandvik and Cell Impact, for the cost-efficient manufacturing of fuel cell plates. At Blechexpo last November, the company focused on lightweight forming – the warm forming of aluminium (fuel cell lines that it believes will grow in the medium term). AP&T now offers automation equipment from NORDA through AP&T in Germany and Poland.

At the FABTECH trade show in November 2015, Otto Bihler Maschinenfabrik presented its new BIMERIC servo production and assembly system. The BIMERIC is designed to be a platform for flexible, productive and cost-efficient sub-assembly production for stamping and forming as well as assembly technology.

Investment in a new high-speed Bruderer BSTA 500-110 precision press is already paying dividends for European Springs & Pressings Ltd, which provides springs, wire forms, pressings and assemblies to a global client base. The acquisition gives it a much larger press bed area (1.1 metres) for multiprogression tooling of complex processes and the high precision construction of the machine offers reduced die wear, especially on high-tensile spring quality materials. The latest B control system of the press has also reduced set-up times, which shortens time to market on new products and improves efficiency on small volume production batches.

Adrian Haller, managing director of Bruderer UK, commented earlier this year: "2016 has also been a good year for our order book, with more than 15 machines sold and the UK launch of the BSTA 410-110 at the 'MACH Show' generating a great deal of interest."

A new £500,000 deal to supply a second high-speed BSV-75 precision servo stamping press to Harwin (a UK electronic component specialist) was the highlight of Bruderer UK's appearance at MACH 2016. The new BSTA 280-75 B2 press will feature Bruderer BSV-75 high-speed servo feeder technology and an integrated planetary gearbox. Just before the exhibition took place, the UK press manufacturer also announced that The Sertec Group, a manufacturer of stampings and welded assemblies, had tapped into its 'machinery' expertise to identify and purchase 12 presses to help it meet growing volume demands from many of the world's largest car-makers. The first tier supplier has committed more than £10m to the three-year modernisation programme that has seen it start to install a host of ZANI Motion Master presses (with ram velocity slow-down kinematics), ranging from 250 to 800 tonnes.

## An eye on forming

Taiwanese manufacturer Chin Fong's stamping presses range from 20-ton single crank presses and up to 2400 metric tons of capacity to 4-point eccentric gear-driven, large panel and multistage transfer presses. Forging presses are further diversified by processes of cold, warm and hot forging.

Chin Fong now has offices in Taiwan, China, Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and the USA as well as representatives in numerous countries around the world. Its A0 tandem press line for the automotive industry includes stages such as deep drawing, following by trimming, piercing etc. The basic A0 line weighs 2400 tons with 800 tons of weight for the rest of the machines. To gain ground in production efficiency, Chin Fong replaced the conventional hydraulic press with a mechanical press featuring a link drive and long stroke.

Above and right: Adrian Haller (left) of Bruderer UK with Stuart McSheehy (European Springs and Pressings)

Chin Fong's straightside double crank power press, with 400-ton capacity, has a bed area of 2.5m x 1m and can produce metal stampings from material measuring up to 400mm wide and 3.5mm thick. It has a variable speed of between 30-50 strokes per minute and a maximum shut height of 450mm.

DEES Hydraulic has successfully developed a closed loop, hydraulic, servo-controlled die spotting press. With microprecision control and energy efficiency at the top of its R&D goals, the servo system's fixed-flow control, as the core technology used in the hydraulic spotting press, can further improve the precision of the conventional design or overcome the ball-screw's wear and tear to reduce overall maintenance costs.

The Chin Fong KW2-260 press

"The energy-efficient servo motor can save up to 30% energy. Adopting a pulse wave signal handwheel jog down for micro precision of 0.01mm can prevent damage when spotting the punch and die. The closed loop, servo-hydraulic system can reach high slide repeatability levels of +/- 0.02mm," explained the manufacturer.

Group Rhodes is one of Europe's largest original equipment manufacturers in its field. The Group actively manufactures and markets over fifty basic ranges of presses/machines, and designs, develops and manufactures bespoke machinery and complete turnkey solutions. Group Rhodes is a double winner of the prestigious Queen's Award for Enterprise in recognition of its continuous development of Super Plastic Forming and Diffusion Bonding Presses (SPF/DB) specifically designed for the high temperature forming of titanium and aluminium alloys, primarily used in the aerospace and automotive industry.

A tandem press line for automotive manufacturers from DEES Hydraulic

As well as its two Wakefield sites in the UK, which offer over 18,500 sq. m. of production capability, Group Rhodes also operates divisions in Sheffield, Manchester and Bangalore (India). In April this year, Group Rhodes won a multi-million pound order from an Indian aerospace manufacturer, for a 300/600-tonne panel stretch forming machine.

"The machine is the first of its kind to offer 5-axis forming on a range of aluminium alloys. Ideally suited to produce aircraft fuselage panels up to 4 metres square and 2mm thick, stretch-formed components offer an accurate finished profile with minimal spring back," said Group Rhodes.

Haulick + Roos highlighted its new ROP 4000-3500 press (with a press force of 4000kN, installation length 3500mm) at this June's Stanztec stamping exhibition in Pforzheim. The new press features a KFS guide system, rotating stroke/pendulum stroke and switchable servo drive. The German manufacturer develops and manufactures precision stamping and forming presses.

INGYU is a precision stamping press manufacturer that has been in operation for 45 years in Taiwan. It aims to provide high-precision, high-speed presses for automotive, can-making, motor lamination and complex forming applications. INGYU will be presenting its new Saga press at the EuroBLECH 2016 exhibition in Hanover from 25 to 29 October on stand H108 in Hall 27.

"Our new Saga multi-slide motion mechanical presses enable users to produce high accuracy sheet metal parts, increase the number of products per minute and reduce their tooling cost. With the onepiece frame and rugged plunger and post guiding system, the press's vertical accuracy is within 0.01mm for extended die life and improved part quality. Slide velocity decreases towards Bottom Dead Centre (BDC) to reduce the impact of stamping, noise and vibration. Customers can choose three slide motion modes, including forming, bending and drawing, in one press to maximize productivity," explained the manufacturer.

Hydroformed parts are stronger and weigh less due to structural integrity and fewer welds on add-on pieces

Interlaken Technology, a manufacturer of production press equipment for hydroforming, elevated temperature forming and hot stamping systems, has introduced a new four-column hydroforming press system for running high volume production while reducing cycle time. Interlaken offers this new hydroforming press system in clamp forces ranging from 250 to 5000 tons.

Hydroformed parts are stronger and weigh less due to structural integrity and fewer welds or add-on pieces. In addition, costs are reduced and time is saved by eliminating secondary operations, reducing scrap, lowering material and manufacturing costs, and increasing design flexibility.

Established in 1937, Chinese pressmaker, JIER, has produced a variety of mechanical presses from C-frame to straight-sided presses (60t to 7,500t) including automated high speed press lines. It also produces transfer presses, progressive presses, cut-to-length lines and blank washers and has made use of servo press technology and NC hydraulic die cushions in its technical solutions.

Komatsu designs large presses for forming automobile roofs and doors. Its large presses are represented by a tandem press for a variety of wide applications and a transfer press for high productivity. Its hybrid AC servo press, H1F, achieves high performance by combining advanced CNC controls and a hybrid drive mechanism (pat.p). Its large high speed transfer blanking press for processing car body panels improves productivity by 75%, it told /SMR. Komatsu also provides blanking presses and coil lines.

The Taiwanese pressmaker has sold hydraulic presses directly to Ford Motor, Toyota Motor and Honda Motor.

Founded in 1962, Shieh Yih Machinery Co. Ltd. (SEYI) has established a strong position in the metal forming industry over the past 50 years. SEYI manufactures mechanical presses, ranging from 25 to 2400 tons, and servo presses, ranging from 80 to 1200 tons. SEYI now has 800 employees globally. It has direct sales sites in the United States; Germany; China; Mexico and Thailand and sales agents in 40+ countries all over the world. SEYI also has factories in Taiwan and China for the manufacturing of machine presses which range from 25 to 3,000 tons.

The company showcased its 200-ton direct drive servo press, Type SD2-200, at Blechexpo in November 2015 and demonstrated its formidable deep drawing control (one-strike operation) to /SMR at the exhibition. This advanced servo press was operating with a highly sophisticated tool to show the intricacies of its advanced deep drawing, stamping and coining operations done in one single cycle. This economy of operation is entirely due to the specially developed SEYI servo motor, motor controller and control system for fully controlled movement and speed.

"We are also demonstrating fineblanking, which is generally employed by very expensive press-makers. YIN SHEN MACHINERY CO., LTD was established in 1979, manufacturing machines for cutting, coiling and forming metal springs. The Taiwanese manufacturer has been working on press forming machines since the 1980s and has sold its high-end press forming machines to the European market. Italian press specialist, ZANI SpA, has spent over fifty years delivering over 5000 machines to five continents. It generally works on around fifteen planned projects over the course of the year, with three press types in its arsenal – the Power Master eccentric press, the Motion Master (double knucklejoint press) and the Servo Master (eccentric or knuckle joint press).

The company introduced servo press technology ten years ago and sees a strong future for it. Its 2000-30,000kN Servo Master press (eccentric or knuckle joint) features fixed or adjustable strokes (up to 800mm) and a working speed of up to 200 SPM. The press consists of four connecting rods, set in motion by four Siemens servo motors which provide great flexibility and the ability to optimise and change the cycle of the press to adapt to each die.

The company has reported strong sales in the UK market, delivering a 300-800-tonne straightside link motion mechanical press in September 2015 to a UK customer (Sertec) with transfer technology and coil lines. Sertec supplies pressings to UK-based automotive OEM, Jaguar Land Rover (JLR). Zani presses are also being supplied as part of a major new contract with Sertec (see Bruderer UK entry in this article).
