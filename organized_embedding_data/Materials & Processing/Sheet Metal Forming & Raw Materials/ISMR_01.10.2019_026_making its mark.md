## Successes and achievements

The LVD Group has celebrated a series of achievements over the past year.

"2018 was our most successful year to date. This is partly due to strong market conditions over the last couple of years as well as to our creation of a well-balanced product portfolio (including 'breakthrough products', which enable us to sell to new customers) designed to address the challenges faced by our customers," Matt Fowles, LVD Group Marketing Manager, told /SMR.

In 2017, LVD set up its first Experience Centre in Belgium (visited by /SMR last year), which not only shows LVD products and technology, but offers an inviting space for customers and business partners to connect and learn. In the last few years, several Experience Centres have been established all over the world; in South Korea, Mexico and Malaysia and, this year, in China (Huangshi, Hubei province). Other centres were updated, as in the USA (Akron – upstate New York).

In 2018 and 2019, LVD was recognised as one of Belgium's best managed companies.

"Deloitte introduced a best managed company initiative (for the strategic management of companies). Four main criteria must be met on strategy, capabilities, commitment and financials. For example, on 'strategy', they look for a formal methodology on strategy, strong governance and consistent communication within the overall organisation. Under 'capabilities', they look for innovative thinking and general innovation plus the ability to develop skills, improve efficiency and retain staff. Under 'commitment', they look for a close-knit team culture and the overall holistics of approach, succession and future planning. For financials, a strong P&L balance sheet and transparent reporting are required," explained Fowles.

LVD also recently acquired COMPAC S.r.l. of Urbino, Italy, an industrial automation solutions provider. LVD has partnered with COMPAC since 2016 to produce automatic warehouse systems for numerous LVD laser cutting equipment installations in Italy. The acquisition expands LVD's portfolio of automation systems.

LVD's Experience Centres help its customers to identify solutions to their day-to-day challenges for flat sheet and tube laser cutting machinery at a time when sheet metal fabricators are increasingly automating their production processes to improve overall efficiency.

## The key to staying agile

"There is current concern about a slight downturn in business. This is because of various factors such as the U.S.-China trade war and Brexit. However, our order intake remains still pretty healthy and confidence in our clients' manufacturing requirements is still high. Having said that, you always need to be prepared for the 'what if'.....," Matt Fowles told /SMR.

To prepare for this, LVD has a standard operating procedure; a counter-cyclical strategy, designed to maintain its agility when business conditions change. In reality, this means two things. Firstly, the huge diversity of industry in which it is involved (twelve main core industries). This is a huge benefit as not every sector will experience a downturn at the same time. LVD is also cushioned by its global presence (18 subsidiaries in 46 countries). The other core factor is its offer of a price-performance balance throughout its product range i.e. different levels of machine pricing for different levels of functionality, designed to help customers if business/market conditions change.

"We are a family-owned business, so we are agile enough to adapt to changing market conditions. In our industry, you have to be ready for changes. Enhancing the efficiency of our customers' manufacturing operations is key, especially in a downturn. Focusing on the minute detail – profitability efficiency and flow – is critical to our customers' operations. It is part of our product strategy to always offer this balanced approach to customers, but it does not stop us from continuing to bring new solutions to the marketplace and challenging conventional thinking. We need to maintain that agility," explained Matt Fowles.

Remaining close to its clients is a key strategy for LVD, which includes staying in tune with its customers' challenges and helping them to find solutions.

"It is important for us to help customers with these challenges – we want to grow with them. There are three main pillars at the core of LVD: innovation (bringing new products and ideas to the marketplace, even in downturns); agility (to markets, technology and geographical changes) and working as a team/nurturing our people/enhancing/ developing their skills to make a difference to our organisation and to our customers," added Matt Fowles.

## Pinpointing trends

LVD has always maintained that cost savings can be made during the 'Art to Part' process and these are crucial to maintain vital margins when manufacturing in either small or large batches. It has scrutinised the process in great detail and provided customers with key data to help them increase their productivity and improve manufacturing processes.

LVD built a new Experience Centre in China this year (Huang Shi, Hubei province).

## FACE TO FACE

"One of the things that has really changed dramatically over the past twenty years is the increasing focus on direct part cost," Fowles highlighted to /SMR. “In the past, people could overcome set-up and non-core processing time issues by amortising indirect costs across batch sizes. The problem now is that a lot of major OEMs are reducing stock levels because they want to use their working capital in different ways and on different things. They are putting pressure on the supply chain, asking it to supply smaller product batches over shorter periods of time. This means that subcontractors need to look at the cost of producing a part, regardless of batch size (one or 500) and they need to have an optimised direct part cost because this affects whether they make money or not."

He underlined three other trends to watch.

“Firstly, small batches, short lead times and greater part complexity is a global trend (even in China). Secondly, there is a shortage of skilled labour – this is an increasing challenge across the world. Thirdly, there is a need for people to protect their margins and optimise their direct part costs. Strong part flow, strong processes and high levels of efficiency are key. This is our ‘art to part' philosophy and the 'art to part' journey determines whether a client makes money or not – optimising their direct part costs and protecting margins," explained Fowles.

"I have also seen an increased focus on automation from customers. It is important not to 'over-automate' as this can directly load the part cost. If users get it wrong, it will be disproportional and the only way to make money will be to produce in volume. With our COMPAC acquisition, we can now offer a wider spread of automation solutions for our laser cutting and punching systems. This is not just driven by productivity, but also by efficiencies of throughput/flow. This is why we needed to expand our portfolio of automation products," he added.

"Some industries (such as transportation) are starting to bend very high-tensile strength steels. These are very challenging to bend and are quite expensive. By using this high-tensile material, manufacturers can reduce material thickness and therefore reduce weight but also maintain rigidity. We see this trend more and more on larger press brakes, which is why we deploy our awarding-winning Synchro-Form and Easy-Form® Laser adaptive bending technology on large press brakes," explained Matt Fowles.

“There are many areas of differentiation with Dyna-Cell. In the past, people have tended to 'over-automate', rather than look at the key parts of the process that really need automation. So, with Dyna-Cell design criteria, we have stipulated that the overall cell cost should not be more than twice the cost of a stand-alone Dyna-Press press brake inside the cell. So, this does not load the direct part cost and users do not need large volumes to make the process cost-effective," explained Matt Fowles.

In some automated press brake operations, users sometimes needed to allow around one hour per bend to teach a robot what to do. So, in conjunction with KUKA, LVD has developed an intelligent automatic programming wizard which uses the 10:10 rule. This means ten minutes for part import, bending and robot program generation. Ten minutes is also set aside for tool set-up and bending of the first part.

"Basically, it is art to part in 20 minutes – the benefit is 'no robot teaching'. We consider this to be a complete-game changer. We will demonstrate this technology at Blechexpo and FABTECH to our stand visitors. Dyna-Cell can also handle both manned and unmanned operations for further flexibility," added Matt Fowles.

Dyna-Cell includes a Dyna-Press 40/15 Pro high-speed, electric-drive press brake. The 40-ton press brake features a 1500mm working length and a precision five-axis back gauge. It can bend at speeds of up to 25mm per second to generate more parts per hour, at a lower cost per part. The press brake uses vertical removal tooling style for rapid tooling changeover, even of heavier tools.

LVD has also developed and patented a new universal gripper which enables users to handle different families of products, and part geometries, without having to change the gripper. It can bend on three sides of a part without regripping. Because one gripper fits all applications, production is continuous and uninterrupted.

As well as the Dyna-Cell, LVD will showcase its Industry 4.0-ready ToolCell automated tool-changing press brake, high-speed Electra 10kW fibre laser cutting machine and CADMAN® software at Blechexpo (Hall 1, stand 1713) and at FABTECH in Chicago (booth A2140).

LVD's ToolCell automated tool-changing press brake is designed to maximise bending productivity and throughput with an integrated storage of tooling, housed within the machine. The on-board patented Easy-Form® Laser adaptive bending system provides in-process angle monitoring and correction, which guarantees the desired angle from the first bending operation.

The Electra FL with high-power 10kW fibre laser is a high-speed laser cutting machine for ferrous and non-ferrous metals. The machine gantry features a linear drive motor for extremely high dynamics and 2G acceleration while cutting.

Software also affects throughput – it is a vital part of the process. LVD's CADMAN® software makes the smart factory possible, enabling a constant flow of data between machines and software.

Laser power continues to increase in laser cutting and laser head technologies try to keep up. LVD has invested in developing new application technologies to address new material types and thicknesses that come with these higher powers.

## A strategic focus

Industry 4.0 arrived with a fanfare around six years ago, but many smaller companies assumed that it would only be relevant for larger sheet metal manufacturers. However, LVD points out that more and more of its customers are now realising that Industry 4.0 is actually for them. This has become a key strategic focus for the Group.

"We always try to demonstrate to customers how the principles of Industry 4.0 can really add value to customers' businesses. That is how we see things continuing to evolve; using that data, and that centralised database, to be able to see what is happening on the machine as well as with process, work orders etc. It can be a major differentiator to whether you can optimise your flow and, ultimately, maximise your profitability," Fowles told ISMR.

“In combination with this, we also look at the process level. Just because things have been done a certain way, it does not mean that there isn't a better way of doing them... How can we improve machine functionality and the user experience? Traditional sheetmetal workers are becoming scarce so you have to put more intelligence into software and controls. You also have to try to combat the issues of process controls and quality management in-process. So, the evolution of technologies, such as Easy-Form® Laser and Synchro-Form, still remain a priority for us," he added.

'Breakthrough products' such as Synchro-Form, ToolCell, Dyna-Cell and CADMAN-SDI have not only enabled LVD to sell solutions to its existing customers, but have also helped it to win market share with new customers.

"For example, our ToolCell automatic toolchanging press brake has really helped us to sell our press brake technology into companies that have not bought LVD technology before. These companies have then also bought other LVD technologies (such as laser cutting systems or CADMAN software) on the back of the ToolCell purchase.... This has not only solved the challenge of getting new accounts but has also helped us to win market share across different technologies," concluded Matt Fowles.
