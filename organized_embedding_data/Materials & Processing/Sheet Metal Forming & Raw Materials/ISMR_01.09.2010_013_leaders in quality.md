“Bill Gaskin, President of the Precision Metalforming Association, sees the start of a tentative recovery in US metal forming markets”

ISMR speaks to Bill Gaskin, PMA President, on his view of metal forming markets, trends and forecasts.

ISMR: What are your thoughts on the current market for metal formers in the US, in the wake of the global economic downturn? What do you forecast for the next few months?

BG: In the US, we experienced the same situation that every manufacturing economy suffered globally – in the fourth quarter of 2008, we really started to see a substantial downturn. The financial meltdown in September 2008 was the visible start of it. However, even before that when we look at our data, we see that the typical US metal forming company began to experience a change in its pace of growth throughout 2008.

2005, 2006 and 2007 were pretty good years but the typical metal forming company’s 12-month rolling average in shipments and orders started to decline in 2008. Things really turned sour in the fourth quarter of 2008 and the first half of 2009 was pretty miserable for our members although the second half of the year was stronger. On average, the typical PMA member was off by about 37% in shipments for 2009 compared to 2008. Employment also contracted quite severely – by April 2009, 85% of our members had reduced their payrolls to some extent. Everyone was concerned about matching their costs with new sales levels and trying to drive down cost.

I’m pleased to say that 2010 has really been pretty good for most of our member companies. Beginning in July 2009, current orders rose above the 12-month average and shipments followed in August so that, for the first time in a couple of years, shipments rose above the 12-month rolling average. Since then, orders and shipments have stayed above the 12-month rolling average and now we have 12 months of orders and 11 months of shipments that are above the 12-month rolling average. This shows a significant level of recovery in our industry.
