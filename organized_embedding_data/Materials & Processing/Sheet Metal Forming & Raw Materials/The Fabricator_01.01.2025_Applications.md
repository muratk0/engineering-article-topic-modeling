Tower system creates sheet metal storage space for manufacturer’s expansion

Situation

With more than 500 employees worldwide, the Kurt J. Lesker Co. (KJLC) is a manufacturer of basic vacuum components, including flanges, fittings, pumps, fluids, valves, feedthroughs, and traps.

To continue supplying customers reliably, the Jefferson Hills, Pa., company expanded its production capacity and invested in additional processing machines. However, this expansion put the sheet metal warehouse in danger of bursting at the seams. Additional space was not available, and complications with storage and retrieval were inevitable.

The company needed a new materials handling system that could accommodate a large amount and variety of materials within a limited space.

Resolution

KJLC invested in a Kastoecostore 3.0 tower storage system, an automated unit that can store all of its sheet metal formats. Featuring a low space requirement and high storage density, the system also offers flexibility in picking up materials and short access times.

The load-handling devices are automatically loaded and unloaded, and a hoist brings them to the optimum loading/unloading height. The system’s ProControl machine control, which offers an intuitive user interface with touchscreen, allows for fast, error-free operation, according to the company.

Plate steel processor streamlines parts cleaning with automated system

Situation

Steel Mart, a processor of light and medium plate steel, is a provider of products and services to the construction machinery and waste handling industries. The company processes raw flat plates into finished, direct-to-consumer products, including parts for construction machine attachments and trenching tools, ranging from 100 to 18,000 lbs.

As the company expanded, with eight CNC tables producing parts, it faced challenges with its labor-intensive, inefficient manual cleaning processes. With every three hours of production time requiring one hour of cleaning time, the manual processes could not keep up, resulting in backlogs and increased labor costs.

Involving chisels, hammers, and hand-held deslagging machines, manual cleaning was not only time-consuming and physically demanding but also one of the least sustainable job positions at the company.

"We were using outdated methods that just couldn’t keep up with the demand," said Nick Alspach, vice president of operations. "Manual cleaning required eight people working full shifts, which was not the type of job we wanted to offer to our community. We needed a safer, cleaner, and more efficient solution."

In addition to the inefficiency, the manual cleaning process posed health risks to employees, including exposure to airborne particulates and the risk of carpal tunnel syndrome from repetitive movements. Realizing the need for a more sustainable and ergonomic solution, Steel Mart spent six months researching and discussing potential options.

Resolution

Determined to find a better solution, company management attended the FABTECH tradeshow to meet with representatives from various manufacturers and explore their offerings.

"Every salesperson we spoke with could not offer us an option for our needs, but LISSMAC’s team was different," said Alspach. "They were the only ones who seemed confident that their equipment could handle our specific needs. They instilled that confidence in us."

Glenn Miller, LISSMAC’s sales manager for the Midwest, visited Steel Mart’s facility within two weeks after the tradeshow to assess the situation firsthand. After examining the parts and discussing options with the company, he recommended the SMB-M 1500-D2 grinding and deburring machine for slag removal to streamline Steel Mart’s cleaning processes.

On the day of installation, Steel Mart’s area manager brought a machinery skid that had required six hours to clean manually. The new machine finished the job in 30 minutes, producing cleaner parts and easing downstream processes.

The ergonomic design of the machine also addressed concerns about worker health, helping to reduce the risk of carpal tunnel syndrome and improving overall air quality by capturing slag in larger chunks and incorporating magnetic collection.

"The difference was night and day," noted Alspach. "We went from struggling to keep up with manual cleaning to having a machine that not only increased our productivity but also made the workplace safer for our employees."

After installation, the drastic reduction in manual cleaning hours helped the company realize six-figure savings annually in labor costs alone. Moreover, productivity increased by 300%, allowing the manufacturer to reallocate labor resources to other critical areas of the business.

The machine’s impact extended beyond just labor and productivity improvements. The cleaner, more efficient process enhanced the overall quality of the parts produced, which in turn improved the performance of Steel Mart’s products in the field.
