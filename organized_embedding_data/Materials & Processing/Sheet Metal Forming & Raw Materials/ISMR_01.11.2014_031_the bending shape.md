# The bending shape

[TARİH: 01.11.2014 ISMR]

WEMO develops, builds and installs special machines for punching, bending, joining and processing both blanks and coil material. This year, the Dutch company celebrates its 50th anniversary. Time for a change: the second half-century started with new corporate colours: dark grey and orange.

Managing Director, Paul Hoekstra, explained: “As a proud family-owned Dutch company, we are happy to bear our national colour, orange. This also represents innovation and the most positive colour. We combine this with dark grey, the colour of steel, our core business.” Over the years, WEMO has developed dedicated and customised machine lines as well as highly flexible machines that can be used as stand-alone systems or combined into completely flexible machines. Since the early days of the company in 1964, the development and manufacture of tools has been another core task.

“We can offer clients one-stop shopping, to include full responsibility for their product design, not just by connecting separate machines, but also by fully integrating the options needed. We are excited to be celebrating our 50-year anniversary this year. Our strength is in fully automated lines with punching, bending and joining (ie clinching, welding etc.). We can combine processes to suit our customers’ wishes.”

At this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition, WEMO showcased a punching, bending and clinching line. The line produces 3-4 LED lighting boxes per minute and consists of decoiler; straightener; punching station; two longitudinal bending stations; corner bending; turning; head bending station and a corner clinching station.

The biggest line that WEMO has ever sold was a 60m line for ceiling panels (shearing, punching, bending and painting) for a US customer. It also sold a large line to a US customer five years ago to make refrigerator panels.

## A strong vision

Hoekstra’s vision is to expand the company’s turnover by 50% in the next four years through a strategy of increased market share.

“Markets are getting smaller and you have to keep innovating and developing – we put 5% of our turnover every year into R&D to stay ahead in the market,” he told ISMR. “Two years ago, we restructured our company to move from a process-oriented business to a project-orientated business. We have invested a lot of time, effort and people in smart customisation to get us on the right track. All the parts of the business are now geared towards project development (ie technical concepts for project teams to develop from the ground upwards). It keeps the focus strong. It also reduced our delivery lead times of nine months to five months, using this strategy. We need to keep increasing our productivity by 5% every year, with the same people.”

“We want to extend the output of our machine lines and their flexibility for different operations,” he added. “We also want to sell more lines into the automotive industry. Our lines can process high strength steels, which will be more accurate with bending than with roll forming, so bending has a great advantage in minimising measurement difference. China and the US are key markets for this. There is a question mark over Russia at the moment because of the Ukrainian situation. We are focusing on steel doors and frames, the elevator door industry – we want to make more of the elevator, side panels and ceiling panels (in stainless steel) etc. – and the shelving industry. Our lines can handle pre-painted, stainless steel and aluminium etc.

“We are developing a fiber laser cutting machine line prototype at the moment and should be ready to test it in the next couple of months. Laser cutting is a complementary process to punching – we have traditionally built laser processing into our lines as an integrator. We have grown with our customers – as they manufacture faster products with higher output, we have developed flexible product lines with high outputs. For example, we can make lighting boxes for LEDs in 17 seconds (20cm to 1.5m) using a mix of large and small parts on the line at our booth in Hanover. Over the years, we have designed more and more flexibility into our machines to make office furniture, elevator doors etc.

“We have key customers that we follow, for example, to countries like China. Now, we sell elevator door lines to China from sheet metal in coil or sheet, that is stamped, punched and bent on a 30m line producing left or right elevator landing doors every 8 seconds. We sell a lot of shelving, elevator door, automotive parts and steel door frame lines in China and we are looking to develop more sales in China as it is a very interesting market region with high output.”

WEMO has also sold several machines to the US, some for automotive and some for office furniture lines, and Hoekstra sees a good market for his automated lines there, particularly in the shelving industry for warehouses in the US. He intends to open an office in the US next year and is looking for an agent to represent the company there.

“People know us for dedicated machines and high output,” he explained. “The machine lines are flexible so that customers can change their product lines on the same machine. We are looking at developing a low entry machine that will use interchangeable modules so that customers can easily change their products on the machines. We have already developed the machine for different production applications but we need to work on lowering the price. It should be ready to market in the first quarter of 2015. We will still be a dedicated machine line builder but the lines will be constructed in modules.”
