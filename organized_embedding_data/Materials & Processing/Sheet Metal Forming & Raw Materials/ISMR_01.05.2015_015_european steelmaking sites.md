Three stages of levelling are used to ensure the final cut-to-length steel sheet is as flat as possible and any residual stress is removed. Levelling is followed by surface brushing, which ensures the steel is as clean as possible. These processes, combined with a supply of coil that has been transported and stored undercover, provide a more consistent surface appearance, resulting in a reduction of post-fabrication surface preparation before final painting to simplify processing and save costs.

The new facility is served by a direct rail link allowing feedstock not produced at Llanwern to be brought in directly and enabling the bulk dispatch of cut sheet to customers. The line was designed by processing machinery firm, FIMI.

## FACE TO FACE

## A tour around the line

ISMR was invited to tour the new decoiling line at Llanwern, near Newport (South Wales). The site manufactures hot rolled, cold rolled and metallic–coated strip steels. The hot-dip galvanised steel from its Zodiac line, for example, is used by BMW in the new Mini, Honda in the new Civic and Nissan in its range of vehicles. The steel is also used for the inner walls of buildings and some ends up in products from Hotpoint, Zanussi, Panasonic and Sony as casings to DVD players, washing machines etc.

“The decoiling line typically processes steel of between 3mm-16mm for the customers we supply in the lifting and excavating market in the UK and Europe. We use mainly Port Talbot feedstock for this. The line was strategically designed to be future-proof to allow us to roll wider, thicker and stronger. We also process 12mm thick Durbar rolled plate for non-slip flooring on the decoiling line. We have two decoiling lines on-site. The new FIMI decoiling line, which processes the heavier gauges, was installed last February in a building which is a third of a mile long. We hold stock/storage levels of approximately 200 coils in front of the line,” confirmed Robert Mayne, Operations Manager, Tata Steel.

The 130m long FIMI line is computer integrated with safety systems and a high level of automation. The decoiler line incorporates levellers for flattening and straightening operations. Operators enter the yield strength and material gauge and the levellers are automatically configured for consistent flattening/levelling coil to coil. The levelling is done in two stages – a straightener at the entry end and two levellers (one to handle 3mm to 12mm material) and another to handle thicker material gauges (8mm to 25mm). Typical lifting and excavating sector customers for the products from this line include Caterpillar, JCB, Volvo Construction Equipment and Terex.

“The stacker on the line can handle short and long products in two batches (in 2 x 8.5m stacker beds). From a continuous production standpoint, this is good for overall productivity and efficiency (a throughput increase of approximately 50/60 per cent). We can link those two stackers together to enable us to cut even longer lengths of product (17m) for products such as lamp posts, cranes and wind towers etc,” continued Robert Mayne.

The line also incorporates a facility to accurately trim coil width, which can eliminate secondary operations for customers and reduces their scrap levels. Brushing units on the line remove scale from the surface of the sheet to remove the need for secondary shot blasting processes, for example. A flying shear on the line, parting the plate from the coil, automatically synchronises with the line speed, then cuts and retracts. It can cut up to 25mm at 40m/minute.

The new Tata Celsius range

“Product diversification to support their growth is a key strategy for us. All our market sectors are moving forward and we have pursued a deliberate strategy of more product diversification to support their growth. Drivers include energy efficiency, high strength, safety considerations etc. The lifting and excavating sector needs high strength materials to enable construction vehicles, for example, to reach further or lift more. For machine operators, it’s all about how many tonnes per hour the excavator etc. as well as having reliable machines which can operate day in, day out under demanding conditions,” explained Dr Rachel Haigh, Product Marketing Manager, Lifting and Excavating, Tata Steel.

“Stiffness, strength and robustness are important qualities for steel in the lifting and excavating market. There have been more machine launches in the last three years in this market than in the previous ten years, because of the changes required from new engine legislation but also because of the increased number of drivers in the market.

“We supply all the major UK manufacturers in this market. The UK is the fifth largest manufacturer of construction equipment in the world. More construction machines are produced in the UK than in Germany, for example. Around 60 per cent of UK production is exported. With major projects in the pipeline, such as Crossrail and HS2, the UK construction market is looking very promising at the moment.”

The new decoiling line, in effect, has streamlined Tata’s operations, made it more productive and efficient, saving cost and adding value for customers. It has also given Tata Steel a future-proof option for new products.

“Listening to our customers, working with them and letting that drive how we act as a company (whether gearing our investments, new product development etc. to meet their needs or to help their operations to run more smoothly) is the key for us,” explained Carlo di Terlizzi, Marketing Communications Manager, Tata Steel. “We can often apply what we have learned from one part of our customer base and apply it to another. For example, we have worked for a long time with car manufacturers to optimise their use of steel. When we were approached by GKN and JCB on the re-design of the new Fastrac tractor, we used our knowledge of automotive modelling techniques to help them create a chassis structure that was as light as possible.”
