# STAM’s latest innovations include a 3D rollforming line and a revolutionary line for variable section profile production

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

Andrea Barazza, Sales Manager, STAM

STAM was established in 1963 in Ponzano Veneto (Treviso), Italy. Today, the company is a recognized European leader in the building of sheet metal working machines from coil material. The company employs 130 people and has additional facilities in Spain and Brazil. More than 1000 machines and lines have been delivered and installed worldwide.

In 2013, STAM reached a new technological frontier. It designed and built ‘the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses of up to 7mm’. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength. The success of the project, followed by the positive testing of the line, was a rewarding way to celebrate 50 years of STAM activities.

At this November’s Blechexpo exhibition in Stuttgart (Germany), ISMR sat down with STAM’s sales manager, Dr. Ing. Andrea Barazza, to unveil the Italian specialist’s latest rollforming developments.

## A focus on rollforming

“We are currently working on a new continuous variable section profile (soon to be named, 3D) rollforming machine – a new development for us,” Barazza told ISMR. This technology is very interesting in different application fields. In general, these kinds of projects require a complete production process, starting from coil or blanks, the strip/blank shaping process, 3D rollforming, pre- and post-punching and handling of the blanks/profiles throughout the process.

“STAM is a global partner able to deliver complete solutions. For example, for a customer in Germany, one particular line incorporates a laser cutting machine to process material of up to 7mm thick at working lengths of up to 30m to prepare the shaped blanks to be rollformed further with variable sections. For other customers, we are also currently delivering CNC edge trimming machines. We are also selling a lot of lines into the automotive sector which can process high- tensile steel parts (e.g. bumpers, reinforcement bars, windows frames etc.) with both open or closed (welded) sections.

“One important market requirement is for flexible rollforming machines with no toolchange needed. We built a new 16mm thickness U beam rollforming line recently, to customer specifications, not only to automatically change the geometry of the Ubeam (web, flange, thickness) but also to roll form beams with three different forming radii without changing the rolls. We believe we are a world leader in this technology.”

STAM is very prolific in the rollforming field and has built rollforming machines to make steel framing (C, U, Z, sigma profiles) for industrial and residential buildings (also called 'prefab lines’ in the market). These kinds of machines can switch production from one profile to the other, in any section, within five minutes and they feature a full automatic pre-punching unit. Integration with CAD software for steel framing design, to directly generate directly the CAM order for the machine, completes the package.

“Another interesting application is our system for the construction of grain siloes,” Barazza added. “These require Omega uprights and corrugated profiles, of up to 5mm, which are curved with the correct radius. They also require a trapezoidal roof (tapered) and flooring (perforated profiles), which can also be produced on our machines. We can therefore deliver a complete package of lines for silo production, with full automation, which can process a range of thinner and thicker profiles.

Machines feature automatic thickness adjustment and strong precision in punching and hole positioning. We have delivered several systems like this to Brazil, Russia and Spain.

“I certainly see more requirements for increased line flexibility and rapid switchover from one profile to another on a line,” Barazza told ISMR. “There is also a greater tendency towards high tensile material usage, in the automotive sector, to reduce weight. In the past, there was more of a demand for higher speeds and larger volumes of the same profile on the line. The tendency now is for rapid changeover and small batches for quick delivery….”

## Cut-to-length, slitting and levelling lines

The Italian manufacturer also produces customised slitter lines and cut-to-length lines.

STAM supplies customised lines to several industry sectors including steel mills, steel service centres, automotive, construction and agriculture. It has continuously developed its range of coil cut-to-length lines which now includes equipment for processing material up to 20mm thick as well as equipment for working high tensile steel. In co-operation with large players in the automotive market, STAM has also supplied a number of blanking lines dedicated to parts for cars.

STAM levelling machines are available as 6HI, 5HI or 4HI type, with or without interchangeable cassettes. Back-up rolls and tilting adjustments are controlled by computer and PLC.

STAM recently tested and delivered a new cut-to-length line, where cutting can be done by hydraulic shear or by a plasma torch on a robot - this last option has the goal of improving blank shape to reduce scrap levels. The line was designed for 30-ton coils of steel (up to 2000mm wide and up to 16mm thick).

STAM slitting lines are suitable for processing material of up to 2,200mm wide and thicknesses of between 0.2 and 16mm. It can supply simple lines for low production rates or more sophisticated lines with coil weights of up to 45 tons and speeds of up to 400m/min. Its slitter lines also incorporate a high degree of automation (automatic toolchange, robots to load tools, automatic tool locking, automatic positioning of separator shafts and coil-loading sequences).

At STAM, every stage of machine production is managed in-house, including mechanical, electrical and software design; building (i.e. fabricating, machining, assembling and painting); and mechanical, electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic testing with tight quality control at all stages.

## Variable section profiles

STAM designed and built ‘the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses of up to 7mm’. The variable section beams help manufacturers to improve the structure of the vehicle’s chassis by lowering the height of the web at one or both ends of the beam whilst drastically reducing production costs, since producers can skip costly processes such as welding and stamping.

“Producing these special beams, in lengths of up to 15m, using the automatic and continuous rollforming method is an enormous advantage in terms of flexibility and number of personnel employed, and consequently in quality and cost of the final product,” STAM explained.

The line can produce, in sequence, very different parts without stopping the line. The machine has more than 100 dynamic axes which are constantly changing position during the rollforming operation, as well as over 40 fixed axes.

The machine is able to roll form pieces of different shapes and thicknesses and is not linked to a fixed vehicle design. Vehicle manufacturers have various production models and various chassis and must be able to change quickly and safely from one product to another through easy programming. To do this, STAM developed a CAD/CAM system which, starting from a CAD drawing in DWG or DXF format from the customer, converts the drawing into a machine programming file. The software automatically calculates the positions of the various rollforming stands and the position of the guides while the profile is being processed in the rollforming machine.
