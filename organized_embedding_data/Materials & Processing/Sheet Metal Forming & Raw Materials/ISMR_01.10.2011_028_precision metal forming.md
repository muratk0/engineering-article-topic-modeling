Musa Saydi, sales and marketing manager, machinery, for Coşkunöz

In 1973, Coşkunöz became a joint stock company and adopted the name Coşkunoz Metal Forming and Machinery Industry Co. Ltd. Coşkunoz Metal Forming produced its first resistance welding machine in 1974 and broke new ground with its die and machinery factories with production of a sheet metal parts press, welding machines and special machinery and apparatus for the white goods and defence industries.

In 1989, the company was granted the AQAP-4 Quality System Certificate and the first export of a hydraulic and mechanical press was made to South Africa and England. Its first sheet metal forming tools were exported to the Isuzu Belford Company in England and to Opel in Germany in 1990. In 1991, exports of transportation and conservation trunks produced from aluminium alloys for the defence industry followed. In 1992, it produced its first CNC press and microprocess-controlled resistance welding machine.

The export of sheet metal parts to GM European Operations began in 1993 and, in 1995, the ISO 9002 Quality System Certificate was granted by BVQI and TSE followed by the QS 9000 and ISO 9001 Quality System Certificates in 1997. In 1999, the VDA 6.1 Quality System Certificate was granted by BVQI and “CE” conformity certificates were granted for its resistance welding machines, resistance welding machine transformers and control units in 1991.

Coşkunöz A.Ş. uses its own dies, press and welding machines to produce single parts or assemblies for the automotive sector. Sheet metal parts production is carried out by the Series Production Group, tools by the Tool Group and welding machines and press production by the Machinery Group. Today, with its SAP IT system-managed administration, TS EN ISO 9001:2000, ISO/TS 16949/2002 and ISO 14001 Environmental Quality Management certificates, Coşkunöz A.Ş. implements a policy to ensure continual improvement for its people, products and process and environmental quality.

## FLEXING NEW MUSCLES

During and in the aftermath of the global economic downturn, the group’s priorities were to economise, improve and work on creating new resources as these are critical success factors for gaining financial strength when working with low capacity and margins. It saw these as an opportunity to concentrate on areas which previously, due to workload, it had been unable to prioritise. Areas included, for example, increasing its employees’ skills, strengthening relationships with its customers, finding new customers and ways to create resources and profit, filling its capacity with more profitable business to increase turnover without investment etc.
