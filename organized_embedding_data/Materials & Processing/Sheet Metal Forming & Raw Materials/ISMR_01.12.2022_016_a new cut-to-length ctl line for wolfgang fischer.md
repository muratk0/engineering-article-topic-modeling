# A new cut-to-length (CTL) line for Wolfgang Fischer Stahl features a rewinding system that enables flat and dimensional sheets to be fabricated in small batch sizes.

[TARİH: 01.01.2022 ISMR]

Systems for the packaging of coils, small coils, slit strips and sheet must meet a range of different requirements, depending upon the material. The finishing formats, as well as the number of pieces, their size, thicknesses and possible coatings also play a role. They need to ensure, for example, particularly gentle handling of very sensitive surfaces as well as compliance with short set up times or the option to handle partial coils.

To supply its customers with smaller batch sizes individually in the future, North Rhine-Westphalia-based Wolfgang Fischer Stahl GmbH installed a new cut-to-length line from b+s Group in December 2020 as part of the expansion of its steel service centre. The line enables the flexible production of flat and dimensional sheet in any number of pieces that are not bound to the size of complete coils. Even different coating systems, e.g. for a wide range of colours, for surface protection or for storage and transport purposes, can be handled by the new highly automated cut-to-length line.

Wolfgang Fischer Stahl GmbH specialises in work processes and performance in coil-coated and surface-treated coils; small coils; slit strip; flat and dimensional plate as well as surface finishing. The company is one of the leading steel service centres throughout Europe in steel construction, building elements, roof and façade technology and building plumbing. Fischer Stahl has a storage and production facility at its Netphen site, centrally located in the border triangle of North Rhine-Westphalia, Hesse and Rhineland-Palatinate (Germany).

## Flexible processing

"When expanding its machine park, Fischer Stahl attached particular importance to the ability to supply its customers with even smaller batch sizes than before, if required," said Dr.-Ing. Lutz-Stefan Heinrich, managing director of Schnutz GmbH (which is part of the b+s Group). "It was therefore clear from the outset that the new cut-to-length line had to be able to process partial coils."

Protecting sensitive surfaces

Another decisive factor in Fischer Stahl's choice was the sensitive treatment needed for coated or pre-painted material surfaces, ensured by a special foiling system upstream and corresponding intermediate rollers in the levelling machines. A flying, high-precision slide shear also minimises the cutting tolerance.

From a coil store with three places, a coil lift truck feeds the decoiler with coils of up to 15t in weight which can be uncoiled either from above or below. If there is an intermediate paper layer in the coil, it can be removed cleanly by a corresponding rewinder. Coils can be rewound that only need to be partially processed. The coil lift truck will return these partial coils to the coil store, where they are automatically weighed.

"This means that Fischer Stahl can also assemble small batch sizes and still always know exactly which quantities are being returned to the coil warehouse," Heinrich explained.

A jig integrated into the infeed group also helps to ensure that the cut-to-length line can flexibly handle a wide range of materials. It eliminates coil curvature, especially of thick starting material, so that the line can process strips across the entire range of 0.5 to 5.0mm thicknesses.

To cover the material thickness range across the entire processing sequence, Fischer Stahl decided on a solution with two integrated highperformance levelling machines from Schnutz GmbH, which are arranged directly one behind the other.

The levelling rollers (with 70mm and 45mm diameters, respectively) are installed in a compact cassette which can be easily removed for maintenance purposes.

"Different materials require various settings of the so-called tilt, which deforms the metal in the leveller's infeed," explained Heinrich. "That's why both levellers have a special tilt adjustment system that is able to compensate for various pre-deformations."

The adjustment is made along the precision-manufactured curved sections and is characterised by its special rigidity. This ensures that the upper and lower levelling rollers are precisely aligned over the entire infeed range. To eliminate edge and centre waves in a targeted manner, the levelling roller supports (in both machines) can also be individually adjusted in a vertical direction.

The trimming shear is placed behind the infeed group to increase the cutting quality of the cut-to-length line. The precise bearing of the cutter shafts means that a bur-free cut is possible whereby the cutting gap is adjusted by axially moving the upper cutter shaft. The edge scrap of the belt, which can be trimmed up to 30mm on both sides, is shredded by a special scrap cutter which can be adjusted for different material thicknesses.

To process coated and surface-sensitive coils, a foil insertion device is placed in front of the two levellers. With their help, the upper side of the belt can be foiled before levelling and therefore protected in the subsequent process. The levellers themselves also feature special intermediate rollers that support the integrity of the surfaces during the levelling process. Behind them is a flying carriage shear which does not require an additional loop pit.

"The high-precision shear control developed by b+s Automation GmbH makes it possible to maintain low shape tolerances of the sheet at the specified belt speeds," added Heinrich. At the end of the cut-to-length line, a Burghardt + Schmidt GmbH swing-arm stacking machine ensures that sensitive material surfaces are treated particularly gently through to the final processing steps. It has two stacking positions, which can also be used together for long sheet. All stops in the stacking area are moved electrically so that there is no damage to the belt edges during de-stacking.

## Energy efficiency and automation

The hydraulic functions of the cut-to-length line are supplied with the necessary pressure and volume flow from a central power unit, with all machine groups having their own valve blocks.

For this purpose, the main drives are equipped with DC intermediate circuits via frequency converters, which enable energy exchange between the components. As soon as a drive is running generatively, it feeds energy into the DC link which is then available to the other units. In this way, a high degree of energy efficiency can be achieved for the running system. The electrical components are housed together in a walk-in and airconditioned control cabinet container so that maintenance can be carried out easily and comfortably.

The entire cut-to-length line can be controlled from one main control panel. All areas of the system can be viewed on screens. In addition, two auxiliary control panels are available. The automation has an interface to Level 3, so that fully automatic transfer of the production data and pre-setting of the system to the products are possible. The finished products are reported back to a user's PPS after processing in the cut-to-length line.

Burghardt + Schmidt GmbH was founded in 1945 and manufactures innovative machines and systems for cutting and levelling metal strip. It has many years of experience in the thin strip sector and specialises primarily in slitting lines, stretch-bend levellers, packaging lines, traverse winders, levellers, cut-to-length lines and strip processing lines.

"When developing the system, we attached great importance to relieving the operator's workload as much as possible," Heinrich concluded. "Should problems ever arise, the automation also enables full remote access by the b+s Service Centre."

Schnutz GmbH's roots go back to a blacksmith's shop founded in 1846 by Martin Schnutz, whose focus increasingly shifted to the design and construction of sheet metal working machines as the sheet metal industry in the Siegerland region grew at the turn of the 20th century. In 1882, Schnutz was manufacturing its first levellers. Its most recent innovations include the Schnutz-Multidrive multi-motor drive and a further development of the electric motor support systems. Since 2014, the company has been part of the b+s Group.

b+s Automation GmbH is a mediumsized automation specialist and was founded in 1995 as DTA Delta Technik Automation GmbH. It offers automation solutions based on the latest technology for the initial equipment of new plants and the modernisation of existing plants. Since 2018, it has been a member of the b+s group and also delivers technology for conveyor systems.
