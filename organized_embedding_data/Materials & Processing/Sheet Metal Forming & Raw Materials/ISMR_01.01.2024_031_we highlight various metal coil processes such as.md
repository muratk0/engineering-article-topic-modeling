## Coil processing in focus

In the steel industry, two key processes (cut-to-length and coil slitting), which are often done at independent steel service centres, are crucial in transforming raw steel coils into usable components for various industries.

Cut-to-length: This process involves unrolling a large coil of steel, flattening it and then cutting it into precisely measured sheets based on customer specifications. The cut-tolength method is a key method when precise, flat sheets of steel are required, making it an essential procedure for industries that require exact measurements and premium-quality steel sheets.

Coil slitting: Coil slitting is a procedure where large steel coils are cut into narrower strips. A large master coil is unrolled and passed through sharp circular blades, known as slitter knives, positioned at the desired widths. The result is multiple smaller coils, each a strip of the original coil. These slit coils, or steel strips, are often used in applications where narrower widths are required, making this process vital for a diverse range of industries.

Other coil processing procedures include recoiling lines. These start with a large 'mother coil' and are used to produce smaller coils (approximately 1-2 tons) which are used in sectors such as the construction industry. The production lines can also produce small coils ('baby' coils) with a length of approximately 30m in zinc, copper, aluminium, pre-painted steel etc. These are used, for example, to manufacture roofing parts in the civil engineering industry.

## Cut-to-length lines

Cut-to-length (CTL) lines can process different materials such as cold- or hot-rolled steel; aluminium; galvanised steel; pre-painted copper; high-strength steel; zinc; stainless steel; alloys etc. The lines can feature many different configurations, according to the type of material to be processed and the performance required. These can include start-stop cutting lines, with roller feeders; continuous cutting lines with flying shears and continuous cutting lines with rotary shears.

CTL lines can feature a range of equipment such as decoilers; levelling machines; edgetrimming machines with scrap-choppers; scrap winders or scrap wrapping machines; cutting/ rotary/flying shears; stackers and packing lines.

The lines can be fully automated throughout the entire process of unwinding, flattening, cutting and stacking, and often include the latest sensor technology to measure metal flatness and surface quality. These are technologically advanced lines that offer high levels of productivity and high-quality standards in surface finishing, flattening, cutting quality and, in some cases, seamless stacking.

Light-gauge lines can include coil cars; uncoilers; peeler tables; levelling equipment; mechanical and hydraulic shears; looping pit machinery and stacking/material handling equipment.

## The slitting process

Slitting is the process used to cut a wide coil of metal into several narrower coils. The process is primarily used to cut thin materials lengthwise and features a machine fitted with circular blades. There are two main slitting modes: straight mode slitting and free loop mode slitting.

"In straight mode slitting, the recoiler pulls the material from the uncoiler through slitters. If the uncoiler and slitter are driven only to feed the material to the recoiler, this is called pullthrough slitting. In driven slitting, the slitter is also driven during the process. In this process, the motors are synchronised to maintain a constant speed as the material moves through the line. Driven slitting allows the slitting of thinner sheets and a better edge quality for all thicknesses," explained Thyssen Krupp.

"In free loop mode slitting, the material forms a free loop between the slitter and the recoiler. A tension device is placed in front of the recoiler to ensure that the coils are properly wound. This method is recommended for poorly shaped coils. Sometimes, a temper mill or a leveller can be added to the slitting line to correct defects in the incoming coil-like buckles, thickness variations and twists. This improves the quality of the slitted strips," it added.
