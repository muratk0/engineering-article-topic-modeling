# Sheet metal component quality is key at Silberhorn Blechtechnik GmbH in Germany, with its focus on laser-cut, bent and processed parts.

[TARİH: 01.09.2021 ISMR]

ISMR SAYS:

“Newly formed Silberhorn Blechtechnik employs 85 people and uses an ARKU precision levelling system for its sheet metal operations."

Officially, Silberhorn Blechtechnik has only existed since 2016. However, the company has much more experience in sheet metal processing. Its parent company Silberhorn Maschinenbau, was founded in 1989 in a garage and began, in 1998, with sheet metal processing for its own cleaning systems.

As a second mainstay, this became the "components manufacturing" division a year later. The aim was to process sheet metal through laser cutting, bending and other processes. The division serves both the company's own cleaning technology division as well as external customers from a wide range of industries such as mechanical engineering, agricultural technology and the electronics industry.

"This division has grown successively - one machine after the other was added," Diane Silberhorn, CEO of Silberhorn Blechtechnik GmbH, told ISMR. The growth at the parent company led to the spin-off of the components manufacturing division, with a focus on laser cutting, as Silberhorn Blechtechnik

Further capacity was added to sheet metal processing with the construction of a new facility. It features 5,500 square metres of production floor space and 250 square metres of administrative floor space in Brunn, Germany. Silberhorn Blechtechnik now employs 85 people and uses an ARKU precision levelling system for its sheet metal operations.

## High degrees of automation

"The new building was a challenge - also because most of the machines were bought new," explained Diane Silberhorn. The heart of the factory is a high-bay warehouse: from there, the sheets are automatically transported to the laser cutting machines. Loading and unloading of the machines and re-storage of the cut components in the high-bay warehouse are also automated operations.

"The ideal interplay of automation and digitalisation ensures the highest efficiency of our processes," said the CEO. Yet for all her innovative spirit, she does not forget sustainability: a photovoltaic system on the roof generates environmentally-friendly electricity. The waste heat from the machines is also used for heating in winter and cooling the buildings, as well as heating water in summer.

A new FlatMaster® 88 precision leveller from ARKU, along with the new machines, has also arrived at the new facility. Even thick plates can be handled by the FlatMaster®.

"With this precision leveller, we can reduce residual stresses in the sheets. The subsequent processing steps then become more productive," explained Diane Silberhorn.

"Thermal processing with the laser introduces stresses into the sheet metal. For further processing, however, it should lie as flat as possible," added Siegfried Bayerl, the company's sales manager.

During visits to several trade fairs, management personnel from the company researched the full range of precision levellers available.

"We wanted a robust machine, made in Germany, with no long journeys for service. That's why we chose ARKU," offered the CEO. After a visit to ARKU for testing purposes, it was quickly clear that the company would choose a precision leveller from the specialist in Baden-Baden.

## A range of requirements

For the precision levelling process, the sheet metal worker receives cut blanks - from small sheets to the oversize format of 4 × 2 metres. Initially, the operator cuts them with the laser. The sheets are then deburred and levelled for further processing.

"Workpieces of various dimensions go into the precision leveller. These can be small aluminium sheets of 300mm × 300mm with a thickness of 3 to 4mm or stainless steel plates of up to 15 or 20mm thick that just fit through the machine," commented Bayerl.

"The precision levelling machine has a servo-hydraulic gap control, a hydraulic overload protection system to prevent damage to the leveller and ensures reproducible levelling results. This makes it suitable for levelling laser-cut, punched and plasma-cut parts," added Christian Nau, ARKU Sales.

In this context, levelling has proved to be very important for quality assurance.

Thermal processing with the laser introduces stresses into the sheet metal. For further processing, however it should lie as flat as possible.

"In the stainless steel sector, there are strong fluctuations in the quality of the material supplied. We don't know the cause, but there are frequent distortions (even in the same batch of material). This is difficult when you have to ensure consistent quality in production," offered production manager, Thomas Pröbster.

Silberhorn Blechtechnik does not use the FlatMaster 88 precision leveller for all its workpieces.

"It is mainly used for parts which need high precision levels in subsequent processes. This is the case, for example, with bending, welding or milling," explained Bayerl. Only a few external customers explicitly ask for levelled sheet metal. However, so many of the company's customers specify high-quality requirements for further processing that the levelling process becomes indispensable. The clientèle is very broad.

"It ranges from locksmith shops or similar small businesses to machine and equipment construction. There, we have the same requirements as for the cleaning systems of our parent company," said Bayerl, noting "it is precisely the wide range of customers that makes the job so interesting. Sometimes you only have to laser-cut a steel plate, sometimes you have a complete assembly...."

## An eye on consistency

With the FlatMaster® 88, levelling has become much easier compared to the manual flattening press in Silberhorn's old facility.

"Back then, every part was different. Today, we have consistently high quality and enormous time savings," explained Diane Silberhorn. The downstream processes are also much more stable and accurate, said the company.

"Thanks to the precision leveller, we have received more orders from two customers. We were able to win a new customer, for whom we are even making a finished assembly," reported Bayerl.

The FlatMaster® 88 also scores with its user-friendliness, said the company. "You enter the parameters and the precision leveller automatically produces the settings - that's it!" said the production manager.

"The machine is uncomplicated to operate, as was the project at the time, ARKU prepared everything very well for this, from start-up to the training of our operators," reported Diane Silberhorn.

Silberhorn Blechtechnik has a lot to offer, and its CEO likes to be at the forefront.

"It's fun to continuously develop the company and be able to take the innovations with us," concluded Diane Silberhorn. The quality of the sheet metal parts produced must always be a key part of that.
