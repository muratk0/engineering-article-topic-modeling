# Flexibility, individuality and precision characterise the latest roll forming technologies on the market....

[TARİH: 01.08.2012 ISMR]

Roll forming is a process in which steel or other metal coil is progressively shaped across its linear axis, through a horizontal series of roller dies, into shapes of uniform crosssection and thickness and then cut to specified lengths. Where medium or large volumes are involved, roll forming can be an economical process compared to other methods.

Roll forming enables manufacturers to produce parts at high volumes with better quality and durability. The industry allows manufacturers to consolidate production and finishing stages into one operation, thereby cutting costs and accelerating speeds.

The custom roll forming industry manufactures metals into complex shapes, rings, trim, channels and structural components using precision equipment. Sophisticated roll formed parts have a variety of uses and applications in industries such as construction/HVAC, automotive, office furniture, medical, rail etc.

## Industry trends

According to preliminary data issued by the US Department of Commerce, hot-rolled steel imports to the US jumped 59% in April to 276,823 metric tons, up from 174,015 metric tons in March. April’s surge ended three months in a row of declining hot-rolled steel imports. Overall US steel imports dipped 2% in April to 2,718,455 metric tons compared to 2,782,068 metric tons in March. Cold-rolled steel imports dropped 25% in April to 92,229 metric tons from 122,583 metric tons in March. Year-to-date, combined hot- and cold-rolled steel imports are up 9% over 2011 levels.

According to Research and Markets, the US custom roll forming industry’s revenue for 2009 was approximately US$ 7.4 billion, with an estimated gross profit of 19.88%. The industry used a projected 64 per cent of its full production capacity in 2009. It could have increased its total shipment value to US$ 11.6 billion under full production capacity.

## High speed pressing

“SOONER OR later, electric motors will probably replace traditional combustion engines as a means of powering our cars. The market for machines to produce electric motors is thus also expected to boom in the coming years,” Schuler told ISMR.

Accordingly, on 21 June 2012, Schuler unveiled its new high-speed press, the Smartline SA-S, to the general public. The new machine is particularly suited to the manufacture of energy-efficient electric motors and can stamp out electric motor laminations which are just 0.2mm thick.

“You could almost call them foils,” said Hans Dieter Jung, divisional manager for the drives and generators market segment at Schuler. During the same process step, the individual laminations are interlocked into packets which will later form the core of the electric motor: “The thinner the laminations, the lower the eddy current losses. This in turn raises efficiency and reduces fuel consumption.

“The electronics of the depth penetration control check the distance between slide and lamination on every stroke and adjust where necessary on the next stroke,” added Hans Dieter Jung. “Consequently, the slide’s bottom dead centre position remains constant. This is important for interlocking.” To protect the electronics system against dangerous vibrations, it is not located in the slide but in the press body.

“The entire process from measurement to adjustment takes a few hundredths of a second. Conventional presses often travel till stop; that means that the slide is abruptly stopped and the machine is overloaded – in the long run, this benefits neither machine nor die. Until recently, the alternative was to reduce possible overloads using manual measurement and adjustment. This step is no longer required with the new Smartline – thus greatly simplifying set-up after die changes,” concluded Schuler.
