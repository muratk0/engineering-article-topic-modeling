## Customers should visit their supplier and meet the design team during the development of their line

■ If only roll tooling is being ordered, where is it being tested? Do you have to take your old rolls off the mill while your supplier spends weeks testing the rolls? And if there is a problem with the rolls, they have to ship them back to their locations for grinding.

Below are some critical elements that must be considered when managing and developing your project:

■ Building layout – are there interfering pillars, footprint and/or ceiling height restrictions?

■ Line direction – will there be right-left, left-right, or U- or L-shaped layouts?

■ Scrap removal systems – forklift access or underground conveyor systems should be considered

■ Quick die change – large dies may need a crane or forklift access.

■ Power requirements – this can be different by country, state or even city.

■ Working pass-line heights – is there “other” equipment interfacing with the newly purchased rollforming line?

■ Equipment colour – ever think about adding this for the builder? Is there a standard colour that you prefer?

■ Hydraulic power-pack location – determine if it should be on the floor or overhead.

■ Electrical panel and operator locations – far too many users don’t think about where the operator will work or where the main power drops currently are in the building. This can cost a lot of money if not communicated in the plan from the start.

■ Guarding – if you need a fence, where will you access the machine for maintenance?

■ Part orientation – is the part legs up or legs down? This is critical if feeding a packaging line or part transfer system. ■

The laser can handle a variety of cutting tasks – these range from micrometre-precise cutting joints in paper-thin semiconductor chips to quality cuts in 30 millimetre-thick steel. In laser drilling, the laser beam generates very small to large holes in metals, plastics, paper and stone, without contact. A short laser pulse melts and vaporises the material with a high power density. The resulting high pressure drives the molten material out of the hole.

Where the focused laser beam strikes the workpiece, it heats the material so extremely that it melts or even vaporises. Once it has completely penetrated the work-piece, the cutting process can start: the laser beam moves along the part contour, melting the material as it goes. Usually, a stream of gas blows the melted material downwards, out of the cut. The gap is barely wider than the focused laser beam itself.

■ GREATER ACCURACY: laser cutting can provide high levels of precision and extremely close tolerances.

■ SHORTER LEAD TIMES: with higher repeatability and an automatic pallet shuttle to automatically remove cut shapes and load new materials, you get your parts faster.

■ HANDLE A LARGE NUMBER OF PARTS AT ONCE: With the ability to handle plate sizes as large as 6' x 12', technicians can simultaneously nest multiple parts on a single sheet, another factor that ensures that lead time and time to market are reduced.

■ BETTER PRODUCT QUALITY: laser cutting allows for less part distortion while virtually eliminating the need for de-burring. It also provides sharper corners and narrower kerfs, resulting in more parts per sheet (or better material yield).

■ INTRICATE SHAPES AND DESIGNS: laser cutting can provide more intricate shapes and designs due to the ability to cut on X, Y and Z axes.

■ COST EFFICIENCY: With less waste, faster lead times and reduced tooling costs, laser cutting provides a cost efficient alternative on laser cut stainless steel and laser cut aluminium to other precision cutting methods.

## SAFETY FIRST

The Laser Zentrum Hannover e.V. (LZH) carries out research and development in the field of laser technology and is supported by the Ministry of Economic Affairs, Labour and Transport of the State of Lower Saxony (Niedersächsisches Ministerium für Wirtschaft, Arbeit und Verkehr) in Germany.

LZH now offers comprehensive consulting concerning laser safety and possible hazardous emissions. Competencies of the "Safety Technology" group range from interpreting current regulations on group pollution control and workplace and laser safety to on-site hazard analyses and suggestions for suitable safety measures.

Staff working with lasers should be better protected against severe health hazards, such as injuries to the skin and the eyes, says LZH. Based on the European workplace safety guidelines, the ordinance on hazardous substances (GefStoffV) as well as the regulations concerning workplace protection against artificial optical radiation (OStrV) have become national regulations in Germany. That means that, in future, more emphasis will be placed on judging dangers and, connected with this, the employer will be expected to take on more responsibility.

The Laser Zentrum Hannover offers assistance in implementing these new regulations in a company.

Ancillary and process automation on the laser cutter itself are currently being promoted by Bystronic. Last autumn, the resourceful Swiss company presented a new cutting head for the BySpeed Pro laser cutting system, which serves as a platform for the expansion of the machine’s autonomy. The first features were the automatic nozzle changer and the automatic changing of the lens cassette that were showcased at the same time. After the BlechExpo 2011 exhibition, further process steps will be automated, in particular the centering of the cutting nozzles.

The automatic centering of the nozzles ensures that the processes run reliably during operation of the laser cutting system, particularly during lightly manned shifts. If, during this time, a collision occurs between a cutting head and, for example, a protruding part, this is detected automatically and the cutting process is interrupted.
