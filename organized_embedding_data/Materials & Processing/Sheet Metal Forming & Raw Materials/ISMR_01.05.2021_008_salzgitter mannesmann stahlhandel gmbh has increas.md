# Salzgitter Mannesmann Stahlhandel GmbH has increased quality and productivity levels with new automated deburring and levelling technology

[TARİH: 01.05.2021 ISMR]

The steel industry is characterised by contrasts. On the one hand, it produces durable, almost timeless, material. On the other, manufacturing processes and technologies are changing so quickly that today's solutions can become obsolete tomorrow.

Salzgitter Mannesmann Stahlhandel GmbH (SMSD) is experiencing these changes. The Düsseldorf-based steel trader sells a broad spectrum of steel products to customers throughout Europe, from Peiner beams to quarto plates from Ilsenburger Grobblech GmbH. Many of these undergo complex processing before the pre-finished products are delivered to manufacturers.

SMSD continuously optimises its production to maintain its position among steel suppliers in a rapidly changing business environment. The company fulfills its customers expectations through increased automation, high repeatability and process reliability.

## A new approach

Together with ARKU, the steel trader recently took another important step towards more efficient production and entry into strategically important markets. One of

SMSD's facilities is in Zeithain, Germany. The site sells sheet metal as trade goods and manufactures precision flame-cut parts.

Where cranes and tractors are made, robots are usually not far away. These automated helpers play an essential role in the industrial production of these massive assemblies. They perform welding and material handling tasks, amongst others. However, the robots require the utmost precision, potentially reacting to even the smallest deviations in materials from specified standards. In serious cases, they may even halt production. That is why the sheet metal blanks used to produce cranes, tractors and load carriers must conform to specification. Blanks are classic supplier parts that most series manufacturers no longer check individually. This involves too much work and heightens the risk of production standstills.

The suppliers themselves are subject to extremely demanding standards. Maximum precision plays a vital role. Parts that fail to meet exact specifications put important business relationships at risk and, sometimes even strategically important markets. Almost all customers in today's expanding industries have their own ideas about the ideal initial condition of the materials.

"Level, burr-free parts are at the top of the list," said Norbert Gärtner, Salzgitter plant manager at the Zeithain site. "Because these customers are strategically important to us, we are constantly upgrading our manufacturing technology. Some machines that were state-of-the-art only five years ago are now considered obsolete."

The steel trader therefore began rethinking its levelling and deburring approach several years ago.

"We had signed an important framework agreement with a customer. The service portfolio included specific flatness specifications that we wanted to offer the customer," explained Daniel Götze, Product Manager Preprocessing, SMSD. This required a general overhaul of the existing processes. Up until then, levelling had been carried out manually on an older machine. However, there was potential to improve flatness. Deburring was also still handled manually with angle and belt grinders.

"We also had to automate this area to satisfy the customer," recalled Götze. Qualitative over quantitative growth was the motto — but with a plan.

## Forging a strong partnership

The steel trader and ARKU, a manufacturer of levelling and deburring machines in Baden-Baden (Germany), had repeatedly met at trade shows and discussed trends and new developments. Over the years, they had established a solid foundation of trust. Building on this, they took an important step together.

"We were looking for a competent partner to advise us on selecting the right equipment," emphasized Götze. "The equipment needed to provide convincing value for money and performance."

ARKU was ready to help. The company tested parts from Salzgitter Mannesmann Stahlhandel on various levelling and deburring machines, obtained feedback from the results and made further suggestions - until the choice came down to two candidates.
