# IN THE CLINCH

[TARİH: 01.03.2012 ISMR]

ISMR analyses the latest developments in sheet metal clinching techniques, processes and product development

ISMR SAYS: “Clinching produces a button-type, positive connection of two or three layers of sheet metal using a cold-forming process”

The clinching process is a method of joining sheet metal by localised cold forming of materials. It is a mechanical fastening method to join sheet metal without additional components using special tools to plastically form a mechanical interlock between sheet metals. The result is an interlocking friction joint between two or more layers of material formed by a punch into a special die. Coated and painted sheet metals can also be joined together with no damage to the surface finish.

The clinching process (DIN 8593) produces a button-type, positive connection of two or three layers of sheet metal using a cold-forming process. The process joins sheet metals of different thicknesses or materials, even with adhesives or other intermediate layers. In industrial applications, clinching is applied from a single sheet thickness of 0.1mm up to a total layer thickness of 12mm / 0.47” and up to 800 N/mm2 / 5.7 lbf/in2 tensile strength. In laboratory tests, significantly higher values have already been achieved. Clinching has become an important means of fastening aluminium panels, such as boots and bonnets, in the automotive industry.

Compared to the welding process, clinching can offer tremendous advantages in terms of environmental protection. No toxic fumes or sparks are generated during the clinching process and there are low noise levels. Electrical energy consumption can also be reduced (10 to 20 times), no cooling water is required and there is no need for refrigeration energy. Fastener manufacturers argue that it is a sustainable production process, eliminating corrosion and polluting paint processes (for example, by using pre-coated materials). Life expectancy for clinching tools (typically, a punch and a die) is high, making the process very economical.

## Self-clinching fasteners

Self-clinching fasteners not only reduce time, labour, weight and inventory on many assembly jobs, but they also opened up a whole new world for engineers and designers, making a wide assortment of thin-metal designs possible for the first time. One of the basics of self-clinching is that as soon as the bottom material touches the die anvil, it starts flowing to the sides and forms a button.

## Benefits of Self-Clinching Fasteners

- Provide strong threads in metal as thin as 0.020"/0.51mm
- May be installed using any parallel acting squeezing force
- Provide high push-out and torque-out resistance
- Do not require special hole preparation, such as chamfering and deburring
- Reverse side of metal sheet remains flush
- Low installed costs

They are used chiefly where good pull-out and torque loads are required in sheet metal that is too thin to provide secure fastening by any other method. ## New technologies

Attexor’s SPOT CLINCH® joining technique can be used to join steel and stainless steel materials as well as aluminium and/or nonferrous materials based on a cost effective, environmentally friendly process. The resulting clinch will either be round or rectangular depending upon the joining tool selected. The SR round joint, without cutting through the material, is gas-tight. Consequently, it protects the coating better and provides high corrosion and fatigue resistance.

The ST rectangular clinch joint is the product of a combined cutting and deforming process. It is primarily suited to multi-layer (up to 5 and more) and harder materials such as stainless steel.

“Advantages include no fumes, no sparks, very little noise, low energy use and an environmentally- friendly workplace,” Attexor explained. “No pre / post treatment is required e.g. no pre-cleaning or subsequent removal of spray deposits around the joint. There is no thermal load on the joining zone and no damage to surface finishes on the workpiece. Interim layers or film/adhesive can be incorporated in most cases. Maintenance cost is low, there are no consumable items and there is good joint reproducibility.”

ATTEXOR Clinch Systems launched its new SPOT CLINCH 0201IP V2 which is particularly suitable for certain applications with an angle or with plane surfaces. It is also well balanced for hand-held operations. The tools and options are the same as those of the 0201IP.

ACS offers, worldwide, a large range of tooling and machines based on SPOT CLINCH (clinching) and TAGGER (stitch-folding) joining techniques.

Bollhoff’s RIVCLINCH joining technique can be used to join steel and stainless steel materials as well as aluminum and/or non-ferrous materials in a cost effective, environmentally-friendly process. The first step of RIVCLINCH joining is to draw the materials to be joined through the punch into the die. As soon as the bottom material touches the die anvil, it starts flowing to the sides and forms a button. After that, the punch moves back into the initial position and the joined part can be removed. A form-fitting and non-positive joint results from local forming.

The RIVCLINCH P75 series is the most powerful variant of Bollhoff’s modular systems. The focus of this series is on high precision and the ability to work with multi-stage tools.

High sheet thicknesses, such as total steel thicknesses of up to 6mm, can be joined with the corresponding tool kits.

BTM Corporation’s Tog-L-Loc is sheet metal joining or “clinching” made simple. Fast, economical and consistent, the Tog-L-Loc sheet metal joining system is an alternative to spot welding and riveting, and is used worldwide to assemble automobile parts, appliances and more. Whether joining plain, painted or coated sheet metal parts with BTM’s patented Tog-L-Loc sheet metal joining technique, there are no fasteners, sparks or fumes.

BTM’s hand-held Tog-L-Loc units are an economical approach to fastening sheet metal assemblies. Stand-alone self-contained, pneumo-hydraulic units, hydraulic units, pneumatic units and battery/electric units are available. The Lit-L-Loc(r) range of hand-held clinch units are lightweight robust units operated via 6.0 bar clean compressed air. The UniClinch unit is a self-contained air hydraulic clinch joining tool. The design allows the user to work using rapid strokes and considerable force. Tog-L-Loc(r) can be used for sheet metal up to 2 x 1.5mm thick.

TOX PRESSOTECHNIK offers several clinching solutions. The TOX Joining System combines two closely related and well established technologies finding new and innovative clinching applications every day in the sheet metal joining industry. TOX clinching, also known as riveting without rivets, usually using the solid die cavity patented by TOX.

A patented spin-off development of clinching is the TOX ClinchRivet. A simple, symmetrical rivet is used as the joining element to produce a clinch connection with higher strength values than those obtained with a pierce rivet and without cutting the material. Applications in the crash zone of passenger cars are a testament to the capabilities of this versatile joining method.

“The patented TOX Round Joint is the core of our sheet metal joining system. A simple round punch presses the materials to be joined into the die cavity. As the force continues to increase, the punch side material is forced to spread outwards within the die side material. The result: an aesthetically pleasing round button, which joins clearly without any burrs or sharp edges on which corrosion could set in. Even with aluminized or galvanized sheet metals, the anti-corrosive properties remain intact as the protective layer flows with the material,” the company told ISMR.

“Corrosion tests have shown that, because the surface coating is not damaged during the round joint process, the anti-corrosive properties of the joined sheet metals e.g. aluminized or galvanized, are maintained. The TOX joining system does not cut the surface of the materials to be joined, as the coating flows with the material and remains intact.”

Available in 1.5 and 2mm point diameters, the TOX-MICROpoint is one answer to miniaturisation in mechatronics, electrotechnics, electronics and micromechanics. It enables the joining of sheet metal parts made of steel, copper and aluminium with a thickness from 0.1 to 1.2mm. ■
