# ARKU history

[TARİH: 01.01.2022 ISMR]

ARKU's history is a family story spanning three generations. In 1928, master craftsman Albert Reiss founded a sheet metalworking company in Haueneberstein in Baden (Germany). His son Artur developed this inheritance into an industrially-oriented machine-tool factory. In the initial years after Artur Reiss took over the metalworking company, it was based in Kuppenheim. The company name ARKU originated from this period: Artur Reiss KUppenheim.

In 1967, ARKU built its first precision leveller. In the 1970s, metalworking companies begin to use coils and ARKU expanded its product range to include decoilers and feeders. Smallseries production of precision levellers transformed into customer-specific systems manufacturing.

In 1986, Albert Reiss, the son of Artur Reiss, became the managing director of ARKU after completing his degree. Today, the company focuses exclusively on coil lines, parts levellers and deburring machines.

## Increasing its reach

"Over the last few years, our turnover has continued to increase. Despite the pandemic, our order income this year has been very good. Sales started steadily growing again after the second quarter of 2020, when the pandemic hit. We have also made significant investments in our U.S. presence in the last two years. We moved to our own building in Cincinnatti last year and increased our facilities and showroom/contract levelling facility there," Hellriegel told SMR.

"We have strengthened our teams and service offerings in China and the U.S., where we can also do contract levelling as we have specialist levelling centres in these countries. Customers simply bring or send us their parts. All in all, we are the only levelling manufacturer to be present on three continents," he continued.

ARKU has also developed distributor partnerships in many countries for local service support. Its largest markets, by size, are Europe, the USA and China. Customers include sheet metal job shops and manufacturers in a variety of sectors such as automotive, machinebuilding, architecture, electronics; construction and shipbuilding.

"We have also increased our deburring product portfolio and have now developed a full range of sizes/capabilities for varied sheet metal applications. A key focus for us has been product development over the last two years. We spend around 5% of our turnover on R&D every year," added Hellriegel.
