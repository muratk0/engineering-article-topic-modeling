Biocompatible nickel-titanium shape memory alloy is used for stents to support collapsed or clogged blood vessels whilst titanium bar and wire are used to make surgical screws for bone repair.

At MEDICA 2012 in October, sheet metal specialist TRUMPF showcased its latest solutions for optimising patient care procedures in and around operating rooms and intensive care units. Its innovations are designed to make the work of surgeons and medical care staff easier, more ergonomic and more efficient such as the new OR tabletop to improve patient handling during surgery.

Its improved second generation of the TruLight 5000 multi-functional surgical light was also displayed at MEDICA 2012. Among its new features is the ability to adjust both the colour temperature and the pattern size. The large illuminated area with its diameter of 23cm can be reduced to 16cm; particularly advantageous for the minimally-invasive surgical procedures that are being carried out more frequently. This means the TruLight 5000 illuminates just the area being worked on, which cuts down on reflections from the surroundings.

TRUMPF Medical Systems develops and produces innovative operating room lighting and communication systems, ergonomic ceiling-mounted workplace solutions for operating rooms and intensive care units, and flexible operating table and patient transport systems. The TRUMPF Medical Technology Division employs around 720 people worldwide. In fiscal year 2011/12 sales were around 184 million Euros.

TRUMPF is addressing surgeons directly with the ViKY® assistance concept. ViKY provides support in general surgery, as well as urological and gynaecological procedures. The system may be used either to guide and hold the endoscope during laparoscopic interventions or as a holding and positioning system for uterine manipulators. It features a control system and a compact robotic unit that attaches to the operating table’s side rail with an articulated arm.

“With the appropriate adapters, ViKY® can interface with all the standard endoscope optical systems and cameras and with uterine manipulators, regardless of the make. Instruments can be guided, positioned and held during the operation in response to commands issued by voice or with a foot control pedal. The system can store different positioning configurations in memory and recreate them later on command. Above and beyond that, up to fifty surgeons can use the system and each of them can configure the system personally,” TRUMPF told ISMR. ■
