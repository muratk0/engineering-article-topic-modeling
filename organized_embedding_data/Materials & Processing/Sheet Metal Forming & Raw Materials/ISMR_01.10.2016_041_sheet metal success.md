# SHEET METAL SUCCESS

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

In May 2016, Schrag Kantprofile GmbH invested in two new machines for manufacturing thin-walled bent profiles for the construction industry.

Schrag specialises in manufacturing thin-walled bent profiles for the interior and exterior components used in the construction of production halls and other industrial/commercial buildings. Schrag was founded in 1892 and is headquartered in Hilchenbach in the German state of North Rhine-Westphalia.

The company set up an additional factory in Seevetal-Meckelfeld in 1987. Ingo Kleinau, who runs the plant in Seevetal-Meckelfeld, and his team, serve a customer base primarily consisting of building contractors who submit orders with prepared lists of specific materials.

"Industrial buildings have to be made from specific materials, inside and out. It's mostly sheet steel with a metal coating – zinc and zinc alloys – plus an organic coating for materials used on the exterior," explained Kleinau.

## Flexibility is key

Every order handled by the 30 employees is a custom order, so prefabricated parts are not something you will find in the company's warehouse. Order volumes range from a single item up to batches weighing 20 tons. As well as high quality, most customers also require very short turnaround times. Deadlines of between one and three days are the norm, so maximum flexibility is essential.

"The order receipt and job preparation stages have to be really fast," said Kleinau. He recalled, with a smile, that “our managing director was once asked how much free capacity we had at the company. He said today we're doing overtime, tomorrow we have lots of work on our plate but I don't know yet what I'll be doing the day after that!"

## Installing new machines

Schrag Kantprofile GmbH, in Germany, spent twenty years using two TRUMPF machines running on three-shift production. But the time had come for something new. In May 2016, it installed a new EHT VarioPress 400, with a bending length of nine metres, and an EHT VarioCut with a cutting length of eight metres.

TRUMPF supplied an EHT VarioPress 400, with a bending length of nine metres, and an EHT VarioCut, with a cutting length of eight metres, to Schrag Kantprofile GmbH.

The three-day period from 18-20 May 2016 was inevitably going to be stressful for Ingo Kleinau at Seevetal-Meckelfeld. His task was to dismantle two long-serving EHT machines to make space for two new machine tool arrivals – an EHT VarioPress 400 press brake and an EHT VarioCut guillotine shear. The entire process of dismantling and installing these huge nine- and eight-metre machines would have to take place in just about the toughest spot imaginable, at the other end of the narrow, 65-metre-long production hall. Kleinau and his colleagues had no other option than to clear everything out of the way.

"We knew how hard it was going to be, so that's why we opted to replace both machines at the same time," said Kleinau. For three days, the site echoed with the sound of trucks coming and going, but finally the new machines were in place.

"The bent profiles we produce are generally eight metres long, but we wanted to give ourselves some leeway," explained Kleinau. That's why the new EHT VarioPress 400, which was tailored to Schrag's requirements, has a bending length of nine metres instead of the eight metres offered by its predecessor. "We've worked with our partner EHT – now known as TRUMPF Werkzeugmaschinen Teningen – for a long time, and they managed to give us nine metres while still using an eight-metre base! We were very impressed. Not everyone can do that," he said.

## Custom design

The VarioPress 400 at Schrag also has a higherpower motor and an additional H-axis.

"We can also bend Z profiles. But, when you're dealing with a profile that has already been bent down one way, you need to adjust the support brackets before you can bend it again. You can either do that manually or have the brackets positioned in a height-adjustable axis at the front. With the latter solution, we can simply use the machine's control system to move the brackets down automatically, and that's obviously a major benefit," said Kleinau.

Another feature that makes work easier and faster is the tool magazine connected to the VarioPress. The ToolShuttle contains the two bending dies that cover 99 per cent of the bending jobs performed at Schrag. As well as the standard folding die, this magazine also provides safe storage of the heavy "housing cover" which can quickly be moved into place.

"The ToolShuttle not only makes it faster to switch between tools, but also offers an ergonomic solution for the people who work here," added Kleinau.
