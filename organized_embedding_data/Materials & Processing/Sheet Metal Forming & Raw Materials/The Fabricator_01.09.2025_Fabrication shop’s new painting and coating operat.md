# Fabrication shop’s new painting and coating operations eliminate previous challenges

Applications

Employees can work in comfort in the fully climate-controlled building.

Situation

Since 1981, Bolt Construction has served the petroleum and natural gas industries through construction of airport fueling systems, gas compressor stations, gathering systems, and bulk storage petroleum piping. The company receives raw pipe, valves, and fittings into its 20,000-sq.-ft. fabrication shop and creates products not only for its own in-house construction projects but for a variety of outside customers as well.

Abrasive blasting and coating are two of the most important processes in the overall operation.

"We specialize in the fabrication of pipe and structural components, so painting and coating play a significant role in the operation of our business," said Todd Miller, president. "Blasting helps create a smooth finish on the components, leading to improved adhesion of all coatings. And while everything is running smoothly now, it wasn’t long ago that we had to address two of the biggest obstacles hindering the productivity of both procedures."

The first was the weather, which had a major impact on abrasive blasting.

"Since we previously did all our blasting outdoors, the elements were a constant issue," Miller noted. "Not only did inclement weather frequently slow down or completely stop the blasting process, but even when there was no precipitation, the employees doing the blasting often had to endure oppressive heat and humidity or biting cold."

The second roadblock was the size of Bolt’s paint booth.

"We had a tiny, 1,000-sq.-ft. painting facility, which severely limited the size and number of components we could fit in there," Miller explained.

Despite the presence of in-house blasting and coating capabilities, these shortcomings forced Bolt to outsource numerous jobs, especially those with tight deadlines or that involved oversized parts. This required transportation (and its associated costs) to and from the outsourced facility, and Bolt was held captive to its subcontractors’ production schedules.

Resolution

In spring 2024, ending a lengthy search for alternate approaches, Bolt took delivery of a new blast room and a modern, fully featured paint booth, both supplied by Titan Abrasive Systems.

The 16- by 16- by 50-ft. blast room includes a mechanical abrasive recovery system with a 16-ft. cross auger and a 14,000-CFM dust collection system. Similar in size to the blast room, the paint booth includes a large vacuum system that captures fumes and dust.

To accommodate these large operations, Bolt constructed a dedicated 10,000-sq.-ft. facility, replacing the 3,000-sq.-ft. building in which the company was founded. The new plant houses the two pieces of machinery, with room to spare for a second paint booth to be added later. The building is fully climate-controlled, allowing the two full-time blasters, two full-time painters, and a "floater" to work in comfort.

After material is delivered from supplier, welders fabricate the parts, which then are picked up and placed strategically on gooseneck trailers, from which the wood has been removed to allow maximum access to all the parts’ surfaces. They remain on the 35-ft.-long trailers throughout the blasting and coating operations, not coming off until they’re ready to ship to the customer.

Miller noted that the new blast room and paint booth have allowed Bolt to generate output that surpasses even its previously high standards: "Once the paint hits, the component immediately looks the way it should. We can get it out and haul in the next item rather than having to potentially sand things down and touch them up to make appealing to the eye."

Miller is analyzing the data to compute the higher efficiencies and improved economics that the new equipment has generated.

"We can now be more efficient with both blasting and coating from the time we get here to the time we leave. We are also no longer limited by the elements, so we can work on our own schedule," he said. "We’re not relying on subcontractors. Everything is under one roof, where we have total control."
