Gutter protection supplier outsources roll forming to save money on equipment investment

SITUATION

Universal Mfg. & Design (UMD), Palm Harbor, Fla., was in search of roll forming assistance for the chassis component of its Gutter Guard product. The cost to purchase full turnkey machinery to form the product was estimated at $400,000.

The company soon realized that finding some manufacturing help would go a long way toward getting the new business off the ground.

"We did a lot of research, talked to a lot of companies that do roll forming," said Troy Merkley, vice president of operations. "I would say that 60 to 70 percent of those we talked to said no, they couldn’t do it. I don’t know if it was because the bends were too difficult or if it was because they were too busy, but they said no."

RESOLUTION

After narrowing the search down to three roll forming operations, company representatives met with the people from Samco face to face at METALCON 2015.

"Honestly, we felt a synergy with the people at Samco," said Merkley. "Samco was open to discussions and suggestions all through the process."

UMD gave Samco a design and drawing for the chassis component of the gutter protection product. After upfront engineering due diligence and collaboration relative to the unique profile of the product, Samco engineers used Copra finite element analysis to test its roll form tooling on a virtual tooling tryout mill before any material was cut to pinpoint potential forming problems and predict actual strip shape at each roll stand.

"This is how we resolve any upfront issues before we build the tooling," said Darryl Levesque, vice president of Samco’s Rolling Division. "You want to avoid cutting tooling more than once or making multiple adjustments to the tooling during initial tryout. This was one of the more challenging parts we’ve [worked] on, as it required over 220 roll tools constructed to make the product."

The tooling fits into a standard Samco 24-pass roll former and can be changed over easily to produce the chassis component for 5- or 6-in. gutters.

The money saved by contracting Samco as opposed to purchasing a machine was used to take the product to market. For Q1 2016, most of the product was sold "south of the Mason-Dixon," Merkley said. Shipping now is migrating north, to Ohio, Virginia, North Carolina, Wisconsin, and Minnesota.

UMD is on schedule to have 900,000 linear feet of the Gutter Guard chassis produced in 2016, or about 225,000 pieces measuring 4 ft. each. Currently the company has no plans to bring the manufacturing of the component in-house.

Coating imparts stainless steel-like corrosion resistance on carbon steel oil coolers

SITUATION

An oil cooler manufacturer makes hundreds of the units for a major producer of heavy equipment. When carbon steel oil coolers had been used inside the heavy equipment, corrosion was found at the six-month preventive maintenance check. Because of this, the manufacturer had to use more expensive stainless steel to make oil coolers that would resist corrosion.

The company decided to search for a high-temperature protective coating that would allow it to make its oil coolers from less expensive carbon steel but still impart corrosion resistance.

RESOLUTION

The high-temperature aluminum solvent-borne silicone coating provides excellent corrosion resistance on metal substrates. It dries tack-free to 5B hardness in about 20 minutes at room temperature and achieves 9H hardness after heating. This coating is suitable for applications that will reach high temperatures because the coating is heat-stable to 1,200 degrees F with prolonged heat resistance from 400 to 1,200 degrees F.

The heavy equipment customer agreed to specify carbon steel and the coating as the materials for making future oil coolers to install in its heavy equipment. This resulted in a cost reduction for the oil cooler manufacturer, which now uses cost-effective carbon steel while maintaining corrosion resistance.
