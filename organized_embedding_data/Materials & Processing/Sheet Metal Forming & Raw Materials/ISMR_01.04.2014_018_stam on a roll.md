# STAM ON A ROLL

[TARİH: 01.04.2014 ISMR]

Italian manufacturer, STAM, has built the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses up to 7mm. The new roll former is able to roll form pieces of different shapes and thicknesses.

In 2007, STAM (along with Spanish company, Valle Perfiladoras, which belongs to the STAM Group) faced a new technological challenge from the automotive sector. For many years, the roll forming industry had been investigating the possibility of roll forming a product of variable section i.e roll forming a product that, in length, would also change its geometrical cross section. For example, for a U-shaped profile, the product would change its web along the profile.

STAM SpA moved from theory to action by manufacturing and putting into operation the first roll forming line for variable section beams and U-profiles used in the mass production of light commercial vehicles. The line was very innovative as each roll forming stand moves cross-wise and rotates through numerical controls, as the strip moves forward in the line, to follow the curvilinear shape of the profile which is in production.

## Making the breakthrough

In 2013, STAM designed and built the first machine in the world capable of roll forming variable section profiles for beams of thicknesses of up to 7mm. The material used was HSLA steel with a 350 to 500 MPa yield strength.

“After seeing the success of the machine producing U-profiles for light trucks, automotive customers began to request a machine capable of producing U or other large thickness sections (up to 7mm) for heavy vehicles. The variable section beams help manufacturers to improve the structure of the vehicle’s chassis by lowering the height of the web in one or both ends of the beam,” STAM explained.

Most of these products are manufactured today using large presses or welding centres, which bend or weld pieces together to produce the beam of the desired section. In addition to the enormous manpower and space needed for production, the pieces obtained from the pressing and welding operations show deformations that need to be corrected using straightening operations. Producing these special beams, in lengths of up to 15m, using the automatic and continuous roll forming method is therefore an enormous advantage in terms of flexibility and number of personnel employed, and consequently for the quality and cost of the final product.”

The machine built by STAM provides roll-forming stands which can withstand not just the energy from the bending action of the strip but also the tension and loads generated by the displacement of the roll forming stands during roll forming, when the material is between the forming rolls. The system must also able to accommodate the shape of the incoming strip and reach the required roll forming speed. The faster the speed of roll forming operations, the quicker are the transversal and rotary movements of the roll forming stands to follow the curved shape of the flanges being formed.

## Precision in focus

A further important aspect is the guidance system used for the input material into the roll forming machine and throughout the roll forming process. The traditional guidance system must simply guide and maintain in position a strip of constant width from the beginning to the end of the process while, in variable section roll forming, the strip width varies continuously and the guides must carefully follow the strip shape. A machine of such mechanical complexity requires a high-level electrical and electronic control system.

The machine has more than 100 dynamic axes which are constantly changing position during the roll forming operation as well as over 40 fixed axes which are positioned according to the profile to be produced and will constantly be maintained in position during the process. Each axis is driven by servo-motors, with position control in a closed loop, with each axis moving in a different manner to the other axes depending upon its physical position along the machine.

The choice of electronic components is important and control software was developed that could control the positioning of all the dynamic axes, following the progress of the strip inside the machine with precision, reliability and repeatability. Movement error of just one single element, during the roll forming of a 7mm thick beam, could cause serious damage. For this reason, a continuous monitoring system was developed which immediately stops all operation as soon as any anomaly is detected.

The machine is able to roll form pieces of different shapes and thicknesses and is not linked to a fixed vehicle design. Vehicle manufacturers have various production models and various chassis and must be able to move quickly and safely from one product to another through easy programming. To do this, STAM developed a CAD/CAM system which, starting from a CAD drawing in dwg or dxf format from the customer, converts the drawing into a machine programming file. The software automatically calculates the positions of the various roll forming stands and the positions of the guides while the profile is being processed in the roll-forming machine.

During the test phase of a new product, as in all other automatic self-configuring roll forming machines, some adjustments might be needed to the positioning of the forming rolls. Once the best set-up is chosen, this will be saved in the control system so that it can easily be recalled using a product code number for the new product produced.

“The machine was built and tested at STAM with excellent results. The customer measured all the pieces produced, checked the repeatability of the process and compared it with the current process,” STAM told ISMR. “At STAM, we work with an eye on the present as well as on the future...”

STAM employs 130 people and has additional facilities in Spain and Brazil. More than 1000 roll forming machines and lines have been delivered and installed. At STAM, every stage of the machine’s production is managed in-house including mechanical, electrical and software design, building (i.e. fabricating, machining, assembling and painting) and mechanical, electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic testing.
