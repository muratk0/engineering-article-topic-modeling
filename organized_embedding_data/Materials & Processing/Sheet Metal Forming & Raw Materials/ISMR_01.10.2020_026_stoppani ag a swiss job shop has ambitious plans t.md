# Stoppani AG, a Swiss job shop, has ambitious plans to bend sheet metal parts throughout the night and reduce part production costs by 35%.

[TARİH: 01.10.2020 ISMR]

STOPPANI AG, A Swiss sheet metal manufacturer with expertise in engineering, contract manufacturing, assembly, metal processing and a range of finishing processes, has approximately 180 employees. It operates from a factory in Neuenegg, Switzerland, with a production area of 10,000m2.

Hans Gattlen, Stoppani’s chairman and co-owner, has a clear vision: a “dual factory”, where people work during the day and machines take over the night shift. This digital factory in Neuenegg, near the Swiss capital of Berne, will produce fully automatically and fully autonomously – saving costs and reducing the staff’s workload. Together with his team, Hans Gattlen has introduced new structures over the past few months, and has, above all, significantly modernised the company’s sheet metal processing operations.

His next step is to comprehensively digitalise Stoppani’s sheet metal processing in line with Industry 4.0. In this way, the entrepreneur intends to attract orders back to Switzerland, where the industry has found it increasingly difficult to compete against companies from abroad in recent years. This is due to lower labour costs in Italy, Portugal and many Eastern European countries, which allow companies there to produce at 20 to 30 per cent lower costs.

“Fully automated production will enable us to reduce our own costs by around 35 percent,” he says.

## Automated bending cell

A Bystronic machine is playing a key role in this process.

“We are the first company in Switzerland to operate a Bending Cell,” adds Hans Gattlen. The cell was installed in the new production hall a few months ago. Although it has 2000 square metres of floor space, there was a limited amount of space available for the Bending Cell. Bystronic solved the problem with a customised solution.

For example, the track on which the robot moves was shortened or the footprint would have been too large and blocked the access route for material supplies. Fred Weber, a Bystronic area sales manager, regularly takes interested customers on tours of the company.

“Bystronic’s approach is very solution-oriented. This enables us to respond to the customers’ specific needs,” he emphasizes. “For us, Stoppani is a flagship project and we are justifiably proud of what we have achieved.”

Visar Veseli, Automation Group Manager, Stoppani, stands in front of the Bending Cell and launches a job on the touch screen. On the other side of the safety barrier, the Kuka robot glides along the track to the high-bay warehouse, retrieves a piece of sheet metal and transports it to the Xpert Pro 150 press brake. The press brake bends it to the desired angle, after which the robot grips the part again, turns it around and places it back in the press brake. This is repeated until the part has been bent into a finished component for the chassis of a commercial coffee machine – and the process starts all over again. Once assembled, the bean-to-cup machines will be shipped all around the globe.

## Fully automatic all through the night

Each new part that is produced must be programmed in advance. After that, the program can be retrieved whenever required. In combination with the Xpert Pro press brake, the robot functions autonomously and changes grippers and bending tools without human intervention. The intelligent BySoft 7 software determines the ideal bending sequence to achieve maximum efficiency for each job.

“Integration with the warehouse was absolutely essential for us, both for the retrieval of the metal sheets and for storage of the bent parts,” explains Hans Gattlen. “If this could not be achieved, an operator would always be tied up at the press brake.”

This was the main reason that Stoppani chose a Bystronic solution. “Our previous supplier could not guarantee that the warehouse integration would be possible, not only for the current machines but also for future models. That’s when I realized that we needed to get a new partner on board,” he adds.

Hans Gattlen has ambitious plans for Stoppani which produces parts for the optics industry; medical technology; food processing; semiconductors and the machine industry – the SME covers the entire value chain from contract manufacturing through to assembly. It spans high-tech, machines and systems right through to complex production plants.

Only recently, the company closed down one of its production locations in Bümpliz and relocated its sheet metal processing operations to its headquarters at Neuenegg in Switzerland.

“If Swiss industry manages to make intelligent use of the potential of digitalisation, we could experience a decade of reindustrialisation after several decades of deindustrialisation.”

In addition to the Bending Cell, Stoppani also purchased several other machines from Bystronic. Among others, the two mobile press brakes Xpert 80 and Xpert 40, the ByStar Fiber laser cutting system and the ByTrans Cross loading and unloading system have taken Stoppani’s sheet metal processing operations to the next level. Stoppani has also kept on several machines from other suppliers, which have now been integrated into the production process as part of the end-to-end solution supplied by Bystronic.

“If we succeed in completely digitalising the planning, storage, intralogistics, toolchanging, production and quality control for certain suitable products, we may become Switzerland’s first and only sheet metal processing job shop that has what is known as ‘a dual factory’,” explains the 56-year-old Chairman: “We would have two factories under a single roof – a human factory and a digital one.”

The principle: In the evening, when the staff have turned off the lights and gone home for the day, the robots and machines continue working. From 6pm to 6am, operations run fully automatically – without human intervention.

The staff no longer has to work night shifts or perform strenuous tasks and can dedicate valuable time to more demanding bending jobs or other activities, such as programming the laser cutting machine.

## Strengthening competitiveness

In production hall 4, Visar Veseli opens the lattice door to the Bending Cell, picks up a finished bent part and measures it. The angle is spot-on. He used to measure every single part, he says. Thanks to the Bending Cell, this is now only necessary for the first and last part of each batch – with an occasional spot check.

“Obviously, the job profile of our staff is changing. It takes motivation to learn new things,” says Hans Gattlen. “But I constantly hear that robots are displacing the human workforce. This is a rather short-sighted point of view.”

Thanks to the digital factory, Stoppani hopes to be awarded orders (on which companies in Switzerland would otherwise not be competitive) which would also cover a portion of the human factory’s indirect costs.

“This means that we are strengthening our competitiveness. Stoppani will be able to grow and hire new employees. Ultimately, this also improves Switzerland’s attractiveness as an industrial location,” Hans Gattlen explains. “If Swiss industry manages to make intelligent use of the potential of digitalisation, we could experience a decade of reindustrialisation after several decades of deindustrialisation.” But Stoppani is not quite at that stage yet.

“Fully automated production facilities and material handling robots alone do not allow a factory to operate autonomously. Specific system applications play a key role,” Hans Gattlen continues. “On the one hand, a digital filter is required to identify those products that are most suitable for the digital factory and to handle the production planning. On the other hand, we need a digital command centre that orchestrates the entire digital production and communicates with other systems via interfaces, particularly with the ERP.”

The Swiss company Misurio, which will apply its expertise in engineering, mathematics and computer science to develop complex algorithms and program the necessary applications, will help him to achieve this. Hans Gattlen hopes that at least part of the dual factory will be up and running by the end of this year.

“This is particularly challenging for a jobshop that produces hundreds of different parts,” he adds. “You must not be afraid of making mistakes. What is important is that you learn from them.”

Together with the management team, he always strives to motivate the employees to have the courage to try out new things.

“You have to be willing to turn your back on the established process. Without a vision – and a measure of courage – you cannot succeed.”
