# Surface finishing showcase

[TARİH: 01.10.2016 ISMR]

ON STAND H58 at Aero Engineering 2016 (NEC, UK), Fintek will be showcasing its subcontract 'super surface finishing services' for aerospace and other precision component manufacturers. The AS9100- accredited company will also show equipment along with a full service to help engineers find the right combination of machine, media and process time to achieve the required surface finish for their part.

Jonathan Dean, Fintek MD, commented: "Reducing component cost is not always the main aim of aerospace manufacturers. There is a constant drive to improve surface finish and every incremental advance produces a better part with greater performance. In many cases, costs associated with making improvements are quickly offset – stream finishing processes are reliably repeatable, quicker than hand finishing and reduce waste due to non-conforming product."

The company is able to process miniature parts from individual turbine blades up to full blisks. Using stream finishing, Fintek can apply more than one surface finishing method in a single process, saving more time. Importantly, it told /SMR, subsequently applied hard coatings will last longer on super-fine finished, peak-free surfaces. Coatings, it explained, are easily compromised when peaks not removed by some traditional finishing methods break during component use, causing costly maintenance for the user.

"Turbine blades are often made from nickeltitanium or tungsten-molybdenum alloys. In line with most customer requirements, surface smoothing of between Ra 0.4μm and 0.25μm is easily achievable in a short cycle time of around five minutes. Where smoothing needs are more stringent, surface finishes down to Ra 0.01μm are equally possible," said the company.

"Throughout processing it is important to ensure that the shape, for example, of a turbine blade is not affected or damaged. Our stream finishing machines have specially designed holders to ensure that there is no contact between components during processing, which might otherwise damage surfaces, and that all surfaces are optimally exposed to the media. Edge rounding by hand or CNC machine often risks corners of the blades being overrounded and inconsistencies along the blade edge. Again, stream finishing mitigates these pitfalls and saves time as it takes place in the same process cycle time."
