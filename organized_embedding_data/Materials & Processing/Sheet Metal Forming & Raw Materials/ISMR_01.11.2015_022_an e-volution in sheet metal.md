# AN E-VOLUTION IN SHEET METAL

[TARİH: 01.11.2015 ISMR]

The US market, Industry 4.0 and a new product range are focus points for bending specialist, SafanDarley.

ISMR SAYS:

SafanDarley is a leading global specialist in developing and constructing press brakes and shears for thin and thick sheet metal. It has two production plants and a head office in Lochem (Netherlands). The company employs approximately 180 members of staff and has steadily grown its turnover this year as it brings new products and technologies to market.

With its servo-electronic press brake, it brought about a veritable e-volution in sheet metal working.

“Quality, innovation, reliability and sustainability are the core values on which we have created an expanding and loyal global customer base,” it told ISMR, as it unveiled new products and technologies at this November’s Blechexpo show in Stuttgart.

## New product ranges

One of the new products on display at SafanDarley’s booth at Blechexpo in Stuttgart was the E-Brake 40 Ton 1600 Ergonomic. The E-Brake 150 Ton 3100 and M-Shear 310-6 (with cutting capacity of 6mm with a hybrid drive requiring just 22 litres of oil) were also showcased on the booth. The shear was equipped with an optional Return to Sender system.

The company has expanded its E-Brake Ergonomic press brake series, a compact, portable, high-speed electric press brake, with a 40-ton model that offers higher capacity and a longer working length of 1600mm. The Ergonomic 40 Tons has an open height of 590mm standard and a stroke of 300mm standard. The ergonomic series is designed for bending small parts. Rapid acceleration and deceleration of the electrical servo-driven ram delivers approach speeds of up to 180mm/sec. The machine has a very compact footprint.

“Our new variant for the ergonomic 40 tonnes 1600mm E-brake press brake is easier to operate and portable for factory installation,” explained Wim van Kranen, sales and marketing director for SafanDarley, to ISMR during Blechexpo. “We also are showing an upgraded version of our laser angle measurement device with a new blue light instead of a red light. We switched to blue light because it is less influenced by light from the environment and therefore ensures a better result. The new system also takes much faster measurements.”

## FACE TO FACE

SafanDarley also showcased the latest developments in its Autopol offline software. With its 2014 acquisition of Swedish software maker, FCC Software (inventor of Autopol off-line software), the company has taken an important step towards continuing to be a major player in the precision press brake market.

“The sheet metal market is demanding that press brake operators produce a wider variety of parts at even faster production times. Autopol off-line software offers a solution to avoid stopping the press brake when making a new bending program. Starting with a 3D file, a user programs the press brake off-line. The user generates a press brake file which produces the best forming program. The part program will contain the correct tooling, tooling set-up sheets, bend sequencing, correct part radius and a 3D file showing the operator how the part is formed during each step. By doing this off-line, any problems that may arise can be accounted for before the part reaches the production floor,” van Kranen told ISMR.

Autopol also produces developed blanks and exports a dxf file that can be used to program existing lasers and turret punch presses. Offline programming reduces scrap rates and machine downtime.

## Bending market trends

“We see a trend for customers bending longer and thicker materials (higher tensile strength) in bending markets,” van Kranen told ISMR. “We have delivered a lot of tandem bending equipment this year in Western Europe for long products. Users want higher tonnage machines to process longer material lengths but they still want the flexibility for the machines to also operate as single, stand-alone machines.

“Industry 4.0 is a huge challenge and we need to invest and talk with customers about their demands. High mix, low volume production is also a challenge and people need software and equipment to handle this. Fortunately, the Dutch market is an early adapter market – it shows no fear and will always try new technology. This market is a good testing ground for our new products and technologies.”

He is adamant, however, that software will be a key focus. Software, he told ISMR, will be the new hardware. “We are very well positioned for Industry 4.0 with our software control,” added van Kranen. “Our vision is that the offline world will integrate with the machine online….”

## New US venture

The US is a rapidly growing market for SafanDarley. It has enjoyed double digit growth there in the past year. It has therefore decided to support the market by opening a new support office in Baltimore, US which will be open for business from January 2016. The company has seven distributors in the US, covering the whole country, and supports customers in Canada and Mexico. It has been selling machines to the US since the 1970s.

“There will be a showroom for our equipment and parts supply for the North American market as well as a helpdesk to support customers and our US distributors,” confirmed van Kranen. “The new office will also be a training centre for customers and distributors. Remote diagnostics is possible on our machines. We will also stock machines for the US market in Baltimore, adding features for the final assembly of the US machines in the Baltimore facility.”

SafanDarley expects that the creation of this new US branch will help strengthen its sales growth which is projected to be 10% to 20% in the North American market over the next few years.

“The US market shows good potential – customers wants safer operation and equipment that conforms to regulations. Our high accuracy equipment fits that need as well as demands for greater energy-saving. The Asian market for us is stable. Chinese demand is slowing, which affects the worldwide economy. It has been a great year for us in Europe and we have grown market share in Western Europe,” concluded van Kranen. ■
