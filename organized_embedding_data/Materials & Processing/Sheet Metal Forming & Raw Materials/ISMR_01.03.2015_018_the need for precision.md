## We analyse growth patterns in global medical device markets for sheet metal manufacturers

The medical device sector was affected by the economic crisis, although it has not deterred the industry’s overall growth. Smaller firms were obliged to curb their activity but some chose to merge with larger firms, thereby continuing technological advances without bearing the full burden of the cost. Domestic and international acquisitions and mergers allow a cross-border sharing of resources and knowledge, facilitating continuous medical technology innovation despite the tough economic climate.

To enter the marketplace, medical devices must comply with pre-defined product life cycle parameters intended to maximize patient safety and ensure the highest quality standards. The requirements for materials, production quality and process documentation are high, leading to a demanding environment for production technology and process planning.

## Market challenges and opportunities

According to ReportLinker, the global medical device industry is likely to continue going from strength to strength with the US keeping its top spot for the foreseeable future. The industry as a whole is growing largely because of an ageing global population and climbing societal risk factors. In particular, diet-related chronic diseases, such as cardiovascular disease and diabetes, continue to create demand for medical healthcare services.

The need for medical devices continues to climb, with life expectancy jumping nearly 20 years over the past 50 years. In fact, worldwide demographics are increasingly shifting towards older populations: between 2000 and 2050, populations in developed regions will see those aged over 60 grow from less than 20% to 34%, while the proportion of children aged under 15 is set to decline by 2% from 18%.

According to a United Nations’ report on ageing, by the 2050s, life expectancy at birth will reach 80 years for almost 60% of the global female population. This ageing population, combined with lifestyle-related disease, is the medical device industry’s assurance of increasing market demand.

More evident challenges to the medical device sector include taxes on products in overseas markets as well as incongruous foreign regulatory controls and varying implementation of standards.

## Sports and portable medical devices

Analysts are reporting that the global sports medical devices market is set for steady growth over the next five years. TechNavio’s analysts forecast the Global Sports Medicine Devices market to grow at a CAGR of 4.6 per cent over the period 2014-2019. This growth will also see the emergence of a new market trend; zero defect reliability. To ensure the ultimate reliability and safety of their sports medical devices, organisations are now improving their technologies and developing more precise medical products.

Sports medical devices are defined as those which prevent sports injuries and/or promote rehabilitation, and are the product of the unification of fitness and medicine. This market is traditionally divided into two distinct areas;

surgical devices, which include joint implants and arthroscopy devices to aid reconstruction; and therapeutic devices, comprised of recovery and support devices like braces and crutches, as well as bandages, wraps, tapes and disinfectant wipes.

Analysts are predicting that, as more people attempt to adopt healthier lifestyles by getting more exercise and playing sports, we can expect to see a significant rise in the number of sports injuries occurring over the five-year period in question. This means that the sports medical devices market will grow as a result of the need to keep in line with increased demand for products. In turn, this will also have a knock-on effect on the wider medical devices and sachet packaging markets, which will also experience growth as a result of increased production and distribution.

Portable medical devices are wireless communication interfaces that have long battery life and share robust data processing to offer sophisticated user interface among patients. They are capable of communicating with a physician and individual patients using various types of networks (WLAN, WMAN, WPAN and WWAN).

Portable medical devices such as blood glucose meters, weight scales and blood pressure connect data with medical cloud hub help to maintain a personal medical record, which is stored in a secured place. These devices help to monitor and track patients' health status. TechNavio's analysts forecast the Global Portable Medical Devices market to grow at a CAGR of 8.19 per cent over the period 2014-2019.

## Outlook for the medical device industry in 2015

Published in January 2015, Emergo’s 2015 Medical Device Industry Survey was conducted in December 2014 – January 2015, with over 5,400 respondents. Despite economic uncertainty in some geographic markets, the industry outlook is generally positive going into 2015, reported Emergo.

“Based on our 2015 survey results, the global medical device industry remains stable but different aspects (pricing, regulatory, reimbursement) of the business appear to have become more challenging in 2014. While it is too early to calculate actual 2014 sales figures for the industry, the results would seem to indicate that last year saw more subdued sales growth than 2013. The number of firms reporting sales decreases went from 12% last year to 19% this year. Reported rates of growth were also lower,” it said.

Some changes in 2014 that may have affected results include:

■ A lacklustre European economy is having an impact on performance of companies exporting to Europe, and German device exporters.

■ Brazil’s economy has slowed compared to recent years and could dampen industry exports to this region.

■ A stronger-than-expected US economy helped to counterbalance a weak market in Europe. The US accounts for 38% of the global device market. Nonetheless, a strong US dollar is making American devices more expensive to overseas buyers.

## Key takeaways from the 2015 survey were:

■ 75% of the 5,400+ survey participants are positive about prospects for the industry in 2015.

■ Companies located in North/South America and Asia are more optimistic than those located in Europe.

■ China, Japan, Russia and Brazil have become more difficult from a regulatory compliance perspective.

■ Funding/capital is the number one challenge for executives of small companies.

■ Pricing pressure and regulatory changes are biggest challenges for large companies.

Companies expect China and US markets to perform well in 2015 compared to others. Larger companies saw more modest sales gains in 2014 compared to smaller companies. The majority of US companies did not make major changes in response to the US medical device excise tax.

“Optimism about the economy is high in the US, with people wondering how long the rally can last. Europe, on the other hand, is lagging, with consumer confidence and economic prospects quite low. Our past surveys have shown high confidence levels among companies in Asia, but those numbers are slipping,” said Emergo.

Notably, confidence among Asian companies seems to be slipping. In 2014, 83% were positive, and that has fallen to 77% this year. The decline is probably due to the much slower rate of growth in the Chinese economy, continued weak economic performance in Japan and diminished hope for export growth to markets in Europe, says Emergo.

“Most firms (nearly 85%) again expect positive outlooks for their companies in 2015, more so than for their industry overall (10% difference). This makes sense, as most people do not expect their own company to fare worse than the industry as a whole. Nearly half of the smallest firms had ‘very positive’ personal outlooks, while mid-sized and large firms were somewhat less exuberant, probably due to the regulatory and pricing pressures they are encountering.

“Even though economic growth in China is slowing, it still is extremely robust (at 7%) compared to every other major market worldwide. The Chinese government continues to pour money into improving access to healthcare, and with that investment and rising standards of living comes a need for medical devices. This will continue and is one reason why executives are bullish on China.

“The US continues to surprise many with its sustained economic performance and rosy outlook for 2015. Even though US healthcare spending growth has slowed and pricing pressures continue, executives still see good potential for revenue growth,” concluded Emergo.

## Surgical robots

Surgical robots are gaining widespread acceptance across the globe as they enhance the surgeon’s abilities in terms of surgical imaging, navigation, planning and instrument manipulation. New analysis from Frost & Sullivan, “Innovations in Medical Robotics”, finds that the global surgical robotics market is top-heavy, with the leading participants having cutting-edge technologies and large patent libraries. The industry also has an abundance of smaller participants – companies, spinoffs and research universities – who are working on innovations in medical robotics.

A pacemaker welded by a laser

Surgical robotics technology is making its way into practically every major surgical discipline despite the long time-to-market caused by stringent regulatory requirements, complex manufacturing processes and extensive R&D.

"The global demand for surgical robots is particularly high for prostatectomy and hysterectomy surgeries, as they are believed to offer greater safety, accuracy and precision, and in turn lead to better clinical outcomes for the surgeon, patient and hospital management," noted Technical Insights research analyst, Geethu Roshan Verghese. "The market will continue to gradually prosper due to rising awareness, higher per capita income and a growing ageing population." ■

We preview the co-located BLECH India and Fastener Fair India shows which will take place this April in Mumbai

Both BLECH India and Fastener Fair India will return to the Bombay Exhibition Centre in Mumbai in Spring 2015. Both events are colocated there with BLECH India taking place from 22-25 April 2015 and Fastener Fair India being held from 23-24 April 2015. The Bombay Exhibition Centre (BEC) in Mumbai is one of the largest exhibition centres in India and is close to airports, train stations and the city centre.

Mumbai, in the state of Maharashtra, is the financial capital of India. The region generates 46 per cent of India’s overall national output. Maharashtra is also India’s most industrialised state and home to companies such as Audi India, Skoda India and Mahindra & Mahindra (passenger vehicles and farm tractors). Major automotive companies are based in Pune, only 150km from Mumbai. The Mumbai/Pune car manufacturing cluster contributes to 33 per cent of the Indian market.

India’s positive investment climate and vast consumer markets have resulted in a decade of outstanding growth. The country has witnessed significant development over the last few decades with the manufacturing and automotive sectors growing rapidly. India’s steel consumption grew 3.4 per cent to 5.8 million tonnes in April 2014 over the same month a year previously. The growth is set to continue over the next few years as the Indian government continues to develop its country’s public infrastructure. Continuing demand for consumer goods, such as cars, white goods, electronic appliances and electronic equipment from its domestic market, is contributing to the growth of the sheet metal working industry.

## BLECH India in focus

BLECH India, the technology exhibition for sheet metal working, is taking place for the fourth time from 22 – 25 April 2015. The event will be held at the Bombay Exhibition Centre (BEC) in Mumbai, one of the largest exhibition centres in India which hosts a variety of prestigious international trade exhibitions. BLECH India encompasses all core sectors of sheet metal working technology such as sheet metal; finished and semi-finished products; forming; separation and joining technology; machine elements; handling; surface treatment and tools/dies.
