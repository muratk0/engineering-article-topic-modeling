## Why galvanize?

"It is estimated that up to four per cent of the world's GDP is lost through corrosion each year, a fact that makes lifecycle costs a key consideration in many modern construction projects. Sustainability is also a significant factor when choosing materials and contractors due to ethical decisions as well as enforced regulations," the Group explained.

“Hot dip galvanizing has long been seen as the most environmentally friendly finishing process available to prevent corrosion. Galvanizing is highly sustainable and produces minimal waste; any zinc that does not instantly form a coating on the metal remains in the galvanizing bath for re-use. The non-ferrous properties of the metal make it indefinitely recyclable without loss of physical or chemical properties," it added.

Galvanized products, once constructed or installed, can also be removed, re-galvanized and re-used. They are also easily recycled with steel scrap in the steel production process. Galvanizing offers durability (a permanently bonded tough coating); long life (70 years plus maintenance-free); value for money (low overall costs compared to other coatings) and a hygienic solution (easy-to-clean surface).

The process is relatively simple, straightforward and closely controlled. The thicknesses (weights) of coatings formed are regular, predictable and simply specified. A full protection coating can be applied in hours; a complicated paint system can require a week. The coating is also tough; the hot dip process produces a coating which is bonded metallurgically to the steel. Galvanized steel therefore has high resistance to mechanical damage during handling, storage, transport and construction (an important factor when steelwork is to be shipped around the world).

“Because it is formed by dipping steel into molten zinc, all parts of the surface of the steel are coated—inside, outside, awkward corners and narrow gaps which would be impossible to protect in any other way. The coating tends to build up at vital corners and edges, rather than thinning out as is often the case with brushed, sprayed and other dipped coatings," explained the Group.

Galvanized steel also simplifies inspection of the protective finish. Thicknesses, specified through EN ISO 1461, can be easily checked with an electronic probe.

"Additionally, because hot dip galvanizing takes place after a product has already been fabricated, the steel is coated with a tough and abrasion-resistant zinc coating externally and internally. This means that the steel product arrives on site ready for immediate use. No further site surface preparation, painting, touchup or inspection is necessary, and installation can begin immediately (speeding up the construction time)," added the Group.
