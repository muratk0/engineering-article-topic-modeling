# New hot stamping process

[TARİH: 01.11.2023 ISMR]

As part of the "best Award 2023" at Blechexpo 2023, Schuler won second place in the 'Press and Forming Technologies' category with its innovative 'Aluminium Hot Stamping FAST Technology'.

Parts made from high-strength aluminium sheets are increasingly being used in vehicles to save weight. Schuler now offers a new process on its hydraulic presses for hot stamping, where the light metal is heated up very quickly using contact heating and formed in a water-cooled die. All process steps are performed in the die so, in contrast to the hot stamping of steel sheets, no separate furnace is required.

“This not only reduces the space requirements for the system, but also very significantly lowers the investment and operating costs," explained Christian Palm, responsible for the hydraulic presses division at Schuler. “That makes hot stamping of aluminium a real alternative in sheet metal forming. The technology makes it possible to produce light and high-strength parts with a significantly higher degree of forming, compared to cold forming, and offers a much greater scope for design." Highly complex geometries and a tensile strength of 300 to 400 newton per square millimetre can be achieved.

In contrast to previous processes for hot stamping aluminium sheet, the components can be further processed immediately and do not require several hours of heat treatment to harden them. This reduces production costs even further. The first system is already being manufactured for an automotive supplier in Asia.

InspecVision's managing director, Dr. Jan Antonis, outlines his view of sheet metal markets, trends and technology advances to ISMR.

ISMR SAYS: “InspecVision's company strategy is focused on two key elements: growth and innovation."

InspecVision designs, manufactures and sells vision systems for automated inspection and reverse engineering. It specialises in the design and manufacturing of measurement systems for industrial applications that utilise computer vision-based technology, with roots that originated in the sheet metal industry. The company is a TRUMPF Partner and was awarded the prestigious Queen's Award for Enterprise in International Trade in 2021 for its export performance.

Its 2D and 3D non-contact optical metrology machines use modern camera technology for automatic quality inspection and reverse engineering of components for industries such as aerospace, automotive and electronics. Its precision metrology equipment captures large quantities of measurement data at high speed without touching the part to enable faster rates of inspection, digitised reporting and continuous quality control at all stages of production.

The founder and managing director of the company, Dr. Jan Antonis, carried out advanced research in computer vision systems at Queens University Belfast and saw a gap in the market for a system which could measure parts quickly and easily. Sales of the Planar inspection system, for quality control and reverse engineering applications, began in 2005. InspecVision products are now sold in over 30 countries worldwide. In this interview, he outlines his view of sheet metal markets, trends, technology advances and new project developments.

ISMR: Please highlight any particular successes and achievements, as well as any significant targets reached, over the past two years (of which you are particularly proud)?

JA: We are particularly proud this year to have won the Federation of Small Businesses Exporter of the Year 2023 for the Northern Ireland region as well as being finalists in the export categories for both the Belfast Telegraph Business Awards and the Made in Northern Ireland Manufacturing Awards. We were also delighted to receive the prestigious Queen's Award for Enterprise in International Trade in 2021.

A major achievement this year was the launch of our latest Opti-Scan 3D scanner. It has a 2m-wide measuring area and is currently the largest 3D scanner available in the market. We are proud to keep delivering innovative inspection products that help our customers across sheet metal markets.

ISMR: What are your views on the current global business climate/markets for sheet metal professionals? How do you view 2024?

JA: There are always external factors of which we need to be mindful such as inflation, higher interest rates and new regulatory environments. I think the trends we see towards digitisation and automation across the entire sheet metal fabrication industry will continue in 2024.

Our customers face the same challenges that we do e.g. rising costs and skills shortages. They are now looking for solutions which streamline production processes and reduce their carbon footprint. Our highspeed, automated inspection systems do just that.

ISMR: Which investments have you made over the past two-three years (machine tools, factory, staff etc.).

JA: InspecVision has made significant investment internally. It has focused a lot on its own automation of processes as well as building a more sustainable workflow within the company. This, I think, reflects what is happening more widely in the sheet metal market. People are turning to software solutions to address rising costs.

ISMR: What percentage of your turnover do you spend on R&D? Which R&D areas are you focusing on now?

JA: We invest around 12% of turnover back into R&D, which is important for us to so we can keep innovating. In general, the industry has spent a lot of time developing solutions to automate production but there are many processes where it is not cost-effective e.g. small production runs.

At Blechexpo in Stuttgart, we demonstrated the first iteration of our guidance and verification system which uses machine learning, AI and augmented reality to guide manual assembly operations and ensure that they have been performed correctly. We see applications for this in welding, aircraft assembly and wiring looms.

## Measurement systems

InspecVision specialises in automated 2D and 3D measurement systems for inspection and reverse engineering of sheet metal parts at every stage of production, flat, folded, welded or assembled. OEMs and sub-contract manufacturers use its technology for quality control, improving efficiency and reducing waste.

The systems are high speed, low maintenance, can be supported remotely and are available in a wide range of sizes and accuracies. Products include the Planar 2D rapid measurement tool for 2D parts and Opti-Scan 3D, a 3D inspection system that can measure surfaces and edges in 3D. InspecVision's Area of Interest (AOI) upgrade measures countersinks; bevels; chamfers; blind holes and surface features.

"Our advantage as a small business is our fast development cycle and our ability to bring products to market quickly when a gap in the market is identified. This allows us to also offer bespoke and customised solutions in addition to our standard products. Continuous development of our software ensures that we retain existing customers and attract new ones. Our systems save companies hours of inspection time, improve part accuracy, increase the percentage of parts inspected and reduce waste," the company explained.

Research and development is at the heart of InspecVision's strategy and we work very closely with our partners in export markets to identify customer needs and provide solutions. Existing products, such as our SurfScan option for the Planar 2D inspection system, utilises Augmented Reality to enable operators to visually identify good and bad parts or to identify if particular features, such as holes, are missing.

ISMR: What is your strategic focus/vision over the next few years? Do you project any growth in turnover for this year, compared to last year?

JA: Our company strategy is focused on two key elements: growth and innovation. 2023 has been a very positive year for InspecVision resulting in 28% of turnover growth compared to last year.

We want to serve the sheet metal industry by continuing to develop new technologies which help our customers to achieve their quality management goals. The existing product range will continue to be developed and updated as we believe that innovation doesn't halt when a system is purchased. We want all our customers to benefit from improvements.

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in global sheet metal/inspection markets?

JA: Increasingly, we see both customers and suppliers working in strategic partnerships to deliver integrated solutions. As part of the drive towards unattended production, greater integration is required between different systems on the factory floor. As part of our partner programme with TRUMPF, we have integrated TRUMPF geo files import/export into our software.

ISMR: How have you embraced sustainability/energy efficiency in your products, processes and operations?

JA: Due to rising energy costs and in an effort to improve sustainability, we are adding solar panels to our production facility which should reduce our running costs significantly within five years.

As a family business, we recognise the importance of value. Our machines are inexpensive to run and durable so are built to last. They use more energy-efficient LED lights. We design our products to be highly modular and upgradable. We are regularly contacted by customers who want to add a newly released option, so are able to keep even our older machines up to date with the latest improvements. This helps our customers to deliver on their sustainability goals.
