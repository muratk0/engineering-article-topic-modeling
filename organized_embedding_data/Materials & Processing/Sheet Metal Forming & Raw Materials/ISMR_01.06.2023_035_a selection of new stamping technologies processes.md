It is a complex process that includes several metalforming techniques — blanking; punching; flanging; drawing; embossing; trimming; coining and piercing, for example. The cold-forming process uses tools, dies and stamping presses to transform sheet metal into different shapes. Stamping presses utilise the force of a moving ram to transmit force, or an amount of tonnage, to specific tooling to achieve a product's final shape, often with little or no scrap and, whenever possible, with minimal operator intervention. Stamping dies are the tools that shape and cut sheet-metal parts.

Hot stamping entails heating the material until it becomes malleable. It is subsequently subjected to a formation and rapid cooling process to produce a transformed and hardened material. This process requires fewer steps than the traditional stamping process to achieve lighter and more resistant products.

Some of the most common types of metal stamping machines include mechanical, hydraulic and servo presses that are fitted with multiple dies to cut and shape the sheets of stainless steel and metals such as aluminium, zinc and copper. Better control, greater speed and versatility, shorter cycle times and higher energy savings are just some of the hallmarks of the latest technologies. In this article, we highlight an alphabetical selection of the latest new global stamping and pressing innovations.

## Stamping innovations

Founded in Honjo, Tokyo, in 1917, AIDA is a global specialist in the design; manufacture; sale; service and support; refurbishment and modernisation of metal stamping presses and metalforming automation equipment. It is a global supplier of servo and mechanical metal stamping presses, automation equipment and complete metalforming systems.

AIDA servo and mechanical stamping presses range in capacity from 35 to 4,000 tons; from a few to 800 strokes per minute; from gap frame to straightside; high-speed to cold forging, transfer and progressive die; material handling and coil-feeding equipment.

A key target for AIDA is the electric vehicle market, where its business has been rapidly growing. The stamping specialist has its eye on two particular areas: battery case production and bipolar plate lines. It is also investigating the market for hydrogen fuel cell production. Two product lines that are dedicated to the production of motor laminations are the AIDA HMX Series and AIDA MSP Series presses. The AIDA MSP press enables users to produce motors such as EV motors, HEV motors and energy-efficient home appliance motors

AIDA's new DSF-NE2 presses are equipped with their own low-RPM, high-torque servo motor for metalforming applications. Its DSF-TE4 servo transfer presses (double-column split presses designed for transfer applications) offer high levels of customisation through multiple configurations. AIDA's press line series also includes servo presses, high-speed automatic presses for lamination and precision forming presses.

“Our new 400-ton DSF-NE2 monolithic servo press line received a 'Product of the Year' award, in the metalforming and punching category, at the EuroBLECH 2022 Awards. We will continue developing this line of presses," AIDA told ISMR at the EuroBLECH exhibition last October.

During the exhibition, AIDA also presented its new AET electronic transfer system, an after-market solution which enables handling of blanks and parts in multi-station or interconnected presses. The new range of transfer equipment can be retrofitted on existing machines (and not necessarily just AIDA presses).

The stamping specialist also arranged live demonstrations of ADMS (AIDA digital motion system), the solution it developed to analyse and optimise transfer management for servo and mechanical presses. Visitors to its stand at EuroBLECH could see demonstrations of AiCARE (AIDA information care system), enabling users to receive information and data from AIDA servo and mechanical presses on machine status and production quality.

AIDA developed simulation software for its transfer presses to make them more productive. The other software package is related to Industry-4.0 solutions (machine monitoring and machine learning so that AIDA can improve press reliability).

Together with GEDIA, AP&T has created what it dubs "the world's most advanced production line for manufacturing presshardened components for the automotive industry."

"Our latest press-hardening production solution lays the groundwork for providing car manufacturers with lighter, safer, more flexible designs that are cost- and energy-efficient," explained press specialist, AP&T. "The solution includes our servo-hydraulic press, with up to 70% lower energy consumption than conventional hydraulic presses, and TemperBox® (which enables soft and hard zones to be combined in the same finished component)."

In October 2022, AP&T received an order for its 150th press-hardening line. The customer was Liuzhou Saike, subsidiary of one of China's largest auto manufacturers, SAIC Motor.

AP&T is now adding factory-rebuilt presses to its customer offering.

Italian manufacturer Balconi Presseccentriche S.p.A. produces transfer, blanking and servo presses, as well as presses for progressive tools, impact extrusion presses and fin die presses. Its press model 4DMRF-LD-2000 has a 2,000kN cushion and three-axis electronic transfer. The press can produce up to 27 strokes per minute.

Boldrini is a manufacturer of hydraulic dished-end presses, flanging machines and manipulators. They feature high speed including approach, return and, particularly, pressure build-up timing that, it told ISMR, results in a substantial increase in head production.

Swiss stamping specialist, Bruderer AG, offers its BSTA range of presses with press forces from 180 to 2500kN and a stroke speed range of 100 to 2300spm. The lever system distributes the load acting on the ram in the system. Its press energy-saving mode enables customers to reduce energy use over several stages and can be set automatically and at staggered intervals.

“This load distribution, the minimal bearing clearances and the efficient lubrication system are the main reasons for the long life and consistently high precision of our stamping presses," it told ISMR.
