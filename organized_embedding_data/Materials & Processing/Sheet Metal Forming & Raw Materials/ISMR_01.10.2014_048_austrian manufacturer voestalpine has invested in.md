# Austrian manufacturer Voestalpine has invested in a slag removal, deburring and edge-rounding system to increase its production capacity and improve consistency

[TARİH: 01.10.2014 ISMR]

Processing over 60,000 tons of steel plate per year, Austrian manufacturer Voestalpine has invested in a slag removal, deburring and edge-rounding system from Lissmac to increase its production capacity and improve consistency.

At the start of the steel plate production process at Voestalpine, the parts are cut by plasma or oxy-fuel cutting machines. Whilst remarkably efficient and fast, this heavy-duty cutting process is well known for leaving large burrs, slag and oxidation on the edges of the plates that are cut.

Walter Radinger, process manager at Voestalpine, explained: “We process over 60,000 tons of steel plate each year for the automotive industry with components including structural parts and doors, bonnets, tail-gates and boot lids to name a few. What we needed was a process that was simple to set up and would provide the best available deburring quality. Additionally, we needed something that could debur both sides of the steel plates in a single pass simultaneously. The machine would need to be energy-efficient, low on maintenance costs and easy to maintain.”

“In the end, we found that only Lissmac could fulfill all our criteria. We were especially convinced by the performance of the slag removal machine. This was particularly the case when we cut thicker plates of 25mm and above where we historically have a lot of issues with large slag and burrs. We immediately started looking at a second machine that could grind off the remaining burr and round off the edges. We visited Lissmac Maschinenbau GmbH in Bad Wurzach, Germany again for a trial at its factory. The results were excellent and made it easy for us to order the second machine,” he added.

## Innovative slag removal

With parts that can weigh up to 35kg each, it made sense for Voestalpine to purchase a machine that could process parts in just one pass. Productivity could be increased and the process could be made more ergonomic and efficient for the machine operators.

To this end, Voestalpine conducted numerous investigations and eventually selected the SBM-M 1500 D2 slag removal machine from Lissmac. This machine is connected in-line with a SMB-XL 1500 G2S2 de-burring and edge rounding machine to efficiently complete the entire process using one production cell. The steel plates are stacked onto a pallet and then placed onto a lifting table in front of the slag removal machine. The table then raises to an ergonomic height for the operator who can then move the parts onto a roller conveyor. From here, they are transported through the machine by drive rollers and conveyor belts.

During this first pass, the heavy slag is removed from top and bottom with hardened steel pins (power pins) mounted on two belts on the top and another two belts at the bottom. The process is simple and user-friendly as it allows the operator to set the through-feed speed and the material thickness of each individual job or batch of parts. The top and bottom units can be adjusted independently for instances where the slag or burrs may be thicker on one face than the other.

According to Walter Radinger, Voestalpine runs the two machine system in-line. This ensures that parts go though the de-slagging machine and then carry on through the de-burring and the edge-rounding machine in one ergonomically flowing production cell. Despite this cell type of set-up, Voestalpine still has the flexibility of just de-slagging or deburring.

## Edge-rounding process

After the initial de-slagging process, the parts will move onto a roller table which leads them into the SBM-XL 1500 G2S2 de-burring and edge-rounding machine. With eight heads used to deburr and round edges, the first four heads are equipped with abrasive belts that grind-off upstanding burrs from the top and the bottom of the profiles. When they are free from burrs, the next four belts (equipped with abrasive flaps) will round off the edges on both sides simultaneously. The edge-rounding belts run in both directions across the parts to make sure that all edges are finished in a uniform way. Depending upon the weight of the parts, they are either hoisted by crane or hand onto a pallet for the next process.

The de-burring machine is adjustable through the touch-screen control system. Normally, it is just necessary to adjust material thickness and processing speed. However, the Lissmac machine enables operators to individually adjust the grinding heads or switch off any heads if they are not needed. The machines are 1.4m and 2.1m long and can process parts that have a maximum thickness of 120mm. To further improve operator health and safety, the machines have a dust-extraction system which is fully integrated to prevent any airborne particulate affecting the operators during the process. ■

## Process efficiency

SALVAGNINI will showcase its innovative spirit and role in the development of systems for sheet metalworking at this year’s EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover with solutions aimed at obtaining maximum process efficiency.

The company’s S4+P4 line, in a new compact version, is designed for effective manufacturing flow of bent and punched parts.

It aims to increase flexibility and production. Its new smart FlexCell solution, stand-alone machines controlled by proprietary software, is designed to guarantee efficient cell management and increase production effectiveness and ease of use.

For manual bending, Salvagnini has launched its ATA device (Automatic Tool Adjuster), the automatic tool changer mounted on a B3 press-brake. Tool length can be adjusted automatically in a few seconds enabling parametric parts, single batches or kit to be produced. The company’s new solution for bending metal parts, the P2 lean, has been optimised to widen the bending capacity to a variety of sheet metal parts, including small and narrow profiles. It boasts an average consumption of around 5kW during operation, as the direct electric drive has made it possible to eliminate the hydraulic power pack and all circuits.

Savagnini will also present its L5 fiber laser, equipped with full rapid automation for unmanned production from the stored metal sheets to cut parts, which are separated, labelled and sent to the downstream operation. Its cutting design has been further enhanced thanks to the latest evolutions in on-the-fly cutting. The machine features important new technological innovations in the cutting process, such as the new APC adaptive piercing control which dramatically reduces overall processing times, simultaneously increasing quality in a vast range of materials and thicknesses.

## Welding simulation

SIMUFACT ENGINEERING has advanced its welding simulation software, Simufact.welding, and is releasing its new software version 4. Significant innovations for the current product version include the integration of a new simplification method to shorten the calculation time for welding processes, the optional availability of an integrated Altair®-based meshing tool and a whole range of functions which considerably improve the software usability.

“Simufact.welding is a Finite-Element-based software with the latest solver technology, with which elastic-plastic material behaviour can be modelled. As one of its most important tasks, the software realistically predicts the distortions and residual stresses that occur during welding, while considering phase transformations and the control of these in the component. Simufact.welding considers microstructural properties in the heat-affected zone enabling conclusions about the properties of the weld seam and, in particular, its strength. The user gains valuable clues to identify welding defects such as hot cracks in the simulation to avoid them in practice,” the company explained.

The software assists in finding the optimal clamping devices for the welding process and delivers facts to help determine the best welding sequence. It also predicts the final contour of the component and helps to produce

parts in series with precise tolerance.   
In Simufact.welding 4, simulation videos can be generated incrementally-based or in real-time. The user can define, by section, at which speed certain processes are shown in the video; he or she can, for example, show processes of   
long-duration such as the component cooling in time lapse. In the result, the user can create a vividly cinematic illustration of the welding process, in which all relevant details are shown in one video by varying the illustration speed and angle. See www.simufact.de ■
