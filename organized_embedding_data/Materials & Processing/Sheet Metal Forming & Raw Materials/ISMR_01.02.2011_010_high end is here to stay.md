# HIGH END IS HERE TO STAY

[TARİH: 01.02.2011 ISMR]

Hilmar F. John explains the strategy his firm, Metalle in Form Geräteteile GmbH, will follow over the next few years.

ISMR SAYS:

"Greater vertical integration will be essential for sheet metal shops of the future"

Metalle in Form Geräteteile GmbH in Karlsruhe, Germany was founded in 1997 and currently has 35 employees. It is a systems supplier of high precision parts, functional units and equipment, offering precision CNC machining of sheet metal with final mounting of complete assemblies. Emphasis has been placed on instrumentation and control technology and laboratory equipment.

Its latest investments have been in TRUMPF machinery – the TruMatic 3000, TruBend 5085 and TrumaBend V130 – which add to its portfolio of CNC punching machines and press brakes on the shop floor.

Hilmar F. John, CEO, is consistent in planning for the future of his company, Metalle in Form Geräteteile GmbH. He explains the strategy his firm will follow during the next few years.

ISMR: WHAT IS THE CRITICAL SUCCESS FACTOR FOR FIRMS LIKE YOURS?

HJ An unmistakable identity. I feel that greater vertical integration will be essential for sheet metal shops in the future. Pure job shop operations are going to face greater challenges. Because manufacturing companies will be thinking about “build or buy”, they will be identifying the manufacturing work they can sensibly do in-house and the major assignments that can be outsourced to system suppliers. That means that the company can concentrate its efforts on development and sales. The objective is to deal competently with short product life cycles and changes at short notice. In addition, it is necessary to master delivery of small lots on demand. It is quite clear that buyers are going to continue to shift inventories toward the suppliers.

## Another factor involves flexibility with regard to batch sizes and piece counts for individual orders

ISMR: AND SO THE CUSTOMER IS LOOKING FOR A LONG-TERM RELATIONSHIP?

HJ Yes, the customer isn’t into vendor switching. This creates mutual dependencies, but can also culminate in constructive partnerships. This is often the case even today. Extended umbrella contracts form the basis.

The customer’s first question is: What is your delivery cycle? Weekly? Bi-weekly? Monthly? System suppliers have to come to terms with that. They need to make their own procedures as transparent as if they were a department in the customer’s organisation.

ISMR: IS THERE A CLEAR TREND THAT FLANKS THIS SCENARIO?

HJ When I look into the future I see an unavoidable split. We will be getting those large volumes of simple parts from Asia. But when I see the high quality of the sheet metal products coming out of Malaysia, for example – it’s simply dizzying. But buying one-off components individually from Malaysia simply makes no sense. So they will be supplying only mass production products. Everything that runs towards the high end – the fields such as medical, laboratory or instrumentation technology that we serve – will stay in Germany. That is why we have clearly decided to concentrate on selected markets.

ISMR: WILL ADVICE, CONSULTING AND SUPPORT BECOME MORE IMPORTANT?

HJ Yes, simply because products are becoming more complex with many considerations involved: design specifications, continuously stretching feasibility limits through process innovations, the best use of automated manufacturing steps, reducing materials consumption and reducing the number of individual parts.

Today, it is not just a single salesperson who calls on customers. Instead, two or three people – from quality assurance, technology and engineering, for example – will be on hand. System suppliers are also being asked to help trim administrative work. Our first step is to examine and improve all the internal procedures that we ourselves find to be disturbing. Only then will the customer be satisfied.

5 TIPS

“We have defined five long-term criteria for success.”

## 1. COMMUNICATION

A change of generations and new media are changing the interaction and the nature of cooperation among partners; we have to be prepared to master the changes.

## 2. QUALIFICATIONS

A structural change is taking place among our personnel. Skilled workers will have to be even better trained and exhibit enormous flexibility. What will be needed in particular are good newcomers to operate complex machines and systems on the shop floor.

## 3. IDENTITY

Manufacturers of assemblies and systems will have to concentrate on their core competencies and clearly distinguish their own capabilities from those of the competition.

## 4. CONSULTING CAPACITIES

Systems suppliers will have to provide comprehensive and capable advice to the customer. Delivering the best quality at the agreed date goes without saying.

## 5. PROCESS INNOVATION

The entire process will become more complex. Attention will have to be paid not only to the technical aspects but also, and to an even greater extent, to administration and to business management capabilities.
