## POSCO

POSTECH has signed an MoU/MoA with Institut Teknologi Bandung (ITB), a research university in Indonesia, pledging to promote joint research on steel technology. The two universities agreed to launch joint research activities on steel technology development, as well as exchanges of research staff. ## TATA STEEL

The Flat Products complex at Tata Steel constitutes the latest facilities comprising LD2 and slab caster, hot strip mill and cold rolling mill with two galvanising lines. The company recently launched the first branded cold rolled steel under the name Tata Steelium. Tata Steel’s coated sheet offers an advantage in corrosion resistance and increased paintability making the product suitable for automotive body parts. The galvanised sheets are widely used in automobile applications due to their easy weldability as the coating layers are made of zinc alloy. Skin-passed galvanised sheets with zero spangles find application in a variety of white goods.

In FY 11, the Flat Division at Tata developed high strength quality galvanised steels for customers in Europe and the Middle East and established a market for shipbuilding grade with customers in Bangladesh. It also obtained new approval for the supply of steel for skin panel and internal components from a leading automotive customer in South East Asia.

Its four research groups in India and more than 10 departments in Europe are actively engaged in developing new products. The research output of these units cater to the needs of the automotive sector that include the development of advanced high strength steels, new forming techniques, new and improved joining techniques, innovative coatings, improved fatigue life of components etc. The aim of its flat steels thrust area is to develop a steel grade with 1000 mpa yield strength and 50 per cent elongation. Its advanced coatings thrust area is aimed at developing a suitable advanced coating on steel sheets to minimise the use of replacement of zinc (zero zinc) and to develop chrome-free passivation for galvanised sheets.

Tata Steel has now unveiled a new centre which will develop and demonstrate ultimate low-carbon, low-energy sustainable construction technologies. Meanwhile the 20 million SPECIFIC project at Baglan in South Wales has been set up to study other new coatings for steel and other substrates that can generate power.

## THYSSENKRUPP

Optimised ThyssenKrupp VDM material offers strong corrosion performance to resist highly aggressive acids. The research laboratories of ThyssenKrupp VDM have succeeded in further developing the tried and tested material Alloy 31. The new Nicrofer 3426 hMo occupies a position between high-alloy nickel materials and stainless steels and is a superaustenitic.

ThyssenKrupp VDM (Werdohl) manufactures high-performance materials, including nickel alloys, special stainless steels and zirconium and titanium mill products. ThyssenKrupp VDM has been supplying sheet, plate, rods, ingots, strip and wire for more than 80 years.

ThyssenKrupp Steel Europe AG is to invest around 300 million euros in its hot strip mills in Bochum and Duisburg. The investments will help the steel producer strengthen its position as a supplier of premium flat-rolled carbon steel products.

Hot-rolled strip is the basis for all ThyssenKrupp Steel Europe’s flat steel products. The company operates four hot strip mills with a total annual capacity of around 15 million metric tons. Hot strip mills 1 and 2 are located at the Duisburg site, along with a casting-rolling line that also produces hot-rolled. Hot strip mill 3 is in Bochum. Among other things, the hot strip mills now to be modernised produce lightweight steels for the automotive industry, starting material for tinplate – which is 100 per cent recyclable and is used to make food and beverage cans – as well as steels for oil and gas pipelines.

Technische Universität Bergakademie Freiberg (Freiberg University of Mining and Technology) and MgF Magnesium Flachprodukte GmbH (MgF) have inaugurated a new hot rolling mill for magnesium. As ferritic stainless steel has low thermal expansion coefficient, high corrosion resistance and more economical costs, it is used more widely in automotive exhaust system components. Built with 7.5 million euros in funding from the state of Saxony, the mill will be operated by the university’s Institute of Metal Forming. It extends an innovative production line for magnesium flat products developed jointly by the university and MgF. MgF is a subsidiary of ThyssenKrupp Steel Europe AG, which is part of the ThyssenKrupp Group. Magnesium is the lightest structural metal. It weighs only around a quarter as much a steel and is 35 percent lighter than aluminium. Parts made from magnesium are particularly attractive for automotive OEMs as their lower weight allows reductions in vehicle CO2 emissions. In the past, however, the material has only been used in the form of castings, for example in the chassis or in transmission housings and engine blocks. For large body parts, where the light material could save a lot more weight, affordable magnesium sheets are required.

The new rolling mill reduces the four to seven millimetre thick magnesium strip from the casting-rolling line to a thickness of just over one millimetre. This opens the way for the use of flat-rolled magnesium in automotive body parts, for example. The mill processes up to two metric tons of magnesium strip per hour at a rolling speed of more than 80 metres per minute.

## VOESTALPINE

In the new business year 2011/12, Voestalpine has set aside more than EUR 120 million for product and technology development. Compared to 2010/11, this is an increase of 10 per cent viewed across the Group, with a 25 per cent surge in the Special Steel Division.

“In addition to continuing development of lightweight materials for the automotive and consumer goods industries, we are also focusing on the most technologically challenging applications in the energy sector such as high-temperature materials that enable a significantly higher efficiency in power plant turbines and aircraft engines,” said Peter Schwab, Voestalpine’s head of research.

A major award for groundbreaking research by Voestalpine Stahl GmbH and Fronius GmbH was announced last year. The jointly developed steel aluminium blank was awarded the Innovation Prize by specialist magazine Autorevue. Although the welding of steel and aluminium has been considered virtually impossible for decades due to the materials’ different material properties, a research team at Voestalpine’s Linz site have achieved the breakthrough. The advantage of the metallurgic combination using a CMT welding and soldering process is that the hybrid blanks can be reformed into construction parts after the soldering process – in the same way as with conventionally laser-welded steel blanks. The front carriage, cabin and roof are possible areas where the blanks could be used in vehicle production. ■
