# SMOOTHING THE WAY

[TARİH: 01.11.2022 ISMR]

Paul Hartendorp, managing director of Timesavers International BV, gave ISMR a sneak peek of the company's latest technologies, strategic roadmap and global market outlook at EuroBLECH 2022.

Timesavers International specialises in the surface finishing of sheet metal, including operations such as deburring; edge rounding; laser oxide and heavy slag removal and finishing/precision grinding. Operating from three facilities in the USA, the Netherlands and China, the organisation supplies customers worldwide.

A continuous focus on R&D and innovation to maintain efficiency and consistency in surface finishing has characterised the company over the years. A firm accent on energy efficiency and productivity marked its product offerings on its booth at this October's EuroBLECH sheet-metal exhibition in Germany.

ISMR sat down with Paul Hartendorp, managing director of Timesavers International BV, at the exhibition to discuss the company's latest technologies, strategic roadmap and global market outlook.

We have set a new strategic path and are growing the company worldwide with acquisitions in the U.S., as well as other global acquisitions

ISMR: What are you showcasing on your booth at EuroBLECH this year?

PH: We are delighted to present the latest version of our compact rotary brush machine: the 32 HWRB series. Not only has the rotary brush carousel been optimised, but the machine also has an additional feature: the Hammerhead. The rotary brush creates a uniform radius on all edges of sheet metal parts up to a width of 1100mm. It is even possible to combine edge rounding with the removal of oxide skin on steel parts. The 32- series HWRB is equipped with a dust extractor (Timesavers x Absaugwerk).

We are also displaying our 22 RB series with pictogram control. The machine has a working width of 600mm so is a great solution for companies that produce deburred and rounded parts on a smaller scale. Our large 42- RB series, with Industry 4.0 functionalities, for removing large burrs can also be seen. It is our largest deburring machine, available in working widths of 1350mm and 1600mm. The machine can create a radius of 2mm on parts, with the eight rotary brushes, to ensure quality of coating and painting.

The 12 series WB is a starter model with a 600mm working width for the deburring and finishing of small parts. This machine has an abrasive grinding belt and brush. Last, but not least, you can find our entry-level deburring machine, the 10 series Manual Grinder, on our stand. This solution is three-to-four times' faster than deburring by hand and is very easy to use.

The rest of our innovations here at EuroBLECH concern modifications, updates and new features on equipment. One of the most important market drivers right now is energy efficiency. All the motor drives that we use are EA3 (the highest energy- and efficiency-saving class). We apply frequency drives to all motors in our machines (they only draw energy for the job that needs to be done and then automatically fall back to lower energy usage). This particularly impacts vacuum-table performance (depending upon the volume of parts on the table) and leads to significant energy and cost savings.

2022 is the first year, after the pandemic, that we are back at big shows again and EuroBLECH is our first major show this year. We view it as a barometer to gauge the economy next year. We are very pleased with the show this year. The number of visitors to our booth has been excellent and the amount of leads is at least as good as four years ago.

ISMR:What is your strategic focus for the company going forward?

PH: We have set a new strategic path and are growing the company worldwide with acquisitions in the U.S., as well as other global acquisitions. These will see the company grow to a turnover of between 150 and 200 million euros (employing between 700-800 people).

As we are already the biggest company in the world in wide-belt sanding (particularly in Europe and the USA), we will be close to making 75-80 million euros in turnover for wide-belt sanding (with the new acquisitions).

The 22 series WRBW 600mm wide rotary brush machine with pictogram control for deburring, edge rounding, the removal of laser oxide and finishing.

Our "bread and butter" business is edge-rounding and deburring machines. We have such a wealth of process and application knowledge in these technologies. The customer base is still strong enough for us to grow, even with the number of competitors and products in the market, so we place a strong focus on continuous innovation every year.

At Blechexpo in Stuttgart, for example, next year, we will be introducing more new technologies for our machines.

We are also working on an exciting new strategic goal and new accompanying technology. In the next year, the first working concepts will be ready and we plan to reveal the prototype at EuroBLECH in two years' time. I cannot say any more on this, I am afraid.

ISMR: How has this year been for Timesavers? What is your forecast for 2023?

PH: This year and, in fact, the last two years have been very good for us. We registered a 70-million-euro order intake worldwide this year. However, we were (like other manufacturers) affected by supply-chain issues for deliveries of microchip-related parts which delayed our delivery lead times. We had to do everything in our capacity to keep our production running to get these parts, using all our global sources (in the U.S., China, India and business partners etc.) to meet customer expectations. Because we were unable to travel in Europe over the last couple of years, due to the COVID-19 pandemic, we ran a lot of webinars which have been very good for us. Ironically, we registered our best years in 2020 and 2021, despite the fact that we were not able to physically visit our customers at the time.

It is very hard to forecast what will happen next year. All our dealers have projected similar budget ranges to this year in their 2023 projections, which is good. However, I believe that the oncoming recession will be worldwide but will probably hit Europe harder, because of the impact of higher energy prices. Because we are globally oriented, we should be able to ride this out. It is one of our strengths as a worldwide company and gives us a lot of flexibility over our competitors. Besides this geographical diversity and global customer base, we also serve multiple markets. This provides not just cross-functional experience and innovation strength, but also protects us against these economic “ups and downs".

ISMR: Can you highlight any potential growth areas for your business?

PH: Two years ago, we introduced our newly designed machines to the U.S. market and now have a new and developed market in the U.S. for our technologies. Our usage of frequency controls, HMls in every machine etc. are attractive features for the U.S. market and their dealers like our 42 and 32 series machines.

Below: Deburring, edge rounding and finishing gear wheel sample (before and after) in aluminium.

Our mother company, Timesavers in Minneapolis, will be at the FABTECH exhibition in Chicago displaying our machines with robot technology (which we have not showcased in Europe for the last few years). The technology was still in its infancy when we first exhibited this six years ago but has matured a lot now.

We have different products for different markets. Most customers know us for our edge-rounding and deburring technologies, but we also make precision grinding machines for a smaller and more niche market. We see huge potential for these machines because this market requires the knowledge and expertise that we have built up over the last ten years and which we have only, to date, been using in niche markets like titanium/exotic materials and very high-precision applications. We have not yet addressed traditional markets where the competitive products in that space are milling machines and stone grinders.

ISMR: Which trends have you identified in global surface-finishing markets?

PH: The introduction of the fibre laser several years ago was a real turning point for a lot of customers. The shift from deburring to edge rounding on sheet-metal parts has now been made by machine builders and customers, with metal burrs getting sharper and smaller.....

Our customers have indicated that they want more 'human-independent' machinery, with no operators in the mix. So, we see a push towards more handling equipment around the machine as well as ramps, conveyors, robots and vision equipment. One of the things we want to improve in the future is training and service, which is so important to help customers get the right results from our machines.

There is still a way to go but I would not be surprised if, in the next few years, we see a generation of machines that are completely automated. We are now investigating, at the request of our customers, ideas to develop a 'human-independent' machine that can determine how to process a part. We are looking for European partners to help us develop the appropriate vision systems and robots and favour long-term partnerships.
