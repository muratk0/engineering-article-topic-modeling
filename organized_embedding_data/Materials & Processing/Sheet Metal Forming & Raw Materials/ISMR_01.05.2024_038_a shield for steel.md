With up to four per cent of the world's GDP estimated to be lost through corrosion each year, it is unsurprising that the entire lifecycle of steel is a huge factor for decision-makers in many industries.

In a bid to avoid rust- and corrosion-impacting projects, hot-dip galvanizing is one of the most popular finishes for protecting and preserving steel (everything from large-scale infrastructure components through to small and intricate nuts and bolts). Dating back to the mid-1700s, the process of galvanizing has evolved over the years to become a sustainable, durable, hygienic, long-lasting and economic finish.

To protect steel items from rust and corrosion, hot-dip galvanizing is often used by many across the engineering, rail and architecture industries (to name just a few). Objects are chemically cleaned to remove any existing surface rust, oil or mill scale, before being completely immersed into a bath of molten zinc at temperatures of around 450°C. The steel is entirely coated (inside and out, in awkward corners and narrow gaps, and even in the hardest-to-reach areas) before being left to cool, allowing a metallurgic-bonded coating to form.

## Why galvanize?

This unique bonding brings an array of benefits. Galvanized steel can withstand harsh conditions for decades, without any maintenance required. It provides a tough and durable finish which boasts high resistance to mechanical damage (a vital factor during transport, storage and installation).

Galvanizing offers long-lasting protection; more than 70 years maintenance-free. This allows the overall lifecycle costs to remain low, as it significantly saves time and labour-intensive resources as well as limiting the need for downtime or replacement. This is particularly attractive for companies seeking economic benefits during the current cost-of-living crisis.

Galvanizing also provides a tough, durable, and permanent finish that, should it ever be required, can only be removed by professionals using abrasive blasting or specialist chemicals.
