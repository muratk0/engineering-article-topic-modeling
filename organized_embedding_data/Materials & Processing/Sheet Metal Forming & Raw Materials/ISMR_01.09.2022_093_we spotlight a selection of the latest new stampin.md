# We spotlight a selection of the latest new stamping processes, concepts and press models.

[TARİH: 01.09.2022 ISMR]

According to analyst Grand View Research, the global metal stamping market size was valued at US$ 206.01 billion in 2021 and is expected to grow at a compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of 4.7% from 2022 to 2030. Asia Pacific accounted for the largest revenue share of more than 36% in 2021, owing to the increasing demand for cars and consumer electronics in the region, and is expected to witness the fastest CAGR over the forecast period. Growth in that region is mainly driven by developing countries such as China, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia and Pakistan.

Metal stamping operations involve the transformation of flat metal sheets, or blanks, into specific shapes. It is a complex process that includes several metalforming techniques — blanking; punching; flanging; embossing; coining and piercing, for example. The cold-forming process uses tools, dies and stamping presses to transform sheet metal into different shapes. Stamping presses utilise the force of a moving ram to transmit force, or an amount of tonnage, to specific tooling to achieve a product's final shape, often with little or no scrap and, whenever possible, with minimal operator intervention. Stamping dies are the tools that shape and cut sheet metal parts.

Some of the most common types of metal stamping machines include mechanical, hydraulic and servo presses that are fitted with multiple dies to cut and shape the sheets of stainless steel and metals such as aluminium, zinc and copper. Greater speed and versatility, shorter cycle times and higher energy savings are just some of the hallmarks of the latest technologies. ## Technology in focus

Italian stamping specialist, AIDA, will be promoting its new DSF-NE2 servo press as well as the entire AIDA press line series (including servo presses, high-speed automatic presses for lamination and precision-forming presses) at EuroBLECH 2022 in Hanover, Germany. It will also carry out live demos at the show of ADMS (AIDA Digital Motion System), the solution it developed to analyse and optimise the management of transfer units for servo and mechanical presses.

It is a global supplier of servo and mechanical metal stamping presses, automation equipment and complete metal forming systems, with thousands of units delivered to date worldwide. Two product lines that are dedicated to the production of motor laminations are the AIDA HMX Series and AIDA MSP Series presses. Its AiCARE (AIDA Information Care System) enables the collection, analysis and management of press information by gathering data in real-time. It can be integrated with all types of AIDA presses as well as on installed presses (also from other manufacturers).

Its DSF-TE4 servo transfer presses (doublecolumn split presses designed for transfer applications) offer high levels of customisation through multiple configurations. New DSF-NE2 presses are equipped with AIDA's own low-RPM, high-torque servo motor for metalforming applications.

AP&T, with its headquarters in Ulricehamn, Sweden, develops and manufactures production solutions for sheet metalforming and press hardening of steel. Renovating, maintaining and rebuilding press tools is a growing business area.

It also offers servo-hydraulic presses, tandem lines and conventional pressing as well as an automation programme for large destackers and tool design. Its global offering includes complete production lines with presses; automation; tools; heat-treatment systems and services such as maintenance; spare part provision; optimisation and rebuilds.

Over the past few years, the company has presented several innovations such as a new production solution for complex parts made from high-strength aluminium. It also helped to deliver 'the world's first production line for hot forming of aluminium' with a new method, patented by Impression Technologies, dubbed HFQ®. It has also developed a method, with Sandvik and Cell Impact, for manufacturing fuel cell plates.

AP&T's process monitoring system for press hardening – in-line process monitoring – has also been introduced to the market. AP&T and Nordgren have also unveiled a turnkey system for the quick handling and feeding of different blank formats, without time-consuming gripper changes.

With its TemperBox®, individually defined areas of press-hardened components can be fully hardened to maximize strength while the other individually defined areas remain soft. This is to achieve desired ductility and/or to facilitate post processing such as joining or mechanical cutting. Machine in a Box, AP&T's automation concept, is aimed at local internal and external integrators. AP&T has also produced a new-generation control system to simplify the use of presses, automation equipment and production lines.

Presseccentriche S.p.A. produces transfer, blanking and servo presses, as well as presses for progressive tools, impact extrusion presses and fin die presses. Its press model 4DMRF-LD-2000 has a 2,000kN cushion and three-axis electronic transfer. The press can produce up to 27 strokes per minute.
