## HFQ® for Automotive Design

• Exchange steel parts for aluminum without the need to simplify or split the geometry

• Consolidate aluminum parts due to improved formability with fewer operations while maintaining acceptable elongation (i.e. better crash performance)

• Use high-strength grades to allow downgauging (reduce weight, reduce material usage)

• Remove the need for springback-compensation in tool and part design

• Remove reinforcement panels otherwise required to add strength

• Replace high strength Al extrusions with high-strength HFQ® pressings

ISMR: Which trends do you see developing in metal stamping markets?

AF: Aluminium take-up is a growing trend. However, the issues of differential thermal expansion in multi-material designs, when specifying part joining and assembly, will become a reason for the increasing take-up of aluminium, especially as very high-strength aluminium grades are used. To get any geometry into your parts, you really need to be using the hot-forming process.

There are generally fewer material joints using HFQ® technology. HFQ® will enable cost-effective aluminium panels to be produced through high levels of part integration which, until now, was not a possibility with conventionally pressed aluminium. I believe we will see HFQ® ultra-high strength A/B Pillars, for example, as both complex geometry and strength can be achieved in a single operation, so we will start to see aluminium replacing advanced high strength steels and Boron grades. To enable aluminium vehicle producers to achieve a leap in terms of cost-effective mass reduction of their next generation aluminium and multi-material structures, hot forming will need to be utilised.

There are generally fewer material joints using HFQ® technology. It also makes designing for thermal expansion (e.g. bonding or joining steel to aluminium) much easier since HFQ® aluminium parts can replace steel parts which are still found within certain areas of aluminium-intensive structures.

In general, there is a move towards higher strength materials and the increasing use of aluminium going forward. Our technology enables easier part design and stronger components – squeezing as much flexibility and margin as possible out of the forming process.

ISMR: Please update us on the HFQ® aluminium forming line and your relationship with AP&T?

AF: AP&T is part of the Locolite project with us and has been very helpful with its production line expertise and support. It helped us to develop the line so that it is best suited to HFQ® - the press line we have is now optimised for HFQ® forming. AP&T has helped us to create the right press line for this technology. We have the IP and expertise around HFQ® and AP&T provides the press line equipment that is suitable for the HFQ® forming process as well as its manufacturing expertise. It collaborates with us on some of the marketing aspects, too.

The production line for HFQ® includes, amongst other things, a 6000kN high-speed, hotforming press, AP&T's Multilayer Feeder Furnace, SpeedFeeders and a process and thermal monitoring system.

There are around fifteen partners on the LoCoLite project, such as UK universities and car companies. The main car company in the project is CRF, the research arm of Fiat. The Diad Group in Italy (a technology company) is also a partner and there are also partners in Germany, France and Romania. The project is closing shortly.

ISMR: Are there any other applications for the technology that you would like to discuss?

AF: For aerospace applications, cabin fit-out is very interesting for HFQ® as the volumes are high which makes the mass production route very competitive. In construction, there are applications in architecture-based projects. For the rail segment, further minimising the weight of carriages reduces track wear which is a major consideration for the sector, in addition to reduced emissions. The rail industry is interested in forming complex panels, using our process instead of slower, more expensive processes. Trucks are also a very interesting example - if you make a truck lighter, you can add a heavier payload and reduce the number of trips required.
