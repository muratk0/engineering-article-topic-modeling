ISMR SAYS: At EuroBLECH, Hans Weber Maschinenfabrik GmbH emphasised its focus on the fine blanking industry.

ISMR caught up with Hans Weber's managing director, Georg Weber, at this year's EuroBLECH exhibition.

Deburring and surface grinding is an essential part of the modern production process. Hans Weber Maschinenfabrik GmbH offers a complete range of grinding machines with different technologies for different requirements. The main focus is on deburring and rounding of thermally separated parts, cut on laser-, plasma- or gas-cutting machines.

When processing stainless steel sheets, a surface finish is also often required. Weber's machines solve this problem through a combination of different grinding techniques in a single machine. Steel components often require a defined radius and stainless steel components also sometimes require a specified finish. WEBER grinding machines use a through-feed method and can be designed for sheet sizes of up to 3.3m in width.

At this year's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover (25-29 October 2016), ISMR caught up with Weber's managing director, Georg Weber, to review its latest developments.

## New investments for increased productivity

"We are focusing nowadays mostly on deburring and edge rounding for sheet metal. They are technologies where we can grind material from 200mm to 3.3m in an economic process that is not too slow. Here, at EuroBLECH, we are also focusing on the fineblanking industry. We entered this market a couple of years ago and have been successful there. As we provide a wide range of grinding technologies, we adapted our machines to customer needs in this industry e.g. for tooling," he told ISMR.

With a long pedigree (the company is almost 100 years old) and a string of patents, Weber places special emphasis on training. Its 370 employees are currently supported by 45 apprentices. Quality, it told ISMR, is its watchword - its Bavarian headquarters boasts an in-house technical centre where innovations are tested, developed further and finally prepared ready for market launch.

"We are a family-owned company, owned by three brothers, and are constantly investing. Our philosophy is always to invest in the things we need, without restriction - this year's annual investments are around 2 million euros as we are also investing in new halls and new machine tools. We will be extending our assembly area by a further 1200 square metres so that we can produce more efficiently and shorten our delivery and production times – work on this will start at the end of the year," confirmed Georg Weber.

Our philosophy is always to invest in the things we need, without restriction.

He added that 2016 had been a good year for the company, with growth estimated at close to 30%.

"Our main market is in Europe - we also have our own office in the US. The US market, for us, has been growing. However, we follow our customers worldwide. It is important to us to keep quality constant and we design our machines so that they can handle large volumes of parts. The smallest machine we offer is 200mm x 3.3m (with top and bottom grinding etc.). The machines can handle most types of materials and processes," he told ISMR.

"Our next steps are to focus on service, maintain and build on the good relationships we have with customers and ensure that their machine uptime and operations run for as long as possible."

Kjellberg Finsterwalde emerged from the former Kjellberg Elektro-Maschinen GmbH. It was founded in 1922 by the Swedish inventor, Oscar Kjellberg, who received a German imperial patent for coated welding electrodes. Kjellberg Finsterwalde has been among the world's leading manufacturers of welding electrodes, welding techniques and plasma cutting systems for decades.

Kjellberg Finsterwalde showed ISMR a number of new plasma cutting, welding and fibre laser innovations at EuroBLECH 2016.

With models from its HiFocus neo and Smart Focus series, Kjellberg Finsterwalde demonstrated its latest plasma cutting units at this October's EuroBLECH exhibition in Hanover. Booth visitors had the opportunity to watch cutting demonstrations with Kjellberg's Contour Cut technology.

Kjellberg's showcase of cutting technology was completed by its XFocus fibre laser for cutting sheet thicknesses ranging from 0.3 up to 20mm. As ready-made complete solutions, the fibre laser systems can be used on all common guiding systems for plasma cutting – separately or in combination with other tools.

## InFocus and submerged-arc welding

Kjellberg Finsterwalde showed the full application range of its InFocus technology at EuroBLECH 2016.

“Using an additive manufacturing SLM (Selective Laser Melting) process, Kjellberg Finsterwalde has developed a highly functional, customised gas shield nozzle to protect the heated area of the metal sheet against oxidation when TIG welding."

Various steels and non-ferrous metals with a broad range of material thicknesses can be welded or soldered for high-quality joints," the company told ISMR.

"This TIG technology achieves process-stable and reproducible results in fully or partly automated production processes.

With InFocus, thick sheets can also be root-welded. By using the InFocus keyhole process, the web can be made bigger. The smaller seam cross-section reduces the number of layers that have to be applied during the subsequent submerged arc welding. When using InFocus as the root layer, there is no need to gouge the root. Savings in labour costs and electric energy, as well as less thermal distortion, have further positive effects on the efficiency of the production process.

Stainless steel welding was demonstrated at a longitudinal seam welding machine using cold wire feeding and a trailing shield for the secondary gas protection of the completed weld seam. Individually-shaped trailing shields prevented a reaction of the material with the ambient air during the welding of tube-to-tube joints or tube-to-flange joints.

"InFocus is suitable for welding or soldering various contours of steel and non-ferrous metals. Due to their low geometric interference, the torches are also suitable for seam welds which are difficult to access. The high-quality joints meet the highest industry standards and, due to the numerous advantages of the InFocus process, increase the efficiency of the production process," Carsten Trentau told ISMR.

This includes the long lifetime of the consumables due to efficient cooling up to the torch tip. The change of consumables can be done quickly because of the small number of consumables and the prefabricated cathode which guarantees a defined tool centre-point.

"This year, we have a new product that completes the InFocus product range," commented Dr. Michael Schnick, MD, R&D. "We have developed a highly functional, customised gas shield to protect the heated area of the metal sheet against oxidation etc. The gas nozzle is produced using an additive manufacturing or SLM (Selective Laser Melting) process.

"The gas shield has to adapt to the workpiece but also to the optimum position needed for the welding process on that workpiece. This may mean adopting a special angle or feed etc. So, we take all the data on the welding process and the product from the customer and bring it to the CAD development centre, then we custom-design the nozzle and print that using the SLM process and send it to the customer. So, we have designed the best solution for the gas shield for each customer product which optimises the gas shield and the gas flow to meet their requirements in terms of higher quality and higher productivity."

SLM technology has proved to be ideal for this application and enabled Kjellberg to design flexibility into the nozzle design, and the gas distribution system within it, to effectively customise gas shield quality to differing customer workpiece and product requirements.

At EuroBLECH, Kjellberg Finsterwalde also presented a tried and tested technique for equipping manual, partly- and fullyautomated welding workstations for submerged arc welding.

## Live plasma cutting demonstrations

Kjellberg Finsterwalde also presented live demonstrations of two plasma power sources on its booth at EuroBLECH 2016. Its patent for FineFocus plasma cutting, developed in close cooperation with Manfred von Ardenne's research institute and filed in 1962, makes Kjellberg one of the oldest plasma machine manufacturers in the market.

The Smart Focus 130 and the HiFocus 161i neo alternately cut 10mm and 4mm mild steel with Kjellberg's patented Contour Cut technology. This double demonstration enabled visitors to experience the CNC-guided plasma cutting process live.

"Contour Cut technology makes it possible to cut the finest contours and small holes with a diameter to material thickness ratio of 1:1 (best quality). The technology enhancement (Contour Cut Speed) enables the highest cutting speeds. With Contour Cut Speed, contours can be cut at similar quality up to 50% faster. Because of the shorter processing time, the costs per cutting metre can be reduced," explained Carsten Trentau.

The basis for this lies in the swirlgas technology developed by Kjellberg Finsterwalde. The plasma arc is constricted and stabilised using a swirl gas, ensuring that cut results in a wide range of material thicknesses require almost no re-work. With efficient torch cooling, long-lasting consumables and individual gas combinations, the costs per cutting metre can further be reduced.

"We have now completed our Smart Focus line, so we can offer models from 130 up to 400A. The 400 A system can cut 100mm stainless steel, aluminium and mild steel. With our plasma cutting demonstrations, you can see the high quality of the holes. No processing work is needed after cutting – the material is ready to weld. The HiFocus and Smart Focus series are highly developed plasma cutting systems which deliver precise, reproducible results when cutting material thicknesses of up to 160mm (HiFocus) or up to 100mm (Smart Focus)," added Carsten Trentau.

With a maximum cutting current of 400 A for material thicknesses of up to 100mm, the Smart Focus 400 expands the application areas of this plasma series. The results, said the company, are sharp cut edges and smooth cut surfaces, fine markings and low angular deviations.

At EuroBLECH 2016, Kjellberg Finsterwalde also announced a new service offering for used plasma cutting systems.

"Kjellberg Service Packages include all necessary original spare parts and consumables which are recommended for the yearly preventive maintenance of a Kjellberg power source. Regular maintenance measures help maintain the operational safety and improve the plant availability permanently, resulting in reduced downtime and repair costs. This simplifies the planning of maintenance dates and costs considerably," explained Kjellberg.

Kjellberg Service Packages include, among others, preventive change of components; change of coolant and environmentallyappropriate disposal; cleaning of the power source and all its components; inspection of the components and training for operators and technicians.

Ficep is a leading supplier of structural steel and plate fabrication equipment over the past 85 years. Established in 1930 and located at the foot of the Alps in northern Italy, 16km from the border with Switzerland, it focuses on the engineering and manufacturing of machine tools for the metalworking industry.

Its portfolio of machinery includes steel section and plate fabrication equipment, automated handling and forging systems. The company manufactures a wide range of machines available for fabrication with options to punch, mark, drill, scribe, machine and shear or thermal cut. Its GEMINI plate processing machine can drill, mark, tap, countersink, mill and cut with plasma or gas and has been further improved with faster positioning and increased tool change capability offering users more options.

At EuroBLECH this year, Ficep introduced its new TIPO G hybrid line for automatic CNC drilling, milling and thermal cutting of plates. This intriguing project evolved from a traditional mobile plate processing line on top of which a series of innovative solutions, featured on the company's Gemini platform, have been installed. To unify the benefits of the two technologies, Ficep adapted the Tipo A roller method and handling and interfaced these to the gantry structure of the Gemini with auxiliary axes and a cutting bevel head.

Ficep's RAPID is a new CNC high speed drilling line for angles and flats. The machine's powerful spindles with their high rotational and feed speeds, together with the new generation high-performance tools, enables the use of Indexable Carbide Drilling tools to further enhance productivity. The RAPID CNC drilling lines are also modular and can incorporate scribing, hard stamp marking, single or double shearing or, alternatively, a high-speed circular carbide saw.

## Technology in focus

Ficep's Excalibur 12 is a small footprint automatic CNC beam line for drilling shaped and flat profiles, aimed at small-to-medium-sized structural steel fabricators who may not have the space or output to justify the installation of a multiple spindle drilling line. It offers fast carbide drilling, scribing, slotting, tapping, countersinking of beams and requires 50% of the space of a conventional beam drilling line, according to Ficep.

The Italian manufacturer was one of the first companies to manufacture the monospindle gantry drilling line to process beams using a cantilever system. The wireless remote control feature of the Excalibur eliminates the need for the operator to stand by the CNC control of the machine. The operator is therefore able to respond quickly to events such as a drill bit starting to fail.

The company has a continuous culture of investment, particularly in new ways to improve its product quality and productivity as well as to reduce time to market of its products. One example is a machine tool for a customer which required no paper or printing to build. This met a growing trend to save costs and is also in line with the principles of Industry 4.0.

Ficep's RAPID 25 and 35 range, launched over the last year, is high-speed drilling, milling and sawing equipment for high spec. work (such as transmission towers, bridges and applications where quality and speed is a prerequisite).

Another major development, the Ficep Endeavour, turns the standard drilling machine into a multi-process machining centre. Its new auxiliary axis movement allows multiple holes to be processed without moving the section in the machine. The drill spindles move in a 250mm stroke. This enables multiple holes, scribing and machining to be made very quickly.

Ficep machinery has been used for a wide range of construction projects globally, including the Burj Khalifa (Dubai), The Shard and Heathrow Terminal 5 in (London), the Freedom Tower in New York and the train station in Vienna.
