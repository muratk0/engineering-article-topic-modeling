# FOCUS ON COIL PROCESSING

With the continued push toward even smaller batch runs, die changes and set-up time have become more critical. Flexibility of the major components (press, feed, straightener, decoiler, coil cradle) is an important factor. The time saved in set-up and the flexibility of roll feeding help manufacturers to increase productivity.

Manufacturers are bringing out new coil processing innovations to help customers continually improve production efficiency, increase accuracy and smooth material flow on the line. ISMR examines a selection of these as well as new machine launches and line upgrades.

## LEVELLING AND FEEDING

Several production processes (such as mechanical and thermal cutting) cause internal tensions and can lead to sheet metal parts that are not flat. These defects in the material impede subsequent working processes. Flat and stress-free sheet metal parts improve the stability of subsequent processes in sheet metal forming. The quality of the final product is therefore improved, whereas the amount of refinishing work and scrap decline significantly.

The most common defects are coil set and cross bow. Coil set is caused by plastic deformation during the coiling process. The cross bow, however, is caused by the irregular cooling down of the rolling stock across the entire coil width. Coil defects are usually caused by the rolling process itself or by subsequent cutting processes.

When unlevelled sheet parts are processed, enclosed tensions in the sheet metal are unleashed by chamfering. As a result defects in angularity occur, which increase scrap levels and the expense of refinishing work. Enclosed tensions and unevenness of sheet metal parts result in shape distortion and thereby affect the dimensional accuracy of the bending process. Selective thermal treatment leads to the release of internal stress, which in turn causes deformation of the material.

Sheet metal parts which are not flat extend throughput times in different ways. Pre-arrangements for welding grow with unlevelled material. Processing uneven sheet metal parts also increases the amount of refinishing required. Process stability of robot welding also declines when using unlevelled material.

“The most common procedure to level laser- and flame-cut parts from 0.5 up to 50mm material thickness is by roller levelling. Mechanical straighteners are used for parts with low requirements for flatness, since mechanical straighteners usually need five to six applications to produce good levelling results,” sheet metal specialist, ARKU Maschinenbau GmbH, told ISMR.

Roller levelling is a bending process. The unlevelled sheet metal part is deformed with a series of alternate bends. The alternate bends are created by passing the sheet between an upper and lower set of levelling rollers. The levelling rollers are offset by half the roller spacing in the direction of travel.

As a result, the sheet metal takes a wave-like path through the precision leveller. The wave should be greatest at the entry of the machine and smallest at the exit (comparable to a decaying sinusoidal curve). The elastic-plastic alternate bends and the constant decline of bending intensity thereby produces flat and nearly stress-free sheet metal parts. Compared to other methods, roller levelling is an easy and rapid way to obtain flat parts, sheets or strips with high accuracy.

“The HiCap 25 is a customer-driven design that was developed with the special requirements of perforated sheet processors in mind,” stressed Ewald Hund, ARKU Technical Director. Its patented EcoPlan drive requires 30 per cent less energy than conventional high-capacity levellers, he added.

ARKU’s FlatMaster 88 was also introduced at the EuroBLECH exhibition. “It delivers increased speed, new user-friendly features and 10 per cent greater energy efficiency than comparable machines. This machine processes oval parts 50 per cent faster, while using less energy overall. And reduced energy use means a more efficient design where components wear less,” ARKU said.

A roller leveller for sheets up to 60mm thickness has been developed at ARKU. The precision leveller was finished and in operation in December 2010 at a German roller mill. This leveller for large workpieces has a maximum material width of 3000mm and weighs over 150 tonnes.

## LINE UPGRADES

Bronx International has provided coil processing solutions to steel and aluminium companies worldwide since 1945.

In 2010, it completed a significant Engineering and Technology Improvement Programme at First Aluminium, Nigeria on its No. 1 Paint Line, commonly referred to as “Bessy”. This line was originally installed in Alcan, Birmingham, UK before being relocated to Nigeria in 1995. “Bessy” has proved to be a very successful line for First Aluminium, so much so that it needed to increase its production capability to meet growing local demand. Product quality was also a key target for improvement. Bronx was invited to conduct a detailed paid audit of the complete line operation to identify those areas where improvements were possible that would provide the greatest returns on investment.

From the initial audit, many areas of improvement were identified as the Bronx Technology Team worked with the customer to develop a short list of key modifications that would meet required expectations for the investment available.

## These included:

■ Replacing the existing gas-fired oven for an electric infra-red oven. Nigeria cannot provide consistent gas supply and this had previously resulted in lost production time that constituted over 30 per cent of available time.

■ Replacing the decoiler and coil car. The existing unit was causing damage to the product causing unacceptable scrap losses.

■ Replacing the paint coater machine. Paint quality and paint control and consumption needed to be significantly improved.

■ Improving strip steering. The strip tracking through the line was causing losses and needed upgrading to guarantee no product losses.

■ Upgrading the exit section. The equipment in this area needed upgrading to ensure no strip damage and a new Edge Guide System was installed.

Further work undertaken by Bronx on the line included installing:

■ Complete new hydraulic systems for sections of the line.

■ New hydraulic piping for the complete line.

■ Complete electrical control system, control panels and drives with Siemens equipment utilising HMI operator controls along with new electrical cabling of the line.

Multiple operations, including punching, on one line are the speciality of manufacturers of customised lines such as IRON SpA. The Italian family-run coil processing specialist recently developed a new concept line for flexible punching and bending from coil (FPB). It is a flexible modular line that incorporates a punch press (with thick turret tooling), a part-turning device (after punching), positive and negative bends on the punched parts and can handle part dimensions ranging from 650 – 1500mm in width x 1500 – 3600mm in length.

Holvoet NV, with plants located in Kortrijk and Gent, Belgium, has purchased a third coil processing system designed and built by Red Bud Industries. The 3.5mm (.138”) thick x 1829mm (72”) wide multi-blanking line combines the slit-to-width and cut-to-length processes into one operation to produce custom-sized blanks straight from a master coil. The line is designed to process surface critical material such as stainless steel, aluminium and pre-painted as well as cold rolled and galvanised materials.

Featured in the line are Red Bud’s precision grip feed system and CNC programmable slitter. This combination of machines enables Holvoet to produce blanks with width and length tolerances of +/- .127mm (.005”). The line is capable of processing coils of up to 15,422kg (34,000 lb) at widths of up to 1830mm (72”) and thicknesses of up to 3.5mm (.138”).

## ROLLFORMING INNOVATIONS

With exports constituting more than 95 per cent of its turnover, Jensen A/S (based in Copenhagen) has developed into a company with an international orientation. Although it has produced rollforming machines since 1961 and supplied more than 800 machines to the global rollforming market, its presence at EuroBLECH last year was an important platform for introducing its latest rollforming technology. Jensen A/S displayed a type M40-16/330 rollforming machine at the EuroBLECH exhibition that had already been sold to a German client who were due to take delivery of a second rollforming machine from Jensen A/S. The roll shaft diameter of this rollforming machine was 40mm and the centre distance was 330mm. The machine is based on Jensen’s flexible cassette system and was designed for the mounting of four cassettes with up to four roller stations each (maximum strip width of 300mm).

Jensen, in collaboration with SSAB, also showed the latest developments in rollforming of high-strength steel, a field with ever expanding possibilities. It also showcased its new Jensen trolleys for internal roller and cassette transport as well as a variety of mechanical auxiliary equipment. At EuroBLECH, it introduced JPR-oil which has been developed specifically for rollforming.

STAM was established in 1963 in Ponzano Veneto (Treviso) Italy. Today, the company is an established European and worldwide leader in the building of sheet metal working machines from coil material. The company employs 130 people and has additional works in Spain and Brazil. More than 1000 machines and lines have been delivered.

At STAM, every stage of the process of building the machines is managed in-house, covering mechanical, electrical and software design, building (including fabricating, machining, assembling and painting) and mechanical, electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic testing. STAM integrates the rollforming process with other technologies such as punching, bending, notching, welding etc. Its rollforming lines have been built to accommodate thicknesses of material from 0.3 – 13mm producing profiles for every industry and application.

In addition to standard machines, STAM has just developed a wide range of specialised lines offering appropriate levels of automation and flexibility and to meet the just-in-time requirements required in many industries. A comparatively recent development is its rollformers that produce variable section profiles previously only possible using ‘inflexible methods’. The flexibility is achieved without compromising high rates of production.

The STAM range of coil cut-up lines has developed and grown continuously and now includes equipment for material of up to 20mm thick as well as equipment for use with high tensile steel. In co-operation with other companies, STAM has in recent years also supplied a number of blanking lines for the motor industry. ■
